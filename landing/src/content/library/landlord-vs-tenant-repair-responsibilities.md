## Which repairs does the landlord pay for?

The landlord pays for repairs that keep the home fit to live in and its systems working. State statutes spell this out. Arizona's ARS 33-1324 requires the landlord to:

1. Meet building codes that affect health and safety.
2. Make all repairs needed to keep the unit "fit and habitable."
3. Keep common areas clean and safe.
4. Keep electrical, plumbing, sanitary, heating, cooling and supplied appliances in good and safe working order.
5. Provide trash receptacles and arrange removal.
6. Supply running water, hot water, and heat or cooling where installed.

Florida's 83.51 sets a similar base. It adds pest control, locks and keys, and garbage removal for buildings other than single-family homes and duplexes, unless the lease says otherwise.

## Which repairs does the tenant pay for?

The tenant pays for damage the tenant, family or guests cause, and for basic upkeep. The tenant duty lists in Arizona (ARS 33-1341), Florida (83.52) and Virginia (55.1-1227) are close. The tenant must:

- Keep the unit clean and safe as its condition permits.
- Remove trash in a clean and safe way.
- Keep plumbing fixtures clean.
- Use electrical, plumbing, heating and cooling systems and appliances in a reasonable way.
- Not destroy, damage or remove any part of the premises, or let others do so.

Virginia adds that the tenant must keep the unit free of insects and pests and report them promptly. Arizona adds that the tenant must promptly tell the landlord in writing about any repair the landlord must make.

## Who pays for what: a table by item

Cause decides most cases. Normal use and age are the landlord's cost. Misuse and damage are the tenant's cost.

| Item | Landlord pays when | Tenant pays when |
|---|---|---|
| Clogged drain or toilet | Old pipes, roots, a failed sewer line | Grease, wipes, hair, toys, misuse |
| Light bulbs | Common areas; fixtures that fail | Bulbs inside the unit, if the lease assigns them |
| HVAC filters | Lease silent; the system itself | Lease assigns filter changes to the tenant |
| HVAC system | Breakdown from age or wear | Damage from a missed filter change the lease required, or misuse |
| Landlord-supplied appliance | Breakdown from normal use | Damage from misuse |
| Tenant-owned appliance | Never | Always |
| Smoke and CO alarms | Install them (Florida requires working units at move-in in single-family homes and duplexes) | Do not remove; in Virginia, maintain to the fire code standard |
| Pests | Building-wide or structural causes | In Virginia, the tenant must keep the unit free of pests and report them |
| Broken window | Storm, age, failed seal | Tenant, family or guest broke it |
| Lockout and rekey | Lock fails from wear | Tenant lost keys, if the lease says so |

## Who pays for clogged drains?

A clogged drain is a landlord repair unless the tenant caused it. Florida's 83.52 and Arizona's 33-1341 both require tenants to keep plumbing fixtures clean and use them in a reasonable way. So a drain clogged by grease or wipes is tenant-caused damage.

What to do:

1. **Send the plumber first.** A backed-up drain can cause water damage fast.
2. **Ask the plumber to write the cause** on the invoice: roots, grease, a foreign object.
3. **Bill the tenant only when the invoice shows misuse.** Attach the invoice and any photo.

## Who pays for appliance repairs?

The landlord pays to repair appliances the landlord supplies, unless the tenant damaged them. Arizona's 33-1324 lists "appliances... supplied or required to be supplied" among the landlord's duties.

For voucher units, 24 CFR 982.404 says the family can be held responsible if it fails to provide and maintain appliances that the owner is not required to provide but that the family must supply. So a tenant-owned washer is the tenant's to fix.

## Can a lease shift repairs to the tenant?

A lease can shift small tasks, and in some states more, but not the core duty to keep the home habitable. Two examples:

- **Arizona:** for a single-family home, the parties may agree in writing that the tenant handles trash, utilities and "specified repairs, maintenance tasks, alterations and remodeling." The deal must be in good faith, supported by consideration, and not used to avoid the duties to meet code and keep the home fit and habitable. For other units, the agreement must be in a separate signed writing.
- **Florida:** the landlord's code and structure duties "may be altered or modified in writing" for a single-family home or duplex.

## How do I write repair duties into the lease?

Write each duty as a short, plain rule. Name the item, who does it, and how often. Sample clauses:

1. **Filters.** Tenant replaces the HVAC filter (size [size]) every [90] days.
2. **Bulbs and batteries.** Tenant replaces light bulbs inside the unit and smoke alarm batteries, where state law allows.
3. **Drains.** Tenant pays for clogs caused by grease, wipes or other objects, as shown on the plumber's invoice.
4. **Reporting.** Tenant reports leaks, mold, pests and broken systems in writing within [24] hours.
5. **Landlord duties.** Landlord repairs structure, roof, plumbing, electrical, heating, cooling and supplied appliances, unless the tenant caused the damage.

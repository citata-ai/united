Kansas gives landlords wide room on rent. I read the relevant statutes on the Kansas Revisor of Statutes site on 2026-10-19. There is no cap, local rent control is banned, and the main clock is the 30-day notice for month-to-month tenancies.

| State rent cap | None | (no statute) |
| Local rent control | Banned for private property | K.S.A. 12-16,120(a) |

## Does Kansas have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

No. Kansas has no state cap on rent increases, and it bars local rent control.

K.S.A. 12-16,120(a) says no political subdivision, "including, but not limited to, a county, municipality or township," shall "enact, maintain or enforce" any ordinance that controls "the amount of rent charged" for privately owned residential or commercial property.

The statute has 2 narrow exceptions:

- **Public ownership.** A city may manage rent on property it owns (K.S.A. 12-16,120(b)).
- **Voluntary deals.** An owner may agree to rent limits in return for grants or incentives (K.S.A. 12-16,120(c)).

A city may not force rent limits as a condition of a building permit, plat or zoning change (K.S.A. 12-16,120(d)).

## How many days notice does a Kansas landlord need to raise the rent?

Kansas statutes set no specific notice period for a rent increase. We found no such section in the Residential Landlord and Tenant Act.

In practice, a rent change for a month-to-month tenant takes effect at the start of a new period. The notice to end a month-to-month tenancy is 30 days, ending on a rent-paying date (K.S.A. 58-2570(b)). So the safe rule is:

- Give written notice of the new rent.
- Make it take effect on a rent-paying date.
- Make that date at least 30 days after the tenant receives the notice.

Worked example: rent is due on the 1st. You hand the notice to the tenant on March 10. The first rent-paying date at least 30 days later is May 1. The new rent starts May 1.

| Notice received | 30 days later | First rent date after that |
|---|---|---|
| March 10 | April 9 | May 1 |
| March 31 | April 30 | May 1 |
| April 2 | May 2 | June 1 |

## Can a Kansas landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

Not unless the lease allows it. A fixed-term lease sets the rent for the term. K.S.A. 58-2572(c) refers to a rent increase that "does not conflict with a lease agreement in effect." That wording assumes the lease controls.

If you want the right to raise rent mid-term, write a clear clause in the lease. Name the amount or the formula and the notice you will give. Without one, wait for the lease to end and offer a new lease at the new rent.

K.S.A. 58-2570(b) adds a point on terms. A lease for a definite term of more than 30 days is not month-to-month, even if rent is paid every 30 days.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Kansas?

A month-to-month tenancy in Kansas ends with written notice that names a rent-paying date "not less than 30 days after the receipt of the notice" (K.S.A. 58-2570(b)). The same rule binds landlord and tenant.

Other periods in the same section:

- **Week-to-week:** written notice at least 7 days before the termination date (K.S.A. 58-2570(a)).
- **Military orders:** a tenant in the U.S. military whose orders require the move needs to give no more than 15 days' written notice (K.S.A. 58-2570(b)).

A landlord may give a tenant a form to sign as the notice to vacate. If that form adds terms not in the lease, it must carry the boldface warning in K.S.A. 58-2570(e). Without it, the extra terms do not bind the tenant.

## Do cities in Kansas add their own rent or notice rules?

Kansas cities may not add rent control. K.S.A. 12-16,120 voids any local ordinance that controls the amount of rent on private property.

The statute speaks to rent amounts and purchase prices. It does not mention rental licensing or inspection programs. Check the city where your unit is for those local rules.

## Retaliation: when a Kansas rent increase is not allowed

K.S.A. 58-2572(a) bars a landlord from "increasing rent or decreasing services" to retaliate after the tenant:

1. complained to a code agency about a health or safety violation,
2. complained to the landlord about a violation under K.S.A. 58-2553, or
3. organized or joined a tenants' union.

K.S.A. 58-2572(c) allows a good-faith increase to cover acts of God, utility rate increases, property tax increases or other higher operating costs. Keep records of those costs if you raise rent soon after a complaint.

## Kansas rent increase checklist

- Check the lease. No mid-term increase unless the lease allows it.
- For month-to-month, give written notice 30+ days before a rent date.
- Keep proof of the date the tenant received the notice.
- Do not raise rent right after a protected complaint without a cost reason.
- Keep cost records: tax bills, utility rates, insurance.

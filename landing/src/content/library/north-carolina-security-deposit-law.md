North Carolina puts its deposit rules in the Tenant Security Deposit Act, G.S. 42-50 to 42-56. The Act applies to anyone in the business of renting or managing residential units by the week, month or year. Single rooms are excluded (G.S. 42-56). We read every section below on ncleg.gov on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit cap, week to week | 2 weeks' rent | G.S. 42-51(b) |
| Deposit cap, month to month | 1.5 months' rent | G.S. 42-51(b) |
| Deposit cap, longer terms | 2 months' rent | G.S. 42-51(b) |
| Where to hold it | Trust account at a licensed, federally insured institution, or a bond | G.S. 42-50 |
| Tell the tenant where it is | Within 30 days after the lease term starts | G.S. 42-50 |
| Return and itemized list | 30 days after the tenancy ends and possession returns | G.S. 42-52 |
| Final accounting, if claim is unclear | 60 days | G.S. 42-52 |
| Tenant address unknown | Hold the balance at least 6 months | G.S. 42-52 |
| Sale or transfer of the unit | Transfer or refund within 30 days | G.S. 42-54 |
| Pet fee | Reasonable, nonrefundable | G.S. 42-53 |
| Interest | No rule in the Act | G.S. 42-50 to 42-56 |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in North Carolina?

The maximum security deposit in North Carolina depends on the lease term. G.S. 42-51(b) sets 3 caps:

- **Week to week:** 2 weeks' rent.
- **Month to month:** 1.5 months' rent.
- **Terms longer than month to month:** 2 months' rent.

Worked example: rent is $1,500 a month.

| Lease type | Cap formula | Maximum deposit |
|---|---|---|
| Month to month | 1.5 x $1,500 | $2,250 |
| 12-month lease | 2 x $1,500 | $3,000 |
| Week to week at $375 a week | 2 x $375 | $750 |

A pet fee is a different thing. G.S. 42-53 lets you charge a reasonable, nonrefundable fee for pets. That fee does not count against the deposit cap.

## How many days does a North Carolina landlord have to return a security deposit?

A North Carolina landlord has 30 days to return the deposit. The clock starts when the tenancy ends and the tenant delivers possession (G.S. 42-52). By that date, mail or deliver the itemized list together with the balance.

Sometimes you cannot know the full claim in 30 days. A large repair bill may still be open. Then G.S. 42-52 asks for 2 steps:

1. **Interim accounting** within 30 days after the tenancy ends and possession returns.
2. **Final accounting** within 60 days after the same date.

If you do not know the tenant's address, apply the deposit as allowed after 30 days. Then hold the balance for the tenant to collect for at least 6 months (G.S. 42-52).

If you sell the property, G.S. 42-54 gives you 30 days to act. Transfer the remaining deposit to the buyer and mail the tenant the buyer's name and address. Or return the remaining deposit to the tenant.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in North Carolina?

A North Carolina landlord can deduct only for the 8 uses in G.S. 42-51(a):

1. **Unpaid rent**, plus water, sewer or electric charges billed under G.S. 62-110.
2. **Damage to the premises**, including smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms.
3. **Damages for not finishing the rental period**, with exceptions for some lawful early terminations.
4. **Unpaid bills** that become a lien on the property because of the tenant's stay.
5. **Costs to re-rent** after the tenant's breach, including reasonable broker fees.
6. **Removal and storage** of the tenant's property after a summary ejectment.
7. **Court costs.**
8. **Any fee allowed by G.S. 42-46**, such as a lawful late fee.

Item 3 has exceptions. You may not keep the deposit for the early end if the tenant ended the lease under G.S. 42-45 or G.S. 42-45.1. The same holds if the landlord's breach of G.S. 42-42(a) or Article 2A forced the tenant out.

Two hard limits apply. G.S. 42-52 bars deductions for normal wear and tear. It also bars keeping more than your actual damages.

## Does a North Carolina landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes, a North Carolina landlord must give a written, itemized list. G.S. 42-52 says the landlord "in writing shall itemize any damage" and mails or delivers it with the balance. The deadline is 30 days.

A clear list shows:

- [ ] The tenant's name, the unit and the move-out date.
- [ ] The total deposit held.
- [ ] Each deduction, with the G.S. 42-51 category and the amount.
- [ ] Invoices or photos for each damage item.
- [ ] The balance refunded, or the amount still owed.
- [ ] A note if a final accounting will follow within 60 days.

Take dated move-in and move-out photos. They help you show the difference between damage and normal wear and tear.

## Must a North Carolina landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

A North Carolina landlord must keep the deposit in a trust account, but the Act has no interest rule. We read all of G.S. 42-50 to 42-56. None of these sections requires interest on the deposit.

G.S. 42-50 gives you 2 options for holding the money:

1. **Trust account** at a licensed, federally insured depository institution, or a trust institution allowed to do business in North Carolina.
2. **Bond** from an insurance company licensed in North Carolina.

You may hold the trust account outside North Carolina only if you also give the tenant an adequate bond. Within 30 days after the lease term starts, tell the tenant the name and address of the bank or the insurer (G.S. 42-50).

## What is the penalty for a North Carolina landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

The penalty is the loss of the right to keep any of the deposit, plus damages and possible attorney's fees. G.S. 42-55 lets the tenant sue for an accounting and the balance. The tenant may also recover damages from the landlord's noncompliance.

A willful failure costs more. If you willfully skip the deposit, bond or notice rules, you lose the right to retain any part of the deposit under G.S. 42-51. If the court finds willful noncompliance, it may also award attorney's fees as court costs.

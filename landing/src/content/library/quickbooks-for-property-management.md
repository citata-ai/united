## What does QuickBooks do well for rentals?

QuickBooks can track business income, expenses and financial reports when the account structure fits the work. Intuit's pricing page lists bookkeeping, invoicing and reporting features by plan. Those functions can help a landlord or management company keep its own books.

Separate the business model before setting up accounts. A landlord tracking owned homes has different needs from a company handling money for clients. Do not reuse a simple owner setup without reviewing that difference.

Intuit's Desktop guidance describes separate company files for rental property activity and the management company's own activity. That is product-specific guidance, not proof that every Online plan follows the same steps.

Ask an accountant to define properties, entities, fees and transfers. Then test an actual closed period. A useful accounting setup should produce reports your accountant can review without rebuilding every entry.

## Where does QuickBooks fall short on trust accounting?

QuickBooks falls short when your configuration cannot clearly explain client balances and reconcile them to the bank and control records. This is a workflow test, not a claim that all QuickBooks use is invalid.

A general bank reconciliation does not by itself prove that every owner or tenant balance is correct. Ask the accountant to show the bank balance, ledger control balance and underlying client balances.

Test deposits, prepayments, owner reserves and money owed to vendors. Add a returned payment and inspect the correction. A system that needs a manually repaired spreadsheet for every exception may be hard to sustain.

Do not treat client receipts as company revenue without accounting review. Keep company fees distinct from money held or moved for others. This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## Which PMS tools integrate with QuickBooks?

DoorLoop advertises QuickBooks synchronization on its pricing page. That makes it a connection to investigate, not proof that every edition, plan or record type is supported. Ask the vendor to demonstrate your exact setup.

PMS means property management system. Decide which system controls accounting before connecting it to another ledger. A connection can otherwise create duplicate entries or conflicting balances.

Test a charge, receipt, expense and correction. Confirm the transfer direction and timing for each. Then change a posted entry and inspect how the other system receives that change.

Ask who resolves errors and how staff find failed transfers. Confirm whether your accountant needs a complete ledger in QuickBooks or only the management company's own business activity. Moving every property transaction can be unnecessary when the purpose is corporate reporting.

## When should you switch to property management software?

Switch when required property work cannot be completed reliably in your current setup. There is no verified universal unit threshold in this guide. Use recurring errors, missing records and staff workload to decide.

Look beyond accounting. Leasing, maintenance, owner statements, tenant communication and payment control can drive the need for a dedicated property system. A spreadsheet workaround may be acceptable when it is simple and reviewed. It becomes a concern when no one can explain or maintain it.

Write a list of tasks that fail today. Ask each vendor to complete those same tasks during a demo. Do not replace working software merely because a competing product has more features.

Keep QuickBooks for corporate books if that remains useful. The choice need not be a single tool for every purpose. Define each system's role and prevent duplicate entry.

## How should you test the accounting setup?

Test the final reports and exception handling before moving live money or records. Use scrubbed data from a period your accountant already understands.

- **Structure:** separate business and property activity as approved.
- **Receipts:** assign rent to the right property and period.
- **Funds:** explain deposits, prepayments and owner balances.
- **Payments:** trace a vendor bill to its payment.
- **Correction:** reverse a returned payment without hiding history.
- **Reports:** compare owner and company outputs.
- **Connection:** test transfers without creating duplicates.

Have another staff member repeat the steps from written instructions. If only the setup expert can explain the records, the process needs more work.

Keep dated source reports before any import. Compare opening balances and retain supporting documents in a company-controlled archive. A successful connection does not prove a complete migration.

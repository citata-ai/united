## Do Public Fees Get More or Fewer Owner Leads?

Public fees can help owners judge fit, but the effect on your leads needs a local test. Treat better lead quality as a hypothesis. Do not claim a sales gain before you measure it.

An owner who cannot afford your service may leave before calling. That can reduce inquiries while saving staff time. Another owner may trust a clear fee page and book a call. Your sales records must tell you which effect matters.

Before you publish, record how inquiries turn into signed agreements. Keep the source, property type, service area and reason for each lost sale. After publication, use the same fields. Note other changes, such as new ads or a changed service area.

Judge the page by the work you win and can serve well. A large list of poor-fit contacts can hide weak sales. A smaller list can still fail if the signed work needs constant discounts.

Ask sales staff what changed in calls. Do owners arrive with better questions? Do they understand leasing charges? Save these notes with the results. They explain changes that a traffic chart cannot show.

## What Should a Property Management Fee Table Include?

A fee table should show the charge, its basis, its trigger and the work included. Put terms next to the fee, where an owner can see them.

Use this draft structure with your own approved prices. The entries are suggested fields, not claims about market rates.

| Fee | Price format | Explain beside the price |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly management | Percent or fixed amount | Collected rent, scheduled rent, or another base |
| New owner setup | Per account, property, or unit | Records, account setup and any site review |
| Tenant placement | Fixed amount or rent share | Advertising, showings, screening and lease work |
| Renewal | Fixed amount or stated formula | What work starts the charge |
| Maintenance support | Included or separate | Any invoice markup and approval terms |
| Exit work | Included or separate | Final records, funds and open tasks |

Do not put a low rate above a long list of hidden conditions. An owner needs to understand the likely total cost. Show what happens during vacancy and late payment. State whether optional services need a separate quote.

Use consistent terms across the website, proposal and agreement. Give the fee page an effective date. When prices change, keep the old terms with existing signed records. Assign someone to check the public page against the current sales offer.

## How Can AI Search Use a Public Fee Table?

A public text table gives search systems information they can read and compare. It does not create a right to appear in an answer.

Google Search Central says important content should be available in text. Its AI features guidance also says special AI files or special schema are unnecessary. Google does not guarantee indexing or serving a page. Those are verified platform rules, not a forecast for your fee page.

Keep the price and the service name together. A plain row such as monthly management with the billing base is easier to interpret than a price inside an image. Repeat key conditions in clear text. Avoid a table whose meaning depends only on color or tooltips.

Give the page a direct link from your service page. Make the service area clear. An owner comparing providers needs to know where the rate applies. Separate residential management from services with different pricing.

For the wider setup, use our [AI search website checklist](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website). Keep this page focused on prices and scope. Do not turn the fee table into a long list of search terms.

## How Do You Show Fees Without Competing Only on Price?

Show the work and decision rights that the fee buys. Owners can compare value only when they can compare scope.

Describe what happens when a resident reports a leak. Who takes the request? Who checks the urgency? Who seeks approval? Which work costs extra? This gives the owner a useful service picture without a promise you cannot keep.

Define exclusions as clearly as included work. Major projects, court work and special reporting can require separate terms. Use plain words for each. Do not rely on a broad phrase such as premium support.

Compare your own service plans by tasks and authority. Avoid claims that a rival lacks a service unless you checked its current offer. Let owners see which plan fits their property and their own time.

Invite owners with unusual buildings to request a scope review. Explain what you need before quoting. A quote-only option can still be clear when you state the review process, cost drivers and next step.

## How Should You Test and Maintain the Page?

Test a fee page as part of the sales process, with records you can audit. The following checklist is a suggested review process.

- Confirm that every displayed fee matches the offer staff can sell.
- Ask a team member to estimate an owner's total charges from the page.
- Check vacancy, renewal, maintenance and cancellation cases separately.
- Record questions owners still ask after they read the table.
- Compare signed work and expected service cost before changing prices again.
- Review complaints for charges owners did not expect.

Choose a review period that covers your sales cycle. Avoid changing the page after every quiet day. Keep the dates of edits so you can connect results with the offer owners saw.

Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to discuss cost scenarios. Treat its output as an estimate, then use the signed scope for the final quote. Give the owner a fee schedule they can keep before they sign.

## Which loans allow an owner-occupied small building?

Owner-occupied loan options can cover a small building when you live in part of it and meet the program rules. HUD describes FHA financing for primary residences with 1 to 4 units. Fannie Mae also addresses owner-occupied small buildings in its rental income guidance.

Tell the lender exactly what you plan to buy and where you will live. Ask it to confirm legal unit count, loan eligibility, occupancy terms, insurance, and required reserves. A room rental and a separate dwelling unit are not the same property layout.

Do not select a loan by the advertised down payment alone. Compare total cash to close, insurance costs, payment, rate, and property conditions. A low initial cash requirement can still leave a difficult monthly budget.

Use this lender question sheet:

| Topic | Written answer to request |
| --- | --- |
| Property | Eligible unit count and legal-use documents |
| Occupancy | Move-in and continued-use terms |
| Income | Rent evidence the lender will accept |
| Cash | Closing funds and post-closing reserves |
| Condition | Work required before closing |
| Exit | Rules that affect a later move |

Get approval for the specific building. A general preapproval does not settle every property or rental-income issue.

## How do you estimate the other units' rent?

Estimate rent from comparable long-term leases for the actual rental space. Separate the space you occupy from the space a tenant can use. Include utilities, parking, laundry, and shared areas in the comparison.

Ask a local manager for a supported rent range. Save the source properties and explain material differences. Do not use the seller's best possible rent without checking current leases and collections.

Fannie Mae's rental income guide makes lender treatment depend on documentation and borrower circumstances. Qualifying income can differ from the rent you expect to collect. Ask the lender to show its calculation separately from your operating budget.

For an assumed duplex example, total monthly property costs are $3,000 and collected rent from the other unit is $1,700. Your remaining cash burden is $1,300. These are sample inputs, not a local rent estimate. They exclude any costs you failed to include in the total.

Test the same property with no rent from that unit during a vacancy. Then add a repair. If your own income and reserve cannot bridge that gap, change the purchase budget. Do not rely on perfect occupancy to afford your home.

## What are the risks of living next to tenants?

Living beside tenants combines your home life with rental duties. Set a communication and privacy plan before you lease. Tenants need a clear way to request service without relying on casual hallway talks.

Define shared spaces, parking, utility arrangements, trash, guests, and access in a local-law-reviewed agreement. Keep the rules clear and consistent. Do not use informal house customs as a substitute for written terms.

Choose a repair contact and a backup for times when you are away. Keep work orders and payment records even when you know the tenant personally. A friendly relationship still needs a reliable record.

Decide how much direct management you want. A manager can help with leasing and service, but the fee belongs in your budget. Use the [management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to compare that option.

Consider your own tolerance for noise, shared access, and less privacy. View the building during normal use if access permits. Ask about sound transfer and separate meters during the inspection. A cash estimate cannot tell you whether the living arrangement fits your daily needs.

## When can you move out and keep it as a rental?

Your loan documents and program rules determine the occupancy promises you make. Ask the lender before planning a move. Do not assume a social media rule about a minimum stay covers your loan.

Fannie Mae's Occupancy Types guide distinguishes a principal residence from an investment property. State your genuine plan when you apply. If your circumstances change, ask the servicer how to handle the change under your documents.

Build a separate all-rental budget before you move. Add the cost of your next home and any expense that you previously supplied yourself. Lawn care, utilities, and on-site response may need paid replacements.

Get an insurance review for the new use. Check local registration and licensing with the relevant office. Have counsel review the lease plan rather than assuming that owner-occupied rules continue unchanged after you leave.

Keep tax records for both uses. IRS Publication 527 covers renting part of a property and a later change to rental use. Give your preparer dates, space details, project records, and prior basis information. Do not start a new depreciation schedule from an unsupported online value.

## What should you prove before you buy?

Prove that the building fits both your housing needs and the rental plan. Get the legal-use records, inspection, rent evidence, loan terms, and insurance quote. Mark any missing fact before deciding.

Keep purchase costs and operating reserves separate. Fannie Mae's reserve guide includes requirements for small owner-occupied buildings under its underwriting rules. Ask the lender for the exact amount for your file. Then set your own cash target from likely repairs and rent gaps.

Review the difficult case with the person who shares your household budget. Ask who pays when rent is late and what you would do if a major system fails. Set a clear limit on cash you can commit.

Your next step is a complete property worksheet and a written lender review. Choose the home only after the plan works with supported rent and enough cash left after closing.

## What is the Seattle RRIO registration and inspection?

The Seattle Rental Registration and Inspection Ordinance (RRIO) requires owners to register almost every rental property with the City and to pass a periodic inspection. You register as soon as you have a tenant (Seattle Department of Construction and Inspections, SDCI). Since January 2019, short-term rentals that are not owner-occupied must register too, before they are listed.

The costs, from SDCI:

| Item | Fee |
|---|---|
| Registration, first unit at a property | $126 |
| Each additional unit at the same property | $31.50 |
| Term | 2 years, then renew at the same fee |
| City inspector, first unit | $241.50 |
| City inspector, each additional unit | $52.50 |
| Private inspector | Inspector's own rate, plus a $63 filing fee |

The registration fees include a 5% SDCI technology fee. City inspectors do up to 2 re-inspections at no charge.

How inspections work: the City selects each property for inspection once in the first 5 years after it registers, so every property is inspected at least once every 5 to 10 years. When you schedule one, give your tenants at least 2 days' notice. If the property passes, you get a Certificate of Compliance. You must post your registration certificate in a common area or give a copy to each tenant.

Worked example, a 4-unit building over 4 years:

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Registration, years 1–2: $126 + 3 × $31.50 | $220.50 |
| Renewal, years 3–4 | $220.50 |
| One City inspection: $241.50 + 3 × $52.50 | $399.00 |
| Total over 4 years | $840.00 |

## How does the Seattle First-in-Time rule change tenant screening?

The Seattle First-in-Time rule requires you to offer the rental to the first qualified applicant who gives a complete application (Renting in Seattle). The Washington State Supreme Court upheld the rule on 2019-11-14 (Seattle Office for Civil Rights). The Office for Civil Rights enforces it.

The steps, in order:

1. **Publish your minimum screening criteria** before you take applications.
2. **List every document** an applicant must provide.
3. **Tell applicants they can ask for more time,** for example for translation or a disability accommodation.
4. **Date and time-stamp** each application as it arrives.
5. **Screen in that order,** one application at a time.
6. **Allow at least 72 hours** for an applicant to supply missing information on an otherwise complete application.
7. **Allow 48 hours** for the first qualified applicant to accept your offer. Then move to the next one.

What changes for an owner: you cannot pick the "best" of 5 qualified applicants. Your written criteria do the choosing. So write criteria you would accept from anyone who meets them, and apply them the same way every time. That also keeps your screening in line with fair housing law.

## What notice does Seattle require for a rent increase?

Seattle requires at least 180 days' written notice for any housing cost increase, of any size, since November 9, 2021 (Renting in Seattle, housing cost increases). The rules that come with it:

- **No increase in the first 12 months** of a tenancy, and none during a fixed-term lease.
- **Start on a rental period.** An increase cannot begin mid-month.
- **Count full days.** For an increase on June 1, the tenant must receive the notice by December 2.
- **Use the state format plus City language** on how to contact Renting in Seattle. Without it, the notice is unenforceable.
- **Serve it properly:** by personal delivery, or by posting at the property and mailing first class.
- **Fix the unit first.** If the unit fails RRIO checklist standards and the tenant tells you in writing, the increase cannot take effect until you correct them.

The cost of a defective notice: a tenant can end the lease with 20 days' notice, and the tenant or the attorney general can go to court. Civil penalties reach $7,500 per violation, plus attorney's fees and damages.

## What does Washington's rent cap (HB 1217) allow?

Washington's rent cap, in RCW 59.18.700, limits an increase in any 12-month period to 7% plus CPI, or 10%, whichever is less. The law also bars any increase in the first 12 months of a tenancy. The CPI is the June 12-month change in the Seattle-area index.

| Year | Cap (Washington Department of Commerce) |
|---|---|
| 2026 | 9.683% |
| 2027 | 10% |

Commerce shows its 2026 math: (364.344 − 354.824) ÷ 354.824 + 0.07 = 9.683%. Exemptions are listed in RCW 59.18.710, so check that section before you rely on one.

Worked example, 2027: on a $2,549 rent (HUD's FY2027 fair market rent for a Seattle-area 2-bedroom), the most you can add is 10%, or $254.90. But a 10% increase also triggers Seattle's EDRA notice, next.

Seattle also limits one more gap: a month-to-month rent can be at most 5% above the rent for a lease (Renting in Seattle). For more on Seattle-area rents, see [Seattle fair market rent 2027](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/seattle-fair-market-rent-2027).

## When does a Seattle owner pay economic displacement relocation help?

A Seattle owner pays Economic Displacement Relocation Assistance (EDRA) when 3 things happen (Renting in Seattle, EDRA):

1. **Housing costs rise 10% or more** within 12 months, in one increase or several.
2. **The household earns 80% or less** of Seattle's area median income.
3. **The household gives notice to move out** and applies to the City.

The City then advances 3 times the current monthly housing cost to the household. The City notifies you, and you must repay the City. Both sides can appeal. When an increase reaches 10%, you must attach the City's EDRA notice to the increase notice.

Worked example: rent of $2,549 with a 10% increase. If an eligible household moves out and applies, you owe the City 3 × $2,549 = $7,647. An increase of 9.9% ($252.35) does not trigger EDRA.

Checklist for a Seattle owner:

- [ ] RRIO registration current and certificate posted or given to tenants.
- [ ] Written screening criteria published before you take applications.
- [ ] Applications time-stamped and screened in order.
- [ ] Rent increase notices served 180 days ahead, in the state format with City language.
- [ ] Increases at or under the state cap: 10% for 2027.
- [ ] EDRA notice attached to any increase of 10% or more.

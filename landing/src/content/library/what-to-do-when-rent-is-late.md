## What should I do on each day rent is late?

Follow the same written schedule for every tenant. A fixed plan keeps you calm, keeps you fair, and gives you a clean record if the case goes to court. Here is a plan for rent due on the 1st. Adjust the days to your lease and state law.

| Day | Action | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Friendly reminder by text or email | Many late payments are mistakes: a failed autopay, a new bank card |
| After grace period (for example day 5) | Add the late fee and send a written statement | Only if the lease states the fee and your state allows it |
| Next business day | Call the tenant. Ask about a date and a plan | Learn if this is one bad month or a pattern |
| When your state allows | Serve the written pay-or-quit notice | Starts the legal clock |
| Notice ends unpaid | Call a local attorney or file, per state rules | Court is the only lawful way to get possession |

## When do I contact the tenant?

Contact the tenant on the first or second day rent is late. Keep it short and kind. A good message names the amount, the due date, and how to pay. It does not threaten.

Sample message:

> Hi [name], our records show the [month] rent of $[amount] has not come in yet. If you already paid, please send me the confirmation. If something came up, call me at [phone] so we can talk about it.

Save a copy of every message. A written record helps you more than a phone call you cannot prove.

## When do I add the late fee?

Add the late fee only after the grace period in your lease or your state's law. Some states set the earliest day. Oregon's ORS 90.260, for example, lets a landlord charge a late fee only if rent is not received by the 4th day of the rental period. The lease must also state the fee.

Then send a written statement: rent due, late fee, total, and the date by which you need payment.

## When do I serve a pay-or-quit notice?

Serve the pay-or-quit notice when your state allows it, and use the exact form your state requires. The notice period and its timing differ a lot by state.

| State | Notice period | Earliest timing and key rules | Statute |
|---|---|---|---|
| Florida | 3 days, excluding Saturday, Sunday and court holidays | Written demand for rent or possession, in the statute's wording | 83.56(3) |
| Arizona | 5 days | Written notice of nonpayment and intent to terminate. The lease is reinstated if the tenant pays rent and a reasonable late fee before filing | 33-1368(B) |
| Oregon | 10 days or 13 days | 10-day notice no sooner than day 8; 13-day notice no sooner than day 5. Must state the amount and the deadline | 90.394 |
| Virginia | 14 days | Written notice of nonpayment and intent to terminate | 55.1-1245(F) |

Watch for: a notice with the wrong amount, the wrong dates or the wrong delivery method can fail in court. Then you must start over. Most states also set how you deliver the notice, such as in person, by mail or by posting.

If the property is in a federal housing program or has a federally backed mortgage, the CARES Act text at 15 U.S.C. 9058(c) may require 30 days' notice to vacate. Ask an attorney whether it applies to you.

## What should I never do when rent is late?

Never try to force the tenant out yourself. Most states ban "self-help" eviction. Florida's statute 83.67 is a clear example. It bars a landlord from cutting off utilities, including water, heat, electricity and gas. It also bars changing the locks or using a bootlock to keep the tenant out.

Do not:

- Change the locks.
- Remove doors, windows or the tenant's things.
- Cut or ask the utility to cut service.
- Threaten the tenant or post the debt in public.

## Should I offer a payment plan?

A short payment plan can cost less than an eviction. Put it in writing with dates and amounts, and say what happens if a payment is missed. Read your state rules on partial payments before you accept money after a notice. In some states, taking partial rent can affect the notice you served.

A simple plan:

1. **Total owed:** rent plus the late fee.
2. **Payments:** 2 or 3 dates within the next 30 days.
3. **Default:** if a payment is missed, you may serve or proceed on the notice.
4. **Signatures:** both of you, with the date.

## When should I call an attorney?

Call a local attorney before you file an eviction case. Call earlier if any of these is true:

- The tenant says the unit needs repairs and is withholding rent.
- The tenant asks for a disability accommodation.
- The property is in a federal program or has a federally backed mortgage.
- You are not sure your notice was right.
- The tenant has a lawyer.

Court rules, filing steps and forms are local. An attorney can check your notice before the hearing, which can save a month of lost rent.

If late rent is a monthly fight, a manager may cost less than your time. See how [property management](/property-management) works, and estimate the cost with the [fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

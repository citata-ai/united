## How much does one vacant month cost per year of rent?

One vacant month costs 1/12 of a year's rent, which is 8.3%. That is only the lost rent. You still pay the mortgage, taxes, insurance, utilities and lawn care while the unit is empty.

| Monthly rent | Lost rent, 1 month | Share of yearly rent | Lost rent per day (rent × 12 ÷ 365) |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1,200 | $1,200 | 8.3% | $39.45 |
| $1,500 | $1,500 | 8.3% | $49.32 |
| $2,000 | $2,000 | 8.3% | $65.75 |
| $3,000 | $3,000 | 8.3% | $98.63 |

There is no tax relief for the lost rent. IRS Publication 527 says you may deduct ordinary expenses while a rental is vacant, but "you can't deduct any loss of rental income for the period the property is vacant."

For context, the US rental vacancy rate was 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026, not seasonally adjusted. That is the Census Bureau figure, as shown on FRED. Your local rate can be much higher or lower.

This is general tax information. Ask a tax professional about your return.

## How do I compare a lower rent with waiting?

Compare the yearly cost of the cut with the days of vacancy it saves. Use one formula:

Break-even days = (rent cut × 12) ÷ (rent × 12 ÷ 365)

If the cut fills the unit faster than the break-even days, the cut wins. Here are common cases:

| Asking rent | Rent cut | Cost of cut over 12 months | Break-even days |
|---|---|---|---|
| $2,000 | $50 | $600 | 9.1 days |
| $2,000 | $100 | $1,200 | 18.2 days |
| $1,500 | $75 | $900 | 18.2 days |
| $1,500 | $100 | $1,200 | 24.3 days |

Read the second row this way. A $100 cut on a $2,000 unit costs $1,200 over a one-year lease. That is the same as about 18 days of rent. If the cut gets a signed lease 3 weeks sooner, you come out ahead.

## How many months at a higher rent pay back one vacant month?

Divide the monthly rent by the extra rent you hold out for. The result is the number of months the premium needs to pay back one empty month.

Worked example: you list at $2,100 instead of the market's $2,000. The unit sits one extra month.

| Step | Math | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Rent lost in the extra month | $2,100 × 1 | $2,100 |
| Extra rent per month after it fills | $2,100 − $2,000 | $100 |
| Months to pay back | $2,100 ÷ $100 | 21 months |

So the higher rent pays back only if the tenant stays almost 2 years. And that ignores the carrying costs of the empty month. Holding out rarely pays unless you are sure the unit is under market.

The lower rent also carries forward. A renewal increase usually starts from the current rent. So a $100 cut today can cost more than 12 months of $100. Weigh that against the vacancy days.

## When should I cut the asking rent?

Cut the rent when the market tells you the price is wrong. Decide the trigger before you list, so you do not wait out of hope.

Signals that the price is too high:

- [ ] Few inquiries in the first 7 days, compared with your past listings.
- [ ] Showings but no applications after 14 days.
- [ ] Similar units nearby rented while yours did not.
- [ ] Visitors name the price as the reason they passed.

Signals that the price is fine and something else is wrong:

- [ ] Many inquiries but few showings: check your photos and listing text.
- [ ] Many showings but no applications: check the unit's condition and your screening rules.
- [ ] Applications but no qualified ones: check that the listing states your criteria.

## What is a price drop step that works?

A price step plan sets the date and size of each cut in advance. The table is an example plan, not a rule. Set your own days and amounts from your market.

| Day on market | Action (example) | Cost of this step, 12 months, $2,000 unit |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | List at market rent from 3–5 recent comparables | $0 |
| Day 10 | Few inquiries: fix photos and text; no price change | $0 |
| Day 14 | No applications: cut $50 | $600 |
| Day 21 | Still no applications: cut another $50, or offer a move-in credit | $600 more |
| Day 30 | Review comparables again; consider a bigger cut | Depends |

A one-time move-in credit is an option. A $500 credit costs $500 once, and the lease rent stays at $2,000 for future renewals. That is less than the $1,200 a $100 cut costs over the year, and about 7.6 days of rent.

Vacancy cost grows with slow turns too. Lessen, a maintenance vendor, says that even under efficient conditions "a make-ready takes the better part of a week." Fast make-ready work and a fair price together cut empty days.

## What does NARPM cost?

NARPM costs $320 a year for an individual national member, or $270 in the first year. NARPM is the National Association of Residential Property Managers. Its membership page says the $50 new member discount brings the first year to $270, "less than $22.50 a month".

| Membership type | Price on narpm.org (read 2026-10-19) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Individual, first year | $270 | $320 less a $50 new member discount |
| Individual, later years | $320 a year | Prorated in year 2, due January 1 |
| Company, 4 members | $760 base fee a year | For companies with 4 or more members |
| Company, each extra member | $125 a year | Added to the $760 base |
| Local chapter | Set by each chapter | Not in the national dues |

Two more points from the NARPM pages:

- **Ethics course.** New members must finish the NARPM Ethics course within 90 days. It is free for new members.
- **First course.** New members get a certificate for one 3-hour virtual course in their first year.

Some NARPM membership pages still show an old 2015 convention note. Confirm the current dues with NARPM before you pay.

A worked example: a company with 6 NARPM members pays $760 plus 2 × $125, so $1,010 a year in national dues. If one directory or referral owner with 3 doors at $120 a month each signs, the fee income is $4,320 a year. Your fee and door count will differ.

## How does the Find a Manager directory work?

The Find a Manager directory lets owners search NARPM members by name, location, chapter or designation. NARPM's home page sends owners to it with "Find a NARPM Property Manager" and a city and state search box.

What a member gets:

- **A free listing.** NARPM's benefits page calls it a "National Website Listing" and says the site gets over 10,000 visitors a month.
- **Order by designation.** NARPM's RMP page says it lists members by designation, so designation holders appear higher.
- **A reason to trust.** The "Why Use a NARPM Member?" page tells owners that members follow a Code of Ethics. It adds that they use consistent screening policies.

What a member does not get: a lead count. NARPM publishes no numbers on how many owner leads the directory sends. The 10,000 monthly visitors are for the whole site, not for one city.

NARPM's use policy also says the directories may not be used for commercial or solicitation purposes. Do not scrape the list to market to other managers.

To measure your own result, add a UTM tag to the website link in your listing, for example `?utm_source=narpm&utm_medium=directory`. Then count leads from that source in Google Analytics and your CRM.

## Do designations like RMP help in marketing?

Designations help with trust and with directory order, but they are not a lead source on their own. NARPM has 4 designations and certifications.

| Designation | Who it is for | Main requirement (narpm.org) |
|---|---|---|
| RMP (Residential Management Professional) | Property managers | Manage 100 units over a 2-year period |
| MPM (Master Property Manager) | Senior property managers | Hold the RMP and manage 500 units over a 5-year period |
| CRMC (Certified Residential Management Company) | Companies | An MPM on staff, 500 unit years, and a passed audit |
| CSS (Certified Support Specialist) | Office support staff | Work for a member with an RMP or MPM |

You must be a national NARPM member to apply for any of them. NARPM says the RMP and MPM are "the designations residential property owners recognize and seek". That is NARPM's view. We found no owner survey that measures it.

How to use a designation in marketing:

1. **Show the badge.** Put it on your owner services page and team page. NARPM gives badges for websites.
2. **Explain it.** Add one line: "RMP: a NARPM designation for managers with 100+ units over 2 years."
3. **Add it to listings.** Put it in your Google Business Profile description and on directory profiles.
4. **Use it in proposals.** Owners who compare 3 managers look for proof of skill.

## Do NARPM members refer out-of-area owners?

NARPM members do refer owners, and NARPM lists referrals as a benefit. The benefits page lists "Business Referrals" through the national network. The membership page says new members get access to the national member database for client referrals.

An example: an owner moves from Ohio to Texas and keeps the Ohio home. Their Texas manager does not work in Ohio, so the manager looks up a NARPM member there.

Referrals come from people who know you. To get them:

- **Go to your chapter.** Local chapters meet and run state conferences.
- **Go to one national event a year.** NARPM runs an annual convention and a Broker/Owner conference.
- **Make your area clear.** Tell members which cities and property types you take.
- **Send referrals out.** The member who sends you owners expects the same back.

NARPM's Code of Ethics, Article 9, also tells members not to take business from other managers by deceptive means. Referral work is a long game.

## Is NARPM worth it for owner leads?

NARPM is worth it for most PM companies, but for education, trust and referrals more than for direct leads. Most owner leads come from Google. In the Goodjuju 2026 lead study (fall update, 35 PM companies), Google Search and the Map Pack brought 50.6% of owner leads. Referral traffic, which includes directories such as NARPM, brought 3.7%.

## How do their features differ?

ShowMojo and Tenant Turner cover much of the same leasing workflow. Their own pricing pages list scheduling, syndication, access control, and owner reports. The difference to test is how each handles your staff rules and exceptions.

Start with an inquiry from a listing site. Follow the lead through questions, booking, reminders, access, feedback, and the final report. Check whether your leasing team can see the full exchange without changing tools.

ShowMojo lists calendar routing and configurable fraud controls. Tenant Turner lists automated follow-up and a range of supported access devices. Treat those lists as demo topics. They do not prove that a feature will work with your current setup.

Ask each vendor to show a schedule change after a prospect has an access code. Then ask how the system records the change. Clear access logs matter more than a polished calendar view.

## Which suits which portfolio size?

Portfolio fit depends on vacancy volume and staff roles, rather than a fixed door count. Both vendors offer unit-based and listing-based plans. Compare the plans against your own pattern of active vacancies.

A firm with steady turnover may want a predictable portfolio bill. A firm with few active listings should also price listing slots. Ask how a slot works when you lease a unit and replace its listing.

Include the cost of field staff in the test. Self-showing software does not install devices, confirm a vacant home is safe, or repair a jammed lock. Assign those tasks before launch.

A larger team should test role limits and agent calendars. A smaller team should test how quickly someone can take over an unusual call. The right choice keeps both groups from losing context.

## How do they pre-screen prospects?

Both products use prospect questions before booking, according to their pricing pages. Write those questions before a demo. Use your approved rental criteria and a clear route for questions or help.

Showing pre-screening is a scheduling step. Do not treat an unanswered question as proof of fraud or a final rental decision. Let staff review unclear cases and fix errors.

Test a prospect who needs help using a phone or accessing the property. Check whether the booking path still works without repeated failed attempts. Keep a person available for access issues.

Also ask where answers go after the showing. Your screening system should receive only the data you need, with the prospect's record kept clear. Avoid asking applicants for the same details in several tools.

## What does each cost?

The vendor pages show a base plan plus possible device and service costs. Get a quote that names the billing basis, included volume, minimum spend, and renewal terms.

ShowMojo and Tenant Turner both list a monthly fee of $3.50 for their own access devices. Both list $5 for third-party devices. These figures come from their pricing pages, read 2026-10-19.

Request a device list by property. Include purchase, replacement, shipping, staff visits, and the recurring connection fee. Ask what happens to a device when a listing goes off market.

Price AI add-ons apart from live answering. Ask who handles an access failure when software cannot help. A low base price offers little value if your staff still must answer every call.

## What changed after the ownership change?

Tenant Turner's About page lists PropertyTek leaders, which confirms the shared group context. That page does not prove that the brands have merged into a single product or contract.

The current pricing pages use similar plan names and device offers. This is a useful reason to ask about the product road map. It is not proof that every feature works the same way.

Ask whether your contract stays with the same entity. Check the support queue, login, data export, and hardware terms. Do not assume a transfer between brands will be simple or free.

Before renewal, request a written list of any planned changes that affect your account. Keep the answer with the contract. Select the workflow you can verify today, then review later changes on their own terms.

## How should you test the choice before you pay?

Test the full workflow with sample records before you move live data. Use the same case in each product. Ask the person who will do the daily work to run the test. A sales demo alone does not show where your team will need help.

Keep a decision sheet with the task, result, missing step, and person who checked it. Mark each result as shown, promised, or still open. Ask for written terms when a feature needs an upgrade or another service. Include staff setup time in your cost review.

Check the exit path while you still control the choice. Export the sample records and open them outside the platform. Confirm that notes, files, dates, and links remain useful. Ask how you will get records after you close the account.

Choose a tool when the test proves a clear gain for your workflow. Keep your current process until the new process passes. Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to see how software costs affect your service margin.

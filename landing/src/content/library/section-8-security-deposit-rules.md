## How much security deposit can a Section 8 landlord charge?

A Section 8 landlord can charge the same security deposit that the landlord charges any other tenant for that unit. The federal rule, 24 CFR 982.313(a), says only: "The owner may collect a security deposit from the tenant." It sets no dollar cap.

The cap comes from two other places:

1. **The housing authority (PHA).** Under 24 CFR 982.313(b), the PHA may prohibit a deposit above private market practice, or above what you charge unassisted tenants.
2. **State and local law.** Your state may cap the deposit, set a return deadline and require interest or a separate account. The federal rule says the owner uses the deposit "subject to State or local law."

Example: New York General Obligations Law 7-108 says a deposit may not exceed one month's rent. The New York landlord must return the balance, with an itemized statement, within 14 days after the tenant moves out. A landlord who misses that step loses the right to keep any part of the deposit.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Can the deposit be higher than for an unassisted tenant?

A higher deposit for a voucher tenant is a risk you should not take. The PHA has the power to refuse it under 24 CFR 982.313(b). Also check if your city or state has a source-of-income law. Where such a law applies, a higher deposit only for voucher holders can be illegal discrimination.

Use one written deposit policy for each unit. Apply it to every applicant. If you charge a higher deposit for a lower credit score, apply that rule to all applicants, not to voucher holders only.

| Situation | Same deposit as other tenants | Higher deposit for voucher tenant |
|---|---|---|
| Federal rule (982.313) | Allowed | PHA may prohibit |
| Where a source-of-income law applies | Allowed | Can be illegal discrimination |
| State deposit cap | Must be at or under the cap | Must be at or under the cap |

## Does the PHA pay the security deposit?

The tenant pays the security deposit, not the PHA. Under 24 CFR 982.452(b)(5), the owner is responsible for collecting from the family "any security deposit." The federal voucher rules do not fund deposits.

The PHA pays only the housing assistance payment (HAP). That is the monthly subsidy part of the rent. 24 CFR 982.451(b)(4) also says the tenant does not owe the part of the rent that HAP covers. So you cannot deduct a missed HAP payment from the tenant's deposit.

## Do some PHAs offer deposit help?

Some PHAs pay deposit help from local or special funds. It is a local program, not a federal right. Ask your PHA's landlord liaison before you sign the lease.

One example: in a March 16, 2022 post, the Philadelphia Housing Authority offered security deposit help of up to one month of the approved contract rent. The money came from American Rescue Plan Act funds for Emergency Housing Vouchers. The same post described an Owner Assurance Fund that reimbursed up to $2,500 for damage beyond normal wear and tear, after the security deposit. The post does not say if these programs are still open, so confirm with the PHA.

## Can I claim damages from the PHA?

Under the current part 982 rules, the PHA does not pay for tenant damage. 24 CFR 982.307(a)(1) says the PHA has "no liability or responsibility to the owner" for the family's behavior or suitability. Your tools are the deposit, the tenant, and any local PHA fund.

The deposit process under 24 CFR 982.313:

1. **Inspect at move-out.** Compare the unit with your move-in photos and checklist.
2. **List each charge.** Give the tenant a written list of every item and its amount (982.313(d)).
3. **Deduct only lawful items.** Unpaid tenant rent, damage, and other amounts owed under the lease (982.313(c)).
4. **Refund the rest promptly.** Follow your state's deadline.
5. **Collect any shortfall from the tenant.** 982.313(e) lets you seek the balance from the tenant.

One more rule helps at move-out. Under 24 CFR 982.311(d)(1), you may keep the HAP for the month the family moves out. The PHA pays no HAP for later months.

## How do state deposit limits apply to a voucher lease?

A state deposit limit applies to a voucher lease the same way it applies to any lease. The federal rule does not override it. 24 CFR 982.308(c) also lets the PHA decline a lease that does not comply with state or local law.

A worked example (made-up numbers):

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Contract rent (rent to owner) | $1,600 |
| HAP paid by the PHA | $1,150 |
| Tenant share of rent | $450 |
| Deposit you charge all tenants for this unit | $1,600 (1 month) |
| Deposit under a one-month cap, as in New York | Allowed: $1,600 |
| Deposit the voucher tenant pays | $1,600, from the tenant |

The cap is based on the full monthly rent in a state like New York, not the tenant share. But the tenant must pay the whole deposit from their own money. A $1,600 deposit is more than 3 months of this tenant's $450 share. That is why some PHAs fund deposit help.

## Checklist: Section 8 security deposit

- [ ] Deposit is the same as for unassisted tenants of the same unit.
- [ ] Deposit is at or under your state and local cap.
- [ ] Deposit amount is in the lease.
- [ ] You asked the PHA if it has deposit help or a damage fund.
- [ ] Move-in condition is on a signed checklist with dated photos.
- [ ] You know your state's return deadline and interest rules.
- [ ] At move-out you give a written, itemized list of charges.
- [ ] You refund the unused balance promptly.

## How is the debt service coverage ratio calculated?

The debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) is the property's rent divided by its debt payment. It shows how many times the rent covers the loan.

Most DSCR lenders use a simple version:

```text
DSCR = monthly rent ÷ monthly PITIA
PITIA = principal + interest + property tax + insurance + HOA dues
```

Baselane gives this example: $24,000 of yearly rent ($2,000 a month) over $19,200 of yearly debt (PITI plus HOA, $1,600 a month) is a DSCR of 1.25.

How to read the ratio:

| DSCR | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Below 1.0 | Rent does not cover the payment |
| 1.0 | Rent exactly covers the payment |
| 1.25 | Rent is 25% more than the payment |
| 1.5 | Rent is 50% more than the payment |

Watch for: lender DSCR is not the same as your real cash flow. The simple version leaves out vacancy, repairs and management. A DSCR of 1.0 can lose money every month. The Census Bureau reported a national rental vacancy rate of 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026. One empty month a year takes more than 8% of the rent.

Where the rent number comes from: lenders use the current lease or an appraiser's market rent estimate. Ask which one, and whether they use the lower of the two.

## What DSCR do lenders want?

Most DSCR lenders want 1.0 to 1.25 or higher. Baselane reports that 1.25 or higher is generally preferred. Some lenders accept 1.0 or even 0.75, based on credit score and down payment.

Typical requirements reported by Baselane:

| Requirement | Typical range |
|-------------|---------------|
| Minimum DSCR | 1.0 to 1.25 preferred; some accept lower |
| Minimum credit score | Often 660; some go to 620 with conditions |
| Down payment | 20% to 25%; up to 40% for weaker ratios |
| Prepayment penalty | Common, for example 3-2-1 or 5-4-3-2-1 |
| Number of financed properties | Usually no limit |

Each lender has its own grid. Get quotes from at least 3.

Worked example: does this house qualify? Example numbers.

| Line | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Market rent | $2,400 |
| Principal and interest | $1,560 |
| Property tax | $300 |
| Insurance | $150 |
| HOA | $0 |
| PITIA | $2,010 |
| DSCR | 1.19 |

At 1.19, the loan clears a 1.0 lender but misses a 1.25 lender. To reach 1.25, the PITIA must fall to $1,920. A larger down payment or a lower price can get there.

## How do DSCR rates compare with conventional loans?

DSCR loans usually cost more than conventional loans. Baselane notes that DSCR loans typically come with higher interest rates and fees than conventional investment property loans, and often larger down payments.

Conventional investment loans are not cheap either. Fannie Mae's loan-level price adjustment (LLPA) matrix, updated 2026-09-30, adds a charge to every investment property loan:

| Loan-to-value | Fannie Mae investment property LLPA |
|---------------|-------------------------------------|
| 60% or less | 1.125% |
| 60.01–70% | 1.625% |
| 70.01–75% | 2.125% |
| 75.01–80% | 3.375% |

For context, Freddie Mac's weekly survey shows the average 30-year fixed rate was 7.40% on 2026-10-15. LLPAs and DSCR pricing come on top of a base rate like that.

Side-by-side comparison:

| Feature | Conventional investment loan | DSCR loan |
|---------|------------------------------|-----------|
| How you qualify | Personal income, debts, credit | Property rent vs payment, plus credit |
| Paperwork | Tax returns, pay stubs | Lease or rent estimate, appraisal, entity papers |
| Max LTV, 1-unit purchase | 85% (Fannie Mae eligibility matrix, 2026-08-05) | Often 75–80%, from Baselane's 20–25% down range |
| Price | Market rate plus LLPAs | Usually higher than conventional |
| Prepayment penalty | Check the note | Common, per Baselane |
| Borrower | A person | A person or an LLC |
| Financed property limit | Yes, with extra reserve rules | Usually none |

## Can a DSCR loan be in an LLC name?

Yes, in most cases. Baselane notes that borrowing through an LLC or other entity is common with DSCR loans. The lender will ask for the entity's papers and usually a personal guarantee from the owners.

Why lenders can do this: DSCR loans are business-purpose credit. Regulation Z is the federal Truth in Lending rule. Under 12 CFR 1026.3(a), it does not apply to credit mainly for a business, commercial or agricultural purpose. It also does not apply to credit to someone other than a natural person.

What that means for you:

- **Fewer consumer protections.** Some Truth in Lending disclosures and rules do not apply. Read every page.
- **State rules still apply.** States can have their own lending laws.
- **The guarantee is personal.** If the LLC defaults, the lender can pursue you under the guarantee.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Who should use a DSCR loan?

A DSCR loan fits investors whose rent is strong but whose personal income is hard to document. It is a poor fit for a first rental bought by someone with a W-2 income that already qualifies.

| Good fit | Poor fit |
|----------|----------|
| Self-employed with low taxable income | W-2 earner who qualifies for conventional |
| Owner with many financed properties | First-time buyer with a thin reserve |
| Buyer who wants the loan in an LLC | Buyer who may sell or refinance within 3 years (penalty) |
| Property with rent well above the payment | Property with DSCR near 1.0 and old systems |

## What is a leasing fee?

A leasing fee is a one-time charge a property manager bills the owner for finding, screening and signing a new tenant. Most managers express it as a percent of one month's rent. Some charge a flat amount.

The fee pays for work that happens only when a unit is vacant: marketing, showings, screening and lease signing. The monthly management fee pays for the work after move-in: rent collection, maintenance and owner reports.

## Is a leasing fee the same as a tenant placement fee?

A leasing fee and a tenant placement fee are the same thing. You will see 4 names for it:

| Name | Where you see it |
|---|---|
| Leasing fee | Management agreements, fee schedules |
| Tenant placement fee | Fee schedules, fee studies |
| Leasing commission | Broker agreements, leasing-only services |
| Letting fee | Some regional and international use |

The name does not set the scope. The management agreement does. Two managers can both say "leasing fee" and include very different work.

## How much is a typical leasing fee?

A typical leasing fee is 50% to 100% of one month's rent, based on 2 public sources.

| Source | Service | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| iPropertyManagement (updated 2026-09-23) | Placement bundled with management | Average 70.6% of rent; range 6% to 125% |
| iPropertyManagement (updated 2026-09-23) | Placement only | Average 81.8% of rent; range 8% to 150% |
| All Property Management | Full-service management | Around 50% of the first month's rent |
| All Property Management | Recruiting-only service | 50% to 100% of one month's rent |

iPropertyManagement says its data comes from advertised fees of 722 branches of 669 companies in 80 metro areas across 31 states.

Placement-only service costs more as a percent because the manager earns no monthly fee afterward. The leasing fee is the whole job.

Worked example: rent is $2,000 a month. At 70.6%, the leasing fee is $1,412. At 50% it is $1,000; at 100% it is $2,000. If the average tenant stays 2 years, a $1,412 fee costs the owner about $59 a month over the tenancy ($1,412 ÷ 24).

## What does a leasing fee cover?

A leasing fee covers the work to fill a vacant unit, but the exact list is in the agreement. Ask the manager to name each item.

Usually included:

- [ ] Rent analysis and a price recommendation
- [ ] Listing photos and a written ad
- [ ] Listing on rental sites and the manager's website
- [ ] Answering leads and booking showings
- [ ] Showings or self-showing access
- [ ] Application processing and screening, under a written policy
- [ ] Lease preparation and signing
- [ ] Move-in inspection with photos
- [ ] Collecting the deposit and first month's rent

Often extra (check the agreement):

- [ ] Professional photos or video tours
- [ ] Paid ad boosts on listing sites
- [ ] Lockbox or smart lock hardware
- [ ] A tenant placement guarantee (a free re-placement if the tenant leaves early)
- [ ] Rekey and make-ready work

Watch for: a leasing fee with no re-placement terms. Ask what happens if the tenant breaks the lease in month 3. Does the manager re-place the tenant at no charge within a set period, or charge the full fee again?

## Do managers charge a leasing fee on renewals?

Many managers do not charge the full leasing fee on a renewal. They charge a lease renewal fee, which is smaller, because the work is smaller: a rent review, a renewal offer and new signatures.

iPropertyManagement's study found:

- **83.4%** of companies that charge for renewals use a flat fee.
- The average flat renewal fee is **$231.77**, with a range of $32 to $1,000.
- Companies that charge a percent average **30.33%** of a month's rent, with a range of 10% to 100%.

Worked example: rent is $2,000. A flat renewal fee of $231.77 is about 12% of one month's rent. A 30.33% renewal fee is $606.60. Over 3 renewals, that gap is $1,124.49. Ask which model the manager uses before you sign.

## When does a property manager charge the leasing fee?

A property manager charges the leasing fee when a new lease is signed, usually by taking it from the first rent collected. The agreement sets the trigger. Common triggers are:

1. **Lease signing.** The fee is earned when the tenant signs.
2. **Move-in.** The fee is earned when the tenant takes possession and pays.
3. **First rent collected.** The fee comes out of the first month's rent before the owner payout.

Some managers include leasing in the monthly fee. For example, a Doorvest management agreement filed with the SEC in 2021 says Doorvest "does not charge leasing fees." Compare the total cost over 2 to 3 years, not one line.

## Questions to ask before you sign

Ask these 6 questions about the leasing fee:

1. **What percent or flat amount**, and of what rent: the first month or the monthly rent?
2. **What is included**, item by item?
3. **When is it earned**: signing, move-in or first rent?
4. **What happens if the tenant leaves early?**
5. **What is the renewal fee**, and is it flat or a percent?
6. **Are there other leasing-related fees**, such as marketing or setup fees?

If you are a manager, publishing your leasing fee and what it covers makes it easier for owners to compare you. It also gives AI answers a clear fact. See [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping).

## How do I calculate my growth ceiling?

Your growth ceiling is the doors you add per year divided by your annual churn rate. Peter Lohmann, who runs a 700+ door company in central Ohio, has written about this idea since 2022. He credits it to a video by Alex Hormozi (Lohmann, issue #74).

**Growth ceiling = doors added per year ÷ annual churn rate**

Lohmann's own example in December 2023 used these numbers:

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Valid owner leads per month | 22 |
| Close rate | 27% |
| New doors per month | about 6 |
| New doors per year | 72 |
| Unit churn | 19.5%, which he gives as the 2021 NARPM / ProfitCoach average |
| Ceiling | 72 ÷ 0.195 = 369 doors |

He then ran his own company: 28 leads a month at a 20% close rate gives 5.6 new clients a month, or 67 a year. At 15% churn, that is a ceiling of 447 clients. At 2.4 units per client, it is about 1,073 units (issue #74).

One note on his January 2026 issue. It says 10 doors a month at 25% churn caps a company at 400 doors. His own formula gives 480 (120 ÷ 0.25). Use the formula, not the rounded example.

### Your growth ceiling table

| New doors per month | 25% churn | 20% churn | 15% churn | 10% churn |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 240 | 300 | 400 | 600 |
| 10 | 480 | 600 | 800 | 1,200 |
| 15 | 720 | 900 | 1,200 | 1,800 |
| 20 | 960 | 1,200 | 1,600 | 2,400 |

Use net new doors from all sources: referrals, web leads, sales staff. Leave out one-time purchases. They change your door count, not your monthly rate.

## Why does churn stay flat as I grow?

Churn stays flat as a percentage because each door has about the same chance to leave, whether you manage 100 doors or 1,000. Lead flow does not work that way. Lohmann points out that your monthly leads do not grow with your company size unless you work to grow them (issue #173).

So the gap closes as you grow. At 200 doors and 20% churn, you lose 40 doors a year. At 600 doors, you lose 120. If you still add 120 a year, growth stops.

Daniel Craig of ProfitCoach gave a large-company example in 2022. At 1,000 doors and 25% churn, you lose 250 doors a year, or about 20 a month (Fourandhalf, 2022).

### Worked example: how growth slows

You start with 200 doors. You add 10 doors a month. Annual churn is 20%, so the ceiling is 600. We ran this month by month:

| Year | Doors at year end | Net doors added that year |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 273 | +73 |
| 2 | 333 | +60 |
| 3 | 382 | +49 |
| 5 | 454 | +33 |
| 10 | 547 | +12 |

The same 120 new doors a year bring 73 net doors in year 1 and only 12 in year 10. Lohmann says you approach the ceiling "quickly at first, then slowly" (issue #74). This is why he sets a growth wall at 75% of the maximum. Here, the wall is 450 doors, and you reach it in about year 5 (issue #173).

Watch for: a book purchase does not raise the ceiling. If you buy doors and go above your ceiling, Lohmann says you shrink back toward it (issue #74).

## How much does each point of churn cost?

Each point of churn costs you doors on the ceiling and revenue each year. At 10 new doors a month, the ceiling moves like this:

| Churn | Ceiling | Change from 20% |
|---|---|---|
| 20% | 600 | — |
| 19% | 632 | +32 doors |
| 18% | 667 | +67 doors |
| 15% | 800 | +200 doors |

Now add revenue. Rentvine's lifetime value example uses $2,200 of revenue per unit per year (Rentvine, 2025). Use your own number if you have it. At $2,200, the 32 extra doors from one point of churn are worth about $70,400 a year in revenue at the ceiling (32 × $2,200).

At 500 doors today, one point of churn is 5 doors a year. That is $11,000 a year in revenue at $2,200 per door, before you count the cost to replace those doors. To see what your fee brings per door, use our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## Which grows doors faster: more leads or less churn?

A cut in churn grows the ceiling a little faster than the same rise in new doors, and it costs less to keep. Compare two 10% changes on a base of 10 doors a month and 20% churn:

| Change | New ceiling | Gain |
|---|---|---|
| None | 600 | — |
| 10% more new doors (11 a month) | 660 | +60 |
| 10% less churn (20% to 18%) | 667 | +67 |
| Both | 733 | +133 |

The math favors churn because the ceiling divides by it. But the bigger reason is cost. A new door costs money each time you win it: ads, sales time, onboarding. A door you keep has no new acquisition cost.

Lohmann's advice for a multi-location company is to track lead flow, close rate and churn per metro (issue #74). Each office has its own ceiling.

## Checklist: find your ceiling this week

1. **Count** net new doors per month for the last 12 months, without purchases.
2. **Calculate** annual unit churn for the same 12 months.
3. **Divide** new doors per year by churn. That is your ceiling.
4. **Multiply** the ceiling by 0.75. That is your growth wall.
5. **Compare** your door count today with the wall.
6. **Pick** one lever for next quarter: close rate, lead volume or churn.

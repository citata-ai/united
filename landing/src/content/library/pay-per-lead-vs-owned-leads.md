## What is the difference between bought leads and owned leads?

A bought lead is an owner inquiry that another company collects and sells to you. An owned lead comes to you through a channel you control: your website, your Google Business Profile, your reviews, your email list or your referral partners.

The difference is control. A pay-per-lead site owns the page, the traffic and the owner's first impression. You rent access to it, one lead at a time. When you stop paying, the leads stop.

An owned channel keeps working after you stop spending on it. A city page that ranks today can still bring calls next year. A review from 2024 still helps a 2026 owner choose you.

| Factor | Pay-per-lead and ads | Owned channels |
|---|---|---|
| Time to first lead | Days | Months |
| Cost when you stop paying | Leads stop | Leads slow down, but continue |
| Who owns the traffic | The vendor or Google Ads | You |
| Owner's first choice | Often shared with other managers | The owner picked you |
| Best use | Fill a gap now, test a new city | Base of your growth |

## How do pay-per-lead costs change over time?

Pay-per-lead costs tend to rise as more managers bid in the same market. Fourandhalf made this point in 2014: as a category gets more competitive, more companies bid for the same leads, so the cost per lead goes up. Owned channels work the other way. They cost more at the start, then the cost per lead falls as the asset grows.

Paid search shows the same pressure. WordStream's 2025 Google Ads benchmarks give these numbers for the Real Estate category:

| Metric (Real Estate, Google search ads, 2025) | Value |
|---|---|
| Average cost per click | $2.53 |
| Average conversion rate | 3.28% |
| Average cost per lead | $100.48 |

Real Estate is a wide category. Your property management cost per lead can be much higher or lower. Use these numbers only until your own account has 90 days of data.

Some lead sites sell per lead with no contract. Buildium says All Property Management works on a pay-per-lead basis, "with no monthly contract or obligation", and sends leads straight into Buildium for its customers. The page does not publish a price per lead, so ask for one in writing.

## Which lead type closes better?

Owned leads usually close better, because the owner already chose you. The best public data comes from the Goodjuju 2026 lead source report. Goodjuju is an SEO agency, so it has an interest in this result. Still, the method is open: GA4 first-touch data from the same 35 companies in two windows, 1,262 owner leads in the fall window.

| Channel (fall 2026) | Share of traffic | Share of owner leads | Conversion rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search and Map Pack | 37.9% | 50.6% | 1.2% |
| Direct / Maps | 45.0% | 35.6% | 0.7% |
| Referral | 3.0% | 3.7% | 1.1% |
| AI tools | 0.8% | 3.4% | 3.9% |
| Paid search | 5.3% | 1.2% | 0.2% |
| Social media | 4.3% | 0.8% | 0.2% |

Read the paid row with care. Only 6 of the 35 companies ran search ads, and the study counts only form leads on the main site. Goodjuju says itself that calls and separate landing pages are not fully counted.

Speed matters more than source for bought leads. A bought lead often goes to 2 or 3 managers at once. In the InsideSales.com and MIT Lead Response Management study (2007), the odds to qualify a web lead dropped 21 times when the first call came at 30 minutes instead of 5. That study measured qualification, not signed contracts, and it used data from 6 companies outside property management. The lesson still holds: a slow reply wastes the money you paid for the lead.

## What do owned channels cost up front?

Owned channels cost time and money before the first lead. The main items are:

1. **A website you own.** Pages for each service city, a clear fee table and a contact form. Check that you own the domain and the content.
2. **Google Business Profile.** Free to set up. It needs real photos, correct categories, current hours and steady reviews.
3. **Reviews.** Ask every happy owner. This costs staff time, not cash.
4. **Content.** Answers to the questions owners ask before they call: fees, Section 8, leasing-only, how to switch managers.
5. **Referral partners.** Agents, lenders and attorneys who meet owners first.

You can estimate the payback with your own numbers. Here is a worked example with hypothetical inputs, not benchmarks:

| Input | Example value |
|---|---|
| Average monthly fee per door | $150 |
| Doors per new owner | 2 |
| Months an owner stays | 36 |
| Fee revenue per new owner | $150 × 2 × 36 = $10,800 |
| Owned-channel spend for 12 months | $18,000 |
| Owners signed from owned channels in 12 months | 6 |
| Cost per signed owner | $18,000 ÷ 6 = $3,000 |

Put in your real fee, door count and retention. Our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) helps you check the fee side.

## How do I mix bought and owned leads?

Mix them by time frame. Use paid leads for the next 90 days. Use owned channels for the next 3 years.

A simple split:

- **Base:** Google Business Profile, reviews and your website get steady work every month. This is where most owner leads come from in the Goodjuju data.
- **Top-up:** a capped paid budget (Google Ads or a pay-per-lead site) when you need more doors now or want to test a new city.
- **Rule:** each quarter, compare cost per signed owner by channel. Move money to the channel with the lowest cost per signed owner, not the lowest cost per lead.

Track the source of every lead. Ask "How did you first hear about us?" on the form and on the first call. GA4 alone misses part of the picture: Goodjuju found GA4 missed about 4 in 10 of the AI leads it could trace on its own site. Our guide on how to [track AI traffic to a property management site](/guides/track-ai-traffic-property-management) shows the setup.

Watch for: a vendor who reports only cost per lead. What to do: ask for leads, signed agreements and doors, by month, for at least 6 months.

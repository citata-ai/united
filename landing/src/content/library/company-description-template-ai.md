## Why does AI repeat your company description?

AI tools repeat your company description because they build an answer from the text they find about you. When an owner asks ChatGPT for a property manager in a city, the engine reads your site, your Google Business Profile and directory pages. Then it writes a short summary of each company it names.

In our test of 50 US cities, every one of 150 AI answers listed sources (our data, 2026-10-19). Directory sites such as Expertise.com, Yelp and iPropertyManagement were the most cited. Expertise.com alone was a source in 93 of 150 answers. So the description on those sites matters as much as the text on your home page.

Mixed facts cost you. SOCi studied more than 350,000 locations of 2,751 brands and found accuracy flaws in business data on ChatGPT and Perplexity (Localogy, 2026-01-28). A company description that is the same everywhere gives the engine one set of facts to repeat.

## What goes in the 50-word description?

The 50-word description answers four questions: who you are, where you work, what you manage and why an owner can trust you. Put the most important words first. BrightLocal says Google often cuts a Business Profile description after about 250 characters, which is about 40 words.

Template (about 50 words):

```text
[Company name] manages [property types] for owners in [city 1], [city 2] and [city 3], [state].
We handle [service 1], [service 2] and [service 3] for [number] doors.
[One fact an owner can check: license, year started, association membership].
```

Example with made-up facts (replace every bracket with your own):

```text
Oak Street Property Management manages single-family homes and small apartment buildings for owners in Columbus, Dublin and Westerville, Ohio.
We handle tenant screening, rent collection and maintenance for 240 doors.
Licensed Ohio real estate brokerage since 2012.
```

Rules for the short version:

- **Use your legal or sign name.** Use the exact name on your Google Business Profile.
- **Name real cities.** Name only the cities where you manage homes now.
- **Give one number.** A door count, a start year or a staff count is easy for AI to repeat.
- **Skip adjectives.** "Best", "top" and "premier" give the engine nothing to check.

## What goes in the 150-word description?

The 150-word description adds the details an owner needs to decide on a call. It keeps the first sentence of the 50-word version, then adds services, fit and proof. Use it on your About page, in schema and in long directory fields.

Template (about 150 words):

```text
[Company name] manages [property types] for owners in [city list], [state].
We started in [year] and manage [number] doors today.

What we do: [service 1], [service 2], [service 3], [service 4] and [service 5].
Who we serve: [owner type 1, for example owners of 1 to 10 homes] and [owner type 2].
We do not manage [type you do not take, for example HOAs or commercial space].

How we work: [one concrete process, for example monthly owner statements on the 10th].
Our fees: see [your site]/fees for the full table.

[License or broker statement, with state].
[Association membership, if real].
[Office address or "We serve the area from [city]"].
```

The line "We do not manage" helps. AI answers often match a company to a narrow owner question. A clear "no" keeps you out of answers that lead to bad calls.

Point to your fee page, but do not put prices in the Google Business Profile text. Google's guidelines ask you to focus on the business, not on promotions, prices or sales.

## Which facts should you not put in?

Leave out any fact you cannot prove or keep current. The table shows what to cut and why.

| Do not put in | Why | Put in instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Best property manager in [city]" | No source backs it; AI cannot check it | A fact: door count, start year, license |
| Prices or sale offers in Google Business Profile | Google asks you to focus on the business, not prices or sales | "Fees: see our fee page" on your own site |
| Links or URLs in the Google Business Profile description | Google allows no links of any type in that field | Use the profile's website field |
| Cities you do not serve | Wrong answers send owners you cannot help | Only cities with homes under management now |
| A door count from 3 years ago | Two numbers on two sites look like a mistake | One current number, updated each year |
| Review scores ("4.9 stars") | Scores change; old text goes stale | Link to your review profiles |
| Tenant or owner names | Privacy, and no consent | A process or a service fact |
| Words that steer renters by a protected class | Fair Housing rules | Describe homes and services, not people |

## Where do you paste the description?

Paste the same text in every place AI reads about your company. Fix the long fields first, then the short ones. Our client playbook puts the listing check in week 1, because directories show up in so many AI answers (our data, 2026-10-19).

Step list:

1. **Your About page.** Paste the 150-word version as plain text near the top.
2. **Home page schema.** Put the 150-word text in the schema.org Organization `description` property. Put one sentence from the 50-word text in `disambiguatingDescription`. Schema.org defines that property as a short text that tells an item apart from similar items.
3. **Google Business Profile.** Paste the 150-word version. Stay under the 750-character limit that Google sets. Check that the first 250 characters hold your name, cities and property types.
4. **Yelp.** Paste the 150-word version in the business details text.
5. **Expertise.com, iPropertyManagement and BBB.** Use the short or long version to fit each field.
6. **Bing Places and Apple Business Connect.** Paste the 50-word version.
7. **LinkedIn company page.** Paste the 150-word version.
8. **NARPM or local association profile.** Paste the 50-word version, if you are a member.

Keep a list of every place in one sheet: site, field, version, date pasted. Check it each quarter. For the full site side (schema, crawlers, owner pages), see the [AI search checklist for property management websites](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website).

## How do you check that AI repeats it?

Ask the engines and read what they say back. Wait a few weeks after you paste the new text, because engines and directories refresh on their own schedule.

1. **Ask a brand prompt.** Type "What is [Company name] in [city]?" in ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude.
2. **Compare the facts.** Check the cities, the property types and your one number against your template.
3. **Find the source.** If a fact is wrong, open the sources the answer lists. Fix the text on that site first.
4. **Write it down.** Record the date, engine, answer and source in a sheet.

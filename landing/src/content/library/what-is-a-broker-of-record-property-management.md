## What is a broker of record?

A broker of record is the licensed real estate broker who is legally responsible for a brokerage. A property management company that leases units or collects rent for owners is a brokerage in many states. The state holds the broker of record to account for the work of the whole team.

The name changes by state. The role stays the same:

| State | Term in the law | Source |
|-------|-----------------|--------|
| Texas | Designated broker of a business entity | Occupations Code 1101.355 |
| California | Designated officer of a corporate license | Business and Professions Code 10159.2 |
| Common use | Broker of record, managing broker, principal broker | Industry terms |

In Texas, a business entity must "designate one of its managing officers as its broker". In California, the designated officer "shall be responsible for the supervision and control of the activities conducted on behalf of the corporation" (B&P 10159.2).

## Do property managers need a real estate license?

In many states, property managers need a real estate license when they lease or collect rent for others for pay. The rules differ by state, so check your own state's law. Two large states show how the rule works.

**Texas.** Occupations Code 1101.002 defines a broker as a person who, for pay, leases real estate, offers to lease it, or negotiates a lease. The list also includes a person who "controls the acceptance or deposit of rent from a resident of a single-family residential real property unit". Section 1101.005 exempts some people. It names an owner or the owner's employee who leases the owner's property, and "an on-site manager of an apartment complex".

**California.** Business and Professions Code 10131(b) covers leasing or renting property for others, soliciting tenants, and the act of a person who "collects rents from real property". Those acts need a broker license, or a salesperson license under a broker.

Quick test for an owner:

- Does the company show and lease your unit? A license is likely needed.
- Does the company collect and deposit your rent? A license is likely needed.
- Does the company only do repairs and never touch rent? A license may not be needed.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What does the broker of record supervise?

The broker of record supervises every licensed act the company does and the money it holds for others. California's Regulation 2725 gives the clearest list. A broker must set up policies, rules, procedures and systems to review, oversee, inspect and manage these areas:

1. **Licensed transactions.** Each lease and each deal that needs a license.
2. **Key documents.** Papers that can change a party's rights or duties, such as leases and management agreements.
3. **Records.** How those documents are filed, stored and kept.
4. **Trust funds.** Rent, security deposits and owner reserves held for others.
5. **Advertising.** Ads for any service that needs a license, including rental listings.
6. **Fair housing.** Staff must know the federal and state laws that ban discrimination.
7. **Reports.** "Regular and consistent reports of licensed activities" from the licensed staff.

Regulation 2725 also says the broker can hand tasks to others but "does not relinquish overall responsibility for supervision". The broker of record cannot blame a junior agent for a trust account error.

For property managers, trust funds carry the highest risk. A gap in a trust account is often the first problem a state auditor finds.

## What happens if a company loses its broker of record?

If a company loses its broker of record, it loses the right to do licensed work until it names a qualified replacement. Texas states this plainly. Under Occupations Code 1101.355(b), a business entity may not act as a broker unless its designated broker is "a licensed individual broker in active status and good standing".

In practice, a manager without an active broker of record cannot legally:

- Sign new leases for owners.
- Show and market units for owners, where the state counts this as a broker act.
- Collect rent and hold deposits, where the state counts this as a broker act.

Texas adds one more rule. If the designated broker owns less than 10% of the business entity, the entity must carry errors and omissions insurance with a minimum annual limit of $1 million for each occurrence (Occupations Code 1101.355(a)(2)).

What an owner should do: if your manager tells you its broker left, ask for the new broker's name and license number in writing. Check it before your next rent payment goes out.

## How to check a property manager's broker of record

You can check a property manager's broker of record in about 10 minutes. Run these 6 checks before you sign a management agreement:

| Check | Where | Pass |
|-------|-------|------|
| Company license | State real estate commission lookup | Active |
| Broker of record name | Same lookup, or ask the company | Named person with a broker license |
| Broker license status | Same lookup | Active, in good standing |
| Public discipline | Commission lookup or orders page | None, or old and resolved |
| Trust account | Ask the company | Separate account in the company's name as trustee |
| Agreement | Your management agreement | Names the licensed company that signs |

Red flags:

- The company cannot name its broker of record.
- The person who signs your agreement is not licensed, and the agreement does not name a licensed company.
- Rent goes to a personal account.

## Why the broker of record matters to an owner

The broker of record matters to an owner because that person carries the legal duty for your money and your leases. A one-person company and a 500-door firm have the same duty. The difference is how many staff the broker supervises.

Ask the broker of record 3 questions in your first call:

1. How many licensed staff do you supervise?
2. Who reconciles the trust account each month, and who reviews it?
3. What would happen to my rent and deposits if you left the company?

## What is the warranty of habitability?

The warranty of habitability is the landlord's duty to keep a rental fit to live in. It applies during the whole tenancy, not just at move-in. Each state defines it in its own way.

| State | How the law sets the duty | Source |
|---|---|---|
| California | A list of required items; a unit that "substantially lacks" one is untenantable | Civil Code 1941.1 |
| Texas | Repair conditions that "materially affect the physical health or safety of an ordinary tenant," after notice | Property Code 92.052 |
| Florida | Follow building, housing and health codes; where none apply, keep structure and plumbing in good repair | Statutes 83.51(1) |
| New York | A right to "a livable, safe and sanitary apartment," implied in every lease | NY Attorney General guide (RPL § 235-b) |

Local housing codes often add more. Check your city code too.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Heat, water and power: what must work?

Heat, plumbing, hot and cold water and electricity must work in every state we checked. The wording differs.

- **California:** Civil Code 1941.1(a) lists plumbing and gas "maintained in good working order," hot and cold running water connected to an approved sewage system, heating facilities, and electrical lighting and wiring.
- **Texas:** Property Code 92.052 names a device that supplies hot water of at least 120 degrees Fahrenheit.
- **Florida:** In buildings other than a single-family home or duplex, 83.51(2) requires "functioning facilities for heat during winter, running water, and hot water," unless agreed otherwise in writing.
- **New York:** The Attorney General lists failure to provide heat or hot water on a regular basis as a breach.

California adds two appliances for new leases. For a lease entered into, amended or extended on or after January 1, 2026, 1941.1 requires a working stove and a working refrigerator. A recalled stove or refrigerator does not count as working. A tenant may choose to supply their own refrigerator by a signed lease statement.

## Structure, roof and windows: what is required?

The building must keep out the weather and be safe to walk in. California 1941.1(a) requires "effective waterproofing and weather protection of roof and exterior walls, including unbroken windows and doors," and floors, stairs and railings "maintained in good repair."

Florida 83.51(1)(b) applies where no local code exists. It requires roofs, windows, doors, floors, steps, porches, exterior walls and foundations "in good repair and capable of resisting normal forces and loads." It also requires screens at the start of the tenancy, repaired once a year when needed.

A quick check before each lease:

- [ ] No roof or wall leaks; no water stains that grow.
- [ ] All windows close and none are broken.
- [ ] Exterior doors close, latch and seal.
- [ ] Floors, stairs and railings are solid.
- [ ] Gas appliances vent safely.

## Pests and mold: who is responsible?

The landlord is usually responsible for pests at move-in and for building-wide problems. Tenant-caused problems are often the tenant's to fix.

California 1941.1(a)(6) requires the building and grounds to be clean and free of "rodents, and vermin" at the start of the lease, and all areas the landlord controls to stay that way. Florida 83.51(2)(a) requires, in buildings other than a single-family home or duplex, "reasonable provisions" for extermination of rats, mice, roaches, ants, wood-destroying organisms and bedbugs. If the tenant must leave for treatment, Florida requires 7 days' written notice, a stay of no more than 4 days, and a rent abatement.

New York's Attorney General lists failure to "rid an apartment of an insect infestation" as a breach. Mold rules vary by state and city. Fix the water source first, then the mold.

In pre-1978 housing, peeling paint can be a lead hazard. The EPA says deteriorating lead-based paint "is a hazard and needs prompt attention."

## Locks and safety items: what must I install?

Many states require working locks, and some list the exact devices. Texas Property Code 92.153 is one of the most specific. Without a tenant request, each dwelling must have:

| Item | Where |
|---|---|
| Window latch | Each exterior window |
| Doorknob lock or keyed dead bolt | Each exterior door |
| Pin lock | Each exterior sliding glass door |
| Handle latch or security bar | Each exterior sliding glass door |
| Keyless bolting device and a door viewer | Each exterior door |

Florida 83.51(2) requires "locks and keys" in buildings other than a single-family home or duplex. For a single-family home or duplex, it requires working smoke detection devices at the start of the tenancy. Check your state's smoke and carbon monoxide alarm rules as well.

## What happens if a unit fails?

If a unit fails, the tenant gets remedies, and the landlord can owe money. The remedies differ by state.

| State | Tenant remedy | Source |
|---|---|---|
| California | Repair and deduct up to one month's rent, no more than twice in 12 months; or vacate. After 30 days, the tenant is presumed to have waited a reasonable time | Civil Code 1942 |
| Texas | Court order to repair, rent reduction, a civil penalty of one month's rent plus $500, actual damages, and more | Property Code 92.0563 |
| Florida | Rent withholding as a defense 7 days after written notice; court reduces rent for the lost value | Statutes 83.60(1)(b) |
| New York | Suit for rent reduction; withhold rent and countersue; the reduction equals rent minus the value without the services | NY Attorney General guide |

The landlord is not liable for every condition. Texas 92.052(b) excludes conditions caused by the tenant, household members or guests, unless caused by normal wear and tear. New York's guide says a condition the tenant caused "does not constitute a breach."

## Wisconsin eviction rules at a glance

Wisconsin splits an eviction in two parts. First, a notice under chapter 704 ends the tenancy. Then a small claims case under chapter 799 gets the court's order. We read the official PDFs of chapters 704, 799 and 814 on 2026-10-19. The live legislature site did not load from our network, so we used the Internet Archive copies of the official files.

| Step or rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Unpaid rent, month-to-month | 5-day pay or vacate, or 14-day notice to vacate | Wis. Stat. 704.17(1p)(a) |
| Unpaid rent, lease of 1 year or less | 5-day pay or vacate; 14-day notice after a repeat within 1 year | Wis. Stat. 704.17(2)(a) |
| Unpaid rent or breach, lease over 1 year | 30-day notice to pay or comply | Wis. Stat. 704.17(3)(a) |

## What notice must a Wisconsin landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

The notice depends on the type of tenancy. Wis. Stat. 704.17 defines "rent" to include past-due rent and late fees owed on it.

**Month-to-month or week-to-week tenant.** You may give a notice to pay rent or vacate by a date at least 5 days after the notice. If the tenant does not pay, the tenancy ends. Or, while the tenant is in default, give a notice to vacate at least 14 days out. That notice gives no right to pay and stay (Wis. Stat. 704.17(1p)(a)).

**Lease of one year or less, or year-to-year.** You give a notice to pay rent or vacate at least 5 days out. A second late payment can come within one year of a prior default with notice. Then you may give a 14-day notice to vacate (Wis. Stat. 704.17(2)(a)).

**Lease of more than one year.** You must give the tenant at least 30 days to pay (Wis. Stat. 704.17(3)(a)).

Proof of service is simpler by certified mail. Wis. Stat. 799.40(1g) says the post office's proof of certified mailing is enough to show notice under 704.21(1)(d). The court may not ask for an affidavit of service.

Accepting late rent after the notice does not end the case. Under Wis. Stat. 799.40(1m), the court may not dismiss the action because you took past-due rent or another payment after the notice or the filing.

## How long does an eviction take in Wisconsin?

A Wisconsin eviction for unpaid rent can reach the first court date in as little as 11 days. The statutes set these minimums:

| Phase | Statutory time | Running total |
|---|---|---|
| 5-day notice | At least 5 days after the notice is given (704.17) | Day 5 |
| File and get the summons | Your choice; day 6 at the earliest | Day 6 |
| Return date | 5 to 25 days from issue (799.05(3)(b)) | Day 11 to 31 |
| Judgment and writ | Ordered at once if you win (799.44) | Same day |
| Hardship stay, if granted | Up to 30 days (799.44(3)) | Up to day 61 |
| Sheriff's execution | After the writ and fee are delivered (799.45(1)) | Varies |

A tenant may also ask for a stay for emergency assistance. That stay ends when eligibility is decided and any aid is paid, but it may not last more than 10 working days. No such stay is allowed after the writ issues (Wis. Stat. 799.40(4)(a)).

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Wisconsin, and what does it cost?

A Wisconsin landlord files an eviction as a small claims action in the circuit court of the county. Chapter 799 controls the process.

1. **Complaint.** Write it, sign it and name the parties and the property. A street address is enough. State the facts that allow the removal (Wis. Stat. 799.41).
2. **Summons.** The clerk sets the return date and time. For an eviction, it is 5 to 25 days from issue (Wis. Stat. 799.05(3)).
3. **Service.** Serve the complaint with the summons at least 5 days before the return date (Wis. Stat. 799.42, 799.05(3)(b)).
4. **Return date.** The tenant may answer orally or in writing (Wis. Stat. 799.43).

You may join a claim for unpaid rent or damage with the claim for possession (Wis. Stat. 799.40(2)).

The base fee is $22. Wis. Stat. 814.62(3)(a) sets it at issuance of the summons in a small claims action. Other fees in chapter 814 may be added, so ask the clerk for the total. The sheriff charges too. Wis. Stat. 814.70(8) sets $8 to serve a writ with the county's aid. It adds $10 per hour for each deputy assigned to inventory property in an eviction, plus necessary expenses.

## Can a Wisconsin landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes to both, with the right notice. For a lease violation other than rent, a month-to-month tenant may get one of two notices (Wis. Stat. 704.17(1p)(b)):

- **5-day cure notice.** Remedy the breach or vacate in at least 5 days.
- **14-day notice to vacate.** No right to cure.

A tenant on a lease of one year or less gets the 5-day cure notice. After a repeat breach within one year of a notice, the landlord may give a 14-day notice to vacate (Wis. Stat. 704.17(2)(b)).

A tenant complies with a cure notice by taking reasonable steps at once and proceeding with diligence. Or the tenant may make a good-faith offer to pay all damages, where money damages are enough.

For no cause, end a periodic tenancy under Wis. Stat. 704.19. Give at least 28 days' written notice, ending at the end of a rental period (704.19(2)(b), (3)). A tenant who holds over after a lease expires may be removed by any lawful means (Wis. Stat. 704.25(1)). For a residential lease, you may instead elect to hold the tenant month-to-month (704.25(2)(b)).

Two limits apply. You may not evict to retaliate against a tenant's good-faith code complaint, repair complaint or use of a legal right (Wis. Stat. 704.45). And a lease clause that lets you act because a tenant called police, health or safety services is void (Wis. Stat. 704.44(1m)).

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Wisconsin?

The county sheriff removes the tenant. When the court finds you are entitled to possession, it orders judgment at once and orders a writ of restitution (Wis. Stat. 799.44(1)-(2)). You deliver the writ to the sheriff and pay the fee. The sheriff may not execute a writ received more than 30 days after it was issued.

Under Wis. Stat. 799.45(2), the sheriff removes the tenant and everyone claiming under the tenant, with reasonable force as needed. The sheriff also removes the tenant's personal property, unless you tell the sheriff that you will remove and store or dispose of it yourself (799.45(3m)). The sheriff may hire a mover. Within 3 days of removal, the sheriff mails the tenant a notice of where the goods are kept (799.45(3)(b)).

The sheriff may ask for a deposit to cover the likely cost of moving the property before executing the writ (Wis. Stat. 799.45(1)).

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Wisconsin?

No. Wisconsin requires the court process. Wis. Stat. 704.44(2m) makes a residential lease void and unenforceable if it allows eviction or exclusion of a tenant other than by judicial eviction under chapter 799. ATCP 134.08(2) repeats the rule.

Utilities need care too. ATCP 134.04(3) requires you to disclose, before signing, whether water, heat or electricity is not included in rent. Do not use a shutoff to force a move-out. A tenant who loses money from a violation of ATCP 134 may sue for twice the loss under Wis. Stat. 100.20(5), as the chapter's note says.

## Wisconsin eviction checklist for landlords

- [ ] Pick the right notice for the tenancy type: 5-day, 14-day, 28-day or 30-day.
- [ ] Give the notice by a method in Wis. Stat. 704.21 and keep proof.
- [ ] Serve by certified mail and keep the post office proof.
- [ ] File a small claims complaint; budget the $22 base fee plus other fees.
- [ ] Serve the summons and complaint at least 5 days before the return date.
- [ ] Get the writ to the sheriff within 30 days; pay the sheriff's fee and any deposit.
- [ ] Never change locks or cut utilities yourself.

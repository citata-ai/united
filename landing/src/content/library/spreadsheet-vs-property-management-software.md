## What does a spreadsheet cost you in time per unit?

A spreadsheet costs the time you spend entering, checking, and finding records. Measure those tasks instead of using a door-count rule. A simple sheet may serve an owner well until the work changes.

Keep a work log during a full rent cycle. Note payment entry, reminders, receipt filing, repairs, and report checks. Include the time spent finding a mistake, not just entering a correct payment.

Use this planning formula: monthly manual cost equals work hours multiplied by your chosen hourly value. Add the cost of any tools you already pay for. Your hourly value is an assumption, not a market wage claim.

Divide the total by occupied units to see the cost per unit. Keep a separate count for mistakes and missed follow-ups. Software may save time while leaving those problems unresolved if the setup is poor.

## Which tasks break first in a spreadsheet?

Repeated data entry and shared handoffs are the first tasks to examine. A formula can add rent correctly while the team still posts a payment to the wrong tenant.

| Task | Spreadsheet warning sign | Software test |
|---|---|---|
| Rent tracking | Bank entry and rent roll disagree | Match receipt to tenant and bank |
| Repairs | Request stays in a text thread | Open, assign, and close a work order |
| Leases | Renewal date lives in one person's notes | Show reminders and responsible staff |
| Files | Receipt has no property link | Retrieve receipt from the expense |
| Team work | Several copies of the sheet exist | Check roles and change history |

These are workflow checks, not claims about error rates. Use your own records to judge which problem matters most.

A manager holding money for clients should assess accounting controls separately. Rentvine describes bank, trust ledger, and property ledger reconciliation. A shared cash sheet does not prove those balances agree.

## What is the cheapest step up from a spreadsheet?

A free landlord platform can be the cheapest subscription step. It is only a step up if it handles the work you now repeat.

Avail and TurboTenant both publish free landlord plans on their pricing pages, read 2026-10-19. Stessa also lists free Essentials with bank feeds and basic reports. Review payment and optional service charges apart from the subscription.

Choose the starting tool by the task. A rent collection problem needs a clear payment ledger and tenant process. A bookkeeping problem needs reliable transaction categories and useful exports.

Do not migrate every task just because a plan is free. Try the area that consumes the most time. Keep a separate plan for owner accounting, staff roles, or complex properties that exceed the trial's scope.

## How do you move spreadsheet data into software?

Move clean records after you agree on a cutoff date and verify opening balances. Importing a messy sheet can create a larger problem with a cleaner screen.

Start by saving a read-only copy of the workbook. Define a stable identifier for each property, unit, lease, and tenant. Separate names from identifiers so a spelling change does not create a duplicate record.

Ask the vendor for its current import fields. Map each field and mark missing data. Do not guess a lease date, deposit amount, or bank balance to finish an upload.

Import sample records before the full file. Include a vacant unit, a tenant balance, a credit, and a deposit. Compare each result with the source sheet and bank evidence.

Check totals before you invite tenants. Have someone else review the balances and charges. Only then turn on payment reminders or recurring billing. Keep the old sheet as a reference, with a clear date when entries stopped.

## Is free software good enough?

Free software is good enough when it meets your daily tasks, record needs, and exit requirements. Price alone does not answer that question.

Run a full rent cycle with a small, permitted pilot. Check how payments settle, how returns appear, and how you correct a receipt. Ask a tenant tester whether the portal makes the amount due clear.

Then close the period. Export income, expenses, tenant balances, and supporting files. Make sure your accountant can use the output without rebuilding the workbook by hand.

A free platform may still charge applicants or tenants. Disclose the payment method and charge before you launch. A lower landlord bill should not hide a confusing tenant experience.

## How do you decide whether the switch worked?

The switch worked if your measured work fell and your records stayed correct. Compare the pilot with the work log you kept before it.

Track the same tasks in both periods. Do not count a task as saved when another person now does it elsewhere. Include support calls, bank checks, and time correcting imported records.

Keep the spreadsheet for forecasts if it remains useful. The accounting record and the planning sheet can serve different purposes. Label each clearly so nobody treats a forecast as the rent ledger.

Write a short decision note before you expand the pilot. Name the tasks improved, gaps still open, and person responsible for each gap. Use the [guides library](/guides) for related operations topics, then test the weakest task next.

## How should you test the choice before you pay?

Test the full workflow with sample records before you move live data. Use the same case in each product. Ask the person who will do the daily work to run the test. A sales demo alone does not show where your team will need help.

Keep a decision sheet with the task, result, missing step, and person who checked it. Mark each result as shown, promised, or still open. Ask for written terms when a feature needs an upgrade or another service. Include staff setup time in your cost review.

Check the exit path while you still control the choice. Export the sample records and open them outside the platform. Confirm that notes, files, dates, and links remain useful. Ask how you will get records after you close the account.

Choose a tool when the test proves a clear gain for your workflow. Keep your current process until the new process passes. Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to see how software costs affect your service margin.

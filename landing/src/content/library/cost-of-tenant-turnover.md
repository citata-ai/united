## What does one tenant turnover cost?

One turnover costs lost rent plus the work to get the unit ready and re-rented. Each line is small. Together they often pass two months of rent.

Industry figures give a range. Lessen, a maintenance vendor, cites Multifamily Executive: turnover costs operators "$3,500–$5,000 per unit" when you count vacancy loss, marketing, make-ready work and leasing commissions. Lessen also says turnover costs rose 17.5% in 2024, citing the National Apartment Association's Income/Expense IQ report. These are apartment-operator figures. A single-family rental can cost more or less.

Your own numbers matter more. The worked example below shows how to build them.

## How much rent do I lose while the unit is vacant?

Lost rent equals the daily rent times the vacant days. Daily rent is the monthly rent times 12, divided by 365.

| Monthly rent | Daily rent | 14 vacant days | 30 vacant days | 45 vacant days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1,200 | $39.45 | $552 | $1,184 | $1,775 |
| $1,500 | $49.32 | $690 | $1,479 | $2,219 |
| $2,000 | $65.75 | $921 | $1,973 | $2,959 |

There is no tax deduction for this loss. IRS Publication 527 says you "can't deduct any loss of rental income for the period the property is vacant." You may still deduct ordinary expenses for the vacant unit.

The US rental vacancy rate was 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026 (Census Bureau, via FRED). A unit in a soft market can sit longer than the examples above.

This is general tax information. Ask a tax professional about your return.

## What do cleaning, paint and repairs cost?

Cleaning, paint and repairs depend on the unit's size and condition, and on local prices. Get two bids for each and save them. They become your price list for the next turn.

Who pays matters too. California Civil Code 1950.5(e) bars deposit claims for "ordinary wear and tear." Texas Property Code 92.104(b) bars keeping any part of the deposit "to cover normal wear and tear." So routine repaint and normal aging are your cost. Damage beyond normal wear is the tenant's cost, if you document it.

Typical make-ready tasks:

- [ ] Deep clean, including appliances and windows.
- [ ] Touch-up or full paint.
- [ ] Carpet clean or replace; floor repairs.
- [ ] Re-key or change locks.
- [ ] Replace filters, bulbs and smoke alarm batteries.
- [ ] Fix items from the move-out inspection.

## What do marketing and screening cost?

Marketing and screening cost cash and time. Listing sites, photos and a sign are the cash part. Showings, calls and application review are the time part.

In some states the applicant pays the screening fee, with a cap. California Civil Code 1950.6 caps the application screening fee at the actual cost, with a ceiling of $30 per applicant adjusted each year. You still spend time on each application. If a manager leases the unit, a leasing fee often replaces your time.

## Worked example on a $1,500 rental

This example uses example inputs, not market averages. Replace each line with your own bids and records.

| Cost line | Example input | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Lost rent | 30 vacant days × $49.32 | $1,479 |
| Utilities and lawn while vacant | 1 month | $150 |
| Cleaning | Deep clean, 2-bed | $300 |
| Paint | Touch-up plus 2 rooms | $550 |
| Repairs | Blinds, door stop, faucet cartridge | $240 |
| Re-key | 2 locks | $120 |
| Marketing | Photos and listing upgrades | $100 |
| Your time | 10 hours of showings and screening at $20/hour | $200 |
| **Total turnover cost** | | **$3,139** |
| Minus tenant-caused damage charged to the deposit | Documented, beyond wear and tear | −$180 |
| **Net cost to you** | | **$2,959** |

Lost rent is 47% of the total in this example. Cut the vacant days from 30 to 14 and you save about $790. That is more than the cleaning and repairs together.

## How does the turnover rate change my yearly cost?

Your yearly turnover cost is the cost per turn times the number of turns. Lessen says "roughly 42% of units" turn each year, with each turnover "averaging nearly $4,000." Your own rate may differ, so use your records.

| Portfolio | Turnover rate | Turns per year | Cost per turn (our example) | Yearly turnover cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 rental | 1 turn every 3 years | 0.33 | $3,139 | $1,046 |
| 10 rentals | 42% | 4.2 | $3,139 | $13,184 |
| 10 rentals | 25% | 2.5 | $3,139 | $7,848 |

Cutting turnover from 42% to 25% in a 10-unit portfolio saves about $5,300 a year in this example. That is why renewals are the cheapest turnover.

## How do I cut turnover costs?

Cut turnover costs by cutting vacant days and by keeping good tenants longer. Five steps help:

1. **Offer renewals early.** Ask 60 to 90 days before the lease ends. A renewal has no turnover cost.
2. **Market before move-out.** List the unit as soon as you get notice, with clear show dates.
3. **Pre-book vendors.** Book the cleaner and painter for the day after move-out.
4. **Use one make-ready checklist.** The same list every time finds problems faster.
5. **Price to the market.** A small rent cut often costs less than two empty weeks.

Lessen says that even under efficient conditions "a make-ready takes the better part of a week." Plan for it, and start leasing before the work ends.

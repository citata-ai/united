## What are the one-time costs?

The one-time costs to start a property management company are the license, the company setup and the bank accounts. Most of them depend on your state.

| One-time cost | Typical amount | Source or note |
|---|---|---|
| Real estate broker or property manager license | Varies by state | Your state real estate commission |
| Pre-license courses and exam | Varies by state | Your state real estate commission |
| LLC or corporation filing | Varies by state | Your secretary of state |
| Business bank accounts (operating plus trust) | $99 each on Buildium Essential | Buildium pricing page; Growth includes 10 free |
| NARPM membership, first year | $270 ($320 less a $50 new member discount) | NARPM membership page |
| Website and logo | Your choice | Build or buy |
| Lawyer review of your management agreement | Your quote | Ask a local attorney |

Watch for: many states require a broker license to manage rentals for others. Some do not. Check your state real estate commission before you sign a client. Wexford Insurance gives the same warning.

The IRS treats most costs before you open as start-up costs. IRS Publication 583 says you can elect to deduct up to $5,000 of start-up costs and up to $5,000 of organizational costs. The deduction falls when those costs pass $50,000. This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## What are the monthly fixed costs?

The monthly fixed costs are software and insurance. Both have public prices.

**Software.** Prices read on the vendor pages on 2026-10-19:

| Software plan | Starting price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Buildium Essential | $62 per month | No unit minimum shown |
| Buildium Growth | $192 per month | Live phone support, lower EFT fees |
| AppFolio Core | $1.49 per unit per month | 50-unit minimum and a minimum spend |

At 50 units, AppFolio Core is at least $74.50 a month before its minimum spend. For a side-by-side view, read [AppFolio vs Buildium](/guides/appfolio-vs-buildium).

**Insurance.** Insureon publishes average premiums for property managers:

| Policy | Average per month | Average per year |
|---|---|---|
| General liability ($1M per occurrence, $2M aggregate) | $44 | $528 |
| Errors and omissions ($1M / $1M, $1,000 deductible) | $83 | about $996 |
| Cyber insurance (real estate businesses) | $58 | $692 |
| Surety bond (real estate professionals) | $21 | $250 |
| Business owner's policy (liability plus property) | $212 | $2,547 |
| Workers' compensation | $73 | $880 |
| Commercial auto | $167 | $2,007 |

Your price depends on your state, revenue, staff and claims. Treat these as planning numbers, then get quotes.

## What does a home-based start cost?

A home-based start costs about $268 a month for software and core insurance. That is the lowest real budget we can build from public prices.

| Home-based monthly budget | Amount |
|---|---|
| Buildium Essential | $62 |
| General liability | $44 |
| Errors and omissions | $83 |
| Cyber insurance | $58 |
| Surety bond | $21 |
| **Total** | **$268 a month, or $3,216 a year** |

Add the one-time costs above and your phone, car and marketing. Many software fees are per use: Buildium Essential charges $5 per eSignature document and $2.35 per incoming EFT payment.

An office start costs more. Swap general liability for a business owner's policy (+$168), add workers' compensation ($73) and move to Buildium Growth (+$130). That adds $371 a month before rent. One admin at the US median receptionist salary of $38,010 (O*NET, BLS 2025 data) adds about $3,168 a month before taxes.

## How long until the company pays for itself?

The company pays its fixed costs fast, but it pays you a fair salary only after about 100 doors. Two numbers from the 2022 NARPM Financial Performance Guide show why.

**Margin by size.** The study gave every owner a market salary, $65,000 a year at the low end of its 2021 pay scale. Then it measured profit:

| Units under management | 2021 profit margin |
|---|---|
| 0–100 | -1% |
| 101–250 | 4% |
| 251–400 | 11% |
| 401–600 | 18% |

**Payback per door.** Each new door costs money to win: ads, sales time, proposals. For companies with 22 to 158 units, the all-in cost to add a unit was $2,471. They earned it back in about 13 months. Companies with 164 to 317 units took about 6 months.

A worked example. You win 50 doors at $222 of revenue per door per month, the 2021 study average. That is $11,100 a month, or $133,200 a year. Your fixed costs from the home-based table are $3,216 a year. Everything else goes to your own pay, marketing and the next hire. At 50 doors, you have a good job, not yet a company with profit.

Wexford Insurance says the same in words: under 50 doors, "most owners are effectively buying themselves a job".

## How do I keep startup costs low?

Keep startup costs low by paying for software and insurance only, and buying time with process, not staff. A short checklist:

- [ ] Confirm the license rule with your state commission before you take a client.
- [ ] Open a separate trust account on day 1. Never mix owner funds with your own.
- [ ] Start on a plan with no unit minimum. Move up when the per-use fees cost more than the next plan.
- [ ] Get quotes for general liability and E&O before you sign the first agreement.
- [ ] Price your fees with our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) before you set them.
- [ ] Track the cost to win each door. Compare it with 13 months of revenue from that door.

Watch for: low fees to win the first doors make every later step harder. The NARPM study found no link between higher pricing and owner churn.

## What are the review reward rules in one table?

Three rule sets apply to a property management company that asks for reviews: Google's policy, the FTC rule, and Yelp's policy. They do not match. Google and Yelp are platform rules: break them and you lose reviews or get a warning label. The FTC rule is federal law.

| Practice | Google | FTC (16 CFR 465) | Yelp |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ask every client for an honest review | Allowed | Allowed | Not allowed: "Don't ask" |
| Reward for any review, any rating | Not allowed | Not banned by the rule, if no sentiment is required | Not allowed |
| Reward only for a 5-star review | Not allowed | Banned (465.4) | Not allowed |
| Ask only happy clients (gating) | Not allowed | No rule section; may break the FTC Act | Not allowed |
| Staff or family write reviews | Not allowed (conflict of interest) | Disclosure required (465.5) | Not allowed |
| Buy fake reviews | Not allowed | Banned (465.2) | Consumer Alert |
| Threaten a reviewer | Not addressed here | Banned if groundless (465.7) | Consumer Alert |

Sources: Google Maps content policy, eCFR Part 465, FTC Q&A, Yelp Support.

## What do Google review policies say about incentives?

Google bans every incentive for a review. Its Maps content policy says merchants may not offer incentives "such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services" in exchange for posting any review. The same ban covers rewards to change or remove a negative review.

Google's Business Profile help repeats the point. It calls incentivized reviews "fake & misleading content" and says they are strictly prohibited.

Google adds 3 more rules that hit property managers:

- **No conflict of interest.** Reviews from current or former staff, contractors or family break the policy.
- **No selective asks.** Merchants may not discourage negative reviews or selectively solicit positive ones.
- **No staff quotas.** Merchants may not ask staff to collect a set number of reviews, or reviews that name a staff member.

Google does allow you to ask for honest reviews with no reward. It suggests a Google review link or a QR code.

## What do FTC rules say about fake reviews?

The FTC rule is 16 CFR Part 465, the Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials. The FTC says it took effect on October 21, 2024. Courts can impose civil penalties for knowing violations.

The sections that matter most for a PM company:

1. **465.2, fake reviews.** A business may not write, create or sell a review that misstates who the reviewer is or their experience. It may not buy such reviews.
2. **465.4, buying positive or negative reviews.** A business may not give a reward conditioned on a review with a particular sentiment.
3. **465.5, insider reviews.** An officer or manager who writes a review must clearly disclose the link to the business.
4. **465.7, review suppression.** Groundless legal threats or intimidation to stop or remove a review are banned.
5. **465.8, fake social influence.** Buying fake followers or views for a commercial purpose is banned.

The key word in 465.4 is sentiment. FTC staff say the rule does not bar a reward for a review. The condition: no express or implied demand for a set sentiment. Staff add that an undisclosed reward could still break the FTC Act. So the federal floor is lower than Google's. Google still bans the neutral reward on its own platform.

## Is review gating allowed?

Review gating is not allowed on Google. Gating means you send a short survey first and invite only the happy clients to post in public. Google's policy bans selectively soliciting positive reviews. Yelp says many businesses ask only happy customers, and it treats that as a reason not to ask at all.

The FTC rule has no section that names gating. But the FTC says the practice could violate the FTC Act. So "the rule does not mention it" is not a safe harbor.

Watch for: review software with a "route unhappy clients to private feedback" switch. Turn it off. Send the same public review link to every client.

## Which reward programs are safe for a property manager?

A safe program gives no reward for a review and asks everyone the same way. Use this checklist:

- **Ask every owner and tenant at the same moments.** For example: 30 days after a lease starts, after a repair closes, at each renewal.
- **Use one message for all.** "Tell us how we did on Google" with the link. No "if you are happy".
- **Give no reward tied to a review.** No gift card, rent credit, fee waiver or raffle entry.
- **Keep thank-you gifts separate.** A holiday card to all clients is fine when it has no review link and no review condition.
- **Do not ask staff, vendors or family to review.** Google calls that a conflict of interest.
- **Reply to every review.** Google suggests short, polite, honest replies, and never sharing private data.
- **Skip Yelp asks.** Yelp says not to ask anyone.
- **Never threaten a reviewer.** Flag a review that breaks policy through the platform instead.

Yelp enforces its rules in public. Its 2025 Trust & Safety Report counts 491 Compensated Activity and Suspicious Review Activity Alerts on business pages. Owners who see that pop-up on your page will doubt every star.

## How many reviews is enough?

There is no legal minimum, but reviews feed your local rank. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking. A steady, honest ask beats a burst of rewarded reviews that Google may remove. AI answers quote your rating too.

## How do their prices compare?

Yardi Breeze publishes a residential per-unit rate with a monthly minimum. Buildium publishes starting prices by plan. Compare both on your actual door count and service mix.

Yardi's residential rate in the table assumes an annual agreement. Ask how a mixed portfolio affects the minimum and whether Premier tools change the price.

Buildium's plan table separates subscription tiers from optional services and transaction fees. Request a quote with your expected payments, screenings, documents, and bank accounts.

Do not calculate a complete Buildium bill from the base alone. Do not treat the Breeze per-unit rate as the price of all Yardi products. Write down the exact plan and modules in each offer.

Include conversion and training work in the first-period budget. Then compare the ongoing bill after temporary offers expire. A useful quote makes those changes clear before signature.

## Which has better support?

Yardi Breeze lists live chat and on-screen learning tools. Buildium lists ticket support for all plans and phone support for Growth and Premium. The better fit depends on how your team gets help.

Ask each vendor to resolve a sample accounting question through the support route included in the quote. Record whether staff receive a useful answer without another sales call.

Check coverage hours and escalation for payment or login failures. A maintenance emergency and a software problem require different response paths. Do not assume software support dispatches repair help.

Look for clear training materials for a new hire. Ask who handles learning after launch and whether live sessions cost extra. The person who builds the account may not stay on your team forever.

Keep support terms in the decision sheet. A helpful demo agent does not establish the service included after purchase.

## Which handles commercial units?

Yardi Breeze has a dedicated commercial offer with lease and expense recovery tools. Buildium lists commercial portfolios, but you should test your actual lease terms in its current plan.

Yardi's commercial page names common area maintenance recovery, percentage rent, and scheduled rent changes. Common area maintenance means shared property costs. Test the terms your leases actually use.

A small office lease and a complex retail lease need different workflows. Bring a sample with expense pools, caps, and reconciliations if those apply. Ask the vendor to produce the tenant charge.

Check whether residential and commercial reports can share the owner view you need. Confirm account and entity separation. A single login does not establish a single suitable accounting process.

If a requirement cannot be shown, mark it open. Do not accept a promise that a spreadsheet export will solve a core commercial task without testing the complete process.

## Which has better reports?

The better reports reconcile to the books and answer your owners' questions with little extra work. Both vendors describe property accounting and financial reporting.

Use the same sample period and accounting basis in each product. Compare the owner statement, income report, rent roll, and cash detail. Ask staff to trace a figure to its source.

Then test an owner with several properties. Check whether reports show property detail and a clear total. Verify how reserves, unpaid bills, deposits, and distributions appear.

Ask whether staff can save a report setup without giving everyone broad edit rights. A repeated close should not rely on a single expert remembering filters.

Export the reports and review them outside the app. Owners and accountants may use files rather than portals. Check that labels and dates remain clear.

## Which is easier to learn?

Ease of learning needs a task test with your staff. Public feature pages and selected testimonials cannot establish which product will be easier for your company.

Give a new user a short set of normal tasks. Ask them to enter a receipt, open a work order, find a lease, and prepare an owner statement.

Record where they need help and whether the help is easy to find. Test correction as well as entry. Staff who can post a transaction but cannot fix it still need training.

Choose after the accounting and service users complete the same tasks. For another plan comparison, read the live [AppFolio vs Buildium guide](/guides/appfolio-vs-buildium), then return to your own test results.

## How should you test the choice before you pay?

Test the full workflow with sample records before you move live data. Use the same case in each product. Ask the person who will do the daily work to run the test. A sales demo alone does not show where your team will need help.

Keep a decision sheet with the task, result, missing step, and person who checked it. Mark each result as shown, promised, or still open. Ask for written terms when a feature needs an upgrade or another service. Include staff setup time in your cost review.

Check the exit path while you still control the choice. Export the sample records and open them outside the platform. Confirm that notes, files, dates, and links remain useful. Ask how you will get records after you close the account.

Choose a tool when the test proves a clear gain for your workflow. Keep your current process until the new process passes. Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to see how software costs affect your service margin.

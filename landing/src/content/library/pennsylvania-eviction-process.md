A tenant misses the October rent. The landlord serves a 10-day notice to quit on October 6. If the tenant stays, the earliest lockout by a constable falls in mid-November. Two sets of rules set that pace: the Pennsylvania Landlord and Tenant Act of 1951 and the court rules for magisterial district judges. I read both on 2026-10-19. This page puts the steps, deadlines and 2026 court costs in one place.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

| Notice to quit, lease end or breach, lease of 1 year or less | 15 days from service | Act, Section 501(b) |
| Notice to quit, lease end or breach, lease over 1 year | 30 days from service | Act, Section 501(b) |

Rule 581 suspends parts of the Act that conflict with the court rules. Where the two differ, follow the rules.

## What Notice Must a Pennsylvania Landlord Give Before Filing an Eviction for Unpaid Rent?

A Pennsylvania landlord gives a written 10-day notice to quit for unpaid rent. Section 501(b) of the Act says the notice tells the tenant to remove within 10 days from the date of service. Section 501(f) allows three ways to serve it:

1. **In person.** Hand it to the tenant.
2. **At the building.** Leave it at the main building on the premises.
3. **By posting.** Post it in a clear spot on the leased premises.

Section 501(e) lets the notice be shorter, or waived, if the lease says so. Many Pennsylvania leases include such a clause. Read your lease before you count days.

## How Long Does an Eviction Take in Pennsylvania?

An uncontested Pennsylvania eviction for unpaid rent takes about 6 to 8 weeks. Here is the count with the shortest and longest waits in the rules. Day 0 is the day you serve the notice.

| Step | Shortest | Longest | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Notice period ends | Day 10 | Day 10 | Section 501(b) |
| File complaint | Day 11 | Day 11 | Rule 502 |
| Hearing | Day 18 | Day 26 | Rule 504 |
| Judgment | Day 18 | Day 29 | Rule 514D |
| Request order for possession | Day 29 | Day 40 | Rule 515B |
| Officer serves the order | Day 29 | Day 42 | Rule 517 |
| Earliest forcible removal | Day 40 | Day 53 | Rule 519B |

The longest column assumes you file the request on the first day you can. Court backlogs, a waiver of notice in the lease, a tenant appeal or a bankruptcy stay change the count.

## Where Does a Landlord File an Eviction Case in Pennsylvania, and What Does It Cost?

A Pennsylvania landlord files with the magisterial district judge for the district where the property sits. Rule 502 says the action may be brought "in and only in" that district. The complaint goes on a form from the State Court Administrator (Rule 503).

The 2026 costs come from 204 Pa. Code § 29.402:

| Landlord-tenant action | 2026 cost |
|---|---|
| $2,000 or less | $102.50 |
| More than $2,000, up to $4,000 | $125.50 |
| More than $4,000, up to $12,000 | $171.00 |

These costs do not include postage and registered mail. Service by the sheriff or a constable also costs extra. Philadelphia has its own court process, so check local fees there.

## Can a Pennsylvania Landlord Evict a Tenant for a Lease Violation or With No Cause?

Yes, for a lease violation. Section 501(a) lets a landlord give notice on forfeiture of the lease for breach of its conditions. The notice is 15 days for a lease of 1 year or less, or of no fixed term. It is 30 days for a lease of more than 1 year (Section 501(b)).

The Act also allows notice "upon the termination of a term." So outside some cities, a landlord may end a lease at its end date with the same 15 or 30 days of notice. Two limits apply:

- **Tenant groups.** Section 205 bars ending or not renewing a lease because a tenant joins a tenants' association.
- **Philadelphia.** A landlord needs "good cause" to end or not renew a lease of less than 1 year, including month-to-month (Fair Housing Commission). The landlord gives at least 30 days' written notice with the reasons.

## Who Removes the Tenant After a Judgment in Pennsylvania?

The sheriff or a certified constable removes the tenant. Rule 517 says the officer serves the order for possession within 48 hours. Service is by hand to the tenant or an adult in charge, or by posting. The court also mails a copy.

For a residential lease, Rule 519B lets the officer use force on or after the 11th day after service. The officer then gives possession to the landlord. Until then, a tenant evicted only for rent may stop the removal. The tenant pays the rent in arrears plus costs to the officer (Rule 518).

After removal, property left behind follows Section 505.1. The landlord sends a written notice by first class mail. The tenant has 10 days from the postmark to take the items or ask for storage of up to 30 days. A landlord who breaks Section 505.1 faces treble damages, attorney fees and costs.

## Is a Self-Help Eviction Legal in Pennsylvania?

No safe self-help path exists in the Act. The Act and Rule 519 give the power to force entry only to the court officer, after a judgment and an order. Section 505.1(f) also bars a landlord from taking control of property in an inhabited unit without the tenant's express permission.

Philadelphia is explicit. Its Fair Housing Commission lists illegal lockouts and utility shutoffs as unlawful self-help eviction practices. Police there can order the landlord to let the tenant back in.

Mississippi evictions of residential tenants follow the Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, Title 89, Chapter 8 of the Mississippi Code. In 2022, SB 2461 added a full eviction procedure to that chapter (sections 89-8-31 to 89-8-45). In 2025, SB 2328 changed the summons text and the rules for the warrant for removal. Here is the whole process in one table.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice for unpaid rent | 3 days to pay | Miss. Code 89-8-13(5)(a) |
| Notice for other material breach | At least 14 days, with up to 14 days to fix | 89-8-13(3) |
| Same breach again within 6 months | 14 days' notice, no chance to fix required | 89-8-13(3)(b) |
| Holdover after a fixed-term lease ends | Eviction starts no earlier than 3 days after notice | 89-8-17(2) |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | 30 days' written notice | 89-8-19(3) |
| End a week-to-week tenancy | 7 days' written notice | 89-8-19(2) |
| Health or safety violation | No notice to terminate required | 89-8-19(4) |
| What to file | Sworn affidavit or complaint plus a copy of the notice | 89-8-33 |
| One adjournment | 10 days max, unless both sides agree | 89-8-41(2) |
| Whole hearing | Not past 30 days from filing because of adjournments | 89-8-41(3) |
| Judgment | Signed and executed the same business day | 89-8-37(3) |
| Move-out after judgment | 7 days, unless the court sets a shorter or longer time | 89-8-39(1) |
| Tenant access after removal | 72 hours to take belongings | 89-8-39(3) |
| Holdover after a fixed term | Double rent, if the lease does not say otherwise | 89-8-45 |

## What notice must a Mississippi landlord give before filing for unpaid rent?

A Mississippi landlord gives a written notice that the rental agreement will end if the rent is not paid within 3 days. That rule is in Miss. Code 89-8-13(5)(a). The notice can go by email or text only if the tenant agreed in writing to get notices that way.

Section 89-8-13(5)(b) adds one more rule. The judge at the hearing must follow the terms of the rental agreement that both sides signed. So your lease terms on rent, due dates and late fees matter in court. Section 89-8-7 counts late fees owed under the lease as "rent".

## What notice applies to other lease violations?

For a material breach other than unpaid rent, the landlord gives at least 14 days' notice under 89-8-13(3). The notice names the acts or omissions that make up the breach. It also says the agreement ends on a date at least 14 days after the tenant receives it, unless the tenant fixes the breach.

Watch for three details in 89-8-13(3):

1. **Fix in time ends the matter.** If the tenant fixes the breach before the date in the notice, the agreement does not end.
2. **A repeat within 6 months is faster.** If the same breach happens again within 6 months, you may end the agreement on 14 days' notice. The tenant gets no right to fix it, unless the tenant shows due care.
3. **Health and safety cases need no notice.** Section 89-8-19(4) says no notice to terminate is required for a substantial violation that materially affects health or safety.

## Can a Mississippi landlord evict with no cause?

Mississippi lets a landlord end a tenancy that has no fixed term without giving a reason. Section 89-8-19 sets 30 days' written notice for a month-to-month tenancy and 7 days for a week-to-week tenancy. If the tenant stays after the end date, section 89-8-31(b) lets you file for eviction.

For a fixed-term lease, the agreement ends on the date in the lease (89-8-17(1)). After that date, you may give notice that you will start eviction no earlier than 3 days later (89-8-17(2)). Section 89-8-45 also lets you charge double rent for the holdover time, if the lease does not say otherwise.

Section 89-8-17(2) puts one limit on you. A rent increase or an eviction after the lease ends must not have retaliation as its main purpose. Retaliation here means a response to the tenant's acts that the chapter allows, such as a written repair notice.

## Where does a landlord file, and what goes in the file?

A Mississippi landlord files in a justice court, a county court or a circuit court. Section 89-8-7 defines "court" for this chapter as one of those three. HB 1200 (2025) kept that definition.

Section 89-8-33 lists what you file:

- [ ] A sworn affidavit or complaint, based on the rental agreement.
- [ ] The facts that require the tenant's removal.
- [ ] The address of the unit.
- [ ] The rent and any added fees owed, if any.
- [ ] A copy of the written notice you gave under 89-8-13, 89-8-17 or 89-8-19.

We did not find an official statewide fee table for evictions. Costs vary by court. Ask the clerk for the filing cost and the service cost before you file.

## How long does an eviction take in Mississippi?

A Mississippi eviction for unpaid rent runs on a few fixed statute clocks. Service of the summons, the court calendar and the sheriff schedule add time, and they vary by county. Here is a worked timeline for a nonpayment case, with the fixed clocks only.

| Step | Day | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Serve 3-day notice | Day 0 | 89-8-13(5) |
| Rent still unpaid, file affidavit or complaint | Day 4 | 89-8-33 |
| Summons served under the Mississippi Rules of Court | After filing (time varies) | 89-8-35(3) |
| Hearing (adjournments cannot extend it past day 34 here) | Day named in the summons | 89-8-35(1), 89-8-41(3) |
| Judgment signed the same business day | Hearing day | 89-8-37(3) |
| Court-ordered move-out date | Judgment plus 7 days | 89-8-39(1) |
| Request a warrant for removal | Day after move-out date | 89-8-39(2) |
| Tenant access to remove belongings | 72 hours after removal | 89-8-39(3) |

The 30-day limit in 89-8-41(3) counts from the filing date. It caps the effect of adjournments. A single adjournment cannot exceed 10 days unless both sides agree.

If the tenant does not appear, 89-8-37(1) tells the court to grant a default judgment. You must still meet 89-8-33, and service must be proper.

## Can the tenant pay and stay?

Yes, in a case based only on unpaid rent. Under 89-8-39(4), the judge does not issue a warrant for removal if the tenant pays in full all rent and other sums in the judgment by the move-out date. The judge also does not issue one if you accept that payment after that date. Section 89-8-39(4)(b) says you must accept full payment in good faith on or before the move-out date.

The summons text in 89-8-35(2) tells the tenant this right in plain words. So plan for it. A tenant who pays in full by the move-out date keeps the unit.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment?

The sheriff or a constable removes the tenant. After the move-out date, you request a warrant for removal and pay the fees. The judge then issues the warrant to the sheriff or any constable of the county (89-8-39(2)).

SB 2328 (2025) added two rules here. Posting the warrant on the door does not count as execution. Law enforcement must remove all occupants and put you into physical possession. The governor approved SB 2328 on 2025-04-10, and it took effect on passage.

After removal, you give the tenant reasonable access for 72 hours to remove belongings, including a manufactured home (89-8-39(3)). After 72 hours, you may move what is left to the curb, a garbage area or a place you both agree on. If the tenant leaves by the court date and leaves items behind, you may dispose of them without more notice (89-8-39(1)).

## Is a lockout or utility shutoff legal in Mississippi?

Chapter 8 gives the court route described above, ending in removal by the sheriff or a constable. We found no section in Chapter 8 that permits a landlord to change the locks or cut utilities to force a tenant out. Section 89-8-31 says a landlord "may commence eviction proceedings" for a breach or a holdover. Use that court process. If you think about any other step, ask a Mississippi attorney first.

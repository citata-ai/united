Kentucky leaves rent levels to the market, but the notice rules depend on where the unit is. I read KRS chapter 383 on the Kentucky General Assembly site on 2026-10-19. One 2024 change matters: KRS 383.198 now bars local landlord-tenant ordinances that conflict with state law.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| State rent cap | None found | (no statute) |
| Rent increase notice | No separate statute | (none found) |
| End month-to-month, URLTA area | 30 days before the periodic rental date | KRS 383.695(2) |
| End week-to-week, URLTA area | 7 days before the termination date | KRS 383.695(1) |
| Tenancy after a written lease ends, URLTA area | 10 days' notice | KRS 383.695(3) |
| End tenancy at will, non-URLTA area | 1 month's written notice | KRS 383.195 |
| Local landlord-tenant law | URLTA in full only; no conflicting ordinances | KRS 383.500, 383.198 |
| Retaliatory rent increase | Banned in URLTA areas | KRS 383.705 |

## Does Kentucky have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

No. We found no Kentucky statute that caps rent or sets rent control.

Local power is narrow. KRS 383.500 lets cities, counties and urban-county governments adopt the URLTA (KRS 383.505 to 383.705) "in their entirety and without amendment." It adds: "No other ordinance shall be enacted" that relates to the subjects in those sections.

The 2024 General Assembly went further. KRS 383.198, effective March 6, 2024 (2024 Ky. Acts ch. 3), says a local government "shall not adopt or enforce any ordinance relating to landlord or tenant laws that is in conflict with any law of this Commonwealth."

## How many days notice does a Kentucky landlord need to raise the rent?

Kentucky statutes set no specific notice period for a rent increase. We found no such section in chapter 383.

For a month-to-month tenant, a new rent takes effect at a new period. The safe path is to give the same notice that ends the tenancy:

- **URLTA area:** written notice at least 30 days before the periodic rental date named in the notice (KRS 383.695(2)).
- **Non-URLTA area:** 1 month's written notice (KRS 383.195).

Worked example in a URLTA area. Rent is due on the 1st.

| Notice delivered | 30 days later | First rental date after that |
|---|---|---|
| June 1 | July 1 | July 1 |
| June 5 | July 5 | August 1 |
| June 28 | July 28 | August 1 |

Deliver it by a method you can prove, such as hand delivery with a signed copy.

## Can a Kentucky landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

Not unless the lease allows it. KRS 383.565(1) lets the parties put "rent, term of the agreement, and other provisions" in the rental agreement. A fixed-term lease sets the rent for that term.

If you want a mid-term increase, write it in the lease: the amount or formula, and the notice you will give. Otherwise, offer the new rent with the renewal.

What happens at the end of a term matters too. In a URLTA area, a landlord may end a tenancy that began when a written lease ended with 10 days' written notice (KRS 383.695(3)). Outside URLTA areas, KRS 383.160 governs a holdover after a fixed term. For a term under 1 year, the landlord may recover possession without notice if the case starts within 30 days after the term ends.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Kentucky?

The notice to end a month-to-month tenancy in Kentucky is 30 days or 1 month, by area.

| Area | Notice | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| URLTA adopted | Written, 30+ days before the periodic rental date | KRS 383.695(2) |
| URLTA not adopted | 1 month, in writing | KRS 383.195 |

In URLTA areas, the same 30-day rule binds the tenant. Unless the lease fixes a term, a URLTA tenancy is month-to-month (KRS 383.565(3)).

## Do cities in Kentucky add their own rent or notice rules?

Kentucky cities may adopt the URLTA, but they may not change it. KRS 383.500 requires adoption "in their entirety and without amendment," and KRS 383.198 bars conflicting local ordinances.

So a local government can switch on the URLTA notice periods. It cannot write its own rent cap or its own notice periods for the same subjects. Ask your county clerk or city code office whether the URLTA is in force where your unit is.

## Retaliation: when a Kentucky rent increase is not allowed

In URLTA areas, KRS 383.705(1) bars a landlord from "increasing rent or decreasing services" to retaliate after the tenant:

1. complained to a code agency about a health or safety violation,
2. complained to the landlord under KRS 383.595, or
3. organized or joined a tenants' union.

A complaint within 1 year before the increase creates a presumption of retaliation (KRS 383.705(2)). The presumption does not arise if the tenant complained after notice of the rent increase.

## Kentucky rent increase checklist

- Check whether your city or county adopted the URLTA.
- Read the lease. No mid-term increase unless it allows one.
- URLTA area: give written notice 30+ days before a rent date.
- Non-URLTA area: give 1 month's written notice.
- Keep proof of delivery.
- Avoid an increase soon after a protected complaint.

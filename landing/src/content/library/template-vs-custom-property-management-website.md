A template site from Property Manager Websites starts at about $1,795. Its custom site starts at $3,895 and takes 60 to 90 days to build. That price gap is real, but it is not the main question. The main question is whether your site needs more than a template can hold. This page puts published prices side by side and gives you a test.

## What does each option cost?

A template property management website costs about $1,700 to $2,000 to set up from a specialist vendor. A custom build costs about $3,900 to $9,300, and more for multi-location firms. These are the published prices we read on 2026-10-20:

| Vendor | Template or entry build | Custom or top build | Monthly part | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Property Manager Websites | Smartsite, about $1,795 setup (included with Rentvine) | Custom from $3,895 | Plans $145, $795, $1,995 a month | Pricing page and blog |
| Goodjuju Marketing | Builds from $1,686; most pick $2,760 | Up to $9,270; $14,800 multi-location tier | Care plans $126 or $340 a month | Website pricing page |
| Fourandhalf | Launch: $600 setup, no full site build | Grow $1,799 / Scale $2,399 setup | $349, $699, $1,199 a month | Pricing page |
| Squarespace (do-it-yourself) | Basic plan | Not applicable | $12 to $19 a month, by billing term | Pricing page |

Monthly fees often cost more than the build. Fourandhalf's Grow plan is $1,799 setup plus $699 a month. Over 36 months that is $1,799 + $25,164 = $26,963. The build is 7% of it.

### Worked example: 3-year cost

| Line | Template path | Custom path |
|---|---|---|
| Build | $1,795 | $3,895 |
| Care or hosting, $126/mo × 36 | $4,536 | $4,536 |
| Extra pages later, 10 × $150 (example) | $1,500 | $0 (in the 30-page base) |
| 3-year total | $7,831 | $8,431 |

The $150 per page is an example number, not a vendor price. Ask your vendor for its real add-on rate. With many add-on pages, the gap between template and custom gets small.

## Which ranks better, template or custom?

Neither type ranks better by itself. Google's guide to helpful content rewards "helpful, reliable, people-first content," and its Core Web Vitals guide sets the page experience bar:

| Core Web Vital | Good score (Google) |
|---|---|
| Largest Contentful Paint (load) | Within 2.5 seconds |
| Interaction to Next Paint (response) | Under 200 milliseconds |
| Cumulative Layout Shift (stability) | Under 0.1 |

A template built on a fast platform can pass all three. A custom site full of large images and plugins can fail them. Rank then comes from what you put on the pages: city pages with real local facts, a full fee table, reviews and a clean Google Business Profile.

Property Manager Websites says even its template is "built for SEO, conversion, mobile responsiveness." The difference it names is page count: about 15 standard pages for the template, about 30 for custom. Page count matters only if you have real content for each page.

## How much can I change on a template?

On most property management templates, you can change brand, colors, logo, text, photos and optional pages. You usually cannot change the layout or the user flow. Property Manager Websites lists "some customization possible (colors, logo, optional pages)" for its template, and "much greater flexibility in design, pages, layout" for custom.

Before you sign, ask these questions:

- [ ] Can I add city pages myself, and is there a limit?
- [ ] Can I add an FAQ block and schema (code that labels facts for search engines) on any page?
- [ ] Can I publish a full fee table as HTML text, not an image?
- [ ] Does the listing feed pull from my software (AppFolio, Buildium, Rentvine)?
- [ ] Who owns the domain and the content if I leave?
- [ ] Can I export the pages?

If the vendor says no to the fee table or the city pages, the template will limit you more than the price suggests.

## When does a custom site pay off?

A custom site pays off when the template blocks growth that you can measure. Use this test. Custom makes sense if 2 or more are true:

1. **You serve 5+ cities** and need a real page for each, with more pages than the template allows.
2. **You run 2+ service lines** (for example, residential and HOA) that need separate paths for owners.
3. **You need a feature** a template cannot hold: an owner calculator, a portal flow or a multi-office locator.
4. **Your market is crowded,** and your brand must look different from 3 rivals on the same template.
5. **You have content ready.** Custom builds need decisions; Property Manager Websites says a late content approval can stretch the 60 to 90 days.

If only 0 or 1 is true, start with a template. Spend the difference on content and reviews.

One owner signed can cover a build. At $180 a month for one door over 36 months, Goodjuju's own example gives $6,480. Our [fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) shows the math for your fee model.

If you later move from template to custom, keep URLs the same where you can. For any changed URL, Google says to use 301 redirects and keep them "generally at least 1 year." Google also warns that rankings can move for "a few weeks or more" while it recrawls a medium-sized site.

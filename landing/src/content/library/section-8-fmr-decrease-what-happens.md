## How often do Fair Market Rents go down?

Fair Market Rents (FMRs) go down more often than many landlords expect. HUD's FY2027 FMRs took effect on October 1, 2026 (Federal Register, 91 FR 56156). In our table of the 50 largest metro FMR areas, the 2-bedroom FMR fell in 22 of them (HUD User data).

The largest 2-bedroom drops among the 50:

| Metro FMR area | FY2026 | FY2027 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dallas | $1,931 | $1,807 | −6.4% |
| San Jose | $3,483 | $3,272 | −6.1% |
| San Diego | $3,001 | $2,823 | −5.9% |
| Phoenix | $1,839 | $1,734 | −5.7% |
| Milwaukee | $1,338 | $1,263 | −5.6% |
| Orange County, CA | $3,236 | $3,057 | −5.5% |

There is a floor. 24 CFR 888.113(b) says HUD sets FMRs "no lower than 90 percent of the previous year's FMR." So one year's drop is at most 10%. See all 50 metros in [Section 8 rent limits by metro](/guides/section-8-rent-limits).

## Does my contract rent change when the FMR goes down?

Your contract rent does not change when the FMR goes down. The rent to owner is in your lease and HAP contract. The FMR is a HUD number that PHAs use to set payment standards. It does not rewrite your lease.

Three numbers are easy to mix up:

| Number | Who sets it | What a lower FMR does to it |
|---|---|---|
| FMR | HUD, each year | Goes down |
| Payment standard | The PHA, 90% to 110% of FMR (24 CFR 982.503(c)) | May go down |
| Rent to owner | You and the PHA, in the lease | Does not change by itself |

The PHA must keep its payment standards inside the basic range. 24 CFR 982.503(c)(3) says the PHA must revise them no later than 3 months after the new FMR takes effect, if needed to stay within 90% to 110%. A PHA at 100% of the old FMR may already be inside the range after a 6% drop, so it may not need to change at all.

## Can the PHA keep the old payment standard?

The PHA can keep the old payment standard for a family that stays in your unit. 24 CFR 982.505(c)(3) says: "The PHA may choose not to reduce the payment standard amount used to calculate the subsidy for a family for as long as the family continues to reside in the unit." People call this "hold harmless."

If the PHA does choose to cut, the rule sets three limits:

1. **2-year wait.** The first cut may not apply earlier than 2 years after the payment standard decrease takes effect.
2. **12 months' notice.** The PHA must give the family at least 12 months' written notice of the new amount.
3. **Partial cuts allowed.** The PHA may cut to the new schedule amount, or to an amount between the old and new ones.

Each PHA writes its choice in its Administrative Plan (982.505(c)(3)(iv)). Ask your PHA which policy it uses.

## Does the tenant share go up when the payment standard drops?

The tenant share goes up only when the gross rent is above the new payment standard. The PHA pays the lower of (payment standard − Total Tenant Payment) or (gross rent − Total Tenant Payment), under 24 CFR 982.505(b). When the payment standard falls below your gross rent, the HAP falls and the family pays the difference.

A worked example. The Dallas FMR is from HUD. The rest are made-up numbers.

| Line | Before | After a cut (2+ years later) |
|---|---|---|
| Payment standard, 2 bedrooms (100% of FMR) | $1,931 | $1,807 |
| Rent to owner | $1,750 | $1,750 |
| Utility allowance | $150 | $150 |
| Gross rent | $1,900 | $1,900 |
| Total Tenant Payment (30% of adjusted income) | $500 | $500 |
| HAP | $1,400 | $1,307 |
| Tenant rent to owner | $350 | $443 |

You still get $1,750 in total. The split changes: the PHA pays $93 less and the family pays $93 more. The risk for you is that the family cannot pay the higher share, or chooses to move.

## When does a decrease take effect?

A decrease reaches a current tenant's subsidy in stages, if at all:

| Event | Earliest date |
|---|---|
| New FMR takes effect | October 1 each year (FY2027: 2026-10-01) |
| PHA revises payment standards to stay in range | Within 3 months after the FMR takes effect (982.503(c)(3)) |
| PHA notice to family of a cut | At least 12 months before the cut (982.505(c)(3)(iii)) |
| Cut applies to the family's subsidy | At least 2 years after the payment standard decrease (982.505(c)(3)(i)) |
| New tenancy in the unit | Uses the current schedule at once |

The last line matters most. When your current tenant moves out, the next voucher family gets the lower payment standard right away.

## Can a lower FMR trigger a rent review?

A big FMR drop can trigger a new reasonable rent review. 24 CFR 982.507(a)(2)(ii) says the PHA must redetermine the reasonable rent if the FMR for the unit size dropped 10% or more, comparing the FMR 60 days before the contract anniversary with the FMR 1 year earlier. None of the 50 largest metros had a 2-bedroom drop that large for FY2027.

## What can I do when the FMR goes down?

- [ ] **Ask the PHA its policy.** Does it hold current families harmless, and for how long?
- [ ] **Check the new payment standard** for your unit size and ZIP code.
- [ ] **Compare your gross rent** with the new payment standard.
- [ ] **Plan the next lease-up.** A new family gets the lower standard at once.
- [ ] **Do not ask for side payments.** The tenant may not pay more than rent to owner minus HAP (24 CFR 982.451(b)(4)).
- [ ] **Give 60 days' notice** for any rent change, and expect a reasonable rent review (24 CFR 982.308(g)(4)).

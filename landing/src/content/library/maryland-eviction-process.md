Maryland's eviction process has many steps, and each step has its own clock. I read the Real Property Article sections on the Maryland General Assembly site on 2026-10-19. Failure to pay rent cases run under § 8-401. Holdovers run under § 8-402, and breach of lease under § 8-402.1.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice of intent to file (rent) | 10 days to cure; Judiciary form | § 8-401(c) |
| Trial date | 5th day after filing | § 8-401(b)(4) |
| Possession after judgment | Within 4 days (up to 15 with a doctor's note) | § 8-401(e)(3), (4) |
| Warrant of restitution | After 7 days if tenant has not complied | § 8-401(f) |
| Warrant must be ordered | Within 60 days or judgment is stricken | § 8-401(f)(1)(ii) |
| Notice of eviction date | At least 6 days before; mail, post, e-mail or text | § 8-407(b) |
| Right of redemption | Pay all past-due amounts before eviction | § 8-401(h) |
| Breach of lease notice | 30 days (14 for clear and imminent danger) | § 8-402.1(a) |
| End month-to-month | 60 days' written notice | § 8-402(c)(2) |
| Appeal, rent case | Within 4 days | § 8-401(i) |
| Summary ejectment surcharge | Up to $43, paid by landlord | Cts. & Jud. Proc. § 7-301(c) |
| Lockout or utility cutoff | Banned | § 8-216 |

## What notice must a Maryland landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

A Maryland landlord gives a written notice of intent to file that lets the tenant cure "within 10 days after the written notice is provided" (Real Prop. § 8-401(c)(1)).

The notice rules (§ 8-401(c)(2)):

- **Form:** use the form created by the Maryland Judiciary.
- **Delivery:** first-class mail with a certificate of mailing, or posted on the door of the unit.
- **Electronic:** e-mail, text or tenant portal, only if the tenant chose it, with proof of sending.

The complaint must state the date you gave this notice (§ 8-401(c)(3)). A tenant can challenge it, and the court may dismiss.

The complaint also states the rent and "any late fees due and unpaid," the rent for each period, and the due day (§ 8-401(b)(2), (3)). For older properties covered by the lead law, add the registration and inspection details in § 8-401(b)(2)(vi).

## How long does an eviction take in Maryland?

A Maryland eviction for unpaid rent takes about 4 to 5 weeks by the statute clocks, if the sheriff can act soon:

| Step | Days | Running total |
|---|---|---|
| 10-day notice of intent to file | 10 | 10 |
| File; trial on the 5th day after filing | 5 | 15 |
| Order to surrender within 4 days | 4 | 19 |
| Warrant after 7 days without compliance | 7 from judgment | 22 |
| 6 days' notice of the eviction date | 6+ | 28+ |
| Sheriff executes the warrant | Varies by county | 4 to 5 weeks |

The court can adjourn the trial 1 day, or longer if both sides agree (§ 8-401(e)(1)). In extreme weather, the court stays evictions day to day. Examples are 32°F or lower, winter storm warnings and excessive heat warnings (§ 8-401(f)(2)).

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Maryland, and what does it cost?

A Maryland landlord files a written complaint, under oath, "in the District Court of the county wherein the property is situated" (§ 8-401(b)(2)). Breach of lease and holdover cases also go to the District Court (§§ 8-402, 8-402.1).

Costs in the statutes:

- **Filing fee:** set by law, rule or regulation (Cts. & Jud. Proc. § 7-301(c)(1)). Check the District Court fee schedule.
- **Surcharge:** up to $43 per summary ejectment case, plus up to $3 for the land records fund (§ 7-301(c)(2), (4)). Baltimore City adds up to $10 (§ 7-301(c)(3)).
- **Who pays:** the $43 surcharge is assessed against the landlord and may not be passed to the tenant as a cost. A court may let it come out of the deposit after a judgment for possession, if the lease says so (§ 7-301(c)(5)).
- **Eviction notice:** the landlord may charge the tenant up to $5 for the § 8-407 notice (§ 8-407(b)(3)).

## Can a Maryland landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes, with the right notice.

**Breach of lease.** The lease must let the landlord end it for a breach (§ 8-402.1(a)). Then give 30 days' written notice that the tenant is in violation and that you want the unit back. For behavior showing "a clear and imminent danger" of serious harm, the notice is 14 days. The court must find the breach was "substantial and warrants an eviction."

**No cause.** Give written notice to end the tenancy (§ 8-402(c)(2)):

- written lease of more than 1 week, or month-to-month: 60 days;
- year-to-year: 90 days;
- week-to-week: 7 days with a written lease, 21 days without.

A landlord with fewer than 5 units facing a foreclosure notice may use shorter periods in some counties (§ 8-402(c)(4)).

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Maryland?

A sheriff or constable removes the tenant under a warrant of restitution. In a rent case, if the tenant does not comply within 7 days, the court issues the warrant (§ 8-401(f)(1)).

Before the eviction date, the landlord gives the tenant at least 6 days' written notice (§ 8-407(b)). Send it by first-class mail with a certificate of mailing, and post it on the front door with a date-stamped photo. If you have the tenant's e-mail or cell number, also send it by e-mail or text. The notice must contain the items in § 8-407(b)(2), including the case number and the warning text about belongings.

Order the warrant within 60 days of judgment, or the judgment for possession is stricken (§ 8-401(f)(1)(ii)).

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Maryland?

No. Real Prop. § 8-216(b) says a landlord "may not take possession or threaten to take possession" by "locking the tenant out or any other action, including willful diminution of services."

The only lawful ways to take possession:

1. under a warrant of restitution executed by a sheriff or constable, or
2. after the tenant abandons or surrenders the unit.

A tenant who wins may recover actual damages, reasonable attorney fees and costs (§ 8-216(c)). A landlord may still secure an unsecured unit for a short time, for example by changing locks (§ 8-216(d)). Give good-faith notice that the tenant can get back in.

## Maryland eviction checklist

- Send the 10-day notice of intent to file on the Judiciary form.
- Note the date you sent it; it goes in the complaint.
- List rent, late fees and due dates in the complaint.
- Attend trial on the 5th day after filing.
- Order the warrant within 60 days.
- Give 6 days' notice of the eviction date by mail, posting and e-mail or text.
- Never lock out or cut utilities.

## Why do a routine inspection?

A routine inspection finds small problems before they become big ones. A slow leak under a sink costs little to fix this month. After a year, it can mean a new cabinet and subfloor.

Inspections also check safety items, such as smoke alarms, and confirm the tenant follows the lease. Done well, they show the tenant that you care for the home. Done badly, they feel like an intrusion. The steps below keep them fair.

## How much notice do I give?

Give written notice that meets your state's entry rule. States differ, so do not use one number for every rental.

| State | Notice rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| California | Reasonable written notice; 24 hours presumed reasonable; 6 days if mailed; normal business hours | Civil Code 1954 |
| Washington | At least 2 days' written notice for entry; notice states the date and time window and a phone number to object or reschedule | RCW 59.18.150 |
| Florida | Reasonable notice for repairs is at least 24 hours; entry between 7:30 a.m. and 8:00 p.m. | Fla. Stat. 83.53 |

California Civil Code 1954 lists the reasons a landlord may enter. They include necessary or agreed repairs and showings to prospective tenants or buyers. Washington RCW 59.18.150 lets a landlord enter to inspect, with 2 days' notice. Florida 83.53 sets its 24-hour rule for entry to repair.

Read your own state's statute for the exact reasons and times. A lease clause cannot cut the notice below what the law requires in most states.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

**What to put in the notice:** the date, the time window, the purpose ("routine inspection"), who will come, and a phone number to reschedule. Washington requires most of these items by law.

## What do I check room by room?

Check the condition of the home, its safety items and its systems. Use the same checklist every time so you can compare one visit with the next.

**Kitchen**
- [ ] Leaks under the sink. Soft or stained cabinet floor.
- [ ] Stove, oven, hood fan work.
- [ ] Fridge seals intact. No water on the floor.
- [ ] Signs of pests.

**Bathrooms**
- [ ] Leaks at the toilet base, sink and tub.
- [ ] Caulk and grout intact. No soft floor near the tub.
- [ ] Exhaust fan works. No mold on the ceiling.

**Bedrooms and living areas**
- [ ] Smoke alarm present and tested.
- [ ] Windows open, close and lock. Screens in place.
- [ ] Walls, doors and floors: note new damage beyond normal wear.

**Systems**
- [ ] HVAC filter date. Replace if due.
- [ ] Water heater: no rust or water at the base.
- [ ] Electrical panel cover in place. No warm outlets.
- [ ] CO alarm present where required.

**Outside**
- [ ] Gutters and downspouts clear.
- [ ] No standing water near the foundation.
- [ ] Steps and railings firm.

The U.S. Fire Administration says to test smoke alarms monthly and replace each alarm 10 years from its manufacture date. Check the date on each alarm at each inspection.

## Can I take photos of tenant belongings?

Take photos of the unit's condition, not of the tenant's life. Photograph walls, floors, fixtures, appliances, leaks and damage. Do not photograph mail, papers, medicine, photos on the wall or the inside of closets and drawers.

Some practical rules:

- **Tell the tenant** in the notice that you will take condition photos.
- **Frame tight.** Point the camera at the leak, not the whole room.
- **Do not post** inspection photos anywhere. Use them only for repairs and records.
- **Date each photo** and store it with the inspection report.

If a state or local rule limits photos, follow it. When unsure, ask the tenant for consent at the door.

## What do I do with the findings?

Turn each finding into an action within a few days. A finding that sits in a notebook does nothing.

1. **Sort the list.** Put each item in one of three groups: urgent repair, normal repair, tenant issue.
2. **Open repair orders** for urgent and normal items. Schedule urgent ones first.
3. **Send a summary** to the tenant within 3 business days. List what you found and what you will fix and when.
4. **For a lease breach,** such as an unapproved pet, send a separate written notice that follows your state's process.
5. **Store the report** with photos. Compare it with the next inspection.

Keep the tone of the summary calm. "We found a slow leak under the kitchen sink. A plumber will come on Tuesday" works better than a list of faults.

## How often should I inspect?

Many landlords inspect an occupied unit once or twice a year, plus a move-in and move-out inspection. More visits can feel like harassment. California Civil Code 1954 says a landlord may not abuse the right of access or use it to harass the tenant.

A good pattern:

- **Move-in:** full condition report with photos, signed by the tenant.
- **6 months:** routine inspection.
- **Before renewal:** routine inspection, so you can plan any upgrade.
- **Move-out:** full condition report with photos.

Put the inspection right and schedule in the lease, so it is no surprise.

## What you can do next

Copy the checklist above into your notes app or software. Send your next routine notice with the date, time window, purpose and a phone number. If you manage many units and inspections slip, see how [property management](/property-management) can take the task.

A property management owner posts a job for a "marketing coordinator". The plan is simple: one person does the website, SEO, ads, social media, email and reviews. Six months later the person posts on social media every day, and owner leads have not moved. The problem is not the person. It is the job. Five specialist roles do not fit into one hire.

## What does an in-house marketer cost?

An in-house marketer costs the wage plus about 43% more for benefits and payroll costs. The numbers from the Bureau of Labor Statistics:

| Role | Median pay, May 2025 | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Market research analysts and marketing specialists | $78,760 per year | BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook |
| Lowest 10% of that group | less than $43,390 | BLS |
| Highest 10% of that group | more than $155,480 | BLS |
| Marketing managers | $166,790 per year | BLS |

In June 2026, wages were 70.0% of private employer compensation costs, and benefits were the other 30.0% (BLS, Employer Costs for Employee Compensation). So the full cost is the wage divided by 0.70.

Worked example at the median:

| Line | Calculation | Cost per year |
|---|---|---|
| Wage | BLS median | $78,760 |
| Full cost with benefits | $78,760 ÷ 0.70 | about $112,500 |
| Tools (SEO, email, design, call tracking) | your quotes | $ |
| Ad spend | separate | $ |

A junior hire near the lowest 10% costs less, about $62,000 with benefits ($43,390 ÷ 0.70). A junior hire also needs more direction from you.

## What does an agency cost?

An agency costs a monthly fee, usually with no benefits, tools or training on your side. Prices vary by market and scope. On 2026-10-19 Goodjuju, a property management SEO agency, listed these plans (Goodjuju pricing):

| Plan level | Per month | Per year |
|---|---|---|
| Lowest SEO plan | $1,185 | $14,220 |
| Highest standard plan | $4,460 | $53,520 |
| Multi-location plan (by application) | $7,930 | $95,160 |

Goodjuju says its plans run month to month with 15 days' notice. Other agencies price differently, so get 3 quotes with the same scope.

## Which tasks need specialist skills?

Tasks that can break your site or waste ad money need specialist skills. Tasks that need knowledge of your business fit an in-house person.

| Task | Best owner | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Technical SEO, site speed, schema | Agency or specialist | Needs code and tools |
| Site redesign or migration | Agency or specialist | A wrong redirect can lose rankings |
| Google Ads and Meta ads | Agency or specialist | Bid and policy skills; Meta housing rules |
| Analytics and lead tracking setup | Specialist, once | GA4, UTM tags, CRM fields |
| Google Business Profile updates | In-house | Needs your photos, hours, news |
| Review requests and replies | In-house | Needs your client list and voice |
| Owner answer pages (facts) | In-house, edited by agency | Needs local costs, laws, timelines |
| Owner newsletter | In-house | Needs your market numbers |
| Lead follow-up | In-house | Speed and trust; nobody outside can do it |

The last line matters most. In Goodjuju's fall 2026 study, Google Search and the Map Pack produced 50.6% of owner form leads (Goodjuju, 2026). An agency can help you get those leads. Only your team can answer them fast and sign the owner.

## Can a hybrid setup work?

Yes, and for many property managers it is the best setup. One person in-house owns the work that needs your knowledge. An agency or freelancer owns the work that needs tools and code.

A sample hybrid:

| Who | Hours or cost | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| Office manager or leasing lead | 4–6 hours a week | Reviews, Google Business Profile, newsletter, lead follow-up |
| Agency or freelancer | Monthly plan | Technical SEO, content editing, ads, monthly report |
| Owner of the company | 1 hour a week | Approves pages, checks lead report, signs owners |

The in-house hours are our planning estimate, not a study result.

Write one rule into any agency contract: the report must show owner leads by source and signed doors, not only traffic and rankings. Ask how they will report AI answers too. ## At what door count does each make sense?

No study sets a door count for this choice. Use the cost math instead. A hire makes sense when the extra doors it brings pay for it.

Break-even example. Say one door brings $150 a month in fees, or $1,800 a year:

| Option | Cost per year | Doors needed to cover cost (at $1,800 per door per year) |
|---|---|---|
| DIY by the owner | Your time | 0 extra |
| Lowest agency plan | $14,220 | about 8 |
| Highest standard agency plan | $53,520 | about 30 |
| Full-time marketer at median | about $112,500 | about 63 |
| Hybrid: part-time staff + agency | $14,220 + staff hours | about 8 + staff cost |

These are doors kept for a full year, not doors signed once. Put your own fee into the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

A practical guide by size, as a planning rule:

- **Under 150 doors.** Owner plus a small agency plan or freelancer. Keep reviews and follow-up in-house.
- **150 to 500 doors.** Hybrid. One staff member part-time on marketing, an agency for SEO and ads.
- **500+ doors or several cities.** A full-time marketer can pay off, often with an agency for technical work.

## Questions to ask before you choose

1. Who will reply to an owner lead within one business day?
2. Who knows your local fees, laws and turn costs well enough to write about them?
3. Who can fix the site if it breaks?
4. Who will measure owner leads by source each month?
5. What will you stop doing if the numbers do not move in 6 months?

Answer these first. The right setup follows from the answers.

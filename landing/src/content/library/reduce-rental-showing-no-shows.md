## Why do renters miss showings?

Renters miss showings for 3 main reasons: the slot is too far away, the time does not fit their day, or they were never a real fit for the unit. Each reason has a fix you control.

- **Long lead time.** A renter who books on Monday for Thursday keeps looking. By Thursday, they may have found another place. RentEngine's Q1 2026 data puts the median lead time at 15.2 hours for self-guided showings and 60.7 hours for accompanied ones.
- **Hours that clash with work.** In the same RentEngine data, 52.8% of prospects chose a showing time outside normal business hours.
- **Poor fit.** A renter who learns at the door that the pet policy or income rule excludes them should have learned it on the phone.
- **Real life.** Bay Property Management Group names traffic, car trouble and medical emergencies. You cannot fix those, but a reminder text makes it easier to cancel than to vanish.

## What no-show rate is normal?

No independent study sets a normal no-show rate for rental showings. The numbers you find come from vendors, and each one measures something different:

| Source | What it measures | Number | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| LetHub | Scheduled tours that do not happen | 20 to 22% | No study, sample or method named |
| Showdigs (2026) | Scheduled tours that are completed | 55 to 60% | Own platform data, method not described |
| RentEngine (Q1 2026) | New leads who complete a self-guided showing | 28% | Measured from lead, not from booking |
| RentEngine (Q1 2026) | New leads who complete an accompanied showing | 14% | Measured from lead, not from booking |

LetHub also notes that a "40% no-show rate" circulates in property management talk with no traceable source. Treat any single number with care.

The useful benchmark is your own. Count showings booked and showings completed for 30 days. Here is the math for one month:

1. **Booked:** 40 showings.
2. **Completed:** 28 showings.
3. **No-shows:** 40 minus 28 equals 12.
4. **No-show rate:** 12 divided by 40 equals 30%.

Run the same count after each change below. Then you know which change worked.

## Do confirmation texts help?

Confirmation texts help, and they cost almost nothing. Tenant Turner writes that Buildium "has found that sending text message confirmations reduces no-shows by 75 percent." Neither page shows the study, so treat 75% as a vendor claim, not a benchmark.

A simple reminder plan:

1. **At booking.** Send the address, date, time, parking notes and a cancel or rebook link.
2. **The day before.** Ask the renter to reply "Y" to confirm.
3. **1 hour before.** Send a short "See you at 5:30" text with the address.
4. **No reply to the day-before text.** Call once. If there is still no reply, offer the slot to the next renter.

Get consent to text in your inquiry form. Keep each message short and plain.

## Should I pre-screen before a showing?

Yes, pre-screen before a showing, with the same short written criteria for every prospect. Pre-screening removes renters who would learn at the door that the unit does not fit. That is a no-show you prevent before it happens.

Ask 4 or 5 questions, the same ones each time:

- [ ] What move-in date do you want?
- [ ] Do you meet the posted income rule?
- [ ] Do you have pets? (State your pet policy.)
- [ ] How many people will live in the unit?
- [ ] Have you read the posted rent, deposit and fees?

Keep the questions tied to your written rental criteria, and apply them to everyone in the same way. The Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. 3604(c), bars any notice, statement or ad that shows a preference or limitation by a protected class. Section 3604(d) bars telling someone a dwelling "is not available for inspection" when it is, because of a protected class.

Do not ask about protected classes, and do not steer. If a renter needs a reasonable accommodation, for example an assistance animal under a no-pet rule, note it and follow your accommodation process.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Do group showings fix no-shows?

Group showings, often called open houses, do not fix no-shows. They make no-shows cheaper. When 6 renters book a 30-minute window and 2 do not come, you still show the unit to 4. One drive covers them all.

Compare the 3 main showing formats:

| Format | No-show cost to you | Best for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Private accompanied showing | High: a wasted trip | Few leads, high-touch units | Long lead times |
| Group showing (open house) | Low: one trip, many renters | Many leads, popular units | Renters who want privacy |
| Self-guided showing (lockbox or smart lock) | Very low: no agent on site | Scattered-site homes, after-hours demand | Identity checks and scam risk |

RentEngine's Q1 2026 data favors self-guided showings: they scheduled at 49.1% of new leads against 37.2% for accompanied showings. Once completed, though, accompanied showings converted to applications at a higher rate. A mix often works: self-guided for reach, an agent for a renter who is close to applying.

## Should I chase a renter who missed a showing?

Send one short message with a rebooking link, then move on. RentEngine's Q1 2026 data says only about 15% of prospects who miss a showing ever complete one at that property. Your time is better spent on the next new lead.

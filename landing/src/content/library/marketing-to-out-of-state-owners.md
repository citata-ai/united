An owner in Denver inherits a house in Ohio. The owner has never been to the street and will never meet the manager in person before she signs. Every decision happens on a screen: a search, a few reviews, a website, maybe an AI answer, then a call.

That is the out-of-state owner. Your marketing has to work without a handshake.

## How do out-of-state owners search for a manager?

Out-of-state owners search with the property's city name, because "near me" gives them managers near their own home. They type "property management [city]" or ask an AI tool "best property manager in [city] for a single-family home".

What they check, in order:

1. **Google results and the Map Pack.** Goodjuju studied 35 PM companies in fall 2026. Google Search and the Map Pack produced 50.6% of owner form leads (Goodjuju, 2026).
2. **Reviews.** BrightLocal's 2026 survey found that 47% of consumers will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews (BrightLocal, 2026).
3. **AI answers.** AI tools brought 3.4% of owner leads in the same study. AI visitors converted at 3.9%, the highest rate of any channel.
4. **Your website.** Fees, services, reports and a fast way to talk.

Three page types serve them well:

| Page | What it holds |
|---|---|
| City page for each service city | Rents, days on market, local rules, your team in that city |
| "Out-of-state owners" page | How you manage remotely: reports, portal, inspections, payout dates |
| Fee page | Every fee in one table |

Our [AI search checklist](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website) covers how to make these pages easy for AI tools to quote.

## How do I target ads to owners outside my market?

Target ads to out-of-state owners by their interest in your city, not by where they live. Each ad platform handles this in a different way.

**Google Ads.** Google's location setting "Presence or interest" reaches people in your target area and people who have shown interest in it. Google calls it a best practice for Search campaigns. "Presence" limits ads to people physically in the area (Google Ads Help). For out-of-state owners, use Presence or interest with keywords that name your city: "property management [city]", "[city] rental management company".

**Meta (Facebook and Instagram).** Meta's housing rules limit targeting. For US housing campaigns, ZIP code targeting and location exclusions are not available, and city targets include a 15-mile radius (Meta Business Help Center). You cannot pick "owners who live in another state". Use retargeting of people who visited your out-of-state owner page instead.

| Channel | Setting | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Google Search Ads | Presence or interest; city-name keywords | "property manager Columbus Ohio" |
| Google retargeting | Visitors to your owner pages | Show the remote reports page |
| Meta retargeting | Visitors to your owner pages | Short video: how you inspect a home |
| Mail | Absentee owners with out-of-state addresses | Letter with fee table and portal screenshot |

## How do I find out-of-state owners to mail?

Find out-of-state owners in your county's parcel data. Many counties publish a property address and an owner mailing address for each parcel. Cook County's assessor, for example, publishes both in its "Parcel Addresses" dataset, used to mail assessment notices (Data.gov, Cook County Assessor).

Steps:

1. Download residential parcels for your service area.
2. Keep rows where the mailing address state is not your state.
3. Group by owner name to find owners with 2 or more homes.
4. Remove recent sales and anyone who asked you to stop.
5. Mail a letter, not a postcard. Remote owners read letters about their asset.

Cook County notes that owner mailing addresses are only intermittently updated in its source data. Clean the list before each mailing.

## What proof do remote owners want?

Remote owners want proof they can check from far away. A local owner can drive past the home. A remote owner needs your reports to do that.

The proof that matters most:

| Proof | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Sample inspection report with dated photos | Shows what they will see after each visit |
| Owner portal screenshot or demo login | Shows that statements and invoices are live |
| Sample monthly statement | Shows income, costs and payout date |
| Reviews from other remote owners | Shows you serve people like them |
| A named owner contact with a direct line | Shows who answers when something breaks |
| Written reply time, such as "within one business day" | Sets a promise they can test |

Put the sample report and statement on the out-of-state owner page. Do not hide them behind a form.

## Which reports and portals reassure them?

Reports that reassure remote owners are regular, dated and easy to read on a phone. Set a schedule and keep it:

- **Monthly owner statement** by a fixed day, with a payout date.
- **Move-in and move-out inspections** with dated photos of every room.
- **Routine inspection** at a set interval, with photos and notes.
- **Maintenance log** with each work order, the invoice and the approval.
- **Year-end statement** with income and expenses for their tax preparer.
- **Portal access** so they can check any of these at 10 p.m. their time.

Time zones matter. If the owner lives 3 hours behind you, an email at 9 a.m. your time reaches them at 6 a.m. Note their time zone in your CRM.

## A checklist for out-of-state owners

- City-named page for each service city, with local numbers.
- One page for out-of-state owners, with sample reports.
- Google Ads with Presence or interest and city-name keywords.
- Mail to absentee owners from county data, cleaned each quarter.
- Named contact and a written reply time.
- Owner's time zone noted on the account.

Price your offer before you send it. The [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) shows what your fees mean for a remote owner's monthly net.

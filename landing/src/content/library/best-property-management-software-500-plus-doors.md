## When do you need Yardi, Entrata, or RealPage?

Evaluate an enterprise suite when the work requires it, rather than when you cross a unit threshold. Complex ownership, reporting, property types, and staff controls can matter more than doors.

Yardi presents Voyager as a broad property platform. Entrata presents integrated rental operations. RealPage offers a range of multifamily products. Compare the specific modules each proposes.

Write down the requirement your current system cannot meet. It might involve entity reporting, approval controls, leasing scale, or specialist housing work. Ask the vendor to show that requirement with sample records.

Do not treat an enterprise brand as proof of easier operations. More modules can mean more setup and support work. Include those tasks in the decision.

Keep the accounting lead and service lead involved. A platform that solves central reporting while making field work harder may need another workflow or integration.

## Which mid-market tools scale past 500 doors?

AppFolio, Buildium, and Rentvine are reasonable candidates to test for a larger management company. Their public offers include accounting, operations, and integration tools. This does not prove a fit for every portfolio.

AppFolio separates capabilities by plan. Buildium lists an Open API in Premium. Rentvine lists open API access within its offer. Match each tier to your actual needs.

Use a larger sample than a single clean property. Include different owners, vacant units, credits, reserves, open repairs, and returned payments. Ask staff to complete normal work without sales help.

Check role limits and bulk work. The right system should keep staff from repeating the same task property by property when a supported batch process is appropriate.

Also check error correction at scale. A bulk update needs a review path before and after execution. Ask how staff identify affected records and reverse mistakes.

## How does enterprise pricing work?

Enterprise pricing needs a written module and service scope. Public product pages do not establish a universal per-door bill.

Ask each vendor to quote the same units, properties, entities, users, and modules. Include data conversion, training, payments, screening, documents, storage, support, and integrations.

Separate required costs from optional ones. Name the service owner for each integration. A connection can require payment to both the PMS and the outside vendor.

Request a view of ongoing cost after setup and temporary discounts. Check minimums, growth pricing, renewal terms, and exit charges. A low starting rate may not be your final contract rate.

Use your own expected volumes for payment and application charges. Label those volumes as planning assumptions. Compare a normal operating period with a busy leasing period.

## Which tools have an open API?

API access differs by plan, permission, endpoint, and partner program. An API is a way for software systems to exchange data. The word open does not mean unlimited access.

AppFolio's pricing page distinguishes read-only and read/write access across higher tiers. Buildium lists Open API in Premium. Rentvine advertises an open API in its offer.

Entrata describes API partner tiers, documentation, and test environments. RealPage has a developer API catalog. Ask what your own company and chosen vendors can actually access.

Test the needed record and action. Reading units differs from writing payments or changing leases. Confirm audit history, error handling, and permission limits for each action.

Ask about rate limits, partner approval, fees, and export rights. Do not sign based on the API label before your technical team reviews the supported workflow.

## How do you plan a migration at this size?

Plan migration around clean balances, stable identifiers, and a controlled changeover. Assign business owners to accounting, leases, repairs, files, and tenant access.

Begin with a source inventory. Decide which history must move and which records can remain in a read-only archive. Get written export instructions before ending the old contract.

Run a sample conversion and reconcile the result. Check tenant balances, deposits, owner funds, bank cash, and open work. Have a separate reviewer verify the accounting totals.

Rehearse payment setup and tenant invitations without triggering real withdrawals. Avoid overlapping automatic billing in old and new systems. Define who handles each exception on launch day.

Keep a fallback plan and a clear point for the final decision. Expand only after staff can operate and correct records. For a narrower comparison, use the live [AppFolio vs Buildium guide](/guides/appfolio-vs-buildium).

## How should you test the choice before you pay?

Test the full workflow with sample records before you move live data. Use the same case in each product. Ask the person who will do the daily work to run the test. A sales demo alone does not show where your team will need help.

Keep a decision sheet with the task, result, missing step, and person who checked it. Mark each result as shown, promised, or still open. Ask for written terms when a feature needs an upgrade or another service. Include staff setup time in your cost review.

Check the exit path while you still control the choice. Export the sample records and open them outside the platform. Confirm that notes, files, dates, and links remain useful. Ask how you will get records after you close the account.

Choose a tool when the test proves a clear gain for your workflow. Keep your current process until the new process passes. Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to see how software costs affect your service margin.

Oklahoma deposit law fits in one statute: 41 O.S. §115, part of the Oklahoma Residential Landlord and Tenant Act. I read the full Title 41 text on the Oklahoma Legislature site on 2026-10-19. The table below holds every rule in this guide.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | No statewide cap | 41 O.S. §115 (none set) |
| Where to hold it | Escrow account, federally insured institution, in Oklahoma | 41 O.S. §115(A) |
| Interest to tenant | None, balance returned "without interest" | 41 O.S. §115(B) |
| Allowed deductions | Accrued rent and damages from tenant noncompliance | 41 O.S. §115(B) |
| Itemized statement | Required, by return-receipt mail or in person | 41 O.S. §115(B) |

## What Is the Maximum Security Deposit a Landlord Can Charge in Oklahoma?

Oklahoma sets no maximum security deposit. 41 O.S. §115 says how you hold, use and return the money. It does not cap the amount.

Title 41 defines a "deposit" as money or property you take as security (41 O.S. §102). You return it when the rental agreement ends, less allowed deductions. A pet deposit or a cleaning deposit fits that definition if you plan to return what is left.

Watch for: an assistance animal is not a pet. 41 O.S. §113.2 points to the federal Fair Housing Act for reasonable accommodation requests. Do not charge a pet deposit for an approved assistance animal.

## How Many Days Does an Oklahoma Landlord Have to Return a Security Deposit?

An Oklahoma landlord has 45 days to return the deposit balance. But the clock starts only after 3 events, all in 41 O.S. §115(B):

1. **The tenancy ends.** By lease expiry, notice or court order.
2. **The tenant gives back possession.** Keys returned, unit empty.
3. **The tenant makes a written demand.** A letter, email or form that asks for the deposit.

The written demand step is unusual. Many states start the clock at move-out. In Oklahoma, a tenant who never asks in writing does not start the 45 days.

The demand also has its own limit. If the tenant makes no written demand within 6 months after the tenancy ends, the deposit reverts to the landlord (41 O.S. §115(B)). The statute says this covers the costs of keeping the escrow account.

Here is a worked timeline for one move-out:

| Date | Event | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-06-30 | Lease ends, tenant returns keys | Tenancy over, possession delivered |
| 2026-07-10 | Tenant emails a written demand with a forwarding address | 45-day clock starts |
| 2026-08-24 | Day 45 | Last day to deliver the balance and the itemized statement |
| 2026-12-30 | 6 months after the tenancy ended | Last day a tenant could have made a first demand |

What to do: ask each tenant for a forwarding address and a written deposit request at move-out. A signed move-out form can do both. Then your 45-day date is clear from day 1.

## What Can a Landlord Deduct From a Security Deposit in Oklahoma?

An Oklahoma landlord may deduct accrued rent and the damages caused by the tenant's noncompliance with the Act and the rental agreement (41 O.S. §115(B)). The statute also mentions "other legally allowable charges" under the Act or the lease.

The tenant duties in 41 O.S. §127 show what noncompliance looks like. Damages from these breaches are fair deductions:

- Damage the tenant, a guest or a pet caused on purpose or by negligence (§127, item 5).
- Garbage and waste the tenant left behind (§127, item 2).
- Damage from unsafe or destructive use of plumbing, wiring, heating or appliances (§127, item 4).
- Unpaid rent up to the end of the tenancy.

Title 41 does not use the phrase "normal wear and tear" in §115. But the deduction must link to a breach by the tenant. Paint that faded over 5 years is not a breach. A hole in a wall is.

Keep proof for each line: move-in and move-out photos with dates, invoices and receipts. A judge looks at that proof if the tenant sues.

## Does an Oklahoma Landlord Have to Give an Itemized List of Deductions?

Yes. An Oklahoma landlord must itemize the rent and damages kept in a written statement (41 O.S. §115(B)). Deliver it in one of 2 ways:

- By mail with return receipt requested, signed for by a person of statutory service age at the address.
- In person, if you can reasonably find the tenant.

Use this checklist for each statement:

- [ ] Tenant name, unit address and tenancy end date.
- [ ] Deposit amount you held.
- [ ] Each deduction on its own line, with the reason and the cost.
- [ ] Copies of invoices or repair estimates.
- [ ] Balance due to the tenant, with the check or transfer.
- [ ] Return-receipt card or a signed hand-delivery record kept in the file.

A worked example: you hold $1,200. The tenant owes $300 of rent for the last month. A carpet burn costs $180 to patch. The statement lists $300 rent and $180 repair, total $480. You return $720 within 45 days of the written demand.

## Must an Oklahoma Landlord Pay Interest or Keep the Deposit in a Separate Account?

Oklahoma requires a separate account but no interest. The deposit "must be kept in an escrow account for the tenant" at a federally insured financial institution in Oklahoma (41 O.S. §115(A)). An out-of-state bank does not meet the rule.

You return the balance "without interest" (41 O.S. §115(B)). The statute does not tell you to give the tenant any interest the account earns.

When you sell the building, the deposits move with it. Within a reasonable time, you must transfer them to the buyer or return them to the tenants (41 O.S. §115(C)). If you transfer them, tell each tenant the buyer's name and address in writing. The buyer then holds all the duties of a landlord for those deposits (§115(D)).

If you manage for owners, put the escrow rule in your management agreement. Name the bank and the account owner.

## What Is the Penalty for an Oklahoma Landlord Who Keeps a Deposit Wrongly?

An Oklahoma landlord who breaks §115 faces civil and criminal risk.

| Violation | Result | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Fail to comply with §115, or fail to return prepaid rent | Tenant may recover the deposit and prepaid rent | 41 O.S. §115(E) |
| Misappropriate the escrowed deposit | Up to 6 months in county jail and a fine up to 2x the amount | 41 O.S. §115(A) |
| Other harm under the Act | Either side may still sue for other damages | 41 O.S. §115(G) |

Title 41 has no double or triple damages rule for a late deposit. The main civil risk is the return of the full deposit. The criminal rule targets misuse of the escrow money, such as spending it on operations.

Watch for: a lease clause cannot remove these rights. 41 O.S. §113 makes a clause that waives Act rights unenforceable. It also bars a clause that makes one side pay the other's attorney fees.

## A Move-Out Checklist for Oklahoma Landlords

Use these steps for each move-out:

1. **Confirm the escrow account.** Deposit sits in a federally insured Oklahoma institution.
2. **Inspect with photos.** Compare to the move-in record.
3. **Ask for a written demand and an address.** Note the date you get it.
4. **Price each repair.** Get invoices for breaches only.
5. **Send the itemized statement.** Return-receipt mail or in person.
6. **Pay the balance by day 45.** Count from the written demand date.
7. **Close the file at 6 months.** No demand means the deposit reverts to you.

## Which license must a Seattle property manager hold, and how do I look it up?

A Seattle property manager who works for other owners for pay needs a Washington real estate license. RCW 18.85.011 lists "performing property management services" as a real estate brokerage service. That includes marketing, leasing and renting. RCW 18.85.331 makes it unlawful to act as a real estate broker, managing broker or firm without a license.

How to check, step by step:

1. **Ask for the license numbers** of the person and of the firm.
2. **Open the DOL License Lookup.** The Department of Licensing says you can search by name, license number, license type or location.
3. **Check three facts:** that DOL issued the license, that it is active, and when it expires.
4. **Search the firm too,** not only the person.

Watch for: a location search can miss licenses that do not list a street address. Search by name or number instead.

## Which local rules must a Seattle manager know?

A Seattle manager must know four city rules that most other cities do not have. Each one has a deadline or a format that can void your action if it is wrong.

| Rule | What it requires | Source |
|---|---|---|
| RRIO registration | Register each rental property. $126 per property including the first unit, plus $31.50 per extra unit. Good for 2 years. Post the certificate in a common area. | SDCI |
| RRIO inspection | Selected once in the first five years after registration, with a 10% chance of a second pick in the next five. At least 60 days' notice. Multi-unit owners can inspect all units or a 20% sample. | SDCI |
| First-in-Time | Post screening criteria first. Date and time stamp applications, screen them in order, give 72 hours for missing information and 48 hours to accept an offer. | Renting in Seattle |
| Housing cost increases | At least 180 days' written notice. No increase in the first 12 months. Since 2025-05-07, a cap of 7% plus CPI, up to 10% a year, unless exempt. | Renting in Seattle |

Worked examples:

- **RRIO fee:** a 4-unit building pays $126 + 3 x $31.50 = $220.50 for 2 years. SDCI's own examples: $126 for a single-family home, $409.50 for 10 units.
- **City inspector:** if you hire a City inspector, SDCI lists $241.50 per property including the first unit, plus $52.50 per extra unit.
- **Rent notice:** for a June 1 increase, the tenant must receive notice by December 2, per Renting in Seattle.
- **Cap:** at the HUD FY2027 2-bedroom fair market rent of $2,549 for the Seattle-Bellevue area, a 10% maximum increase is $254.90. See the [Seattle fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/seattle-fair-market-rent-2027).

Increases of 10% or more in 12 months may also trigger Economic Displacement Relocation Assistance (EDRA), Renting in Seattle notes. Ask the manager how they check this.

The city also says a notice must follow the state format and include city contact language. A notice without it is unenforceable, and you may have to start the 180 days again.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What should a Seattle management agreement say?

A Seattle management agreement should assign each city task to a named party. Use this checklist.

- [ ] **Fees:** monthly fee, leasing fee, renewal fee and maintenance markup.
- [ ] **RRIO:** who registers, renews every 2 years, posts the certificate and schedules inspections.
- [ ] **Screening:** written First-in-Time criteria and who keeps the time-stamped records.
- [ ] **Rent increases:** who drafts the 180-day notice in the required format and checks the cap.
- [ ] **EDRA:** who checks whether an increase triggers relocation assistance.
- [ ] **Repair limit:** what the manager can spend without your approval.
- [ ] **Deposits:** where they sit and who returns them.
- [ ] **Term and cancellation:** notice period and any fee to leave.
- [ ] **Reports:** monthly statements and a year-end 1099.

## Which questions should I ask a Seattle manager before I sign?

Ask questions that test Seattle knowledge. Good answers name the rule and the number.

1. **What are your license numbers, for you and your firm?**
2. **When does my RRIO registration expire, and who renews it?**
3. **How do you prepare for an RRIO inspection?**
4. **Show me your First-in-Time screening criteria.**
5. **How do you time-stamp and store applications?**
6. **How far ahead do you send rent increase notices?** Expect "at least 180 days."
7. **How do you check the 7% plus CPI cap and the EDRA trigger?**
8. **What does your monthly fee leave out?** Get the full fee schedule.
9. **How many doors does each manager handle?**
10. **Can I call 2 owners you manage for now?**

## Where do Seattle owners find manager reviews?

Seattle owners can check a manager in three places. Each has a gap, so use all three.

| Source | What it tells you | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| DOL License Lookup | License status and expiry | Not service quality |
| Google Business Profile and Yelp | Owner and tenant reviews | Tenant complaints often dominate. Read the manager's replies. |
| Owner references | Results on homes like yours | The manager picks them. Ask for owners in Seattle city limits, not only suburbs. |

The last point matters. Bellevue and other nearby cities do not use Seattle's rules, so a manager who mostly works outside Seattle may not know them well.

What to do: shortlist 3 managers, run the DOL check, ask the 10 questions, and compare the agreements against the checklist above.

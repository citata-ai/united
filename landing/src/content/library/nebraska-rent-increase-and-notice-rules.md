## Does Nebraska have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

Nebraska state law, in the sections we read, has no cap on rent increases. Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1414(1) lets the landlord and tenant include rent, the term and other terms in the rental agreement. If they agree on no rent, 76-1414(2) says the tenant pays the fair rental value.

We did not find a rent cap in the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act sections we read. We did not check every statute in Nebraska law. So check your city code and any subsidy contract before a large increase.

## How many days notice does a Nebraska landlord need to raise the rent?

The Nebraska Act sections we read have no separate rule for a rent-increase notice. The notice rule that does exist is for ending a periodic tenancy. Under 76-1437(2), either side ends a month-to-month tenancy with written notice at least 30 days before the periodic rental date in the notice.

A safe method for a month-to-month tenant is to give the same 30 days' written notice. The tenant then chooses to pay the new rent or to move out at the end of the period. Here is a worked example for a new rent that starts on the 1st.

| Step | Example |
|---|---|
| New rent starts | February 1, 2027 |
| Count back 30 days | January 2, 2027 |
| Latest safe delivery date | January 2, 2027 |
| Safer delivery date (room for mail) | December 26, 2026 or earlier |
| Old rent $1,150, new rent $1,210 | Increase $60, or 5.2% |

What to put in a rent-increase notice:

- [ ] Tenant name and unit address
- [ ] Current rent and new rent, in dollars
- [ ] The date the new rent starts, on a periodic rental date
- [ ] Date and method of delivery, with a copy for your file

Watch for: under 76-1414(3), rent is due at the start of each month unless you agree otherwise. Rent is also apportioned day to day unless agreed. A mid-month start date makes the math harder, so start the new rent on the 1st.

## Can a Nebraska landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

A Nebraska landlord sets rent through the lease, so the lease controls during a fixed term. Rent is a term of the rental agreement under 76-1414(1). If the lease fixes the rent for the whole term and has no increase clause, plan the new rent for the renewal.

If you want room to raise rent mid-lease, write the clause into the lease before the tenant signs. Name the date and how you will give notice.

For Section 8 tenants, federal rules add 2 limits:

1. **No increase in the first term.** 24 CFR 982.309(a)(3) says the owner may not raise the rent to owner during the initial lease term.
2. **60 days to the PHA.** 24 CFR 982.308(g)(4) says the owner must notify the housing authority at least 60 days before a change in the rent to owner. The change must also pass rent reasonableness.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Nebraska?

A Nebraska month-to-month tenancy ends with written notice at least 30 days before the periodic rental date in the notice (76-1437(2)). The rule applies to both landlord and tenant. A week-to-week tenancy ends with written notice at least 7 days before the end date (76-1437(1)).

Which tenancies are month-to-month? Under 76-1414(4), a tenancy with no definite term is week-to-week for a roomer who pays weekly rent. In all other cases, it is month-to-month.

If the tenant stays after the end date without your consent, 76-1437(3) lets you bring an action for possession. If the holdover is willful and not in good faith, you may also recover up to 3 months' periodic rent or 3 times actual damages, whichever is greater, plus attorney's fees. If you consent to the tenant staying, 76-1414(4) applies again and a new periodic tenancy runs.

Watch for: 76-1433 says that if you accept rent and you know of a default, you waive the right to end the agreement for that breach, unless you both agree otherwise after the breach. Take care with rent you accept after your notice date.

## Can a Nebraska landlord raise rent after a tenant complaint?

A Nebraska landlord may not raise rent to retaliate. Under 76-1439(1), a landlord may not retaliate by raising rent, cutting services, or bringing or threatening an action for possession after the tenant:

- complained to a government agency about a building or housing code violation that materially affects health and safety; or
- organized or joined a tenants' union or similar group.

If the landlord breaks this rule, the tenant gets the remedies in 76-1430 and a defense to an action for possession. The same section says it does not bar reasonable rent increases. Keep a written record of why and how you set each new rent, for example a market comparison.

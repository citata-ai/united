Most owners read a management agreement once, at signing, under time pressure. Then they read it again a year later, when something goes wrong. This checklist moves the second read to the first day.

We wrote it for owners of 1 to 10 rentals. Each section gives the clause to find, what a fair version looks like, and the red flags.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What Term and Cancellation Clauses Are Fair?

A fair term lets you leave a bad fit without a large penalty. Find the "term", "renewal" and "termination" sections.

| Item | Owner-friendly version | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Initial term | 12 months or month-to-month | Multi-year term with no exit |
| Renewal | Renews only with notice, or month-to-month after year 1 | Automatic renewal for another full year |
| Notice to end | 30 days, in writing, either side | 90 days or more, or only at the end of the term |
| Exit fee | Flat and stated, or none | A share of all fees left in the term |
| End for cause | No fee if the manager breaks the agreement | Same fee with or without cause |
| Notice method | Email allowed | Certified mail only, to a far address |

Ask the manager to explain the exit fee with a number. For example: "If I cancel in month 7, what do I pay?" Write the answer in the agreement.

## Who Controls Repair Spending Limits?

You should. The agreement should set a dollar limit for each repair that the manager can approve alone. Above it, the manager needs your written OK, except in an emergency.

Check these points:

- **Per-repair limit.** A clear dollar amount. Without one, the manager can spend any amount.
- **Emergency definition.** Name the cases: no heat, no water, gas leak, flooding, unsafe conditions.
- **Bids.** Require 2 or 3 bids for jobs above a second, higher limit.
- **Markups.** Ask if the manager adds a fee on vendor invoices, or uses its own staff at set rates.
- **Reserve.** The amount the manager holds back from your payout for repairs.
- **Invoices.** Require copies of invoices with each statement.

Here is an example of a clear clause with invented numbers: "Manager may approve repairs up to $400 per job without Owner approval. Jobs above $400 need Owner's written approval, except emergencies. Jobs above $2,500 need 2 bids. Manager will keep a $500 reserve."

## Who Owns the Lease and Tenant After Cancellation?

You own the rental and the lease. The tenant's lease is with the owner, and the manager signs as your agent. When the agreement ends, the lease stays in force.

The money around the tenant is a separate question. Look for these clauses:

| Clause | What to check |
|---|---|
| Leasing fee | Is it earned at signing, or spread over the lease? Is any part refunded if you cancel? |
| Fee after termination | Can the manager charge you for a tenant it placed, after you leave? For how long? |
| Renewal fee | Who earns the renewal fee if the tenant renews after you cancel? |
| Handover duties | Must the manager deliver leases, ledgers, keys and deposits by the end date? |
| Deposit transfer | Does it follow your state's rule? Florida 83.49(7) requires deposits to move to a new agent with an accounting. |

A fair agreement lists the handover items and a deadline, such as 10 business days after the end date. That one clause makes a later switch much easier.

## How Are Trust Account Funds and Reserves Held?

Your rent and your tenant's deposit should sit in accounts that the manager can't use for its own costs. State law sets many of the rules.

| State | Deposit rule | Law |
|---|---|---|
| Florida | Hold deposits in a separate account in a Florida bank, without mixing with other funds, or post a surety bond | Statutes 83.49(1) |
| Washington | Deposit funds in a trust account in a Washington financial institution or licensed escrow agent | RCW 59.18.270 |

Many states also have license rules for managers. Florida Statutes 475.01 defines a broker to include a person who rents property for another for pay. Texas Occupations Code 1101.002 lists controlling the acceptance or deposit of rent from a single-family tenant as a broker act. License laws often come with trust account rules. Ask your state's real estate commission what applies.

Ask the manager these questions in writing:

- **Where is my rent held** before payout, and in whose name?
- **Where are deposits held,** and is the account separate?
- **When do I get paid** each month?
- **Who earns interest,** if any?
- **What reserve** stays in the account?

## What Other Clauses Should an Owner Read?

A few more clauses cause most later disputes:

- **Fees.** Monthly fee, leasing fee, renewal fee, late fee split, vacancy fee, inspection fee, eviction fee. Get every fee in one list.
- **Services.** A list of what the monthly fee covers, and what costs extra.
- **Reports.** Monthly statement date, owner portal, and the yearly Form 1099-MISC. IRS instructions say the manager must report rent paid over to the owner on that form.
- **Insurance.** Your landlord policy, the manager's liability coverage, and any need to name the manager as an insured.
- **Fair housing.** The manager agrees to follow fair housing law, including 42 U.S.C. 3604.
- **Indemnity.** Who pays if a claim comes from the other side's error.
- **Evictions.** Who decides to file, who pays the attorney, who attends court.

## What Is the Owner's One-Page Checklist?

- ☐ Term is 12 months or less, with a clear renewal rule
- ☐ Notice to cancel is 30 to 60 days, in writing, with a stated fee
- ☐ Per-repair limit is written as a dollar amount
- ☐ Emergencies are defined
- ☐ All fees are in one list
- ☐ Post-termination fees are limited or none
- ☐ Handover items and a deadline are listed
- ☐ Deposits follow your state's law
- ☐ Manager's license is checked
- ☐ Monthly statement date is set

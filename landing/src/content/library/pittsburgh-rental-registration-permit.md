## When did the Pittsburgh rental registration start?

The Pittsburgh rental registration started on 2024-12-19. On that day the Department of Permits, Licenses, and Inspections (PLI) opened the Residential Housing Rental Permit Program and its rules took effect. The legal base is Ordinance 2022-0270, which City Council passed in 2023. It added Chapter 781 to the Pittsburgh City Code.

The first plan had a voluntary period up to 2025-06-01. After that date, PLI said it would start enforcement. That did not happen. A city page from 2025-06-06 says: "Rental registry compliance remains voluntary until further notice." The PLI registration page, updated 2026-09-29, still says the same.

What this means for you today:

- **No penalties yet.** PLI does not enforce the program or seek penalties until full enforcement starts.
- **30 days' notice.** PLI will publish revised Rules and Regulations and send them to City Council at least 30 days before it enforces.
- **Early fees count.** Fees you pay in the voluntary period go toward the amounts due when enforcement starts.

So an early sign-up costs you nothing extra. It also puts your inspection on your own calendar, not in the rush after the notice.

## Who needs a Pittsburgh rental permit?

The Pittsburgh rental permit applies to each owner or landlord who rents a dwelling unit or sleeping unit in the city, for any period. The PLI Rules count short-term homestays in an owner's home as rental units too. You can register yourself or through an authorized agent, such as your property manager.

The PLI Rules exempt these places:

- Dormitories run by a college for its students.
- Certified rehabilitation facilities and long-term medical care facilities.
- Hotels, motels, nursing homes and other places that the Allegheny County Health Department already permits and inspects.

One registration covers all rental units on the same premises, even in separate buildings. Units on separate premises need separate registrations. If you file 6 or more registrations a year, you must file online in the OneStopPGH portal. With 5 or fewer, you can also file on paper at the OneStopPGH counter, 412 Boulevard of the Allies.

## What does the Pittsburgh rental permit cost per unit?

The Pittsburgh rental permit costs $16 to register, $5.50 for the inspection and $14 for each dwelling or sleeping unit. The city's own example: a 4-unit building pays $77.50. That is $16 + $5.50 + (4 × $14).

| Building | Registration | Inspection base | Per-unit inspection | First-year total | Yearly renewal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 unit | $16.00 | $5.50 | $14.00 | $35.50 | $16.00 |
| 2 units | $16.00 | $5.50 | $28.00 | $49.50 | $16.00 |
| 4 units | $16.00 | $5.50 | $56.00 | $77.50 | $16.00 |
| 12 units | $16.00 | $5.50 | $168.00 | $189.50 | $16.00 |

We did the math from the PLI fee list. Only the 4-unit total comes straight from the city page. The renewal of $16 applies after a passed inspection.

Fee rules that change the bill:

- **Section 8 units.** The PLI Rules exempt Section 8 affordable housing units from permit fees, if you give the required papers.
- **Owner-occupied unit.** If you are a natural person and live in one unit, that one unit skips the $14 per-unit fee. The other fees stay.
- **No refunds.** PLI does not refund fees, except fees it collected in error.
- **Late renewal.** Renew at least 30 days before the permit expires, or pay a late fee.

The permit fee is small next to the rent. The HUD fair market rent for a 2-bedroom in the Pittsburgh metro is $1,389 a month for FY2027, up 6.9% from $1,299 (HUD FY2027 FMR). See the [Pittsburgh fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/pittsburgh-fair-market-rent-2027) for all bedroom sizes.

## How does the Pittsburgh permit process work?

The Pittsburgh permit process has 7 steps in the PLI Rules. A Provisional Permit comes first, then the inspection, then the full permit.

1. **Register** in OneStopPGH or at the counter. Pay all fees at the time you file.
2. **Wait for review.** If PLI asks for more data, answer within 60 days.
3. **Get a Provisional Permit.** It shows you registered and paid. It expires 1 year after PLI issues it.
4. **Request an inspection** within 90 days. Ask at least 5 days before the date you want. Pick an AM (8:00–12:00) or PM (12:00–3:30) slot.
5. **Pass the inspection.** PLI then issues a Non-provisional Permit.
6. **Renew each year.** The Non-provisional Permit is valid for 3 years. No new inspection is needed in that time.
7. **Register again** after 3 years, or when a new owner buys the property. A new owner has 60 days.

Watch for: a unit with a valid certificate of occupancy issued within 3 years of registration does not need the inspection.

## What does the Pittsburgh inspection check?

The Pittsburgh inspection checks the unit against the International Property Maintenance Code (IPMC), as the city adopts it in Code Chapter 1004. PLI published a public checklist on 2025-06-04. It has 8 parts.

| Part | Examples from the PLI checklist |
|---|---|
| Access | An adult (18+) is present and lets the inspector in. No access is a failed inspection. |
| Exterior | Address numbers, roof, gutters, stairs, porches, handrails, walks |
| Interior | Sound structure, no rodents or insects, lit common halls in 3+ unit buildings |
| Unit spaces | Use matches the certificate of occupancy, light, ventilation, a full kitchen |
| Electrical | At least 60-ampere service per unit, working outlets with cover plates |
| Mechanical | Heat that holds 68 °F from October 1 to May 31, dryer vent to the outside |
| Plumbing | Required fixtures, hot water of at least 110 °F |
| Fire safety | Clear exit path, working smoke and carbon monoxide alarms |

Pre-inspection checklist for your manager:

- [ ] Book the visit and confirm an adult will be there.
- [ ] Test each smoke alarm and CO alarm. The owner must supply the means to test them.
- [ ] Check the unit count against your certificate of occupancy.
- [ ] Replace missing outlet covers and fix loose handrails.
- [ ] Run the hot water and check that it reaches 110 °F.

If the unit fails, you get the permits you need, fix each item, and ask for a new inspection. The inspector comes back until the unit passes.

## What penalties apply in Pittsburgh?

The PLI Rules allow a fine of $500 for each rental unit rented without a Non-provisional Permit. PLI can also treat the violation as a summary offense. PLI can suspend or revoke a permit, for example when an inspector finds imminent danger. While compliance is voluntary, the city says it does not seek these penalties.

## What does Allegheny County's fair housing law say about source of income?

Allegheny County's fair housing law does not list source of income as a protected class. The county Human Relations Commission page lists race, color, religion, national origin, ancestry, place of birth, sex, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, disability, marital status, familial status, hairstyle, age, and use of a guide or support animal.

The City of Pittsburgh tried to protect voucher holders. Ordinance 2015-2062 made source of income a protected class. In 2021 the Pennsylvania Supreme Court held that the ordinance went past the city's home-rule power (Apartment Association of Metropolitan Pittsburgh v. City of Pittsburgh, 261 A.3d 1036). So a Pittsburgh owner can choose whether to join the Housing Choice Voucher program.

Every other fair housing rule still applies. Screen each applicant with the same written criteria. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What is a portable tenant screening report?

A portable report lets a renter use a qualifying screening report across rental applications. The exact definition depends on the law and reporting product.

Do not treat every downloaded credit file as a complete portable tenant report. The report may need identity information, rental history and other required content. Check the legal definition before deciding whether the file meets it.

A reusable report can reduce repeated collection of the same sensitive data. It can also reduce duplicate screening costs where the applicable process allows. Those benefits depend on reliable delivery and a clear acceptance policy.

Map the report to the application. Confirm the name, provider and report date without using a name match alone. If the report belongs to another person, stop the review and use the provider's identity process.

Keep report acceptance separate from approval. A complete report supplies evidence for your lawful criteria. It does not require you to ignore a relevant issue or approve an incomplete application.

## Which states require acceptance?

State rules differ, so check the property's current law before requesting another fee. Colorado and Washington show why a national yes-or-no answer is unsafe.

Colorado's HB23-1099 requires acceptance except in specified circumstances. The official summary describes a qualifying report prepared within the prior 30 days and made directly available by the reporting agency. When the applicant provides the covered report, the landlord cannot charge an application fee or access fee. [Read the Colorado act summary](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb23-1099).

Washington RCW 59.18.257 requires disclosure of whether the landlord accepts a comprehensive reusable report. A landlord who indicates acceptance may obtain its own report but cannot charge the applicant for that additional report. [Read Washington's rule](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=59.18.257).

These examples do not form a complete state list. Ask local counsel to confirm coverage, exceptions and any city rule. Do not reject a report merely because your software defaults to a paid order.

Publish your reviewed policy where applicants can find it. Make sure the listing, staff script and application form say the same thing.

## How old can a portable report be?

Use the age limit in the governing rule rather than a vendor's general marketing claim. Record the report's issue date and when it became available to you.

Colorado's official summary describes a landlord's ability to require preparation within the previous 30 days. That is a Colorado example, not a nationwide expiry date.

A recent report may still contain stale underlying records. Check the source and any disputed information rather than treating the issue date as proof of accuracy. A new cover page cannot repair a wrong court match.

If the report lacks required content, identify the missing item precisely. Ask whether the provider can supply it through the accepted process. Avoid a vague demand to buy a whole new report without checking the fee rule.

Keep a note of the acceptance decision and the rule you used. That note helps staff answer a later question without reopening the full sensitive file.

## How do I check that the report is real?

Verify the report through a trusted provider channel. Do not rely solely on a PDF forwarded from an email address you cannot authenticate.

| Check | Recommended action | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| Provider | Find the agency through an independent channel | The source exists |
| Delivery | Use the agency's authorized access method | The file came from the source |
| Applicant | Match identity through the approved process | The record concerns the right person |
| Date | Save issue and receipt dates | The report meets the age rule |
| Scope | Compare content with the legal definition | Required fields are present |
| Dispute | Give a way to flag wrong data | Errors can receive review |

The table is an operational checklist. It does not add legal report fields or authorize extra fees.

Do not ask applicants to share account passwords. A provider should offer an appropriate access path. Restrict staff access to the resulting report and avoid general inbox storage.

If something looks altered, document the specific mismatch. Give the applicant a clear way to resolve it. An unfamiliar file design alone is not proof of fraud.

## How should I use a verified report?

Use a verified report against the same lawful criteria that govern other applications. Record the decision basis and any applicant explanation.

The FTC explains that a decision based partly on a consumer report can require an adverse action notice. The duty can apply to denial or less favorable terms. Do not treat portability as an exemption from consumer reporting law. [Read the FTC landlord guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/using-consumer-reports-what-landlords-need-know).

Review the notice process before the first portable report arrives. Identify who sends the notice and which agency details belong in it. Keep proof of the decision and delivery.

Check how your system stores third-party reports. An upload should not trigger an automatic second order and fee. Test that path with a sample record before staff use it on a live application.

Give applicants the acceptance policy early. Clear requirements can prevent repeated submissions and unnecessary costs. For related processes, browse the [rental guide library](/guides).

Your next step is to confirm local acceptance rules and test direct provider access. Then update the application form to match the reviewed process.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

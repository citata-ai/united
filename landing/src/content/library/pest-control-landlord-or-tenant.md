## Who is responsible for pest control in a rental?

The landlord must hand over a unit free of pests and keep it fit to live in. The tenant must keep the unit clean and report pests. After move-in, state law, the unit type and the cause decide who pays.

There is no single national rule. This table shows three state examples.

| State | Base rule | Bed bug rule | Statute |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | A unit is untenantable if areas under the landlord's control are not kept free of rodents and vermin at the start of the lease | Landlord may not show or rent a vacant unit the landlord knows has a current bed bug infestation; written bed bug notice before a new tenancy | Civil Code 1941.1, 1954.602, 1954.603 |
| Florida | Landlord must provide for extermination of rats, mice, roaches, ants, wood-destroying organisms and bed bugs, unless the lease says otherwise in writing. Does not apply to single-family homes and duplexes | Included in the extermination list | Florida Statutes 83.51(2)(a) |
| Arizona | General duties under the landlord-tenant act | Landlord gives bed bug education and may not lease a unit known to have bed bugs; tenant must report bed bugs in writing. Excludes single-family homes | Arizona Revised Statutes 33-1319 |

The unit type matters. Florida and Arizona both treat single-family homes differently. In Florida, the extermination duty in 83.51(2)(a) does not cover single-family homes and duplexes. In other buildings, it applies unless the lease says otherwise in writing.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Who pays for bed bugs?

Bed bug costs usually fall on the landlord first, because the law often bars renting an infested unit and fault is hard to prove. The rules differ by state.

- **California:** Civil Code 1954.602 says a landlord may not show, rent or lease a vacant unit that the landlord knows has a current bed bug infestation. Civil Code 1954.603 requires a written bed bug notice before a new tenancy. Under 1954.605, the landlord must tell inspected tenants the pest control operator's findings in writing within 2 business days.
- **Arizona:** Arizona Revised Statutes 33-1319 says the landlord must give bed bug education to tenants and may not lease a unit known to have bed bugs. The tenant may not knowingly move infested items in and must report bed bugs in writing.
- **Florida:** Florida Statutes 83.51 lists bed bugs among the pests the landlord must exterminate in most multi-unit buildings, unless the lease says otherwise in writing.

The EPA gives landlords one more reason to act fast. Its landlord page says not to blame the tenant, because often there is no fault. It warns that a culture of blame makes residents delay reports, and late reports lead to larger, more costly infestations.

The EPA also says some researchers recommend treating adjacent units: both sides, above and below. In an apartment building, one unit's bed bugs are often everyone's problem.

## Who pays for mice or roaches?

Mice and roaches usually come from building conditions, food sources or both. So cost depends on the cause.

- **Building conditions:** gaps around pipes, holes in walls, leaks and broken screens let pests in. These are landlord repairs in most states.
- **Tenant conditions:** food left out, trash not removed and clutter feed and hide pests. A landlord can often bill for pests the tenant caused, if the lease allows it.
- **Shared buildings:** pests move between units. In a multi-unit building, the landlord is usually the only party who can treat every unit.

Florida Statutes 83.51 says the landlord is not responsible for conditions caused by the negligent or wrongful act of the tenant, a member of the tenant's family, or another person there with the tenant's consent. Your proof matters. Keep dated photos and inspection notes.

## What lease clause covers pests?

A good pest clause sets duties for both sides and follows your state's law. It cannot take away rights the statute gives the tenant. Use this checklist to draft or review your clause.

- [ ] **Move-in condition:** the landlord delivers the unit free of known pests.
- [ ] **Tenant duties:** keep the unit clean, store food in closed containers, take out trash, and do not bring in infested furniture.
- [ ] **Reporting:** the tenant reports pests in writing within a set number of days.
- [ ] **Access:** the tenant allows entry for inspection and treatment after the notice the state requires.
- [ ] **Preparation:** the tenant follows the pest company's prep list before treatment.
- [ ] **Cost rule:** who pays for routine treatment, and when the tenant pays for pests they caused.
- [ ] **Single-family option:** where state law allows it, a written rule that the tenant handles routine pest control.
- [ ] **State notices:** any required disclosures, such as California's bed bug notice.

In Florida, Florida Statutes 83.51 lets the parties change the extermination duty in most buildings only in writing. In California, Civil Code 1954.603 sets the bed bug notice content. Ask a local attorney to review the clause.

## How do I prevent pest problems at turnover?

Turnover is the best time to stop pests, because the unit is empty. The EPA's landlord page tells landlords to do detailed inspections when tenants leave.

Use integrated pest management, or IPM (a mix of methods, pesticides last). The EPA describes IPM as an approach that uses pest life cycles and several control methods, with the least possible hazard to people and property.

Turnover steps:

1. **Inspect every room.** Look for droppings, live insects, shed skins and bed bug spots on walls and baseboards.
2. **Seal entry points.** Close gaps around pipes, under doors and at vents.
3. **Fix moisture.** Repair leaks under sinks and around tubs. Roaches need water.
4. **Remove debris.** Clear left-behind furniture, boxes and food.
5. **Treat if needed.** Hire a licensed pest company. Ask for a written report.
6. **Record the result.** Keep the report and photos with the move-in file.

This record protects you. If the next tenant reports pests in week 3, you can show the unit was clear at move-in.

## How should a landlord respond to a pest report?

Respond to a pest report the same day, and in writing. Fast action keeps costs down and keeps you within the law.

1. Thank the tenant and confirm the report in writing.
2. Book a licensed inspector, with the entry notice your state requires.
3. Check nearby units in shared buildings.
4. Share the findings with affected tenants. California sets a 2-business-day deadline in Civil Code 1954.605.
5. Treat, then follow up until the pests are gone.
6. Decide on cost after treatment, based on the cause and your lease.

## What is an entity in AI search?

An entity is a distinct thing that a search engine knows as one item, with facts attached. A company, a person, a city and a license are all entities. Google launched its Knowledge Graph in May 2012 with the phrase "things, not strings". At launch it held more than 500 million objects and more than 3.5 billion facts about them (Google, 2012).

For a property management company, being a clear entity means this: when an AI tool reads your name, it knows which company you are. It knows where you work, what you do and which website is yours. It does not mix you up with a firm that has a similar name in another state.

We learned this on our own brand. In our run of 2026-10-19, Claude answered about Citate.ai or Citant.ai, not Citata.ai, in 4 of 4 brand answers. Gemini merged us with Citant.ai (our data, 2026-10-19). Similar names and thin facts made us a weak entity.

## Which facts define your entity?

Your entity is defined by a small set of core facts. Write them once, in one file, and copy them from there. Google's organization markup guide recommends most of these as properties (Google Search Central).

| Fact | Example format | Where it must match |
|---|---|---|
| Public name | "Example Property Management" | Site, Google Business Profile, Yelp, BBB, directories |
| Legal name | "Example PM LLC" | About page, schema `legalName`, license record |
| Address or service area | Street address, or cities served | Site footer, Google Business Profile, Apple, Bing |
| Phone | One main local number | Everywhere |
| Website | One home page URL | Every profile |
| Services | Residential, HOA, Section 8, leasing only | Site, directories, Google Business Profile categories |
| Cities served | A list of real cities | City pages, profiles |
| License | Broker license type and number | Site footer, about page |
| Founding year | "Since 2009" | About page, schema `foundingDate` |
| Profiles | LinkedIn, Facebook, BBB, Yelp URLs | Schema `sameAs` |

Google's Business Profile rules add 3 limits. Your name should reflect your real-world name, with no keywords or city names added. A service-area business should hide its street address. There should be only one profile per business (Google Business Profile Help).

## How does AI connect your site, listings and reviews?

AI connects your pages by matching facts. If the name, phone and website on a Yelp page match the ones on your site, the engine can treat both as the same company. Each match adds evidence. Each mismatch adds doubt.

AI answers often use listings more than company sites. We asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude for the best property manager in 50 US cities, 150 answers in total. 132 answers linked a listing or directory site, and 106 linked a company site. ChatGPT linked a listing site in 48 of 50 answers, but a company site in only 15 (our data, 2026-10-19).

So your entity lives mostly on other sites. Those sites must hold the same facts as yours.

SOCi studied local AI answers for multi-location brands in 2026. It found that ChatGPT, Perplexity and other non-Google AI tools pull from Google Maps, business websites, Yelp and Facebook. These tools were only about 68% accurate on business contact info. Gemini, grounded in Google Maps, was more accurate (SOCi, 2026).

## What breaks an entity?

An entity breaks when facts disagree or when two companies look like one. These are the common causes for property managers:

1. **Old phone numbers** on directories after a phone system change.
2. **Two Google Business Profiles** for one office, often after a move.
3. **A name with keywords,** such as "Best Chicago Property Management Rentals".
4. **A similar name** to another firm, with no city or license to tell them apart.
5. **A franchise page and a local site** that give different addresses.
6. **A rebrand** where old listings still use the old name.
7. **Schema that does not match** the visible page, such as a different phone.

Watch for: tracking phone numbers. If each ad channel shows a different number, directories may copy the wrong one. Keep one main number in all listings and schema.

## How do you check your entity?

Check your entity with a fixed test once a month. It takes 4 steps.

1. **Ask AI tools.** In ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity, ask: "What is [company name] in [city]? Give its address, phone, website and services." Use a logged-out window.
2. **Score each fact.** Mark each answer right, wrong or missing.
3. **Check the sources.** Note which pages each answer links. Fix those pages first.
4. **Check Google.** Search your exact name. Look for a Business Profile and any knowledge panel. Developers can also query the Knowledge Graph Search API, which returns entities Google knows by name. Google says the API is not for production-critical use (Google for Developers).

A sample score sheet:

| Fact | ChatGPT | Gemini | Claude | Perplexity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Name | Right | Right | Wrong firm | Right |
| Phone | Old number | Right | Missing | Right |
| Website | Right | Right | Missing | Right |
| Cities | Missing | Right | Missing | Partial |
| Score | 2 of 4 | 4 of 4 | 0 of 4 | 3.5 of 4 |

The sheet is an example, not real results. Track your own score over time.

## How do you fix a weak entity?

Fix the facts at the source, then add proof that ties them together.

- [ ] Write your core facts in one file. Date it.
- [ ] Fix your Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp, BBB and the main directories.
- [ ] Add Organization schema to your home page or about page, with `sameAs` links to each profile. Google says one page is enough (Google Search Central).
- [ ] Put your license number and cities served in the footer.
- [ ] If your name is close to another firm's, add one line on your about page that says how you differ.
- [ ] Re-run the AI check in 4–6 weeks.

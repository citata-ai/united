Hawaii gives month-to-month tenants 45 days' notice of a rent increase. I read the rules in HRS chapter 521, the Residential Landlord-Tenant Code, on the Hawaii State Legislature site on 2026-10-20. The planner's note for this topic named § 521-21(d) and § 521-71. Both are correct.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Cap on rent increases | None in chapter 521 | Chapter 521 |
| Rent increase, month to month | 45 consecutive days' written notice | § 521-21(d) |
| Rent increase, shorter than month to month | 15 consecutive days' written notice | § 521-21(e) |
| Landlord ends a month-to-month tenancy | At least 45 days in writing | § 521-71(a) |
| Tenant ends a month-to-month tenancy | At least 28 days in writing | § 521-71(b) |
| Demolition, condo conversion, vacation rental use | At least 120 days | § 521-71(c) |
| Tenancy shorter than month to month | 10 days, either side | § 521-71(d) |
| Termination used to dodge rent notice | Void | § 521-71(f) |
| Retaliatory rent increase | Banned | § 521-74 |
| Late charge | Max 8% of rent due | § 521-21(f) |

## Does Hawaii have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

Hawaii's Residential Landlord-Tenant Code sets no cap on rent increases. HRS § 521-21(a) says the landlord and tenant "may agree to any consideration, not otherwise prohibited by law, as rent". The rest of § 521-21 sets notice periods, payment timing and a late fee cap, not an amount limit.

Two rules still limit when you can raise rent:

- **Notice.** The 45-day and 15-day periods in § 521-21(d) and (e).
- **Retaliation.** HRS § 521-74 bars a landlord from demanding a rent increase after a tenant's good-faith complaint to a health, building or consumer agency, or a good-faith repair request under § 521-63 or § 521-64. It applies while the tenant keeps paying the usual rent.

I did not review county ordinances or rules for subsidized housing. If your units are in a program with rent rules, those rules apply too. For voucher units, see our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits).

## How many days notice does a Hawaii landlord need to raise the rent?

A Hawaii landlord needs 45 consecutive days' written notice to raise the rent on a month-to-month tenancy (HRS § 521-21(d)). For a tenancy shorter than month to month, such as week to week, the notice is 15 consecutive days (§ 521-21(e)).

The word "consecutive" matters. Count every calendar day, including weekends and holidays.

Worked example: a month-to-month tenant pays on the 1st. You want a new rent to start on August 1.

| Step | Date |
|---|---|
| Effective date of the increase | August 1 |
| Count back 45 days | June 17 |
| Last day to deliver written notice | June 17 |

Deliver the notice earlier if you mail it, so the tenant has it in time. HRS § 521-9 defines notice and notification. I did not read that section for this article, so check it before you rely on mail dates.

## Can a Hawaii landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

A Hawaii landlord can raise the rent during a fixed-term lease only if the lease allows it. Chapter 521 gives no general right to change the rent mid-term. Section 521-21(a) lets the parties agree on rent, so the lease controls until it ends.

At the end of a fixed term, the landlord may offer a new lease at a new rent. If the tenant stays after the term without consent, § 521-71(e) applies. The tenant may owe up to twice the monthly rent, prorated by day, for each day of holdover. If the landlord does not file within the first 60 days of holdover, a month-to-month tenancy at the old rent begins.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Hawaii?

A Hawaii landlord needs at least 45 days' written notice to end a month-to-month tenancy (HRS § 521-71(a)). After the landlord gives notice, the tenant may leave at any time in the last 45 days, with notice of the move date and prorated rent.

Other periods in § 521-71:

| Who | Situation | Notice |
|---|---|---|
| Tenant | Ends a month-to-month tenancy | 28 days; rent is due through day 28 |
| Landlord | Demolition, condo conversion under chapter 514B, or change to transient vacation rentals | 120 days |
| Either | Tenancy shorter than month to month | 10 days |

A revised or reissued 120-day notice starts a new 120-day period.

Watch for: § 521-71(f). A notice of termination given to evade the rent increase notice in § 521-21(d) or (e) is void. Do not end a tenancy with 10 or 45 days just to re-rent the unit at a higher rent on short notice.

## Do cities in Hawaii add their own rent or notice rules?

Chapter 521 sets the rent and notice rules for residential tenancies across Hawaii. I found no section in chapter 521 that gives counties power to cap rent. I also found no section that bars them. I did not review county codes for this article, so I make no claim about local rules.

What to do: before a rent change in a new county, ask a local attorney or the county housing office if any local rule applies to your property type.

## Hawaii rent increase checklist

1. **Confirm the tenancy type.** Fixed term, month to month or shorter.
2. **Count 45 consecutive days** back from the new rent date for month to month.
3. **Put it in writing** and keep proof of delivery.
4. **Check for retaliation risk.** Note any recent complaint or repair request.
5. **Do not use a termination notice** to avoid the rent notice rules.
6. **Keep late fees at 8% or less** of the rent due.

## What are the steps to start a property management company?

A property management company needs eight things before it signs its first owner. Do them in this order, because each step depends on the one before it.

1. Get the license your state requires.
2. Write a short business plan.
3. Form a business entity and get an EIN.
4. Buy insurance.
5. Open an operating account and trust accounts.
6. Pick property management software.
7. Write your management agreement and fee table.
8. Sign your first owners.

Buildium (2026) estimates that most new companies spend $2,000 to $10,000 or more to launch. A home-based start is near the low end. An office lease and heavy marketing push you past $10,000. Buildium also says many new companies set aside 6 to 12 months of operating expenses, because it takes time to sign enough doors.

## Which license do I need to manage rentals?

In most states, managing rentals for others for pay needs a real estate license. Buildium (2026) says that in most states, property managers must hold a property management license or a real estate broker's license. It adds that only a handful of states require no license at all.

State laws define broker activity in different words. Two examples:

| State | Statute | What counts as broker work |
|---|---|---|
| Texas | Occupations Code §1101.002(1)(A)(x) | For pay, controls the acceptance or deposit of rent from a resident of a single-family residential unit |
| Florida | Statutes §475.01 | For pay, rents or offers to rent, or negotiates the rental of, real property of others |

Read your own state's law and its real estate commission's guidance. Ask three questions:

- Do I need a broker license, a salesperson license under a broker, or a separate property manager license?
- Does an exemption apply, such as a resident manager or an employee of one owner?
- Does a community association (HOA) manager need its own license?

Some new managers start as a licensed agent under an existing broker. That broker then supervises the work and often holds the trust account. Check what your state allows.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Which business entity should I form?

Most new property management companies register as an LLC or a corporation, according to Buildium (2026). The entity separates business debts from your personal assets. Your state's rules decide how a licensed brokerage may be owned and named.

Steps:

1. **Pick a name.** Check that the name is free in your state's business registry.
2. **File the entity.** File with your secretary of state and name a registered agent.
3. **Get an EIN.** The IRS issues an EIN (employer identification number) online. The IRS says, "You never have to pay a fee for an EIN."
4. **Put the name on your license.** The California DRE lists the use of a business name that is not on the license as a common audit violation. Your state may have a similar rule.

This is general information, not tax advice. Ask a tax professional which entity and tax election fit you.

## Which insurance do I need first?

Buy errors and omissions (E&O) and general liability insurance before you sign an owner. Buildium (2026) says owners usually require both before they sign.

| Policy | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Errors and omissions (E&O) | Claims that your professional work caused a loss |
| General liability | Injury or property damage claims tied to your business |
| Workers' compensation | Injuries to employees; rules differ by state |
| Fidelity or crime coverage | Theft of funds by an employee |

Ask an insurance broker who serves property managers for limits and prices. Ask owners to name your company as an additional insured on their landlord policy.

## Which software and bank accounts do I set up?

Open bank accounts before you take any owner money. You need at least an operating account for your company and a trust account for owner and tenant funds. Many companies also keep a separate account for security deposits.

Trust accounts carry the strictest rules. In California, for example, the DRE describes these duties:

- Keep a record of all trust funds in and out, with a daily running balance (Regulation 2831).
- Keep a separate record for each owner or tenant (Regulation 2831.1).
- Reconcile those records at least once a month (Regulation 2831.2).

Your state may set other rules. Check them with your real estate regulator.

Then pick software. Look for trust accounting, owner statements, online rent payments, maintenance requests and an owner portal in one system. Our [AppFolio vs Buildium comparison](/guides/appfolio-vs-buildium) shows list prices and unit minimums for two common choices.

## How many doors do I need to break even?

Your break-even door count is your monthly fixed costs divided by your monthly fee per door. The numbers below are an example, not data.

| Item | Example |
|---|---|
| Software | $300 |
| Insurance | $400 |
| Phone, website, marketing | $800 |
| Accounting and legal | $500 |
| Office and other costs | $1,000 |
| Fixed costs per month | $3,000 |
| Average rent | $1,500 |
| Management fee | 8% |
| Fee per door per month | $120 |
| Doors to break even | 3,000 ÷ 120 = 25 |

This break-even point pays the costs but not your salary. Add the salary you need to the fixed costs and divide again. With a $5,000 monthly salary, the example needs 67 doors. Test other fee levels with the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## How do I find the first owners?

Your first owners usually come from people who already know you. Buildium (2026) lists a website, content marketing, social media and local networking as the main channels.

First-owner checklist:

- [ ] Publish a simple website with your services, fee table, service area and license number.
- [ ] Tell every landlord, real estate agent and investor you know that you manage rentals.
- [ ] Ask agents for referrals of buyers who want to rent out a property.
- [ ] Join a local landlord or investor group.
- [ ] Create a Google Business Profile with your real service area.
- [ ] Write one management agreement that states every fee.
- [ ] Decline owners who will not fix safety issues or follow fair housing law.

Start with fewer, better doors. One owner who refers a friend is worth more than three owners who argue about each repair bill.

Two property management websites with the same traffic can end the month far apart. In Goodjuju Marketing's benchmark of 35 client sites (July 8 to October 5, 2026), the top 25% got about 3 times the owner leads of the bottom 25% from the same number of visitors. That gap is the real reason to redesign. A site that looks old but converts well can wait. A new-looking site that converts poorly cannot.

## Which signs show a property management website is out of date?

A property management website is out of date when it fails on speed, conversion or content. Use this table. Each sign has a test you can run in an hour.

| Sign | Test | Bar |
|---|---|---|
| Slow load | PageSpeed Insights or Search Console Core Web Vitals report | Largest Contentful Paint within 2.5 s (Google) |
| Laggy taps | Same report | Interaction to Next Paint under 200 ms (Google) |
| Page jumps while loading | Same report | Cumulative Layout Shift under 0.1 (Google) |
| Low owner lead rate | Owner leads ÷ visitors in Google Analytics | Middle half: 5 to 16 per 1,000 (Goodjuju benchmark) |
| No room for city pages | Try to add a page for each service city | You can add them without a developer |
| Fees hidden or in a PDF | Look at your fee page as an owner | A full fee table in plain text |
| Mixed owner and tenant paths | Open the home page on a phone | Owner path visible without scrolling far |
| Old facts | Check license, address, phone, team | All match your Google Business Profile |

Three or more failed rows point to a rebuild. One or two point to fixes.

### Worked example: is the site the problem?

Your site had 2,400 visitors last month and 7 owner form leads.

- Lead rate: 7 ÷ 2,400 = 2.9 per 1,000.
- Goodjuju's middle half: 5 to 16 per 1,000.
- At 5 per 1,000, the same traffic gives 12 leads. That is 5 more each month.

If 1 in 5 owner leads signs, and each owner brings 2 doors, 5 more leads a month is about 2 more doors a month. That is the value a better site could add. Note the benchmark counts forms only, not phone calls, and leans toward companies that already invest in their sites.

## How often do property management companies rebuild?

No public study measures how often property management companies rebuild. Property Manager Websites, a vendor that sells rebuilds, suggests a new site "every 2-3 years" in a post last updated 2024-07-01. That is a sales view, not data.

A calendar rule wastes money on a site that works and leaves a bad site in place too long. Use the signs above instead, and check them once a year.

## Can a redesign hurt rankings?

Yes, a redesign can hurt rankings for a while. Google's site move guide says to "expect temporary fluctuation in site ranking during the move." For a medium-sized site, it "can take a few weeks or more" for Google to show the new URLs.

The risk is highest when URLs change. Google's rules for a safe move:

1. **Map every old URL** to its new URL before launch.
2. **Use server-side permanent redirects** (301 or 308). Google says 301 redirects "don't cause a loss in PageRank."
3. **Keep the redirects** "generally at least 1 year."
4. **Submit a new sitemap** to speed up discovery.
5. **Check server logs** for old URLs that still get visits.

The real danger is content loss. A redesign that drops your city pages, FAQ answers or fee table loses the words that ranked. Keep every page that brings owner leads, with its text, unless you have a better version ready.

Google Search and the Map Pack gave 50.6% of owner leads in Goodjuju's fall 2026 study of 35 companies. A careless launch puts that share at risk.

## What should I fix before a full redesign?

Fix the cheap things first. Each fix also tells you if the design is really the problem.

- [ ] **Fee table.** Publish every fee as text: management, leasing, renewal, setup, maintenance markup.
- [ ] **Owner pages.** One page per service city with real local facts.
- [ ] **Speed.** Compress images, cut unused plugins, then retest Core Web Vitals.
- [ ] **Calls to action.** One clear owner action per page: "Get a rental analysis."
- [ ] **Tracking.** Mark the owner form as a lead in Google Analytics. Tag the Google Business Profile link with UTM labels.
- [ ] **Facts.** Match name, address, phone and license on the site, Google, Yelp and directories.

Run these fixes for 60 to 90 days. Then measure the owner lead rate again. If it rose to the benchmark range, you saved the rebuild cost. If it did not move and the site still fails 3+ signs, plan the redesign with a URL map.

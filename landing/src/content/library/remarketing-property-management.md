## How do Google and Meta remarketing differ?

Google and Meta provide different ad settings and delivery surfaces, so plan them separately. Remarketing means reaching people after a prior interaction. Do not assume an audience permitted on a platform is permitted everywhere else.

Google Ads now calls remarketing audiences your data segments. Google's help page describes re-engagement through website, search, video, and other supported audience routes. Check which campaign types and settings your account currently supports before designing the test.

For Meta, check the current housing category and audience controls in the campaign flow. Meta's ads-fairness statement describes restrictions on housing targeting and the removal of Special Ad Audiences. Old tutorials may describe tools that no longer apply.

| Planning question | Google review | Meta review |
|---|---|---|
| Is the campaign eligible? | Check personalized advertising policy | Check housing category requirements |
| Where will ads appear? | Confirm campaign and network settings | Confirm selected placements |
| Which data can be used? | Review segment and data requirements | Review audience and business tool terms |
| What counts as success? | Qualified owner inquiries and later sales | The same sales definitions |
| What needs testing? | Tags, exclusions, forms, reporting | Data collection, delivery, forms, reporting |

This is a planning table, not a promise that every audience is available. Save the policy review with your campaign brief. Ask platform support about unclear classifications before you spend.

## Which visitors should an owner campaign consider?

An owner campaign should consider relevant service interest, subject to the platform's rules and applicable privacy duties. A management-fee page or service comparison may show a useful task. A rent payment or repair request does not show new owner demand.

Map your pages before building any audience. Separate owner services, rental availability, tenant support, staff recruitment, and vendor pages. Include a visitor only when the use is allowed and the interaction fits the campaign purpose.

Avoid using sensitive information from applications, screening, payment problems, or tenant disputes. Those records belong in service systems with suitable controls. Do not copy them into advertising tools to make a more precise audience.

Check what your tags collect. Review page paths, form fields, event names, and any details passed to platforms. A helpful page can have an unsafe tracking setup if a form sends private information with an event.

Do not treat existing clients as new prospects in reports. Use allowed exclusions where available and appropriate. If your platform cannot support a clean audience for the campaign, choose a different promotion method rather than disguising the traffic.

## How long should the audience window be?

Set the audience window as a test based on your actual owner sales cycle. The window is how long a prior interaction remains eligible for your campaign. There is no checked universal best duration for property management.

Review how long owners take from first contact to a real discussion. Separate a person researching a future purchase from an owner who needs a manager now. Use those patterns to write useful messages, within the audience options the platform permits.

Write down the proposed window, budget limit, review date, and stop condition before launch. Label these as your test choices. Do not present a suggested setting as an industry benchmark.

Watch repeated exposure and complaints as well as cost. A small local audience can see the same message often. Review available frequency controls, update stale messages, and stop a test that produces nuisance rather than useful interest.

Check audience eligibility when you change the window. A longer period does not automatically solve a lack of suitable visitors. If the audience remains too small or unclear, improve the owner page and direct outreach process first.

## Do housing ad rules affect remarketing?

Housing ad rules can affect targeting, so review them before audience setup. Google's restricted-targeting policy bars specified demographic and ZIP-code targeting for housing ads in the United States and Canada. Meta also describes housing targeting restrictions in its ads-fairness statement.

Do not assume every owner-service campaign has the same classification. Review the ad and destination together. A page that mixes management services with available homes may raise different questions from a page that explains a business service.

Keep the exact campaign and policy decision in the review file. Record who checked it and what changed after review. Platform approval is not a legal opinion, and it does not replace fair housing or privacy review.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Do not evade a policy by changing labels while keeping the same restricted use. If audience settings are unavailable, stop and choose an eligible approach. Search ads based on a current query, partner education, or a useful owner resource may fit a different plan.

## What should the ad and landing page say?

The ad should answer the concern that brought the owner to your site. Give a clear reason to return. A sample report, checked fee explanation, or manager-transfer checklist can support a useful next conversation.

Match the destination to the promise. An ad about repair approvals should link to the repair process, not a broad home page. State service limits and the contact step. Avoid claims that an owner will earn more or avoid every dispute.

Use real evidence with permission. Label sample documents and remove private data. Do not invent testimonials or imply that a single client outcome is typical. Keep any comparison current and support it with a source.

Use the [owner search guide](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai) to understand questions that can arise before the return visit. The page can also help an owner who reaches you without seeing an ad.

## How do you tell whether remarketing adds useful leads?

Measure remarketing with the same sales definitions you use for other owner channels. Track spend, accepted owner inquiries, meetings, proposals, and signed clients. Keep clicks and ad views as supporting measures.

A returning owner might have contacted you without another ad. Record that uncertainty rather than giving the ad full credit for every later sale. Where practical, use a planned comparison that tests the extra value of the campaign.

Ask sales staff whether leads fit the service area, property type, and readiness to hire. Check duplicate contacts and existing-client requests. Use [traffic tracking guidance](/guides/track-ai-traffic-property-management) to keep assisted discovery in view. Continue only when the campaign produces useful work at a cost your business can support.

## What does a cost segregation study do?

A cost segregation study supports how building costs divide among assets with different depreciation treatment.

The IRS Cost Segregation Audit Technique Guide describes methods and elements of a quality study. It focuses on support for asset classification and cost allocation. The guide helps explain what examiners review. The guide itself is not an official IRS pronouncement or legal authority.

A study does not create new purchase costs. It reallocates supported basis among eligible asset categories. Land remains a separate issue. IRS Publication 527 explains that land is not depreciable, while eligible rental property costs follow depreciation rules.

Ask the provider how it will identify assets, estimate costs, and connect the result to your total basis. Supply closing records, construction invoices, plans, photos, and prior depreciation schedules. Missing records can limit what a defensible report can support.

| Study question | Evidence to request |
|---|---|
| Which assets move? | Detailed asset list and classifications |
| How were costs assigned? | Method and source documents |
| Does the total match basis? | Reconciliation to purchase or construction costs |
| Who prepared the work? | Relevant training and experience |
| Who handles tax changes? | Scope agreed with your preparer |

Do not accept a report that only promises a percentage of the building as an immediate write-off. Ask how the provider reached each conclusion for your property.

## What does a cost segregation study cost?

The study cost is quote-specific, so obtain a written scope instead of treating an unsourced online range as a current price.

This guide does not quote an industry study fee. Property complexity, available records, inspection work, and the reporting scope can affect the proposal. Compare providers using the same property information and the same deliverables.

Ask whether the quote includes a site review, detailed asset schedules, preparer support, and responses to examiner questions. Check what happens if the tax professional rejects a classification. Clarify whether amended return or accounting method work falls outside the study fee.

The relevant benefit is tax savings you can actually use. IRS Publication 925 describes passive activity and at-risk limits. A larger depreciation deduction may not create an equal current reduction in your tax bill. Ask the preparer to model your full return.

Use an illustrative test, not a promised result. Assume a study costs $3,000 and produces an extra $20,000 deduction usable now. At an assumed 24% marginal rate, the initial tax reduction is $4,800. Before other costs and later tax, the difference is $1,800. Every figure here is a planning assumption.

Change the same example so the deduction is suspended. The immediate cash tax benefit could be absent. That is why a study estimate needs a loss-limit review before purchase.

## How does bonus depreciation interact with a study?

Bonus depreciation can accelerate eligible asset deductions, but eligibility depends on the asset and acquisition and service dates.

IRS Publication 946 says the law reinstated a 100% special depreciation allowance for certain qualified property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025. The publication also describes elections and other timing categories. That rule does not turn the whole residential building into qualifying property.

Have the preparer apply the current rules to each study category. The analysis needs asset type, acquisition details, placed-in-service date, and any election. Do not use an old phase-down chart without checking current IRS guidance.

A study report and a tax return perform different jobs. The report supports classification and cost. Your preparer decides how the current tax rules apply to those supported facts. Ask the parties to agree on the handover format before work starts.

Keep the original schedules and the revised schedules together. If the building has already been depreciated, the correction process requires its own review. Do not simply overwrite past records in bookkeeping software.

Also review personal use and participation. Publications 527 and 925 address limits that can affect rental deductions. Accelerated depreciation changes timing, but it does not remove those limits. Ask for a written explanation of which benefit is current and which may remain deferred.

## What happens to depreciation when the property sells?

A sale requires a fresh tax calculation using adjusted basis, asset allocations, and applicable recapture rules.

IRS Publication 946 explains depreciation recapture issues and points readers to disposition guidance. Publication 527 also addresses sales of rental property. A study can affect the tax character of amounts tied to different assets. Do not assume every later gain receives the same treatment.

Give the sales tax preparer the full study, accumulated depreciation schedules, and sale allocation documents. Keep records even if you change accountants or managers. The report remains relevant after the initial deduction year.

Ask for hold-period scenarios before commissioning the study. Compare a near-term sale, a longer hold, and a year when loss limits delay deductions. Include study fees and tax preparation work. The present value of earlier deductions matters, but so do later tax and transaction costs.

Separate the tax decision from the property's operating result. A study does not increase rent or fix a weak roof. Include normal management costs in the [rental fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) and keep tax planning as a separate analysis.

Before you order, send the proposed scope and benefit model to your tax professional. Ask whether the expected current benefit justifies the work for this specific rental. Retain the written assumptions with the final report.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## What leasing and renewal fees do Dallas managers charge?

Dallas leasing fees run from 50% of one month's rent to more than a full month. The median in our 10 pages is 75%, or $1,355.25 at a $1,807 rent.

- **50% of one month:** Blue Atlas, Evernest ($500 minimum) and Mynd.
- **75% of one month:** McCaw, Darling ($1,000 minimum) and Northpoint.
- **100% of one month:** Keyrenter Uptown Dallas, and Flat Fee Landlord plus a $350 activation fee.
- **$1,500 flat:** Ziprent and TurboTenant.

Renewal fees vary more. The median is $250. Percent renewals cost more on higher rents: Northpoint and Keyrenter charge 25% of one month's rent, so $451.75 at $1,807.

Watch for the extras. Northpoint and Flat Fee Landlord add 10% to maintenance invoices. Blue Atlas and Darling say they add no maintenance markup. McCaw lists a $95 trip fee for owner-requested visits and eviction fees. Ask each manager for the full fee schedule, not only the headline.

## Which local rules add cost for Dallas owners?

The City of Dallas rental registration adds a fixed yearly cost. Dallas City Code Section 27-31 sets the fee at $74 per single-family home, duplex unit or condo unit since October 1, 2025. A multitenant property of 3 or more units pays $13 per unit.

Worked example, one Dallas house at $1,807 a month for a year, at the median fees:

1. **Management:** $162.50 × 12 = $1,950.
2. **Leasing (one new tenant):** $1,355.25.
3. **City registration:** $74.
4. **Total year one:** $3,379.25, or 15.6% of $21,684 gross rent.
5. **Year two with a renewal instead of a new lease:** $1,950 + $250 + $74 = $2,274, or 10.5% of rent.

The gap between year one and year two is why tenant turnover matters more than the monthly rate. To run your own numbers, use our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

Some managers handle the city filing for you. DFW Property Management lists "City Rental Registrations" as included in its top plan and a paid add-on in others. Ask who files and who pays.

## Must a Dallas property manager hold a TX license?

Yes, in most cases. Texas Occupations Code 1101.002 defines a broker as a person who, for a fee, leases real estate for others or "controls the acceptance or deposit of rent from a resident of a single-family residential real property unit." Section 1101.351 bars anyone without a Texas Real Estate Commission (TREC) license from acting as a broker or sales agent.

Two exceptions in Section 1101.005 matter: an on-site apartment manager, and an owner or the owner's employee who leases the owner's own property. A third-party manager of your single-family rental is neither.

Check the license before you compare fees. A low fee from an unlicensed manager is not a saving.

## Flat fee or percent: which model is common in Dallas?

Both models are common in Dallas. Of our 10 priced managers, 5 charge a percent of rent and 5 charge a flat monthly fee.

| Rent | 8% fee | 10% fee | $150 flat | $175 flat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1,500 | $120 | $150 | $150 | $175 |
| $1,807 | $144.56 | $180.70 | $150 | $175 |
| $2,500 | $200 | $250 | $150 | $175 |
| $3,000 | $240 | $300 | $150 | $175 |

The math is simple. At 10%, a flat $175 fee is cheaper once rent passes $1,750. At 8%, it is cheaper once rent passes $2,187.50. Dallas rents fell this year, which narrows the gap for a typical two-bedroom.

But compare the whole year, not only the monthly line. A flat-fee firm with a $1,500 placement fee and a percent firm at 50% leasing can end up close. Use the year-one example above with each manager's own numbers.

## What sections does a property management proposal need?

A property management proposal needs 7 sections, in the order an owner asks questions: "Do you get my situation? What do you do? What does it cost? Can I trust you? Why you? What happens next?"

## Copy-ready property management proposal template

Copy the template and fill in each bracket.

> **Proposal for [owner name]: management of [property address]**
> Prepared by [your name], [company], on [date]. Valid until [date].
>
> **1. Your property and goals**
>
> - Property: [type, units, bedrooms, year built]
> - Today: [vacant / occupied, current rent $amount, lease ends date]
> - What you told us matters most: [for example "steady rent and no calls at night"]
>
> **2. What we will do**
>
> - Leasing: [marketing, showings, screening under a written policy, lease signing]
> - Rent: [online collection, late rent steps]
> - Repairs: [24-hour line, vendor bids above $amount, your approval above $amount]
> - Reports: [monthly statement on the day, owner portal, year-end 1099]
> - Inspections: [move-in, move-out, and every number months]
>
> **3. Your fees** (see the fee table below)
>
> **4. Proof**
>
> - [Number of doors managed, average days to lease, renewal rate, with the period]
> - [2 or 3 real reviews, with the platform and date]
> - [One short owner story: problem, what we did, result, with consent]
>
> **5. Why us, not a cheaper manager**
>
> [3 short points with numbers. See the section below.]
>
> **6. Your first 30 days**
>
> - Day 1: agreement, documents, portal
> - Day 7: inspection and repair list
> - Day 14: [listing live / tenant handover]
> - Day 30: first statement and payout
>
> **7. Next step**
>
> [One action and one date, for example "Reply 'yes' by Friday and I will send the agreement to sign."]
>
> [Name, title, phone, email]

This is a generic template. Check that every fee and promise in it matches your management agreement.

## How do you present fees clearly?

Present every fee in one table, with its base and a yearly total for the owner's own rent. A headline percent alone hides the real cost. A yearly total shows it.

Example fee table for a $2,000 rent, one new tenant in year one, no renewal. The fees are an example.

| Fee | Base | Rate | Year one |
|---|---|---|---|
| Management fee | Rent collected | 8% | 8% × $2,000 × 11 months collected = $1,760 |
| Leasing fee | One month's rent | 70% | 70% × $2,000 = $1,400 |
| Setup fee | One time | $150 | $150 |
| Renewal fee | Per renewal | $200 | $0 in year one |
| Maintenance markup | Vendor invoices | None | $0 |
| **Total** | | | **$3,310** |
| **Effective rate** | Total ÷ rent collected | | $3,310 ÷ $22,000 = 15.0% |

Compare your rates with the market. In iPropertyManagement's 722-branch sample (updated 2026-09-23), the average management fee was 8.49% and the average leasing fee with management was 70.6% of one month's rent. All Property Management puts the usual monthly fee at 8% to 12% of rent.

State the fee base. All Property Management notes that contracts may charge on rental value, rent due or rent collected. Say which one you use. If it is rent collected, say why that helps the owner: you earn nothing on rent you do not collect.

Owners can also check your numbers with the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator). Invite them to.

## Which proof should you add?

Add proof the owner can check. Numbers with a period, reviews with a platform and date, and one short owner story with consent. Leave out anything you cannot back up.

| Proof | Strong version | Weak version |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio | "212 doors in Cook County, 2026-09" | "Hundreds of happy owners" |
| Leasing speed | "Median 19 days to lease, last 12 months" | "We lease fast" |
| Reviews | "4.8 on Google, 96 reviews, read 2026-10-19" | Quotes with no name or source |
| Owner story | Problem, action, result, owner's consent | "Our clients love us" |

The left-hand numbers are examples of the format, not real data. Use your own.

Follow the FTC's rule on reviews. It bans fake reviews, including AI-generated ones. It bans paying for reviews that must be positive or negative. It also bans insider reviews without a clear disclosure. The FTC can seek civil penalties against knowing violators.

Owners also look you up before they reply. We tested AI answers for a Chicago manager on 2026-10-16 (144 answers). The most cited sources were Yelp in 35 answers, Expertise.com in 22 and Thumbtack in 17 (our data). Make sure the reviews you quote match what owners find there. Disclosure: that manager, Fridman Properties, is owned by the CEO of MyRemotely, our partner.

## How do you answer "why you and not a cheaper manager"?

Answer with the total cost and the cost of problems, not with adjectives. A lower headline fee can cost an owner more if leasing is slow or tenants leave.

Use 3 points, each with a number:

1. **Show the total.** Put both managers' fees in the same table. A 6% fee with a 100% leasing fee and a markup can cost more than an 8% fee without them.
2. **Show the cost of vacancy.** One extra empty month on a $2,000 rent costs $2,000. That is more than a full year's difference between an 8% and a 6% fee: 2% × $2,000 × 12 = $480.
3. **Show what is included.** List the services the cheaper quote charges extra for, such as inspections or after-hours calls.

If the cheaper manager is a better fit, say so. An honest "we are not the right fit" builds referrals.

## How should the proposal end?

End with one next step, one date and your direct contact. Do not end with a summary. For example: "Reply 'yes' by Friday, October 23, and I will send the agreement the same day."

Attach the management agreement so the owner can read the real terms. Arizona's ARS 32-2173, for example, says the agreement must state the fees, the term, cancellation terms and report schedule. Your proposal should match it line by line.

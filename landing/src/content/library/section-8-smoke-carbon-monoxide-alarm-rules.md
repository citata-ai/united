## Where must smoke alarms go in a Section 8 unit?

Smoke alarms must go on each level of the unit and in or near each sleeping area. The rule is in federal law, 42 U.S.C. 1437f(o)(22). It covers every unit with tenant-based or project-based voucher assistance. The alarms must follow codes from the International Code Council or the National Fire Protection Association, and NFPA Standard 72.

The law says alarms go "in each level and in or near each sleeping area," "including in basements but excepting crawl spaces and unfinished attics," and "in each common area" of a project with an assisted unit.

HUD's NSPIRE standard, 24 CFR 5.703(d)(3), is more specific. A unit must have at least one working smoke detector:

1. **On each level** of the unit.
2. **Inside each bedroom.**
3. **Within 21 feet of any bedroom door,** measured along the path of travel.
4. **On the living area side of a door,** when a detector outside a bedroom is separated from the living area by that door.

5.703(d)(3)(iv) adds that following NFPA 72 satisfies these smoke detector rules.

## When is a carbon monoxide alarm required?

A carbon monoxide (CO) alarm is required when the 2018 International Fire Code calls for one. Federal law, 42 U.S.C. 1437f(o)(21), says each unit with voucher assistance "shall have carbon monoxide alarms or detectors installed" to meet or exceed chapters 9 and 11 of the 2018 IFC, or newer standards HUD adopts by Federal Register notice.

In practice, that means fuel and garages. LeadingAge's summary of HUD Notice H 2022-01 (January 31, 2022) says the rule applies to properties with "fire-fueled or fire-burning appliances or an attached garage." It also says HUD began enforcement on December 27, 2022, and that Housing Choice Voucher units are covered.

| Unit has | CO alarm needed? |
|---|---|
| Gas or oil furnace, boiler or water heater | Yes, under the IFC triggers |
| Gas range, gas dryer, or wood or gas fireplace | Yes, under the IFC triggers |
| Attached garage | Yes, under the IFC triggers |
| All-electric unit, no attached garage | Usually no; confirm with your PHA |

We could not read the IFC text itself or HUD's decision tree, because both sites blocked our requests. Ask your PHA for its CO placement rules before you buy alarms.

## Did the rules start before NSPIRE-V?

The smoke and CO rules started before full NSPIRE for vouchers. The legal notes to 42 U.S.C. 1437f give the dates:

| Rule | Law | Effective |
|---|---|---|
| CO alarms, 1437f(o)(21) | Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021, div. Q, sec. 101 | 2 years after 2020-12-27, so 2022-12-27 |
| Qualifying smoke alarms, 1437f(o)(22) | Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2023, div. AA, sec. 601 | 2 years after 2022-12-29, so 2024-12-29 |

Then HUD delayed NSPIRE itself. Its September 30, 2025 notice moved the voucher compliance date to February 1, 2027. The same notice reminds PHAs that the NSPIRE standards for CO devices and smoke alarms "still apply," because they carry out these two laws.

So the alarm rules apply in your next inspection, no matter which inspection standard your PHA uses.

## Hardwired or battery?

The answer depends on when the unit was built. 42 U.S.C. 1437f(o)(22)(B) defines a "qualifying smoke alarm":

| Unit | Qualifying smoke alarm |
|---|---|
| Built before 2022-12-29, and not substantially rehabbed after | Hardwired, or a 10-year non-rechargeable, nonreplaceable battery alarm that is sealed, tamper resistant and has a silencing means |
| Built or substantially rehabbed after 2022-12-29 | Hardwired |
| Any unit | Gives notice for people with hearing loss as NFPA 72 requires |

A worked example, with made-up prices, for an older 3-bedroom house with bedrooms on 1 level and a basement, with plain 9-volt alarms:

| Location | Needed | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 hall, near bedrooms | Smoke | 1 |
| Each of 3 bedrooms | Smoke | 3 |
| Basement | Smoke | 1 |
| Near sleeping area (gas furnace in the basement) | CO | 1 |
| Total | | 6 alarms |
| Cost at $35 per sealed 10-year smoke alarm, $40 per CO alarm (example) | | $215 |

Plain 9-volt replaceable-battery alarms do not qualify in this example. Swap them for sealed 10-year units, or for hardwired alarms.

## Checklist per room

- [ ] **Each bedroom:** one qualifying smoke alarm, tested.
- [ ] **Hall outside bedrooms:** smoke alarm within 21 feet of each bedroom door.
- [ ] **Each level, including the basement:** at least one smoke alarm.
- [ ] **Living area side of a closed hall door:** extra smoke alarm if a door splits the hall from the living area.
- [ ] **Near sleeping areas:** CO alarm if the unit has fuel-burning appliances or an attached garage.
- [ ] **Room with a fuel-burning appliance in it:** ask the PHA about an extra CO alarm.
- [ ] **Common areas** of a multi-unit property: smoke alarms in each, and one on each level.
- [ ] **Hearing-impaired resident:** alarms with a system designed for hearing loss.
- [ ] **Dates:** check the expiry date printed on each alarm.

Ask your PHA if it treats a missing or dead alarm as life-threatening. Under 24 CFR 982.404(a)(3), the owner must fix a life-threatening deficiency within 24 hours of notice. Test every alarm before each PHA inspection.

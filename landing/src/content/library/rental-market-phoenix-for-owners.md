## How much did HUD's fair market rent change in Phoenix for FY2027?

HUD's fair market rent for a 2-bedroom in Phoenix fell 5.7% for FY2027, from $1,839 to $1,734 a month. The drop is $105 a month, or $1,260 a year. Every bedroom size fell. The smallest drop was 4.6% (studio). The largest was 6.7% (3 and 4 bedrooms).

The FY2027 numbers took effect on 2026-10-01. Housing authorities use fair market rent to set voucher payment standards. A lower FMR can lower what a voucher covers when the City of Phoenix Housing Department updates its standards. For all bedroom sizes, see the [Phoenix fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/phoenix-fair-market-rent-2027).

What to do if you rent to a voucher holder: check the payment standard before each renewal. Do not assume the old number holds.

## Are Phoenix rents rising or falling?

Phoenix rents are flat. The Zillow Observed Rent Index (ZORI, a typical asking rent) for the Phoenix metro was $1,720 in September 2026. That is 1.0% above a year before, but 0.3% below three years before.

| Measure (Zillow ZORI, Sept 2026) | Phoenix metro | United States |
|---|---|---|
| Typical asking rent | $1,720 | $1,932 |
| Change in 1 year | +1.0% | +2.7% |
| Change in 3 years | −0.3% | +7.5% |

So a Phoenix owner who bought in 2023 with a plan for 3% rent growth a year is about 9% behind that plan. HUD's FMR fell more than market rents did. The two measures use different data and timing, so do not price a lease from the FMR table. Use 5 local comparable listings.

## What vacancy rate does Phoenix have?

Phoenix vacancy is falling. Marcus & Millichap's 2Q 2026 Phoenix multifamily report says the metro had its highest first-quarter absorption on record in early 2026. That drove year-over-year vacancy declines in every property class.

The same report puts this in a five-year frame:

| Apartment class | Vacancy change over the past five years |
|---|---|
| Class A (newest, highest end) | +100 basis points (+1.0 point) |
| Class B | +160 basis points (+1.6 points) |
| Class C (older, lower rent) | +300 basis points (+3.0 points) |

Source: Marcus & Millichap. The report says Phoenix had the seventh-fastest inventory growth among major markets over the past five years. Renters moved toward newer stock. The public part of the report does not give a single metro vacancy rate, so we do not quote one.

What this means for you: an older single-family home or a Class C unit competes harder for tenants. Fresh paint, clean systems and fast response matter more than in 2021.

## Which local rules affect Phoenix rental returns?

Arizona state law sets most Phoenix rental rules, and the biggest change helps owners. Here is what touches your returns.

| Rule | What it says | Effect on returns |
|---|---|---|
| No city rent tax (ARS 42-6004(H)) | From and after 2024-12-31, a city or town may not levy a transaction privilege tax on renting property for residential purposes | You no longer collect or pay a city rental tax on long-term rents |
| No rent control (ARS 33-1329) | Cities, including charter cities, and towns may not control rents on private residential property | Rents follow the market |
| Deposit cap (ARS 33-1321) | Security, including prepaid rent, may not exceed 1.5 months' rent | Limits your cash cushion |
| Assessor registration (ARS 33-1902) | Keep owner contact details on file with the county assessor, update within 10 days | A city may fine $150 per day after notice |

The tax rule does not apply to hotels, motels and other transient lodging. If you run a short-term rental, ask a tax professional how the change applies to you.

For a newly bought rental, ARS 33-1902 lets a city assess a $1,000 civil penalty plus $100 a month until you comply. A court must dismiss the complaint if you comply within 10 days of the notice.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Should I rent or sell my Phoenix home now?

Rent or sell in Phoenix depends on your cost base and your patience. The market gives you one good sign and one warning. The good sign: renter demand is at a record. The warning: three years of flat rents and heavy new supply.

A worked check at HUD's 2-bedroom rent of $1,734:

| Line | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Rent | $1,734 |
| Vacancy allowance (5%, our assumption) | −$87 |
| Management at 8% of collected rent | −$132 |
| Your mortgage, tax, insurance and HOA | Your number |
| Repairs reserve (your estimate) | Your number |

The 5% vacancy and 8% fee are our planning assumptions, not Phoenix data. Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to test other fee levels.

Checklist before you decide:

1. **Price the rent** from 5 local listings. Expect little growth in the next year.
2. **Check your class.** An older home competes with new Class A buildings. Budget for updates.
3. **Remove the city rent tax** from your old pro forma. ARS 42-6004(H) ended it from 2025.
4. **Compare with a sale.** Get a broker's price opinion. Compare after-tax proceeds with 5 years of net rent.
5. **Ask a tax professional** about depreciation recapture before you sell.

If net rent covers your costs today, a hold can work while supply settles. If it does not, flat rents will not fix the gap soon.

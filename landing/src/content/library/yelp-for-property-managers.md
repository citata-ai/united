## Should a property management company care about Yelp in 2026?

A property management company should care about Yelp because AI engines read it. On 2026-10-16 we ran 24 owner prompts for a Chicago manager in ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, 2 runs each. That gave 144 answers. Yelp was a source in 35 of them. Only manager sites themselves came up more often.

The client was Fridman Properties. Its owner is CEO of MyRemotely, our partner, so this is a related party. The full setup is in the [Fridman Properties case study](/case-studies/fridman-properties).

Owners also see Yelp in plain Google results. But the bigger change is quiet: an AI answer can quote your Yelp rating without the owner ever opening Yelp. So a wrong phone number or an old 2-star review on Yelp can reach owners you never meet.

## Why does Yelp filter some property management reviews?

Yelp filters reviews with automated recommendation software. Yelp says the software checks every review with "hundreds of signals of quality, reliability, and user activity". It recommends the reviews it judges most helpful and reliable.

Reviews that are not recommended stay on Yelp. You find them through a link at the bottom of the business page. They do not count toward the star rating or the review count. Yelp says no employee can override the software, and that a review can move between the two groups over time.

Solicited reviews are a main target. Yelp's "Don't Ask for Reviews" page says its software tries to find reviews that look requested and not recommend them. So the classic move, a mass email that says "please review us on Yelp", can backfire. Your happiest clients write reviews, and Yelp hides them.

Property managers serve 2 groups: owners and tenants. Both can review you on Yelp. You cannot change the mix by asking owners for Yelp reviews. You can answer each review in a calm, factual way.

## Does Yelp show up in AI answers about property managers?

Yelp shows up often in AI answers about local property managers. Besides our 35 of 144, Goodjuju Marketing reported in its fall 2026 study that ChatGPT's local answers lean on Yelp and Bing listings. Goodjuju told readers to keep both listings current.

Our baseline also showed other directories: Expertise.com in 22 answers and Thumbtack in 17. In a wider test (H03, our data), Expertise.com was a source in 93 of 150 answers. The lesson is the same for each one. AI engines trust third-party pages to describe you. Check what those pages say.

## Are Yelp Ads worth it for owner leads?

Yelp Ads are a small test for most property managers, not a main channel. Here are the facts from Yelp's own pages, read 2026-10-19:

| Yelp product | Price | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| Yelp business page | Free | None |
| Yelp Ads | From $150 per month | Cancel anytime, minimums may change |
| Upgrade Package | $180 per month | Cancel anytime |
| Yelp Ads + Upgrade Package | From $270 per month | Cancel anytime |

Yelp says ads show in "Sponsored Results" in Yelp search and can show on competitor pages. Yelp says advertising has no effect on your reviews.

Now compare the channel. Goodjuju's fall 2026 study of 35 PM companies found referral traffic, which includes directories and partner sites, gave 3.7% of owner leads. Google Search and the Map Pack gave 50.6%. Yelp is a small part of that 3.7%.

A worked test, with your own numbers:

1. **Set a budget.** For example, $150 per month for 3 months = $450.
2. **Set a target.** Say one new owner pays you $1,500 a year in fees. Then 1 signed owner pays back the $450 test about 3.3 times.
3. **Track the source.** Ask every lead "How did you first hear about us?" and log "Yelp" as an option.
4. **Decide at day 90.** No signed owner and few owner calls means stop. Most calls from tenants means stop.

The $1,500 is an example, not a benchmark. Use your real fee per door from the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## How do I handle a bad Yelp page?

Fix the facts first, then answer the reviews. Use this checklist:

- **Claim the page.** The Yelp business page is free.
- **Match your facts.** Name, address, phone, hours and website must match your Google profile and site.
- **Pick the right category.** Make sure Yelp lists you as a property management business.
- **Reply to every negative review.** Keep it short, factual and polite. Never share a tenant's private data.
- **Separate tenant and owner issues.** Say what you do for owners in the page description.
- **Do not buy, swap or bribe for reviews.** Yelp runs Consumer Alerts. In 2025 it posted 491 alerts for compensated or suspicious review activity, per its Trust & Safety Report.
- **Do not threaten reviewers.** Yelp posted 6 Questionable Legal Threat Alerts in 2025.

Then put your effort where owners choose. For most managers that is the Google profile and your own site. Our guide on [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping) explains where that search is moving.

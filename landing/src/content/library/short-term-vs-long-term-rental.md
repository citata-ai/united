## Short-term vs long-term at a glance

A short-term rental (STR) rents by the night or week. A long-term rental (LTR) rents on a lease, usually 12 months.

| Factor | Short-term rental | Long-term rental |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Income pattern | Varies by night and season | Fixed monthly rent |
| Platform fee | Airbnb host-only fee: most hosts 15.5% (Airbnb) | None; listing sites are often free or low cost |
| Cleaning | Every stay | At move-out |
| Furniture and supplies | Owner pays | Tenant brings their own |
| Utilities and internet | Owner pays | Often tenant pays |
| Owner hours | High | Low |
| Local rules | Permits, registration, bans in some cities | General landlord-tenant law |
| Tax rules | Can differ (7-day rule, personal use) | Standard Schedule E rental |

## How do gross income and occupancy compare?

Gross income is often higher for a short-term rental, because nightly rates are higher than a monthly rent divided by 30. Occupancy is lower, because nights go unbooked between stays and in slow seasons.

Gross STR income = nightly rate x booked nights

Gross LTR income = monthly rent x months leased

Worked comparison for one 2-bedroom home. All rates, occupancy and costs are made-up numbers. Use local data, such as booked comps on the platform and rent comps for the lease.

| Line | Short-term (per month) | Long-term (per month) |
|------|------------------------|-----------------------|
| Rate | $180/night | $2,000/month |
| Occupancy | 60% (18 nights) | 93% (vacancy 7%) |
| Gross income | $3,240 | $1,860 |

Why 7% vacancy for the lease: the US rental vacancy rate was 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026 (Census Bureau). A short-term host has no such national benchmark that fits every market. Pull booked nights for homes like yours.

On gross income alone, the short-term rental wins by $1,380 a month in this example. Costs change the picture.

## How do costs compare (cleaning, supplies, management)?

Costs are much higher for a short-term rental. Many of them repeat with every stay.

Continuing the example, with made-up costs:

| Cost | Short-term (per month) | Long-term (per month) |
|------|------------------------|-----------------------|
| Platform fee (15.5% host-only, Airbnb) | −$502 | $0 |
| Cleaning (9 stays x $90) | −$810 | $0 |
| Supplies and restock | −$120 | $0 |
| Utilities and internet | −$250 | $0 (tenant pays) |
| Furniture wear and replacement reserve | −$150 | $0 |
| Management (STR co-host 20%, LTR 8.49%) | −$648 | −$158 |
| Repairs | −$100 | −$100 |
| Net before tax, insurance and loan | $660 | $1,602 |

In this example, the lease nets more. Your result may differ. Change the occupancy, the cleaning cost and the management line, and the winner can flip.

Notes on the numbers:

- **Platform fee.** Airbnb says most hosts on the split-fee structure pay 3%, with the guest paying a separate fee. Under the single host-only fee, most hosts pay 15.5%, and others typically pay 14% to 16%. Airbnb says the split fee is being phased out.
- **Long-term management.** iPropertyManagement's 2026 survey found an average of 8.49% of rent.
- **Short-term management.** STR co-host fees are set as a share of revenue too. Get local quotes. The 20% above is a made-up example.
- **Insurance.** A short-term rental may need a different policy than a long-term rental. Ask your insurer.

## How much more time does an STR take?

A short-term rental takes far more owner time, because each stay repeats the work of a whole tenancy.

Per-stay tasks:

1. Answer inquiries and approve the booking.
2. Send check-in details.
3. Answer questions during the stay.
4. Schedule and check the clean.
5. Restock supplies.
6. Inspect for damage.
7. Review the guest.

At 9 stays a month, that is 9 rounds of the list above. A long-term lease does those steps about once every 1 or 2 years.

If you hire a co-host or STR manager, they do most of this, for a larger fee than a long-term manager.

## How do local rules and taxes differ?

Local rules decide whether a short-term rental is legal at all. Tax rules can also change for short stays.

Local rules, one example: New York City adopted Local Law 18 on January 9, 2022. The NYC Office of Special Enforcement says it requires short-term rental hosts to register. It also bars booking platforms such as Airbnb and VRBO from processing transactions for unregistered short-term rentals. Rentals of 30 consecutive days or more are exempt from registration.

Other cities set permits, night caps, owner-occupancy rules or zone bans. Check your city's code and your HOA rules before you buy furniture.

Tax rules from the IRS:

| Rule | What it says | Source |
|------|--------------|--------|
| 7-day rule | An activity is not a "rental activity" for passive loss rules if the average customer stay is 7 days or less | IRS Pub 925 |
| 30-day rule | Same, if the average stay is 30 days or less and you provide significant personal services | IRS Pub 925 |
| Personal use | Using the home more than the greater of 14 days or 10% of rented days makes it a residence, and limits deductions | IRS Topic 415 |
| Under 15 days rented | If you use it as a residence and rent it fewer than 15 days, do not report the rent or deduct rental expenses | IRS Topic 415 |

Check if your city, county or state charges a lodging or occupancy tax on short stays, and whether the platform collects it for you.

This is general information, not tax or legal advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional or a local attorney.

## How to decide: a 5-step test

1. **Check the rules.** Is a short-term rental legal at your address? If not, stop here.
2. **Pull comps for both.** Booked nights and rates for STR; rents for LTR.
3. **Build both net income tables** like the one above.
4. **Count your hours** per month for each option.
5. **Divide the extra net income by the extra hours.** If the hourly gain is too low, the lease wins.

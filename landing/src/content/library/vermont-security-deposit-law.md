A Vermont tenant hands back the keys on a Friday. The landlord now has 14 days to send the deposit and an itemized list. Miss that window and the landlord loses the right to keep any of it. ## What Is the Maximum Security Deposit in Vermont?

Vermont law sets no maximum security deposit. 9 V.S.A. 4461 defines a deposit and controls its return, but it has no cap on the amount.

The definition is broad. Under 4461(a), a security deposit is "any advance, deposit, or prepaid rent, however named," that is refundable at the end of the tenancy. So a "cleaning deposit" or "last month's rent" that you must refund falls under the same rules.

A related rule: you may not charge an application fee to rent a home in Vermont (9 V.S.A. 4456a(a)). Do not move that charge into the deposit.

## How Many Days Does a Vermont Landlord Have to Return a Deposit?

A Vermont landlord has 14 days to return the deposit and the itemized statement (9 V.S.A. 4461(c)). The clock starts on one of two dates:

- the date you discover that the tenant vacated or abandoned the unit; or
- the date the tenant vacated, if the tenant gave you notice of that date.

A seasonal rental that is not the tenant's primary residence gets 60 days.

You must hand-deliver or mail the statement and any payment to the tenant's last known address (4461(d)). Keep proof of the date you mailed it.

Worked dates: a tenant gives notice and moves out on May 1. Day 14 is May 15. Mail the refund and the list no later than May 15.

## What Can a Landlord Deduct From a Security Deposit in Vermont?

A Vermont landlord may keep all or part of the deposit for 4 reasons, listed in 9 V.S.A. 4461(b):

1. **Unpaid rent.**
2. **Damage to the landlord's property**, unless it is normal wear and tear or the result of events beyond the tenant's control.
3. **Unpaid utility or other charges** that the tenant had to pay to the landlord or to a utility.
4. **Removal of abandoned items** that the tenant left in the unit.

"Normal wear and tear" has a definition in 9 V.S.A. 4451(5). It is deterioration from the reasonable use of the unit, "without negligence, carelessness, accident, or abuse" by the tenant, the household or guests. Faded paint from years of sun is wear. A hole in a door is damage.

For property the tenant leaves behind, see 9 V.S.A. 4462. If the tenant gave actual notice of moving out, or left at the end of the lease, you may dispose of what remains without notice (4462(d)). If the tenant abandoned the unit, you must store the property and mail a 60-day notice first (4462(c)).

## Worked Example: Building the Itemized Statement

Here is a sample statement for a $1,800 deposit. The numbers are an example, not a rate.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Security deposit held | $1,800.00 |
| Unpaid rent, last 10 days of the final month | − $500.00 |
| Replace a broken interior door (receipt attached) | − $240.00 |
| Unpaid water bill the tenant had to pay you | − $85.00 |
| Haul away a couch the tenant left | − $120.00 |
| Repaint after 4 years (normal wear and tear) | $0.00 |
| Refund due to the tenant | $855.00 |

Each line names a reason from 4461(b). The repaint line shows $0 because wear and tear is not a deduction. Send the statement and the $855 together, within 14 days.

## Does a Vermont Landlord Have to Give an Itemized List of Deductions?

A Vermont landlord must give a written statement that itemizes every deduction (9 V.S.A. 4461(c)). The statement goes with the refund, in the same 14-day window.

Use this checklist for each move-out:

- [ ] Write down the move-out date and how you learned of it.
- [ ] Mark day 14 (or day 60 for a seasonal unit) on your calendar.
- [ ] Walk the unit and take dated photos.
- [ ] Sort each issue: damage or normal wear and tear.
- [ ] List each deduction with its reason and amount.
- [ ] Attach receipts or estimates where you have them.
- [ ] Mail or hand-deliver the statement and the refund to the last known address.
- [ ] Keep a copy and proof of mailing.

## Must a Vermont Landlord Pay Interest or Keep the Deposit in a Separate Account?

Section 4461 has no statewide interest rule and no separate-account rule. The text sets the return, deduction and transfer rules only.

Towns can add rules. Under 9 V.S.A. 4461(g), a town or municipality may adopt a security deposit ordinance that adds to the state minimum. The ordinance:

- may authorize the payment of interest on a deposit;
- may not limit how a deposit is held;
- may let a local housing board of review hear deposit disputes, with review under 24 V.S.A. 5006.

We did not verify any city ordinance for this page. If your unit is in Burlington or another city with a housing code, check the local code for interest and dispute rules.

When you sell the building, the deposit goes with it. The new landlord must give the tenant actual notice of the new landlord's name and address, with a statement that the deposit moved (4461(f)).

## What Is the Penalty for a Vermont Landlord Who Keeps a Deposit Wrongly?

A Vermont landlord who misses the 14-day deadline forfeits the right to keep any part of the deposit (9 V.S.A. 4461(e)). That applies even if the tenant caused real damage.

If the failure is willful, the penalty grows. The landlord is liable for double the amount wrongfully withheld, plus reasonable attorney's fees and costs (4461(e)).

Worked example: you keep $600 of a deposit with no statement, on purpose. You owe the $600 back, plus $600 more, for $1,200. Then add the tenant's attorney's fees and costs.

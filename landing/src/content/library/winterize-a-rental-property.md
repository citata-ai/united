## What does winterizing a rental include?

Winterizing a rental means 4 jobs before the first hard freeze: protect the pipes, service the heat, plan for snow and ice, and brief the tenants. Most of the work is cheap. A burst pipe or a dead furnace in January is not.

Use this table to plan the work. The timing is a suggestion, not a rule.

| Task | Who does it | When |
|---|---|---|
| Drain and shut off outdoor hose bibs | Owner or vendor | Before the first freeze |
| Insulate pipes in unheated areas | Owner or vendor | Before the first freeze |
| Inspect, clean and tune the heating system | Licensed HVAC tech | Before the heat season |
| Test smoke and CO alarms | Owner or vendor | Before the heat season |
| Book snow removal or confirm the lease duty | Owner | Before the first snow |
| Send the tenant winter notice | Owner or manager | Before the first freeze |
| Set vacant units to 55°F or higher | Owner | All winter |

## How do I prevent frozen pipes?

You prevent frozen pipes by draining outdoor lines, insulating exposed pipes and keeping heat steady. The American Red Cross names the pipes that freeze most often: outdoor hose bibs, pool supply lines and sprinkler lines, pipes in unheated basements, crawl spaces, attics and garages, and pipes on exterior walls with little insulation.

Before cold weather, the Red Cross advises these steps:

1. **Drain pool and sprinkler supply lines.** Follow the maker's directions.
2. **Remove and store outdoor hoses.**
3. **Close the inside valve** that feeds each outdoor hose bib.
4. **Open the outside hose bib** to let water drain. Leave it open.
5. **Add insulation** to attics, basements and crawl spaces.
6. **Insulate exposed pipes** with pipe sleeves or UL-listed heat tape or heat cable.

During a cold snap, the Red Cross advises:

- Keep garage doors closed if water lines run through the garage.
- Open kitchen and bathroom cabinet doors so warm air reaches the plumbing.
- Let cold water drip from faucets on exposed pipes. A trickle helps prevent a freeze.
- Keep the thermostat at the same setting day and night.

If a pipe does freeze, keep the faucet open and apply heat with a heating pad, a hair dryer or towels soaked in hot water. The Red Cross says never use a blowtorch, a kerosene or propane heater, or any open flame. If you cannot find or reach the frozen part, call a licensed plumber.

Show every tenant where the main water shutoff is. A tenant who can close the main in 2 minutes can save you a ceiling.

## What heat must a landlord provide in winter?

The heat you must provide depends on your city and state. No single national rule applies. Two large cities show the range.

| City | Heat season | Day | Night |
|---|---|---|---|
| New York City | October 1 to May 31 | 68°F, 6 a.m. to 10 p.m., when outside is below 55°F | 62°F, 10 p.m. to 6 a.m., at any outside temperature |
| Chicago | September 15 to June 1 | 68°F, 8:30 a.m. to 10:30 p.m. | 66°F, 10:30 p.m. to 8:30 a.m. |

New York City HPD also requires hot water at a constant minimum of 120°F, 365 days a year. Chicago's 2019 notice says landlords face fines of up to $500 per day, per violation, and that the reason for the lack of heat does not matter.

Check your local housing code for the exact numbers. Many cities and states have their own.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I service the heating system and alarms?

Book a heating service once a year, before the heat season. The EPA advises having a trained professional "inspect, clean and tune-up central heating system (furnaces, flues and chimneys) annually". The EPA also advises an annual check of other fuel-burning appliances, such as water heaters, stoves, fireplaces, clothes dryers and space heaters.

Ask the technician to check these items:

- [ ] Burner, heat exchanger and flue
- [ ] Air filter, replaced
- [ ] Thermostat, tested
- [ ] Chimney and vent, clear
- [ ] Water heater, checked for leaks

The EPA page quotes CPSC: every home should have carbon monoxide (CO) alarms on each level, outside each sleeping area. Test each alarm on the same visit.

## What should a vacant unit get in winter?

A vacant unit needs heat, a water plan and regular checks. An empty unit has no one to notice a drip or a cold room.

1. **Keep the heat on.** The Red Cross advises a setting no lower than 55°F when you are away in cold weather.
2. **Open cabinet doors** under sinks so warm air reaches the pipes.
3. **Shut off and drain** if the unit will stay empty for a long time. Ask a plumber to do it. Drain the water heater only if the plumber advises it.
4. **Visit on a schedule.** Check the thermostat, the ceilings and the pipes after each cold snap.
5. **Read your insurance policy.** Many policies change coverage for a vacant building. Ask your agent what the policy requires.

Log each visit with the date, the inside temperature and what you saw. The log helps you if you file a claim.

## Who handles snow removal?

Snow removal belongs to the person the lease and local law name. In a single-family rental, many leases give the walk and driveway to the tenant. In a multi-unit building, the owner usually keeps the common areas. Local law can override the lease for the public sidewalk.

New York City is a clear example. The Department of Sanitation says property owners must clear a path at least 48 inches wide, without pushing snow into the street. The deadline depends on when the snow stops:

| Snow stops between | Time to clear |
|---|---|
| 7:00 a.m. and 4:59 p.m. | 4 hours |
| 5:00 p.m. and 8:59 p.m. | 14 hours |
| 9:00 p.m. and 6:59 a.m. | Until 11:00 a.m. the next day |

Fines start at $150 for the first offense and reach $350 by the third (NYC DSNY). Other cities set other rules. Check your city code and write the duty into the lease.

## What do I tell tenants before a freeze?

Send each tenant a short winter notice before the first freeze. Put it in writing so you have a record.

The notice should say:

- **Where the main water shutoff is,** with a photo.
- **Keep the heat on,** at a set minimum, even when away.
- **Open cabinet doors** under sinks on very cold nights.
- **Let a faucet drip** on very cold nights if pipes run on outside walls.
- **Report no heat, a cold room or a drip at once,** with the phone number to call.
- **Who clears snow** from walks, steps and the driveway.
- **Do not use an oven or a grill to heat the unit.**

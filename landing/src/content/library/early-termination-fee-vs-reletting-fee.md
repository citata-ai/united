## What is a reletting fee?

A reletting fee covers the work or cost of finding a replacement renter. The label alone does not release the departing tenant. Read the lease and the proposed exit agreement together. State which costs the payment covers and which duties remain.

A flat early termination fee serves a different aim. The parties agree on an exit price and the scope of release. A clear agreement states the surrender date, final rent date and deposit process. Do not call a fee a buyout if future rent keeps accruing without a defined limit.

| Issue | Flat termination option | Actual reletting costs |
|---|---|---|
| Main purpose | Defined contractual exit | Recovery of allowed replacement costs |
| Records | Signed release and payment record | Invoices, marketing and replacement lease |
| Future rent | Depends on release and law | Depends on remaining duties and mitigation |
| Main risk | Invalid penalty or unclear release | Duplicate or unsupported charges |
| Tenant question | What ends when I pay? | Which costs did you actually incur? |

Treat the table as a drafting tool. It does not establish permission to charge either fee in your state.

## How much is a typical early termination fee?

There is no national standard you can safely insert into every lease. Use verified local rules rather than a market average from a blog.

Florida Statutes section 83.595 offers an optional agreed remedy capped at 2 months' rent. The statute requires acceptance in a separate addendum when the rental agreement is made. For that early termination option, required notice cannot exceed 60 days. The landlord waives additional rent beyond the month of retaking possession. The section separately addresses accrued charges and property damage. [Read the Florida statute](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0083/Sections/0083.595.html).

Texas uses a different framework. The Texas State Law Library explains the landlord's duty to reduce losses under Property Code section 91.006. Its guide also separates reletting fees from the rent that can remain due. A Florida addendum does not settle a Texas lease dispute. [Read the Texas guide](https://guides.sll.texas.gov/landlord-tenant-law/ending-the-lease).

Ask local counsel to review the actual words you plan to use. A fee that looks common can still fail under the rule that applies to your property.

## Which option is easier to enforce?

The easier option is the lawful one you can explain from signed terms and clear records. A short fee clause with no release date can create more work than an itemized claim.

For a flat option, write down the event that activates it. State how the tenant elects it, how notice works and what happens if payment fails. Include the scope of release. Make sure the exit terms fit the existing lease.

For actual costs, build a file as you work. Keep the listing date, asking rent, inquiries, showing notes and invoices. Record when the new tenant gains possession. Explain any work that improved the property rather than merely restored it.

Separate costs by purpose. A leasing commission, cleaning invoice and unpaid rent balance are not interchangeable labels. An owner statement should let a reviewer see each charge without guessing.

Use a consistent approval process across properties. Let staff flag protected termination requests before they quote any fee. The federal SCRA bars early termination charges for a qualifying military termination under 50 USC 3955. [Read the federal provision](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/50/3955).

## Can I charge both fees?

Do not charge both merely because the lease lists both names. Have counsel check whether they cover distinct losses and fit the chosen remedy.

Start with a charge map. Put every proposed amount beside its purpose and supporting document. Mark any overlap. If a flat payment already covers replacement work, a second charge for that work needs careful review.

Do the same with rent. Show the vacancy period, any replacement receipts and any credits. Avoid an account that treats the unit as occupied by the new tenant while billing the old tenant for the same covered loss.

A tenant's request may involve more than a voluntary move. Ask whether a protected termination rule applies before choosing a remedy. Do not require the tenant to accept an ordinary buyout when the law supplies another route.

Explain the result in plain words. The tenant should understand the balance, the release and the next step. A long list of fee names does not supply that clarity.

## How should I close the account?

Close the account with a written reconciliation that another person can reproduce. Keep the exit agreement separate from the deposit statement, then cross-reference them.

Use this recommended workflow:

- **Confirm the request.** Record the proposed move date and reason without promising a fee.
- **Review the law.** Check protected exits, local limits and the lease terms.
- **Select the remedy.** State whether the tenant receives a release or remains liable for allowed losses.
- **Record possession.** Save the key return and inspection notes.
- **Track replacement work.** Preserve invoices, listing records and credits.
- **Explain the balance.** Show each allowed charge and payment without double counting.

Before offering a fee discount, review the real cost of vacancy and leasing work. The [management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) helps you plan the management budget. It does not decide which costs a tenant legally owes.

If staff cannot explain the final balance from the file, pause the demand. Resolve the missing proof before sending a collection message. Clear exit terms help both parties plan the move.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

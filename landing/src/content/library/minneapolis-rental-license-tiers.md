## Who needs a Minneapolis rental license?

Every Minneapolis rental property must have a license. The city's rule is simple: you need a rental license for any dwelling unit where the owner does not live in the unit.

| Situation | License needed? |
|---|---|
| Owner does not live in the unit | Yes |
| No rent is paid | Yes |
| A relative lives in the unit, including a relative homestead | Yes |
| Vacant unit advertised for rent | Yes |
| Foreclosed property, until the redemption date | Yes |
| Rentals of 30 days or less | Most need a short-term rental license instead |
| Roommates in a unit the owner owns and lives in | No |
| A family member lives with the owner in the same unit | No |

Source: City of Minneapolis, "Rental license rules and regulations." Licenses are not transferable. Fees are due by March 1 each year.

Two more rules catch new owners:

- **Buying a rental:** apply for a license within 60 days of closing. If you miss it, the administrative fee is $250 for the first unit plus $20 for each extra unit.
- **Living far away:** you need a local contact if you live more than 60 miles from the property or outside the listed nearby counties.

## How does Minneapolis set tier 1, 2 or 3?

Minneapolis sets each rental building's tier from a points score. The city looks at the last two years of records and scores 11 items on property condition and property management.

| Total score | Tier | What it means | Routine inspection |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 19 | Tier 1 | Presumed well-maintained, meets housing code | Every 8 years |
| 0 to 19, no routine inspection in over 5 years | Tier 1D (by default) | Same as Tier 1 | Every 8 years |
| 20 to 39 | Tier 2 | Well-maintained, but one or more issues may affect renter safety | Every 5 years |
| 40 and over | Tier 3 | Not presumed well-maintained, several urgent issues | Every year |

Source: City of Minneapolis, "Rental license tiers."

What raises your score:

- **Property condition:** issues reported to the city and found in inspections. Violations that affect renters most get more points.
- **Property management:** how long you take to meet standards. Repeat inspections, late payments and citations add points.

The management score affects your fee level but not your inspection cycle. So a fast, clean fix keeps both numbers low.

You can appeal. Pay the license fee first, then ask Regulatory Services for an administrative review and say what is wrong. The tier changes only if the city made a mistake.

Watch for: the city plans to update tiering in January 2028 and is collecting feedback now.

## How much does a Minneapolis rental license cost?

A Minneapolis rental license costs a building fee plus a fee per unit, both set by tier. Some properties also pay a supplemental fee set by the management score.

| Fee (2026 schedule) | 1 to 3 units | 4 or more units |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1: building + per unit | $98 + $41 | $167 + $12 |
| Tier 2: building + per unit | $105 + $75 | $197 + $29 |
| Tier 3: building + per unit | $121 + $190 | $231 + $98 |
| Supplemental fee, Fee Level 2 | $121 | $347 |
| Supplemental fee, Fee Level 3 | $243 | $578 |

Source: City of Minneapolis, "Rental license fees." Fee Level 1 has no supplemental fee.

Worked examples:

| Property | Calculation | Yearly fee |
|---|---|---|
| Single-family home, Tier 1, Fee Level 1 | $98 + $41 | $139 |
| Duplex, Tier 2, Fee Level 2 (the city's own example) | $105 + 2 x $75 + $121 | $376 |
| Single-family home, Tier 3, Fee Level 3 | $121 + $190 + $243 | $554 |
| 10-unit building, Tier 1, Fee Level 1 | $167 + 10 x $12 | $287 |
| 10-unit building, Tier 3, Fee Level 3 | $231 + 10 x $98 + $578 | $1,789 |

Other fees on the same page: renewals after March 15 pay a 25% late fee. A unit that becomes a rental needs a $1,000 conversion inspection, and an owner change triggers a $450 inspection, with some exemptions. If you apply between September 1 and February 28, the fee is prorated by 50%.

For scale, HUD's FY2027 fair market rent for a 2-bedroom in the Minneapolis-St. Paul area is $1,734 a month. A Tier 3 single-family license at $554 equals about a third of one month's rent. See the [Minneapolis-St. Paul fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/minneapolis-st-paul-fair-market-rent-2027).

## What can cause a license revocation?

A Minneapolis rental license can be revoked when the property fails the city's licensing standards. A 2006 Minnesota Court of Appeals opinion quotes Minneapolis Code section 244.1910. Failure to meet its standards is adequate grounds to deny, refuse to renew, revoke or suspend a license. Court cases name examples such as over-occupancy.

Getting a license back is slow and costly. The city's reinstatement page lists the steps:

1. **Wait three years** before you reapply. A new owner does not wait.
2. **Pay** all taxes, citations and special assessments, plus a reinstatement fee: $3,000 if you owned part of the property at revocation, or $1,000 for a new owner.
3. **File** a rental property management plan.
4. **Pass** a rental license inspection, or show a recent certificate of code compliance.
5. **Attend** a rental property owner workshop within one year of reapplying.
6. **Get City Council approval.** You cannot rent until the new license is issued.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What do Minneapolis tenant notice rules require?

Minneapolis requires owners to share set information at set times. The city's renter notifications page lists them.

- **Before the lease is signed:** the name and physical address of a real person (owner or manager) for legal papers. Add the tier status, related and open code violations, and a waste summary. Lead paint disclosure also applies. If you skip this, the renter can end the lease with short notice.
- **Within 90 days after the lease starts:** a disclosure of renter rights under city, state and federal law.
- **Before an eviction for non-payment:** a pre-eviction notice with the amount due, how to pay and when fees are due. Then wait 30 days.
- **Always:** post the rental license and a Who to Call poster near the main entrance.
- **Before selling some affordable housing:** advance notice of sale to the city and renters.

Screening has rules too. The city's renter screening page offers two paths: follow its inclusive screening criteria, or screen more strictly but review each case individually. Under inclusive criteria, you may ask for monthly income of at least 3 times the rent. But you must make an exception for an applicant who earned less and still paid on time. You may not consider a credit score. The city also says owners must accept renters who use public assistance.

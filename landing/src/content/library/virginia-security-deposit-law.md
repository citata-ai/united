Virginia deposit rules sit in one section: Va. Code § 55.1-1226, part of the Virginia Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (VRLTA). We read the current text on the official Code of Virginia site on 2026-10-19. Here are the limits and deadlines.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | 2 months' periodic rent | Va. Code § 55.1-1226(A) |
| Move-in damage report | Within 5 days after occupancy | § 55.1-1214(A) |
| Notice of right to attend move-out inspection | Within 5 days of the tenant's notice to vacate | § 55.1-1226(G) |
| Move-out inspection, if the tenant asks | Within 72 hours of delivery of possession | § 55.1-1226(G) |
| Itemized list and refund | Within 45 days after the tenancy ends or the tenant vacates, whichever is later | § 55.1-1226(A) |
| Extra time for contractor repairs above the deposit | 15 more days, with notice in the 45 days | § 55.1-1226(E) |
| Deductions during the tenancy | Written notice within 30 days | § 55.1-1226(E) |
| Records of deductions | Keep 2 years | § 55.1-1226(F) |
| No forwarding address | Hold 1 year after the 45 days, then send to the State Treasurer | § 55.1-1226(B) |
| Penalty for willful breach | Deposit, actual damages, attorney fees | § 55.1-1226(E) |

## What is the maximum security deposit in Virginia?

The maximum security deposit in Virginia is 2 months' periodic rent. § 55.1-1226(A) says no landlord may "demand or receive a security deposit, however denominated," above that amount.

"However denominated" matters. § 55.1-1200 defines a security deposit as any refundable deposit, including a pet deposit. So a refundable pet deposit counts toward the 2-month cap.

Worked example: rent is $1,600 a month. The cap is $3,200. If you take a $2,800 deposit, you can add no more than $400 as a refundable pet deposit.

A tenant may offer damage insurance instead of a deposit, if you allow it. The policy must meet 5 conditions in § 55.1-1226(I). For example, the insurer must be licensed or approved by the Virginia State Corporation Commission. The tenant can later switch to paying the full deposit without your consent (§ 55.1-1226(J)).

## How many days does a Virginia landlord have to return a deposit?

A Virginia landlord has 45 days. The clock starts on the date the tenancy ends or the date the tenant vacates, whichever comes later (§ 55.1-1226(A)).

If damage exceeds the deposit and needs a third-party contractor, you get 15 more days. You must send written notice of that fact within the 45 days (§ 55.1-1226(E)).

Utility bills have their own rule. You may hold back a reasonable part of the deposit for an unpaid water, sewer or other utility bill in the tenant's name. You must give the tenant prior written notice of this right, for example in the termination notice. After the bill is paid, you send written confirmation and the balance within 10 days (§ 55.1-1226(C)).

With more than one tenant on the lease, send one check payable to all tenants. Mail it to a forwarding address that one tenant gives you (§ 55.1-1226(B)).

## What can a landlord deduct from a deposit in Virginia?

A Virginia landlord may apply the deposit only to 4 things (§ 55.1-1226(A)):

1. **Accrued rent,** including reasonable late charges in the lease.
2. **Damage** from the tenant's breach of § 55.1-1227 duties, less reasonable wear and tear.
3. **Other damages or charges** in the rental agreement.
4. **Actual damages** for breach of the lease under § 55.1-1251, such as rent lost after an early move-out.

The tenant cannot demand that you apply the deposit to unpaid rent before move-out (§ 55.1-1226(C)). A court may also give you a judgment for rent and possession without a deposit credit (§ 55.1-1251).

Your evidence starts at move-in. Under § 55.1-1214, you give the tenant a written report of existing damage within 5 days after occupancy. The report is correct unless the tenant objects in writing within 5 days.

## Does a Virginia landlord have to give an itemized list?

Yes. § 55.1-1226(A) requires a written notice that itemizes the deposit and every deduction, damage and charge. Send it with any refund within the 45 days.

Use this move-out checklist:

- [ ] Within 5 days of the tenant's notice to vacate, tell the tenant of the right to attend the inspection.
- [ ] If the tenant asks in writing, set the inspection within 72 hours of move-out.
- [ ] Compare the unit with the move-in report.
- [ ] Take photos with dates.
- [ ] Subtract reasonable wear and tear.
- [ ] Itemize each deduction with the cost.
- [ ] Send the list and refund within 45 days.
- [ ] Keep the record for 2 years.

You must also give written notice within 30 days of any deduction you make during the tenancy (§ 55.1-1226(E)). A tenant or the tenant's attorney may inspect your deduction records during business hours (§ 55.1-1226(F)).

## Must a Virginia landlord pay interest or use a separate account?

The current text of § 55.1-1226 does not require interest on a deposit. It also does not require a separate bank account.

One exception applies to real estate licensees. If you or your managing agent hold a Virginia real estate license, § 55.1-1226(B) links to § 54.1-2108. That section bars a licensee from diverting or misusing escrow funds. Ask your broker how your deposits are held.

If you sell the building, the new owner must return deposits that the tenant is owed. This applies even if the old owner did not transfer the money (§ 55.1-1226(E)).

## What is the penalty for keeping a deposit wrongly in Virginia?

If a Virginia landlord willfully fails to comply with § 55.1-1226, the court must order the deposit returned. The tenant also gets actual damages and reasonable attorney fees (§ 55.1-1226(E)).

There is one limit. If the tenant owes rent, the court credits an amount equal to the deposit against that rent instead.

The statute names no multiple of the deposit as a penalty. The risk is the full deposit plus the tenant's attorney fees. A late or vague list can cost more than the repair you wanted to charge.

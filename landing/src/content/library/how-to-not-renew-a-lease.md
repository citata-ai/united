## Do I need a reason to not renew a lease?

In many states, a landlord needs no reason to let a fixed lease end. But a growing group of states and cities limit non-renewal. Check your state law before you write the letter.

Two examples:

- **Washington.** RCW 59.18.650 says a landlord "may not evict a tenant, refuse to continue a tenancy, or end a periodic tenancy except for the causes" the law lists. A no-reason non-renewal is not one of them in most cases.
- **Oregon.** ORS 90.427 lets a landlord end a tenancy without cause during the first year of occupancy with at least 30 days' written notice. After the first year, the landlord may end it only for a tenant cause or a "qualifying landlord reason", with the notice the law sets.

Some cities add their own rules on top of state law. If your rental is in a city with rent control or just-cause rules, read the city code too.

Even where no reason is needed, the reason must not be illegal. The federal Fair Housing Act bars a refusal to rent based on race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status or national origin. A non-renewal for one of those reasons breaks the law.

## How much notice is common for a non-renewal?

Non-renewal notice in many states runs 30 to 90 days, and it often grows with the length of stay. The lease can ask for more notice than the law, but not less in most states.

| State | Rule | Notice | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| New York | Non-renewal or a rent rise of 5% or more | 30 days (under 1 year), 60 days (1 to 2 years), 90 days (2+ years) | Real Property Law 226-c |
| California | Ending a periodic tenancy | 60 days if the tenant lived there 1 year or more; 30 days if less | Civil Code 1946.1 |
| Florida | Ending a month-to-month tenancy | At least 30 days before the end of a monthly period | Florida Statutes 83.57(3) |
| Texas | Ending a month-to-month tenancy | Tenancy ends 1 month after notice, or a later date in the notice | Property Code 91.001 |
| Washington | Ending a fixed lease without cause | Only in limited cases; 60 days' written notice before the end of the first term is one condition | RCW 59.18.650 |
| Oregon | No-cause ending in the first year | At least 30 days' written notice | ORS 90.427 |

In New York, Real Property Law 226-c counts the longer of the total time the tenant has lived there or the length of the lease. A tenant on a 2-year lease gets 90 days.

A simple rule of thumb: send notice at least 90 days before the end date unless your state asks for more. That gives you room in most states and gives the tenant time to find a new home.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I send a non-renewal notice? A step list

Use these 7 steps for every non-renewal. The same steps for every tenant help you show fair treatment.

1. **Read the lease.** Find the end date, the renewal clause and any notice rule. Some leases renew by themselves unless one party gives notice.
2. **Check state and city law.** Confirm the notice period, any cause rule and the legal ways to deliver notice.
3. **Count the days.** Count back from the lease end date. Add a buffer for mail time.
4. **Write the notice.** Use the checklist below.
5. **Deliver it the legal way.** Washington, for example, ties delivery to RCW 59.12.040. Keep proof of delivery.
6. **Follow up in writing.** Send move-out steps and offer a pre-move-out walk-through.
7. **Keep records.** File the notice, proof of delivery and all replies in the tenant file.

Non-renewal letter checklist:

- [ ] Tenant names, as on the lease
- [ ] Rental address and unit number
- [ ] Lease end date and the date the tenant must move out
- [ ] Statement that you will not renew the lease
- [ ] The legal reason, if your state or city requires one
- [ ] Move-out steps: keys, cleaning, forwarding address
- [ ] How and when you return the deposit
- [ ] Your name, signature, date and contact details

## What is the retaliation risk when you do not renew?

Retaliation risk is highest when a non-renewal follows a tenant complaint. Many states make it unlawful to end or refuse a tenancy to punish a tenant who used a legal right.

- **Texas.** Property Code 92.331 says a landlord may not, "within six months after the date of the tenant's action", retaliate by listed acts. Protected actions include a good-faith use of a legal right, a complaint to a building or housing code agency, and joining a tenant organization.
- **Florida.** Florida Statutes 83.64 makes it unlawful to raise rent, cut services, or bring an action for possession "primarily because the landlord is retaliating against the tenant." A complaint to a code agency is one protected act.

How to lower the risk:

- Decide on non-renewal for a clear business reason, and write the reason in your file at that time.
- Do not send a non-renewal right after a repair request or a code complaint, unless you have a strong, documented reason.
- Fix the repair anyway. A fixed repair shows good faith.

## What if the tenant stays after the end date?

A tenant who stays after the lease ends without your consent is a holdover tenant. Do not change the locks or remove the tenant's things. Use the court process in your state.

In Texas, Property Code 24.005 asks for at least three days' written notice to vacate before a landlord files a suit against a tenant who holds over, unless the lease sets a different period. Other states set other periods.

Watch what you accept. In some states, rent taken after the end date can create a new tenancy. Oregon ORS 90.427 turns some fixed terms into month-to-month tenancies when they end. Ask a local attorney before you take any payment after the end date.

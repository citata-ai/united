## How much notice does a landlord need to enter?

A landlord needs between 24 hours and 2 days of notice in most states we checked, given in writing. The rule comes from your state's landlord-tenant statute, and some cities add more. Here are 4 states side by side.

| State | Minimum notice | Hours | Statute |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | Written notice; 24 hours presumed reasonable; 6 days if mailed | Normal business hours, unless the tenant agrees | Cal. Civ. Code 1954 |
| Florida | At least 24 hours (for repairs) | 7:30 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. for repairs | Fla. Stat. 83.53 |
| Arizona | At least 2 days | Reasonable times | A.R.S. 33-1343 |
| Oregon | At least 24 hours' actual notice | Reasonable times | ORS 90.322 |

Watch for: "presumed reasonable" is not the same as "always enough." In California, 24 hours is the starting point "absent evidence to the contrary." Give more time when you can. A 3-day notice for a routine visit costs you little and earns goodwill.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What reasons for entry are allowed?

The allowed reasons for entry are a short, fixed list in each statute. Outside that list, you have no right to enter.

- **California (Civ. Code 1954):** emergency; necessary or agreed repairs, alterations or improvements; agreed services; showing the unit to buyers, lenders, tenants, workers or contractors; after abandonment or surrender; under a court order; and some purposes listed in other sections.
- **Florida (Stat. 83.53):** inspect; make necessary or agreed repairs, decorations, alterations or improvements; supply agreed services; show the unit to buyers, lenders, tenants, workers or contractors.
- **Arizona (A.R.S. 33-1343):** inspect, make repairs, supply services, show the unit, and other listed purposes. Beyond these, entry needs a court order or abandonment.
- **Oregon (ORS 90.322):** entry at reasonable times with notice; the tenant may deny consent to a non-emergency entry after notice.

All 4 states say the landlord may not abuse the right of access or use it to harass the tenant, in nearly the same words.

## When can a landlord enter without notice?

A landlord can enter without notice in an emergency in all 4 states. Other exceptions differ.

1. **Emergency.** Fire, a burst pipe, a gas leak or a similar threat. Oregon defines it to include a repair problem "likely to cause serious damage to the premises" if not fixed now.
2. **Tenant consent at the time.** California allows entry without written notice when the tenant is present and agrees.
3. **Abandonment or surrender.** California and Arizona allow entry once the tenant has left the unit.
4. **Tenant repair request (Oregon).** A written repair request lets the landlord enter for that repair without more notice. The permission ends after 7 days unless work is in progress.
5. **Agreed repairs within a week (California).** Landlord and tenant may agree orally to an entry for agreed repairs or services within 1 week, with no written notice.

After an emergency entry in Oregon, tell an absent tenant within 24 hours. State the entry, date and time, the emergency and the names of the people who entered. That is a good practice in every state.

## How do you deliver the notice?

Deliver the notice in a way your statute allows and that you can prove later. California lists 3 ways for written notice: hand it to the tenant, leave it with a suitable person at the unit, or leave it near the usual entry door where a reasonable person would find it. Mail also works, with 6 days presumed reasonable. Florida's notice rules in 83.56(4) allow mail, hand delivery, email under 83.505, or leaving a copy at the residence if the tenant is absent.

What to do:

- **Keep a copy** of every notice with the date and time you delivered it.
- **Take a photo** if you post it at the door.
- **Use the portal or email too,** when the lease and state law allow it, so the tenant gets it fast.

## Notice of entry template

Copy this text, fill in the brackets, and check it against your state and city law before use.

> **Notice of Intent to Enter Dwelling Unit**
>
> Date of notice: [date]
> To: [tenant name(s)]
> Address: [street, unit, city, state, ZIP]
>
> This notice tells you that [landlord or management company] will enter your unit:
>
> **Date of entry:** [date]
> **Time window:** between [time] and [time]
> **Purpose:** [for example: repair the kitchen sink leak you reported on (date) / annual smoke alarm and safety inspection / show the unit to a prospective tenant / pest control service]
> **Who will enter:** [name and company of staff or vendor]
>
> You do not need to be home. If you are not home, we will knock, announce ourselves and lock the door when we leave. We will leave a note that says what we did.
>
> If this time does not work for you, contact us by [date] at [phone] or [email] and we will try to find another time.
>
> Please secure pets during the visit. Tell us about any pet that may be loose in the unit.
>
> This notice is given under [state statute, for example: California Civil Code 1954].
>
> [Name], [title]
> [Company], [phone], [email]
>
> Delivery method: [hand / posted at door / mail / email] on [date and time] by [name]

## How do you use the template well?

Use one template for every unit and every tenant, and change only the brackets. The same words for everyone help you meet Fair Housing rules and keep records clean.

Worked example: a tenant reports a leaking kitchen faucet on Monday at 10 a.m. in Florida. You send the notice by email and post a copy on the door Monday at 3 p.m. The plumber may come Tuesday after 3 p.m., between 7:30 a.m. and 8:00 p.m. You set the window Wednesday 9 to 11 a.m. That gives the tenant about 42 hours, more than the 24-hour minimum.

Common mistakes to avoid:

- **A window that is too wide.** "Sometime this week" does not give a date or approximate time.
- **No purpose.** California requires the purpose on the notice.
- **Showing up early.** If the window starts at 9 a.m., do not knock at 8:45.
- **Repeat visits with no clear need.** Many visits in a short time can look like harassment.

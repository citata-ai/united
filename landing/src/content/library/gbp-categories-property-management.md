## What is the right primary category for a property management company?

The right primary category for most firms is Property management company. Google's category help page says to choose one primary category that best describes your business, and to pick a specific option from the list. You cannot type your own category.

Google's guidelines give a simple test. A category must complete the sentence "This business IS a", not "this business HAS a". A firm that manages rentals for owners IS a property management company. It HAS a leasing service, a maintenance team and an owner portal. Those are services, not categories.

Property Manager Websites, a website vendor for managers, adds one more reason. Its support page says Google turns on extra features after you pick Property management company. One example is a way for owners to book an appointment. We did not test that feature ourselves.

## Which category IDs exist for rental businesses?

Google keeps a fixed list of categories, each with an internal ID. Google does not publish the full list on a help page. PlePer, a local SEO tool, keeps a copy of it. Here are the rental and real estate categories we found there on 2026-10-19.

| Category ID (PlePer list) | When it fits a property manager |
|---|---|
| property_management_company | Primary category for a firm that manages homes or units for owners |
| real_estate_agency | Only if you also act as a brokerage that sells or buys homes |
| real_estate_rental_agency | Only if you also find tenants for owners as a separate leasing office |
| apartment_rental_agency | Only if you lease apartments as a core business |
| condominium_rental_agency | Only if condo leasing is a core line of work |
| vacation_home_rental_agency | Only if you run short-term or vacation rentals |
| commercial_real_estate_agency | Only if you broker commercial space |
| property_maintenance | Only if maintenance is a business you sell on its own to the public |
| homeowners_association | Not for a manager: this is the association itself |

The labels in your profile can differ by country. PlePer showed some labels in UK English, for example "Estate Agent" for real_estate_agency. Always pick the label in your own category picker.

## Which secondary categories fit, and how many?

Add secondary categories only when your firm truly is that kind of business. Google's guidelines say: "Choose the fewest number of categories it takes to describe your overall core business." Google also says: "Do not use categories solely as keywords or to describe attributes of your business."

Google's help pages do not state a maximum number of categories. Vendor guides give different limits. ClearLead Digital, for example, says you can add up to 10 secondary categories. The number matters less than the rule: each category must be true.

Watch for: Google says it "can also detect category information from your website and from mentions about your business throughout the web." So a category that your website does not support looks odd. If you add Real estate agency, your site should show a sales page and your broker license.

## How do you write services on a Business Profile?

Services describe the work, so list every owner service there. Google's services help page says you can pick suggested services for your business type, or add custom services. You can group services and add a description and a price.

Google sets some rules for custom service names. Avoid "profanity, gibberish, personal information, prices, or phone numbers" in the name. Google rejects custom services that break its policy.

A good service list for a residential manager:

1. **Tenant screening.** Say what you check, for example credit, income and rental history.
2. **Rent collection.** Say how owners get paid and when.
3. **Leasing and tenant placement.** Say if you offer it on its own, without full management.
4. **Maintenance coordination.** Say if you have in-house staff or vendors.
5. **Property inspections.** Say how often, for example move-in, move-out and yearly.
6. **Owner reporting.** Name the software and the report schedule.
7. **Eviction coordination.** Say that you work with a local attorney, if you do.
8. **Section 8 or housing voucher leasing.** Add it only if you do this work today.

Write each description in plain words, 1–3 sentences. Use the same service names as on your website. That way the profile and the site say the same thing.

## Do categories change AI answers?

Categories may change AI answers, but no public test proves it for property managers. Here is what we know from Google and from our own data.

- **Google Maps uses the profile.** Google says Gemini in Maps is "grounded in our trusted data about 250 million places around the world". It also summarizes reviews (Google, 2024-10-31).
- **Google search AI wants current data.** Google's AI features page says to check "that your Merchant Center and Business Profile information is up-to-date". It also says there is "no special schema.org structured data that you need to add".
- **Local rank uses relevance.** Google says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity". Relevance is "how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for".
- **ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini read listings.** We asked "What is the best property management company in [city]?" and got 150 answers. 132 of them (88%) used a listing site as a source. Expertise.com was a source in 93 answers, Yelp and iPropertyManagement in 50 each (our data, 2026-10-19, 50 cities, 3 engines).

So the profile is one input among many. A wrong category can make the profile less relevant to an owner's question. A correct category will not, by itself, put you in an AI answer. ## The Business Profile checklist for property managers

Use this list once now, then once each quarter.

- [ ] Primary category is Property management company.
- [ ] Each secondary category passes the "This business IS a" test.
- [ ] No category is there only as a keyword.
- [ ] Every owner service is in Services, with a 1–3 sentence description.
- [ ] Service names match the service names on your website.
- [ ] No prices or phone numbers in custom service names.
- [ ] Business name matches your sign and your website, with no added keywords.
- [ ] Address or service area, phone and hours are correct.
- [ ] Website link goes to your owner page, not the tenant portal.
- [ ] You reply to new reviews. Google says "Positive reviews and helpful replies can help your business stand out."
- [ ] You plan category changes for a quiet week, because a change might ask for verification again.
- [ ] Your site and your other listings (Yelp, Bing Places, Expertise.com) use the same name, address and phone.

The last item matters for AI search. The [AI search checklist for property management websites](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website) covers the website side of the same job.

## What should you not do?

Do not add categories for a business you do not own or operate. Google's guidelines say so in plain words. Google's example is a coffee shop inside a book store: each business keeps its own category. Do not use extra categories as keywords. Google also says "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google."

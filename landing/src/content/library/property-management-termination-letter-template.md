## How much notice does the agreement need?

The notice period is whatever your management agreement says, so read the termination clause before you write. Look for 4 things: the notice period, the form of notice, any cancellation fee, and what survives the end of the agreement.

One public example: a Doorvest management agreement filed with the SEC in 2021. It runs 1 year and then renews each year "until either party terminates by providing at least 30 days written notice." It also has a satisfaction guarantee that lets the owner cancel at any time, and a clause that applies cancellation fees if the owner signs with another broker.

| Clause to find | What to check |
|---|---|
| Term and renewal | Fixed term, auto-renewal date |
| Notice period | Days, and whether it runs from sending or receipt |
| Form of notice | Email, certified mail, a named address |
| Cancellation fee | Flat amount, months of fees, or none |
| Tenant protection clause | A fee if you keep a tenant the manager placed |
| Survival | Duties that continue: deposit handover, final accounting |

Watch for: the end date. If the clause needs 30 days and you send the letter on the 5th, do not ask for handover on the 1st of next month. Count the days.

## What must the manager hand over?

The manager must hand over everything that belongs to you or to your tenants: money, keys, documents and data. Put the full list in the letter, with one deadline.

**Money**

- [ ] All tenant security deposits, with an accounting for each tenant
- [ ] Prepaid rent and advance rent
- [ ] The owner reserve and any owner balance
- [ ] Interest owed on deposits, if your state requires it
- [ ] A final owner statement and a trust account ledger for your properties

**Keys and access**

- [ ] All keys, fobs, garage remotes and mailbox keys, labeled by unit
- [ ] Lockbox and smart lock codes, then a code change
- [ ] Logins for utility, HOA and vendor portals you own

**Documents and data**

- [ ] Current leases, addenda and renewal letters
- [ ] Tenant ledgers and payment history
- [ ] Move-in reports and photos
- [ ] Applications and screening records for current tenants, as allowed by law
- [ ] Maintenance history, open work orders and warranties
- [ ] Vendor list with contacts and any service contracts
- [ ] Lead-paint disclosures and other signed forms
- [ ] Tax forms (1099s) and the year-to-date income and expense report

In Florida, when an agent changes, deposits and advance rent with earned interest and an accounting for each tenant must move to the new owner or agent. The old agent is released once it transfers the funds, the records and gets a written receipt (Fla. Stat. 83.49(7)).

The California Department of Real Estate says brokers must keep a separate record for each property managed and keep trust records for 3 years (DRE, Trust Funds, RE 13). So the manager should be able to export a clean ledger for each property. Ask for it in a file format your next system can import.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Are there termination fees?

Termination fees exist in some agreements and not in others. The Doorvest agreement mentions cancellation fees but does not state an amount on the copy we read. Your clause decides.

Common fee types to look for:

1. **Early termination fee.** A flat amount or a number of months of management fees.
2. **Leasing fee clawback.** A share of the leasing fee if you end the contract soon after a placement.
3. **Tenant protection fee.** A fee if you keep a tenant the manager placed, for a set period.
4. **Transfer or file fee.** A charge to prepare and send records.

What to do: ask the manager to list any fee they claim, with the clause number, in writing. Do not let a fee dispute hold up the deposit handover. Tenant deposits belong to tenants, and the transfer rules protect them.

## Property management termination letter template

Copy this text, fill in the brackets, and check it against your agreement and state law before use.

> **Notice of Termination of Property Management Agreement**
>
> Date: [date]
> To: [management company], [address], [email]
> From: [owner name], [address], [email], [phone]
> Properties: [list each address and unit]
> Agreement: [title], dated [date]
>
> This letter is my written notice that I am ending the agreement above under section [number]. The agreement requires [number] days' notice. The last day of your management is **[end date]**.
>
> Please do not sign new leases, renewals or vendor contracts for these properties after the date of this letter without my written OK. Please keep handling rent, emergencies and open work orders until the end date.
>
> By [end date], please deliver to [me / my new manager, (name), (address)]:
>
> 1. All tenant security deposits, prepaid rent and any interest owed, with an accounting for each tenant.
> 2. My reserve and any balance due to me, with a final owner statement.
> 3. All keys, fobs, remotes and access codes, labeled by unit.
> 4. Current leases, addenda, tenant ledgers, move-in reports and photos.
> 5. Maintenance history, open work orders, warranties and the vendor list.
> 6. A data export of my properties in [CSV / Excel / format].
>
> Please send any fees you believe are due, with the section of the agreement for each, in writing by [date].
>
> Please help us send one joint letter to tenants about the change, so they know where to pay rent from [date].
>
> Thank you for your work on these properties.
>
> [Owner signature], [printed name]

## How do you tell tenants about the change?

Tell tenants in one letter, sent before the first rent due date under the new manager. Tenants need 4 facts: the new manager's name and contact, the new way to pay rent, the date of the change, and who now holds their deposit.

Worked example: you manage 12 units with the old manager until March 31. You send the termination letter on February 25, which gives 34 days against a 30-day clause. The new manager sends the tenant letter on March 15, so tenants know before the April 1 rent date. Deposits of $1,500 for each of 12 units, $18,000 in total, arrive with a per-tenant accounting on April 3.

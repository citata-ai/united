## Which lines should every owner statement show?

A useful owner statement explains where cash came from and where it went. The checklist below is our report design recommendation, not a legal form or a claim about every software default.

| Section | What to show | Owner question answered |
|---|---|---|
| Header | Owner, property and reporting period | Which records am I reading? |
| Opening cash | Starting balance | What did the property hold? |
| Receipts | Rent and other receipts by type | What money arrived? |
| Expenses | Dated charges with clear categories | What did the property pay? |
| Management fees | Basis and charge | How did you calculate your fee? |
| Owner transfers | Contributions and distributions | What moved to or from me? |
| Closing cash | Reconciled ending position | What remains? |
| Held funds | Reserve and other restrictions | What cannot be paid out yet? |
| Available payout | Amount after valid holds | What can I receive? |

Buildium's accounting guide explains income, expense and property accounting records. Rentvine's trust accounting page describes property-level and portfolio-level statements. Use those capabilities as a starting point, then inspect the actual output.

Keep property names consistent across the statement, invoices and portal. A readable report should not require owners to translate internal account codes.

## How do you show reserves and held funds?

Show held funds in a separate, clearly labelled section. A cash balance does not automatically equal an owner payout.

State why you hold each amount. A maintenance reserve, an unpaid bill and another restricted balance are different items. Ask the bookkeeper how each appears in the ledger before deciding how to display it.

Avoid double counting. An expense already deducted from cash should not be deducted again as a hold unless a separate obligation remains. Make the calculation easy to trace.

Use a formula in plain words: closing cash less valid holds equals available distribution, subject to the accounting setup. Label it as the report's method. Do not present that formula as a universal legal rule.

Reconcile underlying balances before sending the statement. A note that explains the reserve cannot fix an incorrect ledger. Keep supporting records accessible to authorized staff and owners.

## How do you show management fees?

Show enough detail for owners to compare the fee with their agreement. The report should explain the basis and amount without a separate call.

If the agreement uses a percentage, identify the income included in the calculation. If it uses a fixed fee, name the period and service. Keep leasing, renewal and other charges separate from regular management fees.

Do not invent a standard fee or imply every owner contract uses the same basis. Read the signed agreement and the software rule together. Check exceptions such as vacancies, concessions and owner-paid expenses.

Ask staff to trace the statement charge to its ledger entry. Then compare the entry with the agreement. If a correction is needed, keep a clear record and explain it on the revised report.

## How do you explain a negative month?

Explain the specific costs or timing that caused the negative result. Avoid a vague note saying expenses exceeded income.

Distinguish profit from cash. A report can show unpaid charges, held funds or owner transfers differently depending on its basis. State which basis the owner is reading.

Use a short explanation linked to supporting records. Name the repair, vacancy period or correction that mattered. Explain whether an owner contribution is needed and who will confirm the amount.

Keep hypothetical calculations clearly labelled if you use them for training. For live owner reports, use the actual ledger and approved invoices. Do not replace the detailed statement with a narrative alone.

Ask someone outside accounting to read the explanation. They should understand what happened, what cash remains and what action comes next. If those answers are unclear, revise the note before sending it.

## Which software makes the clearest statements?

No independent clarity test supports a vendor winner here. Rentvine describes both property-level and consolidated portfolio statements. DoorLoop lists detailed and summary owner statements. Those are features to inspect, not proof of readability.

Ask each vendor for the same sample report. Include rent, a repair, a fee, an owner contribution and held funds. Then request a correction and inspect the revised statement.

Give the report to an owner-service colleague. Ask them to locate the expense, explain the fee and identify the payout. Record each point where they need help.

Test the portal view and downloaded file separately. A clear staff screen may export poorly. Check dates, property labels, missing attachments and page breaks. Confirm access controls before sharing a statement, especially when an owner has interests in several properties.

## What should you check before delivery?

Approve the numbers before the report leaves your team. Use a named reviewer and a repeatable process.

- Match opening balances to the prior close.
- Reconcile receipts and payments.
- Check property and owner assignments.
- Inspect fees against agreements.
- Confirm reserve and held-fund labels.
- Trace unusual charges to documents.
- Verify the payout and bank status.
- Check the intended recipient's access.

Save the approved report and correction history. Give owners a clear way to ask about an entry. A portal should support that conversation rather than hide the person responsible.

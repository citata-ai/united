## Can unlicensed staff show units?

Showing rules depend on the state and the actual work performed. Check the property's jurisdiction before assigning access, tours or open houses. Do not treat a job title such as field coordinator as legal authority.

TREC's Texas guidance says an unlicensed assistant cannot show a broker's listings. Its explanation includes opening doors and allowing access. The same guidance allows staff to schedule an appointment for a license holder. Those tasks belong in separate rows of your staffing plan.

California DRE takes a broader approach on its assistant guidance page. Unlicensed staff may help licensees, but they cannot perform acts that require a license. Do not read that statement as approval for a particular showing method.

Ask the responsible broker to review each field task. State the property type, employment relationship and conduct involved. Exemptions can depend on facts that a general online checklist does not capture.

Check third-party showing services too. A vendor contract does not answer who needs a license or who supervises the work. Request evidence of authority and a clear process for resident concerns.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Can unlicensed staff quote rent or sign leases?

Repeating an approved fact and negotiating a lease are different tasks. Build scripts that keep those acts separate. Refer requests to change price, terms or approval criteria to the authorized person.

Texas TREC permits identified unlicensed clerical staff to confirm information that was previously advertised. That permission does not turn an assistant into a leasing negotiator. Use the exact approved listing as the source and stop when the caller asks for a change.

Create a proposed task-control sheet:

| Task | What staff need before assignment |
|---|---|
| Book a showing | State rule review and approved scheduling script |
| Repeat advertised information | Broker approval and current source record |
| Offer a concession | Authorized licensed decision-maker |
| Explain disputed lease meaning | Referral to the responsible professional |
| Prepare a draft document | Specific instructions and review before use |
| Sign for an owner | Verified authority and state-specific legal review |

Do not infer signing authority from access to an electronic signature tool. Software permission can exceed legal authority. Ask who signs, in what capacity, and under which document.

Use a stop phrase that staff can say without embarrassment: the request needs the licensed manager's review. Give callers a named contact and a next step so a lawful boundary does not become a dead end.

## What supervision does the broker owe?

Broker supervision must cover what assistants actually do, including calls, messages and field access. California DRE says assistants need adequate supervision and monitoring. Its guidance cites the prohibition on compensating unlicensed people for licensed acts.

Use that rule as a reason to inspect workflows, not just job descriptions. A written support role can drift into negotiation when the leasing team is busy. Check the messages residents and applicants receive.

Have the broker approve scripts, task lists and escalation paths. Keep a record of the rule source and the date reviewed. Replace old scripts when a rule or operating process changes.

Train staff on likely edge cases. A caller may ask whether an owner will accept lower rent, waive a term or approve an unusual use. Staff need to recognize the decision and refer it.

Check access rights against the task list. Remove the ability to change lease terms or approve applications when a role does not need those controls. Review exceptions rather than allowing shared credentials.

Measure whether staff use the referral route. A manager who is never available can push assistants past their limits. Give the team a backup authorized contact during leave and shift changes.

## How do rules differ by state?

State rules differ in scope, exemptions and permitted support work. Texas provides detailed assistant examples, while California's DRE page stresses the boundary around licensed acts. Neither page creates a national task list.

Keep a separate operating sheet for each state you serve. Ask the broker to link the current statute, regulation and regulator guidance. Where sources conflict, seek a written answer before assigning the task.

Do not copy a rule for an owner's employee into a third-party management company. The relationship can matter. Likewise, an on-site role and a remote leasing role may not share an exemption.

If you enter a new state, review the full service path before taking the first property. Include prospect calls, showings, applications, leases, rent handling and vendor work. Administrative tasks can connect to restricted decisions.

Recheck the plan when you change staffing or software. Automation can move a decision from a license holder to an assistant without anyone changing a job title.

## How should I audit task assignments?

Audit real work against the approved state task sheet. Select a call, a showing record and a lease workflow. Ask who made the decision, who communicated it and what authority supported it.

Use a test request that changes an advertised term. Watch whether the assistant refers the request or tries to close the deal. Coach the handoff and check that the authorized person responds.

Review outsourced staff with the same method. Ask for the actual script and the account permissions, rather than accepting a service description. Confirm who trains substitutes when the regular assistant is away.

Record each gap with a process owner and a fix. Remove access when needed, revise the instruction and verify the next completed task. Keep the audit focused on conduct.

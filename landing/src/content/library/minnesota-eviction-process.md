## What notice must a Minnesota landlord give for unpaid rent?

A Minnesota landlord must give a written 14-day notice before filing an eviction for unpaid rent. Minn. Stat. 504B.321, subd. 1a covers "nonpayment of rent or other unpaid financial obligation in violation of the lease". Laws 2023, chapter 52, article 19 added the rule for actions filed on or after January 1, 2024. We read the current text on the Revisor's site on 2026-10-19.

The notice must include:

1. **The total amount due.**
2. **An itemized accounting** of unpaid rent, late fees and other charges.
3. **The name and address** of the person who takes rent for you.
4. **The legal help statement**, word for word from the statute.
5. **The financial help statement**, word for word from the statute.
6. **The 14-day statement**, word for word from the statute.

You or your agent deliver it in person or by first class mail to the unit address. If the tenant does not pay or move within 14 days, or a longer local notice period, you may file. Attach the notice to the complaint. If you skip it, the court must dismiss the case and expunge the file (subd. 1(d)).

## How long does an eviction take in Minnesota?

An uncontested Minnesota nonpayment eviction takes about 3 to 6 weeks, by our reading of the statute deadlines. This is our estimate, not a court figure. Court calendars and a tenant's defenses change the real time.

| Step | Statute | Days |
|---|---|---|
| 14-day notice runs | 504B.321, subd. 1a | 14 |
| File complaint, court issues summons | 504B.321, subd. 1 and 4 | 1 to a few |
| Serve at least 7 days before the hearing | 504B.332 | inside the next step |
| First appearance | 504B.321, subd. 1 | 7 to 14 |
| Stay of writ after judgment | 504B.345, subd. 1 | 0 to 7 |
| Officer's demand to leave | 504B.365, subd. 1 | 1 |
| Total, uncontested | | about 23 to 40 |

At the first appearance, the tenant may answer. If the tenant contests, the court sets a trial date that allows "a fair, thorough, and timely adjudication" (Minn. Stat. 504B.335). Either side may ask for a jury. An appeal must come within 15 days (Minn. Stat. 504B.371).

The tenant can also redeem. In a nonpayment case, the tenant can pay the rent in arrears, interest, costs and an attorney fee up to $5 at any time before possession is delivered (Minn. Stat. 504B.291). This right does not apply if you also alleged a material lease violation.

## Where do you file a Minnesota eviction, and what does it cost?

A Minnesota landlord files the eviction complaint with the district court. Minn. Stat. 357.021, subd. 2 sets the civil filing fee at $310 for the party who files first. A tenant who files a first paper pays the same $310. A jury demand costs $100. The state court site, mncourts.gov, blocked our reader on 2026-10-19, so ask your county court administrator for the full total.

Minn. Stat. 504B.321, subd. 3 lists what the complaint must include:

- [ ] The current or most recent written lease and addenda.
- [ ] For unpaid rent, a detailed itemized accounting.
- [ ] For a lease breach, the lease clause, the conduct, the dates and the clause that allows eviction.
- [ ] A copy of any notice to vacate or notice to quit.
- [ ] Whether a subsidy such as Section 8 or tax credits applies, and the agency's name.
- [ ] The 14-day notice, for a nonpayment case.

Service follows Minn. Stat. 504B.332. Use personal or substitute service first. If that fails, you may mail and post the papers, after at least 2 personal service attempts on different days, one between 6:00 p.m. and 10:00 p.m. If you often text or email the tenant, you must also send the hearing date that way.

The case file stays private until final judgment (subd. 6). Since August 1, 2026, you may not list a tenant's minor child as a defendant (Laws 2026, chapter 81, section 3). The fine is the greater of actual damages or $300.

## Can a Minnesota landlord evict for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes, Minnesota allows both, with limits. Minn. Stat. 504B.285, subd. 1 lets you evict a tenant who holds over contrary to the lease, or a tenant at will who stays after a notice to quit. Subd. 4 keeps your right to end a tenancy for a violation of a "lawful, material provision" of the lease.

For a tenancy at will, Minn. Stat. 504B.135 requires written notice at least as long as the rent interval or 3 months, whichever is less. For a monthly rent, that is 1 month.

Watch for retaliation. Under 504B.285, subd. 2, a tenant has a defense if you ended the tenancy to punish a good faith complaint or report of a code violation. If you served the notice to quit within 90 days of that act, you must prove it was not retaliation.

Serious cases move faster. If the tenant seriously endangers other residents or intentionally and seriously damages property, you may ask for an expedited hearing 5 to 7 days after the summons (504B.321, subd. 2). Since August 1, 2026, this also covers a tenant who assaults you, your staff or contractors. The fine for an unfounded request rose from $500 to $750 (Laws 2026, chapter 81, section 5).

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Minnesota?

A Minnesota officer, not the landlord, removes the tenant. Under Minn. Stat. 504B.345, the court issues a writ of recovery of premises and order to vacate when you win. Unless the case was a default or a safety case, the court stays the writ for a reasonable period of up to 7 days.

Under Minn. Stat. 504B.365, the officer who holds the order demands that the tenant leave within 24 hours. A licensed police officer may also do it. If the tenant stays, the officer removes people and property at your cost. You must mail the tenant the date and time of removal and try to call.

If you store property on site, you prepare a signed inventory with the officer present. You must take reasonable care of the property. You have a lien for removal and storage costs, and may hold a public sale after 60 days without payment.

## Is a self-help eviction legal in Minnesota?

No, a lockout or utility shutoff is illegal in Minnesota. Minn. Stat. 504B.375 lets a tenant who is removed or kept out, including by cut utilities or removed locks, ask the court for an immediate order. The sheriff then puts the tenant back in.

| Act | Tenant can recover | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Lockout or forced removal in bad faith | Treble damages or $500, whichever is greater, plus attorney fees | 504B.231 |
| Interrupt electricity, heat, gas or water | Treble damages or $500, whichever is greater, plus attorney fees | 504B.221 |
| Intentional ouster or shutoff to remove the tenant | Misdemeanor | 504B.225 |

## Which trust facts should a property manager show?

Show five facts: your license, your broker, your insurance, your memberships and your complaint record. An owner can check all five before handing over a rental. AI answers can repeat them only when they find them in text and can match them to your company.

| Trust fact | Where to show it | How an owner or AI can check it |
|---|---|---|
| Broker license number | Footer of every page, About page | State license lookup |
| Responsible broker's name | Footer, About page | State license lookup |
| Insurance (for example errors and omissions, general liability) | About page, management agreement page | Certificate on request |
| Trade memberships (NARPM, local landlord group) | About page, with a link to your member profile | The association's member search |
| BBB profile | About page or reviews page, with a link | bbb.org |

Write each fact as a sentence. "Acme Property Management is licensed by the California DRE, license 01234567" is a fact a person or a machine can check. A badge image with no text is not.

## Where should you show the license number?

Show the license number in the site footer, so it is on every page. In some states the law tells you where.

**California.** 10 CCR 2773(a) sets the rule. A licensee must disclose the 8-digit license number and the responsible broker's name. This applies to "all solicitation materials intended to be the first point of contact with consumers." The rule lists "websites owned, controlled, and/or maintained by the soliciting real estate licensee." In written ads, the number may not be in smaller type than the smallest type in the ad.

**Texas.** Under 22 TAC 535.155(a), each ad must include the name of the license holder or team. The broker's name must be at least half the size of the largest contact information for any agent in the ad.

Texas also sets two home page links:

| Link label (one of two options) | Minimum font | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| "Texas Real Estate Commission Consumer Protection Notice" or "TREC Consumer Protection Notice" | 10 pt or 12 pt | 22 TAC 531.18(b)(2) |
| "Texas Real Estate Commission Information About Brokerage Services" or "TREC Information About Brokerage Services" | 10 pt or 12 pt | 22 TAC 531.20(b)(1) |

The longer label needs at least 10-point font. The shorter label needs at least 12-point font. Both links must be "in a readily noticeable place on the homepage."

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Does a property manager need a broker license at all?

Licensing rules for property managers are state law, and they differ by state. Texas is a clear example. Its broker definition is in Tex. Occ. Code 1101.002. It includes a person who "controls the acceptance or deposit of rent from a resident of a single-family residential real property unit."

If your state requires a license, the license is the first trust fact an owner can check. If your state does not, say which license or registration you hold, if any.

## Should you link the state license lookup?

Yes. A link to the state lookup turns a claim into a fact that anyone can check in 30 seconds. California's Department of Real Estate has a public license lookup. For other states, search "[state] real estate license lookup" on the regulator's site.

Steps:

1. **Find your record** in your state's lookup by license number.
2. **Check the name.** It must match your company name on your site and on Google Business Profile.
3. **Link it** from your About page: "Check our license on the DRE site."
4. **Repeat it** for each licensed person you name on the site, such as your broker.

Watch for: a mismatch between the name on the license and the name on your site. AI tools match facts by name. Say the license reads "Smith Realty Inc." and your site reads "Sunrise Property Management." Then write a sentence that connects the two: "Sunrise Property Management is a brand of Smith Realty Inc., DRE license 01234567."

## Which memberships matter: NARPM, BBB or others?

NARPM and BBB are the two memberships we see in AI answers. In our test of 150 AI answers for "best property management company" in 50 US cities on 2026-10-19:

| Source | AI answers that used it | Engine that used it most |
|---|---|---|
| bbb.org | 14 of 150 | ChatGPT, 11 of 50 |
| NARPM sites (national and chapters) | 5 of 150 | ChatGPT, 5 of 50 |
| expertise.com, for scale | 93 of 150 | All three |

Source: our data, [AI Property Manager Index 2026](/research/ai-property-manager-index-2026).

**NARPM** is the National Association of Residential Property Managers. Its site lists the designations RMP (Residential Management Professional), MPM (Master Property Manager), CRMC (Certified Residential Management Company) and CSS (Certified Support Specialist). It also runs a public "Find a NARPM Property Manager" search. Keep your member profile complete, because it is one more page that ties your name to your city.

**BBB** lists 8 standards for accredited businesses, such as "Advertise honestly" and "Honor promises." BBB says that not all businesses qualify for accreditation. A BBB page can appear in AI answers either way, so check what it says about you.

## Does AI repeat trust facts?

AI can repeat a trust fact only if a page states it in text. We have not measured how often ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude say that a manager "is licensed." Treat any agency claim about that number with care.

What we do know: AI answers about local managers lean on listing and review pages. Those pages often show license and accreditation fields. So the fix is the same in two places: state the fact on your site, and make your listings match.

You can also mark up the facts. Schema.org's RealEstateAgent type takes `hasCredential` ("a credential awarded to the Person or Organization") and `memberOf`. Markup must match the visible text on the page. It helps machines read the fact. It does not replace the text.

New Hampshire eviction law lives in RSA chapter 540 (court steps) and RSA 540-A (banned acts). We read the 2025 edition text on Justia on 2026-10-19, because the General Court site blocked our requests. The court fee comes from the New Hampshire Judicial Branch.

## What notice must a New Hampshire landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

A New Hampshire landlord gives a written demand for rent and a 7-day eviction notice. RSA 540:3, II allows 7 days when the reason is neglect or refusal to pay rent that is due. RSA 540:4 lets you make the demand before the notice or at the same time.

The notice must follow these rules:

1. **State the reason.** RSA 540:3, III says the notice must give the reason "with specificity".
2. **Tell the tenant how to cure.** For unpaid rent, the notice must tell the tenant of any right to stop the eviction by payment under RSA 540:9.
3. **Serve it in the right way.** RSA 540:5 lets any person serve the notice in hand or at the tenant's last and usual place of abode.
4. **Keep proof.** You need a true and attested copy plus an affidavit of service. The affidavit does not need an oath.

The District Division gives free forms for the demand and the notice. You do not have to use them. But RSA 540:5, II says your notice must hold the same information.

## How long does an eviction take in New Hampshire?

A New Hampshire eviction for unpaid rent with no defense takes about 3 to 4 weeks from the first notice. Add the time the sheriff needs to serve the writ. Here is the fastest path, step by step.

| Step | Day count | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Serve demand and 7-day notice | Day 0 | RSA 540:3, 540:4 |
| Notice period ends | Day 7 | RSA 540:3, II |
| Enter the writ, pay $150, attach proof of notice | Day 8 or later | Rule 5.3 |
| Sheriff serves the writ | Varies | RSA 540:13, III |
| Return day: tenant must file an appearance | 7 days after service | RSA 540:13, III |
| Court mails a default notice | After the return day | Rule 5.4 |
| Writ of possession can issue | 5 or more business days later | Rule 5.4 |

If the tenant files an appearance, the court holds a hearing within 10 days. It mails notice of the hearing at least 6 days before (RSA 540:13, V). After a decision, the tenant has 7 days to file a notice of intent to appeal. During an appeal, the tenant must pay rent into court each week (RSA 540:25).

Watch for: the court may delay the move-out for up to 3 months if "justice requires" it (RSA 540:13-c). The tenant then pays you the weekly rent in advance. One missed payment, and the writ issues.

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in New Hampshire, and what does it cost?

A New Hampshire landlord files a landlord and tenant writ in the Circuit Court District Division. RSA 540:13, I makes the writ returnable before a district court. Under District Division Rule 5.3, you enter the writ with the court before the sheriff serves it. You pay the entry fee at that time and attach proof of service of the eviction notice.

The Judicial Branch fee list dated 2025-07-01 shows a Landlord/Tenant Entry fee of $150. Card payments at the clerk's window carry a 3% surcharge, says the Judicial Branch forms and fees page. Sheriff service fees come on top. We did not find a sheriff fee on an official page, so we leave it out.

You may add a claim for unpaid rent to the writ. RSA 540:13, III caps that money judgment at $1,500. You can sue for more in a separate case.

## Can a New Hampshire landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

A New Hampshire landlord can evict for a lease violation. No-cause rules depend on the type of property. RSA 540:1-a splits rentals into two groups.

| Group | What it covers | Reason needed? |
|---|---|---|
| Nonrestricted | Single-family houses of an owner with 3 or fewer; units in an owner-occupied building of 4 units or fewer | No. Any reason, with 30 days' notice |
| Restricted | All other residential rentals | Yes. One reason from RSA 540:2, II |

For restricted property, RSA 540:2, II lists these reasons:

- **Unpaid rent** after demand (7-day notice).
- **Substantial damage** by the tenant, household or guests (7-day notice).
- **Failure to comply with a material lease term** (30-day notice).
- **Behavior that harms health or safety** of others (7-day notice).
- **Other good cause** (30-day notice). RSA 540:2, V says this includes "any legitimate business or economic reason".
- **End of a lease of 12 months or longer**, effective 2026-07-01. You give written notice at least 60 days before the lease ends. You file within 6 months of the end date (RSA 540:2, II(i)).

Before you use "other good cause" based on tenant conduct, give a written warning first. RSA 540:2, III requires it.

Watch for retaliation. RSA 540:13-a lets a tenant defend if you act after a code complaint or a tenant meeting. The defense does not apply if the tenant owes one week's rent or more. A tenant who wins this defense can get up to 3 months' rent (RSA 540:14, II).

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in New Hampshire?

The sheriff removes the tenant in New Hampshire. RSA 540:14, I says the writ of possession "shall authorize the sheriff to remove the defendant". You may instead ask the court to hold the tenant in civil contempt.

After the tenant leaves, you must store their property with reasonable care for 7 days. The tenant may collect it with no rent or storage fee. After 7 days, you may dispose of it without notice (RSA 540-A:3, VII).

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in New Hampshire?

No, self-help eviction is not legal in New Hampshire. RSA 540-A:2 bans any attempt to get around the RSA 540 process. RSA 540-A:3 bans these acts:

- [ ] Shutting off water, heat, electricity, gas or other utilities (paragraph I).
- [ ] Changing locks or blocking access to the unit without court process (paragraph II).
- [ ] Holding the tenant's belongings without court process (paragraph III).
- [ ] Entering without consent, except for emergency repairs (paragraph IV).

The tenant files a petition with no filing fee (RSA 540-A:4, III). The court can issue temporary orders at once. Damages follow RSA 358-A:10: actual damages or $1,000, whichever is greater. A willful violation gets 2 to 3 times that amount, plus attorney's fees. Each day a violation continues after a temporary order counts as a new violation. If you re-let the unit after a lockout, damages are at least $3,000 (RSA 540-A:4, IX(e)).

## What does AB 1482 cap statewide?

AB 1482, the Tenant Protection Act of 2019, caps a yearly rent increase at 5% plus the change in the cost of living, up to 10% in any 12 months (Civil Code 1947.12). The California Attorney General publishes the current cap for each area. The cost-of-living number comes from April CPI data of the Bureau of Labor Statistics, or the California Department of Industrial Relations for counties without their own BLS series.

| Area | Increase starts 8/1/2025 to 7/31/2026 | Increase starts 8/1/2026 to 7/31/2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Los Angeles and Orange counties | 8% | 8.7% |
| Riverside and San Bernardino counties | 7.5% | 8.1% |
| San Diego County | 8.8% | 8.2% |
| San Francisco area (Alameda, Contra Costa, Marin, San Francisco, San Mateo) | 6.3% | 8.8% |
| All other counties | 7.7% | 8.6% |

Source: California Attorney General, rent caps page, read 2026-10-19.

AB 1482 also adds just cause eviction rules after a tenant has lived in the unit for 12 months. The cap and the just cause rules cover Section 8 voucher tenancies at market rent, the Attorney General says.

## Which cities in LA, the Bay Area, San Diego and Orange County have local rent control?

Local rent control in California means a city or county cap that is lower than AB 1482. When the local cap gives a lower increase, the local rule wins. The table below takes the Attorney General's 2026 chart and sorts it by the 8 California metros in our HUD Fair Market Rent data.

| Metro | Cities or counties with a local cap | Example formula and current max |
|---|---|---|
| Los Angeles | City of Los Angeles, Baldwin Park, Bell Gardens, Beverly Hills, Commerce, Cudahy, Culver City, Huntington Park, Inglewood, LA County (unincorporated), Maywood, Pasadena, Pomona, Santa Monica, West Hollywood | City of LA (pre-10/1/1978 units): 100% of CPI, min 3%, max 8%. 3% for 7/1/2026 to 6/30/2027 |
| Oakland (Alameda and Contra Costa) | Alameda, Berkeley, Hayward, Oakland, San Leandro, Antioch, Concord, Richmond | Oakland (pre-1/1/1983 units): 60% of CPI, max 3%. 2.3% for 8/1/2026 to 7/31/2027 |
| San Francisco | City and County of San Francisco | Pre-6/13/1979 units: 60% of CPI. 1.6% for 3/1/2026 to 2/28/2027 |
| San Jose (Santa Clara) | San Jose, Mountain View | San Jose (3+ units, pre-9/7/1979): up to 5% a year |
| Orange County | Santa Ana | 80% of CPI, max 3%. 2.87% for 9/1/2026 to 8/31/2027 |
| San Diego | None listed | AB 1482 only: 8.2% |
| Riverside-San Bernardino | Palm Springs | Most units: 5% plus CPI, max 10% |
| Sacramento | City of Sacramento | 5% plus CPI, max 10%. 8.6% for 7/1/2026 to 6/30/2027 |

Source: California Attorney General, local rent increase caps chart, read 2026-10-19.

Watch for: two of these laws may change soon. The chart says Pomona's ordinance ends on December 31, 2026 unless the council renews it. It also says Salinas, outside our metros, votes on a repeal in November 2026.

## Which rentals does Costa-Hawkins exempt?

Costa-Hawkins is the state law that limits what local rent control can cover. Per the Attorney General, local caps do not apply to buildings with a certificate of occupancy issued after February 1, 1995. They also do not apply to most single-family homes or condos.

Costa-Hawkins also allows vacancy decontrol (Civil Code 1954.53). When the tenants move out and new tenants move in, you may set the new starting rent. The local cap applies again from that new base.

Many cities set an earlier cutoff date than 1995. The City of Los Angeles covers units first occupied before October 1, 1978. Oakland covers units before January 1, 1983. San Francisco covers units before June 13, 1979. A building from 1990 in Oakland is under AB 1482, not under the Oakland cap.

## Do single-family homes fall under local rent control in California?

Single-family homes are mostly outside local rent caps, because Costa-Hawkins exempts them. Single-family homes are also outside AB 1482, with one exception. The Attorney General's page says AB 1482 applies to a single-family home or condo owned or controlled by a corporation or a REIT.

Other exemptions from AB 1482, per the Attorney General:

- **New units.** A certificate of occupancy issued in the last 15 years, on a rolling basis.
- **Owner-occupied duplex.** The owner lived in one unit when the tenant moved into the other, and still lives there.
- **Deed-restricted affordable housing.** Housing restricted by deed or by a government agreement.
- **Dorms.** The Attorney General lists dorms as exempt.

A single-family home can still fall under a local just cause or relocation rule, even when no rent cap applies. Check the city before you send a notice.

## How do I check one property in 4 steps?

You can test a property against both layers in about 10 minutes.

1. **Find the city.** Use the parcel address, not the mailing city. Unincorporated LA County has its own rules.
2. **Find the certificate of occupancy date.** Compare it with the city cutoff (for example, October 1, 1978 in LA) and with February 1, 1995.
3. **Check the owner type.** For a house or condo, note if a corporation, REIT or LLC owned by one holds title.
4. **Pick the lower cap.** Compare the local cap with the AB 1482 cap for your county and date. The lower one applies.

Worked example: a 1975 fourplex in the City of Los Angeles at $2,964 a month, the HUD FY2027 two-bedroom Fair Market Rent for the LA metro. The LA cap for July 2026 to June 2027 is 3%, so the most you can add is $88.92 a month. Under AB 1482 alone, at 8.7%, the limit would be $257.87. For the full LA rent table, see the [Los Angeles Fair Market Rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/los-angeles-fair-market-rent-2027).

## What does rent control mean for a property manager in California?

A California property manager must track two caps, two cutoff dates and the notice rules for each city. That is a good test question when you hire one. Ask the manager to name the cap for one of your units and the date it changes.

A good manager can also show you how the cap affects your yearly income. A 3% cap and an 8.7% cap on the same $2,964 rent differ by $168.95 a month, or $2,027.40 a year.

## Which data must move?

Move the records needed to keep accounts, service and decisions accurate after cutover. Inventory data before discussing import formats. A successful file upload does not prove that the company can operate from the new records.

Start with owners, properties, units, residents, leases and vendors. Add account balances, security deposit records, open charges, unpaid bills, work orders and supporting documents. Identify custom fields and attachments that a basic export may omit.

Buildium's switching guide recommends evaluating software around business needs and planning the change. Use your task inventory to test fit. Ask the receiving vendor to show which records import, which need manual work and which cannot move.

Use this proposed inventory:

| Record group | Check after import | Approval lead |
|---|---|---|
| Parties and properties | Relationships, names and authority | Operations |
| Leases and documents | Dates, current terms and accessible files | Responsible manager |
| Accounting balances | Bank, control and beneficiary records | Accountant and broker |
| Payments | Active routes, pending items and duplicates | Payment lead |
| Open service work | Status, assigned person and next action | Maintenance lead |
| History and archive | Searchable reports and source documents | Records lead |

Mark each item as import, rebuild or archive. Assign a person to confirm the result. Do not leave that choice hidden in a vendor's scope note.

## How long does a switch take?

A switch takes as long as the export, mapping, training and verification work require. Ask for a written migration plan based on your data. Do not promise owners a standard duration copied from another company.

Request a test export early. Check whether it includes attachments, historical reports and the relationships between records. A folder of files without clear property links can create expensive manual work.

Map the old chart of accounts to the new one. Review fee calculations, property ownership changes and unusual balances. Keep the mapping with the migration records so staff can explain later comparisons.

Test a sample that includes difficult cases. Use a property with open repairs, a resident with a balance and an owner with several homes. A clean demonstration account cannot reveal every migration gap.

Have reviewers reproduce key reports from both systems at the same cut-off. Explain differences rather than forcing totals to match through unexplained entries. Keep a clear list of unresolved issues.

Set the launch date after the test proves the critical work, not before the vendor sales process ends. Allow staff to learn routine tasks before they face a live payment or urgent request.

## When in the year should I switch?

Choose a cutover period with manageable payment, reporting and staffing demands. Your local leasing pattern and company calendar matter more than a universal best month. Check leave, vendor support and accountant availability.

Avoid placing the hardest data work beside another major company change. A new fee schedule or ownership transfer can make differences harder to explain. Keep the scope controlled until the core system works.

Build a cutover plan that identifies the last transaction in the old system and the first in the new one. State who freezes edits, exports final records and approves opening balances. Record late-arriving items separately.

California DRE's trust-fund guidance requires control records, separate beneficiary records and reconciliation. For California brokers, migration does not remove those obligations. Ask your broker to identify the applicable rules in every state you serve.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Keep a fallback decision with clear triggers. If payment routes or balances fail approval, the team needs to know whether to delay launch. Do not force cutover merely because the announcement has been drafted.

## How do I tell owners and residents?

Tell owners and residents what changes, when it changes and where they can get help. Separate instructions by audience. Owners need reporting and payout details; residents need payment and service access information.

Send the announcement through an established contact route. Explain whether existing payment arrangements need action. Do not tell everyone to enroll again until the payment lead confirms how old authorizations end.

The FBI warns that criminals use fake payment changes in business email compromise schemes. Apply that caution to migration notices. Give people a trusted way to verify instructions without using a contact supplied only in the new message.

Prepare separate messages for the announcement, launch and any delay. Keep dates and links consistent. Tell staff which message version applies so callers receive the same answer.

Provide help for people who cannot use the portal. Record the alternative route without exposing private information. A new system should not leave an urgent repair report dependent on a successful login.

Check that owner statements and resident balances display correctly before asking people to rely on them. If a record is under review, state the issue clearly and give a named contact.

## What proves the migration is complete?

Migration is complete when authorized reviewers confirm that the company can operate and account for funds correctly. An import summary is only one piece of evidence. Test the work that follows the import.

Run a lawful sample payment workflow, a management-fee calculation, an owner statement and a repair request. Confirm roles, approvals, records and communication. Use controlled test data where live movement is unnecessary.

Check the old archive from an account that did not create it. Can the reviewer find a lease, read a statement and identify the related property? Fix access and file mapping before ending the old subscription.

Record unresolved items with a lead and a completion condition. Keep the migration team available until those items close. Do not hand them to ordinary support without an agreed owner.

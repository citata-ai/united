## Which KPIs should a property management owner track?

A property management owner needs 12 KPIs (key performance indicators, the numbers you track on a schedule). They fall into three groups: growth, service quality and profit. The table is the full scorecard.

| KPI | Formula | Review | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Doors won | New doors signed in the period | Weekly | Sales / business development |
| Doors lost | Doors that left in the period | Monthly | Owner of the company |
| Net door growth | Doors won − doors lost | Monthly | Owner of the company |
| Unit churn (annual) | Doors lost in 12 months ÷ average doors | Monthly | Owner of the company |
| Days to lease | Days from vacant to signed lease, average | Weekly | Leasing lead |
| Work order response time | Hours from request to first action | Weekly | Maintenance coordinator |
| Rent collection rate | Rent collected by the due date ÷ rent due | Monthly | Accounting |
| Client churn (annual) | Owners lost in 12 months ÷ average owners | Monthly | Property manager lead |
| Revenue per door | 12-month fee revenue ÷ average doors | Monthly | Owner of the company |
| Doors per employee | Doors ÷ full-time-equivalent staff | Quarterly | Owner of the company |
| Cost to win a door | Sales and marketing cost ÷ doors won | Monthly | Sales / marketing |
| Net profit margin | Net profit ÷ total revenue | Monthly | Accounting |

Buildium's KPI guide lists 11 KPIs, many of them about the rental property (NOI, rent-ready cost). The list above is for the company itself: the business that earns fees.

## Which KPIs show growth?

Growth KPIs show if your door count moves up, and why. The most useful one is net door growth: doors won minus doors lost.

Buildium makes the same point: if you add doors at the front door while you lose them at the back, real growth is slower than it looks. Track both on one line.

Unit churn tells you where growth stops. Peter Lohmann, who runs a 700+ door company, gives an example in his 2026-01-09 newsletter: a company that adds 10 doors a month and churns 25% a year maxes out at 400 doors. The math is simple:

`Growth ceiling = doors won per year ÷ annual unit churn rate`

With this simple formula, 120 doors a year ÷ 0.25 gives 480 doors. Lohmann's own model gives 400. The models differ, but the lesson is the same: churn, not lead flow alone, sets the top of your door count. Treat your result as a range, not a target.

## Which KPIs show service quality?

Service KPIs show how owners and tenants feel before they say it. Four numbers do most of the work:

- **Days to lease.** Long vacancies cost owners rent and are a common reason they leave. The U.S. Census Bureau put the national rental vacancy rate at 7.3% in Q2 2026. Compare your vacancy to your own market.
- **Work order response time.** Measure the hours from tenant request to first action, not to job done. Slow first replies show up later as tenant move-outs and owner complaints.
- **Rent collection rate.** Rent collected by the due date, divided by rent due. A drop is often the first sign of a screening problem.
- **Client churn.** Lohmann calls client churn the best metric for your service, and unit churn the best one for growth forecasts. One owner with 12 doors who leaves is 1 client but 12 units.

For each owner who leaves, record one reason in a fixed list: sold the property, fee, communication, maintenance cost, moved to self-manage, other. After 12 months, the list tells you what to fix.

## Which KPIs show profit?

Profit KPIs show if each door pays for the work it creates. Track four:

1. **Revenue per door.** All fee revenue for 12 months, divided by average doors. This is your price check.
2. **Doors per employee.** Benchmarks cited by LetHub give about 40 to 59 doors per person. Count the same staff as the benchmark you compare to.
3. **Cost to win a door.** Buildium defines it as total sales and marketing budget divided by new units won. Watch the trend: if it climbs while your close rate holds, your spend is less efficient.
4. **Net profit margin.** Net profit divided by revenue. Look at 12 months, because leasing season moves single months.

Worked example for one month:

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Fee revenue | $36,000 |
| Average doors | 240 |
| Revenue per door (monthly) | $150 |
| Sales and marketing cost | $3,000 |
| Doors won | 6 |
| Cost to win a door | $500 |
| Net profit | $5,400 |
| Net profit margin | 15% |

The example numbers are not benchmarks. Use your own books.

## How often should I review each KPI?

Review a KPI as often as you can act on it. Weekly numbers are the ones a team can move this week: doors won, days to lease, work order response time. Monthly numbers move slowly, so a weekly look only adds noise.

A simple rhythm:

- **Monday, 15 minutes:** the 3 weekly KPIs, with the person who owns each.
- **First week of the month, 1 hour:** all 12 KPIs, this month and the 12-month trend.
- **Each quarter:** doors per employee and the list of churn reasons. Decide on hires and price changes here.

Buildium's first-100-doors guide adds one more fast number for small companies: reply to a new owner lead within 15 minutes. If your company is under 100 doors, add lead response time to the Monday review.

## Who owns each KPI?

Each KPI needs one named owner. Two owners means no owner. The owner does not do all the work. The owner reports the number, explains the change and proposes one action.

Checklist for setup:

- [ ] Put the 12 KPIs in one sheet or software dashboard.
- [ ] Write the formula next to each, so everyone counts the same way.
- [ ] Name one owner for each.
- [ ] Set a target only after 3 months of data.
- [ ] Record every lost owner's reason in a fixed list.

Revenue per door depends on your fee. Test fee levels with the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) before you set a target.

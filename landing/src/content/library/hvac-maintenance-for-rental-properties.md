## How often should rental HVAC filters change?

Rental HVAC filters need a check once a month. ENERGY STAR says to "inspect, clean, or change air filters once a month" in a central air conditioner, furnace or heat pump. A monthly check does not mean a monthly change. It means someone looks at the filter and changes it when it is dirty.

A clogged filter cuts airflow. The system then runs longer to heat or cool the unit. In a rental, that shows up as a higher utility bill for the tenant and more wear on equipment you own.

Change filters more often in these cases:

- **Pets.** Hair and dander clog a filter fast.
- **Heavy use.** Peak summer and winter months load the filter more.
- **Dust.** A unit near a road or a building site collects more dust.
- **Turnover.** Put in a new filter at every move-in, so the new tenant starts clean.

Angi puts the cost of a replacement filter at $10 to $50. That is the cheapest HVAC item you will ever buy.

## Who changes the filters: landlord or tenant?

The lease decides who changes the filters. No national rule assigns the task. Many landlords give it to the tenant, because the tenant is in the unit every month and the landlord is not.

The landlord still owns the duty to keep the system working. State law sets that duty, and it differs by state:

| Place | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| California | A unit is untenantable without heating facilities "maintained in good working order" | Civil Code 1941.1(a)(4) |
| Arizona | Landlord keeps heating, ventilating and air-conditioning facilities in "good and safe working order" | A.R.S. 33-1324(A)(4) |
| Arizona | Landlord supplies reasonable heat and reasonable cooling where such units are installed and offered | A.R.S. 33-1324(A)(6) |
| New York City | Heat season is October 1 to May 31. Indoors must reach 68°F by day when it is below 55°F outside, and 62°F at night | NYC HPD |

Arizona also lets the parties move some tasks to the tenant, with limits. For a single-family home, A.R.S. 33-1324(C) allows a written agreement that the tenant handles heat supply. For other units, 33-1324(D) allows a separate signed writing for set maintenance tasks. Neither agreement can cover work needed to fix a code or habitability problem.

If you give filters to the tenant, make it easy:

1. **Name the task in the lease.** For example: "Tenant changes the HVAC filter at least every 3 months and checks it monthly."
2. **Give the size.** Write the filter size on the move-in sheet and on the air handler.
3. **Supply filters.** A box of filters at move-in costs little and removes the excuse.
4. **Send a reminder.** A text on the first of each quarter works.
5. **Check at inspections.** Look at the filter at each routine inspection.

## How often should a pro service the HVAC?

A professional should service rental HVAC once a year per system, before its season. ENERGY STAR advises "annual pre-season check-ups": the cooling system in spring and the heating system in fall. A heat pump does both jobs, so it gets 2 visits.

ENERGY STAR lists what a check-up includes. Use the list to judge a contractor's visit:

- [ ] Check thermostat settings.
- [ ] Tighten electrical connections and measure motor voltage and current.
- [ ] Lubricate moving parts.
- [ ] Check and clear the condensate drain.
- [ ] Test system controls and the start, run and shutdown cycle.
- [ ] Cooling: clean the evaporator and condenser coils and check refrigerant level.
- [ ] Cooling: clean and adjust blower parts.
- [ ] Heating: check gas or oil connections, gas pressure, burner combustion and the heat exchanger.

Only a certified technician should touch refrigerant. EPA rules under Clean Air Act Section 608 require certification for technicians who "maintain, service, repair, or dispose of" equipment that can release ozone-depleting refrigerants. Do not let a tenant or a handyman top up a system.

## What does an HVAC service visit cost?

A routine HVAC service visit costs about $75 to $200. That is Angi's range for annual HVAC maintenance, read on 2026-10-19. Angi lists an AC tune-up at $70 to $200, and up to $750 for a large AC in a big home.

Angi also puts yearly maintenance contracts at $150 to $500. A contract often bundles the spring and fall visits.

Here is a worked budget for a small portfolio. It uses the top of Angi's range, so it is a ceiling, not a quote.

| Item | Per system | 10 systems |
|---|---|---|
| Spring cooling visit | $200 | $2,000 |
| Fall heating visit | $200 | $2,000 |
| Filters, 4 a year at $50 | $200 | $2,000 |
| Yearly total | $600 | $6,000 |

Your real cost depends on your market and your equipment. Ask 2 or 3 local contractors for a per-visit price and a multi-unit price. Many give a discount when you book several units on one day.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I run HVAC maintenance across many units?

You run HVAC maintenance across many units with a calendar, not with memory. Set 3 dates a year and attach every unit to them.

1. **March or April.** Book cooling check-ups for every unit with AC or a heat pump.
2. **September or October.** Book heating check-ups. In New York City, finish before October 1, when heat season starts.
3. **Each quarter.** Send the filter reminder and log who changed it.
4. **Each turnover.** Fit a new filter and note the date on the move-in sheet.
5. **Each work order.** Record the unit, the date, the technician, the work and the cost.

Give the tenant notice before each visit. Entry rules differ by state, and a tune-up is not an emergency.

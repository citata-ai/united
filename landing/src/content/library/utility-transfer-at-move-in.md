## Which utilities should the tenant put in their name?

The tenant should put a utility in their own name when the lease says the tenant pays it. The unit should also have its own meter for it. In most rentals that means electricity and gas. Internet, cable and phone are always the tenant's own accounts.

Water, sewer and trash vary. Some cities bill the owner only. Some let a tenant open an account. Call each provider once and write down its rule for the address.

A split you can put in the lease:

| Utility | Typical payer | Account name | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electricity | Tenant | Tenant | Separate meter per unit |
| Gas | Tenant | Tenant | Separate meter per unit |
| Water and sewer | Varies by city | Owner or tenant | Some cities bill the owner only |
| Trash | Varies by city | Owner or tenant | Often on the water bill |
| Internet, cable, phone | Tenant | Tenant | Never in the owner's name |
| Common area power | Owner | Owner | Hallways, laundry, exterior lights |

Shared meters need care. California Civil Code 1940.9 says that if a tenant's gas or electric meter also serves an area outside the unit, the landlord must disclose it. The landlord then signs a written agreement with the tenant on who pays for the outside use. One option in the statute: the landlord becomes the customer of record for that meter.

## What is a landlord revert-to-owner agreement?

A revert-to-owner agreement tells the utility to move service into your name, instead of shutting it off, when a tenant closes the account. Utilities name it in different ways: landlord agreement, landlord program, or revert to owner.

SRP, the Phoenix-area power utility, publishes its terms (SRP Landlord Agreement, 2017-07-01):

- **Purpose.** Service moves to the owner's name so you can clean and show the unit with the power on.
- **Size.** You need a permanent account and at least 10 rental units. SRP says single-family homes and condos may find the program hard to manage.
- **Deposit.** A reduced deposit of $40 per residential unit, held on your primary account.
- **Fees.** You pay the turn-on fee once. Later automatic transfers have no fee.
- **Billing.** You pay for usage until the new tenant asks for service in their name. Unpaid balances move to your primary account.
- **Limits.** You cannot ask SRP to disconnect a tenant while the agreement is active. If you rent with utilities included, SRP cancels the agreement.
- **Cancel.** Either side can end it with 30 days' written notice.

Other utilities have their own terms. Ask each one for its landlord form, its fees and its minimum number of units.

## Can I require proof of transfer before keys?

You can make utility transfer a lease condition and check it at key handover. Ask for the confirmation or account number for each tenant-paid utility. Write the number on the move-in checklist.

Move-in checklist:

- [ ] Lease names each utility and who pays it.
- [ ] Lease sets the transfer date, usually the lease start date.
- [ ] Tenant got a list of providers with phone numbers and web links.
- [ ] Tenant gave a confirmation number for electricity.
- [ ] Tenant gave a confirmation number for gas, if any.
- [ ] Water and trash: tenant account opened, or owner account confirmed.
- [ ] Meter readings taken and photographed on move-in day.
- [ ] Your revert-to-owner agreement covers the unit, if the utility offers one.

Send the provider list with the signed lease, at least 1 week before move-in. The tenant then has time to call. If a tenant has no confirmation at handover, write the gap down and set a deadline in writing.

Check your state's rules before you hold back keys from a tenant who signed a lease. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Who pays the gap days?

The person whose name is on the account pays. Between tenants that is usually you. Under SRP's agreement, the owner pays for usage until the new tenant requests service.

A worked example of a 9-day gap at move-out:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Days between move-out and new lease | 9 |
| Electric cost per day (hypothetical) | $3.20 |
| Gap cost | 9 × $3.20 = $28.80 |

The daily cost is a hypothetical. Use your own bills for the unit. Many owners treat the gap as a normal turnover cost.

If a tenant's lease ends on the 31st but they close the account on the 25th, the 6 days are a lease issue. The lease should say the tenant keeps service on until the lease ends and pays for that time.

## Can a landlord shut off utilities to make a tenant leave?

No. State law bars utility shutoffs as a way to push a tenant out. Here are 3 examples.

| State | Statute | Rule | Tenant remedy named in the statute |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | Civil Code 789.3 | No willful cut of utilities with intent to end the tenancy | Actual damages, up to $100 per day, at least $250 per cause of action |
| Florida | Fla. Stat. 83.67(1) | No direct or indirect cut of utilities, even if the landlord pays them | See the statute for damages |
| Texas | Property Code 92.008 | No interruption except for bona fide repairs, construction or an emergency | Actual damages, 1 month's rent plus $1,000, attorney's fees |

These rules protect the tenant during the tenancy. At move-out, you close or revert the accounts after the tenant has left and returned the keys.

## Move-out checklist

1. **Remind the tenant** 2 weeks before move-out to schedule service to end on the last lease day.
2. **Confirm your revert-to-owner agreement** covers the unit.
3. **Take final meter readings** with photos on move-out day.
4. **Keep service on** in your name for cleaning, repairs and showings.
5. **Send the new tenant** the provider list for the next lease.

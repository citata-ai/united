## What is a lease violation notice?

A lease violation notice is a written notice that tells a tenant they broke a lease term and what they must do about it. It can be a friendly first warning or a formal legal step toward ending the lease. Know which one you are sending before you write it.

| Type | Purpose | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Courtesy notice | Ask the tenant to fix a minor issue | First small issue, such as trash left out |
| Lease violation notice | Put the violation on record with a deadline | Clear breach of a lease term |
| Cure or quit notice | Statutory notice: fix it or leave | Before you can end the tenancy for a non-rent breach |
| Unconditional quit notice | Statutory notice to leave, no cure | Serious breaches the law says cannot be cured |

Common lease violations include an unauthorized occupant or pet, noise complaints, parking rules, unsanitary conditions, unapproved changes to the unit and smoking where the lease bans it.

## What details must the notice include?

The notice must include enough facts that the tenant knows exactly what to fix, and that a judge could read it later. Use this list:

- [ ] Date of the notice
- [ ] All tenant names on the lease, and the address and unit
- [ ] The lease section that was broken, quoted or cited
- [ ] What happened: facts, dates, times, and how you know (inspection, photo, complaint log)
- [ ] What the tenant must do to cure it
- [ ] The deadline to cure, under your state law
- [ ] What happens if the tenant does not cure
- [ ] How and when you delivered the notice
- [ ] Your name, company and contact details

Oregon sets clear content rules. A 30-day notice for cause must describe the specific acts or omissions, give a termination date, and, if the violation can be cured, say so, describe at least one possible remedy, and set a deadline (ORS 90.392).

Watch for: opinions and labels. Write "a dog was seen in the unit on May 3 and May 9; the lease (section 12) bans pets," not "you are irresponsible." Facts only, the same way for every tenant.

## How long should the tenant have to fix it?

The tenant should have at least the cure period your state sets, and more time is fine for a courtesy notice. Here are 4 states.

| State | Cure period for a curable non-rent violation | Source |
|---|---|---|
| California | 3 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays and court holidays | Code Civ. Proc. 1161(3) |
| Florida | 7 days from delivery | Fla. Stat. 83.56(2)(b) |
| Arizona | 10 days; 5 days for breaches that materially affect health and safety | A.R.S. 33-1368(A) |
| Oregon | At least 14 days, inside a 30-day termination notice | ORS 90.392 |

California also has a just-cause rule. For many covered tenancies after 12 months, a breach of a material lease term is just cause only after a written notice to cure for curable violations (Civ. Code 1946.2).

Florida's statutory form says: "Demand is hereby made that you remedy the noncompliance within 7 days of receipt of this notice." Florida notices may be mailed, hand-delivered, emailed under 83.505, or left at the home if the tenant is absent, and these rules cannot be waived in the lease (Stat. 83.56(4)).

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## When does a violation lead to a cure or quit notice?

A violation leads to a cure or quit notice when the tenant does not fix it, when it repeats, or when it is serious enough that the law allows no cure. Each state draws these lines in its own way.

**Repeat violations.**

- **Florida:** if the same conduct recurs within 12 months after a 7-day notice, the landlord may start an eviction without a new notice (Stat. 83.56(2)(b)).
- **Oregon:** if a new violation is substantially the same as one noticed within the prior 6 months, the tenant has no right to cure, and the termination date must be at least 10 days after delivery (ORS 90.392).
- **Arizona:** a similar breach in the same lease term after a cure lets the landlord file 10 days after written notice of the second violation (A.R.S. 33-1368(A)).

**Violations with no cure.**

- **Florida:** examples include intentional damage to property and continued unreasonable disturbances. The tenant gets 7 days to leave (Stat. 83.56(2)(a)).
- **Arizona:** a breach that is "both material and irreparable" on the premises, such as drug activity or assault, allows a notice of immediate termination.
- **California:** assigning or subletting against the lease, waste, nuisance or unlawful use allows a 3-day notice to quit, with no cure (Code Civ. Proc. 1161(4)).

What to do: for any formal notice, use your state's statutory wording, or have an attorney prepare it. A defect in a formal notice can restart the clock.

## Lease violation notice template

Use this text as a first written notice. Fill in the brackets and check it against your state law. If you need a formal cure-or-quit notice, use your state's statutory form instead.

> **Notice of Lease Violation**
>
> Date: [date]
> To: [all tenant names on the lease]
> Address: [street, unit, city, state, ZIP]
> Lease dated: [date]
>
> This notice tells you that you are not following your lease.
>
> **Lease term:** Section [number], which says: "[quote the term]."
>
> **What happened:** [Facts only, with dates. Example: "On (date) and (date), our staff saw a dog in the unit during scheduled visits. No pet addendum is on file."]
>
> **What you must do:** [Specific cure. Example: "Remove the pet from the unit, or apply for a pet addendum under our pet policy."]
>
> **Deadline:** You must fix this by **[date and time]**. This gives you [number] days, as [state statute or lease section] requires.
>
> **If you do not fix it:** We may [send a formal notice under (statute) / take other steps the lease and law allow, which may include ending the tenancy].
>
> If you believe this notice is wrong, or you need a reasonable accommodation related to a disability, contact [name] at [phone] or [email] before the deadline.
>
> [Name], [title], [company]
>
> Delivered by: [hand / posted at door and mailed / email if allowed] on [date and time] by [name]

## How do you keep violation notices fair and on record?

You keep violation notices fair by using one template, one standard and one log for all tenants. Consistency is your best defense against a claim of unfair treatment.

Worked example log for a 60-unit property:

| Month | Notices sent | Cured by deadline | Repeat within 12 months | Formal notices |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| January | 4 | 3 | 0 | 1 |
| February | 2 | 2 | 1 | 0 |
| March | 3 | 3 | 0 | 0 |

The log shows owners that rules are enforced the same way, and it helps you spot a rule that causes too many notices. If parking notices pile up, the parking rule may need a change.

Ahrefs looked at about 14 billion pages in its index. It found that 96.55% of them get no traffic from Google (Ahrefs, 2023). Most of those pages are not bad. They are lone posts with no clear topic and no links around them. A content cluster is the fix for lone posts on a property management site.

## What is a pillar page and a cluster?

A pillar page is the main guide on one broad owner topic. A cluster is the group of pages that each answer one related question. The pillar gives the short answer to every sub-question. Each cluster page goes deep on one of them.

Example for a manager in one city:

| Role | Page | Owner question |
|---|---|---|
| Pillar | Property management costs in [city] | What will I pay in total? |
| Cluster | Leasing fee in [city] | What does it cost to place a tenant? |
| Cluster | Maintenance markup | Do you add a fee to repair bills? |
| Cluster | Lease renewal fee | What do I pay when the tenant renews? |
| Cluster | Vacancy cost calculator | What does an empty month cost me? |
| Cluster | Flat fee vs percent fee | Which model is cheaper for my rent? |
| Cluster | Early termination of the management agreement | What if I want to leave? |

Each page answers one question in its first sentence. That matches how AI tools read pages: they cut a page into chunks and quote the clearest one.

## Which owner topics make good clusters?

Good cluster topics are decisions an owner must make before they sign with a manager. A blog post about spring cleaning tips does not lead to a signed door. A page about what you charge to fill a vacancy can.

Five cluster themes that fit most property management sites:

1. **Cost.** Fees, markups, vacancy cost, total cost per year.
2. **Local law.** Security deposits, notice periods, eviction steps, rent rules in your state.
3. **Rent-ready and maintenance.** Turn costs, inspection rules, who pays for what.
4. **Decision guides.** Rent or sell, self-manage or hire, how to compare managers.
5. **Programs.** Section 8, short-term rental rules, HOA rules, if you serve those owners.

Pick topics from your own data first. Read your last 50 owner calls and forms. Write down each question an owner asked. Questions that come up 3 or more times are your first cluster pages.

Google's people-first content guidance asks if your page shows first-hand expertise and if a reader leaves with enough to reach their goal (Google Search Central). A local manager has that expertise: real turn costs, real days on market, real court timelines. Put those numbers on the page.

## How should cluster pages link?

Cluster pages should link up to the pillar, across to 1 or 2 close pages, and the pillar should link down to every cluster page. Use link text that says what the target page holds. Google's SEO Starter Guide says link text tells users and Google what the linked page is about (Google Search Central).

The link plan:

| From | To | Link text example |
|---|---|---|
| Pillar | Each cluster page | "leasing fee in [city]" |
| Cluster page | Pillar | "full cost of property management in [city]" |
| Cluster page | 1–2 related cluster pages | "how a maintenance markup works" |
| Cluster page | Your owner contact or rental analysis page | "get a rental analysis" |
| Service city page | Pillar | "what management costs here" |

Avoid "click here" and "read more". Avoid linking every page to every page. A link should help the reader take the next step.

Check the plan once a month. Open each cluster page and confirm 3 things: a link to the pillar, a link from the pillar, and no broken links.

## How many posts does a cluster need?

Google gives no fixed number of posts per cluster. Start with 6 to 12 pages, one per real owner question, and stop when you run out of real questions. A cluster with 8 sharp answers beats one with 30 thin posts.

Agency plans show the usual pace. Goodjuju's lowest SEO plan, $1,185 per month on 2026-10-19, lists 3 blogs per month and a "content cluster blog strategy" (Goodjuju pricing). At 3 posts a month, a 9-page cluster takes 3 months.

A simple build schedule for one cluster:

| Month | Pages | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pillar + 2 cluster pages | Write the pillar first, with short answers to all questions |
| 2 | 3 cluster pages | Link each to the pillar; update the pillar links |
| 3 | 3 cluster pages | Add one tool or table, such as a fee calculator |
| 4 | 0 new | Check leads, AI answers and links; fix weak pages |

Google says some changes show in hours and others take several months (Google Search Central). Judge a cluster after 3 to 6 months. Use owner leads and AI citations as the score, not page count.

## How do I measure a cluster?

Measure a cluster by the owner leads and AI citations it earns. Traffic alone can mislead you, because tenant visits inflate it.

Track these per cluster:

- **Owner form leads** that started on a cluster page.
- **AI citations.** Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode 5 to 10 owner questions from the cluster each month. Write down if the answer links or names your page.
- **Search Console clicks** on the cluster pages, per month.

Our guide on [tracking AI traffic](/guides/track-ai-traffic-property-management) shows how to set up the AI part. Our [AI search checklist](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website) covers the page details that help AI tools quote you.

## Cluster planning checklist

- One pillar per owner decision, not per keyword.
- Each cluster page answers one question in its first sentence.
- Local numbers on every page: costs, days, rules, with sources.
- Pillar links down, cluster pages link up and across.
- Descriptive link text, no "click here".
- Monthly link and AI answer check.
- Review the cluster at month 3 and month 6 by owner leads.

## Who needs a KCMO rental permit?

Every owner of a rental property in Kansas City, Missouri needs a yearly Healthy Homes rental permit. The KCMO Health Department says all rental property owners must register their rentals under Ordinance 180248. Voters passed the Healthy Homes Rental Inspection Program by initiative petition in August 2018, and it became active on August 7, 2018.

The program covers all kinds of rentals. The city's tenant resources page says it handles market-rate rent, rent-to-own, federally subsidized properties and Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher units.

When you must file, per the Health Department:

- **Before you open a new rental.** Submit the full application with supporting documents.
- **When the owner or the manager changes.** File again before the change takes effect.
- **Every year.** Permits run from January 1 to December 31.

If a person other than the owner fills in the form, the owner must give a letter that delegates the task. That matters if your property manager files for you.

## What does the Kansas City permit cost per unit?

The Kansas City Healthy Homes permit costs $25 per unit per year in 2026, plus a one-time $25 application fee. The Health Department lists this as a 2026 increase.

| Item | 2026 amount |
|---|---|
| Yearly permit fee | $25 per unit |
| One-time application fee | $25 |
| Permit term | January 1 to December 31 |

Source: KCMO Health Department, landlord information page, read 2026-10-20.

Worked example from the city's own page: a first-year owner of a 10-unit building pays $25 + (10 × $25) = $275. The next year, the owner pays 10 × $25 = $250.

Now scale it to a mixed portfolio: 6 single-family homes and a 24-unit building, all new to the program. Year one is $25 application fee + 30 × $25 = $775, if you file one application. Year two is 30 × $25 = $750. Ask the program whether each building needs its own application fee.

Put the fee next to rent. HUD's FY2027 two-bedroom Fair Market Rent for the Kansas City metro is $1,546, up 13.8% from $1,358. That is the largest two-bedroom rise of the 50 metros in our HUD data. The $25 permit is about 1.6% of one month's rent. See the [Kansas City Fair Market Rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/kansas-city-fair-market-rent-2027) for all sizes.

## What triggers a Healthy Homes inspection?

A tenant complaint triggers a Healthy Homes inspection. The Health Department says a tenant may file if they cannot reach the landlord, or if the landlord has not fixed a health or safety problem.

The process, per the Health Department:

1. **The tenant files** by calling 311, calling the program at 816-513-6464, emailing, or walking in at 2400 Troost Ave., Suite 3600.
2. **The program logs it** and assigns an inspector.
3. **The inspector contacts the tenant within 48 hours** to set an inspection time.
4. **The inspector checks the unit** against the program's health and safety rules and regulations.

The 2026 Tenant/Landlord Handbook lists the basic facilities a landlord must provide:

- Ventilation and heat
- Fire safety, such as clear exits and stairways
- Water and sewer lines
- Plumbing
- Electrical fixtures
- Lighted common halls and stairways
- Cooking equipment, if the unit has it

The handbook adds that a landlord may not shut off utilities unless there is a health or safety reason.

Watch for: the best way to avoid an inspection is a fast repair. The handbook tells tenants they can file without waiting once the landlord has had a reasonable time to fix the problem.

## What does the KC Tenants' Bill of Rights cover?

The Kansas City Tenant Bill of Rights is a 2019 ordinance passed by Mayor Quinton Lucas and the City Council, the city says. The city's 2026 Tenant/Landlord Handbook sets out the rights owners must respect in daily work.

| Right | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Entry notice | Give at least 24 hours' notice before entry, with who will enter, the date and time, and the reason |
| No retaliation | Do not raise rent or refuse repairs because a tenant reported a code violation or joined a tenants' union |
| Manager details | Give the tenant the name and address of the property manager in writing |
| Owner change | Give written notice if the property is sold to a new landlord |
| Lead disclosure | For homes built before 1978, give the EPA lead-based paint disclosure |
| Fair housing | No discrimination on the protected traits the handbook lists, including sexual orientation, gender identity, and status as a survivor of domestic violence, sexual assault or stalking |

Source: KCMO 2026 Tenant/Landlord Handbook.

The tenant may be present at an entry. But if you and the tenant cannot agree on a time, the handbook says you may enter at the time in your 24-hour notice.

Kansas City also funds a Right to Counsel program for tenants facing eviction, which started on June 1, 2022. Expect a tenant in an eviction case to have a lawyer.

## What are the deposit rules for Kansas City landlords?

Missouri sets the deposit rules, and the city's handbook sums them up for landlords:

- **Maximum deposit:** two months' rent.
- **Return deadline:** 30 days after the tenant moves out.
- **Penalty:** a landlord who keeps money wrongly may owe twice the amount withheld.
- **Move-out inspection:** give written notice of the time and date. The tenant may attend.
- **Itemized list:** required if you keep any part of the deposit.

At $1,546 a month, a two-month cap is $3,092.

A short checklist for each KCMO rental:

- **Register** before the first tenant moves in.
- **Renew** each year before January 1.
- **Re-file** when the owner or manager changes.
- **Delegate in writing** if your manager files.
- **Fix fast** when a tenant reports a health or safety issue.
- **Use a 24-hour entry notice** with all 3 details.

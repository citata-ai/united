## What is the ROI formula for a rental?

The ROI formula for a rental divides your yearly return by the cash you invested. ROI means return on investment.

ROI = yearly return / total cash invested x 100

Owners use three versions. Each answers a different question.

| Measure | Formula | Answers |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Cap rate | NOI / property price | How does the property earn, with no loan? |
| Cash-on-cash return | Yearly cash flow after loan / cash invested | What does my cash earn each year? |
| Total ROI | (Cash flow + principal paid down + value growth) / cash invested | What did I earn, all in, this year? |

NOI means net operating income: rent and other income, minus vacancy and operating costs. NOI does not subtract the loan payment.

Use cash-on-cash for decisions about buying. Use total ROI to look back at a year you already owned. Value growth is a guess until you sell.

## How does ROI differ for cash and financed purchases?

A cash purchase has a lower ROI on more cash. A financed purchase has a higher ROI on less cash, plus more risk.

With cash, the return is NOI and the cash invested is the full price plus costs. With a loan, the return is NOI minus the loan payment, and the cash invested is the down payment plus costs.

The loan limit sets the smallest down payment. Fannie Mae's eligibility matrix, dated August 5, 2026, allows up to 85% loan-to-value on a 1-unit investment purchase. That means at least 15% down.

Leverage cuts both ways. If NOI falls below the loan payment, you pay the gap from your own pocket. A cash buyer has no such gap.

## Which costs go into the calculation?

Every cost the property creates goes into the calculation, including costs you do not pay every month. Leaving one out is the most common way to overstate ROI.

Cash invested (the bottom of the formula):

- Down payment
- Closing costs
- Repairs and make-ready before the first tenant
- Furniture or appliances you buy for the rental

Operating costs (subtracted to get NOI):

- **Vacancy.** The Census Bureau put the US rental vacancy rate at 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026.
- **Property tax and insurance.**
- **Repairs and a capital reserve** for the roof, HVAC and water heater.
- **Management.** iPropertyManagement's survey, updated 2026-09-23, found an average of 8.49% of rent. Count it even if you self-manage.
- **HOA dues and owner-paid utilities.**

Leave out depreciation. IRS Publication 527 describes depreciation as the way to recover your cost over the property's life. It lowers tax but costs no cash that year, so it does not belong in cash flow.

## Can you show a worked example?

Yes. This example uses one $300,000 house, bought two ways. The rent and costs are made-up numbers.

Yearly NOI, the same for both:

| Line | Per year |
|------|----------|
| Gross rent ($2,300 x 12) | $27,600 |
| Vacancy (7%) | −$1,932 |
| Property tax | −$3,600 |
| Insurance | −$1,500 |
| Repairs and capital reserve (10% of rent) | −$2,760 |
| Management (8.49% of rent) | −$2,343 |
| NOI | $15,465 |

Cash purchase:

| Line | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Price | $300,000 |
| Closing and make-ready | $12,000 |
| Cash invested | $312,000 |
| Yearly return (NOI) | $15,465 |
| Cash-on-cash return | 5.0% |

Financed purchase, 15% down:

| Line | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Down payment (15%) | $45,000 |
| Closing and make-ready | $12,000 |
| Cash invested | $57,000 |
| Loan ($255,000, example 7%, 30 years), yearly principal and interest | −$20,358 |
| Yearly cash flow (NOI − loan payment) | −$4,893 |
| Cash-on-cash return | −8.6% |

At this rate and price, the financed version loses money each year. The cash version earns 5.0%. Leverage only helps when NOI covers the loan payment with room to spare.

Try 25% down instead. The loan drops to $225,000. At the same example rate, principal and interest is about $17,963 a year. Cash flow is still negative: −$2,498. This house does not work with a loan at this price and rent.

The lesson: run ROI before the offer, not after. To check the management line, use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## What is a good ROI for a rental?

There is no official good ROI for a rental. A good ROI is one that beats your next-best use of the same cash, with a margin for work and risk.

Questions to set your own bar:

1. **What would this cash earn elsewhere** at low risk?
2. **How much work will this rental take** each month?
3. **How much could go wrong:** an eviction, a roof, a rate rise at refinance?
4. **Do you need income now,** or growth later?

A rental with a 3% cash-on-cash return can still make sense for an owner who expects value growth and pays down a loan. A rental with negative cash flow needs a strong reason and a large reserve.

## ROI checklist before you offer

- **Use local comps for rent**, not the seller's number.
- **Include vacancy, capital reserve and management.**
- **Get a real loan quote**, not a guess.
- **Compute cap rate and cash-on-cash** both.
- **Run a stress test:** rent 5% lower and vacancy 3 points higher.
- **Walk away** if the deal works only on a perfect year.

This is general information, not financial or tax advice. Ask a lender or a tax professional about your case.

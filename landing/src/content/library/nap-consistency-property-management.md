## Why does NAP consistency matter for AI answers?

NAP consistency matters because AI assistants copy your contact facts from many sites, not only from your website. SOCi's 2026 Local Visibility Index found that only 68% of business contact data in ChatGPT and Perplexity matches the Google Business Profile (SOCi, 2026-08). That means about 1 in 3 contact details can be wrong.

For a property manager, a wrong phone number is a lost owner lead. The owner asks ChatGPT for a manager, gets your name, and calls a number you closed in 2022. You never know the call happened.

Our own test shows where the AI reads. In October 2026 we asked ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini "What is the best property management company in [city]?" for 50 US cities. That gave 150 answers. Listing sites appeared as sources in 132 of the 150 answers (our data, 2026-10). Expertise.com was a source in 93, Yelp in 50, iPropertyManagement in 50 and BBB in 14. Those listings feed the answer, so their NAP must be right.

## Which sites must match?

Your website and Google Business Profile must match first, because many other sites copy from them. Then fix the listings that AI cites for property management.

| Priority | Site | Why it matters | What to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your website (footer, contact page, schema) | The source you control | Name, address, phone, hours, service area |
| 2 | Google Business Profile | Feeds Google Maps, AI Overviews and Gemini | Name, address or service area, phone, category |
| 3 | Expertise.com | Source in 93 of 150 city answers (our data, 2026-10) | Name, phone, website link, service area |
| 4 | Yelp | Source in all 50 Claude answers in our test; Perplexity licenses Yelp data (Maginative, 2024) | Name, address, phone, website |
| 5 | iPropertyManagement | Source in 50 of 150 answers (our data, 2026-10) | Name, phone, website, cities |
| 6 | BBB | Source in 14 of 150 answers (our data, 2026-10) | Name, address, phone, years in business |
| 7 | Bing Places | Bing's own business listing | Same as Google |
| 8 | Apple Maps (Apple Business) | Maps on iPhone | Name, address, phone, logo |
| 9 | Facebook, LinkedIn, Nextdoor | Entity checks | Name, phone, website |
| 10 | NARPM member directory | Trade body; narpm.org was a source in 3 of 150 answers (our data, 2026-10) | Name, phone, website |

Write your NAP once in a text file. Then copy and paste it. Do not type it again on each site. "Suite 200" on one site and "Ste. 200" on another is a small change, but it is a change a machine must guess about.

## What about a PO box or a virtual office?

A PO box or a virtual office is not a valid address on Google Business Profile. Google's guidelines say "P.O. boxes or mailboxes located at remote locations aren't acceptable". A rented mailing address where you do not work is "also known as a virtual office". Google says it is not eligible for a Business Profile (Google Business Profile Help).

Many property managers work from home or meet owners at the property. Google has a rule for that case: "If you're a service-area business, you should hide your business address from customers." You then list the cities or ZIP codes you serve.

What to do:

1. **Pick a type.** Storefront (owners can visit an office with staff) or service-area business (you go to them).
2. **Keep the same type everywhere.** If Google hides your address, do not show a home address on Yelp.
3. **Put the suite in line 2.** Google asks you to enter the street address in line 1 and the suite or mailbox number in line 2.
4. **Use a local phone.** Google asks for "a local phone number instead of a central call center helpline number whenever possible". The number must be under your direct control.

## How do you find old wrong listings?

Find old listings with 5 searches. Each one takes a few minutes.

1. **Search your old phone numbers.** Put each number you ever used in quotes in Google: `"(312) 555-0100"`. Every result is a listing to fix.
2. **Search your old addresses.** Do the same with each past office address.
3. **Search old and short names.** Try your name with and without "LLC", "Inc." and "Property Management". Also try the name before a rebrand.
4. **Ask the AI.** Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity: "What is the phone number and address of [your company]?" Write down each answer and its source link.
5. **Check duplicates on Google Maps.** Search your name on Maps. Two pins for one office split your reviews.

Watch for: a listing you cannot log in to. Use the site's "claim this business" or "suggest an edit" flow. Keep a list of logins in your password manager. One person should own the list.

Paid tools scan hundreds of sites at once. Manual checks cost time but no money. Pick by door count and time.

## The NAP checklist

Copy this list into your task tool. Tick each line once a quarter.

- [ ] One master NAP file exists, with the exact name, address line 1, line 2, city, ZIP and phone.
- [ ] Website footer, contact page and LocalBusiness schema match the master file.
- [ ] Google Business Profile matches, with the right type (storefront or service area).
- [ ] No PO box and no virtual office on any listing.
- [ ] Expertise.com, Yelp, iPropertyManagement and BBB match.
- [ ] Bing Places and Apple Maps match.
- [ ] Facebook, LinkedIn and the NARPM directory match.
- [ ] Each old phone number and old address is searched. Each hit is fixed or closed.
- [ ] No duplicate pins on Google Maps.
- [ ] ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity give the right phone when you ask.
- [ ] Logins for every listing are in one shared password vault.

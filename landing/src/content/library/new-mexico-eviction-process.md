New Mexico evictions follow the Uniform Owner-Resident Relations Act, NMSA 1978 sections 47-8-1 and after. We read the current text on NMOneSource, the site of the New Mexico Compilation Commission, on 2026-10-19. Here are the main rules in one table.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice for unpaid rent | 3 days to pay after written notice | NMSA 47-8-33(D) |
| First material lease violation | 7 days to fix, after the tenant gets the notice | NMSA 47-8-33(A) |
| Second violation within 6 months | 7 days, no right to fix | NMSA 47-8-33(B) |
| Time to serve a violation notice | Within 30 days of the breach or of your knowledge of it | NMSA 47-8-33(C) |
| Substantial violation (for example, unlawful use of a deadly weapon) | 3 days, no right to fix | NMSA 47-8-33(I), 47-8-3 |
| Notice period ends on a weekend or federal holiday | Moves to the next business day | NMSA 47-8-33(H) |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | 30 days before the periodic rental date | NMSA 47-8-37(B) |
| End a week-to-week tenancy | 7 days before the termination date | NMSA 47-8-37(A) |
| Trial on the owner's petition | 7 to 10 days after service of summons | NMSA 47-8-43(A)(1) |
| Continuance for good cause | Up to 7 days | NMSA 47-8-43(B) |
| Writ of restitution | Sheriff restores possession 3 to 7 days after judgment | NMSA 47-8-46(A) |
| Tenant appeal | Tenant pays rent into escrow or to you within 5 days to stay the writ | NMSA 47-8-47(A) |
| Property left after the writ | No duty to store it after 3 days | NMSA 47-8-34.1(C) |
| Lockout or utility shutoff | Banned; 2 times the monthly rent | NMSA 47-8-36, 47-8-48(C) |
| Filing fee | $77.00 (NM Courts self-help page) | Not in statute |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What notice must a New Mexico landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

A New Mexico landlord must give a written 3-day notice of nonpayment before filing. NMSA 47-8-33(D) says the notice states the unpaid rent and your intent to end the rental agreement. If the tenant does not pay within 3 days, you may end the agreement.

The tenant can stop the case. If the tenant tenders the full amount due, in the way the notice states, before the 3 days end, you cannot sue for that nonpayment.

Watch for: do not file too early. In Cheng v. Rabey (2023-NMCA-013), the Court of Appeals said the owner may file only on the day after the third day. That landlord filed on day 3, and the court said the petition should have been dismissed.

What to do:

1. **Write the exact amount.** List rent only, or rent plus allowed charges, and say how the tenant can pay.
2. **Count the days.** If day 3 falls on a weekend or federal holiday, the period runs to the next business day (NMSA 47-8-33(H)).
3. **Keep proof.** Note the date, the time and how you delivered the notice.

## How long does an eviction take in New Mexico?

A New Mexico eviction for unpaid rent can end in about 2 to 3 weeks if nothing goes wrong. The statute sets each step. Here is a worked timeline for a fast case.

| Step | Days | Running total |
|---|---|---|
| 3-day notice runs | 3 | Day 3 |
| File the petition the next day (Cheng v. Rabey) | 1 | Day 4 |
| Serve the tenant (assume 1 day) | 1 | Day 5 |
| Trial, at least 7 days after service (NMSA 47-8-43) | 7 | Day 12 |
| Writ date, at least 3 days after judgment (NMSA 47-8-46) | 3 | Day 15 |

The slow end of the same path adds up fast. The trial can be 10 days after service. The court can continue the hearing for up to 7 days for good cause. The writ date can be 7 days after judgment. That puts a contested case near day 30, before any appeal.

An appeal by the tenant stays the writ only if the tenant pays. Under NMSA 47-8-47(A), the tenant must pay the rent through the end of that rental period. The tenant pays you or an escrow agent within 5 days of the notice of appeal. Then the tenant keeps paying monthly rent.

A violation case starts slower. The 7-day notice under NMSA 47-8-33(A) must run before you file. A 30-day notice to end a month-to-month tenancy adds a full month.

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in New Mexico, and what does it cost?

A New Mexico landlord files a petition for restitution with the clerk of the district court or the magistrate court (NMSA 47-8-42). In Bernalillo County, the Bernalillo County Metropolitan Court hears landlord-tenant cases. The New Mexico Courts site lists magistrate and metropolitan courts for landlord-tenant cases from $0 to $10,000.

The petition must state 3 things under NMSA 47-8-42:

- the facts, with particularity, on which you rely;
- a reasonably accurate description of the premises;
- how you complied with the notice rules of the Act.

The New Mexico Courts self-help page says to attach copies of all written notices and the lease. The same page lists a $77.00 fee to file a Petition by Owner for Restitution (read 2026-10-19). Service costs extra. The sheriff, a private process server, or any adult who is not a party can serve the papers.

Watch for: the court can grant relief only on the grounds in your written notice (NMSA 47-8-33(G)). A weak notice can lose a strong case.

## Can a New Mexico landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

A New Mexico landlord can evict for a lease violation, and can end a periodic tenancy with no stated cause. Each path uses a different notice.

**Lease violation.** For the first material noncompliance, the notice gives the tenant 7 days to fix it (NMSA 47-8-33(A)). The notice lists the acts, the dates and the facts. It must also say that a second breach within 6 months ends the agreement (NMSA 47-8-33(C)). For that second breach, a 7-day notice ends the agreement with no right to fix (NMSA 47-8-33(B)).

**Substantial violation.** The Act defines a short list in NMSA 47-8-3. It includes some drug crimes, unlawful use of a deadly weapon and unlawful acts that cause serious physical harm. A 3-day notice ends the agreement (NMSA 47-8-33(I)). The tenant has defenses, such as being a victim of domestic violence (NMSA 47-8-33(J)).

**No cause.** For a month-to-month tenancy, give written notice at least 30 days before the periodic rental date (NMSA 47-8-37(B)). For a week-to-week tenancy, give 7 days (NMSA 47-8-37(A)).

Retaliation is banned. NMSA 47-8-39 protects a tenant who complies with the lease. You may not bring or threaten an eviction because that tenant took a protected step in the last 6 months. Examples are a complaint to a code agency, a fair housing complaint, or a written repair request.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in New Mexico?

The sheriff removes the tenant after a New Mexico eviction judgment. Under NMSA 47-8-46(A), the court declares the rental agreement forfeited. At your request, it issues a writ of restitution. The writ directs the sheriff to restore possession on a date 3 to 7 days after the judgment.

After the writ runs, NMSA 47-8-34.1(C) applies to items the tenant leaves behind. You have no duty to store them after 3 days, unless you and the tenant agree on more time.

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in New Mexico?

A self-help eviction is not legal in New Mexico. NMSA 47-8-36(A) bans these acts without a court order:

- [ ] changing, adding or removing a lock;
- [ ] blocking an entrance;
- [ ] cutting electricity, gas, water, heat or other utilities;
- [ ] removing the tenant's property;
- [ ] removing appliances, except for real repairs.

The cost is high. The tenant may abate 100% of the rent for each day of the violation (NMSA 47-8-36(C)). You can owe 2 times the monthly rent under NMSA 47-8-48(C), plus damages.

Watch for: a judgment is not a court order for this purpose. In Roser v. Hufstedler (2023-NMCA-040), the owners had the water cut after a judgment for restitution. The Court of Appeals held that the cutoff was unlawful.

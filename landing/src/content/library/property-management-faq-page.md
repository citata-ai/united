## Which fee questions should an owner FAQ answer?

An owner FAQ should explain what the fee buys and when extra charges apply. Start with the questions owners ask before signing. Use your current agreement as the source for company facts.

Do not answer a cost question with a sales phrase. If a quote depends on property type or service scope, say so. Explain what information you need to quote. Tell the owner where the written fee schedule appears before they commit.

Cover management, leasing, lease renewals, setup, inspections, maintenance coordination, and contract exit costs where relevant. State when a fee does not apply. Avoid implying that every company charges each type of fee.

Build a review sheet before writing. Put the question, approved answer, agreement section, reviewer, and review trigger in separate fields. This makes the FAQ easier to maintain when your service changes.

| FAQ subject | Company fact to check | Useful supporting page |
|---|---|---|
| Management fee | Basis, collection timing, exclusions | Fee schedule |
| Leasing | Included work and separate charge | Leasing process |
| Repairs | Approval rules and extra fees | Maintenance scope |
| Owner payments | Report and payment process | Sample owner report |
| Contract exit | Notice and handoff duties | Agreement summary |

The table is an editorial checklist. It does not replace your contract. If your FAQ and agreement disagree, stop and resolve the conflict before publishing.

## Which legal and eviction questions belong?

Legal questions belong when they explain your role and show where local advice starts. Do not turn a general FAQ into a national eviction guide. State law, local rules, the lease, and case facts can change the answer.

Explain who reviews notices, who speaks with counsel, and which costs need owner approval. Avoid a fixed eviction timeline unless a qualified local reviewer can support the exact claim. Do not promise a court outcome or imply that hiring a manager removes owner duties.

Write screening answers around consistent criteria and a documented process. Do not describe preferred renters by protected class. Ask a local attorney to review screening, deposits, entry, notices, and termination claims before the page goes live.

Use a clear caution where legal topics appear: This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Keep the published answer focused on the service you provide. Link to a checked local guide when a longer legal explanation is needed. Give the owner a safe way to ask about their facts without posting tenant information in a public form.

## What full FAQ template can you adapt?

Use this generic template as a draft, then replace every bracketed field. The template makes no claims about your fees or local law. Remove a question if the service does not apply.

- **What properties do you manage?** We manage [property types] in [actual service area]. We review [property condition and service requirements] before offering management.
- **What does your management fee include?** Our fee covers [included tasks]. It does not cover [excluded work]. We provide the full fee schedule before you sign.
- **Do you charge while a home is vacant?** Our agreement states [vacancy fee policy]. Other approved costs may include [applicable items]. Ask for a written quote for your property.
- **How do you set asking rent?** We review [documented market evidence], property condition, and current competing listings. We discuss the proposed rent and leasing plan with you.
- **How do you screen applicants?** We use [approved written criteria] and a consistent review process. Ask us for the current criteria and local review process.
- **Who approves repairs?** The agreement sets [approval process]. We handle urgent issues under [checked contract process]. We explain charges and provide [records supplied].
- **When do owners get reports and funds?** We provide reports through [channel]. Our payment process is [actual process], subject to [checked conditions].
- **What happens if a tenant stops paying?** We follow [reviewed local process] and involve [qualified counsel or other approved role] where needed. Costs and outcomes depend on the case.
- **How do I end the agreement?** Review [contract section] for the notice process, charges, and records handoff. We explain the exit steps before you sign.
- **How do I transfer from another manager?** We first check [authority and notice requirements]. We agree a handoff plan for records, funds, keys, and open work.

Have the staff member who does each task check its answer. A sales writer should not invent a repair promise that the maintenance team cannot meet. Review legal wording with a local attorney.

## How long should each FAQ answer be?

Each answer should resolve the question before adding context. There is no need to fill space with a company history. Start with the rule or process, state the key condition, then show the next step.

For a complex fee, use a small table instead of a dense paragraph. For a handoff, use steps. If an answer needs a long legal explanation, write a separate checked guide and keep the FAQ brief.

Google's helpful-content guidance rejects the idea of a preferred Google word count. Use answer length as an editing choice, not a ranking claim. Read the answer as an owner who has not seen your sales deck.

Ask a staff member to find a repair approval rule without help. Ask an owner to explain the fee back to you. If either task fails, change the wording or page structure. Keep internal jargon out of the answer unless you define it.

Use the [management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) as an aid for cost comparisons. Make clear that your own written quote sets your company's actual fee.

## Should a property management FAQ use schema?

Do not add FAQ schema to chase a Google FAQ rich result. Google's documentation update says that feature stopped appearing in Search on May 7, 2026. The same update log records removal of the feature documentation.

Structured data means machine-readable labels for page content. A useful FAQ still helps owners even without a special search display. Focus on visible answers, clear headings, working links, and current company facts.

If your site already produces FAQ markup, ask the developer what it serves and how it stays aligned with the visible page. Do not pay for a guarantee that markup will secure rankings or AI citations.

Before publishing, remove placeholders, test the contact link, and confirm the answer owner for each section. Add a review task whenever the agreement changes. The page is ready when an owner can understand your service and your team can stand behind each answer.

## Which directories do AI tools cite most?

AI engines lean on listing sites when they name a property manager. On 2026-10-19 we asked "What is the best property management company in <City, ST>? Give names." for the 50 largest US cities. We sent the prompt to 3 engines with live web search: ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, through their APIs. That gave 150 answers (our data, 2026-10-19).

A listing site was a source in 132 of 150 answers (88%). A company's own site was a source in 106 (71%). These listing sites showed up most:

| Listing site | Answers (of 150) | ChatGPT | Claude | Gemini |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Expertise.com | 93 | 30 | 47 | 16 |
| iPropertyManagement | 50 | 1 | 42 | 7 |
| Yelp | 50 | 0 | 50 | 0 |
| ThreeBestRated | 21 | 7 | 5 | 9 |
| PropertyManagement.com | 19 | 8 | 8 | 3 |
| Birdeye review pages | 19 | 17 | 2 | 0 |
| BBB | 14 | 11 | 3 | 0 |
| Clutch | 10 | 0 | 10 | 0 |
| Thumbtack | 7 | 0 | 4 | 3 |

Gemini also cited reddit.com in 22 answers. The other 2 engines did not cite it at all.

Three lessons. First, each engine has its own favorites, so one listing is not enough. Second, Expertise.com is the one source all 3 engines use often. Third, this is one prompt on one day. AI answers change, and a citation in our test is a link, not proof of cause.

## Which directories should I claim first?

Claim the free listings first, then fix the ones you cannot claim. This table sorts the main sites by cost and control.

| Directory | Cost to list | Can you claim it? | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Free (Google) | Yes | Map pack and the base fact source for Google |
| Yelp | Free page (Yelp); ads extra | Yes | Claude cited it in all 50 of its answers |
| Expertise.com | Not for sale; selected by research | No application in this category | Top AI source in our test |
| iPropertyManagement | Check the site | Check the site | Claude cited it in 42 of 50 answers |
| BBB | Accreditation has annual dues (BBB) | Profile via accreditation | ChatGPT cited it in 11 of 50 answers |
| Bing Places | Check the site | Yes | Bing's own business listing |
| Apple Business | Check the site | Yes | Puts your business on Apple Maps (Apple) |
| State or local association directory | Membership | Members only | Trust signal for owners |

Watch for: Expertise.com says "the vast majority" of its listed providers are picked by its own research and review. It accepts review requests only in its Legal and Home Improvement categories. Property management is not one of them. You influence it through what its process reads: reviews, licenses, years in business, clear services and how fast you answer calls.

## Which directories are free?

Google Business Profile and the Yelp Business Page are free, by their own pages. Google says: "You can add or claim your business at no charge." Yelp says its Business Page "is a free profile" with 20+ tools. Both sell paid ads on top.

BBB accreditation is not free. BBB says "Accredited businesses pay an annual dues investment," and does not list the amount. Expertise.com has no paid entry. It says a provider "may choose to advertise" only after it passes review and selection.

For other sites, read the price page the day you sign up. Free listings often push you to a paid plan or a pay-per-lead model.

## Which listings send real owner leads?

Few directories send many owner leads on their own. Their larger value is trust: an owner checks a name on Yelp or BBB, and AI engines quote them.

The Goodjuju 2026 study tracked 35 property management companies in fall 2026. Owner leads by channel:

| Channel | Share of owner leads |
|---|---|
| Google Search and map pack | 50.6% |
| Direct and Maps | 35.6% |
| Other search | 4.3% |
| Referral (includes directory clicks) | 3.7% |
| AI tools | 3.4% |

Source: Goodjuju lead source report, fall 2026. Leads are web forms only. Phone calls from listings are not counted, so directory value is likely higher than 3.7%.

So measure your own. Add a UTM tag (a source label in the link) to each listing link you control. Ask every new owner, "Where did you first hear about us?" Log the answer in your CRM.

## How do I keep directory facts the same everywhere?

Pick one master record and copy it to every site. Google asks you to represent the business "as it's consistently represented and recognized in the real world." It also says it reads facts "from mentions about your business throughout the web."

Your master record:

```text
Name:           [exact legal or brand name, no keywords]
Address:        [staffed office] or "service area only"
Service area:   [city 1], [city 2], [city 3]
Phone:          [one local number, the one a person answers]
Website:        [https://example.com]
Hours:          [phone hours]
Year founded:   [year]
License:        [state] license [number]
Services:       Full-service management; leasing only; rent collection;
                tenant screening; maintenance; owner accounting
Doors managed:  [number, as of date] (only if true)
Short bio:      [2 sentences, same on every site]
```

Then audit each site against it. A small spreadsheet works: one row per site, one column per field, a cell turns red when it differs.

Directories still help local rank, but less than before. The Whitespark 2026 survey of 47 local search experts says citations "lost about half a percentage point" of weight. So fix wrong facts first. Do not chase hundreds of low-value listings.

## Directory checklist

1. **Write the master record** above and get sign-off from the owner of the business.
2. **Claim Google Business Profile** and match it to the record.
3. **Claim Yelp** and fix name, phone, hours and category. Do not ask for Yelp reviews: Yelp bans it.
4. **Find your Expertise.com city page.** If you are listed, check every fact. If not, work on reviews and public facts.
5. **Check iPropertyManagement, BBB, ThreeBestRated and PropertyManagement.com** for your name and fix wrong facts.
6. **Claim Bing Places and Apple Business** with the same record.
7. **Join your state or local association** if it fits, and fill its member profile.
8. **Remove duplicates** with an old name, an old phone or an old address.
9. **Tag every link** you control with a UTM source.
10. **Repeat each quarter** and after any change of phone, address or name.

## Which tools can write rental listings?

DoorLoop advertises listing writing through its AI Assistant. TenantCloud lists AI listing descriptions on its pricing page. Check access in your proposed plan rather than assuming every account includes the feature.

A general writing assistant can also draft from facts you supply. The core task remains the same: turn approved information into readable text. A connection to property records may reduce copying, but it does not establish that every field is current.

| Tool approach | Possible benefit | Required check |
|---|---|---|
| Built-in PMS assistant | Uses the property workflow | Source fields and plan access |
| Separate writing assistant | Flexible draft instructions | Only approved data supplied |
| Human editor | Can resolve unclear claims | Facts checked against records |

Sources: DoorLoop and TenantCloud pricing pages, read October 19, 2026. The table is our workflow comparison, not an accuracy ranking.

Do not give a drafting tool unnecessary resident records. Use property facts and approved public terms. Keep the draft separate from live publishing until review passes.

## Which words create fair housing concerns?

Review claims about preferred residents, protected groups and access to housing. A word list alone cannot establish whether an advertisement complies.

Federal section 3604(c), reproduced by Cornell LII, prohibits housing ads that indicate preferences or limits based on listed protected characteristics. Those include race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status and national origin.

Use property descriptions rather than statements about who belongs there. An adults-only preference in an ordinary rental advertisement needs legal review. Do not let an assistant add a preferred age, family type or religion.

Describe confirmed physical features plainly. If access details matter, verify them rather than guessing from photographs. Avoid unsupported claims about neighborhood safety or the people who live nearby.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney. Review local protections and the actual context before approving a listing. AI drafting does not remove the publisher's need to inspect the words.

## How do you add facts AI does not know?

Start with an approved fact sheet. Mark missing information clearly and instruct the tool to omit it.

Zillow's listing guidance identifies property details, rent, lease duration, fees, parking and other listing facts. Use current records for those fields. Do not ask the model to infer them from a nearby home or an old advertisement.

Keep a source beside each fact. A floor plan may support layout, while an approved lease schedule supports charges. Record when a person checked availability and condition.

Use this reusable input template:

```text
Task: Draft a rental property description.
Use only the approved facts below.
Do not infer amenities, measurements, fees or availability.
Describe the property, not a preferred type of resident.
Do not add neighborhood safety or demographic claims.
Omit unknown fields and list questions for the reviewer.

Approved property facts:
Property type:
Location description approved for publication:
Bedrooms and bathrooms:
Verified size:
Confirmed amenities:
Parking:
Utilities included:
Rent and required fees:
Deposit terms:
Lease term:
Availability:
Approved application and showing route:

Unknown or unresolved details:
Required wording approved by the manager:
```

Keep the template separate from resident or applicant information. The model needs a fact sheet for the home, not private details about people.

## How do you check the result?

Compare every claim with the fact sheet before editing style. Fluency does not establish accuracy.

Check numbers, dates, fees and included services first. A draft may add a washing machine, parking space or renovation that no record confirms. Delete unsupported details instead of softening them into vague promises.

Then inspect tone and fair housing language. Remove statements about preferred residents. Keep the description focused on the home, lease and application route.

Use this approval checklist:

- Confirm the correct property and unit.
- Match all measurements to records.
- Verify amenities and current condition.
- Check availability and lease dates.
- Compare rent and charges with approved terms.
- Review protected-class preferences.
- Check application and showing links.
- Save the approved version.
- Inspect each published destination.

A second person should resolve uncertain facts before release. If the source record remains unclear, omit the claim or pause publication. Do not ask the model to make the uncertainty sound convincing.

## Does AI-written text hurt listing reach?

No verified penalty or reach gain for AI-written rental descriptions is established in the reviewed sources. Do not promise more views merely because the wording changed.

Track your own results with care. Record listing date, price, photos, availability and distribution. Changes in those factors can affect inquiries, so do not attribute every change to copy.

Inspect the published page after syndication. Confirm important terms survived formatting and truncation. An accurate source description can still appear with stale prices or missing fees elsewhere.

Use questions from real inquiries to improve clarity. If people repeatedly ask about utilities, check whether the listing explains them correctly. Do not fill the text with repeated phrases in pursuit of an unsupported ranking claim.

Keep the evidence simple: the approved text, the publication record and the questions it did or did not answer.

## What should the team do after publishing?

Assign a person to keep the listing current. AI does not know a unit became unavailable unless the correct information reaches the workflow.

Check updates to price, terms and amenities against approved records. Inspect the live destinations after a change. Remove stale drafts from staff instructions so someone does not copy them into a later listing.

Keep an error log with the original claim, source and correction. Use it to improve the fact sheet and review checklist. Do not treat a clean draft as permission to remove human approval.

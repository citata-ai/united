## Texas eviction rules at a glance

The Texas eviction process lives in Chapter 24 of the Texas Property Code. Senate Bill 38 (Acts 2025, 89th Leg., Ch. 960) changed many sections of Chapter 24 on January 1, 2026. The table shows the rules for a case filed on or after that date.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice before you file | At least 3 days, written, unless the lease says otherwise | Prop. Code 24.005(a) |
| Notice form, first-time nonpayment | Notice to pay rent or vacate | Prop. Code 24.005(a) |
| Notice delivery | Mail, inside the unit, hand delivery to a tenant 16+, or email if agreed in writing | Prop. Code 24.005(f-3) |
| Where to file | Justice court in the precinct where the unit is | Prop. Code 24.004, 24.0041 |
| Rent claim in the same case | Up to $20,000 | TRCP 510.6(e) |
| Service of citation | Officer tries by the 5th business day after filing | Prop. Code 24.0051(f) |
| Trial date | 10th to 21st day after filing, and not before the 4th day after service | Prop. Code 24.0051(g) |
| Appeal | Within 5 days after the judgment is signed | Prop. Code 24.005107(a) |
| Writ of possession | Not before the 6th day after judgment | Prop. Code 24.0061(b) |
| Writ service | Officer serves by the 5th business day after issue | Prop. Code 24.0061(b-1) |
| Warning before removal | Posted at least 24 hours before the writ is executed | Prop. Code 24.0061(d)(1) |

Days count under Property Code 24.0042. You skip the day of the event and count weekends and holidays. A deadline that falls on a weekend or holiday moves to the next business day.

## What notice must a Texas landlord give for unpaid rent?

A Texas landlord must give at least 3 days' written notice before filing (Property Code 24.005(a)). The written lease can set a shorter or longer period. SB 38 added a rule on the form of that notice in a nonpayment case.

- **Tenant who was not late before this month:** the notice must be a notice to pay rent or vacate.
- **Tenant who was late or delinquent before this month:** you may use either a notice to pay rent or vacate or a notice to vacate.

You can deliver the notice by mail (first class, registered, certified or a delivery service). You can also put it inside the unit in a clear place, or hand it to a tenant aged 16 or older. Email works only if you and the tenant agreed to it in writing (24.005(f-3)). If the tenant actually receives the notice, the method rules do not apply (24.005(f-4)).

Watch for: federal notice rules. If a federal law requires a longer notice, you may still file after the Texas notice. But the writ cannot be served until the full federal period has passed since your notice (24.005(c-1)).

## How long does an eviction take in Texas?

An uncontested Texas eviction often takes about 3 to 4 weeks from notice to removal. Here is a worked timeline with real 2026 dates. It uses the fastest dates the statute allows.

1. **Monday, March 2:** you deliver a 3-day notice to pay rent or vacate.
2. **Thursday, March 5:** the 3 days end (day 1 is March 3, per 24.0042).
3. **Friday, March 6:** you file the sworn petition and pay the fees.
4. **By Friday, March 13:** the constable tries to serve the citation (5th business day, 24.0051(f)).
5. **Monday, March 16:** earliest trial date, the 10th day after filing. The latest is March 27.
6. **March 16:** the judge signs a judgment for possession.
7. **Monday, March 23:** the 5-day appeal period ends. Day 5 is Saturday, March 21, so the deadline moves to Monday.
8. **March 23:** you request the writ. The 6th day after judgment is Sunday, March 22.
9. **By Monday, March 30:** the officer serves the writ and posts a 24-hour warning.
10. **About March 31:** the officer executes the writ.

That is about 4 weeks. A trial set later in the 10-to-21-day window, a slow service or an appeal adds time. In a county court appeal, the county court must hold a trial within 21 days after it gets the papers (24.005107(c)).

## Where do you file a Texas eviction, and what does it cost?

You file a Texas eviction in the justice court of the precinct where the property is (Property Code 24.004 and 24.0041). The petition must be sworn (24.00505). Under the amended Rule 510.6(c), the judge must dismiss a case filed in the wrong precinct. The filing fee is not refunded.

The petition must name all tenants on the written lease who live in the unit and whom you want to evict (Rule 510.6(d)). It must also say when and how you gave the pre-suit notice, and which kind of notice it was (Rule 510.6(a)).

Fees differ by county. Here is one example, the Travis County Justices of the Peace schedule as of January 1, 2026:

| Fee | Travis County amount |
|---|---|
| Eviction filing fee | $54.00 |
| Service on 1 party | $90.00 |
| Total to file against 1 tenant | $144.00 |
| Writ of possession | $205.00 |
| Appeal | $54.00 |

So a Travis County case with 1 tenant and a writ costs at least $349 in court fees ($144 + $205). Check your own precinct's schedule before you file.

A note on the court rules: the Supreme Court of Texas rewrote Rule 510 for cases filed on or after January 1, 2026. Its order of December 31, 2025 (Misc. Docket No. 25-9105) gave preliminary approval and invited comments until February 1, 2026. Read the current rule text before you file.

## Can a Texas landlord evict for a lease violation or with no cause?

A Texas landlord can evict a tenant who "defaults" under the lease, with the same 3-day written notice (Property Code 24.005(a)). The lease defines what counts as a default. So write clear lease terms.

No-cause rules depend on the tenancy:

- **Fixed-term lease:** you can file for a default, or when the tenant holds over after the term ends (24.005(a)).
- **Holdover after the term ends:** you give the 3-day notice. You must also meet the termination rules of Section 91.001 (24.005(a)).
- **Month-to-month:** under 91.001(b), the tenancy ends on the later of the date in your notice or 1 month after you give the notice. A signed agreement can set a different notice period (91.001(e)).

Fair Housing law still applies to every reason you give.

## Who removes the tenant after a Texas eviction judgment?

A sheriff or constable removes the tenant, not the landlord. The writ of possession orders the officer to post a warning at least 24 hours before removal (Property Code 24.0061(d)). Then the officer gives you possession and tells everyone in the unit to leave. If they do not, the officer physically removes them.

Since January 1, 2026, the sheriff or constable must serve the writ by the 5th business day. If not, you may use another trained law enforcement officer (24.0061(b-1)). The officer may store the tenant's things with a bonded warehouse. The officer cannot make you store them (24.0061(e), (f)). Property is not put outside while it is raining, sleeting or snowing (24.0061(d)).

## Is a self-help eviction legal in Texas?

A self-help eviction is not legal in Texas. Only a court judgment and a writ end the tenant's right to possession. Two sections of Chapter 92 set the limits:

- **Lockouts (92.0081):** you may change the locks for unpaid rent only if the lease allows it and you give advance written notice. You must give the tenant a new key at any hour, even if the tenant pays nothing.
- **Utility shutoffs (92.008):** you may not cut utilities except for bona fide repairs, construction or an emergency. A narrow exception covers submetered electricity, with its own notice rules.

The penalty for each is the tenant's actual damages, 1 month's rent plus $1,000, attorney's fees and court costs. The tenant can also take back possession or end the lease (92.008(f), 92.0081(h)).

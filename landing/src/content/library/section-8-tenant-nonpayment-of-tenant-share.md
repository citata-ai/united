## Does the PHA cover the missing tenant share?

The PHA does not cover a missing tenant share. 24 CFR 982.515(c) says the PHA "may not use housing assistance payments or other program funds" to pay any part of the family share. "Payment of the whole family share is the responsibility of the family."

The two parts of the rent are separate:

| Part of rent | Who pays | Who collects | If unpaid |
|---|---|---|---|
| HAP | PHA | PHA pays you | Not the tenant's debt (24 CFR 982.451(b)(4)(iii)) |
| Tenant share | Family | You (24 CFR 982.452(b)(5)) | A lease violation by the tenant |

The reverse is also true. 24 CFR 982.310(b) says a missed HAP is not the tenant's lease violation. You may not end the tenancy because the PHA paid late.

## Can I charge a late fee on the tenant share?

You can charge a late fee on the tenant share if your lease allows it, state law permits it, and you charge the same fee to unassisted tenants. The federal rules do not set a late fee for tenants. But 24 CFR 982.451(b)(5)(ii) shows that late fees on the family's rent are a normal practice: the HAP contract must give owners late penalties against the PHA when the owner charges the same penalties to all tenants, including late payment of family rent.

Rules of thumb:

- **Put the fee in the lease.** Send the PHA a copy of the lease, as you must for every assisted lease.
- **Base the fee on the tenant share,** not the full contract rent. The tenant does not owe the HAP part.
- **Follow your state's cap.** Some states limit late fees or set grace periods.

A worked example, with made-up numbers and a 5% late fee:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Contract rent | $1,600 |
| HAP, paid on time | $1,250 |
| Tenant share | $350 |
| 5% late fee on the tenant share | $17.50 |
| 5% of the full contract rent (do not use) | $80 |

## Should I tell the PHA?

Tell the PHA early, and you must send it a copy of any eviction notice. 24 CFR 982.310(e)(2)(ii) says: "The owner must give the PHA a copy of any owner eviction notice to the tenant." An eviction notice includes a notice to vacate, or the court filing that starts an eviction.

A short email to the PHA when the tenant first falls behind also helps. If the family's income fell, the family may ask the PHA for an interim reexamination (24 CFR 982.516(c)). The PHA should generally finish it within 30 days. It may decline if adjusted income drops by less than 10%. A new, lower tenant share may make the rent payable again.

## Is nonpayment grounds for eviction?

Nonpayment of the tenant share is grounds for eviction. 24 CFR 982.310(a)(1) lists "failure to pay rent or other amounts due under the lease" as a serious violation. That ground works during the initial lease term too.

Steps to follow:

1. **Send a written notice of grounds.** It must state the grounds for ending the tenancy (982.310(e)(1)). It may be part of your state's pay-or-quit notice.
2. **Check the federal 30-day notice.** Under the CARES Act, 15 U.S.C. 9058(c), the lessor of a covered dwelling may not require the tenant to leave before 30 days after a notice to vacate. Covered properties include those in programs under 42 U.S.C. 1437f, which is Section 8 (34 U.S.C. 12491). Ask a local attorney how courts in your state apply it.
3. **Send the PHA a copy** of the notice the same day (982.310(e)(2)(ii)).
4. **File in court** if the tenant does not pay or leave. You may evict only by court action (982.310(f)).
5. **Keep taking HAP.** During the eviction, the PHA must keep paying HAP until you get a court judgment, if the family still lives in the unit (982.311(b)).
6. **Use the deposit at move-out** for unpaid tenant rent, with an itemized list (24 CFR 982.313).

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Can the tenant lose the voucher?

The tenant can lose the voucher for nonpayment. Two rules apply:

- **Mandatory end after eviction.** 24 CFR 982.552(b)(2): "The PHA must terminate program assistance for a family evicted from housing assisted under the program for serious violation of the lease."
- **Optional end before eviction.** 24 CFR 982.551(e) says the family may not commit any serious or repeated lease violation. 982.552(c)(1)(i) lets the PHA end assistance for a family that breaks its obligations.

The PHA must still consider the facts. 982.552(c)(2) lets it weigh the seriousness of the case and the effect on family members who were not involved. Note one exception: under HUD's 2024 HUD-VASH notice, a PHA "may," not "must," end a HUD-VASH voucher after an eviction.

Because the voucher is at stake, a tenant has a strong reason to agree to a payment plan. A written plan, signed by both of you, with a copy to the PHA, can cost you less than a court case.

## Checklist: tenant share not paid

- [ ] Confirm the HAP arrived, so you know the shortfall is only the tenant share.
- [ ] Check the tenant share on the PHA's latest notice.
- [ ] Charge a late fee only as the lease and state law allow.
- [ ] Send written notice of grounds, with your state's required notice period.
- [ ] Send the PHA a copy the same day.
- [ ] Offer a written payment plan if you want to keep the tenancy.
- [ ] File in court only after the notice period ends.

## Who needs a Denver residential rental property license?

Every landlord who rents a dwelling unit in the City of Denver for 30 days or more needs a Residential Rental Property License (Denver Business Licensing Center, FAQ). That covers a single house, a condo, a duplex or an apartment complex. Property managers who run rentals for owners fall under the same rule.

The license basics, from the city's pages:

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Who | Anyone who offers or operates a rental of 30 days or more |
| Term | 4 years, unless the ownership changes |
| Before you apply | A passing inspection by a certified private home inspector |
| Apply by | Within 90 days of the first inspection |
| One license or many | One per single-unit property. One license can cover a multiunit property on one or adjoining parcels under the same owner. |
| Ads | Show the license number in every rental ad |
| Tenant notice | Give the Denver Tenant Rights and Resources document at lease signing, and with any rent demand |
| Renewal | Apply 90 days before expiry. Renew on or before the expiry date to avoid penalty fees. A renewal needs a new inspection. |

What counts as renting: the city says any benefit from a tenant counts. If the tenant pays rent, or helps with the mortgage, property tax or HOA fees, you need a license. If the tenant pays only the utilities they use, you do not.

Not covered: an owner who lives on site and rents a room without its own kitchen and bathroom. A basement apartment or ADU with its own entrance, kitchen and full bathroom does need a license. Hotels, rooming houses and short-term rentals need other license types.

The fee: the city says it kept the fees "as low as possible". The pages we read do not list the amounts, so check the fee in Denver's online permitting portal before you budget. Some affordable and nonprofit housing qualifies for a fee waiver.

## What does the Denver rental inspection check?

The Denver rental inspection checks minimum, existing health and safety standards in the housing code. The city says it is not meant to be a full home inspection. The inspector fills in a city-approved checklist, which the Residential Rental Checklist Guidebook explains.

Who can inspect: a third-party inspector certified as a home inspector by ASHI, InterNACHI or the Master Inspector Certification Board, and also certified by the International Code Council as an R5, C5 or C8 Combination Building Inspector. Pick one from the city's registry of qualified inspectors.

How many units: a single-unit property needs 1 inspection. A multiunit property needs 10% of its units inspected, plus shared utilities and building-wide items.

Exemptions: new construction with a certificate of occupancy from the last 4 years can skip the inspection. Some affordable housing can submit a federal, state or local agency inspection from the last 4 years instead.

Step list for a single-family owner:

1. **Confirm the address** is a valid Denver address.
2. **Hire an inspector** from the city registry.
3. **Fix any failed items.** Structural, mechanical, electrical or plumbing repairs may need a permit.
4. **Apply online** within 90 days of the inspection, with the checklist and the inspector's certificates.
5. **Pay the fee** in the portal.
6. **Put the license number** in every ad.
7. **Calendar the renewal** for 3 years and 9 months later.

The cost of skipping it: unlicensed rentals face fines and citations from $150 to $5,000 under DRMC 27-203. With a housing code violation, the first citation is $250. And the city says you cannot legally rent the unit without a license.

## What does Colorado HB24-1098 require for ending a lease?

Colorado HB24-1098 requires cause to evict a residential tenant, with some exceptions. The Governor signed it on 2024-04-19, and it took effect that day (Colorado General Assembly bill summary). If you evict without cause, the tenant can use that as a defense in court.

Cause exists only in 3 groups of cases:

| Group | Examples from the bill summary |
|---|---|
| Unlawful detention | Situations in existing law, such as nonpayment, as amended by the act |
| Nuisance or damage | Conduct that disturbs the landlord or other tenants, or negligent damage to the property |
| No-fault grounds | Demolition or conversion; major repairs or renovation; move-in by the landlord or family; taking the unit off the market to sell; a tenant who refuses a new lease with reasonable terms; a history of nonpayment |

What this means in practice: the end of a lease term is not a reason by itself. Write down which ground you rely on before you send any notice. The bill summary says "with certain exceptions" and "with certain limitations", so read the act or ask an attorney about your case.

## Can Denver cap rents?

No. C.R.S. 38-12-301 declares rent control a matter of statewide concern. It says no county or municipality may enact an ordinance or resolution that would control rent on private residential property. Voluntary agreements with a developer or owner to limit rent, and voluntary deed restrictions, are allowed.

State law does limit how often you raise rent. Under HB21-1121, a landlord may raise residential rent only once in a 12-month period. With no written lease, you must give 60 days' notice before an increase.

For context on Denver rents: HUD's FY2027 fair market rent for a Denver-area 2-bedroom is $2,169, up 3.8% from $2,089. See [Denver fair market rent 2027](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/denver-fair-market-rent-2027) for each unit size.

## What fees can a Colorado landlord charge?

Colorado limits screening fees and requires all-in pricing. Two laws matter most:

- **HB23-1099 (Rental Application Fairness Act).** If a prospective tenant gives you a portable screening report from a consumer reporting agency, made in the last 30 days, you cannot charge an application fee or a fee to use it. Before you collect data that triggers a fee, you must tell the applicant you accept such reports. A violation costs $2,500 plus court costs and attorney fees. If you fix it within 7 days of notice, you pay a $50 penalty instead.
- **HB25-1090 (deceptive pricing).** Any advertised price must show the total of all amounts a person must pay. The act also bars landlords from charging certain fees and from putting them in a lease. A tenant can demand a refund of a banned fee. If you do not pay within 14 days, you owe actual damages plus 18% interest.

Checklist before you list a Denver rental:

- [ ] License in hand and the number in the ad.
- [ ] The total monthly price in the ad, with all required charges.
- [ ] Screening process that accepts portable reports.
- [ ] Tenant Rights and Resources document ready for lease signing.
- [ ] A written ground for any future non-renewal under HB24-1098.

## What goes in the first line of a rental listing?

The first line of a rental listing holds the facts renters filter on: rent, beds, baths, area and move-in date. Renters often scan a list of results. A headline that shows these facts gets the click from the right person and saves a call from the wrong one.

Use this pattern:

`$[rent] [beds]BR/[baths]BA [area or street] · [1 top feature] · Available [date]`

Two examples:

- Weak: "Charming home, must see."
- Strong: "$1,850 2BR/1BA near Lincoln Park · in-unit laundry · Available Nov 1"

The strong version answers 5 questions before the renter opens the ad. Pick the top feature by what renters in your area ask about most. Laundry, parking, a yard or pets allowed are common choices.

## How long should the description be?

The description should be long enough to answer the common questions in one read. For most units that is 150 to 300 words. Write short lines. Put the facts in a list, not in a block of prose.

A clear structure:

1. **Opening, 2 sentences.** The unit type, the floor or layout, and the best 2 features.
2. **Fact list.** Rent, deposit, fees, size, beds, baths, parking, laundry, heat and cooling, appliances.
3. **Utilities.** Which ones the tenant pays and which ones the owner pays.
4. **Pets.** Allowed or not, limits, and pet fees. Note that assistance animals are not pets.
5. **Location, 1–2 sentences.** Transit lines, main roads, distance to a large employer. State facts about the place, not about the people who live there.
6. **Screening criteria.** See the section below.
7. **Next step.** How to book a showing or apply, with one link.

Photos carry as much weight as the text. Show each room, the outside, the parking and the laundry. Add a floor plan if you have one. Put the best photo first.

## Which details cut unqualified calls?

The details that cut unqualified calls are the ones that make a renter rule themselves out. Each unanswered question becomes an email or a call. Answer it in the ad.

| Detail | Example text | Calls it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| Full move-in cost | "Move-in: first month $1,850 + deposit $1,850 + $45 application fee per adult" | "How much to move in?" |
| Lease length | "12-month lease, starts Nov 1 or Nov 15" | Short-term seekers |
| Pet terms | "Up to 2 cats or dogs, $300 pet deposit, $35/mo pet rent" | Pet owners outside the limits |
| Parking | "1 assigned space, street parking only for a second car" | Two-car households who need two spaces |
| Utilities | "Tenant pays electric and gas; owner pays water, sewer and trash" | "What are the utilities?" |
| Income rule | "Household income of 3x rent, or a voucher or other lawful income where the law requires it" | Applicants under the income rule |
| Showing method | "Book a showing at [link]; self-tour or agent tour" | "When can I see it?" |

Make sure each fee you list is legal in your state. Fee rules differ by state.

A worked example shows why speed matters. A unit at $1,850 a month loses about $61 of rent each day it sits empty ($1,850 × 12 ÷ 365). Ten fewer vacant days is about $610 kept. Clear ads do not guarantee that, but each call you avoid is time you can spend on real applicants.

## Which words break Fair Housing advertising rules?

Words that show a preference for or against a protected class break Fair Housing advertising rules. The federal rule is 24 CFR 100.75. It bars any notice, statement or ad that "indicates any preference, limitation or discrimination" because of race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status or national origin. It covers words, photos, symbols and where you choose to advertise.

The safe test: describe the property, not the person.

| Avoid | Why | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Perfect for a young couple" | Familial status preference | "2 bedrooms, 900 sq ft" |
| "Adults only", "no kids" | Familial status | Omit; state the occupancy facts that apply to everyone |
| "Christian home", "near our church" | Religion preference | "Near Main St and 5th Ave" |
| "Ideal for a single professional" | Familial status preference | "Quiet building, close to downtown" |
| "No wheelchairs", "must climb stairs" | Disability | "Third-floor walk-up, no elevator" |
| "English speakers only" | Can show national origin preference | Omit |
| "Safe, exclusive neighborhood" | Can imply who lives there | "2 blocks from the Blue Line" |

Also check your state law. Some states protect more classes than federal law. Two examples:

- **California.** Government Code 12955(c) bars ads that show a preference based on a long list of classes. The list includes source of income, sexual orientation, marital status, gender identity, veteran or military status, and more. Section 12955(p) defines source of income to include Section 8 vouchers.
- **New York.** Executive Law 296(5)(a)(3) bars ads that show a preference based on lawful source of income, familial status, and other classes.

So "no Section 8" in an ad is illegal in California and New York. Check your own state and city before you write any income or voucher line.

## Should I list the screening criteria in the ad?

Yes, list the screening criteria in the ad or link to them. Written criteria that you apply to every applicant help in 2 ways. Unqualified renters rule themselves out. You also have a record that you treat every applicant the same.

A short criteria block for a listing:

- Household income of [X] times the monthly rent, from any lawful source.
- No eviction judgment in the last [X] years. State the rule your local law allows.
- Credit check: state what you check and what counts.
- Rental history: [X] years of verifiable history, or a co-signer.
- Every adult 18+ applies. Application fee: $[X] per adult.
- We follow federal, state and local fair housing law.

Keep each rule tied to the lease: paying rent, caring for the unit, following the rules. Check state and local rules on criminal history, credit and income criteria before you post. They differ by place.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Rental listing checklist before you post

Use this checklist before you publish.

- [ ] Headline has rent, beds, baths, area, top feature and date.
- [ ] Description is 150–300 words, with a fact list.
- [ ] Full move-in cost is stated, and each fee is legal in your state.
- [ ] Utilities, pets, parking and lease length are stated.
- [ ] Screening criteria are posted or linked.
- [ ] No words describe the ideal renter. Only the property.
- [ ] Income rule allows lawful income and vouchers where the law requires it.
- [ ] 15+ photos, best photo first, every room shown.
- [ ] One clear next step: a showing link or an application link.

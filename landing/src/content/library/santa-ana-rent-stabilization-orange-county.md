## Which Santa Ana rentals are covered?

Santa Ana's rent cap covers older rental buildings and older mobilehome parks. The city says the cap applies to residential structures built on or before 1995-02-01 and to mobilehome parks set up before 1990, regardless of ownership. It does not apply to units that state law exempts.

The state law is the Costa-Hawkins Rental Housing Act. California Civil Code 1954.52(a) lets an owner set the rent for:

- **Newer units.** A certificate of occupancy issued after 1995-02-01.
- **Separately sold units.** A unit "alienable separate from the title to any other dwelling unit." This covers many single-family homes and condos.

The just cause rules reach further. They protect most tenants after 30 days in the home. The city lists limited just cause exemptions, such as housing built in the last 15 years, deed-restricted affordable housing, hotels, care facilities and dormitories. You must apply for an exemption through the Rental Registry, and renew the claim each year by July 1.

| Your rental | Rent cap | Just cause | Registry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fourplex built in 1978 | Yes | Yes | Yes, register and pay |
| House built in 1965, owned by a person | Usually no (Costa-Hawkins) | Yes, unless exempt | Register; claim the exemption yearly |
| Apartment built in 2018 | No | No (built in the last 15 years) | Claim the exemption yearly |
| Mobilehome space, park from 1985 | Yes | Yes | Yes |

This table shows typical cases. Confirm each unit with the city's Property Activity Tool or the Rent Stabilization Division at (714) 667-2209.

## What is the yearly allowed increase in Santa Ana?

The yearly allowed increase in Santa Ana is the lower of 3% or 80% of the CPI change. For 2026-09-01 to 2027-08-31 it is 2.87%. The city says the CPI rose 3.59% from May 2025 to May 2026, and 80% of that is 2.87%.

| Period | Allowed increase | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 2024-09-01 to 2025-08-31 | 3.00% | 80% of CPI was 3.11%, so the 3% cap applied |
| 2025-09-01 to 2026-08-31 | 2.42% | 80% of CPI |
| 2026-09-01 to 2027-08-31 | 2.87% | 80% of 3.59% |

Worked example: a covered 2-bedroom rents for $3,057, the HUD FY2027 fair market rent for the Santa Ana-Anaheim-Irvine area. The most you can add is 2.87% × $3,057 = $87.74, for a new rent of $3,144.74. Under the state cap in Civil Code 1947.12, the limit would be 5% plus the Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim CPI change, up to 10%. Santa Ana's cap is much tighter.

Rules that apply to every increase:

- **Once in 12 months.** Only 1 increase in any 12-month period.
- **Registry first.** No increase takes effect until the unit's registration is complete and accurate.
- **Written notice.** Give tenants written notice of the ordinance and their rights at the start of a lease and with any rent increase notice, in the language you used to negotiate the lease.
- **Petitions.** You can file a Fair Return Petition for more than the cap, or a Capital Improvement Petition for a pass-through. A hearing officer decides each one.

For HUD rents by size in the same market, see the [Orange County fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/orange-county-ca-fair-market-rent-2027).

## Must Santa Ana owners register units?

Yes. Santa Ana owners must register each rental unit in the online Rental Registry every year and pay the Rental Registry Fee. For the 2026-2027 program year, the deadline was 2026-07-01. A registration on 2026-07-02 or later is late.

| Fee item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Registry fee, paid 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31 | $100 per unit |
| Registry fee, paid 2026-08-01 to 2027-06-30 | $104 per unit |
| Late fee, October 2026 | 8.2% of the fee |
| Late fee, November 2026 | 18.2% of the fee |
| Late fee, December 2026 to June 2027 | 28.2% of the fee |
| Pass-through to the tenant | Up to $50 a year, in 12 equal monthly parts |

The pass-through is per unit, not per tenant. It is not part of the rent, so you cannot add it before you calculate a rent increase. Show it on the rent statement as a separate fee. If you charge more than $50, you must refund the extra.

Registration checklist:

- [ ] Claim each property in the Rental Registry. A manager can get access with the management agreement.
- [ ] Submit the registration form every year, even if nothing changed.
- [ ] File exemption claims with papers by July 1, or the unit is treated as covered.
- [ ] Report a sale within 30 days of the close of escrow, through the Registry.
- [ ] Pay the fee and keep the receipt. Tenants can ask for it.

What is at stake: the city says no landlord may advertise, demand or accept rent, or evict, for a unit whose registration is not complete and accurate (SAMC 8-3160(k)).

## What are the just cause and relocation rules?

Santa Ana's just cause rules start after a tenant has lived in the home for 30 days. After that, you need a just cause in writing to end the tenancy. Before an at-fault notice, you must first give written notice of the violation and a time to fix it.

The 4 no-fault causes:

1. **Owner or family move-in.** The owner, spouse, domestic partner, child, grandchild, parent or grandparent.
2. **Withdrawal from the rental market** for at least 24 months, with a written affidavit to the city.
3. **A government or court order.**
4. **Demolition or substantial remodel.**

For any no-fault termination, you must pay 3 months of relocation assistance or waive rent for the final 3 months. At $3,057 a month, that is $9,171.

## Do other Orange County cities have rent control?

Outside Santa Ana, Orange County owners fall under state law unless their own city adopts a stricter rule. California Civil Code 1947.12 caps increases on covered units at 5% plus the Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim CPI change, at most 10% in 12 months, until 2030-01-01. Civil Code 1946.2 adds just cause after 12 months and 1 month of no-fault relocation pay.

| Topic | Santa Ana | State law (Civil Code 1946.2, 1947.12) |
|---|---|---|
| Rent cap | Lower of 3% or 80% of CPI (2.87% now) | 5% + CPI, max 10% |
| Just cause starts | After 30 days | After 12 months |
| No-fault relocation | 3 months | 1 month |
| Registry fee | $100 to $104 per unit | None |

A city can pass its own rules at any time, so check your city's website before you send a notice. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

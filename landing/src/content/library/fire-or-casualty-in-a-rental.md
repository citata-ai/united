## Does the lease end after a fire?

A fire can create a right to end the lease, but the legal test varies. First obtain the official access instructions and assess the damage. Do not treat a smoke smell, a repair estimate and a condemned building as the same condition.

Florida Statutes section 83.63 permits tenant termination when qualifying casualty damage substantially impairs use and did not result from tenant wrongdoing or negligence. It also addresses partial loss of use. [Read Florida's casualty rule](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/83.63).

Virginia Code section 55.1-1240 permits termination under its substantial impairment or necessary vacancy test. As of this guide's date, the tenant notice period after leaving is 14 days. The landlord's qualifying termination also uses 14 days' notice. The official page shows a different version effective January 1, 2027. Use the version in force when the event occurs. [Read Virginia's statute and effective dates](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title55.1/chapter12/section55.1-1240/).

Have counsel review the notice and its effect on possession. A planned repair does not by itself settle whether the tenant can stay. Keep the inspection finding with the termination decision.

## Is rent still owed after casualty damage?

Rent depends on the applicable casualty rule and the actual loss of use. Record the incident date, access limits and any formal termination date before changing the ledger.

Florida section 83.63 allows a reduction by the fair rental value of the unusable portion in the circumstances it covers. Virginia section 55.1-1240 addresses proration as of the casualty date. Neither example supplies a blanket national formula.

Separate the rent question from the insurance claim. An insurer's payment schedule does not itself decide the tenant's rent duty. Likewise, an unpaid claim does not prove full rent remains due.

Give the tenant a clear written account. Show the period affected, the adjustment and any balance under review. Stop automated messages that conflict with the decision you have made.

If the parties disagree about the unusable area, preserve photos and professional findings. Ask counsel how local law measures the reduction. Avoid inventing a daily discount simply to close the task.

## Who pays for temporary tenant housing?

Temporary housing depends on the coverage and legal duties that apply to the loss. Ask the tenant to contact their renters insurer, and contact your own insurer promptly.

Do not promise reimbursement before you confirm authority and coverage. Ask your adjuster which costs need advance approval. Request written instructions for emergency lodging, security, drying and temporary repairs.

Keep different kinds of loss apart:

| Loss or cost | File to review | Useful supporting record |
|---|---|---|
| Building damage | Owner policy and claim | Inspection and repair estimate |
| Tenant belongings | Tenant policy and access plan | Tenant inventory and photos |
| Lost rental income | Owner policy and rent record | Lease and adjusted ledger |
| Temporary lodging | Policy, lease and local duty | Receipts and written approval |
| Emergency work | Claim and mitigation log | Vendor scope and invoice |

This table is a claim checklist, not a statement of coverage. A policy exclusion or deductible can change the result. Let the policy and insurer's written response supply that answer.

Avoid assigning fault in an early email. A fire report, contractor opinion and insurer decision may address different questions. Share confirmed facts and mark the cause as under review when appropriate.

## What should I file with insurance?

Send a dated loss record, the policy details and the evidence your insurer requests. Create a single claim folder before repair records scatter across staff messages.

Photograph damage only after safe entry is allowed. Keep wide views and close views. Record who took the pictures and which room they show. Preserve damaged items when the adjuster asks and safe storage permits.

Save the lease, rent ledger, inspection history and work orders. Add the official incident report when available. Include estimates, temporary repair invoices and the adjuster's instructions.

Put safe entry before cleanup in your response plan. Obtain access clearance from the responsible officials before staff enter. Do not send staff into a damaged building merely to meet an internal photo deadline. Record who authorized access and what limits apply.

Track expenses separately from claim payments. Mark estimates as estimates and paid invoices as paid. Keep a list of unresolved coverage questions so the next conversation has a clear purpose.

## How do I manage access, repairs and tenant contact?

Use a written response plan with named contacts and current facts. The plan should tell the tenant who can answer access, repair and account questions.

Start with the recommended checklist:

- **Confirm safety.** Follow fire, building and utility access instructions.
- **Contact insurers.** Record claim contacts and emergency work approval.
- **Preserve evidence.** Save reports, photos and damaged-item instructions.
- **Review the lease.** Check casualty terms against the current local law.
- **Update rent.** Explain any suspension, reduction or termination accounting.
- **Plan belongings access.** Arrange a safe supervised visit when allowed.
- **Track repairs.** Record inspections and the basis for any return date.

Florida section 83.63 specifically requires a safe belongings opportunity or notice of when collection can occur within a reasonable time. Keep that access plan distinct from a demand to surrender keys.

Do not give a firm return date based only on a contractor's first estimate. Identify which inspection or repair controls the next decision. Send an update when that fact changes.

For routine management roles, see [property management support](/property-management). Before the next emergency, collect insurer and vendor contacts in the same response file.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

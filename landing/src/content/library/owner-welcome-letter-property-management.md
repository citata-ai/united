## Which contacts and timelines go in the owner welcome letter?

The welcome letter gives the owner one name for each type of question and a dated plan for the first 30 days. New owners call most in the first month. A clear contact list sends each call to the right person the first time.

Contacts to list:

| Question type | Contact | How fast you reply |
|---|---|---|
| Account, statements, payouts | Owner accounting | 1 business day |
| Repairs and approvals | Maintenance coordinator | Same day for urgent, 1 business day for routine |
| Leasing, rent, renewals | Leasing manager | 1 business day |
| Emergencies at the property | 24-hour line | Immediately |
| Anything else, or a complaint | Your property manager | 1 business day |

Put only reply times you can keep. A missed promise in week one costs more trust than a slow promise kept.

Then give the first-30-day plan with real dates:

| Date | What happens | What you need from the owner |
|---|---|---|
| [Day 1] | Welcome letter, portal invite | Send missing documents |
| [Day 7] | First inspection with photos | Approve or decline repairs |
| [Day 14] | Tenant handover or listing goes live | Confirm rent and terms |
| [Day 30] | First owner statement and payout | Read the statement, send questions |

Arizona's ARS 32-2173 says the management agreement must state the type and frequency of reports to the owner. So match this plan to what your agreement says.

## How do you explain when owners get paid?

Explain the payout in 3 parts: the day, the method and the math. Most owner calls in month one are about money that is smaller or later than expected.

**The day.** Name the day of the month you send payouts, for example "on the 12th, after rent clears and the books are reconciled". Buildium's guide notes that payout schedules follow the management agreement and can be monthly, quarterly or custom.

**The method.** Say how long each method takes. Buildium gives these times:

- ACH: 1 to 2 business days
- Check: 5 to 7 business days
- Wire: same or next day

**The math.** Show a short example. The numbers below are an example.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Rent collected | $2,100.00 |
| Minus repairs paid | − $180.00 |
| Minus management fee (8% × $2,100) | − $168.00 |
| Minus first reserve deposit | − $250.00 |
| **First payout** | **$1,502.00** |

Tell the owner that the reserve deposit happens once, and later payouts will be higher if no repairs come up. Buildium also mentions a minimum payout, such as $50 or $100. Smaller balances roll to the next month. If you use one, say so.

## What documents does the owner still need to send?

List each missing document with a due date and the reason you need it. Owners send documents faster when they know why.

- [ ] **Form W-9.** The IRS says Form W-9 gives your correct taxpayer ID to a person who must file an information return. You need it for the 1099-MISC you send by January 31.
- [ ] **Payout form.** Bank account for ACH, signed.
- [ ] **Insurance declarations page.** Landlord policy, with your company added if the agreement requires it.
- [ ] **Leases and ledgers.** For each tenant, from the owner or the old manager.
- [ ] **Security deposit list and funds.** Amount for each tenant.
- [ ] **Keys, codes and remotes.**
- [ ] **HOA contact and rules.**
- [ ] **Warranties and service contracts.**
- [ ] **Lead-based paint records (homes built before 1978).** The EPA says landlords must disclose known lead hazards and give tenants the EPA pamphlet. Property managers share this duty.

## Copy-ready owner welcome letter template

Copy the letter and fill in each bracket.

> [Date]
>
> Dear [Owner name],
>
> Welcome to [Company name]. Thank you for trusting us with [property address]. This letter tells you who to call, what happens in the next 30 days, and when you get paid.
>
> **Your contacts**
>
> - Your property manager: [name], [phone], [email]
> - Statements and payouts: [name or team], [email]
> - Repairs: [portal link] or [phone]
> - Emergencies, 24 hours: [phone]
>
> **Your first 30 days**
>
> - [date]: Portal access. Please check your bank and tax details.
> - [date]: We inspect the property and send photos and a repair list.
> - [date]: [Tenant handover / listing goes live].
> - [date]: First statement and payout.
>
> **When you get paid**
>
> We send payouts on the [day] of each month by [ACH / check]. Your payout is rent collected, minus repairs paid, our management fee of [rate] and the reserve of $[amount] we hold for repairs. In the first month, we also set up the reserve, so the first payout is smaller.
>
> **What we still need from you**
>
> Please send these by [date]:
>
> 1. [Document]
> 2. [Document]
> 3. [Document]
>
> **Your fees, as in our agreement**
>
> [Management fee], [leasing fee], [renewal fee], [other fees]. We will not charge any fee that is not in the agreement.
>
> If anything is unclear, reply to this letter or call me. I will answer within [1 business day].
>
> Sincerely,
> [Name, title]
> [Company name]

This is a generic template, not a legal form. Check that every line matches your management agreement before you send it.

## How do you follow up after the letter?

Call the owner 2 to 3 days after the letter. Ask one question: "What is still unclear?" Then fix the letter for the next owner.

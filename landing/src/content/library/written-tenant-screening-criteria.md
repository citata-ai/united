## Why should every landlord have written screening criteria?

Written screening criteria make you apply the same rules to every applicant. That consistency is your best protection under fair housing law. It also saves time: an applicant who reads the rules first can decide not to apply.

Some states go further and require them. Texas Property Code 92.3515 says that when you give an applicant a rental application, you must make printed notice of your tenant selection criteria available. The notice must list the grounds for denial, such as criminal history, rental history, current income, credit history, or false or incomplete information. The applicant signs an acknowledgment. If the acknowledgment is not signed, the law presumes you did not give the notice.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Which criteria are common: income, credit, rental history?

Screening criteria usually cover five areas. The table shows what each rule says and what to watch for.

| Area | Example rule (you set the number) | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Income | Gross monthly household income of at least 3 times the rent | Count voucher and benefit income where source-of-income laws apply |
| Credit | No open rent-related collections; a minimum score you choose | Voucher holders in California may offer other proof of ability to pay |
| Rental history | No eviction judgment in a set number of years; positive landlord reference | Some states restrict use of past landlord-tenant cases |
| Criminal history | Only what local law allows, after an individual review | Many cities and states limit this area |
| Application | Complete, true and verifiable information; ID for each adult | Accept more than one ID type |

The numbers in the example column are your choice, not a legal standard. Pick each number, write it down, and use it for everyone.

Two state rules show why you must check local law. In California, Government Code 12955(o) says that when a renter has a government rent subsidy, you may not use an income standard that ignores the subsidy. You also may not use credit history unless you let the applicant offer other proof of ability to pay. In New York, the Attorney General says a landlord cannot deny an applicant because of a past legal conflict with a landlord, such as a suit for repairs.

## How do I set a minimum credit score?

Set the credit score rule from the screening product you use, and say which score it is. A FICO score and a tenant score from a screening company are not the same thing.

For example, TransUnion SmartMove gives a ResidentScore from 350 to 850. RentRedi's help page lists its bands: 350–523 is "Decline", 524–537 "Conditional", 538–559 "Low Accept" and 560–850 "Accept". If you use those bands, write them in your criteria. Also say what happens with "no score", for example a renter with a thin credit file.

A good credit rule has three parts:

1. **Name the score.** "TransUnion ResidentScore" or "FICO 8", not just "credit".
2. **State the line.** One number, or a band that leads to a conditional approval.
3. **State the option.** For example, a co-signer or a larger deposit where your state allows it.

A conditional approval is still an adverse action under the FCRA if a consumer report caused it. The FTC says that requiring a co-signer or a larger deposit counts. You must then send an adverse action notice.

## Should the criteria be in the listing?

Yes, put a short version or a link in the listing, and give the full page with the application. Applicants then know the rules before they pay a fee.

Fees have their own limits in some states:

| State | Fee rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| California | Screening fee of no more than actual cost, capped at $30 per applicant, adjusted each year for inflation; give the applicant a copy of the credit report within 7 days | Civil Code 1950.6 |
| New York | Credit and background check fee of no more than $20; give the applicant a copy and the invoice; accept a check done in the past 30 days | NY Attorney General guide |
| Texas | Printed criteria with the application; if you deny an applicant without that notice, you must return the application fee and any application deposit | Property Code 92.3515 |

## How does HUD view criminal history criteria?

HUD's 2016 guidance on criminal records under the Fair Housing Act is no longer in force. A Federal Register notice of 2026-07-17 lists the April 4, 2016 guidance among documents withdrawn, effective September 25, 2025.

The Fair Housing Act did not change. HUD's discriminatory effect rule, 24 CFR 100.500, is still in the eCFR. It says a practice can be unlawful if it has a disparate impact on a protected group, even without intent. HUD proposed to remove that rule in January 2026 and published a supplemental proposal on 2026-08-10. As of this writing, we found no final rule.

State and local law also applies, and it is often stricter:

- **California:** The Civil Rights Department lists "refusing to rent to anyone with a criminal history" as a form of housing discrimination.
- **New Jersey:** Under the Fair Chance in Housing Act, you may look at criminal history only after a conditional offer. Look-back periods are set by offense degree.
- **Cook County, Illinois:** The Just Housing Amendment bars use of arrests without conviction. It requires an individualized assessment before a denial based on a conviction in the past 3 years.

So do not write a blanket ban. Write which records you consider, the look-back period, and that you review each case.

## How often should I update the criteria?

Review your criteria at least once a year, and when a law changes. HUD withdrew its criminal records guidance in 2025, and state laws change each session.

Use this checklist when you review:

- [ ] Version date on the page.
- [ ] Income rule counts all lawful income, including vouchers where required.
- [ ] Credit rule names the score and the "no score" path.
- [ ] Rental history rule fits state limits on landlord-tenant records.
- [ ] Criminal history rule fits city, county and state law.
- [ ] Fee matches the state cap.
- [ ] Adverse action notice template is ready.
- [ ] Reasonable accommodation contact is on the page.
- [ ] Old versions are saved with the files they applied to.

If you work with a manager, ask to see their written criteria before you sign. Our guide to [property management](/property-management) explains what to check.

## Which first clients come from my own network?

The first 10 doors almost always come from people who already know you. A new company has no reviews and no case studies, so trust must come from the person, not the brand.

Make a list of 100 names. Then sort it.

| Group | Why they say yes | First ask |
|---|---|---|
| Friends and family who own a rental | They know you will pick up the phone | "Can I manage it for 90 days and show you the reports?" |
| Past real estate clients | You sold them the home | "Do you still rent the old place?" |
| Coworkers from your last job | They saw your work | "Who do you know with a rental?" |
| Owners who self-manage and complain | They are tired of 2 am calls | "What would you hand off first?" |
| People who just moved away | Their home is now a rental | "Who is watching the house?" |

Send a short, personal note to each name. Do not send a mass email. Ask for one of two things: their own rental, or one name of someone with a rental.

Watch for: in many states you need a real estate broker license to manage rentals for others for pay. Check your state's license rules before you sign the first owner.

## Which partners refer new managers?

The best partners meet rental owners every week and do not manage property themselves. Real estate agents come first. Fourandhalf, a property management marketing agency, notes that agents care more about ongoing business than a one-time fee. So your best pitch is: "I manage. I do not sell. When your owner wants to sell, I send them back to you."

Other partners to meet in your first 90 days:

- **Mortgage lenders** who close loans for investors.
- **Real estate attorneys** who handle estates and divorces, where a home often becomes a rental.
- **Investor meetups** and local landlord groups.
- **Contractors and handymen** who work for self-managing owners.
- **Insurance agents** who write landlord policies.

Before you offer any of them a fee, read your state law. Florida statute 475.25(1)(h) lets the state discipline a licensee who pays "a person not properly licensed" for the referral of real estate business. Texas Occupations Code 1101.651 and California Business and Professions Code 10137 limit pay to licensed people too. Fourandhalf mentions "$200 or $300" as an example agent fee; if you pay an agent, pay through the agent's broker where your state requires it.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Can a new company rank on Google?

Yes, a new company can rank on Google, but Maps comes before organic search. Google says local results depend on 3 factors:

- **Relevance:** how well your profile matches the search.
- **Distance:** how far you are from the person who searches.
- **Prominence:** how well-known you are. Google says "more reviews and positive ratings can help".

A new company can match relevance and distance on day 1. Prominence grows with time. Google also says "there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking".

Google Search is also where owner leads come from. In the Goodjuju fall 2026 study of 35 companies, Google Search and the Map Pack brought 50.6% of owner leads, and direct visits and Maps brought 35.6% more. All 35 companies worked with an SEO agency, so the numbers may run high.

Your first-month Google checklist:

- [ ] Create a Google Business Profile with your real business name. Google says the name "should reflect your business's real-world name".
- [ ] Use a real office, or hide your address as a service-area business. Google says a virtual office "isn't eligible for a Business Profile".
- [ ] Pick "Property management company" as the main category.
- [ ] Add your service cities, hours, phone and website.
- [ ] Ask your first 5 owners and partners for an honest review.
- [ ] Build one owner page per real service city on your site.
- [ ] Put your full fee table on the site.

## What offer helps a company with no track record?

An offer helps when it moves risk from the owner to you. An owner who hires a new company fears 3 things: hidden fees, a long contract, and an empty home.

| Owner fear | Offer that answers it | Cost to you |
|---|---|---|
| Hidden fees | Publish every fee on one page | None |
| Long contract | Cancel with 30 days' notice, no cancel fee | Some churn risk |
| Empty home | No monthly fee until the home is rented | Cash flow in month 1 |
| "You are new" | A written 30-day plan and a weekly update | Your time |
| "Who are you?" | A founder who answers the phone | Your time |

Do not promise rent amounts, days on market or "best in town". You have no data yet. Promise what you control: response time, reports, and a clear fee table. Set fees you can live on with the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) before you discount anything. A low fee wins a door. It does not pay for the second hire.

## How do I plan doors 1 to 50?

Plan by stage, because each stage has a different source.

| Stage | Main source | Main task |
|---|---|---|
| Doors 1 to 10 | Your network | 100-name list, personal asks, first reviews |
| Doors 10 to 25 | Agent and lender partners | Meet 2 partners a week, send the one-page promise |
| Doors 25 to 50 | Google Business Profile and site | Reviews, city pages, fee table, fast follow-up |

Track each door's source from the first day. In 6 months you will know which source paid off, and you can drop the rest.

## How do AI answers fit in for a new company?

AI answers fit in after the basics, because AI assistants pull facts from your profile, your site and list sites. In the Goodjuju fall 2026 study, AI tools sent 3.4% of owner leads, and those visits converted at 3.9%, the highest rate of any channel. That is 43 leads, so the authors call it directional.

A new company with clear public facts (name, city, services, fees, reviews) gives AI tools something to cite. Read [how owners choose a property manager with AI](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai) to see what they look for.

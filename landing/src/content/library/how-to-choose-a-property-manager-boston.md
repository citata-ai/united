## Which license must a Boston property manager hold, and how do I look it up?

Massachusetts has no separate property manager license. But the state's real estate license law covers most of what a manager does when a unit turns over. MGL chapter 112, section 87PP defines a real estate broker. It is a person who, for another and for a fee, rents or leases real estate, or negotiates a rental. It also covers a person who holds out as in the business of renting or leasing.

So a Boston manager who finds tenants and signs leases for you, for pay, should work under a broker license. A salesperson must work under a broker.

How to check, step by step:

1. **Ask for the license type and number** of the person who will lease your unit, and the broker's name.
2. **Look up the license** with the state's online license check, linked from the mass.gov "Check a professional license" page.
3. **Match names.** The broker on the license should match the company on your agreement.
4. **Ask about other duties.** Rent collection and repairs alone are not leasing, so ask who does each task and under whose license.

## Which local rules must a Boston manager know?

A Boston manager must know two sets of rules well: the state deposit law and the city's rental registration. Mistakes in either cost money.

The deposit law first. MGL chapter 186, section 15B is strict.

| Rule | What section 15B requires |
|---|---|
| Move-in payments | Only first month's rent, last month's rent, a security deposit equal to first month's rent, and the cost to buy and install a key and lock |
| Agents | Since 2025-08-01, the limit applies to payments to the landlord or the landlord's agent |
| Statement of condition | Required for the security deposit |
| Where the deposit sits | A separate, interest-bearing account in a Massachusetts bank, out of reach of the landlord's creditors |
| Receipt | Within 30 days, with the bank's name and location, the amount and the account number. Without it, the tenant can get the deposit back at once |
| Last month's rent | The tenant is entitled to interest at 5% a year, or the lower rate the bank paid |
| Interest penalty | If the lessor fails to pay interest due within 30 days after the tenancy ends, damages of 3 times the interest, plus costs and attorney fees |

A worked example uses $3,008, the HUD FY2027 2-bedroom fair market rent for the Boston area. The most you can collect at move-in is $3,008 x 3 = $9,024 (first, last and deposit), plus the key and lock cost. See the [Boston fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/boston-fair-market-rent-2027) for other sizes.

Now the city rule. Boston's rental registration page says:

- **Every year:** register each rental property by July 1.
- **Fees:** $25 per unit for a first-time registration, $15 per unit for a renewal. The cap is $2,500 per building or $5,000 per complex.
- **Late:** a penalty of $300 each month until you register and pay. Owners who never registered owe fees back to 2013.
- **Still required:** vacant units, units under renovation, adult relatives not on the deed, and units with no rent.
- **Inspections:** at least once every five years after registration.

A worked example: a 3-unit building pays 3 x $15 = $45 a year on renewal. Missing the deadline by 4 months can cost 4 x $300 = $1,200.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What should a Boston management agreement say?

A Boston management agreement should name who does each compliance task, not only the fee. Use this checklist.

- [ ] **Fees:** monthly fee, leasing fee, renewal fee and maintenance markup, in dollars or percent.
- [ ] **Who pays the leasing broker.** Since August 2025, a tenant may not be required to pay your agent at move-in beyond the section 15B items. Expect the leasing cost to fall on you.
- [ ] **Deposit handling:** the bank, the account type, the 30-day receipt and the yearly interest.
- [ ] **Statement of condition:** who prepares it and when.
- [ ] **Rental registration:** who files by July 1 and pays the fee.
- [ ] **Inspections:** who meets the city inspector and fixes any violations.
- [ ] **Repair limit:** what the manager can spend without your approval.
- [ ] **Term and cancellation:** notice period and any fee to leave.
- [ ] **Records:** monthly statements and a year-end 1099.

## Which questions should I ask a Boston manager before I sign?

Ask questions that show whether the manager knows section 15B and the city rules. Good answers are exact.

1. **Who leases my unit, and under which broker license?**
2. **Which bank holds deposits, and is each one in a separate, interest-bearing account?**
3. **When does the tenant get the deposit receipt?** Expect "within 30 days, with bank and account number."
4. **How do you pay interest on deposits and last month's rent?**
5. **Who prepares the statement of condition?**
6. **What do you charge tenants at move-in?** Anything beyond first, last, deposit and lock is a red flag.
7. **Who files my Boston rental registration by July 1?**
8. **How do you prepare for the city's five-year inspection?**
9. **How do you screen tenants?** Expect one written standard for all applicants, in line with Fair Housing rules.
10. **Can I call 2 owners you manage for now?**

## Where do Boston owners find manager reviews?

Boston owners can check managers in three places. Each has a gap, so use all three.

| Source | What it tells you | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| State license check | Whether the broker or salesperson license is valid | Says nothing about service quality |
| Google Business Profile and Yelp | Owner and tenant reviews | Tenant complaints often dominate. Read how the manager replies. |
| Owner references | Results on homes like yours | The manager chooses them. Ask for owners of your building type. |

Read tenant reviews about deposits with care. Deposit disputes are where section 15B penalties start.

What to do: shortlist 3 managers. Check each license, ask the 10 questions, and compare the agreements line by line.

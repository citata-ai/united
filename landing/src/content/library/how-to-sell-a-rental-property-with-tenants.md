Selling a rented home has one extra person in the deal: the tenant. The tenant did not choose the sale, but the tenant controls access, condition and timing. A good plan treats the tenant as a partner, not an obstacle.

This guide covers what happens to the lease, how to schedule showings, when cash for keys helps, and which buyer fits.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Does the Lease Transfer to the Buyer?

In most sales, the buyer takes the home with the lease in place. The sale does not end the lease by itself. Read your lease for any clause about a sale, and check your state's law.

Lenders plan for this. The Fannie Mae Selling Guide (B3-3.1-08) lets a lender use a lease to qualify a buyer on a purchase "where there is an existing lease agreement(s) on the property that will be transferred to the borrower".

Deposits move with the lease in many states. Two examples:

| State | Rule on deposits at sale | Law |
|---|---|---|
| Florida | Deposits and advance rent transfer to the new owner, with interest and an accounting for each tenant. After the transfer and a written receipt, the seller is free of the duty to hold them. | Statutes 83.49(7) |
| Washington | Deposit funds transfer to an equivalent trust account of the new landlord. The new landlord must notify the tenant of the new depository. | RCW 59.18.270 |

Put the deposit transfer in the purchase contract and on the closing statement. Send the tenant a letter with the new owner's name, rent address and the deposit amount.

## What Notice Do Tenants Get for Showings?

State law sets the notice. Some states give a number of days. Others say "reasonable". Here are three examples:

| State | Notice for showing to buyers | Law |
|---|---|---|
| Arizona | At least 2 days' notice, entry at reasonable times, except emergency or where impractical | ARS 33-1343(D) |
| Washington | Tenant may not unreasonably refuse entry with at least 1 day's notice to show the unit to buyers; landlord may not show excessively | RCW 59.18.150(6) |
| Florida | Tenant may not unreasonably withhold consent to show the unit to buyers; the 24-hour notice rule in the statute is for repairs | Statutes 83.53(1)-(2) |

All three laws bar the landlord from using access to harass the tenant. Many leases also add notice rules. Follow the stricter of the lease and the law.

Make showings easier for both sides:

- **Bundle showings** into 2 or 3 set windows a week.
- **Give written notice** with the date and time range each time.
- **Ask the agent** to attend each showing.
- **Offer a rent credit** for a clean home during the listing. Put it in writing.
- **Use video and photos** to cut the number of in-person visits.

## Is Cash for Keys an Option?

Yes, if the tenant agrees. Cash for keys is a written deal: you pay the tenant to move out by a set date, with the home in a set condition. It is voluntary. It is not an eviction. Some cities may have their own rules on tenant buyouts, so check before you offer.

A good cash-for-keys agreement covers these points:

1. **Move-out date.** A clear date, with time to find a new home.
2. **Payment.** The amount, and when you pay it. Many owners pay part at signing and the rest when the keys come back.
3. **Condition.** Broom clean, all belongings removed, keys returned.
4. **Deposit.** How you will handle the deposit under your state's law.
5. **Release.** What each side gives up. An attorney should draft this part.

Here is a worked example with invented numbers. Selling empty might bring $20,000 more than selling with a tenant. Cash for keys costs $4,000. One month vacant costs $2,500 in carrying costs. The net gain is about $13,500. If the price gap is small, keeping the tenant may be the better deal.

## Should I Sell to an Investor or an Owner-Occupant?

Each buyer wants a different home. An investor wants the rent. An owner-occupant wants to move in.

| Factor | Investor buyer | Owner-occupant buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Tenant in place | Often a plus: rent from day one | Usually needs the home empty |
| Down payment on a conventional 1-unit loan | Up to 85% LTV, so about 15% down (Fannie Mae Eligibility Matrix) | Up to 97% LTV on a fixed-rate loan, with conditions |
| What the buyer studies | Lease, rent roll, deposits, expenses | Condition, layout, school and commute |
| Timing | Can close with the lease in place | Must wait for the tenant to move |

A low-down-payment buyer needs to live in the home. Fannie Mae defines a principal residence as a property the borrower occupies as a primary residence. So an owner-occupant buyer needs the tenant out by closing or soon after.

For an investor buyer, prepare a clean rental file: the lease, payment history, deposit records, a list of repairs, and 12 months of income and expenses.

## What Tax Steps Come With the Sale?

The sale ends your rental activity for that home. IRS Publication 925 says suspended passive losses are generally allowed in full in the year you dispose of your entire interest in a taxable sale to an unrelated person. The gain may include depreciation, taxed at up to 25%. Ask a tax professional before you list.

## What Is the Step-by-Step Plan?

1. **Read the lease.** Note the end date and any sale or showing clause.
2. **Talk to the tenant.** Explain the plan before the sign goes up.
3. **Choose the buyer type.** Investor with the lease, or owner-occupant after move-out.
4. **Set showing rules** that follow your state's notice law.
5. **Offer cash for keys** in writing, if you need the home empty.
6. **Transfer deposits** at closing under your state's law.
7. **Send the tenant a letter** naming the new owner.

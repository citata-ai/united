A service member gets PCS orders (a permanent change of station) to a base 2,000 miles away. The family owns a home near the current base. They have weeks, not months, to decide: sell or rent. Military OneSource says PCS orders "generally" last 2 to 4 years. Many families expect to come back, or to keep the home as an investment. They need a manager they can trust from far away.

## What do military owners need from a manager?

Military owners need a manager who runs the home fully from a distance and knows military rules. Their needs differ from a local owner's in five ways.

| Need | Why it matters | What to show |
|---|---|---|
| Remote everything | Owner may be in another state or overseas | Online owner portal, e-sign, digital statements |
| Time zones | A deployed owner may answer once a week | One named contact, written updates, set response times |
| Power of attorney | A spouse or agent may sign for the owner | A process to accept and verify a POA |
| SCRA know-how | Tenants near a base are often service members | SCRA-ready lease terms and a notice process |
| Return date | The owner may move back in after 2 to 4 years | Lease end dates planned around the owner's return |

Military tenants matter too. Under 50 U.S.C. 3955, a service member tenant may end a residential lease after PCS orders or a deployment of at least 90 days. For monthly rent, the end takes effect 30 days after the next rent due date after the tenant delivers notice. Build that into your pricing and vacancy plans. Owners near a base should hear it from you first.

## When in the PCS cycle should I reach them?

Reach military owners before and right after orders arrive. A Military OneSource moving guide notes that orders can be "received on short notice." That makes the decision window short. Once orders come, the family plans the move, the shipment and the housing at the new base all at once.

A simple timeline:

1. **Before orders:** the family reads about rent vs sell. Your rent-or-sell page and local rent data should be there.
2. **Orders arrive:** they ask friends, spouse groups and the internet for a manager. Your reviews and referrals count most.
3. **Weeks before the move:** you inspect, photograph, price and list. Fast, remote onboarding wins here.
4. **During the tour:** monthly statements, an annual inspection report and early notice of lease ends.
5. **Before return:** plan the last lease to end near the owner's return date.

### Worked example: rent vs sell for a 3-year tour

| Item | Example value |
|---|---|
| Monthly rent | $1,900 |
| Mortgage, taxes, insurance | $1,550 |
| Management at 8% | $152 |
| Average upkeep, $100/month | $100 |
| Monthly net | $98 |
| Net over 36 months, before vacancy | $3,528 |

These are example numbers. Add vacancy, tenant turnover and repairs for the real case. The tax side matters too. Military OneSource says the home sale exclusion needs 24 of the last 60 months as a main home, and active duty owners with PCS orders can pause that 60-month clock for up to 10 years. This is general information, not tax advice. Owners should ask a tax professional. Our [fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) shows the management cost for any rent.

## Which local groups and offices help?

Military families get help from official programs, and each has its own rules for outside businesses. Military OneSource lists these supports:

- **PCS planning help:** moving consultants and online tools, available 24/7.
- **Relocation Assistance Program:** local support at installations.
- **Personal Financial Management services:** money help at each installation.
- **MilitaryINSTALLATIONS:** base overviews, housing contacts and programs.

Do not assume you can market on base. Call each office and ask what it allows: a resource table, a class, a listing or nothing. Some offices may list local services as information only.

Other channels that reach military owners:

- [ ] Military spouse groups on Facebook, where rules allow business posts
- [ ] Local agents who work with military buyers and sellers
- [ ] Veteran-owned business networks
- [ ] Your current military owners, asked for referrals in the same way as all clients
- [ ] A clear "PCS owners" page on your site

Fair Housing applies to all of this. Market your services to owners. Never advertise rentals as "military only" or steer tenants by any protected class. The federal Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C. 3604) lists familial status among the protected classes.

## What proof builds trust with military families?

Proof that you handle distance builds trust. Military owners rely on referrals from other military families, so each one you serve well is your best ad.

Proof checklist for your site and your pitch:

- [ ] Number of military owners you manage now (only if true and countable)
- [ ] A sample monthly statement and inspection report
- [ ] Your response time in writing: for example, owner emails within 1 business day
- [ ] Your SCRA notice process, in plain steps
- [ ] Reviews from military owners, with their consent
- [ ] Full fee table, including fees when a tenant leaves early under the SCRA
- [ ] A named contact and a backup

Never claim "military-friendly" or veteran status unless it is true. Make the page specific: name the base, the commute zones and the property types you manage. Then link it to your [property management overview](/property-management) and your rent analysis offer.

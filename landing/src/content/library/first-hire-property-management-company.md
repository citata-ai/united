## At what door count should I hire?

You should hire when your door profit can pay the salary and your own week is full. A door count alone does not tell you. The two tests together do.

The money test is one division:

`Doors to pay for the hire = monthly cost of the hire ÷ monthly profit per door`

Monthly profit per door is your fee revenue per door minus the costs each door adds. Here is the math for a full-time admin at the BLS median wage of $48,310 a year (May 2025). I add an assumed 20% for payroll tax and benefits; your real load depends on your state and plan.

| Profit per door, monthly | Hire cost, monthly | Doors to pay for the hire |
|---|---|---|
| $100 | $4,831 | 49 |
| $130 | $4,831 | 38 |
| $160 | $4,831 | 31 |

Those doors are on top of the doors that already pay your fixed costs and your own salary. If 60 doors pay you today and profit per door is $130, the hire is safe at about 98 doors. You can hire earlier if you accept a lower salary for a few months, or if the hire frees you to win doors faster.

The time test is a calendar check. You pass it when you work more than you can keep up for two months in a row, and calls or owner requests start to wait.

## Admin, leasing or maintenance coordinator first?

Hire first for the largest block of repeat work in your week. To find it, log your time for 5 working days in 30-minute blocks. Then sort the hours into these groups:

| Work group | Typical tasks | Who can take it |
|---|---|---|
| Admin | Phone, email, data entry, owner statements, filing | Admin assistant |
| Leasing | Inquiry replies, showings, applications, lease signing | Leasing agent |
| Maintenance | Work order intake, vendor calls, follow-up, invoices | Maintenance coordinator |
| Accounting | Bank reconciliation, owner draws, 1099s | Bookkeeper (often part time) |
| Owner sales | Owner calls, proposals, onboarding | Keep this yourself at first |

Buildium's first-100-doors guide lists the same core roles: property manager, leasing agent, maintenance coordinator, bookkeeper and business development. It suggests you wear most of these hats yourself at first, then hand them off.

Keep owner sales. You know the business best, and in the early years each new owner often comes from your own network. Hand off the work that follows a fixed process.

## Does the first hire need property management experience?

No. Experience helps less than most owners expect. In DoorGrow's podcast episode DGS 260, Sarah Hull says training takes one to three months with or without experience. A hire from another company knows how that company works, not how yours works.

What to hire for instead:

- **Values fit.** Write down 3 to 5 values for your company before you post the job. Hull says she cannot help an owner hire well without them.
- **Reliable follow-up.** Ask for a story about a task that fell through and what the person did next.
- **Clear writing.** Give a short written test: reply to an upset owner email.

Then write down each process as a checklist before the start date. A new hire with a checklist learns faster than an expert without one.

## Should I hire locally or a virtual assistant?

Hire local for work that needs a person at the property. Hire remote for work done by phone, email and software.

| Factor | Local hire | Virtual assistant (remote) |
|---|---|---|
| Showings and inspections | Yes | No |
| Phone, email, data entry | Yes | Yes |
| Time zone | Same as you | Check overlap with your office hours |
| Training | In person | Screen share and recorded videos |
| Labor law, payroll | Your state rules | Depends on contract and country |

There is one data point on remote staff. LetHub reports that in the 2022 NARPM Financial Performance Guide, firms that used 6 or more non-US team members averaged 16% profitability, against 9% for firms with none. That is a link between two numbers, not proof that remote staff cause profit. I could not open the NARPM guide itself, so treat the figure as reported.

If you hire remote, keep tenant data safe. Give each person their own software login, never a shared password.

## How do I pay for the first hire?

You pay for the first hire with door profit, a lower owner salary for a short time, or new revenue the hire makes possible. Plan which one before you sign the offer.

Checklist before you hire:

- [ ] Monthly profit per door is calculated from the last 12 months.
- [ ] Doors needed to pay for the hire is calculated with the formula above.
- [ ] You have cash for 3 months of salary, in case growth is slow.
- [ ] The tasks to hand off are written as checklists.
- [ ] You know which of your freed hours go to winning new doors.

The last point matters most. A hire that frees 15 hours a week only pays off if some of those hours win new owners. Our guide on [how owners choose a property manager with AI](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai) shows where many owners now start that search.

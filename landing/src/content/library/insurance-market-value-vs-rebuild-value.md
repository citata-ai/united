## What does replacement cost mean?

Replacement cost concerns the cost to replace damaged property under the policy's terms. It is not an estimate of what a buyer would pay for your rental. Ask your insurance agent which settlement basis applies to the building and its different parts.

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners, or NAIC, says dwelling coverage should be enough to rebuild the insured home. Its glossary generally describes actual cash value as replacement cost less depreciation. The contract's definitions and claim conditions control the actual result.

A landlord should identify the policy form before comparing limits. Tell the agent that tenants occupy the property. State whether the home is vacant, under construction or used for short stays. Request written confirmation that the quoted policy fits the actual use.

Separate these terms during your review:

| Term | Question it answers | Record to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Market value | What might the property sell for? | Local sale evidence |
| Rebuild estimate | What could replacement construction cost? | Insurer or contractor estimate |
| Dwelling limit | How much building coverage is stated? | Declarations page |
| Settlement basis | How does the policy value a covered loss? | Policy wording and endorsements |
| Deductible | What part do you bear under the contract? | Deductible schedule |

Do not rely on a coverage label alone. Ask whether roof surfaces, fixtures or other items have different settlement terms. Request the relevant policy wording with the quote.

## Why is rebuild cost different from market value?

Rebuild cost measures construction, while market value measures a property sale. California's Department of Insurance says dwelling coverage should reflect labor and materials needed to rebuild. Its guidance explains that land value generally is not part of dwelling coverage.

A low-priced purchase therefore does not prove that a low dwelling limit is sufficient. Likewise, a high land value does not prove the structure needs an equally high limit. Use an estimate for the insured structure itself.

Ask the agent to review the estimate's inputs. Confirm living area, construction type, roof, finishes and special features. Flag additions or conversions that an old record may omit. Keep a copy of the estimate and your corrections.

Do not simply multiply a local sale price by a guessed building share. That approach mixes different measures. Ask a qualified local contractor for rebuilding input when the insurer's estimate seems unclear. Tell the contractor you need replacement construction information, not a sales appraisal.

Ask whether the estimate and policy address demolition, debris removal and code-related work. Coverage can place those costs in different provisions or limits. Get the agent's explanation in writing before choosing the amount.

For a condominium, request the association's insurance documents as well as your own quote. California insurance guidance explains that association and unit-owner policies can cover different property. Identify the interior finishes and improvements that your policy must address.

## What is a coinsurance penalty?

A coinsurance clause can reduce a covered-loss payment when the owner carries too little insurance. The NAIC glossary describes this underinsurance mechanism. The required amount and calculation depend on the actual contract, so do not assume a universal threshold.

The NAIC notes that these clauses usually require at least 80% of the relevant value. That figure describes a common clause, not a recommended target for your coverage. Meeting a threshold may still leave a shortfall after a total loss.

Ask the agent to show a partial-loss calculation under your policy. Request the valuation basis, required insurance, amount carried and deductible. A verbal statement that you have replacement cost coverage does not answer how underinsurance affects payment.

Use this illustrative setup only as a discussion prompt. Assume a rebuild estimate of $400,000 and a policy condition requiring insurance equal to 80% of that value. The assumed required amount is $320,000. A $240,000 limit would fall below it.

Those amounts are hypothetical, not a quote or claim calculation. The example does not predict a payment. Ask your agent to apply the actual wording to a sample partial loss and a total loss. Save both explanations with the policy.

Also distinguish a deductible from underinsurance. A deductible is an agreed part of the loss you bear under the policy. A coinsurance adjustment arises from the contract's insurance-to-value condition. Ask whether both can affect the same claim.

## How do you check your coverage amount?

Review the rebuild estimate and policy together at purchase, renewal and after material property changes. California's Department of Insurance recommends checking dwelling limits and telling the agent about modifications in writing. Do not assume an automatic limit increase reflects your home's actual changes.

Ask the agent to answer a short checklist. Does the policy match the rental use? Does the rebuilding estimate use correct property details? Which parts use replacement cost and which use another basis? What settlement conditions require further action after a loss?

Then review the endorsements and exclusions. California guidance notes that some policies lack building code upgrade coverage. A large dwelling limit does not establish that excluded work is covered. Ask which protection is included and which requires an endorsement.

Review rental income coverage separately from building coverage. Ask what event triggers it, what documentation supports it and which limit applies. Do not assume a dwelling limit covers every month of lost rent.

Keep the declarations, full policy, endorsements and estimate in the same folder. Write down any question the quote does not answer. Get a written reply from the agent before you choose between proposals.

Add the confirmed premium and deductible exposure to your ownership budget. Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) for management costs alongside that budget. Then schedule a coverage review using the actual building details and policy terms.

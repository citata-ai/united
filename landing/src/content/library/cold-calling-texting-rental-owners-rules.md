## Can a property manager cold call rental owners?

A property manager may cold call rental owners, but three federal rules apply to each call. Two agencies write these rules. The FTC enforces the Telemarketing Sales Rule (TSR). The FCC enforces the Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA) through 47 CFR 64.1200.

Many self-managing landlords answer on a personal cell phone. So plan as if each owner is a consumer, not a business. That plan is safe, and it is simple to train.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Here is the short version, with the source for each rule.

| Rule | What you must do | Source |
|---|---|---|
| National Do Not Call Registry | Remove registered numbers from your list at least every 31 days | FTC, TSR guide |
| Registry access fee (FY 2026) | First 5 area codes free, then $85 per area code, max $23,425 | FTC, TSR guide |
| Calling hours | Call only between 8 a.m. and 9 p.m., owner's local time | 47 CFR 64.1200(c)(1) |
| Company do-not-call list | Honor a stop request within 10 business days, keep it 5 years | 47 CFR 64.1200(d) |
| Autodialed or prerecorded marketing call to a cell | Prior express written consent | 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(2) |
| Marketing text | Written consent | FCC consumer guide |
| Penalty | Up to $53,088 per violation | FTC, TSR guide |

## What does the Do Not Call registry mean for property managers?

The National Do Not Call Registry is a list of home and cell numbers that telemarketers must not call. The FTC says sellers and telemarketers must delete Registry numbers from their call lists at least every 31 days. You pay for the data by area code. For fiscal year 2026, the first 5 area codes are free. Each more area code costs $85, or $23,425 for all of them, whichever is less (FTC).

Two exceptions can apply to a property manager:

- **Business-to-business calls.** The FTC says the Registry rule "does not apply to business-to-business calls." A call to the office line of an LLC that owns 40 units can fit. A call to a landlord's personal cell usually does not.
- **Established business relationship.** The FTC says the relationship lasts 18 months after the last payment or transaction. After an inquiry or application, it lasts 3 months. So an owner who filled in your rental analysis form 6 weeks ago is fair to call back.

Also check your state. The FCC notes that many states have their own do-not-call lists. Scrub against your state list too, and read your state's telemarketing law.

## What does the TCPA say about texts?

The TCPA treats a marketing text like a marketing call, and texts need consent. The FCC says its rules ban autodialed texts to a mobile phone unless the owner gave consent first. It adds: "Commercial texts require written consent." The FCC also says these rules apply even if the number is not on the Registry.

Prior express written consent has a strict meaning in 47 CFR 64.1200(f). It is a signed agreement that names the phone number. It must say clearly that the person agrees to marketing calls or texts. It must also say that they do not have to agree in order to buy anything. An electronic signature counts if federal or state law accepts it. The FCC says a website form or a phone keypress can work.

What this means for your owner outreach:

1. **Call by hand.** A person who dials one number at a time is the low-risk path for cold calls.
2. **Do not cold text.** Send a text only after the owner opts in, for example on your rental analysis form.
3. **Do not use a prerecorded or AI voice.** The FCC says AI-generated voice calls are illegal unless the consumer agreed or the caller is exempt.
4. **Respect a stop at once.** The FCC says a person may opt out "at any time and in any reasonable manner."

## What is A2P 10DLC registration?

A2P 10DLC is the carrier standard for business texts sent from software over a normal 10-digit number. "A2P" means application-to-person. Twilio's documentation says anyone who sends texts from an application to US numbers over 10DLC must register. That includes small senders.

Registration has two parts (Twilio):

- **Brand.** The business that sends the texts.
- **Campaign.** What you send: the purpose, how people opt in, how they opt out and how they get help.

Twilio says unregistered senders pay extra carrier fees. Registered senders get less filtering and higher throughput. Your property management software or phone system may handle the registration for you. Ask your vendor which brand and campaign it registered in your name.

A2P 10DLC registration does not replace consent. Carriers check your opt-in flow. A campaign that says "we text owners who never opted in" will fail review, and it also breaks the FCC rule.

## How do I keep consent records?

Keep one record per phone number that shows when, where and how the owner said yes. If a complaint comes in, this record is your proof. Use this checklist.

- [ ] Date and time of consent, with the time zone.
- [ ] The exact form or page, with the URL and a saved copy of the wording.
- [ ] The consent text the owner saw, word for word.
- [ ] The phone number the owner gave.
- [ ] The owner's name and the source (form, call, signed agreement).
- [ ] Each opt-out, with date, channel and who processed it.
- [ ] Date of your last National Registry download, not older than 31 days.
- [ ] Your written do-not-call policy and staff training date.

The FTC lists a "safe harbor" for Registry mistakes. To use it, you need written procedures, trained staff, your own do-not-call list and Registry data not older than 31 days. The call must also be an error. So write the policy down before your team makes the first call.

Store records in your CRM, not in a staff member's phone. When an owner says "stop" on a call, add the number to your internal list that day. The 10-business-day limit is a maximum, not a target.

## Where do owner phone numbers come from, and which sources are risky?

The source of a number matters as much as the call. Here are common sources and the risk with each.

| Source | Risk |
|---|---|
| Owner filled in your form with a consent box | Low. You have consent for the purpose stated |
| Past client or inquiry in the last 3 or 18 months | Low to medium. Check the established relationship dates |
| Public records or a skip-trace list | Medium. Scrub against the Registry and state lists |
| Number taken from a craigslist rental ad | High. The craigslist terms of use set $500 in damages per unsolicited call or text to a user |

The craigslist terms ban use of its user data for unsolicited calls, texts or email. Rental listing sites have their own terms, so read them before you collect numbers.

## Should a property management website use live chat, a chatbot, or neither?

A property management website should use chat only when someone can answer owners fast. Live chat means a person types the replies. A chatbot means software replies, from rules or from an AI model. "Neither" means a short owner form and a phone number that a person picks up.

Here is how the 3 options compare for a company with 100 to 500 doors.

| Option | Who answers | Typical cost (2026-10-19) | Best for | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Live chat | Your staff | $19–$29 per seat a month | Offices with a person at the desk all day | Chats that wait 10 minutes for a reply |
| AI chatbot | Software | Base plan plus a fee per answer | After-hours owner questions | Wrong answers about fees or law |
| Bot + person | Bot first, then staff | Both costs | Most PM companies that add chat | Setup time for the hand-off rules |
| Neither | Form and phone | $0 extra | Small teams, low site traffic | Owners who want an answer now and do not call |

The "neither" row is a fair choice. A chat box that nobody answers is worse than no chat box. An owner who waits and gets no reply learns something about your service.

## Do chat tools raise owner lead volume?

Chat tools have no public proof of more owner leads for property managers. We looked for one and found none. The vendor pages promise more leads, but they show no PM data.

The best public PM lead data is the Goodjuju 2026 lead source study. In its fall update, 35 PM companies got 2,525 owner leads over two 90-day windows. Google Search and the Map Pack brought 50.6% of fall owner leads. AI tools such as ChatGPT brought 3.4%. Social media brought 0.8%. The study counted owner form leads only and did not test chat.

What the study does tell you: most owners arrive from Google with a clear goal. They want fees, service area and a call. Chat can help if it gives those 3 things faster than your page does. It cannot create owners who never visit.

Run your own test:

1. **Pick one page.** Put chat on your main owner services page only.
2. **Run 90 days.** Shorter tests are noise for a channel this small.
3. **Tag each lead.** Add a hidden field or CRM tag that says "chat".
4. **Count the end result.** Count signed management agreements, not chats or emails.
5. **Compare.** Put the 90 days next to the same 90 days of last year.

If chat brings 0 or 1 signed owner in 90 days, remove it. That is a valid result.

Tidio also lists a free plan. Intercom offers a 14-day trial.

A worked example: 2 staff on LiveChat Essential cost $38 a month billed yearly, or $456 a year. If that chat signs one owner with 2 doors at $100 a month each, the fee income is $2,400 a year. Use your own fee and door count. Our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) helps with the fee side.

The bigger cost is staff time. Each chat seat needs a person who answers in under 1 minute during the hours chat shows "online".

## How do I keep tenants from flooding chat?

Keep tenants out of chat by limiting where it shows and by sorting visitors in the first message. Tenant traffic is heavy on PM sites. Goodjuju writes that about 80% of calls into one Philadelphia client's office came from people looking for an apartment.

Use this checklist:

- [ ] **Show chat on owner pages only.** Hide it on listings, the tenant portal link and the maintenance page.
- [ ] **Ask first.** The first message asks "Do you own a rental, or are you looking to rent?" with 2 buttons.
- [ ] **Route renters away.** Renter answers go to your listings page or showing tool, with a polite note.
- [ ] **Route tenants away.** Current tenants go to the portal for repairs and payments.
- [ ] **Owners go to a person.** Owner answers reach a staff member or book a call.
- [ ] **Count owners only.** Keep tenant chats out of your owner lead count, as the Goodjuju study did with tenant forms.

Treat renters with the same care in every reply. The Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. § 3604(c), bans rental notices or statements that show a preference based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status or national origin. That rule applies to a chatbot reply too. Give your bot one neutral script for every renter, and test it.

## Can an AI chatbot answer fee questions?

An AI chatbot can answer fee questions if you give it your exact fee table and strict limits. Owners ask about fees first, so a good answer here has value.

Set these rules before you go live:

1. **One source.** The bot reads only your published fee table and service area list.
2. **Quote, do not guess.** If the table has no answer, the bot says so and offers a call.
3. **Hand off.** Questions about contracts, eviction, law or one specific property go to a person.
4. **No promises.** The bot never promises a rent, a vacancy time or a result.
5. **Review weekly.** Read 20 transcripts each week for the first month. Fix wrong answers in the source text.

Goodjuju's AI agent page describes the same pattern for phone calls. The agent answers from rules you approve. It hands the call to a named person when a question falls outside them.

Remember that your company is responsible for what the bot says. A wrong fee in a chat is still your quote to that owner.

## Which option should you pick?

Pick by team size and hours. If a person sits at the desk all day, start with live chat on owner pages. If owners often contact you after hours, a bot that books calls fits better. If you have 1 or 2 staff, skip chat and make the owner form and phone fast.

Whatever you pick, measure it to the signed owner. For more on where owner leads come from now, read [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping).

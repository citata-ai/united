A family moves for a new job. Their old house sits on the market for 60 days. The offers are low. Someone says, "Why not rent it out?" Now they are landlords, with no lease template, no screening process and a home 500 miles away.

That family is an accidental landlord. They did not choose the business. They need a guide more than a sales pitch, and the manager who guides them first often gets the account.

## Who are accidental landlords?

Accidental landlords are owners who rent out a home they did not buy to rent. The common paths:

| Path | Trigger | Main worry |
|---|---|---|
| Relocation | New job or military move | Managing from far away |
| Slow sale | Home did not sell at the price they need | Losing money either way |
| Upsize or downsize | Bought a new home before selling the old one | Two mortgages |
| Inheritance | A parent's home passed to them | Family, repairs, taxes |
| Low mortgage rate | Want to keep a cheap loan | Is the rent enough? |
| Life change | Divorce, care for a parent, a long work posting | Time and stress |

They are a large group. In the 2021 Rental Housing Finance Survey, about 86% of US rental properties held one rental unit. Individual investors owned about 70% of rental properties. Only about 22% of small properties with 1 to 4 units had professional management (HUD and Census, 2022). No survey we found counts how many of them started as accidental landlords.

## Where do accidental landlords search for help?

Accidental landlords search for the decision, not for a manager. Their first questions look like these:

- "Should I rent or sell my house?"
- "How much can I rent my house for?"
- "How to rent out my house for the first time"
- "Taxes on renting out my old home"
- "Property management cost [city]"

They also ask people: their listing agent, their lender, a friend who rents a home out. More and more, they ask AI tools. In Goodjuju's fall 2026 study of 35 PM companies, AI tools brought 3.4% of owner form leads, up from 2.1% in spring. AI visitors converted at 3.9% (Goodjuju, 2026). An owner who asks ChatGPT "rent or sell my house in [city]" may get a list of managers.

Channels that reach them:

1. **Answer pages on your site** for each question above, with local numbers.
2. **Real estate agent partners.** Agents meet these owners when a sale stalls. Offer a referral fee where your state allows it, and a clear hand-back if the owner sells later.
3. **Relocation and HR contacts** at large local employers and bases.
4. **Probate and estate attorneys** for inherited homes.
5. **Expired and withdrawn sale listings,** by mail, where the data source allows that use.

## Which messages and offers work for them?

Messages that work for accidental landlords lower stress and give a clear next step. These owners fear a bad tenant, a big repair and a tax mistake. They do not want a pitch about "maximizing ROI".

| Message | Instead of |
|---|---|
| "Not sure if you should rent or sell? Get both numbers for your address." | "Maximize your investment returns" |
| "We handle tenants, repairs and rent while you live elsewhere." | "Full-service portfolio management" |
| "Your first-time landlord checklist for [state]" | "Download our brochure" |
| "If you decide to sell later, we hand the home back clean." | Long contracts with exit fees |

Offers that fit:

- A free rent-vs-sell analysis.
- A first-time landlord checklist: insurance change, lease, deposit rules, local rental license.
- A short call with no contract: "Tell us your plan, we tell you what it costs."

## How does a rent-vs-sell analysis help?

A rent-vs-sell analysis helps because it answers the owner's real question with their own numbers. It also shows your skill before they pay you.

A simple layout, with example numbers for the math only:

| Line | Rent | Sell |
|---|---|---|
| Expected rent | $2,200/month | — |
| Mortgage, tax, insurance | −$1,500/month | — |
| Management fee (8%) | −$176/month | — |
| Repairs and vacancy reserve (10%) | −$220/month | — |
| Monthly cash flow | $304 | — |
| Sale price | — | $380,000 |
| Selling costs and loan payoff | — | −$300,000 |
| Cash from sale | — | $80,000 |

Get the management line from the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator). Add one row the owner must check with a tax professional: the tax effect of each path.

## Which tax rules should they know?

Two IRS rules often change the answer. Name them and send the owner to a tax professional. Do not give tax advice.

1. **Home sale exclusion.** IRS Publication 523 says you may exclude up to $250,000 of gain on the sale of your home, or $500,000 for a married couple filing jointly. To qualify, you generally must own the home and live in it as your main home for at least 24 months of the 5 years before the sale. The 24 months do not need to be in a row (IRS Pub 523). A long rental period can end the exclusion. Pub 523 also covers depreciation you may need to recapture.
2. **Depreciation basis.** IRS Publication 527 says that when you change a personal home to rental use, the basis for depreciation is the lesser of your adjusted basis or the fair market value on the date of the change. Residential rental property is depreciated over 27.5 years under the general system (IRS Pub 527).

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

The water heater fails on a holiday weekend. The tenant has no hot water. You pay for the unit, the install and the rush fee in one week. If the money came from a reserve, it is a bad day. If it came from your grocery budget, it is a bad month.

A reserve turns big, rare bills into small, steady ones. This guide shows how to size it from real life span data.

## What Is the Difference Between Repairs and Capital Expenses?

A repair keeps the home working. A capital expense replaces or improves a major part. The IRS draws the line for taxes, and it is a good line for budgets too.

IRS Publication 527 says you must capitalize any expense that improves the rental. An expense is an improvement if it does one of three things:

| IRS test (Publication 527) | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Betterment | Fixes a pre-existing defect, enlarges, or raises capacity or quality | Adding a bathroom |
| Restoration | Replaces a substantial structural part or rebuilds to like-new | Replacing the whole roof |
| Adaptation | Changes the property to a new or different use | Converting a garage into a unit |

Repairs that are not improvements are usually deductible in the year you pay them. Improvements are depreciated over time. Publication 527 also describes a de minimis safe harbor and a routine maintenance safe harbor that can let you deduct some costs. Ask a tax professional which apply to you.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

For your budget, use two buckets:

- **Repairs bucket.** Leaks, clogs, broken handles, service calls.
- **CapEx bucket.** Roof, HVAC, water heater, appliances, flooring, paint.

## How Long Do Roofs, HVAC and Water Heaters Last?

Life spans vary with climate, use and care. InterNACHI, a home inspector association, publishes estimates based on normal wear and regular maintenance. It says to use them as guidelines, not guarantees.

| Component | InterNACHI estimated life (years) |
|---|---|
| Asphalt shingles, 3-tab | 20 |
| Asphalt shingles, architectural | 30 |
| Furnace | 15 to 25 |
| Central air conditioner | 7 to 15 |
| Heat pump | 10 to 15 |
| Water heater, conventional | 6 to 12 |
| Refrigerator | 9 to 13 |
| Dishwasher | 9 |
| Clothes dryer | 13 |
| Washing machine | 5 to 15 |
| Carpet | 8 to 10 |
| Interior paint | 10 to 15 |
| Exterior paint | 7 to 10 |
| Asphalt driveway | 15 to 20 |

InterNACHI says hot climates sharply cut asphalt shingle life. Severe weather such as hail or hurricanes can also shorten roof life. It publishes a separate chart for Florida and coastal areas.

## What Is a Reserve Fund Per Door?

A reserve fund per door is the money you set aside each month for each rental unit. You build it from your own home's parts. Divide each item's replacement cost by its expected life in months. Then add the items.

Here is a worked example for one single-family rental. The replacement costs are invented for the example. Get local quotes for your own numbers.

| Item | Example cost | Life used (years) | Monthly reserve |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roof, 3-tab asphalt | $12,000 | 20 | $50 |
| Furnace | $5,000 | 20 | $21 |
| Central air | $6,000 | 11 | $45 |
| Water heater | $1,800 | 9 | $17 |
| Appliances (fridge, range, dishwasher, washer, dryer) | $5,000 | 11 | $38 |
| Carpet and flooring | $4,000 | 9 | $37 |
| Interior paint | $3,500 | 12 | $24 |
| Exterior paint | $5,000 | 8 | $52 |
| CapEx subtotal | | | $284 |
| Repairs bucket (example) | | | $100 |
| Total monthly reserve | | | $384 |

For "Life used" we took a value near the middle of each InterNACHI range. In this example, the owner sets aside about $284 a month for CapEx and $100 for repairs. On $2,200 of rent, that is about 17% of rent.

Keep the reserve in a separate account. Lenders also look at reserves. Fannie Mae requires 6 months of the mortgage payment in reserves for an investment property purchase. That reserve is for the loan, not for repairs, so keep a repair fund on top.

## How Does Home Age Change the Number?

Home age changes how soon each bill comes. The monthly math above assumes each item starts new. In an older home, many items are already near the end of their life.

Use a remaining life method for an older home:

1. **List each big item** and its install year. Use receipts, labels or an inspection.
2. **Find the remaining life.** Subtract its age from the InterNACHI life.
3. **Divide the cost by the remaining months.**

Here is the roof from the example with invented ages:

| Roof age | Remaining life | Monthly reserve for a $12,000 roof |
|---|---|---|
| New | 20 years | $50 |
| 10 years | 10 years | $100 |
| 16 years | 4 years | $250 |
| 20 years | 0 years | Replace now; $12,000 due |

A 16-year-old roof costs five times as much per month as a new one. The house is the same. The timing changed.

## How Do You Build the Reserve Without Draining Cash Flow?

Use these steps:

- **Start with an inspection** to learn the age and condition of each system.
- **Fund the worst item first.** Put the most urgent item at the top.
- **Set an automatic transfer** each month when rent arrives.
- **Keep a starting balance.** Aim to hold enough for one major item before you buy the next rental.
- **Review once a year.** Update costs, ages and the monthly number.
- **Track each bill** as a repair or an improvement for your taxes.

A reserve is not a tax deduction. You deduct or depreciate the cost when you pay it, under Publication 527.

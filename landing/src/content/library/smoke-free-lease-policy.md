## Can I ban smoking in a lease?

A smoking ban needs clear lease terms and a check of applicable state, local and program rules. Do not assume the same adoption process works for new and existing tenants. Review the current agreement before changing a permitted use.

California Civil Code 1947.5 allows residential landlords to prohibit smoking under its terms. It requires specified lease disclosures about prohibited areas and respects rules on changing existing terms. Washington RCW 59.18.140 addresses reasonable rules and how changes take effect. These examples require a local review rather than a national notice template.

HUD's rule in 24 CFR 965.653 requires covered public housing agencies to use smoke-free policies in specified areas. That is a public-housing rule. Do not describe it as a federal ban covering every private rental.

Write down exactly which spaces the policy covers. Include the unit, balconies, shared rooms and exterior areas as applicable. Use a site map where a boundary would be unclear. Give the tenant a contact for questions before signing.

Apply the policy to conduct at the property. Avoid personal labels about smokers. The goal is a clear use rule that staff can explain and enforce consistently.

## Does a ban cover vaping and cannabis?

A ban should name the conduct and products it covers rather than rely on assumptions. Have counsel define tobacco smoking, vaping and cannabis use where lawful. Check whether state or local definitions already control part of the wording.

Distinguish smoking from possession and other forms of use. A rule about smoke does not necessarily answer every question about cannabis. Do not write a broad claim that state cannabis law either cancels or automatically authorizes every lease restriction.

| Policy item | Question to settle |
|---|---|
| Products | Which smoke-producing or aerosol-producing activities are covered? |
| Places | Which indoor and outdoor areas are restricted? |
| Visitors | How does the rule apply to guests and vendors? |
| Current leases | What lawful process changes existing terms? |
| Complaints | Who receives reports and checks the facts? |
| Enforcement | Which lawful notices and remedies can follow? |

Keep staff scripts aligned with the signed policy. If the rule covers vaping, say that clearly at leasing. If an outdoor area is permitted, show where it is. Do not leave residents to infer a boundary from an ash container.

Route disability-related requests through the appropriate legal review. Staff should not make health judgments or request broad medical records on the spot. Keep that review separate from ordinary complaint handling.

## How do I prove smoking?

Prove the relevant conduct through documented observations and a fair investigation. A smell complaint is a starting point, not a complete finding. Record what the person observed and whether they know the source directly.

Ask for the location, time and pattern. Check whether smoke may come from another unit, an outdoor area or a building opening. Do not assume the nearest tenant caused the problem. Keep each report distinct so repeated forwarding does not look like independent evidence.

Arrange entry only through the lawful local process. Record observations in plain language. Identify visible residue or burns when present. Do not use intrusive monitoring or private-space cameras to gather evidence of smoking.

Compare the current condition with move-in records and prior maintenance notes. Ask the tenant for an explanation. A contractor's condition report may help define the needed work. It should describe observations and scope rather than make unsupported claims about who smoked.

Keep the enforcement question separate from the repair question. You may need to address smoke entering another home while responsibility remains under review. The CDC says smoke can move between apartments. Its guidance also explains that ventilation and air cleaning do not eliminate secondhand smoke exposure.

## What can I charge for smoke damage?

Charge only for supported losses that local law and the agreement allow. A lease breach does not automatically justify replacing every finish. Determine which work restores the actual damaged area and what evidence supports it.

California Courts explains that damage deductions exclude normal wear and tear. Washington RCW 59.18.280 requires supporting documentation for covered deposit deductions and limits charges under its terms. Check the rules where the rental sits before posting a damage balance.

Build a work scope before requesting quotes. Identify affected surfaces, materials and furnishings. Ask the contractor to separate cleaning, repair and replacement. Do not accept an unexplained smoke-removal package as the only support for a tenant charge.

Keep photographs, inspection notes, estimates and invoices together. Record prior condition and any relevant item history. Avoid charging for an unrelated upgrade that the owner chose during turnover. Ask counsel how to account for useful life or partial damage where required.

Provide the required itemized statement and delivery evidence. A disagreement about smoking does not extend the deposit deadline. If work is unfinished, ask counsel which estimate procedure is lawful in your state. Do not invent a flat penalty at move-out.

## How do I introduce and maintain the policy?

Introduce the policy through a reviewed adoption plan and consistent resident communication. Confirm the effective date for each tenancy. Keep the lease version and notice record together so staff know which terms apply.

Give residents a clear complaint channel. Explain what details help and how the manager will respond. Do not promise a smoke-free policy guarantees that smoke will never enter the building. Explain the process you can actually deliver.

Inspect shared areas through your normal lawful process. Check signs and remove conflicting instructions. Tell vendors the rules before work starts. An owner-approved contractor should not receive an informal exception that tenants cannot understand.

Review incidents for recurring building issues. If smoke travels through an opening, arrange a qualified review of the building condition. Do not make a tenant dispute the only record of a maintenance need. Track repair and enforcement actions separately.

## How Do You Calculate the Resident Renewal Rate?

Divide renewed leases by the defined group of expiring leases, then multiply by 100. Keep the group and measurement date consistent before comparing results.

Property Meld's retention guide calculates renewals from expiring leases and describes above 70% as favorable. The article also repeats older outside figures. We do not treat those older figures as a verified current national benchmark. Use the vendor target as context, then build a baseline from your records.

Define what renewed means in your report. A signed new fixed term, an accepted offer and continued month-to-month occupancy are different events. Choose a rule and show separate categories where needed. Do not count an unanswered offer as a renewal.

The example below uses invented figures to demonstrate arithmetic. It does not describe an observed portfolio or a suggested industry goal.

| Example group | Leases |
|---|---:|
| Expiring leases in the chosen period | 60 |
| Signed renewals from that group | 42 |
| Other outcomes or pending decisions | 18 |
| Renewal rate | 42 ÷ 60 × 100 = 70% |

Record the snapshot date and explain pending decisions. If you exclude properties sold or withdrawn, disclose that choice. Keep an unadjusted view too, so readers can understand the full group.

## What Does Resident Turnover Cost the Owner?

Estimate turnover from the property's actual vacancy, work and placement costs. A universal cost figure can hide large differences in condition and service scope.

Start with lost rent during the period the property cannot earn it. Add cleaning, owner-funded repairs, marketing, placement and approved administration costs. Separate normal wear, damage and planned upgrades in your records. Do not assume that every cost can lawfully be charged to the departing resident.

Avoid double counting work. If a placement fee includes advertising, do not add the same ad expense again. If the owner planned an upgrade regardless of the move-out, show that separately from avoidable turnover cost.

Use an estimate before deciding on an offer, then compare it with actual invoices later. The difference helps you improve the next estimate. A repair allowance based only on memory can make renewal look better or worse than it is.

Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to explore management and placement charges. Add property-specific work and vacancy assumptions yourself. Label every assumed input so the owner can change it. A cost model helps a decision but does not predict the next resident's behavior.

## How Does Resident Retention Affect Owner Retention?

Stable occupancy can support an owner's cash plan, but resident and owner retention are different measures. Do not assume that a renewal guarantees a satisfied owner.

Property Meld's retention guide connects resident satisfaction and continuity with owner confidence. That is a useful service argument, not proof that a particular renewal rate causes an owner to stay. Review owner feedback alongside the rental results.

Show owners the work behind renewal outcomes. Explain open maintenance, the offer process and known property needs. An owner may prefer a lawful nonrenewal or a major renovation in a particular case. A high renewal target should not override those facts or resident rights.

Track why residents leave with care. A move for personal reasons differs from an unresolved repair. Keep the resident's words separate from your analysis. Use unknown when no reliable reason is available rather than guessing.

Compare like properties and lease groups. A small portfolio can swing sharply when a few leases expire. Show the underlying counts so an owner sees the scale. Review trends over a suitable period rather than promoting a single strong month as a lasting result.

## Which Actions Can Improve Resident Renewals?

Improve the service and offer process before assuming that a concession will solve retention. Ask residents about specific unresolved issues and act on verified problems.

Check repair records for repeated faults, missed visits and poor updates. Confirm that completed work actually resolved the request. A renewal message can sound careless when an old repair remains open without a next step.

Make the renewal offer easy to understand. State the proposed term, price, response route and applicable dates clearly. Have local counsel review notice and renewal requirements before using a shared template. Different locations and housing programs can need different terms.

Review the property condition with the owner. Preventive work and planned replacements may reduce avoidable disruption. Do not promise an improvement before the owner approves it and the team can deliver it.

Ask departing residents what affected their decision without pressuring them to stay. Use consistent service practices and lawful criteria. Do not favor or exclude residents based on protected traits. Send accommodation, discrimination or legal questions to trained staff and qualified advisers.

## How Should You Set a Realistic Retention Target?

Set an internal target from checked history, property needs and service capacity. Keep it clearly separate from a vendor's general suggestion.

- Define the expiry group and what counts as a renewal.
- Check signed documents against software status.
- Show counts, pending cases and exclusions.
- Compare similar property groups and time periods.
- Review resident feedback and unresolved repairs.
- Agree actions with the people responsible for delivery.

Pair renewal rate with property condition, complaints and owner economics. A high rate achieved by delaying needed work is not sound service. A low rate needs explanation before you decide that staff performed poorly.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney. Use our [property management guides](/guides) for related service checks. Start with the next expiry group and confirm every outcome from the signed record before choosing a target.

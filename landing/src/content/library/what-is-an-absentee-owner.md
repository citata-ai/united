## What is an absentee owner?

An absentee owner is a person or company that owns a property but does not live in it or near it. The owner also does not run it day to day. In rentals, the term most often means an owner in another city or state. A landlord who lives 5 minutes away but never visits can also act like an absentee owner.

Many rental owners are small investors. The Congressional Research Service reports that individual investors owned 70.2% of US rental properties in 2020. That figure comes from the 2021 Rental Housing Finance Survey by HUD and the Census Bureau. The same report says 85.6% of the 19.3 million rental properties were single-unit properties. So a large part of US rental housing belongs to individuals with 1 or a few homes, and some of them live far away.

Absentee ownership is not a problem by itself. It only means the owner needs a system that works without being there.

## What risks does an absentee owner face?

An absentee owner faces the same risks as a local owner, but finds out later. Distance turns small problems into large ones.

| Risk | What happens | Early sign |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| Slow repairs | A small leak becomes mold or a failed inspection | Work orders stay open more than 7 days |
| Long vacancy | The unit sits empty while no one shows it | Few showings in the first 2 weeks |
| Unpaid rent | Late rent grows into months of arrears | Balance due on the rent roll each month |
| Listing scams | Someone posts a fake ad for your unit | Calls about a "listing" you did not post |
| Missed legal deadlines | Notices, permits or inspections lapse | Letters from the city that you did not see |
| Money errors | Charges or fees that no one checks | Statement totals that do not match the bank |

The scam risk is real for vacant units. The Federal Trade Commission warns that "Scammers copy the pictures, description, or virtual tour from a real online rental listing." The FTC also notes that a fake owner "will usually claim to be out of the country." A vacant unit with a far-away owner fits that story well. Search your address online during each vacancy.

## How do out-of-state owners manage rentals?

Out-of-state owners manage rentals in 1 of 3 ways. Each one trades money for control and time.

| Model | Who does what | Best for | Watch for |
|-------|--------------|----------|-----------|
| Full-service property manager | Manager leases, collects, repairs, reports | Owners who want little hands-on work | Fees and how well the manager reports |
| Hybrid | Local leasing agent and handyman; owner keeps the books and tenant contact | Owners with time and 1 to 3 units | Owner must answer fast, across time zones |
| Self-managed from far away | Owner does all of it with software and vendors | Owners with a trusted local person | No one to see the unit in person |

Steps to set up any model:

1. **Get local eyes.** A manager, an agent or a trusted person who can visit within 24 hours.
2. **Put rent online.** Online payments give a clear record and fast deposits.
3. **Set a repair limit.** For example, the manager may approve repairs under $300 without a call. Above that, the manager asks you.
4. **Set a reserve.** Keep money on hand for repairs, so a broken water heater does not wait for a wire.
5. **Write the local rules down.** Notice periods, inspection rules, rental licenses and tax dates for that city and state.
6. **Plan 2 site visits a year.** Even a full-service owner should see the unit, or get a video walk-through.

## What reports should an absentee owner get each month?

An absentee owner should get 5 reports each month. Together they show the money, the tenants and the condition of the unit.

Checklist:

- **Owner statement.** Rent collected, fees, repairs, and the owner draw for the month.
- **Rent roll.** Each unit, tenant, lease dates, rent and balance due.
- **Delinquency report.** Who owes rent, how much, and what step the manager took.
- **Work order log.** Each repair, its cost, its status and a photo of the finished work.
- **Trust account proof.** A statement that the trust account was reconciled for the month, with the date and who did it.

Add these each quarter or year:

- **Inspection report** with photos, at least once a year.
- **Lease renewal plan** 60 to 90 days before each lease ends.
- **Year-end package** for taxes: income and expense summary and 1099 forms.

How to read the owner statement fast. This example uses made-up numbers:

| Line | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Rent collected | $1,800 |
| Management fee (8%) | −$144 |
| Repair: kitchen faucet | −$185 |
| Reserve top-up | −$0 |
| Owner draw | $1,471 |

Check 3 things each month. Does rent collected match the rent roll? Does each repair have a work order? Does the draw reach your bank on the expected date?

## What an absentee owner needs from a property manager

An absentee owner needs a property manager who reports without being asked and who answers fast. Distance makes the manager the owner's eyes, so the quality of reports matters as much as the fee.

Questions to ask before you sign:

1. What is your repair approval limit, and can I change it?
2. How fast do you answer an owner email? A tenant emergency?
3. Do you send photos for each repair and each inspection?
4. Who reconciles the trust account, and can I see the proof?
5. How do you check for fake listings during a vacancy?

## Tax and legal notes for absentee owners

Absentee owners often owe duties in the state where the property is, not where they live. Examples are state income tax filings, local rental licenses and local notice rules. The rules differ by state and city. This is general information, not legal or tax advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney or tax professional.

## Rhode Island eviction rules at a glance

Rhode Island eviction law sits in Chapter 34-18, the Residential Landlord and Tenant Act. We read each section on 2026-10-19. The rilegislature.gov server did not answer that day, so we read Internet Archive copies of the official pages, dated 2025-05 to 2026-06.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Nonpayment: when to send notice | Rent 15 days in arrears | § 34-18-35(a) |
| Nonpayment: cure period | 5 days from mailing | § 34-18-35(a) |
| Nonpayment: earliest filing | 6th day after mailing | § 34-18-35(b) |
| Nonpayment: hearing date | 14 to 24 days after filing | § 34-18-10(a)(1) |
| Service before hearing | At least 5 days | § 34-18-10(a)(2) |
| Lease violation: cure period | 20 days from mailing | § 34-18-36(a) |
| Lease violation: termination date | At least 21 days after mailing | § 34-18-36(a) |
| Repeat violation within 6 months | 20 days' notice, no cure | § 34-18-36(e) |
| Month-to-month end | 30 days' written notice | § 34-18-37(b) |
| Answer time (non-rent cases) | 20 days from service | § 34-18-36(c), § 34-18-38(b) |
| Execution | Issues on 6th day after judgment if no appeal | § 34-18-48 |
| Self-help | Banned | § 34-18-44 |

## What notice must a Rhode Island landlord give before filing for unpaid rent?

A Rhode Island landlord must mail a written demand notice once any part of the rent is 15 days in arrears (§ 34-18-35(a)). The notice follows the form in § 34-18-56(a). It must state:

- The amount of rent that is 15 days in arrears.
- A demand for that rent.
- That the tenant must cure within 5 days of the mailing date, or the lease ends and the landlord files in court.

The landlord may file a "Complaint for Eviction for Nonpayment of Rent" no earlier than the 6th day after mailing (§ 34-18-35(b)).

Watch for: the tenant may cure by paying the full rent before the suit starts. If the tenant got no demand notice in the 6 months before filing, the tenant may also cure at the hearing. The tenant pays the full arrears plus court costs (§ 34-18-35(e)).

## How long does an eviction take in Rhode Island?

A fast, uncontested Rhode Island nonpayment eviction takes about 6 to 7 weeks from the rent due date. Here is the shortest path the statute allows, with rent due on the 1st:

| Step | Day (approx.) | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Rent due | Day 1 | Lease |
| Rent 15 days in arrears, demand mailed | Day 16 | § 34-18-35(a) |
| Cure period ends (5 days) | Day 21 | § 34-18-35(a) |
| Earliest filing (6th day after mailing) | Day 22 | § 34-18-35(b) |
| Hearing (14 to 24 days after filing) | Day 36 to 46 | § 34-18-10(a)(1) |
| Execution issues (6th day after judgment) | Day 42 to 52 | § 34-18-48 |

That gives about 42 to 52 days before the sheriff or constable can act. Real cases can take longer. A tenant who files an answer and asks for discovery can get a continuance (§ 34-18-35(d)). An appeal also delays the end. Section 34-18-48 issues execution on the 6th day only if no appeal is claimed.

Lease-violation and holdover cases are slower. The tenant has 20 days from service to answer, and the court then sets a hearing under its own rules (§ 34-18-36(c), § 34-18-38(b)).

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Rhode Island, and what does it cost?

A Rhode Island landlord files in the District Court or the appropriate housing court (§ 34-18-9). The clerk writes the hearing date on the summons when the complaint is filed and the fee is paid (§ 34-18-10(a)(1)).

For nonpayment cases, service has two parts. The landlord mails the summons, complaint and a blank answer form to the tenant by first-class mail on the filing day. A deputy sheriff or constable also serves the papers in hand, at the home, or by posting on the door (§ 34-18-10(a)(1)).

Fees from the Rhode Island Judiciary's District Court page, read 2026-10-19:

| Fee | Amount |
|---|---|
| Civil filing fee | $80.00 |
| Civil case processing fee (e-filed or counter-filed) | $17.50 |
| Technology surcharge | $3.25 |
| Civil execution fee | $20.00 |
| Credit card fee | 3.25% of the total |
| Court total, filing to execution, before service | $120.75, plus card fee if used |

Sheriff or constable service fees come on top. The Judiciary page does not list them.

## Can a Rhode Island landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes, for both, but each path has its own notice.

1. **Lease violation.** For a material breach, or a breach of § 34-18-24 that affects health and safety, mail a 20-day notice. The notice names the breach, the fix, and a termination date at least 21 days after mailing (§ 34-18-36(a)). If the tenant fixes the breach in time, the lease goes on.
2. **Repeat breach.** If the same breach comes back within 6 months, give at least 20 days' written notice. No cure period is needed (§ 34-18-36(e)).
3. **Serious conduct.** For breaches of § 34-18-24(8), (9) or (10), the landlord may file at once, with no notice of noncompliance (§ 34-18-36(f)).
4. **No cause, periodic tenancy.** End a month-to-month tenancy with 30 days' written notice, or a week-to-week one with 10 days (§ 34-18-37). If the tenant stays, file a holdover case the next day (§ 34-18-38(a)).

Watch for retaliation. Section 34-18-46 bars an eviction because the tenant complained to a code agency or joined a tenants' union. A complaint within 6 months before the landlord's act creates a presumption of retaliation.

Willful holdover has a cost for the tenant. The landlord may recover the greater of 3 months' rent or 3 times actual damages, plus attorney fees (§ 34-18-38(c)).

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Rhode Island?

A deputy sheriff from the division of sheriffs or a certified constable removes the tenant. If no appeal is claimed and the judgment is unpaid, execution issues on the 6th day after judgment (§ 34-18-48). The execution stays in force for 1 year. The clerk may add the officer's costs, including reasonable moving costs, with court approval.

## Is a self-help eviction legal in Rhode Island?

No. A Rhode Island landlord may not take back a unit by lockout (§ 34-18-44). The landlord also may not cut heat, water, hot water, electricity, gas or other essential service. The only exceptions are abandonment, surrender, or a process the chapter allows.

The penalty is high. Under § 34-18-34 the tenant may get back in or end the lease. The tenant also recovers the greater of 3 months' rent or 3 times actual damages, plus attorney fees.

- [ ] No utility shutoff, even for nonpayment.
- [ ] No removal of the tenant's property before execution.
- [ ] Only a sheriff or constable carries out the execution.

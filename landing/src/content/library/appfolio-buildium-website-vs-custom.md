## What do built-in PM software sites include?

Built-in PM software sites include a template, your listings, the rental application and portal links. AppFolio sells its site as an add-on called AppFolio Sites. Buildium includes a hosted website with its subscription.

Here is what each vendor page says (read 2026-10-19):

| Feature | AppFolio Sites | Buildium website |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $139 or $159/month, by page count | Included with the subscription (plans from $62/month) |
| Setup fee | $1,200 to $10,000+, one time | None shown |
| Design | Built for your brand; self-service editing of copy and images | Template; upload your logo |
| Listings | Integrated with AppFolio | Unit details, photos and price from Buildium |
| Application | Integrated | Hosts your custom rental application |
| Portals | Integrated | Add Resident Center and Owner Portal login links |
| Property sites | Separate site for each property | Not shown |
| SEO | "Basic" SEO based on industry terms and your location | Named as a benefit; no details |
| Analytics | Built-in dashboard | Not shown |
| Own domain | Not shown | Yes, per the page FAQ |
| Blog | Not shown | Not shown |

AppFolio's main pricing page (Core from $1.49 per unit a month, 50-unit minimum) does not mention a website. So budget AppFolio Sites as a separate line. For a full software price comparison, see our [AppFolio vs Buildium guide](/guides/appfolio-vs-buildium).

## What SEO limits do they have?

The main limit is content, not code. Both vendor pages describe a template for a leasing site. Neither page describes a blog, owner landing pages per city, or a fee page builder. AppFolio calls its SEO "basic," based on "popular industry terms and your geographic location."

That matters because owners search for different things than renters. An owner types "property management fees Naperville" or "who manages rentals near me." A renter types "3 bedroom house for rent." A listings site serves the renter. It rarely has a page that answers the owner.

Check your built-in site against this list:

- **Owner page per service city.** Can you add 5 or more owner pages, each with local facts?
- **Fee page.** Can you publish a full fee table, not "call for pricing"?
- **Blog or guides.** Can you post articles with your own headings?
- **Title and meta control.** Can you set the title tag and description per page?
- **Schema.** Can you add LocalBusiness or FAQ structured data?
- **Page limit.** AppFolio prices by page count. How many pages does your tier allow?
- **Export.** If you leave, can you take your pages and URLs with you?

If you answer "no" to three or more, the template will hold your listings but not your owner marketing.

## How do listings feed into a custom site?

A custom site can show the same listings, applications and portal links as the built-in site. You keep AppFolio or Buildium as the source of truth and put your own pages around it.

| Method | How it works | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Link out | "View rentals" button goes to the vendor listings page | Easiest; renters leave your domain |
| Embed (iframe) | Vendor listings page shown inside your page | Fast; styling is limited |
| Plugin or widget | Code pulls listing data into your own design | Matches your brand; depends on the plugin |
| Agency integration | A PM web agency connects listings, apply buttons and portals | Most polish; monthly cost |

For AppFolio on WordPress, the Listings for AppFolio plugin uses a shortcode. Its page says the iframe approach blocks styling changes, while the shortcode lets you restyle listings with CSS. The free version fetches 6 listings; the paid version shows all. Some 2025 reviews on the plugin page say the free version stopped working, so test before you rely on it.

For Buildium, Goodjuju (a PM marketing agency) lists four pieces that show on its client sites: rental listings, online applications, the resident portal login and the owner portal login. That is the same set the built-in site gives you.

## Does the website really bring owner leads?

Yes, for most companies the website is the main owner lead source. Goodjuju's fall 2026 report covers 35 companies and 2,525 leads tracked in GA4. Google Search and the Map Pack brought 50.6% of leads in fall, and direct and Maps traffic brought 35.6%. The average website conversion rate was 0.9%.

Those leads land on the pages Google ranks. If your site has only listings and a contact page, owner searches have little to land on. Our [AI search checklist for property management websites](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website) shows which owner pages AI answers also read.

## When should I move to my own site?

Move to your own site when owner growth is the goal and the template blocks the pages you need. Stay on the built-in site when you are small, leasing is the main job, and owners find you by referral.

Here is a worked cost comparison for 3 years. The custom site cost is an example, not a quote:

| Cost item | AppFolio Sites | Buildium site | Custom site (example) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup | $1,200 to $10,000+ | $0 | $5,000 (example) |
| Monthly | $139 to $159 | $0 extra | $150 hosting and care (example) |
| 3-year total | $6,204 to $15,724+ | $0 extra | $10,400 |

AppFolio math: $139 x 36 + $1,200 = $6,204; $159 x 36 + $10,000 = $15,724. Get real quotes from 2 or 3 agencies for the custom column.

Then compare with one signed door. If a door brings $1,728 a year (example: $1,800 rent x 8% x 12), 2 extra doors a year from owner pages cover the example custom site in about 2 years: $3,456 in year 1 plus $6,912 in year 2, if the doors stay. Use your own rent and fee.

Use this decision test:

1. You want owner leads from search in 2 or more cities.
2. You need a full fee page and a rental analysis form.
3. Your template cannot hold 10+ owner pages.
4. You want to own your pages and URLs if you change software.

Three or four "yes" answers: plan a custom site that keeps the software listings. One or two: improve the built-in site first.

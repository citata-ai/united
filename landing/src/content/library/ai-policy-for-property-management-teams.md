## Which data must never go into an AI tool?

Your AI policy should keep credentials and unapproved private records out of AI tools. Start with the actual task. A draft reminder needs approved wording, not a tenant's bank details.

Use invented names when staff test prompts. Remove addresses, account numbers, signatures, access codes, and full screening reports. A file can still identify someone after you delete the name. Review notes and attachments too.

Treat a connection to your property management system as a separate approval. A chatbot account does not grant permission to connect live records. Review what the connector can read, change, retain, and send elsewhere.

Rentvine's MCP page says connections use the linked user's permissions and keep financial access read-only. That describes Rentvine's design. It does not establish how another connector works.

Keep the approved tool list in a place staff can find. Name the account owner and permitted data types. Include the process for removing access when a staff member leaves.

## Which tasks need human review?

Require human review when an AI output can change housing access, money, safety, or a person's rights. Keep low-risk drafting separate from sending or posting.

| Task | Suggested policy | Required check |
|---|---|---|
| Draft a general office update | Allow in an approved tool | Confirm facts and tone |
| Summarize a repair thread | Allow with approved data access | Check urgency and omitted details |
| Draft an owner statement explanation | Allow as a draft | Match the ledger and report date |
| Recommend an applicant decision | Escalate to a trained person | Apply the written screening policy |
| Change payment instructions | Block autonomous action | Use the approved verification process |
| Send a legal notice | Require legal review | Check wording, timing, and service |

These are proposed controls, not claims that a vendor already enforces them. Test the controls in the product. Ask staff to show where a draft ends and an external action starts.

Do not let a polished response hide missing evidence. Ask the reviewer to open the source record. Record what the reviewer changed and why when the decision carries material risk.

## How do you keep Fair Housing compliance?

Keep rental standards consistent and review AI drafts for unequal treatment. Federal housing law still matters when software helps prepare the text.

Cornell's text of 42 USC 3604 prohibits specified discrimination in housing transactions, services, and advertising. The law includes disability-related protections. Do not use an AI tool to steer renters by a protected class.

Ask a trained person to review listing language, screening criteria, and accommodation-related messages. A tool's promise to remove biased words does not prove that its full workflow is fair.

Use neutral tests in training. Ask whether the draft describes the home or makes assumptions about people. Check whether the same approved criteria apply across applicants. Keep accommodation requests in a defined human review process.

Do not tell staff that AI makes a decision objective. Require the source, the relevant policy, and the person responsible for the final action.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Who approves new AI tools?

Assign approval to named people who can review both operations and data access. NIST describes its AI Risk Management Framework as voluntary guidance for managing AI risks. It is a useful reference, not a housing compliance certificate.

Use this generic policy template. Replace the bracketed fields and check local law before adopting it.

- **Purpose:** Staff may use approved AI tools to draft, summarize, and find information within the limits below.
- **Owner:** [Role or name] owns this policy and the approved tool register.
- **Approval:** [Operations role] and [security role] approve each tool, connector, and use before access starts.
- **Data:** Staff must use only approved data types. Staff must not share passwords, access codes, bank credentials, or unapproved private records.
- **Drafts:** Staff must check names, dates, amounts, source records, and tone before external use.
- **Decisions:** A trained person must approve housing decisions, legal notices, payment changes, and safety-related actions.
- **Housing:** Staff must use the written screening and accommodation processes. Staff must report suspected unequal treatment.
- **Access:** Each user must have an individual account. Access must match the user's job and end when that job ends.
- **Records:** Keep the source, final output, reviewer, and approval record where the company requires them.
- **Incidents:** Stop the task and contact [role and channel] after a suspected disclosure or wrong action.
- **Review:** [Policy owner] reviews the policy after material tool changes or incidents.

## How do you train staff?

Train staff with work samples and observable checks. Reading the policy alone does not show that a person can follow it.

Use a repair request, a listing draft, and an owner email with invented details. Ask the trainee to identify private data, check source records, and stop an unsafe action. Keep the expected result beside each exercise.

Show a failed example too. A summary might omit a leak's location or turn a tentative date into a promise. Ask staff to correct the draft before they send it.

Give each team a clear escalation path. Maintenance staff need a safety contact. Leasing staff need a screening policy owner. Accounting staff need a payment verification process.

Review errors without hiding them. Update the example set when a new failure appears. Keep the training focused on the task and its effect on people.

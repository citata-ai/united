## Why does reply speed matter for rental inquiries?

Reply speed matters because renters often send inquiries to several listings at once. The first landlord who answers with clear facts and a showing time often gets the showing. A slow reply means a cold lead, a longer vacancy and lost rent.

We have no public, primary study on rental reply times that we trust, so we give no percentage here. The logic is simple math. Each day a unit sits empty costs one day of rent.

Worked example, a $1,800 a month unit:

| Item | Number |
|---|---|
| Monthly rent | $1,800 |
| Rent per day ($1,800 × 12 ÷ 365) | about $59 |
| Vacancy if slow replies add 7 days | about $414 lost |
| Vacancy if slow replies add 14 days | about $828 lost |

Speed also helps fairness. One reply target for every renter is easy to prove later.

A good standard:

- **Business hours:** reply within 2 to 4 hours.
- **Evenings and weekends:** an auto-reply with the facts and a booking link, then a personal reply the next morning.
- **Every inquiry:** reply the same day it arrives in business hours.

## What goes in the first reply to a rental inquiry?

The first reply should answer the renter's next five questions before they ask. Give the facts, then one next step. Use the same template for every renter.

First reply checklist:

- [ ] Thank the renter and name the unit address
- [ ] Monthly rent and deposit
- [ ] Available date and lease length
- [ ] Pet rules and any pet fees
- [ ] Utilities the tenant pays
- [ ] A link to your written screening criteria
- [ ] Application fee and how to apply
- [ ] Two or three showing times, or a booking link
- [ ] Your name and a direct phone number

Sample first reply:

> Hi, thanks for asking about 123 Main St, Unit 2. Rent is $1,800 a month, with a $1,800 deposit. The unit is available November 1 on a 12-month lease. Cats are allowed; dogs are not. The tenant pays electric and gas. Our screening criteria are here: [link]. You can book a showing here: [link]. Open times this week are Tuesday 5 pm, Thursday 6 pm and Saturday 11 am. Questions? Call me at [number]. Thank you, [name].

Send the same text to each renter for the same unit. Change only the name and the open times.

## Should I pre-screen in the first message?

Light pre-screening in the first message saves time for both sides. Share your written criteria and let renters decide if they qualify. Ask only questions you ask everyone, and only questions about the rental itself.

| OK to ask everyone | Do not ask |
|---|---|
| "When do you want to move in?" | "Do you have kids?" |
| "How many people will live in the unit?" (for occupancy limits) | "Where are you from?" |
| "Do you have pets?" (service and support animals are not pets) | "Do you have a disability?" |
| "Have you read our screening criteria?" | "What church do you go to?" |

Some states add more protected classes. Washington RCW 59.18.255, for example, bars a refusal to rent based on a renter's lawful source of income. So "Do you use a voucher?" as a filter is a risk there. Check your state and city list.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Can I use an auto-reply or AI chatbot for rental inquiries?

You can use an auto-reply or an AI chatbot for rental inquiries, but you are still responsible for what it says. A tool that gives one renter a different answer than another can create the same risk as a person who does that.

The federal rule is clear. 24 CFR 100.80 lists "providing false or inaccurate information regarding the availability of a dwelling" to any person, "including testers", as a prohibited act when it is based on a protected class. Fair housing groups use testers: people who pose as renters to compare how landlords treat them.

Rules for a safe auto-reply or chatbot:

1. **Feed it one fact sheet** per unit: rent, deposit, date, pets, criteria, fees.
2. **Block opinion questions.** "Is this a good area for families?" should get a neutral answer that points to public facts, not a judgment.
3. **Give every renter the same showing slots.**
4. **Log every chat** with the time and the full text.
5. **Read a sample each week.** Check for wrong facts or different answers to the same question.
6. **Hand off to a person** for any accommodation request, such as a request about an assistance animal.

## How do I treat all rental inquiries the same for Fair Housing?

Treat all rental inquiries the same by fixing the process before the first inquiry arrives. The Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. 3604(d), bars telling any person that a unit is not available when it is, because of race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status or national origin.

Ads and replies count as statements. 24 CFR 100.75 bars any "notice, statement or advertisement" that shows a preference based on a protected class. That includes words like "perfect for singles" or "no kids".

A simple inquiry log keeps you honest:

| Date and time in | Channel | Unit | Reply time | Facts sent | Showing booked | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oct 14, 9:12 am | Zillow | Unit 2 | 10:05 am | Template A | Oct 16, 5 pm | Applied |
| Oct 14, 9:40 am | Email | Unit 2 | 10:20 am | Template A | Oct 16, 5 pm | No reply |

If a complaint comes later, this log shows that each renter got the same facts at the same speed.

## Must security deposits go in a separate account?

The property's law decides the required account. Check the rule before collecting funds, rather than choosing a bank account based only on convenience.

Washington RCW 59.18.270 requires prompt placement of security deposits in a qualifying trust account. It permits specified financial institutions or a licensed escrow agent in Washington. The landlord must give a receipt and written notice of the depository's name, address and location. [Read Washington's deposit rule](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=59.18.270).

Massachusetts chapter 186 section 15B requires a separate interest-bearing account in a bank within the commonwealth. The account must protect the deposit from the landlord's creditors under the stated conditions. [Read Massachusetts section 15B](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section15B).

These examples show why a national banking checklist needs local review. An account suitable for operating cash may not meet a trust requirement. A bank's product name alone does not prove compliance.

Ask the bank for written confirmation of account title, ownership treatment and permitted use. Then have the manager or attorney compare those terms with the statute.

## Do I owe interest on security deposits?

Interest depends on the governing law and sometimes a written agreement. Do not assume that whoever owns the bank login owns the interest.

Massachusetts section 15B requires interest under its statutory formula for deposits held for the covered period. Washington RCW 59.18.270 generally gives the landlord the account interest unless the parties agree otherwise in writing. The rules point in different directions.

Before quoting an interest amount, check when accrual begins, how the rate is set and when payment is due. Also check required statements and the effect of a midyear move-out. Store the rate source with the calculation.

Do not copy a rate from another owner's lease. A local ordinance or a changed law can affect the answer. Calendar the review before interest statements are due.

Give accounting staff a written instruction on posting interest. Keep interest owed to tenants separate from deposit principal and rent receipts. Record the payment method so the ledger and bank balance agree.

## Can I spend a tenant's deposit?

Do not use deposit funds as a working cash reserve. Keep money available for its lawful purpose and return obligations.

A separate bank balance helps protect that practice. A separate ledger explains the tenant's claim to the funds. You need both when the law calls for the account, and good records even where it does not.

IRS Publication 527 distinguishes refundable security deposits from rental income. If you plan to return a deposit, the IRS says not to include it in income when received. Later treatment depends on how the deposit is used. [Read the IRS rental publication](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527).

Tax treatment does not authorize a deduction from the tenant's money. First decide the tenant law question. Then record the tax and accounting result with the proper adviser.

Do not transfer a deposit to income just because an invoice exists. Review lease authority, condition proof and return procedures. Save the explanation with the transfer so a future reviewer can follow it.

## How do I track deposits across rental units?

Use a deposit ledger for each tenancy and reconcile the total to the bank. A building-level total alone does not show who owns each share.

| Field | Why it matters | Supporting proof |
|---|---|---|
| Tenant and property | Identifies the deposit owner | Signed lease |
| Receipt date and amount | Establishes original funds | Payment record |
| Bank placement | Shows where funds went | Deposit confirmation |
| Interest entry | Separates earnings from principal | Rate source and calculation |
| Approved deduction | Explains a lawful use | Condition record and invoice |
| Return or transfer | Closes the obligation | Payment or successor receipt |

The table is a recommended record format. Add any fields your local statute requires. Avoid putting full bank credentials in tenant-facing exports.

Reconcile when bank activity posts and before a tenancy closes. Compare the bank statement, ledger control total and tenant balances. Investigate a difference rather than forcing the numbers to agree through an unexplained adjustment.

Keep pending receipts and outstanding refunds visible. A payment initiated in software may not have settled. A refund marked approved may still await release.

## What happens when ownership or management changes?

Transfer the money and the records together under the applicable law. Do not let the sale file show a credit while nobody confirms the tenant balances.

Washington RCW 59.18.270 expressly addresses transfer to a successor's equivalent trust account and notice of the new depository. Check the property's rule for your transaction rather than assuming a closing adjustment is enough.

Ask the receiving manager to confirm the tenant list, principal, interest entries and disputed amounts. Both sides should retain a dated reconciliation. Record who sends required notices and how delivery will be proved.

Check account access after the transfer. Remove former staff permissions and update bank contacts. Preserve the audit history so the new manager can explain earlier deductions or interest payments.

Use this recommended setup checklist before the next deposit arrives:

- **Identify the law.** Record state and city requirements for the property.
- **Confirm the bank.** Check location, account title and trust treatment.
- **Prepare notices.** Review receipt and depository disclosures.
- **Set the ledger.** Track principal, interest and pending returns by tenancy.
- **Assign review.** Name the person who reconciles funds and checks changes.

Plan management costs separately with the [management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator). Deposit money should not hide a shortage in the operating budget.

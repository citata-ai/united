## How much does a Google Ads owner lead cost?

A Google Ads owner lead costs property managers about $250 to $350, in the one agency figure we found. Property Manager Websites (PMW) says in its "Know Your Numbers" worksheet that its clients see an average of $250 to $350 per qualified lead from PPC (pay per click) ads.

Here is how that compares with the broad benchmarks:

| Figure | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| PMW clients, qualified PM owner lead | $250 to $350 | PMW worksheet |
| Real estate median cost per lead, 2026 | $102.51 | WordStream 2026 |
| Real estate median cost per lead, 2025 | $100.48 | WordStream 2025 |
| All industries median cost per lead, 2026 | $66.69 | WordStream 2026 |

WordStream's 2026 data covers 13,474 US search campaigns in Google and Microsoft Ads, from 2025-04-01 to 2026-03-31. Its "real estate" group is broad. It mixes agents, home sales and rentals. So it is a floor for owner leads, not a target.

## What is a typical cost per click for property management keywords?

A typical click on a property management owner search costs more than the real estate average, in our view. We could not get a measured national number for property management terms alone. Here is what the public data does show.

| Year | Real estate median CPC | All industries median CPC | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | $2.53 | $5.26 | WordStream 2025 |
| 2026 | $3.22 | $5.42 | WordStream 2026 |

WordStream says real estate had the biggest click price rise of any industry in 2026: up 27.27% in one year. Real estate also had one of the lowest conversion rates in 2025, at 3.28%. In 2026 it was 3.70%.

To see the price in your own city, open Google Ads Keyword Planner. Search "property management [city]" and read the top-of-page bid range. That number is for your market today.

## Why can owner leads pass $300 in big markets?

Owner leads can pass $300 because cost per lead equals cost per click divided by conversion rate. Both parts work against property managers in a big city.

- **Many bidders.** Every manager, franchise and lead service in the city bids on the same few owner searches. Fourandhalf names competition as the biggest driver of click price.
- **Mixed intent.** Many people who search "property management" are tenants looking for an apartment or a portal login. You pay for their clicks too.
- **Few form fills.** PMW's Phoenix example expects 750 clicks a month to bring about 45 leads, or 6%.

Worked math, cost per lead = CPC ÷ conversion rate:

| Cost per click | Click-to-lead rate | Cost per lead |
|---|---|---|
| $3.22 | 3.70% | $87 |
| $5.00 | 3.00% | $167 |
| $8.00 | 3.00% | $267 |
| $10.00 | 2.50% | $400 |
| $12.00 | 3.00% | $400 |

The first row uses WordStream's 2026 real estate medians. The other rows use example inputs, not measured data. Run the math with your own Keyword Planner bid and your own landing page rate.

The Goodjuju 2026 report shows the same effect in owner data. Across 35 property management companies, paid search visits turned into owner form leads at only 0.2% in fall 2026. Goodjuju warns that this is not a benchmark: only 6 companies ran paid search, and ad landing pages and ad calls were not tracked.

## How has property management click cost changed since 2018?

Property management click prices rose fast from 2018 to 2020, in the one study we found. Fourandhalf, a property management marketing agency, compared 6 competitive US cities.

| Finding, 2018 to 2020 | Value |
|---|---|
| Average CPC rise across 6 cities | 117.2% |
| Smallest rise (Tampa) | 9% |
| Largest rise (Honolulu) | 372.97% |
| Average leads per month, 2018 | 6 |
| Average leads per month, 2020 | 5.88 |

So in that sample, companies paid about twice as much per click for about the same number of leads. Fourandhalf does not name the other 4 cities or its tool. Its data stops in 2020. WordStream's newer numbers show the rise has not stopped for real estate.

## What budget do I need to test Google Ads?

You need enough budget for about 10 owner leads in a month. Fewer than that tells you little about your close rate.

| Monthly budget | Leads at $250 | Leads at $350 |
|---|---|---|
| $1,000 | 4 | 3 |
| $2,500 | 10 | 7 |
| $5,000 | 20 | 14 |

PMW says its client campaigns run from $500 to more than $10,000 a month. Google Ads Help says a campaign can spend up to twice its daily budget on one day. Over a month it charges no more than 30.4 times the daily budget. So set the daily budget at your monthly test budget divided by 30.4. For $2,500, that is about $82 a day.

Before you start, check what a lead is worth to you. At $1,696 of yearly fees per door, 1.2 doors per owner and 4 years, one owner is worth about $8,139. At a 20% close rate and a 4:1 return target, you can pay up to about $407 per lead. Check your own fees with the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## How do I keep owner lead cost down?

Lower cost per lead by paying for fewer wrong clicks and turning more right clicks into leads.

- [ ] **Add negative keywords:** "apartments", "for rent", "jobs", "login", "portal", "pay rent".
- [ ] **Send ads to an owner page**, not your home page or tenant listings.
- [ ] **Show your fees** on that page, so owners can decide fast.
- [ ] **Track calls and forms** as separate conversions in Google Ads.
- [ ] **Target only the ZIP codes you serve.** A lead outside your area costs the same and never signs.
- [ ] **Reply the same business hour.** A paid lead you do not call back is spend with no return.

Ads stop when the money stops. Google search and Maps brought about 86% of owner leads in the Goodjuju 2026 study. Read [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping) before you move your whole budget to ads.

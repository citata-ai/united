## What is the average monthly management fee in San Antonio?

The usual monthly management fee in San Antonio is 7% to 10% of rent. On 2026-10-19 we read the published prices of 10 managers in the San Antonio metro. 6 of them charge a percent of rent and 4 charge a flat fee. We did not use old survey averages. Each number below is on the manager's own page.

What the table shows:

- **Percent fees are low to mid.** No percent-based manager we checked charges more than 10%. Two charge 7%.
- **Lower rate, higher leasing fee.** Both 7% managers charge 70% of the first month to place a tenant. The 10% managers charge 50% (Pyramis, Global Realty Group).
- **Flat fees vary 3 times.** Renters Warehouse starts at $119 a month. Flat Fee Landlord's Concierge plan is $349 on annual billing.

Watch for: Pyramis lists a $25 yearly technology fee and a $150 annual property review. Flat Fee Landlord adds a 10% coordination fee on repair invoices. Ask for the full fee list before you sign.

## Which local rules add cost for San Antonio owners?

Short-term rental permits add the most local cost in San Antonio. Airbnb's San Antonio page lists two permit types. Type 1 is the owner's or operator's primary home. Type 2 is not. The application fee is $300 for Type 1 and $450 for Type 2. A permit is valid for 3 years and covers 1 unit.

Type 2 rentals are allowed by right only up to 12.5% of the units on a block face. Above that, you need a special exception from the Board of Adjustment. Hosts also collect the city's 9% hotel occupancy tax and Bexar County's 1.75% tax.

Long-term rentals have fewer local costs. Texas Local Government Code 214.902 lets a city set rent control only after it finds a housing emergency due to a disaster, and only with the governor's approval. So San Antonio has no rent cap in normal times.

## Must a San Antonio property manager hold a Texas license?

A San Antonio property manager who works for pay must hold a Texas real estate license in most cases. Texas Occupations Code 1101.002(1)(A) defines a broker as a person who, for others and for pay, leases real estate or negotiates a lease. It also covers a person who "controls the acceptance or deposit of rent from a resident of a single-family residential real property unit." The Texas Real Estate Commission (TREC) issues these licenses.

Checklist before you sign:

- [ ] Ask for the broker's name and TREC license number.
- [ ] Look up the license on the TREC site.
- [ ] Ask where the manager holds your rent and the security deposit.
- [ ] Ask about a military rate if you or your tenant serve. Pyramis and Hendricks list one.
- [ ] Get every fee in the management agreement.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Flat fee or percent: which model is common in San Antonio?

The percent model is still more common in San Antonio. 6 of the 10 managers we checked charge a percent of rent, and 4 sell a flat fee. San Antonio has more flat-fee choices than many metros we checked.

Use rent to choose. At 8%, a $150 flat fee breaks even at $1,875 rent. At 10%, it breaks even at $1,500. Renters Warehouse's $119 equals 8% of $1,487.50. Most San Antonio 2-bedrooms rent below these points, so a low percent often costs less. Compare the full year, with leasing and renewal fees, before you choose.

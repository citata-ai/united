Mississippi puts its whole security deposit law in one short section: Miss. Code 89-8-21, part of the Residential Landlord and Tenant Act. The text dates from 1991 (Laws 1991, ch. 478). We checked the Mississippi Legislature's bill index for the 2025 and 2026 sessions and found no bill filed to amend 89-8-21. The 2023 and 2024 bills that listed it died. Here is every rule in one table.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | No cap in the statute | Miss. Code 89-8-21 |
| Who holds the deposit | The landlord, for the tenant | 89-8-21(2) |
| Tenant's claim vs. landlord's creditors | Tenant comes first | 89-8-21(2) |
| Allowed deductions | Unpaid rent, tenant damage (not ordinary wear and tear), cleaning, other reasonable costs of the default | 89-8-21(3) |
| How to claim deductions | Written notice delivered to the tenant, with each amount itemized | 89-8-21(3) |
| Return deadline | No later than 45 days after tenancy ends, possession is delivered and the tenant demands it | 89-8-21(3) |
| Bad-faith penalty | Up to $200 plus actual damages | 89-8-21(4) |
| Interest or separate account | Not in the statute | 89-8-21 |
| Return when the lease ends for breach | Landlord returns prepaid rent and recoverable security | 89-8-13(4) |

## What is the maximum security deposit in Mississippi?

Mississippi Code 89-8-21 sets no maximum security deposit. The statute regulates how you hold, use and return a deposit. It does not cap the amount.

That does not mean any amount is wise. A large deposit can push good applicants away. A program contract, such as a Housing Choice Voucher HAP contract, can also set its own rules. Read your program documents and your lease before you set the amount.

Section 89-8-21(1) also draws a line between two kinds of money:

- **Covered:** money whose main purpose is to secure the tenant's performance of the rental agreement.
- **Not covered:** a payment made to secure the execution of the rental agreement, including an advance payment of rent.

Label each payment in your lease. A clear label avoids a dispute about which rule applies.

## How many days does a Mississippi landlord have to return a deposit?

A Mississippi landlord returns the remaining deposit no later than 45 days after three things happen. The tenancy ends. The tenant delivers possession. The tenant demands the deposit. That is the text of 89-8-21(3).

The demand matters. The statute ties the clock to the tenant's demand, so ask each tenant for a written demand and a forwarding address at move-out. Then put the date on your calendar.

Worked example:

| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| Lease ends | 2026-11-30 |
| Tenant returns keys (possession delivered) | 2026-11-30 |
| Tenant emails a demand with a forwarding address | 2026-12-02 |
| Last day to return the balance with the itemized notice | 2027-01-16 |

We count the 45 days from the last of the three events, here the demand on 2026-12-02. If you are not sure, return it sooner. The statute sets a latest date, not a target.

## What can a Mississippi landlord deduct?

A Mississippi landlord may keep only amounts "reasonably necessary" for four purposes under 89-8-21(3):

1. **Unpaid rent.** Rent the tenant owes under the rental agreement.
2. **Damage.** Repairs for damage the tenant caused, exclusive of ordinary wear and tear.
3. **Cleaning.** Cleaning the unit when the tenancy ends.
4. **Other costs of default.** Other reasonable and necessary expenses that result from the tenant's default.

The statute adds one condition. The deposit must be made for any or all of those purposes. So write the purposes into your lease.

Watch for: "ordinary wear and tear" is not defined in 89-8-21. So each deduction must stand on its own facts. Take dated photos at move-in and move-out to support each line.

## Does a Mississippi landlord have to give an itemized list?

Yes. Section 89-8-21(3) says you claim any part of the deposit by written notice delivered to the tenant. The same subsection says that notice "shall itemize the amounts claimed".

A clear itemized notice has these parts:

- [ ] Tenant name, unit address and move-out date.
- [ ] Total deposit held.
- [ ] Each deduction on its own line, with the reason and the amount.
- [ ] Invoices, receipts or photos for each repair or cleaning cost.
- [ ] The balance returned, and how and when you sent it.
- [ ] Your contact details for questions.

Send the notice and the balance together, before day 45. Keep proof of delivery.

## Must a Mississippi landlord pay interest or use a separate account?

Section 89-8-21 does not require interest on a deposit, and it does not require a separate bank account. It does set two rules on holding the money in 89-8-21(2):

- The landlord holds the deposit for the tenant who signed the agreement.
- The tenant's claim to the deposit comes before the claim of any creditor of the landlord.

A separate account is still a simple way to show which money belongs to tenants. If you hold deposits for owners as a licensed broker, check your license rules too.

## What is the penalty for wrongly keeping a deposit?

A Mississippi landlord who keeps a deposit in violation of 89-8-21, and without good faith, may owe damages of up to $200 in addition to actual damages. That rule is in 89-8-21(4). It also applies to a transferee, such as a new owner who takes over the deposit.

Here is how the exposure adds up on a $1,200 deposit kept in full, with $300 of real cleaning costs:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Deposit kept | $1,200 |
| Cost you can support with an itemized notice | $300 |
| Actual damages: amount wrongly kept | $900 |
| Extra damages for bad faith (maximum) | $200 |
| Total exposure | Up to $1,100 |

The math is ours, from the text of 89-8-21(4). Court costs and attorney fees are separate questions. Ask a local attorney about them.

## What happens to the deposit when a lease ends early for breach?

When a rental agreement ends for a material breach under 89-8-13, the landlord returns all prepaid and unearned rent and the security the tenant can recover under 89-8-21. That is 89-8-13(4). The same deduction rules and the same itemized notice apply.

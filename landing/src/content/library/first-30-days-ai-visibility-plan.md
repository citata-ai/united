## Why does the order of the 30 days matter?

The order matters because AI answers about local property managers lean on other sites first. In our city run (our data, 2026-10), ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini answered 150 prompts like "best property management company in Phoenix". Expertise.com was a source in 93 answers. iPropertyManagement.com and Yelp were sources in 50 each. Reddit was a source in 22.

So the plan starts with access and facts, not blog posts. A great page does nothing if a crawler cannot read it. A good listing does harm if it shows an old phone number.

Set your hopes before you start. SOCi's 2026 guide plans for 3 to 6 months of steady work before clear movement in AI answers. ClearLead Digital says "months not weeks" is the realistic timeline. It adds that pages already in the top 10–20 results can earn citations within weeks. The 30 days below build the base. They do not promise a result.

| Week | Focus | Main output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline, access, core listings | Prompt list with day-1 results, matching NAP |
| 2 | Owner pages on your site | Fee page, service area page, city pages |
| 3 | Directories and reviews | 4+ complete profiles, review request flow |
| 4 | Measure and plan | Day-30 prompt results, monthly routine |

## What to fix in week 1?

Week 1 fixes what blocks everything else: the baseline, crawler access and your core facts.

- [ ] **Write 10–20 owner prompts.** Example: "best property manager in <city> for a single-family home". Use your 3 main cities.
- [ ] **Run the prompts** in ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity. Save who each answer names and which sources it cites. This is your day-1 baseline.
- [ ] **Check robots.txt.** OpenAI says to allow OAI-SearchBot to appear in ChatGPT search answers. Search changes take about 24 hours to apply. Blocking GPTBot (training) does not block search.
- [ ] **Check your CDN or host.** Google lists "ensuring that crawling is allowed in robots.txt, and by any CDN or hosting infrastructure" as a basic for AI features.
- [ ] **Write one facts sheet**: exact name, address, phone, website, hours, service area, door count and fees.
- [ ] **Fix Google Business Profile** to match the sheet. Google bans taglines in the name and does not accept P.O. boxes or virtual offices.
- [ ] **Claim Bing Places and Apple Business Connect.** SOCi says both feed data that AI assistants use.

Skip llms.txt and new schema this week. Google says no special schema is needed for AI Overviews or AI Mode. On 2026-06-15 Google added that llms.txt files are not needed for Google Search.

## Which pages come in week 2?

Week 2 adds the pages that answer the owner's first questions with exact facts. AI engines quote clear text. Vague pages give them nothing to quote.

- [ ] **Fee page.** Every fee in a table: monthly, leasing, renewal, vacancy, setup, markup, cancel fee. Use real numbers.
- [ ] **Service area page.** List each city or ZIP code you serve. Name the property types and the door count with a date.
- [ ] **One page per main city.** Start with your top 3 cities. Add local facts an owner needs, not a copied template.
- [ ] **Owner FAQ block.** 10–20 short answers. Start each answer with one direct sentence.
- [ ] **About page** with founding year, license number and the people who run the company.

ClearLead suggests a 40–60 word direct answer at the top of key pages. Use that as a guide. To check your fees against the market, try the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## Which listings and reviews come in week 3?

Week 3 completes the directory profiles that AI answers cite, then starts honest review requests. In our data, these sites appeared most often in property manager answers.

| Site | AI answers citing it (of 150) | Week 3 task |
|---|---|---|
| Expertise.com | 93 | Check if you are listed; update facts |
| iPropertyManagement.com | 50 | Claim or update profile |
| Yelp | 50 | Claim page; match NAP; add photos |
| ThreeBestRated.com | 21 | Check listing facts |
| BBB | 14 | Claim profile; match NAP |

Source: our data, 2026-10, 50 city prompts across ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini. This is a correlation from a small sample. A listing does not guarantee a mention.

- [ ] **Copy each fact from your facts sheet.** Do not retype it.
- [ ] **Fix or close old listings** with an old address or phone.
- [ ] **Start review requests.** Ask owners after a clear win: a lease signed, a repair done fast, a year-end report sent.
- [ ] **Ask every owner the same way.** Google's review policy bans paid reviews and bans asking only happy customers ("selectively solicit positive reviews").
- [ ] **Reply to recent reviews**, good and bad, in calm, short words.

## What to measure in week 4?

Week 4 runs the same prompts again and sets up a monthly routine. Change nothing in the prompt list. Only the same prompts give a fair before and after.

- [ ] **Rerun all prompts** in all 4 engines. Use the same words and the same cities.
- [ ] **Count** answers that name you, answers that cite your site and answers that cite a listing you fixed.
- [ ] **Note wrong facts** in any answer. Trace each one to the site that holds it and fix it there.
- [ ] **Verify your site in Bing Webmaster Tools.** Its AI Performance report, in public preview since February 10, 2026, shows Copilot citations and grounding queries.
- [ ] **Add "How did you hear about us?"** to your owner lead form, with "ChatGPT or another AI" as an option.
- [ ] **Book a monthly prompt run** on the same date.

Use this simple score sheet:

| Metric | Day 1 | Day 30 |
|---|---|---|
| Answers that name you (of N) | __ | __ |
| Answers that cite your site | __ | __ |
| Answers with a wrong fact about you | __ | __ |
| Listings that match the facts sheet (of 8) | __ | __ |
| New owner reviews this month | __ | __ |

Expect small changes on day 30. The listings row is the one you control fully, so fix it first.

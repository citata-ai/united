## What are SERP, map pack, and organic results?

SERP means search engine results page: what a person sees after a query. Query means the words used in a search. A property owner might search for a local manager, a fee question, or a service comparison.

Organic results are unpaid search listings. The map pack is the local business group that can appear with a map. Paid ads are purchased placements. These labels describe different parts of the search experience, not equal types of owner interest.

Google's SEO Starter Guide explains the basics of helping search engines understand content. Use that guide as a reference when an agency introduces a new term. Ask how each proposed task helps a person find or use your service page.

| Term | Plain meaning | Question for your report |
|---|---|---|
| Query | Words used in search | Does the query show owner intent? |
| Impression | A recorded appearance | Which page or listing appeared? |
| Click | A selected result | Where did the visitor land? |
| Organic result | Unpaid search listing | Was the page useful to owners? |
| Map pack | Local business results with a map | Which office and market are measured? |
| Conversion | A defined useful action | Is it a real inquiry or just a click? |

Treat the table as a reporting aid. A tool may apply a precise definition that differs from your sales team's use. Ask the report owner to state that definition before comparing totals.

Do not ask only whether rankings rose. Ask which searches, locations, devices, and pages the report covers. A renter search can grow while owner inquiries stay flat. Keep the audience visible in the report.

## What are backlinks and Domain Authority?

A backlink is a link from another website to yours. An internal link joins pages within your own site. Anchor text is the readable text of a link. These terms help you discuss where a visitor can go and why.

For a manager, a useful link might come from a genuine partner resource or a local organization page. Ask whether the link helps a real reader understand the service. Do not judge a placement by a score alone.

Domain Authority is a metric made by Moz to compare likely search performance. Moz says Domain Authority is not a Google ranking factor. Use the score as a limited comparison measure, not as a business result or an instruction from Google.

Ask an agency to separate links earned through useful work from paid placements. Request the actual source URLs and the reason each page fits your audience. A spreadsheet total cannot tell you whether a link is useful.

For example, compare a relevant local resource page with a generic directory entry that no owner would use. The business value may differ even if a report lists each as a new backlink. Open the pages yourself and assess their context.

Keep your own internal links clear. An owner reading about repairs should be able to find your maintenance scope and contact route. Helpful navigation is a practical task your team can review without learning a scoring system.

## What do crawl and index mean?

Crawling means a search system fetches page content. Indexing means the system analyzes and stores information for possible search use. Google's guide describes crawling, indexing, and serving results as separate stages.

A discovered URL has been found. A crawled URL has been fetched. Neither statement alone proves that owners can find the page for the query you want. Ask which stage the report actually shows.

A sitemap is a file that lists URLs you want search systems to know about. A canonical URL is the preferred page address when similar versions exist. A redirect sends a request from one address to another. These terms matter when you change or duplicate pages.

Ask your developer to inspect the exact page, not just the home page. Provide the URL and the problem the owner sees. A vague request to fix SEO makes testing harder.

Use a simple page record: intended audience, main question, public URL, current search status, contact path, and next repair. Keep technical checks attached to the page's purpose. An indexed page with no useful owner answer still needs editorial work.

Google does not guarantee crawling, indexing, or serving a page. Treat a submission as a request, not a paid route to placement. Ask anyone who promises guaranteed inclusion to show the specific source for that claim.

## What is E-E-A-T for a property manager?

E-E-A-T means experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness. Google's helpful-content guide says E-E-A-T itself is not a specific ranking factor. Do not treat the term as a badge you can buy or a score you must reach.

For your site, ask plain questions. Does the writer know the process? Can the reader check the source? Are legal claims reviewed? Is the company clear about who does the work and where it operates?

Show actual service knowledge with an approved sample report or repair workflow. Name the person who checked a local legal explanation. Link to the law used. Do not invent credentials, reviews, or a fictional expert biography.

Separate a company claim from an independent fact. Your agreement can support what your service includes. It cannot prove your company is the best in the city. A client result needs its own evidence and permission.

Use this review for owner pages first. An old article with vague advice does not become trustworthy because someone adds an author photo. Correct the content and the sources before adding decoration.

## How should you use these terms in an agency meeting?

Use the terms to ask for evidence and a clear next action. You do not need to sound like a search specialist. You need to know what changed, why it matters, and whether it led to useful owner work.

Ask the agency to show the source report and a sample page. Connect queries to landing pages, then connect accepted inquiries to sales outcomes. Keep a note of what the tracking cannot prove.

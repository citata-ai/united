## What is the difference between a co-signer and a guarantor?

A co-signer usually signs the lease and shares the tenant's duties from day one. A guarantor usually signs a separate guaranty and promises to pay only if the tenant does not. State law and the exact wording of your documents decide the real duties, so the words alone do not settle it.

| Point | Co-signer | Guarantor |
|---|---|---|
| Signs | The lease | A separate guaranty |
| Lives in the unit | No | No |
| Duty starts | With the lease | When the tenant defaults |
| Rights to the unit | Depends on the lease; may have some | Usually none |
| Typical use | Student, first renter, thin credit | Same, plus guarantor companies |
| Who you collect from first | Either signer, per the lease | The tenant, then the guarantor |

Many landlords prefer a separate guaranty. It keeps the backer's duty clear and avoids giving them rights to the unit. Ask a local attorney to write or review the form once.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## When should I accept a co-signer or guarantor?

Accept a backer when the applicant fails one of your criteria but passes the rest. A backer fixes a gap. It does not fix a pattern of bad rent history.

Good cases:

- **Low or new income,** such as a student or a new graduate with a job offer.
- **Thin credit file,** such as a young adult or a person new to the U.S.
- **Credit below your floor,** with clean rent history.

Weak cases:

- **Recent eviction** or unpaid rent to a past landlord.
- **A backer who does not pass** your own criteria.

Write your backer policy and apply it to every applicant. Offering a guarantor option to some applicants but not others can break Fair Housing law.

## What income should a guarantor have?

A guarantor should have more income than you would ask of a tenant. The guarantor pays for their own home too. If they must cover your rent, they pay two housing costs at once.

Many landlords ask for a higher multiple of rent for guarantors than for tenants. Guarantor companies show how high the bar can go. Insurent says it asks some backers, called a Responsible Party, for annual income of 50 times the monthly rent, or liquid assets of 80 times the monthly rent.

**Worked example:** rent is $2,000 a month.

| Rule | Annual income needed |
|---|---|
| Tenant at 3× monthly rent, yearly (36× monthly) | $72,000 |
| Guarantor at 50× monthly rent (Insurent example) | $100,000 |

Use these as examples, not rules. Set your own multiple and apply it to every guarantor.

## Do I screen the guarantor the same way?

Screen a guarantor for income, credit and identity, with written consent. You can skip rental history and household checks, because the guarantor will not live in the unit.

**Steps:**

1. **Get written consent** to pull a consumer report. The Fair Credit Reporting Act (15 U.S.C. 1681b) limits when you may pull one. A transaction the person starts, with their consent, is the safe path.
2. **Verify identity** with a government ID.
3. **Verify income** at the source: pay stubs plus a call to the employer, or tax returns.
4. **Check credit** against your written guarantor criteria.
5. **Sign the guaranty** at the same time as the lease, or before keys.
6. **Send an adverse action notice** if a consumer report leads you to reject the guarantor. 15 U.S.C. 1681m requires it.

Ask for the guarantor's address and phone. Keep them current at each renewal.

## What about third-party guarantor companies?

A guarantor company acts as the backer for a fee the renter pays. It is a good option when an applicant has no family member or friend who qualifies.

Two examples:

- **Insurent** says its guaranty fee for U.S. renters averages 70% to 90% of one month's rent for a one-year lease. For renters without U.S. credit history, it says 98% to 110%. The fee is per lease, not per person.
- **TheGuarantors** offers a lease guarantee product and renters insurance for owners and operators.

| Point | Personal guarantor | Guarantor company |
|---|---|---|
| Cost to renter | None | A fee per lease |
| Your screening work | Full screening of the backer | The company approves |
| Collecting on a default | You chase a person | You file a claim under the company's terms |
| Coverage limit | The guaranty text | The policy or guaranty terms |
| Your building must be approved | No | Often yes |

Read the company's terms before you accept it. Check the coverage limit, what counts as a default, the claim deadline and what you must send with a claim.

## What should the guaranty say?

The guaranty should name the lease, the amount covered, the period and what happens at renewal. Gaps in the form cause most disputes.

- [ ] Names of tenant, guarantor and landlord.
- [ ] Address of the unit and the lease date.
- [ ] What the guarantor covers: rent, fees, damage, legal costs.
- [ ] Any cap on the total amount.
- [ ] Whether the guaranty covers renewals and rent increases.
- [ ] How you will give notice of a default.
- [ ] Signature and date, with ID checked.

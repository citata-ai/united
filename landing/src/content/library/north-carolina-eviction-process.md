North Carolina calls an eviction "summary ejectment". A magistrate hears it in small claims court. We read each rule below in the official statute text on ncleg.gov on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Demand for past-due rent | Tenant has 10 days to pay after the demand | G.S. 42-3 |
| Notice to end a month-to-month tenancy | 7 days | G.S. 42-14 |
| Notice to end a week-to-week tenancy | 2 days | G.S. 42-14 |
| Notice to end a year-to-year tenancy | 1 month before the end of the year | G.S. 42-14 |
| Hearing date | Within 7 days of the summons, not counting weekends and legal holidays | G.S. 42-28 |
| Small claims money limit | $10,000 | G.S. 7A-210 |
| Tenant appeal | 10 days after judgment | G.S. 7A-228 |
| Sheriff executes the writ | Within 5 days after the sheriff gets it | G.S. 42-36.2(a) |
| Tenant property after removal | 7 days to ask for it back | G.S. 42-25.9(g), 42-36.2(b) |
| Retaliation defense window | Protected act within 12 months before filing | G.S. 42-37.1 |
| Self-help eviction | Not allowed | G.S. 42-25.6 |

## What notice must a North Carolina landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

A North Carolina landlord must demand all past-due rent and wait 10 days. G.S. 42-3 implies a forfeiture of the lease if the tenant fails to pay within 10 days after the demand. This rule applies to every oral or written lease with a fixed rent due date.

Many leases also hold their own forfeiture clause. G.S. 42-26(a)(2) lets you remove a tenant whose lease ended under its own terms. The lease may also say that partial rent does not waive the breach (G.S. 42-26(c)).

Watch for: G.S. 42-26(b) says unpaid water, sewer or electric charges billed under G.S. 62-110 cannot be a reason to end the lease. A tenant payment goes to rent first, unless the tenant says otherwise.

## How long does an eviction take in North Carolina?

A North Carolina eviction for unpaid rent can take about 4 to 6 weeks if the tenant does not appeal. That number is our estimate from the statute deadlines, not court data. Clerk and sheriff workloads change it.

Here is a worked timeline for a lease with rent due on the 1st:

1. **Day 1.** You deliver a written demand for all past-due rent (G.S. 42-3).
2. **Day 12.** The 10 days end with no payment. You file the complaint with the clerk.
3. **Day 12 to 21.** The hearing comes within 7 days of the summons, not counting weekends and holidays (G.S. 42-28).
4. **Hearing day.** The magistrate gives judgment for possession if you prove your case (G.S. 42-30).
5. **Next 10 days.** The tenant may appeal (G.S. 7A-228).
6. **After that.** You ask the clerk for a writ of possession. The sheriff executes it within 5 days after receipt (G.S. 42-36.2).

An appeal adds time. The case goes to district court for a new trial. To stop the removal, the tenant pays undisputed back rent to the clerk. The tenant also signs an undertaking to pay rent as it comes due (G.S. 42-34(b)). If the tenant misses a payment by 5 business days, the clerk issues execution on your request (G.S. 42-34(f)).

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in North Carolina, and what does it cost?

A North Carolina landlord files with the clerk of superior court in the county. The landlord asks for assignment to a magistrate. G.S. 7A-210 lets small claims hear summary ejectment, with a money claim up to $10,000.

The North Carolina Judicial Branch civil costs chart lists these fees. They are in effect from January 1, 2026.

| Cost item | Amount | Basis on the chart |
|---|---|---|
| General Court of Justice fee | $80.00 | G.S. 7A-305(a)(2) |
| Facilities fee | $12.00 | G.S. 7A-305(a)(1) |
| Telecommunications and data fee | $4.00 | G.S. 7A-305(a)(1a) |
| Magistrate total | $96.00 | G.S. 7A-305 |
| Sheriff service, each item of process | $30.00 | G.S. 7A-311(a)(1) |

So one tenant served by the sheriff costs $126 to start. Two adult tenants, each served, cost $156. The sheriff mails the papers and tries to serve in person (G.S. 42-29).

G.S. 42-46(i) lets you recover the court filing fees and service costs from the tenant. A written lease may also add one administrative fee under G.S. 42-46(e) to (g). More landlord guides are on our [guides page](/guides).

## Can a North Carolina landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes, a North Carolina landlord can evict for a lease violation if the lease says the tenancy ends on that breach. G.S. 42-26(a)(2) covers a tenant whose "estate has ceased" under the lease terms. Write that forfeiture clause clearly.

North Carolina also allows an eviction at the end of a tenancy without a stated cause. G.S. 42-26(a)(1) covers a tenant who holds over after the term ends. For a periodic tenancy, you end the term first with the G.S. 42-14 notice:

- **Month to month:** 7 days' notice.
- **Week to week:** 2 days' notice.
- **Year to year:** 1 month before the end of the current year.
- **Manufactured home space only:** 60 days before the end of the period.

Watch for: a tenant can raise retaliation as a defense. G.S. 42-37.1 protects good faith repair requests, code complaints and tenant organizing within 12 months before you file. You can still win in 3 common cases. The tenant did not pay rent or broke a substantial lease term. Or the tenant held over after a fixed term with no option to renew.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in North Carolina?

The sheriff removes the tenant in North Carolina, not the landlord. G.S. 42-36.2(a) says the sheriff must execute the writ within 5 days after receipt. The sheriff first tells the tenant the approximate time of removal.

The sheriff gives that notice in one of 3 ways (G.S. 42-36.2(d)):

1. **Hand delivery** at least 2 days before the time in the notice.
2. **Copy left at the home** with a resident of suitable age, at least 2 days before.
3. **First-class mail** at least 5 days before.

After removal, the tenant's property has rules too. For 7 days you may move it for storage, but you may not throw it away or sell it (G.S. 42-25.9(g)). If the tenant asks within 7 days, you must release it. If all property is worth less than $500, it counts as abandoned after 5 days (G.S. 42-25.9(h)).

If the judgment is more than 30 days old, sign an affidavit before execution. It states that you made no new lease and took no rent after the judgment (G.S. 42-36.1A).

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in North Carolina?

No, self-help eviction is not legal in North Carolina. G.S. 42-25.6 says a residential tenant may be "constructively or actually removed" only through the court procedure. A lockout or a utility shutoff to force the tenant out falls on the wrong side of that rule.

The penalties are in G.S. 42-25.9. The tenant can recover possession or end the lease. The tenant can also claim actual damages. G.S. 42-25.7 bans distress, so you may not seize the tenant's things for unpaid rent. G.S. 42-25.8 voids any lease term that says otherwise.

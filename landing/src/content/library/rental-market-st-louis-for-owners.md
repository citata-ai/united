## How much did HUD's fair market rent change in St. Louis for FY2027?

HUD's fair market rent for a 2-bedroom in St. Louis rose 10.8% for FY2027, from $1,218 to $1,349. The FY2027 figures took effect on 2026-10-01 (Federal Register notice FR-6553-N-03). The St. Louis, MO-IL area covers St. Louis city, St. Louis County, St. Charles County and 11 more counties in Missouri and Illinois.

| Unit size | FY2026 FMR | FY2027 FMR | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Studio | $955 | $1,064 | +11.4% |
| 1 bedroom | $995 | $1,104 | +11.0% |
| 2 bedrooms | $1,218 | $1,349 | +10.8% |
| 3 bedrooms | $1,568 | $1,731 | +10.4% |
| 4 bedrooms | $1,812 | $1,997 | +10.2% |

Source: HUD FY2027 and FY2026 FMR files. Every size rose by more than 10%.

What this means for you: under 24 CFR 982.503, a housing authority can set its payment standard at 90% to 110% of the FMR without HUD approval. A higher FMR gives the authority room to pay more for a voucher unit. Ask the St. Louis Housing Authority or the county authority for its current payment standard. See all sizes on the [St. Louis fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/st-louis-fair-market-rent-2027).

## Are St. Louis rents rising or falling?

St. Louis rents are rising faster than the US average. Zillow's Observed Rent Index (ZORI, the typical asking rent for all home types) hit a record for the St. Louis metro in September 2026.

| Point in time | Zillow rent index | Change |
|---|---|---|
| September 2019 | $965 | Before the boom |
| September 2025 | $1,371 | +42.1% from 2019 |
| September 2026 | $1,427 | +4.1% in 1 year, a record |

We did the math from the Zillow file. The US index rose 2.7% in the same year, to $1,932. St. Louis rents are still about 26% lower than the US index.

Yardi Matrix measures apartments only. Its May 2026 report put the St. Louis average asking rent at $1,344, up 0.3% over the 3 months through March.

## What vacancy rate does St. Louis have?

St. Louis has a moderate vacancy rate. Yardi Matrix reported occupancy of 93.4% in its May 2026 report, which leaves about 6.6% of units empty.

New supply is small for a metro of 2.75 million people. Yardi Matrix counted about 4,700 units under construction and close to 2,700 units delivered in the past year.

Watch for: vacancy in St. Louis varies a lot from block to block. Metro numbers hide that. Check recent listings and days on market near your property before you set the rent.

## Which local rules affect St. Louis rental returns?

The City of St. Louis and St. Louis County are separate governments with separate permit rules. Missouri state law sets the limits for both.

| Rule | City of St. Louis | Unincorporated St. Louis County |
|---|---|---|
| Inspection at a new tenant | Certificate of Inspection before occupancy; reinspection every 3 years or at a change in occupancy | Occupancy inspection at each occupant change |
| Fee | $120 vacant unit; $200 if already occupied without a certificate; $65 each more unit at the same address | Fee not shown on the county page; call 314-615-4100 |
| Repairs after inspection | 30 days | 90 days, or the application lapses |
| Owner-occupied homes | Exempt | Not shown |

Missouri rules that apply in both:

- **No rent control.** RSMo 441.043 bars a county or city from regulating the amount of rent for private rental property.
- **No local deposit caps or voucher mandates.** Since 2025-08-28, RSMo 441.043 also bars local rules that limit security deposits or that make landlords accept housing assistance as a source of income.
- **Deposit cap of 2 months.** RSMo 535.300 caps a security deposit at 2 months' rent. You must return it, or send an itemized list, within 30 days after the tenancy ends.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Should I rent or sell my St. Louis home now?

Renting still pays a fair yield in St. Louis. Zillow's home value index (ZHVI) for the St. Louis metro is $273,164 for September 2026, up 3.3% in a year and a record.

| Metro | Typical rent (ZORI) | Typical value (ZHVI) | Gross yield |
|---|---|---|---|
| St. Louis | $1,427 | $273,164 | 6.27% |
| United States | $1,932 | $366,157 | 6.33% |

We did the math from the Zillow September 2026 files. A gross yield is before tax, insurance, repairs, vacancy and management.

Worked example for a City unit with 1 tenant change a year:

| Cost line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Rent, 12 months at $1,427 | $17,124 |
| Certificate of Inspection (vacant unit) | -$120 |
| 2 weeks of vacancy at turnover | -$658.62 |
| Rent left before other costs | $16,345.38 |

Assumptions: Zillow's typical rent, 1 turnover, a vacant-unit inspection, 2 weeks empty. Add your own tax, insurance, repairs and loan costs.

Questions to answer before you decide:

- [ ] Is the home in the City or the County? The permit steps differ.
- [ ] Will the home pass the inspection now, or do repairs come first?
- [ ] Does the rent cover all costs with a reserve for vacancy?
- [ ] Will you manage it, or pay a manager? Run the cost in the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

With rents and values both at records, you can sell into a strong market or keep a home that yields close to the US average. The choice depends on your loan and your plans, not on a market turn.

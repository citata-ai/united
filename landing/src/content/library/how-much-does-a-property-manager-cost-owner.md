## What is a typical monthly management fee?

A typical monthly management fee is about 8% to 10% of the rent the manager collects. iPropertyManagement's survey, updated 2026-09-23, found an average of 8.49% across 80 metro areas. The lowest advertised fee was 3.75%. The highest was 14.0%.

Some managers charge a flat dollar fee instead. In the same survey, flat-fee plans averaged $101.04 per unit per month, with a range of $49 to $250.

Home365's 2026 fee guide gives 8% to 12% for a single-family rental, with 10% as the most common rate. Note that Home365 sells management services, so it has a stake in the number.

What the monthly fee usually pays for:

- Rent collection and late notices
- Tenant calls and repair requests
- Vendor scheduling
- A monthly owner statement
- Lease enforcement

Ask one question first: is the fee on collected rent or on scheduled rent? On collected rent, you pay nothing in a month the tenant does not pay.

## What is a leasing fee or tenant placement fee?

A leasing fee, also called a tenant placement fee, pays the manager to fill a vacant home. It covers listing photos, ads, showings, screening and the lease signing.

iPropertyManagement's 2026 numbers:

| Placement fee type | Average | Range found |
|--------------------|---------|-------------|
| With full management, as % of one month's rent | 70.6% | 6% to 125% |
| Placement only, as % of one month's rent | 81.8% | 8% to 150% |

Home365 gives a typical range of 50% to 100% of the first month's rent. Some managers charge a flat amount. One example: Ziprent's Las Vegas page lists a $1,500 tenant placement fee, due at lease signing.

You pay this fee each time a new tenant moves in. A tenant who stays 3 years costs you one leasing fee. A tenant who leaves each year costs you three.

## What are renewal, setup and maintenance markup fees?

Renewal, setup and maintenance fees are the smaller line items. Together they can add hundreds of dollars a year.

| Fee | What it pays for | 2026 data |
|-----|------------------|-----------|
| Setup or onboarding | Adding your home, account and documents | Average $185.24, range $45 to $850 (iPropertyManagement) |
| Lease renewal | New lease or extension with the same tenant | Average $211.92 (iPropertyManagement); $100 to $500 flat or 25% to 50% of a month's rent (Home365) |
| Inspection | A walk-through visit and report | Average $106.72 (iPropertyManagement) |
| Maintenance markup | A percentage added to vendor invoices | Varies; ask for it in writing |
| Eviction coordination | Filing and court time | Varies; ask for it in writing |
| Late fee split | Share of late fees the tenant pays | Varies; some managers keep part or all |

Watch for: the maintenance line. A markup is not wrong. Some managers use it to pay for 24-hour repair coverage. But it should be on the fee schedule, with a percentage or a cap.

## What does the total cost look like for one house in a year?

For one house renting at $2,000 per month, a full year costs about $2,250 to $3,600 at average fees. The number depends most on whether a new tenant moves in that year.

Worked example with the iPropertyManagement averages:

| Line | Year 1, new tenant | Year 2, tenant renews |
|------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| Monthly fee (8.49% x $2,000 x 12) | $2,038 | $2,038 |
| Leasing fee (70.6% x $2,000) | $1,412 | $0 |
| Setup fee | $185 | $0 |
| Renewal fee | $0 | $212 |
| Total | $3,635 | $2,250 |
| As a share of yearly rent ($24,000) | 15.1% | 9.4% |

The example assumes 12 months of rent. A vacant month lowers the monthly fee on a collected-rent plan, but it also costs you a month of rent. The US rental vacancy rate was 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026 (Census Bureau). That is close to one month in 12.

Now try a flat-fee plan at $150 per month with a $1,500 placement fee. The same house would cost $3,300 in a year with a new tenant. Fee models change the total less than the headline rate suggests.

## How to read a fee schedule before you sign

A fee schedule is the one document that shows the full cost. A phone quote usually gives only the monthly rate. The written schedule shows the rest, and the management agreement makes it binding.

Ask each manager for the full written fee schedule, not a quote by phone. Then check these 6 items:

1. **Base.** Is the monthly fee on collected rent or scheduled rent?
2. **Leasing.** Percent or flat? Is there a refund or a free re-lease if the tenant leaves early?
3. **Renewal.** Is there a fee? How much?
4. **Markup.** Is there a markup on repairs? What is the cap?
5. **Approval limit.** Above what dollar amount must the manager ask you first?
6. **Exit.** What does it cost to end the agreement?

Put three schedules side by side and compute one full year for each. The cheapest monthly rate is not always the cheapest year.

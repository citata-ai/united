## Which owner updates should be routine, and which should follow events?

Routine updates should explain property results and outstanding decisions. Event updates should report a material change while the owner can still act. The exact schedule is a service choice to agree in writing.

Buildium's owner distribution guide pairs reconciled accounts with clear statements and explanations of holds. Property Meld's communication guide emphasizes transparency and timely owner decisions. Use those ideas to design a plan suited to your portfolio.

| Update | Suggested route | Information to include |
|---|---|---|
| Regular financial statement | Portal with notification | Income, costs, reserve and payout |
| Repair approval request | Agreed decision channel | Scope, estimate and action needed |
| Urgent property event | Direct contact and recorded follow-up | Confirmed facts and current response |
| Leasing progress | Agreed routine update | Stage, activity and next action |
| Renewal decision | Direct request with records | Terms, options and decision date |
| Service complaint | Named contact | Issue, review and next update |

The table is a suggested operating plan, not a legal reporting calendar. Check agreement and local requirements separately.

Do not send every system event as an owner alert. Ask what the owner needs to decide or understand. A flood of low-value notices can make the important message harder to find.

Keep the source records available so a short update does not replace the detailed account.

## Should I use email, a portal, text or a call?

Use the channel that fits the urgency, sensitivity and need for a durable record. Agree the primary channel and a backup with each owner.

A portal can hold statements and supporting files. Email can explain context and link to the approved record. A call may help when an event needs discussion or the owner has misunderstood the facts.

Text can alert an owner to a waiting decision, but do not assume a brief reply contains complete approval. Record the approved scope, amount and any conditions in the work system. Keep the agreement's requirements in view.

Avoid placing sensitive resident or bank details in an informal message. Use the approved secure route for files and account changes. Confirm contact changes through your established process.

Give every owner a named service contact and a backup. Explain how to report an urgent issue outside normal hours. Do not leave the owner to guess which employee inbox someone monitors.

After a call, record the decisions and unresolved questions. Send a checked follow-up when appropriate so both parties can see what happens next.

Review channel preferences when the owner adds an agent, partner or bookkeeper. Confirm who may receive information and who may approve a decision. Receiving a report does not necessarily grant spending authority.

## What response time should I promise owners?

Promise a response target your staffing and service hours can support. State what the target measures: receipt, meaningful reply, decision or final resolution.

A quick automatic receipt does not answer an account question. Label it clearly and tell the owner when a person will review the request. Do not count a receipt as a completed case in your internal report.

For a suggested service design, separate urgent reports from routine questions. Give each a staffed route and escalation path. Set the actual time commitments after measuring workload and checking coverage.

Do not adopt an unsupported industry response benchmark. Your promise needs to fit the agreement, local duties and vendor availability. Ask the team to test the promise during busy periods and absences.

Tell the owner what happens if the deadline changes. A short update that explains the missing fact is better than silence followed by a late answer. Give a next check-in that someone owns.

Measure response and resolution separately. Review requests that reopened after a reply because the answer did not solve the issue. This shows whether fast responses also provide useful service.

Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) when planning coverage costs. The communication service you promise must fit the staff time your fees support.

## How should I discuss bad news with an owner?

Explain bad news with confirmed facts, the current response and a clear decision request. Do not hide the problem inside a long general update.

Start with what happened and when you learned it. Distinguish observed facts from estimates and unanswered questions. Name the source of a repair estimate or account figure.

Then state what your team has done within its authority. Explain the options that remain and any timing issue. Avoid a promise that a vendor will finish before the vendor confirms it.

If your team made an error, say what the error was and what you are doing to correct it. Do not shift blame to software or a contractor without evidence. Keep the detailed review in the case record.

For a lower owner payout, show the calculation and the reasons for any hold. Buildium recommends documenting exceptions such as unpaid bills and requested reserves. Connect those records to the statement the owner sees.

Close the message with a decision or next update. If the owner must approve work, make the scope and estimated amount clear. If no action is needed, say who continues the work and when you will report again.

## How should I maintain the communication plan?

Keep the plan in the owner account and review it when contacts, authority or service scope change. A plan that only appears in a welcome email will become hard for staff to use.

Track missed updates, repeated questions and owner complaints about unclear information. Read a sample of real cases to learn whether staff follow the plan. Counts alone can miss an answer that arrived but did not help.

Use a short intake checklist: preferred channel, approved contacts, urgent route, statement access and approval authority. Confirm that the owner can open the portal and find the first statement.

Have local counsel check record, notice and approval duties. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Choose an owner who has recently asked the same question more than once. Review the messages and improve the part of the plan that failed to give a clear answer.

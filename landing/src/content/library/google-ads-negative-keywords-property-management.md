## Which tenant searches waste the most property management ad budget?

Renter searches waste the most budget, because "property management" is also a word renters use. A renter types "property management company near me" to find a vacant unit or to pay rent. Your ad shows, the renter clicks, and you pay for a visit that can never become an owner client.

Goodjuju Marketing's 2026 lead source report gives a reason to keep paid clicks clean. In its fall window, only 6 of 35 property management companies ran paid search. Paid search brought 5.3% of traffic but 1.2% of owner leads, at a 0.2% conversion rate. Goodjuju warns that this number reflects only 6 companies and misses separate landing pages and calls. Still, it shows how fast paid clicks can go to the wrong people.

The four groups of waste are:

1. **Renters looking for a home.** "apartments", "houses for rent", "rentals near me", "section 8 housing", "move in specials".
2. **Current tenants.** "pay rent", "tenant portal", "maintenance request", "resident login", plus your own company name with "login".
3. **Job seekers.** "jobs", "hiring", "careers", "salary", "property manager certification", "license course".
4. **Do-it-yourself landlords.** "free lease template", "property management software free", "how to be a landlord".

## How do I block apartment and job searches in Google Ads?

Build one shared negative keyword list and apply it to every owner campaign. Google Ads Help says you can add up to 5,000 negative keywords per list and create up to 20 lists. One list for all campaigns means you fix a waste term once.

Pick the match type with care. Google Ads Help explains three negative match types:

| Negative match type | Syntax | Blocks the ad when the search... | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Broad (default) | jobs | contains all the words, in any order | single words that never mean an owner: jobs, salary, apartments |
| Phrase | "for rent" | contains the words in the same order | phrases where one word alone is fine: "pay rent", "for rent" |
| Exact | [property management] | is exactly the words, no extra words | rare; a short search you want to stop alone |

Watch for one trap. Google Ads Help says negative keywords do not match close variants. A block on "apartment" does not block "apartments". Add singular, plural and common misspellings yourself.

Do not block the single word "rent". Owners search "rent out my house" and "how to rent my condo". Block renter phrases like "for rent" and "rentals near me" instead.

### Starter list (copy, then check against your search terms)

- **Renter terms (broad):** apartments, apartment, condos for rent, townhomes, studio, roommate, sublet, craigslist, zillow rentals, hotpads
- **Renter terms (phrase):** "for rent", "rentals near me", "homes for rent", "move in specials", "no credit check", "section 8 housing", "low income housing"
- **Tenant terms (phrase):** "pay rent", "tenant portal", "resident portal", "maintenance request", "rent payment"
- **Job terms (broad):** jobs, job, hiring, careers, career, salary, resume, internship, indeed
- **Training terms (phrase):** "property manager certification", "property management course", "property management license"
- **DIY and software terms (phrase):** "free lease template", "free property management software", "how to be a landlord"
- **Wrong service (broad):** hoa, condo association, commercial lease, storage unit (only if you do not offer these)

Remove any term that matches a service you sell. If you manage HOAs, do not block "hoa".

Fair Housing note: block searches by topic (renting, jobs), never by any group of people. Your ads target owners; your rental listings must stay open to all renters.

## How do I read the search terms report for wasted clicks?

The search terms report shows the real searches that triggered your ad. Google Ads Help separates two ideas: a keyword is what you bid on, and a search term is what a person typed. With broad match, the two can be far apart.

To open the report in Google Ads, select Campaigns, then Insights and reports, then Search terms. Then follow these steps:

1. Set the date range to the last 14 days.
2. Sort by cost, highest first.
3. Turn on the "Keyword" column. Google Ads Help says it does not show by default.
4. Read each of the top 50 terms. Ask: "Could this person own a rental?"
5. Mark each "no" term. Add it to the shared negative list with the narrowest match type that stops it.
6. Note which of your keywords pulled the waste. If one broad keyword pulls many renter terms, change it to phrase match.

Google Ads Help also says the report hides some low-volume searches for privacy. You will not see every wasted click. Search terms insights group those hidden terms into themes, so check that view too.

### Worked example

Say you spend $1,500 a month at $6 a click. That is 250 clicks. If the report shows 40% of cost on renter and job terms, you waste $600 a month. Blocking those terms leaves $600 for owner searches, or 100 more owner-intent clicks at the same price. These numbers are an example, not a benchmark. Use your own report.

## How often should I update the negative keyword list?

Update the list every week for the first 4 weeks of a new campaign. After that, every 2 weeks is enough for most small accounts. New waste terms appear when Google expands broad match, when you add keywords, and when rental season starts.

| When | Task | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 to 4 | Read the top 50 search terms by cost, add negatives | 20 minutes a week |
| Every 2 weeks after | Same check, last 14 days | 15 minutes |
| Each quarter | Read the full list, remove terms that now block owners | 30 minutes |
| After any keyword change | Check the search terms for 7 days | 10 minutes a day |

Google Ads Help warns that too many negative keywords can make your ads reach fewer customers. The quarterly check stops the list from growing into a wall that blocks owners too.

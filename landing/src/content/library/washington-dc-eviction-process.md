## Washington DC eviction rules at a glance

A DC eviction has 4 parts: a written notice, a court case, a judgment and a Marshals' visit. Each part has a deadline in the DC Code. We read each section on the official DC Code site (code.dccouncil.gov) on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice before filing for unpaid rent | At least 10 days before you file | D.C. Code 42-3505.01(a-1)(1) |
| Minimum unpaid rent for a possession case | $600 | 16-1501(b), 42-3505.01(a-1)(1) |
| Late fees as a reason to evict | Never allowed | 42-3505.01(a)(1) |
| Lease violation: time to correct | 30 days after notice | 42-3505.01(b) |
| Owner moves in (natural person) | 90-day notice to vacate | 42-3505.01(d) |
| Sale for the buyer's own use | 90-day notice to vacate | 42-3505.01(e) |
| Renovation that needs an empty unit | 120-day notice to vacate | 42-3505.01(f)(1)(D) |
| Demolition or end of housing use | 180-day notice to vacate | 42-3505.01(g), (i) |
| Serve the summons | At least 14 days before the first hearing | 16-1502(a) |
| Eviction date notice from you | At least 21 days before the eviction | 42-3505.01a(b)(1) |
| Tenant's property after the eviction | Stays in the unit 7 days | 42-3505.01a(d)(2) |

## What notice must a DC landlord give before filing for unpaid rent?

A DC landlord must give a written notice of past-due rent at least 10 days before filing (D.C. Code 42-3505.01(a-1)(1)). You may not send the notice at all if the tenant owes less than $600.

The notice must say how much rent the tenant owes, with a ledger of charges and payments attached. Section (a-1)(2) gives the required text. It also names the Office of the Tenant Advocate (202-719-6560) and the Landlord Tenant Legal Assistance Network (202-780-2575).

Send the notice 2 ways (42-3505.01(a-1)(3)):

1. **Tracked mail.** Certified mail or a delivery service with tracking, return receipt requested.
2. **Hand delivery or posting.** Give it at the unit, or post it on the front door.

Watch for: the DC Code text we read on 2026-10-19 says 10 days. The 2022 Office of the Tenant Advocate class said 30 days, so the rule changed. But the model text in (a-1)(2) still tells the tenant they have 30 days to pay. Ask a DC attorney to check your form before you use it.

If you post a notice, the court needs a photo with a readable timestamp (42-3505.01(a)(2)). If you know the tenant speaks a language other than English or Spanish that D.C. Code 2-1933 covers, the notice must be in that language (42-3505.01(a)(3)). Notices for any reason other than unpaid rent go to both the tenant and the Rent Administrator (42-3505.01(a)(1)).

## Can a DC landlord evict for a lease violation or with no cause?

A DC landlord cannot evict without cause. Under 42-3505.01(a)(1), a tenant who pays the rent cannot be evicted just because the lease expired. You need one of the reasons in the section.

The main reasons and their notice periods:

- **Lease violation.** The tenant has 30 days after your notice to correct it (42-3505.01(b)).
- **Illegal act in the unit.** A court must first find the act. Then you serve a 30-day notice to vacate (42-3505.01(c)(1)). For a dangerous crime or crime of violence, cases filed after December 31, 2025 use a 10-day notice and a hearing within 20 days (42-3505.01(c)(2)).
- **Personal use.** A natural person owner who wants to live in the unit serves a 90-day notice (42-3505.01(d)). You cannot rent the unit out for 12 months after you get it back.
- **Sale for a buyer's own use.** A 90-day notice, after you tell the tenant of the right to buy under Chapter 34 (42-3505.01(e)).
- **Renovation, demolition, rehab or end of housing use.** 120 or 180 days, with extra filings and relocation help (42-3505.01(f) to (i)).

A tenant who is a victim of an intrafamily offense may have a defense to a case under (b) or (c) (42-3505.01(c-1)).

## Where does a DC landlord file, and what must the filing show?

You file a complaint for possession in the Landlord and Tenant Branch of DC Superior Court (D.C. Code 16-1501(a)). The court issues the summons in English and Spanish.

At filing, D.C. Code 16-1501(c)(1) asks for 2 things:

- [ ] A valid rental registration or claim of exemption under D.C. Code 42-3502.05.
- [ ] A current rental housing business license under 47-2828(c)(1).

The court may waive the license rule for good reasons, such as an agency delay or a tenant who blocks the inspection (16-1501(c)(2)). The court may dismiss a case that does not meet DC law, or let you fix it (16-1501(d)).

Serve the summons at least 14 days before the first hearing (16-1502(a)). If you post it, mail a copy within 3 calendar days and give the court a timestamped photo (16-1502(b)).

We did not include court filing fees. The DC Courts site blocked our reader on 2026-10-19, and we use fees only from the court's own page. Check the Landlord and Tenant Branch for the current amounts.

## How long does an eviction take in Washington DC?

A DC eviction takes at least several weeks, and often months. The statutes set minimum gaps, not a fixed total. Here is a worked timeline with the shortest legal gaps for a rent case:

| Step | Earliest day | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Send the past-due rent notice | Day 0 | 42-3505.01(a-1) |
| File the complaint | Day 10 | 42-3505.01(a-1)(1) |
| Serve the summons | Day 10 | 16-1502(a) |
| First hearing | Day 24 (14 days after service) | 16-1502(a) |
| Judgment, then writ issued | Depends on the case | Court |
| Your eviction-date notice | 21+ days before eviction | 42-3505.01a(b)(1) |
| Marshals evict | Within 75 days of the writ | USMS |

So the legal minimum before a first hearing is about 24 days. A trial, a payment plan or a pending rental help application adds time. Under 42-3505.01(r), the court may stay a case once while an ERAP (Emergency Rental Assistance Program) application is pending.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in DC?

The U.S. Marshals Service removes the tenant. The USMS says this duty is unique to DC; in other places a sheriff does it. Writs live 75 calendar days, and you must pick a date within 67 days of issue (USMS).

Your duties on the way to eviction day:

1. **Answer the phone.** The Marshals make 3 attempts to schedule. After the third failed attempt, the writ goes back to court and is canceled.
2. **Send your own notice.** Deliver an eviction-date notice at least 21 days before, by text or email, first-class mail and a posting (42-3505.01a(b)(1)). The USMS notice does not count.
3. **Show up with access.** Have a working key or a locksmith who can get in within 10 minutes (USMS).
4. **Change the locks with the Marshals there.** You pay for it (42-3505.01a(c)(1)).
5. **Store the tenant's property.** Leave it in the unit 7 days, Sundays and federal holidays excluded. Give access for at least 16 hours, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m., over no more than 2 days (42-3505.01a(d)).

The Marshals do not evict when rain or snow falls, or when the forecast is below 32 degrees F. D.C. Code 42-3505.01(k) also bans evictions when the 8 a.m. forecast is above 95 degrees F. The tenant can stop a rent eviction by paying the full amount on the writ before the eviction ends (OTA, 2022).

## Is a self-help eviction legal in DC?

A self-help eviction is not legal in DC. The Office of the Tenant Advocate says a landlord "CANNOT physically take back possession of the rental unit on their own" and must wait for the Marshals (OTA, 2022). D.C. Code 42-3505.01(a)(1) bars any eviction without a proper written notice. Section 42-3505.01a(a) bars removing a tenant's property except as that section allows.

So do not change locks, remove doors or cut utilities to force a tenant out. Use the notice, the court and the Marshals.

## What do I do in the first hour?

Stop the water first. Every other step can wait a few minutes; the water cannot. Then make the unit safe and start a record.

The first-hour step list:

1. **Tell the tenant to shut the fixture valve.** Toilets, sinks and washers have a small valve on the supply line. Turn it clockwise.
2. **If water still runs, shut the main.** The main shut-off is often where the water line enters the building. Also look near the water heater or in a utility room.
3. **Cut power to wet areas.** If water reaches outlets, fixtures or the panel, turn off the breaker for that area. Do not stand in water to do it.
4. **Call your plumber.** Give the address, the source if known, and whether the water is off.
5. **Check the unit below or next door.** Water travels. Tell those tenants too.
6. **Take photos and video.** Record the source, the ceiling, walls, floors and any wet belongings.
7. **Move belongings out of the water.** Ask the tenant to move their things. You move fixtures and building items.

Put your answers in a short table before the next leak. Label the main shut-off in each unit and share its location in the move-in packet.

## Can I enter the unit without notice?

Yes, in most states an active leak is an emergency, and emergencies allow entry without the usual notice. Three examples:

- **California.** Civil Code 1954(a) lets a landlord enter "in case of emergency". The normal-hours rule does not apply in an emergency.
- **Florida.** Fla. Stat. 83.53(2) lets a landlord enter "at any time for the protection or preservation of the premises" and in case of emergency.
- **Arizona.** A.R.S. 33-1343(C) lets a landlord enter without consent in an emergency.

Knock and announce first. After the entry, send the tenant a written note: the time you entered, what you found and what you did.

## Do I call insurance first or a plumber?

Call the plumber first, then the insurer the same day. A leak that runs while you wait on hold makes the claim bigger. Read your policy for any duty to prevent more damage and for the report deadline.

Before you call the insurer, collect:

- [ ] Date and time the leak started or was found.
- [ ] The source, for example a burst supply line under the sink.
- [ ] Photos and video from before the cleanup started.
- [ ] The plumber's invoice and notes on the cause.
- [ ] A list of damaged building items: drywall, flooring, cabinets, ceiling.
- [ ] Contact details for any other unit owner or tenant affected.

Keep damaged materials until the adjuster has seen them or approved removal, if you can do so without causing mold. Photos with a tape measure help when you cannot.

## Who pays for tenant belongings?

The tenant's renters insurance usually pays for the tenant's belongings, not your policy. The Insurance Information Institute says it directly: the landlord "may have insurance to protect the building", but "your landlord's policy won't replace your personal possessions". Standard renters insurance covers belongings against water and other listed perils. It does not cover floods.

The split, in short:

| Loss | Usually paid by | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Building: drywall, floors, cabinets, pipes | Landlord's property policy | Subject to your deductible and policy terms |
| Tenant's furniture, clothes, electronics | Tenant's renters policy | Floods are not covered by standard renters policies |
| Tenant's hotel while the unit is repaired | Tenant's renters policy (additional living expenses) | Check the lease for any rent abatement terms |
| Damage the tenant or a guest caused to others | Tenant's liability coverage | Renters policies include liability |

Who caused the leak can change who pays. Ask your insurer and, if the amount is large, a local attorney. A lease clause that requires renters insurance helps every party.

## Do I have to fix the leak, and how fast?

Yes. Most states require the landlord to keep plumbing in working order. How fast depends on the state.

- **California.** Civil Code 1941.1 lists plumbing "maintained in good working order" and effective waterproofing as basic standards of a habitable home.
- **Florida.** Fla. Stat. 83.51(1) requires the plumbing to be kept "in reasonable working condition" where no local code applies.
- **Texas.** Property Code 92.056 presumes 7 days is a reasonable time to repair a condition that affects health or safety after notice. A burst pipe often needs much faster action.

## How do I prevent mold after a leak?

Dry everything fast. The EPA says to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold. In most cases, mold will not grow if materials are dry in that time.

Drying checklist:

- [ ] Extract standing water the same day.
- [ ] Run fans and dehumidifiers. Keep them on until materials are dry.
- [ ] Pull wet carpet pad. It rarely dries well in place.
- [ ] Open the wall or ceiling if water got behind it.
- [ ] Remove wet insulation.
- [ ] Check moisture with a meter before you close walls.
- [ ] Do not paint or caulk over mold. The EPA says to clean and dry first.

Size the job before you pick who does it. The EPA says you can often clean mold under about 10 square feet (roughly 3 ft by 3 ft) yourself. If there was a lot of water damage, or mold covers more than 10 square feet, the EPA points to its guide for larger cleanups. A water damage company with drying equipment is the safe choice at that size.

Check the unit again 1 week later. Look for stains, a musty smell or bubbling paint.

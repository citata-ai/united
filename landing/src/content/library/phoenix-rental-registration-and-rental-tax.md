## Why must Arizona owners register rentals with the county assessor?

Arizona owners must register rentals because ARS 33-1902 says a rental "shall not be occupied" unless the owner's details are on file with the county assessor. The law gives tenants and cities a way to find the person responsible for a property. In Phoenix, you file with the Maricopa County Assessor, online through its Customer Portal or on a paper form.

What you must keep on file (ARS 33-1902(A)):

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Owner | Name, address and phone |
| Entity contact | For an LLC, the managing member. For a corporation, an officer. For a trust, a trustee. For a partnership, a general partner. |
| Property | Street address and parcel number |
| Building | The year it was built |
| Out-of-state owner | A statutory agent who lives in Arizona and accepts legal service |

You must update the data within 10 days of any change. The assessor may charge up to $10 for each first registration and each change (ARS 33-1902(I)). The Maricopa County Assessor notes that rental registration is a separate step from the Affidavit of Property Value you file at purchase.

What happens if you skip it:

- **The tenant can leave.** A tenant may send a written 10-day notice. If you do not register within 10 days, the tenant may end the lease, and you must return all prepaid rent within 10 days.
- **A newly bought rental draws a fixed fine.** The city or town assesses $1,000, plus $100 for each month until you comply.
- **Other rentals can draw a daily fine.** The city or town may impose $150 a day after the latest notice of assessed valuation.
- **There is a grace period.** If you comply within 10 days after the notice or complaint, the court dismisses it with no penalty.

Worked example: you buy a rental, a tenant moves in, and you never register. The city finds it 6 months later. Under 33-1902(E), the penalty is $1,000 + 6 × $100 = $1,600, unless you comply within 10 days of the complaint. Registering costs no more than $10.

## Did Phoenix stop the residential rental tax in 2025?

Yes. Starting 2025-01-01, owners should no longer collect or pay city transaction privilege tax (TPT) on residential rentals of 30 days or more (Arizona Department of Revenue). The change comes from Laws 2023, Chapter 204, which amended ARS 42-6004(H). The amended law bars any city, town or other taxing body from taxing the business of renting real property for residential use.

What ADOR says owners should know:

1. **No new returns.** For periods from 2025-01-01 on, you do not collect, file or pay residential rental TPT.
2. **No step to cancel.** If business code 045 was your only activity, ADOR may already have cancelled your license, effective 2024-12-31.
3. **Old periods still count.** Cancelling the license does not clear tax owed for periods before 2025. Owners who under-reported must file amended returns.
4. **Short stays still pay.** Hotels, motels and other lodging booked for fewer than 30 days still collect TPT as transient lodging.
5. **Registration still applies.** ADOR reminds owners that county assessor registration under the landlord-tenant law is still required.

What this means for a budget: the tax line on a long-term lease is now $0. For the past rate in your city before 2025, check your own old TPT returns. We did not find a current official page with the old city rates, so we do not quote them.

This is general information, not tax advice. Ask a tax professional about your past filings.

## Can Arizona cities cap rents?

No. ARS 33-1329 states that rent control on private homes is a matter of statewide concern and is preempted by the state. Cities and towns, including charter cities, "shall not have the power to control rents". The ban does not apply to housing owned, financed, insured or subsidized by a state agency, city or town.

So in Phoenix, the rent is what the lease says. The market sets it. Here is where the market stands on HUD's numbers: the FY2027 fair market rent for a Phoenix-area 2-bedroom is $1,734, down 5.7% from $1,839. That was one of the 4 biggest drops among the 50 largest metro areas. See [Phoenix fair market rent 2027](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/phoenix-fair-market-rent-2027) for each size.

## What does the Arizona Residential Landlord and Tenant Act require on deposits?

The Arizona Residential Landlord and Tenant Act caps the security deposit at 1.5 months' rent (ARS 33-1321(A)). The cap counts prepaid rent too. A tenant may still choose to pay more rent in advance.

The deposit rules, step by step:

1. **State the purpose of any nonrefundable fee in writing.** A fee or deposit not labeled nonrefundable is refundable (33-1321(B)).
2. **At move-in, give 3 items:** a signed copy of the lease, a move-in form to list existing damage, and written notice that the tenant may attend the move-out inspection (33-1321(C)).
3. **After move-out, send the itemized list.** Within 14 days, not counting weekends and holidays, after the tenancy ends, possession returns and the tenant asks (33-1321(D)).
4. **Mail it first class** to the tenant's last known address, with any refund, unless the tenant agreed in writing to another method.

The penalty for getting it wrong: the tenant can recover the amount due plus damages of 2 times the amount wrongfully withheld (33-1321(E)).

Worked example at the FY2027 Phoenix 2-bedroom FMR:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Monthly rent | $1,734 |
| Maximum deposit, 1.5 months | $2,601 |
| If you wrongly keep $600 | You owe $600 plus $1,200 in damages |

Two more lease rules from ARS 33-1414:

- **Late fees** need at least 5 days of grace after the due date before a penalty applies.
- **Guest fees** are banned for a guest who stays no more than 14 days in a calendar month.

Checklist for a Phoenix rental owner:

- [ ] Register the parcel with the Maricopa County Assessor before the tenant moves in.
- [ ] Name an Arizona statutory agent if you live out of state.
- [ ] Update the registration within 10 days of any change.
- [ ] Stop charging city rental tax on leases of 30 days or more.
- [ ] Keep deposits at or under 1.5 months' rent.
- [ ] Give the move-in form and inspection notice at move-in.

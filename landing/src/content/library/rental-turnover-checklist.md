## What happens on day 1 after move-out?

Day 1 after move-out is for keys, the inspection and the deposit clock. The order matters, because state deposit deadlines start when the tenant leaves or surrenders the unit.

Day 1 checklist:

- [ ] Collect all keys, fobs, garage remotes and mailbox keys. Count them against the move-in list.
- [ ] Write down the date and time you got possession back.
- [ ] Get the tenant's forwarding address in writing.
- [ ] Walk the unit with the move-in report in hand. Take dated photos and video of each room.
- [ ] Note damage beyond normal wear, with a photo for each item.
- [ ] Read the utility meters. Move the utilities into the owner's name so the turn crews have power and water.
- [ ] Change or rekey the locks.
- [ ] Put the deposit deadline in your calendar.

The deposit deadline is the hard date in every turn. Here are 4 state examples.

| State | Deadline to return the deposit or send the itemized list | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| New York | 14 days after the tenant vacates; miss it and you forfeit the right to keep any part | GOL 7-108(1-a)(e) |
| Arizona | 14 days, not counting weekends and legal holidays, after the tenancy ends, possession is delivered and the tenant asks | A.R.S. 33-1321(D) |
| Florida | 15 days if you make no claim; 30 days to send a notice of claim by certified mail | Fla. Stat. 83.49(3)(a) |
| Texas | On or before the 30th day after the tenant surrenders the premises | Tex. Prop. Code 92.103(a) |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Watch for: in New York, the itemized statement must go out within the 14 days. A late statement costs you the whole claim. Write the statement from the day 1 photos so you do not wait on repair invoices.

## Which trades go first?

The trade order in a rental turnover goes from dirty work to clean work. Each step should not damage the step before it.

| Step | Trade or task | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trash-out and removal of left-behind items (after your state's abandoned-property rules) | Crews need clear rooms |
| 2 | Repairs: drywall, doors, plumbing, electrical, appliances | Makes dust and debris |
| 3 | Pest treatment, if needed | Needs an empty unit |
| 4 | Paint and caulk | Needs repaired walls |
| 5 | Flooring: repair, replace or deep clean carpet | Paint drips stay off new floors |
| 6 | Final clean: kitchen, baths, windows, vents | Removes dust from all trades |
| 7 | Final check: smoke and CO alarms, locks, lights, every tap and appliance | Confirms the unit is ready |

Lead paint: if the home was built before 1978, plan the paint step with care. EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) Rule applies to rentals. Anyone paid to do work that disturbs painted surfaces in a pre-1978 home must be certified in lead-safe work practices. EPA says property managers who do or offer that work must become lead-safe certified firms.

Book trades before move-out when you can. A vendor list with confirmed prices for paint, cleaning and carpet saves days in a busy month.

## When do I re-list?

Re-list as soon as you know the ready date. Every vacant day costs rent you cannot get back.

Ways to start leasing earlier:

1. **Pre-list.** If the lease allows showings before move-out, list before the tenant leaves. Give the current tenant the entry notice your state requires for each showing.
2. **Set the ready date from the trade plan.** Add 1 or 2 buffer days for delays.
3. **Take new photos after the final clean.** Keep old photos only if the unit looks the same.
4. **Check the rent.** Compare to current local listings before you post the price. For voucher units, check the local payment standard; our guide on [Section 8 rent limits](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) shows how fair market rents are set.
5. **Open applications** with the same written screening criteria for every applicant.

Worked example of vacancy cost. Example numbers, not averages:

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Monthly rent | $1,500 |
| Daily rent (× 12 ÷ 365) | $49.32 |
| Days from move-out to new lease | 21 |
| Lost rent | 21 × $49.32 = $1,035.72 |
| Lost rent if you cut the turn to 14 days | 14 × $49.32 = $690.48 |

Cutting 7 days saves $345.24 in this example. That is often more than the cost of a rush fee for a cleaner.

## How do I track the turn?

Track the turn on one board, with one owner for each task and a due date for each step. Use your property management software, a shared sheet or a project tool. The tool matters less than the habit of updating it every day.

Columns for a turn tracker:

| Column | Example |
|---|---|
| Unit | 12B |
| Move-out date | 2026-10-31 |
| Deposit deadline | 2026-11-14 (New York, 14 days) |
| Task | Paint, 2 bedrooms |
| Vendor | Name and phone |
| Quote and actual cost | $900 / $940 |
| Status | Booked, in progress, done, checked |
| Photo of finished work | Link |
| Ready date | 2026-11-12 |
| Lease start | 2026-11-15 |

Two numbers tell you how well your turns run:

- **Days vacant:** move-out date to new lease start.
- **Turn cost:** total spent, minus what you kept from the deposit.

Review both after every turn. A pattern, such as carpet that fails every time, tells you where to upgrade. If you would rather hand off turns, see what a full-service manager charges with our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

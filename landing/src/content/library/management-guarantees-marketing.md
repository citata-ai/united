An owner who hires a manager takes a risk. They hand over a home worth hundreds of thousands of dollars to a company they met last week. A guarantee is a written answer to the question in their head: "What if this goes wrong?"

Our competitor research found the same thing. All 3 leading agencies for property managers put a full owner fee table on client sites. They add owner protections too, such as guarantees and cancel terms (our data, 2026-10). So guarantees are now normal. The question is which ones to offer and how to back them.

## Which guarantees do PM companies offer?

Property management companies offer guarantees on cancel terms, leasing speed, tenant stay, pet damage, eviction cost and payout timing. Two real examples, read on 2026-10-19:

| Guarantee | Realty Masters of Florida (Pensacola) | PMI Hudson |
|---|---|---|
| Cancel | Cancel at any time with no penalties | Cancel without penalty with 30 days' notice; invoices paid |
| Leasing time | Not leased in 30 days: one month of management free | Quality tenant in less than 21 days, or first month's fee waived; home must be vacant, ready and allow up to 2 approved pets |
| Tenant stay | Tenant leaves before 9 months: new tenant found free | — |
| Pet damage | Up to $1,000 above the security deposit, for approved pets | — |
| Eviction | — | Eviction costs up to $1,500 under an Eviction Protection Plan; no lost rent or damage |
| Payout timing | — | Owner paid within 10 business days when rent is paid by the 1st, or that month's fee back |
| Results | No leasing fee until a qualified tenant | Not paid until the owner is paid |

Sources: Realty Masters of Florida guarantees page; PMI Hudson residential guarantees page.

Note the conditions on the PMI Hudson leasing guarantee. The home must be vacant and ready, allow up to 2 approved pets, and rent at a price both sides agree on. Those conditions make the guarantee affordable to back.

## What does each guarantee cost to back?

Each guarantee costs the expected number of claims times the cost of one claim. Work it out before you publish. Use your own history for the rates.

| Guarantee | Cost of one claim | How to estimate the rate |
|---|---|---|
| Cancel anytime | Lost future fees from that owner | Your past cancellations per year |
| Lease in 30 days | One month's management fee | Share of your listings that took over 30 days |
| Tenant stays 9 months | Your leasing fee | Share of tenants who left before month 9 |
| Pet damage up to $1,000 | Damage above the deposit, up to the cap | Pet claims per year from your move-out files |
| Eviction up to $1,500 | Legal costs up to the cap | Evictions per 100 leases |
| Payout by day 10 | One month's fee | Months you missed the date |

Worked example for a tenant-stay guarantee. Say you place 120 tenants a year and your leasing fee is $1,000. If 4% left before month 9 last year, you would replace about 5 tenants: 120 × 4% = 4.8. The guarantee costs about $4,800 a year. The 4% is an example rate; use your own.

Some managers fund a guarantee from a fee. PMI Milestone says approved tenants pay a monthly pet administrative fee. It uses those fees to run the program and to pay for approved-pet damage up to $1,000. It does not cover pets a tenant hid (PMI Milestone help center). Check your state's rules on tenant fees before you copy that model.

## Do owners trust guarantees?

Owners trust a guarantee when it is specific and when the manager pays without a fight. No public survey measures owner trust in PM guarantees, so judge by your own sales calls. Signs that a guarantee helps:

- Owners mention it on the call without your prompt.
- Fewer owners ask for a long trial or a short contract.
- Your close rate rises after you publish it.

A vague guarantee does the opposite. "100% satisfaction guaranteed" with no remedy reads as a slogan. "Cancel any time with 30 days' notice and no fee" reads as a promise.

## What do the FTC rules say?

The FTC's Guides for the Advertising of Warranties and Guarantees (16 CFR Part 239) set the base rules for any guarantee you advertise:

1. **Satisfaction or money-back guarantees.** Use those words only if you refund the full price on request. Disclose any material limits or conditions clearly (16 CFR 239.3).
2. **Performance.** Advertise a guarantee only if you promptly and fully perform it (16 CFR 239.5).
3. **"Lifetime" claims.** If you say "lifetime", say whose life or what period it means (16 CFR 239.4).

State real estate and consumer laws may add rules. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I word guarantees on the website?

Word each guarantee with four parts: the promise, the number, the condition and the remedy. Then link to the full terms in your management agreement.

Template:

```text
[Name] Guarantee
We promise [what] within [number] [days/months].
This applies when [main conditions].
If we miss it, we will [remedy].
Full terms: Section [x] of our management agreement.
```

Examples:

- **Leasing Guarantee.** We find a qualified tenant within 30 days of the home being rent-ready. This applies when the rent is set at our market analysis price. If we miss it, your next month of management is free.
- **Cancel Guarantee.** Cancel any time with 30 days' written notice and no cancellation fee. Open vendor invoices are paid from the account before transfer.

Put the guarantees on your owner page and your fee page, next to the fee table. Owners compare both at once. Check the fee side with the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## Guarantee checklist

- Each guarantee priced from your own claim history.
- Every condition written in plain words, next to the promise.
- Full terms in the management agreement.
- One person owns paying claims within a set number of days.
- Claims tracked each quarter; terms reviewed each year.

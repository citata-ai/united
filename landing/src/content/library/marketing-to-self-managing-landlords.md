## How big is the self-managing landlord market?

The self-managing landlord market is most of the small rental stock. HUD and the Census Bureau released the 2021 Rental Housing Finance Survey in 2022. Multifamily Executive reported from that release that only about 22% of properties with 1 to 4 units are managed professionally. For properties with 150 or more units, the share is 84%.

The owners are mostly people, not companies. A Congressional Research Service report on the same survey found that individual investors owned 70.2% of US rental properties. BatchData says owners of 1 to 5 homes hold 92.14% of investor-owned single-family homes (Q2 2026).

| Fact | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Small rentals (1–4 units) with professional management | About 22% | 2021 RHFS, via Multifamily Executive |
| Large rentals (150+ units) with professional management | 84% | 2021 RHFS, via Multifamily Executive |
| Rental properties owned by individual investors | 70.2% | CRS report R47332 |
| Investor-owned homes held by owners of 1–5 homes | 92.14% | BatchData, Q2 2026 |

So about 3 in 4 small rentals in your area have no manager. You compete less with other managers here. You compete with the owner's own time.

## Why do self-managing landlords resist a manager?

Self-managing landlords resist a manager for 4 common reasons. Each one has an honest answer.

| Objection | What the owner thinks | Your honest answer |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | "The fee eats my profit." | Show the full fee math next to vacancy and repair costs |
| Control | "I will not know what happens." | Show the owner portal, the approval limit and a sample statement |
| Trust | "My friend's manager was bad." | Give a short cancel term and real reviews |
| Ease | "Two homes are easy." | List the tasks and the hours per year, from your own data |

Do not argue with the cost objection. Do the math with them. Our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) shows the fee in dollars for a given rent. Some owners will see that self-management is the better deal for them. Say so. That honesty is what makes the next owner trust you.

## Which pain points make them switch?

Self-managing landlords switch when the work or the risk grows faster than the rent. Most switch after one event, not after slow thinking.

The common trigger events:

- **A long vacancy.** The home sits empty and the owner pays the mortgage alone.
- **A tenant who stops paying.** The owner faces a notice, a court date and lost rent.
- **A big repair.** A roof, a sewer line or mold, with no trusted vendor list.
- **A move.** A job or military move puts the owner far from the home.
- **A life change.** A new baby, an illness or retirement leaves less time.
- **A new rule.** A rental registration, inspection or notice rule they do not know how to follow.
- **A second or third purchase.** One home was easy. Three homes are a part-time job.

Write one page or post for each trigger. Use the words owners type: "tenant not paying rent what to do", "renting my house while I move". Then offer help at the end of the page, not at the top.

## What offer lowers the risk of trying a manager?

An offer that limits the owner's loss lowers the risk of trying a manager. The owner fears a long contract with a bad company. So make the first step small and easy to undo.

| Offer | How it works | Your cost | Owner's risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leasing only | You find and screen the tenant for a one-time fee; the owner manages after | Low | One fee |
| 30-day cancel | The owner can end the agreement with 30 days' notice and no fee | Some churn | Very low |
| First month free | No management fee for month one | One month's fee | Very low |
| Fee table with no extras | Every fee in writing, before the call | None | Lower surprise |
| Written rent estimate | Rent estimate with local comps, free | Staff time | None |

Leasing only is the best first step for many DIY owners. They see your screening, your listing photos and your speed. Some come back for full management at the next vacancy.

Write every offer into the management agreement in plain words. Check your state's rules for agreement terms and trust accounts. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Which channels reach DIY landlords?

Google search reaches DIY landlords when they have a problem, and local groups reach them before they do. Social media rarely brings owner leads.

The Goodjuju 2026 lead source study (fall update, 35 PM companies, 2,525 owner leads over two 90-day windows) shows the split:

| Channel | Share of owner leads, fall 2026 |
|---|---|
| Google Search and Map Pack | 50.6% |
| Direct and Maps | 35.6% |
| Other search | 4.3% |
| Referral | 3.7% |
| AI tools (ChatGPT, Gemini and others) | 3.4% |
| Paid search | 1.2% (only 6 of 35 companies ran ads) |
| Social media | 0.8% |

Use the channels in this order:

1. **Search pages for each trigger.** One page per pain point, linked from your owner services page.
2. **Google Business Profile.** Add "leasing only" and "tenant placement" as services.
3. **REIA groups.** National REIA says it has over 120 local chapters and associations. Many DIY landlords go to learn.
4. **Agent referrals.** Agents meet owners who rent instead of sell.
5. **Your own owners.** Ask happy owners to name one friend who self-manages.

AI answers are a small but growing source. To see how owners ask ChatGPT and Gemini for a manager, read [how owners choose a property manager with AI](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai).

## What should you do this month?

Pick 2 trigger events that are common in your area. Write one page for each. Add a leasing-only offer with a clear price. Then track how many DIY owners sign in 90 days, and which page or channel brought them.

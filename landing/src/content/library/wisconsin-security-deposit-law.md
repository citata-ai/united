## Wisconsin security deposit rules at a glance

Wisconsin splits its deposit rules between Wis. Stat. 704.28 and the consumer rule Wis. Admin. Code ATCP 134.06. We read both from the Wisconsin Legislature's official PDFs on 2026-10-19. The live site did not load from our network, so we read the Internet Archive copies of the official files. The statute PDF says it is current through May 22, 2026.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | No cap | Ch. 704, ATCP 134 |
| Check-in sheet | Give at move-in; tenant has 7 days to return it | Wis. Stat. 704.08 |
| Notice of inspection rights | Before you take the deposit; deadline at least 7 days after move-in | ATCP 134.06(1)(a) |
| Prior tenant's damage list | On request, within 30 days or 7 days after the prior tenant's notice, whichever is later | ATCP 134.06(1)(b) |
| Return deadline | 21 days | Wis. Stat. 704.28(4), ATCP 134.06(2) |
| Allowed deductions | Damage, unpaid rent, some utilities, permit fees, nonstandard items | Wis. Stat. 704.28(1) |
| Normal wear and tear | Never deductible | Wis. Stat. 704.28(3) |
| Statement of amounts withheld | Required, with each item and amount | ATCP 134.06(4) |
| Prepaid rent over one month | Treat as a deposit | ATCP 134.06 note |
| Remedy for a loss | Twice the loss, plus costs and attorney fees | Wis. Stat. 100.20(5), ATCP 134 note |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Wisconsin?

Wisconsin sets no maximum security deposit. Neither chapter 704 of the statutes nor chapter ATCP 134 names a dollar or month limit.

Watch prepaid rent. A note to ATCP 134.06 says a rent payment above one month's prepaid rent counts as a security deposit. You may collect more than one month in advance. But if you still hold any excess when the tenant leaves, you must treat it as a deposit and return it under the 21-day rule.

Before you accept a deposit, ATCP 134.06(1)(a) requires a written notice. It tells the tenant that, by a deadline at least 7 days after the tenancy starts, the tenant may:

1. Inspect the unit and report damage or defects that were already there.
2. Ask for a list of damage charged to the prior tenant's deposit.

If the tenant asks, give the list within 30 days of the request. Or give it within 7 days after you notify the prior tenant of deductions, whichever is later (ATCP 134.06(1)(b)). Separately, Wis. Stat. 704.08 requires a check-in sheet for each new tenant, with 7 days to fill it in and return it.

## How many days does a Wisconsin landlord have to return a security deposit?

A Wisconsin landlord has 21 days. Wis. Stat. 704.28(4) and ATCP 134.06(2) start the clock on one of three dates:

| Situation | The 21 days start on |
|---|---|
| Tenant leaves on the lease end date | The date the rental agreement ends |
| Tenant leaves or is evicted before the end date | The date the agreement ends, or the date a new tenant's tenancy starts if you rerent sooner |
| Tenant leaves or is evicted after the end date | The date you learn the tenant has left or been removed by the sheriff under Wis. Stat. 799.45(2) |

**Worked example.** A 12-month lease ends on May 31. The tenant leaves the same day. The 21 days start on May 31, so the deposit and statement are due by June 21.

You must "deliver or mail" the deposit. A note to ATCP 134.06 says delivery may be by an agent, such as a private courier. If the postal service cannot deliver to the address, you are not in violation for that reason alone, as long as you followed the rest of the rule (ATCP 134.06(5)).

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Wisconsin?

A Wisconsin landlord may withhold only amounts reasonably needed to pay six kinds of costs. Wis. Stat. 704.28(1) and ATCP 134.06(3)(a) list them:

1. **Tenant damage.** Damage, waste or neglect of the premises by the tenant.
2. **Unpaid rent.** Rent the tenant is legally responsible for, subject to the landlord's duty to mitigate in Wis. Stat. 704.29.
3. **Landlord-supplied utilities.** Utility charges owed under the lease and not included in rent.
4. **Government utility bills.** Direct service from a government-owned utility, to the extent you become liable for the tenant's nonpayment.
5. **Municipal permit fees.** Unpaid monthly fees under Wis. Stat. 66.0435(3), to the extent you become liable.
6. **Nonstandard items.** Any other reason listed in a separate "NONSTANDARD RENTAL PROVISIONS" document.

The nonstandard document has strict rules (Wis. Stat. 704.28(2)). It must be separate and carry that exact title. You must identify each provision with the tenant before the lease is signed. If the tenant signs or initials next to a provision, the law presumes you did so and the tenant agreed.

You may never deduct for normal wear and tear, or for loss the tenant cannot reasonably be held responsible for (Wis. Stat. 704.28(3)). A note to ATCP 134.06 applies this to routine painting and carpet cleaning with no unusual damage. Even if the lease says the tenant pays for routine carpet cleaning, you may not take that cost from the deposit.

## Does a Wisconsin landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. If you withhold any part of the deposit, ATCP 134.06(4)(a) requires a written statement within the same 21 days. Deliver or mail it with the balance. The statement must:

- Describe each item of physical damage or other claim.
- Give the amount withheld as reasonable pay for each item.

You may not misrepresent or falsify any claim, including repair costs (ATCP 134.06(4)(b)). The annotation to Wis. Stat. 704.95 in the statute book cites State v. Lasecki (2020). It says the duty to give this statement comes from ATCP 134.06(4), not chapter 704.

## Must a Wisconsin landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

Chapter 704 and chapter ATCP 134 do not require interest on a deposit. They also do not require a separate bank account. We read both chapters on 2026-10-19 and found no such rule.

A city or county may have its own rules. Check the local code where the unit is.

## What is the penalty for a Wisconsin landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

A tenant who suffers a money loss from a violation of ATCP 134 may sue for twice the loss. The note at the start of ATCP 134 says the tenant may sue under Wis. Stat. 100.20(5) and recover twice the amount of the loss, plus costs and reasonable attorney fees. The state may also prosecute violations under Wis. Stat. 100.20(6) and 100.26.

Wis. Stat. 704.95 adds that a breach of 704.28 or 704.44 may also be an unfair trade practice under 100.20.

**Worked example.** You hold a $1,500 deposit. You keep $400 for routine repainting with no unusual damage, which the rule does not allow. If a court finds the $400 is a loss caused by the violation, the tenant may get $800 (twice $400), plus costs and attorney fees.

| Mistake | Risk | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit or statement after day 21 | Violation of 704.28(4) and ATCP 134.06(2) | Statute, rule |
| Deduction for normal wear and tear | Violation of 704.28(3) | Statute |
| Nonstandard deduction not in a separate titled document | Not allowed | Wis. Stat. 704.28(2) |
| Falsified claim | Violation of ATCP 134.06(4)(b) | Rule |
| Tenant's money loss | Twice the loss, costs, attorney fees | Wis. Stat. 100.20(5) |

## Wisconsin deposit checklist for property managers

- [ ] Inspection-rights notice given before you take the deposit, with a deadline at least 7 days after move-in.
- [ ] Check-in sheet given at move-in.
- [ ] Any extra deduction reasons in a separate "NONSTANDARD RENTAL PROVISIONS" document, reviewed with the tenant.
- [ ] Move-out date and the right 21-day start date recorded.
- [ ] Written statement lists each item and amount.
- [ ] No deductions for normal wear, routine paint or routine carpet cleaning.
- [ ] Prepaid rent above one month returned as a deposit.

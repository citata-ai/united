## How many investors buy rental homes?

Investors buy about 1 in 4 US homes, and almost all of them are small. BatchData, with the CJ Patrick Company, counted 284,068 homes bought by investors in Q2 2026. That was 26.7% of all purchases, the lowest share in 5 quarters. Cotality, with a different method, put investors at 27.7% of single-family purchases in March 2026.

The size of the buyer matters more for your marketing. BatchData says owners of 1 to 10 homes hold 96% of investor-owned single-family homes. Owners of 1 to 5 homes alone hold 92.14%. Owners of more than 1,000 homes hold 2.19%.

| Fact | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Investor share of home purchases, Q2 2026 | 26.7% | BatchData |
| Homes bought by investors, Q2 2026 | 284,068 | BatchData |
| Investor-owned single-family homes | About 15.9 million, about 18% of the stock | BatchData |
| Share held by owners of 1–10 homes | 96% | BatchData |
| Investor share of single-family purchases, March 2026 | 27.7% | Cotality |
| Rental properties owned by individual investors (2020) | 70.2% | CRS, 2021 RHFS |

So your investor prospect is often a person with a job, 1 to 5 homes, and a plan to buy more. That person reads, compares and asks other investors. Big funds run their own management. Do not build your plan around them.

## Where do local investors meet and read?

Local investors meet in a few places you can list in an hour. Go to the ones that meet often and have real buyers in the room.

- **REIA groups.** A REIA is a real estate investors association. National REIA says it has over 120 local chapters and associations and 40,000 members. Many local groups sell vendor tables or sponsor slots.
- **BiggerPockets meetups.** BiggerPockets runs a Real Estate Events & Meetups forum. Local hosts post deal nights, tax talks and networking events there, by city.
- **Investor-friendly agents.** Agents who sell to investors often run their own meetups or buyer lists.
- **Lenders.** DSCR and hard money lenders (loans based on rent, not salary) know who buys in your area.
- **Online groups.** City investor groups on Facebook and Reddit, and the BiggerPockets forums for your market.

Do not walk in with a pitch. Bring one useful thing: a one-page rent sheet for 3 local zip codes, or a short talk on local rental rules. Investors remember the person who helped them run numbers.

## What do investors want that accidental landlords do not?

Investors want numbers and speed. An accidental landlord, who rents a home because they moved or inherited it, often wants comfort and a person to trust. Write for both, but know which one you talk to.

| Topic | Investor wants | Accidental landlord wants |
|---|---|---|
| Rent | A rent estimate in writing, with comps | To know the home will rent |
| Fees | A full fee table, every line | A simple monthly price |
| Reports | Monthly statements, year-end 1099, export to their spreadsheet | A clear summary |
| Growth | Help with the next purchase, rehab bids | Little or none |
| Speed | A reply within one business day | A friendly first call |
| Proof | Days on market, renewal rate, turnover cost from your data | Reviews and a local office |

The fee table matters most. Investors compare the cost of management with the rent. Our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) shows the math they run. If your site says "call for pricing", many investors skip you.

## Which content brings investor leads?

Content with local numbers brings investor leads, because investors search for data before they buy. Generic posts such as "5 benefits of a property manager" do not help them.

Build these 6 pieces, in this order:

1. **A fee table page.** Every fee, including leasing, renewal, lease-up and maintenance markup.
2. **A rent page by area.** Typical rents by zip code or neighborhood from your own leases. Update it every quarter.
3. **A sample owner statement.** One redacted monthly statement as a PDF or image.
4. **A "buying a rental in [city]" guide.** Local rules, rental registration, inspection rules, property tax dates.
5. **A rent estimate form.** The owner enters an address and gets a written estimate within one business day.
6. **A Section 8 page, if you serve that market.** Many investors buy for voucher tenants. Link to public data such as our [Section 8 rent limits by metro](/guides/section-8-rent-limits).

Most owner leads still come through Google. The Goodjuju 2026 lead study (fall update, 35 PM companies) shows this. Google Search and the Map Pack brought 50.6% of owner leads, and referrals brought 3.7%. So these pages must rank and must be in your Google Business Profile services list. Meetups build trust. Search sends the form.

## How do I partner with investor-friendly agents?

Partner with agents who sell to investors and do not manage rentals themselves. NARPM writes on its "Why Use a NARPM Member?" page that most of its members do not sell property; they only manage and lease. NARPM adds that this makes them a source of buyer and seller referrals for agents.

Use these steps:

1. **Find the agents.** Look for agents who speak at REIA events or post on investor forums.
2. **Offer the trade.** You send buyer and seller leads from your owners. The agent sends new buyers to you for management.
3. **Make it easy.** Give the agent a one-page rent sheet for each listing they show to an investor.
4. **Agree in writing.** Who handles the sale, who handles the lease, who talks to the owner, and when.
5. **Check referral fee rules.** Rules on paying referral fees differ by state. Ask your state license board or a local attorney first.
6. **Track the source.** Tag each lead with the agent's name in your CRM. Review the list each quarter.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What should you do first?

Do 3 things this month. Publish your full fee table. Go to one local REIA meeting with a rent sheet. Call 2 investor agents and offer the referral trade. Then count how many investor owners sign in the next 90 days, and from which source.

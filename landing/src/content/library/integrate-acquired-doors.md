## What must happen before I tell anyone about the acquisition?

Before you tell owners or tenants, confirm that the management agreements can move to you. A deal that buys doors without the contracts buys nothing. Ask your attorney this question first.

State rules differ. New Mexico rule 16.61.24.13(E) is clear. Before a brokerage transfers a management agreement to another brokerage, both qualifying brokers must sign an assignment of contract. Because the agreement is a personal services contract, the broker must have the owner's written consent to assign it. Without an assignment, the receiving broker must sign a new agreement.

Your state may treat assignment in a different way. Read the assignment clause in each agreement you buy, and read your state's real estate rules.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What do owners and tenants hear, and when?

Owners hear first, from a named person, before tenants hear anything. Tenants hear next, before the next rent due date. An owner who learns the news from a tenant feels skipped.

| When | Who | What they get | From |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 0 (closing) | Staff of both companies | The plan, the dates, their role | Both company owners |
| Days 1 to 5 | Every owner | A call or letter: who you are, what stays the same, what changes, a named contact | The selling owner and you, together |
| Days 1 to 10 | Owners whose consent you need | The assignment or a new agreement to sign | Your account lead |
| At least 10 days before rent is due | Every tenant | New payment method, repair channel, where the deposit is | Your company |
| Cutover date | Owners and tenants | Portal logins and a reminder | Your software |

Tell owners what stays the same. Same fee for a set term, same account lead if that person joins you, same payout day.

Tell tenants only facts. The lease stays in force. Do not change lease terms in the notice. Send the same notice to every tenant.

Florida adds a tenant notice duty. Under Section 83.49(2), a landlord who changes the manner or place of holding a deposit must tell the tenant within 30 days. That subsection does not apply to a landlord with fewer than 5 units.

## How do I move trust funds and security deposits?

Move trust funds and deposits by bank transfer, with a written accounting per owner and per tenant, and sign a receipt for the exact amount. Reconcile the seller's trust accounts on the closing date before any money moves.

| State | Rule on the transfer | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Florida | On a sale or a change of rental agent, deposits and advance rent go to the new owner or agent, with interest and an accounting per tenant. After transfer and a written receipt, the transferor is free of the duty to hold them. | Fla. Stat. 83.49(7) |
| Illinois | On a sale or transfer of the property, the transferee is liable for deposits with statutory interest. The transferor stays jointly and severally liable. | 765 ILCS 710/1.1 |
| Oregon | Security deposits go in a separate security deposits account, within 5 banking days after receipt. The manager must notify the Agency within 10 business days of opening, closing or transferring a clients' trust account. | OAR 863-025-0025(3), (4), (6), (7) |
| Texas | Trust money, including rent and security deposits, goes in a trust account by the close of the second working day after receipt. | 22 TAC 535.146(b)(3) |

Run this 7-step funds transfer:

1. Reconcile each of the seller's trust accounts on the closing date.
2. List every owner balance, tenant deposit and prepaid rent.
3. Check that the list total equals the reconciled bank balance.
4. Write down every gap, and agree with the seller who covers it.
5. Transfer funds to your trust and deposit accounts.
6. Sign a receipt for the exact amount you got.
7. Post opening balances in your software from the checked list.

A gap in step 4 is the seller's to cover before closing. Put that duty in the purchase agreement.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I move data between property management software?

Move all data on one cutover date, best the 1st of a month. One date gives every ledger one start point and stops double entry.

Rentvine's case study on Rosenbaum Realty Group shows this in practice. That company had about 500 units the year before, bought a 300-unit portfolio during software onboarding, and moved old and new units to Rentvine on the same date, October 1. It now has about 1,260 doors and 65 employees, by the case study's numbers.

Export this data from the old system:

- [ ] Owners: contact, tax ID form on file, bank details form, agreement dates
- [ ] Properties and units: address, unit, features, keys
- [ ] Tenants: contact, lease dates, rent, deposit amount
- [ ] Ledgers: opening balance per tenant and per owner
- [ ] Recurring charges: rent, pet rent, utility bill-backs
- [ ] Vendors: contact, insurance, W-9
- [ ] Open work orders and recent inspections
- [ ] Documents: leases, addenda, inspection photos
- [ ] Year-to-date owner income and expense, for year-end reports

Check a sample before you go live. Pick 20 tenants at random. Compare rent, deposit and balance in the new system to the source documents. If 1 of 20 is wrong, check them all.

Plan the year-end report. The IRS puts the 1099-MISC duty on the manager that pays rent over to the owner. In a mid-year acquisition, ask a tax professional how the seller and you split the year.

## What churn should I expect after closing?

Expect some owners to leave, and measure it from day 1. We found no reliable public number for owner loss after a property management acquisition. Any figure you read without a source is a guess.

Track these 4 numbers each month for 12 months:

| Metric | How to calculate |
|---|---|
| Owner churn | Owners lost this month ÷ acquired owners at closing × 100 |
| Door churn | Doors lost this month ÷ acquired doors at closing × 100 |
| Consent rate | Owners who signed assignment or new agreement ÷ owners asked |
| Reason mix | Count of each exit reason, in the owner's words |

Example: you close on 300 doors from 140 owners. In month 2, 4 owners with 9 doors leave. Owner churn is 4 ÷ 140 × 100 = 2.9%. Door churn is 9 ÷ 300 × 100 = 3.0%.

Read the reasons each month. If most exits cite the new fee, the price change came too soon. If most cite slow replies, staff the account leads before the next deal.

## How do I keep the acquired owners after the first 90 days?

Keep acquired owners with the basics: a named contact, fast replies and a clean first owner statement. The first statement from your system is the test. Make it right.

Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to see what each acquired door earns at its current fee. Doors that lose money need a fee talk or a fair exit, not silence.

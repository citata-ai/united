## What is the maximum late fee in Minnesota?

The maximum late fee in Minnesota is 8% of the overdue rent payment. Minnesota Statutes 504B.177(a) says: "In no case may the late fee exceed eight percent of the overdue rent payment." The rule applies to a landlord of a residential building. We read the 2025 Minnesota Statutes text on the Revisor's site on 2026-10-19.

The cap is a percent of the overdue payment, not a flat dollar amount. So the cap moves with the rent. The statute also says a late fee is not interest and not liquidated damages.

Here is the cap at 4 common rents:

| Monthly rent | 8% cap on a full missed payment |
|---|---|
| $900 | $72 |
| $1,200 | $96 |
| $1,600 | $128 |
| $2,500 | $200 |

Watch for: the cap is on the overdue payment. If the tenant pays $1,000 of a $1,600 rent on time, the overdue amount is $600. The cap is then 8% of $600, which is $48.

## Does Minnesota require a grace period before a late fee?

Minnesota state law does not set a grace period for late fees. Minn. Stat. 504B.177 has no number of days. It says a landlord may not charge a late fee "if the rent is paid after the due date" unless both parties agreed in writing. The agreement must specify when the fee will be imposed.

So the lease sets the timing. If your lease says the fee applies on the 4th day of the month, it applies on the 4th, and not before.

One trap: an early-payment discount does not create a new due date. The statute says the "due date" does not include an earlier date by which the tenant earns a discount. If rent is due on the 1st and you give a discount for payment by the 25th of the month before, a payment on the 1st is on time.

## Must the late fee be written in the lease in Minnesota?

Yes, a Minnesota late fee must be in a written agreement. Minn. Stat. 504B.177(a) requires that "the tenant and landlord have agreed in writing that a late fee may be imposed." The written agreement must also say when the fee will be imposed. An oral lease with no written late fee term does not let you charge one.

A short lease checklist:

- [ ] The lease says a late fee may be imposed.
- [ ] The lease says the day or event that starts the fee.
- [ ] The fee is a percent or a dollar amount that stays at or under 8% of the overdue rent.
- [ ] The lease does not treat an early-payment discount date as the due date.
- [ ] For a HAP tenancy, the fee is based only on the tenant's share.

## Can a Minnesota landlord charge a daily late fee?

A Minnesota landlord may structure a late fee in steps, but the total may not exceed 8% of the overdue payment. Minn. Stat. 504B.177(a) does not mention daily fees. It does say the fee may "in no case" go above 8%. That limit covers the full fee on one overdue payment.

Worked example: rent is $1,500. The cap is 8% of $1,500, which is $120. A lease that charges $10 a day reaches $120 on day 12. After that, you may not add more for that payment.

## How do late fees work with Section 8 in Minnesota?

With a housing assistance payments contract, a Minnesota late fee is calculated only on the tenant's portion of the rent. This is Minn. Stat. 504B.177(c). Laws 2024, chapter 118, section 13 added it. That act is effective January 1, 2025, unless a section says otherwise, and section 13 sets no other date. The rule covers programs in 42 U.S.C. 1437f (Section 8) and 1485, and other programs where you get rent from the tenant and a payment from the government.

Worked example:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Contract rent | $1,400 |
| Housing authority pays | $980 |
| Tenant pays | $420 |
| Old math: 8% of $1,400 | $112 (not allowed now) |
| Current cap: 8% of $420 | $33.60 |

Paragraph (b) adds one exception. If a federal statute, regulation or handbook allows late fees for a federally subsidized tenancy and conflicts with paragraph (a), you may publish and use a fee schedule that follows the federal rule. ## What changed in 2026 for rent payment apps?

Since August 1, 2026, a Minnesota landlord may not charge a late fee when a digital payment platform and the backup method both fail. Laws 2026, chapter 81, section 2 rewrote Minn. Stat. 504B.118. If you require or permit a payment app or portal, you must offer an alternative when it does not work. You may not charge a fee for the alternative.

The law bans "any adverse action, including but not limited to filing an eviction or assessing late fees" when rent is unpaid because the platform and the alternative both failed. If you violate the section, the tenant has a defense to an eviction for nonpayment. The court must dismiss the case and may award attorney fees. Subdivision 4 applies to eviction actions filed on or after August 1, 2026.

What to do:

1. **Name a backup method** in the lease, such as a check to a mailing address.
2. **Log outages** of your portal with date and time.
3. **Waive the late fee** for any day the portal and the backup were both down.

## Can a Minnesota landlord evict for unpaid late fees?

The Minnesota pre-filing notice covers late fees, but the redemption statute speaks of rent. Before an eviction for "nonpayment of rent or other unpaid financial obligation in violation of the lease", Minn. Stat. 504B.321, subd. 1a requires a written notice. The notice must give the total due and "a specific accounting" of unpaid rent, late fees and other charges. The tenant then has 14 days to pay or move, or longer if a local rule requires it.

In a nonpayment case, Minn. Stat. 504B.291 lets the tenant redeem before possession is delivered. The tenant pays "the amount of the rent that is in arrears, with interest, costs of the action, and an attorney's fee not to exceed $5". The text names rent, not late fees. Ask a local attorney before you build an eviction around fees alone.

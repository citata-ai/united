## Does my city require an STR permit?

Many cities require a permit, license or registration before you host a short-term rental (STR). Each city sets its own limit for what counts as short: in New York City, rentals of 30 consecutive days or more are exempt from registration. Check your city's code before you create the listing.

New York City is a clear example. The NYC Office of Special Enforcement says the city adopted Local Law 18, the Short-Term Rental Registration Law, on January 9, 2022. The law requires hosts to register with the office. It bars booking platforms such as Airbnb, VRBO and Booking.com from processing transactions for unregistered short-term rentals. Enforcement with the platforms began on September 5, 2023.

The NYC page names 2 exemptions: rentals of 30 consecutive days or more, and units in "Class B" multiple dwellings approved for legal short-term occupancy. The city also keeps a Prohibited Buildings List of buildings where short-term rentals are not allowed, either by law or by the building's leases.

What to look up in your own city:

| Question | Where to find it |
|----------|------------------|
| Are STRs allowed in my zone? | City zoning code or planning department |
| Must I live on site (primary residence rule)? | City STR ordinance |
| Do I need a permit, license or registration number? | City licensing or STR office |
| Is there a cap on nights per year? | City STR ordinance |
| Must the registration number appear on the listing? | City STR ordinance |
| Is a safety inspection required? | Building or fire department |
| What are the fines? | City STR ordinance |

Watch for: platform listing does not mean legal. In cities like New York, the platform must check registration. In other cities, the host carries the full duty.

## Does my HOA or lease allow STRs?

Your HOA, condo board or landlord can ban short stays even where the city allows them. Private rules sit on top of city rules.

Where to look:

- **HOA declaration (CC&Rs).** Search for "lease", "rental", "transient" and "minimum term".
- **Condo bylaws and house rules.** Some set a minimum lease term.
- **Board minutes.** A rental ban can be on the agenda right now.
- **Your own lease, if you rent.** Most leases bar subletting without consent.
- **Your mortgage.** The standard Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac security instrument has an occupancy rule in section 6. It asks you to live in the home for at least 1 year after you move in. Renting the whole home to guests in that year can conflict with it. A second-home loan has its own rules.

Ask the board in writing: "Are rentals of fewer than 30 days allowed at this address?" Keep the reply.

## What occupancy taxes apply?

Occupancy taxes are taxes on short stays, often called hotel, lodging, transient or tourist taxes. They can come from the state, the county and the city at once. Your duty is to know each one.

Airbnb says it collects and remits certain taxes in places where it has agreements with governments or where the law requires it. Its help page lists many US states. But Airbnb also says hosts in those areas are responsible for assessing all other tax obligations, including state and city taxes.

Steps to set up taxes:

1. **Find every tax** that applies at your address: state, county, city and any special district.
2. **Check Airbnb's tax page** for your state and city. Note what it collects and what it does not.
3. **Register** with each tax office for the taxes the platform does not collect.
4. **Check other platforms.** VRBO or direct bookings may not collect the same taxes.
5. **Set a calendar** for each filing date, even for months with zero bookings.

Income tax is separate. IRS Publication 527 has special rules when you also use the home yourself:

| Your use | IRS treatment (Publication 527) |
|----------|----------------------------------|
| You rent the home fewer than 15 days in the year and also use it as a home | Do not include the rent in income; do not deduct rental expenses |
| Your personal use is more than the greater of 14 days or 10% of the days rented at a fair price | The home counts as used as a home; rental deductions may be limited |

Publication 925 adds a rule for the passive loss limits: an activity where the average guest stay is 7 days or less is not treated as a rental activity. That changes how losses work.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## Does my insurance cover STR guests?

Do not assume it does. A standard homeowner or landlord policy is written for you or a long-term tenant, not a stream of paying guests. Ask your insurer in writing if the policy covers short-term guests, and for what.

Platform programs are not a full answer. Airbnb's help page says Host damage protection, part of AirCover for Hosts, reimburses hosts up to $3 million for certain guest damage. The same page says Host Damage Protection is not insurance or a financial service. It lists exclusions:

- Normal wear and tear.
- Loss of currency.
- Acts of nature, such as earthquakes and hurricanes.
- Injury or property damage to guests or others (Airbnb points to its separate host liability insurance).
- Normal check-out cleaning.

Airbnb also sets a time limit. If you want its support involved in a claim, submit the request and documents within 14 days of the damage or loss.

Questions to ask your insurer:

- [ ] Does my policy cover paid stays of fewer than 30 days?
- [ ] Does it cover guest injuries (liability)?
- [ ] Does it cover lost income if the home cannot be booked after damage?
- [ ] Is there a home-sharing or STR endorsement, and what does it cost?
- [ ] Does my HOA's master policy have any STR exclusion?

## The before-you-list checklist

- [ ] City allows STRs at the address; permit or registration number in hand.
- [ ] Primary residence or night cap rules understood.
- [ ] HOA, condo and lease rules allow stays under 30 days, in writing.
- [ ] Mortgage occupancy terms checked.
- [ ] Every occupancy tax listed; registrations done for those the platform does not collect.
- [ ] Insurer confirmed STR coverage in writing.
- [ ] Smoke and CO alarms, fire extinguisher and exit plan in place.
- [ ] House rules written, including a guest limit set by local code.
- [ ] Income tax treatment checked with a tax professional.

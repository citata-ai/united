## How do commercial leases differ?

Commercial leases can assign different costs, notice duties and operating responsibilities to each tenant. Read the actual documents before treating the building like residential management. A lease label does not replace its terms.

Buildium's commercial lease guide distinguishes gross, modified gross and net lease structures. It also discusses common area maintenance, often shortened to CAM. Use those categories to organize review, while asking counsel to interpret the signed wording.

Create a lease abstract, a short record of the terms staff must act on. Include rent changes, options, notice routes, repair responsibilities and expense treatment. Attach each entry to the source clause.

Do not assume leases in the same building match. A negotiated exclusion or cap can change how staff bill shared costs. Keep exceptions visible in the accounting process.

Use this proposed readiness table:

| Work area | Evidence to review before acceptance |
|---|---|
| Lease administration | Current leases, amendments and a checked abstract |
| Expense recoveries | Defined recoverable costs and allocation method |
| Building systems | Maintenance scope, access and qualified providers |
| Owner reporting | Sample report and required accounting detail |
| Legal authority | License and agreement review for the actual service |
| Insurance | Written assessment of the proposed activities |

Ask the owner for missing documents before issuing a firm scope. A quote based on incomplete leases should clearly state what still needs review.

## How is commercial management priced?

Commercial management should be priced from the actual service burden and the fee base agreed with the owner. Do not copy a residential percentage without checking what it covers. A low-looking percentage can hide extensive separate work.

Map recurring lease administration, accounting, site work and reporting. Then separate leasing brokerage, major projects and specialist work. Identify who provides each service and whether the proposed management agreement authorizes it.

If the fee uses rent, define the relevant receipts. Ask how the calculation treats expense recoveries, taxes, deposits and other amounts. Have the accountant and attorney review the wording before staff configure billing.

Compare a fixed building fee, a percentage and a mixed structure using the same scope. Include the effect of vacant space and unusual reporting needs. Label the comparison as your own proposal, not a market rate study.

Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) for a basic price comparison. Add commercial work separately because a residential cost model may not capture lease-specific accounting.

Test billing with a sample month and a shared-expense reconciliation. If the team cannot explain the calculation clearly, improve the scope or system before signing. Price should follow a workable service design.

## What new skills and insurance do I need?

The new skills depend on the leases and building, but start with lease administration, expense accounting and commercial vendor oversight. Identify a qualified lead for each area. Do not assume a residential manager learns every new duty during live service.

Ask the accounting lead to demonstrate an expense allocation from invoice to tenant bill. Check the lease authority and exclusions. Review whether the software can preserve the calculation and explain later adjustments.

Ask operations staff to review building systems with appropriate specialists. Understand access, service contracts and owner responsibilities before taking over. Keep uncertain technical findings with the relevant professional.

HUB's property management insurance page discusses the relationship between owner coverage and the manager's professional liability coverage. Use that distinction when asking the broker to review commercial work. A property policy and a manager's professional coverage serve different purposes.

Give the broker the proposed services, building use and contract. Ask which policy wording applies and whether any changes are needed before work begins. Keep the answer in writing with the issued documents.

Have counsel review licensing, service authority and contract terms for the jurisdiction. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## When does commercial management make sense?

Commercial management makes sense when the company can deliver a defined service at a sustainable cost. A familiar owner or nearby building can create an opportunity, but does not remove the need for review.

Start with an explicit fit decision. Can your team understand the lease file, perform the accounting and support the field work? If a critical task lacks a qualified lead, close that gap before accepting the account.

Compare the new work with demands on the residential portfolio. A commercial assignment that consumes the founder's attention can reduce service elsewhere. Include management time in the cost estimate.

Consider a limited scope only if it is lawful, clear and useful to the owner. Do not describe a narrow coordination role as complete management. State which decisions and tasks remain with the owner or another provider.

Ask the owner to review a sample report and service plan. Confirm what success means and where they expect approval rights. Unstated reporting expectations can change the workload after signing.

Decline or refer the work when it falls outside your skills. A suitable referral can serve the owner better than an agreement your company cannot perform.

## How should I run the first account review?

Run the first account review around evidence that the new workflow works. Read the lease abstract beside the source documents. Check upcoming decisions and the person responsible for each action.

Follow a shared expense from vendor invoice to owner report and any tenant recovery. Confirm that staff can explain the treatment. Resolve unclear allocation before it repeats across the building.

Review field coverage during leave and urgent events. The new account should have a backup who knows the building and can access the required records. Keep that person involved before the first absence.

Compare actual staff work with the quoted scope. Record where work exceeded assumptions and why. Address the cause through process changes or a properly reviewed scope amendment.

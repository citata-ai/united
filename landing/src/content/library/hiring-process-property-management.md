## Which interview questions help identify a strong hire?

Ask questions that reveal how the applicant handles work your company actually needs. Use a shared question set and record evidence before discussing impressions.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics describes property manager duties that include repairs, financial reports, rental records and owner requests. Use the duties that match your opening to build the interview. Do not assume every manager role needs the same experience.

| Interview topic | Suggested question | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Priorities | How would you handle an urgent report and a waiting owner? | Safe triage and clear next steps |
| Records | Describe a time your records did not match | Verification rather than a guess |
| Communication | How would you explain an unexpected repair bill? | Facts, ownership and follow-up |
| Limits | When would you ask a supervisor to decide? | Clear authority boundaries |
| Learning | How did you learn an unfamiliar process? | A method for checking work |

Ask for a specific example, then clarify what the applicant personally did. Follow the sequence from report to decision to result. Give the applicant room to explain an error and what changed afterward.

Score the evidence against written role needs. Avoid using a shared personal interest as evidence of job fit. Keep comments factual enough that another reviewer can understand the decision.

## Should I use a work test?

A work test can help when it measures an actual job task with a clear scoring guide. Keep the scope limited and avoid using candidates to complete unpaid productive work.

Use fictional records. For a coordinator, provide a sample repair request and ask for an internal note and owner update. For an accounting role, provide a sample ledger difference and ask what the applicant would check next.

Write the expected qualities before you see submissions. These might include accurate facts, a clear next action, sensible escalation and no invented promise. Accept different wording when the underlying decision is sound.

Give each applicant the same task, materials and instructions. Explain what tools they may use. If tool use is part of the role, ask the applicant to show how they checked the output rather than only presenting a polished answer.

The EEOC says employers may ask applicants to describe or demonstrate how they would perform specific tasks. Its guidance also limits pre-offer questions about disability. Review the test and application process with your adviser, including accommodation needs.

Do not judge speed alone. A fast answer that misses a safety issue may be weaker than a careful answer that asks for a missing fact. Discuss the work sample during the interview so you can assess the reasoning.

## Does the hire need property management experience?

Prior property management experience matters most when the role requires judgment the team cannot closely supervise. For a trainable administrative role, related service or recordkeeping experience may be useful evidence.

Separate required skills from skills you can teach. A candidate may learn your software while already showing careful records and reliable follow-up. Another may know the software but make unsupported decisions.

Check the role's legal boundaries before you relax credential requirements. TREC says paid showing or leasing for an owner requires a license in Texas. Its guidance also addresses control of rent acceptance or deposits for single-family residential property. That is a state example, not a national rule.

Ask the responsible broker to list which tasks the new hire may perform locally. Build training and access rights around that list. Do not assume a different job title changes the permitted duties.

If you hire someone new to the field, name the trainer and review points. Give the person a place to ask questions before acting. Do not assign a broad portfolio and rely on informal help when the first issue arises.

If you hire an experienced manager, still teach your service promises and approval process. Experience at another company does not tell the person how your team records exceptions or reports risk.

## How should I check references?

Check references with the candidate's knowledge and a consistent set of job-related questions. Confirm the contact through a reasonable route rather than relying only on an unfamiliar email address.

Ask about the person's role, working relationship and observed duties. Request a concrete example of follow-through, judgment or response to feedback. Keep questions tied to work the reference actually saw.

Avoid asking for personal or medical information. EEOC guidance says the hiring restrictions it describes apply to reference checks as well as direct interviews. Review the process with counsel when you are unsure what to ask.

Some employers only confirm role and dates. Do not treat a limited response as proof of poor performance. Note what the reference could and could not verify.

Use references to test a specific uncertainty. If the work sample showed weak escalation, ask how the person handled decisions outside their authority. Do not fish for negative comments unrelated to the role.

Record the source and date of the reference check. Keep candidate records in an approved location with access limited to the hiring team. Ask your adviser about local retention and privacy requirements.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How should I make the final choice?

Compare applicants against the role scorecard after you collect interview, task and reference evidence. Separate an observed fact from an unresolved concern.

If candidates remain close, identify the missing evidence and use a consistent follow-up. Do not keep adding interviews without knowing what decision the next interview should resolve.

Check whether your team can support the chosen person's training needs. A promising hire can fail if nobody has time to teach the process. Include that supervision cost in the hiring decision.

Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) when checking whether the role fits your service budget. The tool does not set pay; your budget needs the full cost of employment and training.

Start by writing the tasks and decisions for your next opening. Build the interview and work sample from that list before you advertise the role.

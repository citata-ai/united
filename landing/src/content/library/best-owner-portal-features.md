## Which reports do owners check most?

An owner portal should first answer what came in, what went out and what the owner received. We have not verified a broad usage ranking of owner reports. Use the questions your own clients ask to set the order.

AppFolio's owner help page describes income, expenses, contributions, distributions and published report packets. It also describes property-level views. These functions provide a useful test list, not proof that every owner uses them equally.

Start with this checklist:

| Owner question | Portal record to test |
|---|---|
| Did rent arrive? | Dated charges, receipts and open balances |
| Why was my payment lower? | Expenses, fees, reserves and held funds |
| What work did I approve? | Estimate, approval and final invoice |
| Where is the statement? | Searchable report archive |
| Which home needs attention? | Property view and clear status |

Ask an owner to explain a distribution from the portal alone. If they cannot, revise the report labels and supporting notes. More charts will not fix an unexplained cash movement.

## How often should statements arrive?

Statements should arrive on a clear schedule that your team can meet after review. The right date depends on your close process and service agreement. This guide does not claim a universal legal delivery deadline.

Separate the live dashboard from the closed statement. AppFolio describes real-time reports and published packets as distinct portal functions. A current display may change when staff post another transaction. A reviewed statement should clearly identify its period.

Tell owners when the next statement will appear and how corrections work. If a statement changes, preserve the reason and notify affected owners. Avoid quietly replacing a file with a different balance.

Test date filters. Owners should know whether a report covers transaction dates, payment dates or another basis. Ask your accountant to review the labels. A clear schedule and basis reduce avoidable questions without promising fewer calls.

## Should owners see work orders live?

Owners should see enough repair information to understand status, approvals and spending. That does not require unrestricted access to every internal note or renter message. Set the sharing rules before enabling broad visibility.

AppFolio's help page describes shared inspections and documents. Buildium lists an owner portal in its plan comparison. Those pages do not prove that every work-order event automatically reaches every owner. Ask for the exact configuration.

Test a repair from request to invoice. Show the owner the issue, approved scope, appointment status and final cost. Use plain status labels that describe completed work or the next step.

Review photos and attachments before sharing. Remove unrelated personal information and sensitive access instructions. Keep a path for the owner to ask a question or approve an estimate. A portal should support the manager's explanation rather than force owners to interpret staff shorthand.

## Which software has the best owner app?

The best owner app is the one your owners can use to finish required tasks without coaching. Public feature pages cannot establish a universal usability winner. This guide therefore gives a test method rather than an unsupported ranking.

AppFolio describes owner access from computers, tablets and phones, including statements and contributions. Buildium lists its owner portal across plans. Rentvine describes owner contributions and distributions in its accounting material.

Use the same tasks in every finalist. Have an owner find a past statement, locate an invoice and identify a reserve. Ask them to send a question and check that staff receive it with context.

Test a person who owns through several entities. Then remove access to a former property. The portal should keep the right records visible without exposing a new owner's information. Include sign-in recovery in the test. A useful app must remain usable after a forgotten password.

## How does the portal help owner retention?

A portal can support trust by making records and next steps clear. We have not verified a causal retention gain from a portal feature. Treat retention as a business outcome to measure, not a vendor claim to repeat.

Look for recurring gaps in owner questions. Perhaps an owner cannot explain a reserve increase or find a repair invoice. Fix the report or process that causes the confusion.

Do not use the portal as a reason to avoid contact. A difficult month still needs a clear explanation. Put the statement facts in the portal and send a short note that points to the relevant records.

Track unresolved owner questions and the time staff need to answer them. Compare those records after changing portal setup. If you claim improvement later, use your own dated results. Avoid promising that a new app will prevent cancellations.

## What security and access checks should you run?

Portal access should match ownership and current authority. AppFolio's help page describes two-factor authentication and account settings. Confirm the actual controls in your chosen product rather than assuming all portals work alike.

- **Ownership:** show only the properties and entities the user can access.
- **Staff roles:** limit who can publish or change owner reports.
- **Sign-in:** test account recovery and extra verification.
- **Documents:** inspect shared files for unrelated personal data.
- **Payments:** verify contribution and payout-change workflows.
- **Changes:** remove former owners and staff when authority ends.
- **Archive:** download important reports into company-controlled storage.

Treat bank-detail changes as a separate review process. Ask how staff receive notice and verify the request. Avoid approving a payout change solely from an unverified message.

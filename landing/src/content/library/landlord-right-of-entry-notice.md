## When can a landlord enter a rented unit?

A landlord can enter a rented unit only for set reasons. Most state statutes list the same core reasons: repairs, inspections, agreed services, and showings to buyers, lenders, new tenants or contractors. An emergency, a court order and an abandoned unit are the other common reasons.

The tenant has the right to possession during the lease. So the landlord's right to enter is a limited exception. It is not a general right to check on the tenant.

Here is how 3 states write the rule.

| Rule | California | Florida | Arizona |
|---|---|---|---|
| Statute | Civil Code 1954 | Fla. Stat. 83.53 | A.R.S. 33-1343 |
| Notice for normal entry | Written, 24 hours presumed reasonable | 24 hours for repairs | At least 2 days |
| Notice by mail | 6 days before entry presumed reasonable | Not stated | Not stated |
| Hours | Normal business hours | 7:30 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. for repairs | Reasonable times |
| Emergency | No notice needed | No notice needed | No consent needed |
| Tenant refusal | Not stated in the parts quoted here | Tenant may not unreasonably withhold consent | Tenant may not unreasonably withhold consent |
| Harassment | Landlord may not abuse access or harass | Landlord may not abuse access or harass | Landlord may not abuse access or harass |

Rules differ in other states. Always read your state statute and your lease before you enter.

## What counts as reasonable notice?

Reasonable notice means the notice period your state sets, or the period a court would accept. The 3 states above show the range.

- **California.** Civil Code 1954(d) requires reasonable written notice. The notice must state the date, the approximate time and the purpose. The statute presumes 24 hours is reasonable. If you mail the notice, 6 days before entry is presumed reasonable.
- **Florida.** Fla. Stat. 83.53 defines reasonable notice for repairs as at least 24 hours before entry.
- **Arizona.** A.R.S. 33-1343 requires at least 2 days' notice of the intent to enter.

California also allows other ways to deliver. You can hand the notice to the tenant. You can leave it with a person of suitable age at the unit. You can also leave it on, near or under the usual entry door where a reasonable person will find it.

A good entry notice holds 5 facts:

1. The unit address.
2. The date of entry.
3. The time window, for example 10:00 a.m. to 12:00 p.m.
4. The purpose, for example "replace the kitchen faucet".
5. Who will enter: you, a vendor, or both.

Send it by the method the lease names. Keep a copy with the date and time you sent it.

## When can I enter without notice?

You can enter without notice in an emergency. California, Florida and Arizona all allow this. A fire, a burst pipe, a gas smell or water that pours into the unit below are clear cases.

Other cases where state law allows entry without the usual notice:

- **Abandonment.** California and Arizona allow entry when the tenant abandoned or surrendered the unit.
- **Court order.** California and Arizona allow entry by court order.
- **Protection of the property.** Florida lets the landlord enter at any time to protect or preserve the premises.
- **Long absence.** Florida allows entry if the tenant is gone for half the rent period. If the rent is current and the tenant told you about the absence, you need consent or a reason to protect the property.
- **Tenant request.** In Arizona, a tenant's service request is permission to enter for that request. The tenant waives separate notice for it.
- **Oral agreement.** In California, you and the tenant can agree orally to an entry for agreed repairs or services. The entry must be within 1 week of the agreement.

After an emergency entry, tell the tenant in writing. State what happened, when you entered and what you did. This record protects you if the tenant asks later.

## Can a tenant refuse entry?

A tenant cannot refuse entry without a good reason when the landlord gave proper notice for a lawful purpose. Florida and Arizona both say the tenant "shall not unreasonably withhold consent" to entry for inspections, repairs, services and showings.

A tenant can refuse an entry that breaks the rules. For example, no notice, the wrong hours, or no lawful purpose.

If a tenant refuses a proper entry, use these steps:

1. **Talk first.** Ask why. Offer 2 other time windows.
2. **Put it in writing.** Send a new notice. Cite the lease clause and the state statute.
3. **Document it.** Keep each notice and each reply in the tenant file.
4. **Do not force entry.** Do not change locks or remove the tenant. Those acts can be an illegal eviction.
5. **Get advice.** Ask a local attorney about your options under the lease and state law.

Florida adds one more option. If the tenant unreasonably withholds consent, the landlord may enter for the listed purposes (Fla. Stat. 83.53(2)(c)). Many other states do not say this. Ask an attorney before you rely on it.

## What hours are reasonable for entry?

Reasonable hours are the hours your state sets, or daytime hours if the state is silent. Florida sets 7:30 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. for repair entries. California requires normal business hours, unless the tenant agrees at the time of entry to another time. Arizona requires entry at reasonable times.

A safe default for most units:

- Weekdays, during normal business hours.
- A 2-hour window, not "sometime Tuesday".
- No early morning, late night or holiday entries unless the tenant agrees in writing.

## How do I avoid harassment claims?

You avoid harassment claims by keeping entries few, planned and recorded. California, Florida and Arizona all say the landlord may not abuse the right of access or use it to harass the tenant.

Use this checklist for each entry:

- [ ] The purpose is one the statute and the lease allow.
- [ ] The notice meets the state period and is in writing.
- [ ] The notice states the date, time window and purpose.
- [ ] The entry is within the allowed hours.
- [ ] You knock and announce before you open the door.
- [ ] Only the people named in the notice enter.
- [ ] You stay in the rooms the task needs.
- [ ] You log the entry: date, time in, time out, work done.
- [ ] You send a short note after the entry if the tenant was not home.

For showings at the end of a lease, group them. Five showings on one Saturday are easier on a tenant than 5 visits on 5 days.

## How does the All Property Management lead model work?

All Property Management (APM) is a pay-per-lead marketplace: owners fill in a form, and APM sends the lead to managers who pay for it. APM's site footer says it is "A Buildium Company." Its About page says it started in 2004 and has connected 400,000 property owners to managers.

On the owner side, APM shows four steps: select your property, review local results, compare quotes, then choose a manager. A sample search for Boston, MA 02110 on the home page showed 15 property managers. So the owner sees several managers at once.

On the manager side, APM's Join Our Network page lists these terms (read 2026-10-19):

| Term | What APM says |
|---|---|
| Contract | No term contracts |
| Setup and monthly fees | None; "only pay for the leads you receive" |
| Targeting | Lead mix and volume by portfolio type, ZIP code and budget |
| Start time | New leads "in just 48 hours" |
| Delivery | Real-time, by email |
| Volume, past 365 days | 59,818 leads, 696,724 doors referred |
| Lead value claim | $143.6M "potential lifetime value," based on assumed conversion rates |

APM sells to residential and association managers. Its value math assumes 10% conversion for single-family and multifamily leads and 5% for association leads.

## What does a lead cost?

APM does not publish a price per lead. The Join Our Network page sends you to "Request a demo." Price likely depends on ZIP code, property type and volume, but APM does not say so on its public pages, so ask.

Ask these questions on the demo:

- **Price per lead** by property type and ZIP code.
- **How many managers** get the same lead.
- **Lead credits.** What happens to a fake, duplicate or out-of-area lead?
- **Budget cap.** Can you set a monthly maximum?
- **Pause rules.** How fast can you pause or change ZIP codes?
- **Lead fields.** Which fields does each lead include (doors, property type, timing)?

Get the answers in writing. Then you can test the source with real numbers.

## How do I measure close rate on these leads?

Measure close rate as signed management agreements divided by leads paid for, over at least 90 days. A short test gives a noisy number, because owners take weeks to decide.

Tag each APM lead in your CRM with its source and date. Track five numbers:

| Number | Example month 1 | Example month 2 | Example month 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leads paid for | 20 | 20 | 20 |
| Reached by phone | 14 | 15 | 15 |
| Proposal sent | 6 | 7 | 7 |
| Signed | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Doors signed | 1 | 3 | 2 |

These numbers are made up to show the method. In this example, close rate is 5 of 60 leads, or 8.3%. That is under APM's own 10% value assumption.

Now the break-even. Use your own numbers here:

1. Annual revenue per door. Example: $1,800 rent x 8% fee x 12 months = $1,728. Our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) helps with this step.
2. Doors signed in the test. Example: 6.
3. Year-one revenue from the test: 6 x $1,728 = $10,368.
4. Total lead spend. If 60 leads cost $X each, spend is 60 x $X.
5. Break-even price per lead for year one: $10,368 / 60 = $172.80.

If APM quotes you less than your break-even price, the source pays back in year one. Each year an owner stays adds more value. Include staff time: each lead needs calls, a proposal and follow-up.

## What do managers report about lead quality?

We did not find public, checkable data on APM lead quality. Reviews in forums are scattered and hard to verify, so we do not quote them here. The only firm facts are APM's own terms and its own advice to managers.

That advice tells you a lot. APM's "Converting Your Leads" page says the owner "may be contacting other property managers" and will compare services and price. It says the owner "should receive a phone call within minutes," and that a marketing package should go out within an hour. It also says "leads that are not actively managed are wasted leads and are not worth buying."

So quality depends on you as much as on APM. A team that calls in 5 minutes will report better leads than a team that calls the next day.

## How does APM compare with your own website leads?

Your own website leads cost you once (site and content), and the owner contacted only you. APM leads cost per lead, and the owner may contact several managers. Both can work. The mix matters.

| Factor | APM leads | Own website leads |
|---|---|---|
| Cost model | Per lead, price by quote | Site, content and SEO, mostly fixed |
| Time to first lead | About 48 hours (APM) | Months for SEO |
| Shared with others | Owner compares quotes | Usually only you |
| Who owns the channel | APM | You |
| Stops when you stop paying | Yes | No, pages keep working |

For a benchmark on own-site leads, Goodjuju's fall 2026 report (35 companies, 2,525 leads) found Google search and the Map Pack brought about half of all website form leads. If your own site brings few owner leads, read [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping) before you buy more paid leads.

## Who should buy APM leads?

APM fits a company with a fast phone process and spare capacity to grow. It fits less well if no one can call a lead within minutes.

Use this quick checklist before you sign up:

- [ ] A person can call each lead within 5 minutes in business hours.
- [ ] You have a proposal and fee sheet ready to send in 1 hour.
- [ ] Your CRM tags lead source on every new owner.
- [ ] You know your annual revenue per door.
- [ ] You set a 90-day budget and a stop rule (for example, stop if fewer than 3 doors sign).

If you check all five, run the test. If not, fix your follow-up first. Paid leads do not fix a slow process.

## Connecticut late fee rules at a glance

Connecticut sets a hard cap on late rent fees and a fixed grace period. We read the official statute text on the Connecticut General Assembly site, including the 2026 Supplement, which holds the 2025 changes. The table puts each rule in one line.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Grace period, monthly or longer tenancy | 9 days after the due date | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-15a(a) |
| Grace period, one-week tenancy | 4 days after the due date | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-15a(a) |
| Extra grace if the landlord's online payment system fails | 5 more days, that month or week only (from 2026-01-01) | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-15a(a) |
| Late fee cap | Lesser of $5 per day up to $50, or 5% of the late payment | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-15a(b) |
| Subsidized tenancy | 5% of the tenant's share only | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-15a(b) |
| Number of late fees | 1 per late payment | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-15a(b) |
| Lease term | Written agreement to pay the fee must be in the lease | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-15a(b), 47a-4(a) |
| Order of payments | Rent first, then fees | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-7d(e) |

## What is the maximum late fee a landlord can charge in Connecticut?

The Connecticut late fee cap is the lesser of two numbers. Section 47a-15a(b) says the charge may not exceed (1) $5 per day, up to a maximum of $50, or (2) 5% of the delinquent rent payment. Public Act 23-207 added this cap in 2023.

The 5% test is the one that bites on lower rents. The $50 ceiling bites on higher rents. Here is the maximum fee for a few monthly rents, for a payment that is late long enough for the daily count to reach $50:

| Monthly rent | 5% of rent | $50 ceiling | Maximum late fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| $700 | $35 | $50 | $35 |
| $1,000 | $50 | $50 | $50 |
| $1,400 | $70 | $50 | $50 |
| $2,500 | $125 | $50 | $50 |

**Worked example.** Rent is $900. The tenant pays after the grace period ends. 5% of $900 is $45. The daily limit can reach $50. The lesser number is $45, so the fee can be at most $45, and less if the daily count is lower.

**Subsidized rent.** If a government or charitable program pays part of the rent, the 5% applies to the tenant's share only. Say a voucher covers $1,100 of a $1,500 rent and the tenant owes $400. The cap is 5% of $400, which is $20. Section 8 owners see this case often. Our [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator) shows how the tenant share is set.

**Watch for:** the statute does not say how to count the days for the $5 limit. Ask a local attorney before you set a per-day rule in your lease.

## Does Connecticut law require a grace period before a late fee?

Yes. Connecticut gives every residential tenant a grace period by statute. Section 47a-15a(a) sets it at 9 days after rent is due, or 4 days for a one-week tenancy. The statute defines "grace period" as these 9-day or 4-day periods.

A landlord may charge a late fee only on a payment made after the grace period. Section 47a-4(a)(8) also bans a lease term that makes the tenant pay a late charge before the grace period ends. The same clause bans a scheme that cuts the rent if the tenant pays inside the grace period. That stops a "discount for paying on time" that works as a hidden late fee.

**New in 2026.** Public Act 25-1 of the November 2025 special session added an extension, effective 2026-01-01. If the landlord's online rental payment system prevents payment when due, the grace period is 5 days longer. The extension applies only to the week or month when the system failed. Section 47a-23(a)(1)(D) also bars a nonpayment notice to quit in that case.

**Worked dates.** Rent is due on the 1st of the month. The 9-day grace period runs through the 10th. A late fee can apply to a payment made on the 11th or later. If your payment portal was down on the 1st, the grace period runs 5 more days, through the 15th.

## Must the late fee be written in the lease in Connecticut?

Yes. A Connecticut landlord may charge a late fee only if the rental agreement contains "a valid written agreement to pay a late charge" (Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-15a(b)). No written term means no late fee.

The lease term must also stay inside the limits. Section 47a-4(a)(9) bans a lease term for a late fee larger than the 47a-15a cap. Section 47a-4(b) says a prohibited term is unenforceable. So a lease that says "$75 late fee on the 2nd" fails twice: it starts too early and it is too large.

Two 2025 laws add paperwork to check with your lease:

- **Standard summary page.** From 2026-04-01, a written lease must open with the state's standardized rental terms summary form as its first page (Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-7d(d)).
- **Ledger on request.** From 2025-07-01, a landlord must give an occupant, on request, a copy of the ledger. It shows charges, payments and any balance (Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-3a(d)).

Checklist for a Connecticut late fee clause:

1. The clause is in the written lease.
2. The fee starts only after the 9-day (or 4-day) grace period.
3. The amount is at most the lesser of $5 per day up to $50, or 5% of the late payment.
4. For subsidized tenants, the 5% uses the tenant's share.
5. The lease charges one late fee per late payment, not a fee each month the debt stays open.

## Can a Connecticut landlord charge a daily late fee?

A Connecticut landlord may not stack daily fees. Section 47a-15a(b) says the landlord "may not assess more than one late charge upon a delinquent rent payment, regardless of how long the rent remains unpaid."

The words "$5 per day" are a size limit for that one charge, not permission for a new fee each day. The charge also stops at $50 and at 5% of the late payment, whichever is less. A clause like "$10 per day until paid" breaks the cap and the one-fee rule.

## Can a Connecticut landlord evict a tenant for unpaid late fees?

A Connecticut nonpayment eviction rests on rent, not on fees. Section 47a-23(a)(1)(D) lets a landlord serve a notice to quit for "nonpayment of rent within the grace period" in section 47a-15a. The statute does not list unpaid late fees as a separate ground.

Payment order matters here. Section 47a-7d(e), added by Public Act 25-44, says all payments "shall be applied first toward the payment of rent, and then toward the payment of any other fees or charges." So a landlord cannot apply a full rent payment to the late fee first and then call the rent short. A landlord who breaks section 47a-7d owes the tenant a civil penalty of one month's rent, and the court may award attorney's fees (47a-7d(f)).

If a tenant does not pay a valid late fee, ask a local attorney about your options. Do not serve a nonpayment notice to quit for a fee alone.

## What this means for a property manager

A Connecticut late fee policy is simple to get right once the numbers are in your software. Set the fee to trigger on day 11 for rent due on the 1st. Cap it at the lesser of $50 or 5% of the late payment. Use the tenant share for voucher units. Turn off any recurring daily fee. Then check that your payment software posts payments to rent before fees.

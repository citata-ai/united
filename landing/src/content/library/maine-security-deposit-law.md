Maine's deposit rules are in Title 14, chapter 710-A, sections 6031 to 6038. I read each section on the Maine Legislature site on 2026-10-19. Two other sections touch deposits: § 6022 (cash receipts) and § 6022-A (a cap on move-in money, added by PL 2023, c. 594).

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | Rent for 2 months | § 6032 |
| Total move-in money | First month + deposit + disclosed mandatory fee | § 6022-A |
| Cash receipt | Written receipt for each cash deposit payment | § 6022 |
| Written lease return | Time in the lease, max 30 days | § 6033(2)(A) |
| Tenancy at will return | 21 days after end or surrender, whichever is later | § 6033(2)(B) |
| Normal wear and tear | Never a deduction | § 6033(1) |
| Itemized statement | With full payment of the balance | § 6033(2) |
| Missed deadline | Landlord forfeits right to keep any part | § 6033(3) |
| Wrongful retention | Double the amount, plus fees and costs | § 6034(2) |
| Account | Not commingled; beyond creditors' claims | § 6038(1) |
| Account violation | Greatest of actual damages, $500 or 1 month's rent | § 6038(2) |
| Small owner-occupied buildings | 5 units or fewer, 1 owner-occupied: exempt | § 6037(2) |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Maine?

The maximum Maine deposit is 2 months' rent. A lease or tenancy at will "may not require a security deposit equivalent to more than the rent for 2 months" (§ 6032).

| Monthly rent | Maximum deposit |
|---|---|
| $1,000 | $2,000 |
| $1,400 | $2,800 |
| $1,800 | $3,600 |

Maine also caps the total money due at the start. Under § 6022-A, a landlord, agent or broker may not require more than the first full month's rent, a deposit within § 6032, and any mandatory recurring fee properly disclosed under § 6030-J. So no extra last-month's rent on top of a 2-month deposit.

If the tenant pays any of the deposit in cash, give a written receipt at once. It must show the date, amount, payer, that it is a deposit, and the signer's printed name (§ 6022).

## How many days does a Maine landlord have to return a security deposit?

The Maine deadline depends on the type of tenancy (§ 6033(2)):

- **Written rental agreement:** within the time stated in the agreement, "not to exceed 30 days."
- **Tenancy at will:** "within 21 days after the termination of the tenancy or the surrender and acceptance of the premises, whichever occurs later."

The landlord meets the deadline by mailing the statement and payment to the tenant's last known address.

Worked timeline for a tenancy at will:

| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| 30-day notice ends the tenancy | May 31 |
| Tenant returns keys; landlord accepts | June 3 |
| Return deadline (21 days after the later date) | June 24 |

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Maine?

A Maine landlord may keep the deposit only for "actual cause" (§ 6033(2)). The statute lists examples:

- costs of storing and disposing of unclaimed property,
- unpaid rent, and
- unpaid utility charges the tenant had to pay directly to the landlord.

"Normal wear and tear" is never a deduction (§ 6033(1)). Section 6031(1) defines it as deterioration from intended use "without negligence, carelessness, accident or abuse." It does not include the cost of removing trash or other things the tenant left. If the unit was habitable at move-in, it also does not include costs, including cleaning, to make it habitable again, unless someone other than the tenant's household caused the need.

## Does a Maine landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. If the landlord keeps any part, the landlord gives "a written statement itemizing the reasons for the retention" (§ 6033(2)). The statement "must be accompanied by a full payment of the difference between the security deposit and the amount retained."

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Deposit held | $2,400 |
| Unpaid rent, final month | $1,200 |
| Haul away furniture left behind | $180 |
| Total kept | $1,380 |
| Check enclosed | $1,020 |

## Must a Maine landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

Maine requires a protected account but no interest. Section 6038(1) says a deposit "may not be treated as an asset to be commingled with the assets of the landlord." Deposits must be held in a bank account "beyond the claim of creditors of the landlord," including a foreclosing lender or a bankruptcy trustee.

- One escrow account for all tenants is allowed.
- On request, tell the tenant the bank and account number.
- On a sale, transfer the funds and an accounting by the closing, and mail the tenant notice (§ 6035).

We found no interest rule in §§ 6031 to 6038.

A court that finds a § 6038 violation awards the greatest of actual damages, $500 or 1 month's rent, plus costs, and may add attorney fees (§ 6038(2)).

## What is the penalty for a Maine landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

Maine has 3 layers.

1. **Forfeit.** Miss the deadline for the statement or refund, and the landlord "shall forfeit his right to withhold any portion" (§ 6033(3)).
2. **7-day demand.** The tenant must give at least 7 days' notice before suing. If the landlord does not return the whole deposit in those 7 days, wrongful retention is presumed (§ 6034(1)).
3. **Double damages.** Wrongful retention makes the landlord liable for "double the amount" wrongly withheld, plus reasonable attorney fees and court costs (§ 6034(2)). The landlord has the burden of proof (§ 6034(3)).

Worked example: $1,600 kept wrongly means $3,200 in damages, plus fees and costs. A lease clause that waives these rules is void (§ 6036).

Owner-occupied buildings of 5 or fewer units are exempt from chapter 710-A (§ 6037(2)).

## Maine deposit checklist

- Cap the deposit at 2 months' rent and the move-in total under § 6022-A.
- Give a written receipt for any cash.
- Hold deposits in a protected escrow account.
- Return within the lease time (max 30 days) or 21 days for a tenancy at will.
- Send an itemized statement with the balance.
- Never deduct for normal wear and tear.

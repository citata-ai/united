Two listings sit side by side. One says "8% cap rate". The other says "5% cap rate". The first looks better. It may not be. The cap rate is a ratio, and both parts of it hide choices.

This guide shows the formula, why a good cap rate moves by market, what the number leaves out, and how to run it on your own house.

## What Is the Cap Rate Formula?

Cap rate equals net operating income (NOI) divided by property value. NOI is one year of income minus vacancy and operating costs. It comes before mortgage payments and income tax.

**Cap rate = NOI ÷ value**

Use this checklist to build NOI:

- **Gross rent.** 12 months of market rent.
- **Other income.** Parking, laundry or pet fees, if any.
- **Minus vacancy.** An allowance for empty months.
- **Minus operating costs.** Property tax, insurance, repairs, management, HOA dues, utilities you pay, and a reserve for replacements.
- **Leave out.** Mortgage principal and interest, depreciation and income tax.

Depreciation stays out because it is not a cash cost. IRS Publication 527 calls it a capital expense recovered over time.

## Can You Show a Cap Rate on One House?

Yes. Here is a worked example with invented numbers for a single-family rental worth $300,000.

| Line | Yearly amount |
|---|---|
| Gross rent ($2,200 × 12) | $26,400 |
| Vacancy (1 month) | −$2,200 |
| Property tax | −$4,200 |
| Insurance | −$1,500 |
| Repairs and maintenance | −$1,800 |
| Replacement reserve | −$1,500 |
| Property management | −$2,100 |
| Net operating income | $13,100 |
| Value | $300,000 |
| Cap rate ($13,100 ÷ $300,000) | 4.4% |

Now take management out, as a self-managing owner might. NOI rises to $15,200 and the cap rate to 5.1%. Same house, two cap rates. That is why you must ask what a listing's NOI includes.

## Why Does a Good Cap Rate Differ by Market?

A good cap rate depends on risk, growth and interest rates. Buyers accept a lower cap rate where they expect steady demand and rising rents. They need a higher cap rate where risk is higher.

| Factor | Pushes cap rate down | Pushes cap rate up |
|---|---|---|
| Rent growth outlook | Strong, steady demand | Flat or falling rents |
| Vacancy risk | Low turnover, many renters | Long vacancies |
| Building age and condition | Newer, fewer big repairs | Older roof, HVAC and plumbing |
| Tenant risk | Stable payment history | More late rent or turnover |
| Interest rates | Lower rates | Higher rates |

Interest rates set the floor for many buyers. On 2026-10-16, the 10-year Treasury yield was 5.09%, per FRED data from the St. Louis Fed. A Treasury bond has no tenants and no roof. A rental with a cap rate near that yield pays you little for the extra risk and work.

Mortgage rates matter too. Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed average was 7.40% on 2026-10-15, per FRED. If your cap rate is below your loan rate, each borrowed dollar costs more than it earns. That is often called negative leverage.

Here is a simple test, not a rule:

| Your cap rate | Compared with the 10-year Treasury (5.09%) | Compared with a 7.40% mortgage |
|---|---|---|
| 4.4% | Below: you earn less than a safe bond | Below: debt lowers your cash return |
| 6.0% | Above by 0.91 points | Below: debt still lowers your cash return |
| 8.0% | Above by 2.91 points | Above: debt can raise your cash return |

## What Does Cap Rate Leave Out?

Cap rate is a one-year snapshot of income before debt. It misses several things owners care about:

- **Financing.** Your loan rate, term and down payment do not show up.
- **Appreciation.** A rising value adds return that the cap rate does not show.
- **Big repairs.** A roof due next year does not show in this year's NOI unless you reserve for it. InterNACHI estimates 20 years for 3-tab asphalt shingles and 15 to 25 years for a furnace.
- **Taxes.** Depreciation and the passive loss rules change your after-tax return.
- **Your time.** Self-managed NOI looks better, but your hours have a cost.
- **Rent changes.** Cap rate uses current rent. A below-market lease can make a good home look weak.

For a view that includes your loan, use cash-on-cash return. For the full picture over years, use a multi-year cash flow model.

## How Do I Calculate Cap Rate for My Own House?

Follow these steps:

1. **Get market rent.** Find 3 similar rentals nearby and use the middle rent.
2. **Pick a value.** Use a recent appraisal, a broker opinion or recent sales.
3. **Pull real costs.** Use last year's tax bill, your insurance premium and your repair receipts.
4. **Add vacancy.** Use at least 1 month a year unless your records show better.
5. **Add a reserve.** Set money aside for roofs, HVAC, water heaters and appliances.
6. **Price management.** Even if you self-manage, add a management line to compare fairly.
7. **Divide NOI by value.** Then compare with the Treasury and mortgage rates above.

For the management line, use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator). More owner guides are at [/guides](/guides).

## What Is a Good Cap Rate, Then?

A good cap rate is one that pays you enough for the risk of that home, in that market, at today's interest rates. Start with the Treasury test. Then check the loan test. Then ask what the number leaves out. If the cap rate still looks good after all three, the deal is worth a deeper look.

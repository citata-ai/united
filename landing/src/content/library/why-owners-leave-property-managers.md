## Which reasons for owner churn can I control?

Most reasons owners leave a property manager are in your control. Buildium asked 300 small-portfolio owners in March 2026 why they would switch. The top three reasons were all about service:

| Reason to switch manager | Share of owners | Can you control it? |
|---|---|---|
| Poor communication | 57% | Yes |
| Decline in service quality | 54% | Yes |
| Lack of transparency | 34% | Yes |
| Unexpected fees | Named, no share given | Yes |

Source: Buildium 2026 Rental Owners' Survey. Owners could give more than one reason.

The same survey found that only 23% of owners say their property manager gives excellent value. That leaves a large group of owners who are open to an offer from a competitor.

Mark Ainley, a managing broker at GC Realty & Development, listed other reasons from his own lost owners in a BiggerPockets post. Some owners could not give up control. Some could not pay for needed repairs. Some were emotionally attached to a former home. These are fit problems more than service problems. A clear owner handbook and a good intake call screen for them before you sign.

## How much does communication matter?

Communication matters more than any other single reason in owner surveys. In Ainley's interviews with 300 owners over 18 months, 207 said communication was the main reason they fired their last manager. That is 69%. Buildium's 2026 survey puts it at 57%.

The owners in Ainley's study named three problems:

1. **Slow replies.** Owners had to contact the manager 2 to 3 times to get one answer, sometimes a week later.
2. **No updates on events.** Move-outs, late owner payouts and renewals came without a message.
3. **No sign of oversight.** With no updates, owners assumed the manager did nothing, even when the work was done.

Ray Hespen of Property Meld gave a different view in 2022. His data across 190,000 investors showed that personal calls and touches mattered less than managers think (Fourandhalf, 2022). The two findings fit together. Owners do not want more calls. They want fast, short answers when something happens.

Buildium's 2026 numbers show this. Most owners prefer non-urgent updates monthly. But 43% expect a same-day reply when an issue comes up, and 42% expect a reply by the next business day.

What to do: set a written reply target, for example same business day for owner messages. Send automatic messages for move-out, renewal and payout events. Put the monthly statement and work orders in an owner portal.

## How do maintenance costs drive churn?

Maintenance costs drive churn when they surprise the owner or cut too deep into rent. Hespen named a threshold in 2022: once an owner's maintenance costs go over 12% of the rent they collect, the owner is far more likely to leave (Fourandhalf, 2022). He said this applies to small investors with one or two properties, not to institutional clients.

Hespen also found something that surprised him. Owners who got 15% more maintenance work than average were more likely to stay. More small, low-cost jobs build trust. One big surprise bill breaks it.

### Worked example: check your 12% line

| Owner | Rent collected (12 months) | Maintenance billed | Maintenance ÷ rent | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | $24,000 | $1,900 | 7.9% | Low |
| B | $18,000 | $2,300 | 12.8% | High |
| C | $30,000 | $3,500 | 11.7% | Watch |

Run this report each quarter. Call owner B before the next statement, not after. Explain the big jobs and the plan to bring costs down.

Buildium's survey adds two facts here. Maintenance is the top source of stress for 46% of owners. And 84% of owners want to approve large repairs before the work starts. A clear repair approval limit in your agreement prevents the surprise. ## How do owner sales count?

Owner sales count as churn, because the door and its fees leave either way. But tag them apart from switches. A sale is the main reason you cannot fully control.

Some sales are still linked to your service. Property Meld lists three reasons owners sell: high tenant turnover, too much work for the owner, and high maintenance costs. Its blog notes that a vacant unit can cost an owner up to $2,000 in lost rent in 30 days, before turnover costs. If your year-end owner report shows weak returns, the owner may sell.

What to do with a sale:

- **Refer** the owner to a sales agent you trust, if your license and state rules allow it.
- **Ask** the buyer if they need a manager. A tenant-occupied sale is a warm lead.
- **Log** the sale reason so your controllable churn stays a clean number.

## Which early signs show an owner will leave?

Owners show early signs months before they send a termination notice. Watch for these:

- [ ] The owner asks the same question twice. Ainley's owners used the phrase "manage the manager".
- [ ] The owner pushes back on more repair approvals than last quarter.
- [ ] Maintenance cost passes 10% of rent and moves toward 12%.
- [ ] A tenant moves out and the unit sits vacant past your normal days on market.
- [ ] The owner stops opening statements or portal messages.
- [ ] The owner gives a low score on your owner survey.

Peter Lohmann measures the last sign with Net Promoter Score (a 0 to 10 "would you recommend us" survey). In 2024 his company's client NPS was 12 on a scale of -100 to +100. He said it should be closer to 35 and called it a likely cause of his 22% unit churn (Lohmann, issue #108).

Pick 3 of these signs. Put them in one weekly report. Give one person the job to call each flagged owner within 2 business days.

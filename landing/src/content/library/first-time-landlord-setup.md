## What should I set up first?

Set up the money, the paperwork, the insurance, the vendors and the records, in that order. Each one is hard to fix after a tenant moves in.

| Area | Set up | Done before |
|---|---|---|
| Money | Separate bank account; rent collection method | You take the first deposit |
| Paperwork | Lease, addenda, disclosures, screening criteria | You list the unit |
| Insurance | Landlord policy; liability coverage | The lease starts |
| Vendors | Plumber, electrician, heating tech, handyman, locksmith | Move-in day |
| Records | Folder or software for leases, photos, receipts, notices | The first payment |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I set up the bank account and rent collection?

Open a separate account for the rental, and pick one way to collect rent. Mixing rent with personal money makes taxes and deposit disputes harder.

Some states make the separate account a legal rule for deposits:

- **Florida:** Statutes 83.49(1) says you must hold deposits in a separate account in a Florida financial institution, interest-bearing or not, or post a surety bond. You may not commingle deposit money with your other funds.
- **New York:** The Attorney General's guide says landlords, "regardless of the number of units," must treat deposits as trust funds.

Rent collection checklist:

- [ ] One rental checking account, and a separate deposit account if your state requires it.
- [ ] An online payment portal, or a written list of accepted methods.
- [ ] Due date, grace period and late fee in the lease, within state limits.
- [ ] A receipt for every payment.

There is a tax point too. IRS Publication 527 says you do not include a security deposit in income if you plan to return it. If an amount is to be used as final rent, it is advance rent and is income when you receive it. Rental income and expenses usually go on Schedule E.

This is general tax information. Ask a tax professional about your return.

## Which lease, criteria and disclosures do I need?

You need a written lease, written screening criteria, and the disclosures your federal, state and local law require. Disclosures are the part new landlords most often miss.

| Disclosure | Where it applies | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Lead-based paint disclosure, EPA pamphlet, Lead Warning Statement | Most housing built before 1978 | EPA; 40 CFR 745 Subpart F |
| Owner and manager name and address; who receives rent | California | Civil Code 1962 |
| Landlord name and address, or an agent for notices | Florida | Statutes 83.50 |
| Name and address of the record owner and any off-site manager | Texas | Property Code 92.201 |
| Flood: 100-year floodplain and flooding in past 5 years, in a separate document | Texas | Property Code 92.0135 |
| Emergency phone number | Texas | Property Code 92.020 |
| Printed tenant selection criteria with the application | Texas | Property Code 92.3515 |

Keep the signed lead disclosure for at least 3 years from the start of the lease, under 40 CFR 745.113(c).

Also check your city. Some cities require a rental license, registration or inspection before you rent.

## What insurance do I need?

Ask your agent for a landlord policy. Ask in writing whether your current homeowner policy covers a home you rent out.

Insurance checklist:

- [ ] Landlord (dwelling) policy on the building.
- [ ] Liability coverage for injuries on the property.
- [ ] Loss of rent coverage, if a fire or storm makes the unit unlivable.
- [ ] Flood coverage, if the home is in or near a flood zone.
- [ ] An umbrella policy, if your agent advises it.
- [ ] A lease clause that asks tenants to carry renters insurance.

The Texas flood notice text in 92.0135 reminds tenants that "most tenant insurance policies do not cover damages or loss incurred in a flood." Ask your agent whether your landlord policy covers flood.

## How do I plan vendors and maintenance?

Find vendors before you need them. An emergency at 2 a.m. is the wrong time to search.

1. **List core trades.** Plumber, electrician, heating and cooling tech, handyman, locksmith, cleaner.
2. **Get two names for each.** One may not answer at night.
3. **Check licenses and insurance.** Ask for proof before the first job.
4. **Set a spending limit.** For example, approve jobs up to $500 by text.
5. **Give the tenant one emergency number.** In Texas it is required (92.020).
6. **Plan routine work.** Filter changes, smoke alarm checks, gutter cleaning, a yearly walk-through.

Write a short tenant guide: how to send repair requests, what counts as an emergency, and where the water shut-off and breaker panel are.

## What records and receipts should I keep?

Keep every document that shows what you agreed, what you did and what you spent. Records win deposit disputes and support your tax return.

- [ ] Lease, addenda and signed disclosures.
- [ ] Screening criteria, applications, reports and adverse action notices.
- [ ] Move-in checklist and dated photos.
- [ ] Rent ledger and payment receipts.
- [ ] Repair requests, work orders and invoices.
- [ ] Notices sent and received, with dates and method.
- [ ] Insurance policy and claims.
- [ ] Purchase records and improvement costs, for depreciation.

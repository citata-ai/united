## What is iPropertyManagement?

iPropertyManagement.com is a free resource site for landlords, tenants and investors. It has run since 2017, according to its About page. It publishes state landlord-tenant law guides, lease templates, research pages and software reviews.

For property managers, one section matters most: "Companies". It holds city lists such as "The 40 Best Property Management Companies in Chicago". The Locations page says the site lists over 1,000 property management companies in cities across the US.

These lists are editorial. A staff writer researches each company and writes a short profile. The company does not fill in a form.

## Does AI cite iPropertyManagement?

AI cites iPropertyManagement often, but mostly in Claude. In our [AI Property Manager Index](/research/ai-property-manager-index-2026), we asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude for the best property manager in each of the 50 largest US cities on 2026-10-19. That gave 150 answers (our data, 2026-10).

| Engine | Answers | Cited ipropertymanagement.com |
|---|---|---|
| Claude | 50 | 42 |
| Gemini | 50 | 7 |
| ChatGPT | 50 | 1 |
| Total | 150 | 50 |

Only Expertise.com was cited more often, in 93 of 150 answers. Yelp tied iPropertyManagement at 50.

The engine split matters. If owners in your market use Claude, an iPropertyManagement listing is close to a must. If they use ChatGPT, Expertise.com and BBB matter more in our data. We ran each prompt once, so treat the split as a pattern, not a fixed rule.

## Is the listing free?

The listing is free in the sense that you cannot buy one. The affiliate disclosure says: "We do not accept paid advertisements or sponsorships." The site earns affiliate fees when readers sign up for software, tenant screening and similar tools through its links.

The editorial policy adds a "No pay-to-play" rule for its reviews of software and landlord tools. It does not spell out a separate rule for the city lists of property managers. We saw no "claim your profile" button, no "list your company" form and no price page in October 2026.

So be careful with any vendor that offers to "get you on iPropertyManagement" for a fee. Ask them how, in writing.

## How do reviews work there?

iPropertyManagement does not show user reviews on its city pages. Each company gets an editorial profile. The Chicago page (last updated August 7, 2026) says the team reviewed companies "based on fees, range of services, and client satisfaction" over 3 weeks.

Each Chicago entry has the same fact block, then 2–3 paragraphs about services:

| Field on the listing | What to have ready on your own site |
|---|---|
| Address and phone | The same as your Google Business Profile |
| Service types | Residential, commercial or both |
| Years in business | Year founded on your About page |
| Rentals managed | Door count, with a date |
| Better Business Bureau | Your BBB rating, if you have a BBB profile |
| Management fee | Your fee as a percent or flat amount |
| Tenant placement fee | Your leasing fee |
| Lease renewal fee | Your renewal fee |
| Other fees | Setup, inspection and admin fees |

We counted the Chicago page on 2026-10-19. It listed 40 companies. For 21 of the 40, the "Rentals Managed" field said "Unknown". Many entries showed "Contact for pricing" in place of a fee.

That is a gap you can close. An editor who cannot find your door count or fees fills in "Unknown". An AI engine that reads the page then has no number to quote for you. Publish these facts on your own site in plain text, and an editor can confirm them.

## How do you get on a city list?

You get on a city list by being easy to research, then by asking. The site gives no public form for new companies. Follow these steps:

1. **Check your city.** Open the Locations page and find your city. Read the current list.
2. **Publish your facts.** Put fees, door count, years in business and service areas on one page of your site. Use the table above as a checklist.
3. **Fix your BBB and Google profiles.** The listing shows a BBB rating, and the address and phone should match Google.
4. **Ask by contact form.** Use the Contact page. Name your city, link your fee page and ask the editors to consider your company at the next update.
5. **No city page yet?** The Locations page has a "Request A City" link for this.
6. **Check after the next update.** Each page shows a "Last Updated" date. The editorial policy says each article gets a review at least once a year.

Keep the message short and factual. Clear, checkable facts make the editor's job easier, so give them the link to those facts.

## How do you fix wrong data?

You fix wrong data through the site's Corrections process. The Corrections page asks for 3 things:

- **The page URL** where the error is.
- **A short description** of the error.
- **Supporting details**, such as a link to your fee page or a document.

Send it through the Contact page or the email address on the Corrections page. The page says the team verifies each report, corrects confirmed errors and updates the date.

Look for these common errors: an old office address, an old fee, an "Unknown" door count, an old BBB rating or a closed second office.

This step matters beyond one site. AI engines repeat what they read. An old fee on iPropertyManagement can show up in a Claude answer about your city. Fix it at the source.

## What is the iPropertyManagement checklist?

Use this checklist once now and once after each fee change:

- [ ] Your city has a list on iPropertyManagement, or you requested one.
- [ ] Your company is on the list, or you asked the editors by contact form.
- [ ] Address and phone match Google Business Profile.
- [ ] Fees on the listing match your published fee page.
- [ ] Door count is a number, not "Unknown".
- [ ] BBB rating is current.
- [ ] Any wrong fact sent through the Corrections process, with a proof link.
- [ ] Date of the next check written in your listings sheet.

No one can promise a spot on an editorial list. Clear public facts and a polite request give you the best chance.

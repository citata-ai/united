## South Dakota security deposit rules at a glance

South Dakota puts its deposit rules in two short sections of chapter 43-32. We read both on the South Dakota Legislature site on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | 1 month's rent | SDCL 43-32-6.1 |
| Larger deposit | Only by agreement, when special conditions put the unit at risk | SDCL 43-32-6.1 |
| Return the deposit or send written reasons | 21 days after the tenancy ends and you get the tenant's address | SDCL 43-32-24 |
| Itemized accounting, if the tenant asks | 45 days after the tenancy ends | SDCL 43-32-24 |
| Allowed deductions | Unpaid rent, other money due under an agreement, damage beyond ordinary wear and tear | SDCL 43-32-24 |
| Penalty for breaking the rule | Loss of the right to keep any part of the deposit | SDCL 43-32-24 |
| Penalty for bad faith | Punitive damages up to $200 | SDCL 43-32-24 |

## What is the maximum security deposit in South Dakota?

The maximum South Dakota security deposit is one month's rent. SDCL 43-32-6.1 says a residential landlord "may not demand or receive" a deposit above one month's rent.

The statute has one exception. You and the tenant may agree to a larger deposit "where special conditions pose a danger to maintenance of the premises." The statute does not list those conditions. If you rely on the exception, write the special condition into the lease and get the tenant's signature.

The cap covers any deposit that secures the lease, "however denominated." So the label does not matter. A deposit called a "pet deposit" or a "cleaning deposit" counts toward the one-month cap when its job is to secure the lease.

## How many days does a South Dakota landlord have to return a deposit?

A South Dakota landlord has 21 days to return the deposit or send a written statement of the reasons for keeping part of it. The clock starts after two events: the tenancy ends, and you receive the tenant's mailing address or delivery instructions (SDCL 43-32-24).

This deadline is new. Senate Bill 4 (2026 Session Laws, chapter 179), signed on February 11, 2026, changed "two weeks" to "twenty-one days." The act names no start date. Under SDCL 2-14-16, an act from a regular session with no start date takes effect on July 1 after it passes. So the 21-day rule applies from July 1, 2026.

Watch for: the address. If the tenant leaves no forwarding address, the 21 days do not start. Ask for the address in your move-out notice and keep the reply.

## What can a South Dakota landlord deduct from a deposit?

A South Dakota landlord may keep only the amounts "reasonably necessary" for three things (SDCL 43-32-24):

1. **Unpaid rent.**
2. **Other money the tenant owes under an agreement**, for example a fee the lease names.
3. **Repairs that restore the unit** to its condition at the start of the tenancy, with ordinary wear and tear excepted.

The statute does not define ordinary wear and tear. Move-in and move-out photos are your best proof of what changed. Date each photo.

### Worked example: a $1,200 deposit

Here is a sample move-out. The numbers are an example, not market data.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Monthly rent | $1,200 |
| Deposit taken (the cap is 1 month's rent) | $1,200 |
| Unpaid rent for the last half month | −$600 |
| Repair a hole in a wall (tenant damage) | −$180 |
| Repaint faded walls after 3 years (ordinary wear) | $0, not deductible |
| Amount to return within 21 days | $420 |

Send the $420 with a written statement. Name each deduction and its specific reason. Keep the repair invoice: if the tenant asks, you must give an itemized accounting within 45 days.

## Does a South Dakota landlord have to itemize deductions?

A South Dakota landlord must send a written statement that shows "the specific reason" for each amount kept, within the 21 days. A full itemized accounting is due only when the tenant asks for it. You must give it within 45 days after the tenancy ends (SDCL 43-32-24).

A good practice is to itemize at once, in the first statement. Then one letter meets both rules.

Use this move-out checklist:

- [ ] Ask for the tenant's mailing address or delivery instructions in writing.
- [ ] Inspect the unit and take dated photos.
- [ ] Compare the photos with the move-in record.
- [ ] List each deduction with its specific reason and amount.
- [ ] Remove any charge for ordinary wear and tear.
- [ ] Send the balance and the statement within 21 days of the later event: move-out or the address.
- [ ] Keep invoices for 45 days or more, for an itemized accounting request.

## Must a South Dakota landlord pay interest or use a separate account?

South Dakota's deposit sections set no interest rule. SDCL 43-32-6.1 and 43-32-24 do not tell a landlord to pay interest or to hold the deposit in a separate account. No section title in chapter 43-32 names such a rule.

Your lease can still promise interest or a separate account. If it does, the lease binds you.

## What is the penalty for keeping a deposit wrongly?

A South Dakota landlord who does not follow SDCL 43-32-24 "forfeits all rights to withhold any portion of the deposit." So a late or missing statement can cost you every deduction, even a fair one.

Bad faith adds a second penalty. Bad faith retention, or a bad faith failure to send the statement or the itemized accounting, "subjects the lessor to punitive damages not to exceed two hundred dollars" (SDCL 43-32-24).

| Mistake | What you can lose |
|---|---|
| Statement sent on day 25 | The right to keep any part of the deposit |
| No reason given for a deduction | The right to keep any part of the deposit |
| Deposit kept in bad faith | The whole deposit, plus up to $200 in punitive damages |

## What about items a tenant leaves behind?

South Dakota has separate rules for a tenant's property left in the unit. Property worth $500 or less, left for 10 days after the tenant quits the unit, is presumed abandoned, and you may dispose of it (SDCL 43-32-25). Property worth more than $500 must be stored. After 30 days or more of storage, you may treat it as abandoned (SDCL 43-32-26).

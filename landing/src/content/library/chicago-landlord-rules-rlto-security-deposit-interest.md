## What does the Chicago RLTO require from landlords?

The Chicago RLTO requires you to follow one set of city rules for deposits, entry, disclosure, notice and lease terms. The City Council passed the ordinance in 1986, and it is Chapter 5-12 of the Municipal Code (City of Chicago Department of Housing). A lease term that breaks the RLTO does not hold.

Here are the core duties, with the section for each one:

| Duty | Rule | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Give the RLTO summary | Attach it to each new lease and each renewal. Give it with an oral lease too. | 5-12-170 |
| Name the owner and agent | In writing, at or before move-in: name, address and phone of the owner or manager, and of a person who takes legal notices. | 5-12-090 |
| Give notice to enter | At least 2 days, except for an emergency or an urgent repair elsewhere in the building. | 5-12-050 |
| Hold the deposit apart | A federally insured, interest-bearing account at a bank in Illinois. Never mix it with your own money. | 5-12-080(a) |
| Give a deposit receipt | Amount, date, who took it, the unit, and a signature. | 5-12-080(b) |
| Pay deposit interest | Each year, within 30 days after each 12-month period. | 5-12-080(c) |
| Return the deposit | Within 45 days after move-out. | 5-12-080(d) |
| Give notice before a lease ends | 30, 60 or 120 days, by length of tenancy. | 5-12-130(j) |
| Cap the late fee | $10 for the first $500 of rent, plus 5% above $500. | 5-12-140(h) |

The penalties are real. If you skip the summary, the tenant can end the lease with 30 days' written notice and win $100 (5-12-170). If you skip the owner disclosure and do not fix it after notice, the tenant can recover 1 month's rent or actual damages, whichever is more (5-12-090). If you try to enforce a banned lease term, the tenant can recover 2 months' rent (5-12-140).

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How does Chicago security deposit interest work each year?

Chicago security deposit interest is owed on any deposit or prepaid rent you hold for more than 6 months. Section 5-12-080(c) says interest runs from the first day of the lease term. You pay it within 30 days after the end of each 12-month rental period, in cash or as a credit against rent.

The rate comes from the City Comptroller. Under Section 5-12-081, the Comptroller checks the rates on savings accounts, money market accounts and 6-month CDs at the commercial bank with the most branches in Chicago. The Comptroller then announces the average on the first business day of each year. That rate applies to leases made or renewed after the announcement.

For 2026, the rate is 0.01%. The Chicago Association of Apartment Professionals (CAAPTS) reported this on 2026-01-06, and Domu reports the same rate for every year from 2017 through 2025. We could not open the city's own rate PDF (the server refused the request), so we cite those two sources.

Worked example:

1. **Deposit:** $1,780, about one month at the FY2027 HUD fair market rent for a 1-bedroom in the Chicago area ($1,780).
2. **Rate:** 0.01% for a lease signed in 2026.
3. **Interest for 12 months:** $1,780 × 0.0001 = $0.18.
4. **When you pay:** within 30 days after month 12, as cash or a rent credit.

The amount is tiny. The penalty for a mistake is not, as the next sections show. Do not confuse the city rate with the state rate. The Illinois Security Deposit Interest Act (765 ILCS 715) covers buildings with 25 or more units, and IDFPR set its 2026 rate at 0.005% (IDFPR, 2026-01-05).

## Which Chicago rentals are exempt from the RLTO?

Chicago rentals in owner-occupied buildings with 6 units or fewer are the main exemption. Section 5-12-020 lists every excluded type:

- **Owner-occupied, 6 units or fewer.** But the notice rules in 5-12-130(j) and the lockout ban in 5-12-160 still apply.
- **Hotels, motels, inns, B&Bs, rooming and boarding houses,** until a tenant stays 32 or more days in a row and pays monthly rent.
- **Hospitals, shelters, care homes, school dormitories and most student housing** run by a school.
- **A unit a buyer or seller occupies** under a purchase contract, before or after the title transfer.
- **A unit for an employee** whose right to live there depends on the job.
- **A co-op unit** occupied by the holder of a proprietary lease.

Watch for: the exemption fails if you write a lease to avoid the RLTO. Section 5-12-020 says so in its first sentence. If you move out of your 2-flat and rent both units, the building is no longer owner-occupied, and the full RLTO applies.

## What does the Chicago Fair Notice rule require before a lease ends?

The Chicago Fair Notice rule requires written notice before you end a periodic tenancy, refuse to renew a fixed-term lease, or raise the rent. It lives in Section 5-12-130(j). The City Council amended the RLTO on 2020-07-22 so that 5-12-130(j) also covers owner-occupied buildings with 6 units or fewer (5-12-020).

| Length of tenancy | Written notice before the end date |
|---|---|
| Less than 6 months | 30 days |
| 6 months to 3 years | 60 days |
| More than 3 years | 120 days |

What happens if your notice is late or defective: the tenant may stay up to 60 days after the date you give proper written notice. The lease terms during that time stay the same as in the month before the notice. So a late rent-increase notice can delay the increase by up to 2 months.

What to do: put each lease end date in a calendar 130 days ahead. Then send the notice at 120, 60 or 30 days, based on how long the tenant has lived there.

This rule does not apply when you end the lease for nonpayment or another breach under 5-12-130(a), (b) or (d).

## What penalties does a Chicago landlord face for deposit mistakes?

A Chicago landlord who breaks any deposit rule in Section 5-12-080(a) to (e) owes the tenant 2 times the security deposit plus interest (5-12-080(f)). The tenant can also claim other damages under the RLTO.

The rule is strict. A missing receipt, a deposit in a non-Illinois bank, a missing bank name in the lease, or a late return can each trigger it. Missing the receipt also gives the tenant the right to the immediate return of the deposit (5-12-080(b)).

Worked example for a $1,780 deposit:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Deposit | $1,780 |
| Damages at 2 times the deposit | $3,560 |
| Plus interest at 0.01% for 1 year | $0.18 |
| Total before other damages | $3,560.18 |

There is one safe harbor. If you pay interest on time but the amount is short, the tenant must first send written notice. You then have 14 days to pay the correct interest plus $50, or to send a written explanation of your math (5-12-080(f)(2)).

Deposit checklist for each Chicago lease:

- [ ] Bank name and address written in the lease (or sent within 14 days for an oral lease).
- [ ] Deposit moved to a separate Illinois account within 5 business days if it came with first month's rent.
- [ ] Signed receipt given the day you take the deposit.
- [ ] Current RLTO summary and the deposit interest summary attached.
- [ ] Interest paid within 30 days after each 12-month period.
- [ ] Itemized damage statement with costs mailed within 30 days of move-out.
- [ ] Balance returned within 45 days of move-out.
- [ ] Tenants notified within 14 days if you sell the building and transfer deposits.

## Where do Chicago owners go from here?

Chicago owners who rent to voucher holders also follow HUD and Chicago Housing Authority rules on top of the RLTO.

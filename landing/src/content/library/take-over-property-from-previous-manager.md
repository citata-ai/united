## Which files and keys must I collect from the previous manager?

You must collect every document that proves what each tenant owes and what the owner holds. A missing file becomes your problem on day 1. Ask the owner to sign your management agreement first, so the old manager knows you act for the owner.

Send the old manager one written request with this list. Ask for a delivery date.

| Item | Why you need it | Check on receipt |
|---|---|---|
| Signed lease and all addenda | It sets rent, term, fees and rules | Names, dates and rent match the ledger |
| Move-in inspection and photos | It decides deposit deductions later | Date and tenant signature are present |
| Tenant ledger since move-in | It shows every charge and payment | Balance matches the old manager's statement |
| Deposit amount, location and interest | You will hold or account for it | Amount matches the lease |
| Prepaid rent list | You must credit it, not bill it again | Amount matches the ledger |
| Open work orders | Repairs in progress stay your duty | Vendor, status and cost are clear |
| Vendor, warranty and utility details | You need to pay and call them | Account numbers and contacts |
| Keys, fobs, garage and lock codes | Access for repairs and turnover | Count and label each key |
| Owner year-to-date report | You need it for the year-end tax forms | Totals match bank deposits |
| Notices served and legal files | A notice in progress has deadlines | Dates and copies of service |

The year-end report matters more than it looks. The IRS instructions for Form 1099-MISC say the property manager must use that form to report rent paid over to the owner. In a mid-year switch, two managers paid the owner in the same year. Ask a tax professional how each of you reports your part.

## How do I transfer security deposits from the old manager?

Security deposits move with a written accounting per tenant, and you sign a receipt only for the money you got. The rules differ by state. Here are three examples from statute text.

| State | What the statute says | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Florida | On a sale or a change of rental agent, deposits and advance rent go to the new owner or agent, with earned interest and an accounting per tenant. After transfer and a written receipt, the old holder is free of the duty to hold the money. A new owner or agent is presumed to have received the deposit, but only up to 1 month's rent. | Fla. Stat. 83.49(7) |
| Illinois | On a sale or transfer of the property, the transferee is liable to the tenant for the deposit, with statutory interest. The transferor stays jointly and severally liable. | 765 ILCS 710/1.1 |
| Texas | When an owner's interest ends, the new owner is liable for deposits from the date it takes title. The new owner must give each tenant a signed statement with the exact deposit amount. The former owner stays liable until the new owner gets the deposit or assumes the liability. | Tex. Prop. Code 92.105 |

If you are a broker in Texas, the trust money rule also applies. 22 TAC 535.146 says a broker must deposit trust money by the close of business of the second working day after receipt. Trust money includes rent and security deposits. Your state may have a different deadline.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What notice do tenants get when a new manager takes over?

Each tenant gets a written notice before the next rent due date. The notice stops rent from going to the old manager. Send it by the methods your lease allows, and keep a copy in each tenant file.

Put these points in the notice:

- **Who you are.** Company name, license number if your state requires it, phone and email.
- **The start date.** The first day you manage the property.
- **How to pay rent.** The new portal, address or account, and the first month it applies.
- **Where the deposit is.** The amount you received and where you hold it.
- **How to ask for repairs.** The new request channel and the emergency line.

Florida adds a deadline for some landlords. Under Section 83.49(2), a landlord who changes the manner or place of holding a deposit must tell the tenant within 30 days. That subsection does not apply to a landlord with fewer than 5 units. Texas requires the new owner's signed deposit statement in Section 92.105(b), as shown above.

Write the notice in plain words. Treat every tenant the same way. Do not change terms of an active lease in the notice.

## How do I check the rent ledger before I accept the account?

You check the ledger by matching three numbers per tenant: the lease rent, the ledger balance and the deposit held. Any gap goes on a written exception list before you sign the receipt.

Worked example, one tenant:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Rent in lease | $1,450 per month |
| Ledger balance on old manager's report | $0 |
| Payments seen in the last 3 months | $1,450, $1,450, $725 |
| Real balance owed | $725 |
| Deposit in lease | $1,450 |
| Deposit funds received from old manager | $1,000 |
| Deposit gap | $450 |

In this example, the old report hides a $725 short payment and a $450 deposit gap. Neither is your error. Put both on the exception list. Send the list to the owner and the old manager the same day.

Use this 10-step order for the whole handover:

1. Get the signed management agreement from the owner.
2. Send the written file request to the old manager.
3. Set a cutover date, best on the 1st of a month.
4. Receive leases, ledgers and keys. Count and label each key.
5. Match lease rent, ledger balance and deposit for each tenant.
6. Write the exception list and send it to both parties.
7. Receive deposit funds. Sign a receipt for the exact amount.
8. Put the funds in your trust account within your state's deadline.
9. Send the tenant notice before the next rent due date.
10. Set up the property, tenants and owner in your software.

Rentvine's case study on Rosenbaum Realty Group shows why the cutover date matters. That company moved its old units and a newly acquired 300-unit portfolio to new software on the same date, October 1. A clean date gives every ledger one start point.

## What if the old manager will not cooperate?

Escalate in writing, in steps, and keep the owner in the lead. The old manager's contract is with the owner, not with you.

1. **Send a written demand.** Quote the termination clause of the old agreement and list each missing item.
2. **Ask the owner to send the same demand.** The owner has the contract right to the files and funds.
3. **Give a short deadline.** For example, 10 business days, and say what happens next.
4. **Rebuild what you can.** Get lease copies from tenants and a payment history from bank records.
5. **Escalate.** The owner can contact the state real estate regulator or an attorney.

In Texas, 22 TAC 535.146(d)(2) says a broker must pay trust money to the party entitled to it within 30 days of a written demand. Rules in other states differ. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

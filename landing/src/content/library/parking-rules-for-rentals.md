## How do I assign spaces?

Assign spaces through a site map and a written record that match the lease. Give each space a stable identifier. Check that signs, pavement marks and the resident file use the same identifier. A mismatch can send enforcement to the wrong car.

State whether the parking right attaches to the unit, tenant or vehicle. Explain guest parking and temporary replacement cars. Record any separate fee and the rules for ending the parking arrangement. Review those terms with local counsel before you use them.

Texas Property Code 92.0131 requires covered multiunit landlords to provide applicable parking and towing rules before lease execution. It also addresses signed records and changes during the lease. Follow the section's actual requirements rather than giving a tenant an unsigned map after move-in.

Build a process for reasonable accommodation requests. The Fair Housing Act, in 42 USC 3604(f)(3)(B), requires reasonable accommodations in covered housing when necessary under its terms. Do not let a first-come parking policy prevent that review. Route requests to a trained person and keep the decision record private.

## Can I tow a tenant's car?

Towing depends on state and local law, the actual violation and the required process. Do not treat an owner request as enough. Have counsel and your towing provider review the site before enforcement begins.

Texas TDLR publishes rules for tow-away signs, including placement at parking entrances and required sign content. A generic sign bought online may not meet the rule. Check each entrance on the actual property. Keep dated photographs of the approved setup.

Use a documented review before a nonemergency tow. Verify the location, vehicle and applicable rule. Check for a temporary permit or an accommodation record. Save the facts that support the action. Do not ask a towing driver to guess what the lease means.

| Before enforcement | Question to resolve | Record |
|---|---|---|
| Identify the vehicle | Is this the correct car and plate? | Current photo and staff note |
| Confirm the space | Is the car in a restricted location? | Site map and location photo |
| Check permission | Is there an exception or temporary approval? | Permit or approval log |
| Confirm process | Are required signs and notices in place? | Reviewed checklist |
| Authorize | Who has lawful authority to request removal? | Signed action record |

Set a contact for disputed tows. Preserve the records even if staff later discover a mistake. Correct an error promptly through the proper process. Do not change a log to hide an unsupported action.

## Can I ban boats and RVs?

A boat or RV restriction should define the vehicle and the affected area in an enforceable written rule. Review local rules and the lease first. Avoid a broad sentence that staff interpret differently for similar vehicles.

Describe permitted use in practical terms. Address trailer storage, oversized vehicles, vehicle repair and blocked access. Use measurements only when they come from the actual site or an applicable rule. Do not invent a national size limit for a rental lot.

Check the turning area, emergency access and neighboring spaces before setting restrictions. Ask the relevant local authority about safety requirements. Record which limits come from law and which are owner choices. That distinction matters when a resident asks for an exception.

Handle requests individually where the law requires it. A mobility-related vehicle or equipment question may need an accommodation review. Do not dismiss the request because it does not fit your normal vehicle list. Keep the discussion focused on the need and the lawful options.

For existing tenants, confirm how changes can take effect. A newly posted sign does not resolve every lease issue. Give staff a version-controlled rule sheet so old and new rules do not get mixed.

## What about EV charging?

EV charging requires a separate review of tenant rights, electrical capacity and installation terms. Do not add a blanket charger ban without checking the law. A parking policy and an electrical work agreement answer different questions.

Santa Monica's official guidance explains California's conditional approval requirement under Civil Code 1947.6. It describes tenant cost duties and exceptions. That California example differs from Texas parking-notice rules. Neither creates a national rule for all properties.

Ask for a written request with the proposed location and equipment. Have a qualified electrician assess the work. Check permits, service capacity and how electricity will be measured. Record maintenance, damage, removal and restoration terms in the approved agreement.

Keep ordinary outlets separate from an approved charging station plan. A resident's extension cord across a path creates a site issue that needs review. Give staff a clear contact for that report. Do not encourage improvised electrical work while a formal request is pending.

Explain how charging spaces work after installation. State who may park there and whether the space remains assigned. Keep billing and access terms clear enough for a new manager to apply.

## How should I maintain the parking policy?

Maintain parking rules as an operating record, with a named owner and a change log. Review the map whenever construction, repainting or space reassignment changes the site. Check that staff can identify each space from the ground.

Give residents an accessible way to register temporary needs and report blocked spaces. Tell staff which reports need urgent review. A blocked route can need a different response from an expired visitor permit. Avoid making every issue a tow request.

Track recurring disputes by location and cause. A poorly marked space may need clearer paint. An unclear guest rule may need a better notice. Fix the underlying process before adding more penalties.

Ask vendors to use approved work areas. A contractor truck can block residents just as easily as a tenant car. Include the parking instructions in vendor work orders and give residents advance information when lawful work changes access.

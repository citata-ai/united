## What does a property management fee cover?

A property management fee pays the manager to run the rental each month. It is the main recurring charge in a management agreement. Most agreements include these tasks in the monthly fee:

- Rent collection and late rent follow-up
- Tenant calls and requests
- Repair coordination with vendors
- Paying property bills from the owner's funds
- Monthly owner statements and payouts
- Lease enforcement and notices
- Basic records and year-end tax forms

What is in and out changes from one manager to the next. The agreement is the only list that counts. Read it before you compare prices.

## Is the management fee a percent of rent collected or rent due?

The management fee can be charged on rental value, rent due or rent collected. All Property Management lists all 3 options. The base matters as much as the rate.

| Fee base | What the owner pays when a tenant misses a $2,000 month at 8% | Who carries the risk |
|---|---|---|
| Rent collected | $0 | Manager shares the loss |
| Rent due | $160 | Owner pays a fee on money not received |
| Rental value (even when vacant) | $160 | Owner pays a fee on an empty unit |

All Property Management notes that a manager paid only on rent collected has a stronger reason to keep rent coming in. If your agreement says "rent due" or "monthly rental value", ask why.

## What is the typical management fee range?

The typical range is 8% to 12% of monthly rent, according to All Property Management. iPropertyManagement's sample of 722 branches in 80 metro areas (updated 2026-09-23) found:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Average percent fee | 8.49% of monthly rent |
| Range of percent fees | 3.75% to 14.0% |
| Average flat fee | $101.04 per unit per month |
| Range of flat fees | $49 to $250 per unit per month |

Worked example for a $1,800 rent, 12 months collected:

| Fee | Calculation | Per year |
|---|---|---|
| 8.49% average | 8.49% × $1,800 × 12 | $1,833.84 |
| 10% | 10% × $1,800 × 12 | $2,160.00 |
| Flat $101.04 | $101.04 × 12 | $1,212.48 |

The rate changes with the market, the size of the portfolio and the services included. Lower is not always cheaper once the extras are added.

## What is a flat-fee management model?

A flat-fee manager charges a fixed dollar amount per unit each month, not a percent of rent. The fee does not move when the rent moves.

The flat fee and the percent fee cost the same at one rent level. Find it with one division: flat fee ÷ percent rate.

- Break-even rent = $101.04 ÷ 8.49% = $1,190 (rounded)

Above about $1,190 of rent, the average flat fee costs less than the average percent fee. Below it, the percent fee costs less. Use your real quotes, not the averages, to decide.

| Model | Good for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Percent of rent | Lower rents; manager shares the rent risk if the base is rent collected | Cost grows with each rent increase |
| Flat fee per unit | Higher rents; easy to budget | Fee due even when rent is late, if the agreement says so |

## What services cost extra beyond the management fee?

Several services usually cost extra. They can add more to the yearly cost than the monthly fee itself in a year with a new tenant.

| Extra fee | Typical level | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Leasing (tenant placement) fee | 50% to 100% of one month's rent; average 70.6% with management | All Property Management; iPropertyManagement |
| Lease renewal fee | Average $231.77 (flat) | iPropertyManagement |
| Setup fee | Average $185.24 per contract | iPropertyManagement |
| Inspection fee | Average $106.72 each | iPropertyManagement |
| Eviction | $200 to $500 plus court costs | All Property Management |
| Vacancy fee | One month's rent, upfront, for a vacant unit | All Property Management |
| Share of late fees | 25% to 50% of late fees collected | All Property Management |
| Maintenance reserve | Typically $250 per unit (held, not a fee) | iPropertyManagement |

Worked example for one year on a $1,800 rent with one new tenant. The rates are an example:

| Line | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Management fee | 8% × $1,800 × 11 months collected | $1,584.00 |
| Leasing fee | 70% × $1,800 | $1,260.00 |
| Inspection | 1 × $100 | $100.00 |
| **Total** | | **$2,944.00** |
| Effective rate | $2,944 ÷ ($1,800 × 11) | 14.9% of rent collected |

So an "8% manager" costs about 14.9% of rent collected in this year. In a year with a renewal instead of a new tenant, the total drops.

## How do you compare management fees between managers?

Compare the full yearly cost, on the same rent, for the same year. Ask each manager for the fee list in writing. Then:

1. **Put each fee in one table** with its base.
2. **Add a year with a new tenant** and a year with a renewal.
3. **Divide the total by rent collected** to get the effective rate.
4. **Check what is included** in the monthly fee.

The [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) does this math for you. For a deeper look at the contract behind the fee, see [/guides](/guides).

The IRS lets owners deduct operating expenses of a rental on Schedule E, and management fees are one of them. This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current rules or ask a tax professional.

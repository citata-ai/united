## Which upgrades raise rent the most?

The upgrades that raise rent most are the ones local renters search for and nearby homes lack. Check 10 listings of similar homes nearby. Note what the higher-priced ones have that yours does not. That gap is your upgrade list.

Common upgrades, with what to check before you spend:

| Upgrade | Why renters pay for it | Check before you spend |
|---------|------------------------|------------------------|
| In-unit washer and dryer | Saves trips and time | Hookups, space, water and power |
| Central air or mini-splits | Comfort in hot months | Electric capacity, local climate |
| Updated kitchen surfaces and appliances | Photos drive showings | Cost vs rent gain over 5 years |
| Fenced yard | Wanted by pet owners and families | HOA rules, fence permit |
| Off-street parking or a garage | Scarce in dense areas | Zoning and HOA rules |
| Smart lock and video doorbell | Easier entry, fewer lockouts | Privacy rules for cameras |
| Fast internet wiring | Remote work | Provider coverage |
| Energy-saving windows, insulation | Lower bills if tenant pays utilities | Payback in years |

A simple payback test: divide the cost by the extra monthly rent. A $3,600 washer and dryer install that adds $75 a month pays back in 48 months. If you plan to sell in 2 years, skip it.

Tax note: many upgrades are improvements that you depreciate, not repairs you deduct at once. Ask a tax professional.

## Can I add fees (pets, parking, storage)?

Yes, in many places, if the fee is in the lease and local law allows it. Fees for optional services are the easiest to justify, because the tenant chooses them.

| Fee | Typical basis | Watch for |
|-----|---------------|-----------|
| Pet rent or pet fee | Per pet, monthly or one time | Assistance animals are not pets; no pet fee for them |
| Parking space | Per space, monthly | Only for extra or reserved spaces |
| Storage unit or garage | Monthly | Clear access rules |
| Furnished option | Monthly | More wear, more insurance |
| Utility package (internet, trash) | Monthly | Billing rules differ by state |
| Early move-in or flexible lease term | One time or monthly | Write the terms clearly |

Fee rules differ by state and city. Some places cap fees or ban certain ones. Check your state's landlord-tenant law and any city ordinance before you add a fee.

Fair Housing: apply each fee the same way to every tenant. Do not set fees or terms that treat applicants differently by a protected class.

## How does a renewal rent increase compare with turnover cost?

A modest renewal increase almost always beats a large increase that makes the tenant leave. Run the numbers before you send the renewal offer.

Turnover cost, example on a $2,000 home:

| Turnover cost | Amount | Source of the rate |
|---------------|--------|--------------------|
| 3 weeks vacant | $1,385 | Example |
| Leasing fee at 70.6% of one month | $1,412 | iPropertyManagement 2026 average |
| Make-ready: paint, clean, repairs | $1,200 | Example |
| Total | $3,997 | |

Turnover is getting more expensive. Baselane reports that NAA found turnover costs rose 17.5% year over year in 2024.

Two renewal choices for the same tenant, year 1, example numbers:

| Line | Offer A: 3% at renewal, tenant stays | Offer B: 8% at renewal, tenant leaves |
|------|--------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| New rent | $2,060 | $2,160 |
| Months of rent in year 1 | 12 | About 11.3 (3 weeks empty) |
| Rent collected | $24,720 | $24,425 |
| Renewal fee (iPropertyManagement flat average) | −$232 | $0 |
| Leasing fee | $0 | −$1,412 |
| Make-ready | $0 | −$1,200 |
| Net for the year | $24,488 | $21,813 |

Offer A wins by about $2,675 in year 1 in this example. Offer B gets the higher rent in later years, but it takes years to make up the gap. And the new tenant might leave sooner than the old one.

For context on vacancy, the Census Bureau reported a national rental vacancy rate of 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026.

## What is a reasonable rent increase?

A reasonable increase brings you closer to market rent, keeps a good tenant, and follows the law. There is no single national number.

Benchmarks:

- **Inflation.** The BLS reported that the shelter index rose 3.2% over the 12 months to September 2026. The rent index rose 0.2% in September alone.
- **Market.** Rent on 3 to 5 similar homes leased in the last 90 days.
- **Your tenant.** On-time payment and care of the home are worth something.

State limits, 2 examples:

| State | Rule | Source |
|-------|------|--------|
| Oregon | No increase in the first year; at least 90 days' written notice; no more than one increase in any 12 months; no more than the state maximum | ORS 90.323 |
| Oregon, the number | The Office of Economic Analysis set the 2026 maximum at 9.5%; the formula is the lesser of 7% plus CPI or 10% | Oregon OEA |
| Washington | No increase in the first 12 months; no more than 7% plus CPI or 10%, whichever is less, in any 12-month period | RCW 59.18.700 |

Many other states and cities have their own rules, including some local rent control. Exemptions also apply in some states. Check your own state and city before you send a notice.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## A checklist to raise income and keep tenants

- [ ] Check 10 nearby listings once a year for the rent and features gap.
- [ ] Plan small yearly increases instead of large jumps.
- [ ] Send renewal offers 90 to 120 days before lease end, or earlier if the law requires.
- [ ] Fix repair requests fast; slow repairs drive move-outs.
- [ ] Offer optional paid services: parking, storage, laundry.
- [ ] Price each upgrade with the payback test.
- [ ] Cut vacancy: list before move-out and show the home with notice.
- [ ] Compare each increase with the turnover cost table.

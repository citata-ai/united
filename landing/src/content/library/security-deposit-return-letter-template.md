## What is the deadline to return a deposit?

The deadline to return a deposit is set by state law and runs from the day the tenant moves out. Here are 4 large rental states.

| State | Deadline | Key rule | Penalty for getting it wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| New York | 14 days | Itemized statement and balance within 14 days | Landlord forfeits the right to keep any part of the deposit (GOL 7-108) |
| California | 21 days | Itemized statement, receipts, and balance within 21 days | Bad-faith retention: up to twice the deposit plus actual damages (Civ. Code 1950.5) |
| Texas | 30 days | Refund within 30 days; forwarding address rule applies | Bad faith: $100 plus 3 times the amount wrongfully withheld, plus attorney's fees (Prop. Code 92.109) |
| Florida | 15 or 30 days | 15 days if no claim; 30 days to send a notice of claim by certified mail or allowed email | Missing the 30 days forfeits the right to claim (Stat. 83.49) |

In Texas, a landlord who misses the 30-day deadline "is presumed to have acted in bad faith" (Prop. Code 92.109). In Florida, the tenant has 15 days after getting the notice of claim to object (Stat. 83.49(3)(b)).

Watch for: count calendar days, and send early. A letter sent on day 20 in California leaves room for a delivery delay.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do you itemize deductions?

You itemize deductions by listing each one on its own line: what was wrong, where, the work done, and the cost. One line per item. No "general cleaning and repairs: $900."

What you may deduct is set by statute. California limits deductions to unpaid rent, damage beyond ordinary wear and tear, cleaning to return the unit to its move-in level of cleanliness, and some other costs the lease allows (Civ. Code 1950.5(b)). New York allows unpaid rent, damage beyond normal wear and tear, unpaid utilities payable to the landlord, and moving and storage of the tenant's items (GOL 7-108). Texas lets a landlord deduct damages and charges the tenant is legally liable for, but not normal wear and tear (Prop. Code 92.104).

| Damage (often deductible) | Normal wear and tear (not deductible) |
|---|---|
| Large holes in walls | A few small nail holes |
| Pet stains or burns in carpet | Carpet worn thin in walk paths |
| Broken window or door | Loose door handle from normal use |
| Unapproved paint colors | Paint faded by sun |
| Missing blinds or appliances | Blinds faded or slightly bent |

Use photos to tie each line to the move-in report. In California, photos that support deductions must go to the tenant with the statement (Civ. Code 1950.5(h)(2)(D)).

## Must you send receipts?

You must send receipts in some states, and you should keep them in every state. California is the strictest of the 4.

- **California:** attach copies of receipts or invoices for repair and cleaning work. You do not need receipts if those deductions total $125 or less. If the work is not done or a bill has not arrived within 21 days, you may deduct a good-faith estimate, then send the final documents within 14 days of finishing or getting them (Civ. Code 1950.5(h)).
- **New York:** the statute requires an itemized statement. It does not, on its face, require receipts. The landlord bears the burden of proving the amounts are reasonable, so keep them (GOL 7-108).
- **Texas:** a written description and itemized list of all deductions. The list is not needed if the tenant owes rent and there is no dispute about the amount (Prop. Code 92.104).
- **Florida:** the notice of claim follows a set statutory form that begins "This is a notice of my intention to impose a claim for damages in the amount of" (Stat. 83.49(3)).

## Security deposit return letter template

Copy this text, fill in the brackets, and check it against your state law before use. In Florida, use the statutory notice form for a claim.

> **Security Deposit Itemized Statement and Return**
>
> Date: [date]
> To: [tenant names], [forwarding address]
> Property: [address, unit]
> Lease dates: [start] to [end]. Move-out date: [date]
>
> We received a security deposit of **$[amount]** on [date]. [Interest owed: $[amount], if required.] Here is the accounting.
>
> | # | Item and location | Reason | Cost | Proof |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | [e.g. Living room wall, 3 holes over 1 inch] | Damage beyond normal wear | $[ ] | Photo 1, invoice A |
> | 2 | [e.g. Unpaid rent, (month)] | Unpaid rent | $[ ] | Ledger |
> | 3 | [e.g. Oven not cleaned] | Cleaning to move-in level | $[ ] | Photo 2, invoice B |
>
> Total deposit (and interest): $[ ]
> Total deductions: $[ ]
> **Balance returned to you: $[ ]**, by [check number / direct deposit] on [date].
>
> [If deductions exceed the deposit:] Deductions exceed the deposit by $[ ]. Please pay this amount by [date] to [method].
>
> Copies of photos and receipts are attached [as your state requires]. If you dispute any item, contact [name] at [phone] or [email] by [date].
>
> This statement is sent under [state statute].
>
> [Name], [title], [company]
> Sent by: [mail / certified mail / email] on [date]

## What if you keep the full deposit?

If you keep the full deposit, you still send the itemized statement by the deadline. The letter is the legal record, not just a courtesy. Show each deduction, the total, and the amount the tenant still owes, if any.

Worked example: a California tenant paid a $2,000 deposit and moved out on May 1. You find $1,400 of carpet damage from a pet (invoice), $450 of unpaid rent for the last half-month, and $300 of cleaning to the move-in level (invoice). Deductions are $2,150. You send the statement with invoices and photos by May 22, keep the full $2,000, and ask for the $150 balance. Repair and cleaning deductions are over $125, so the invoices must be attached.

What to do:

1. **Check the forwarding address.** Texas ties the refund duty to it (Prop. Code 92.103 and 92.107).
2. **Use the same format for every tenant.** One template and one standard cut disputes and Fair Housing risk.
3. **Keep the file** with move-in and move-out photos side by side.

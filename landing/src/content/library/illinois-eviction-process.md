Illinois calls its eviction law the Eviction Act: Article IX of the Code of Civil Procedure, 735 ILCS 5/9-101 and on. I read each section below on the Illinois General Assembly site on 2026-10-20. Section 9-106 was last amended by Public Act 104-317, in force since January 1, 2026. The current text bars naming a minor as a defendant in an eviction complaint.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice for unpaid rent | At least 5 days after service | 9-209 |
| Partial payment after the notice | Does not void the notice if the notice has the "FULL PAYMENT" sentence | 9-209 |
| Notice for another lease breach | 10 days' notice to quit | 9-210 |
| End a week-to-week tenancy | 7 days' written notice | 9-207(a) |
| End other tenancies under 1 year (such as month to month) | 30 days' written notice | 9-207(b) |
| End a year-to-year tenancy | 60 days' written notice before the end of the year | 9-205 |
| Bona fide lease after foreclosure | At least 90 days' written notice | 9-207.5 |
| Where to file | Circuit court of the county | 9-106(a) |
| Minor named as defendant | Whole complaint dismissed and sealed | 9-106(b), (c) |
| Enforcement window for the eviction order | 120 days, unless extended | 9-117 |

## What notice must an Illinois landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

An Illinois landlord must give a written demand for the rent with at least 5 days to pay, counted from service (735 ILCS 5/9-209). The notice says the lease will end unless the tenant pays in that time. If the tenant does not pay, the landlord may consider the lease ended and file "without further notice or demand".

Add this sentence to the notice, word for word, so that a partial payment does not void it:

"Only FULL PAYMENT of the rent demanded in this notice will waive the landlord's right to terminate the lease under this notice, unless the landlord agrees in writing to continue the lease in exchange for receiving partial payment."

Collecting past rent after you file does not void the suit (9-209).

Service rules come from 9-211. Use one of these:

- Hand a copy to the tenant.
- Leave it with a person 13 or older who lives at or is in possession of the unit.
- Send it by certified or registered mail with a return receipt from the addressee.
- If no one is in actual possession, post it on the premises.

## How long does an eviction take in Illinois?

Illinois law does not set a total eviction time. The statute sets the notice period and the life of the order. Court dates depend on the county.

| Step | Period | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rent demand runs | At least 5 days after service | 9-209 |
| File the complaint | After the demand expires | 9-209, 9-106 |
| Summons and court date | Set by the clerk and court | 9-106(a) |
| Service by posting, if needed | Sheriff posts notices at least 10 days before the appearance date | 9-107 |
| Eviction order enforced by the sheriff | Within 120 days of entry, or with an extension | 9-117 |

If the tenant cannot be served in person, 9-107 allows posting and mailing, or publication and mailing, after an affidavit. In that case, the court can enter only the eviction order, not a money judgment for rent, unless the tenant later appears or is served.

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Illinois, and what does it cost?

An Illinois landlord files an eviction complaint in the circuit court for the county where the premises are (735 ILCS 5/9-106(a)). The complaint states that the landlord is entitled to possession, describes the premises, and names the defendant who unlawfully withholds possession.

Content rules in 9-106:

- A claim for rent may be joined, and the court may enter judgment for the rent found due.
- A complaint may not name a minor as a defendant, unless the minor is emancipated. A complaint that does so is dismissed in full and sealed. A minor named wilfully and wantonly may recover fees, actual damages and $1,000 in liquidated damages.
- Other claims not related to possession may not be joined, except as 9-120 allows.

Costs: filing and sheriff fees are set county by county. I did not verify a fee schedule for this article. Ask your circuit clerk and sheriff for current fees.

## Can an Illinois landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

An Illinois landlord can evict for a lease violation after a 10-day notice to quit (735 ILCS 5/9-210). The notice states the default, describes the premises, says the landlord has elected to end the lease, and gives the tenant 10 days to leave. The statute gives a sample form. The lessor or the agent signs it.

State law has no just-cause rule for periodic tenancies:

| Tenancy | Notice to end it | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Week to week | 7 days, written | 9-207(a) |
| Any term less than 1 year (for example month to month) | 30 days, written | 9-207(b) |
| Year to year | 60 days, written, before the year ends; may be given in the 4 months before that | 9-205 |
| Bona fide lease of a unit in foreclosure | 90 days, written | 9-207.5 |

Local rules can add limits. Chicago's Residential Landlord and Tenant Ordinance, chapter 5-12, has sections on landlord remedies (5-12-130) and retaliatory conduct (5-12-150). I read only the section list. If you manage in Chicago, read those sections before you serve a notice.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Illinois?

The sheriff removes the tenant in Illinois. The Eviction Act refers to the sheriff as the officer who evicts under the order, for example in the notice form in 9-117.

The eviction order has a time limit. Under 735 ILCS 5/9-117, no eviction order may be enforced more than 120 days after entry, unless the court grants an extension on the landlord's motion. The motion must include the notice text in 9-117. The court grants the extension unless the tenant shows that the tenancy was reinstated, the breach was cured or waived, a post-judgment agreement was kept, or another legal reason applies.

Watch for: if you agree to a payment plan after judgment, the 120 days still run. Track the date.

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Illinois?

The Illinois Eviction Act is the legal path to recover possession from a tenant. Under 9-102(a)(4), the person entitled to possession may be restored to it through the action when a lessee holds possession without right after the tenancy ends. The court order and the sheriff do the removal.

I did not find, in the sections I read, a statewide section that sets a penalty for a lockout or utility shutoff. Chicago has one in its ordinance: section 5-12-160 is titled "Prohibition on interruption of tenant occupancy by landlord". Do not change locks, remove doors or cut utilities to force a tenant out. Ask a local attorney before you act without a court order.

## Illinois eviction checklist

1. **Write the 5-day demand** with the exact rent due and the "FULL PAYMENT" sentence.
2. **Serve it** by a method in 9-211 and keep proof.
3. **Check the defendants.** Do not name a minor.
4. **File in circuit court** in the county of the unit.
5. **Get the order**, then deliver it to the sheriff.
6. **Track 120 days** from the order date.

## Does the PHA screen Section 8 tenants for me?

The PHA does not screen voucher tenants for you. 24 CFR 982.307(a)(1) says the PHA "has no liability or responsibility to the owner" for the family's behavior or suitability. 982.307(a)(2) says the owner "is responsible for screening and selection of the family."

The two checks are different:

| Check | Who does it | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Program eligibility | PHA | Income limits, family size, citizenship status, program history |
| Tenancy screening | Owner | Rent history, care of past units, conduct, criminal activity |
| Unit approval | PHA | Inspection, reasonable rent, lease review |

A voucher means the family is eligible for the program. It does not mean the family passed your rental criteria. You still decide, with the same rules you use for everyone.

## What may a landlord look at in a voucher applicant?

A landlord may look at the same tenancy factors for a voucher applicant as for any applicant. 24 CFR 982.307(a)(3) lists factors an owner may consider:

1. **Payment of rent and utility bills.**
2. **Care of a unit and premises.**
3. **Respect for the rights of other residents** to peaceful enjoyment.
4. **Drug-related or other criminal activity** that is a threat to the health, safety or property of others.
5. **Compliance with other essential conditions of tenancy.**

The PHA must also give you some data. Under 982.307(b)(1), you get the family's current and prior address and, if the PHA knows them, the names and addresses of the landlords there. Call those landlords. They are your best source on rent history.

## Can I use a minimum income rule for a voucher holder?

You can keep a minimum income rule, but where a source-of-income law applies, you must apply it to the tenant's share. The voucher pays part of the rent. A test on the full rent would screen out most voucher holders for a reason the voucher already solves.

Virginia is one example. Code of Virginia 36-96.3 makes it unlawful to refuse to rent, or to set different terms, because of "source of funds." Virginia REALTORS guidance dated May 14, 2024 says a landlord "must analyze the tenant's portion of the monthly rent, not the total rent."

Check your own state and city law. If no source-of-income law applies to you, the federal voucher rules still let the PHA review your criteria and your lease.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I count the voucher in an income test?

Count the voucher by taking it out of the rent before you apply your multiple. The formula:

**Income needed = (contract rent − HAP) × your multiple**

The HAP is the PHA's monthly payment, from the PHA's notice. Under 24 CFR 982.505, it is the lower of the payment standard or gross rent, minus the family's Total Tenant Payment.

A worked example, with the Virginia REALTORS figures and one more case:

| Case | Rent | Voucher pays | Tenant share | 3x test on full rent | 3x test on tenant share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Virginia REALTORS example | $1,500 | $500 | $1,000 | $4,500 | $3,000 |
| Example 2 (made-up) | $1,800 | $1,350 | $450 | $5,400 | $1,350 |

In example 2, the full-rent test asks for 4 times more income than the tenant share test. That is why the method matters.

If you do not know the HAP yet, estimate it with the [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator). Then confirm it with the PHA before you decide.

## What criteria must be the same for all applicants?

Every criterion you use must be the same for every applicant for the same unit. The Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. 3604, bars different terms based on race, color, religion, sex, familial status, national origin and disability. Many states and cities add more protected classes, such as source of income in Virginia.

Apply these the same way to all:

- Application fee and what it covers.
- Credit score floor, or no floor.
- Income multiple, on the share the applicant pays.
- Rental history period, for example 2 years.
- Criminal history policy, with the look-back period and the types of offenses.
- Number of people per bedroom, under local occupancy code.
- Order of review: first complete application, first reviewed.

Do not ask about the source of a person's disability, family plans or religion. Do not deny a person because they are a victim of domestic violence, dating violence, sexual assault or stalking. 24 CFR 982.452(b)(1) says that is not an appropriate basis for denial if the applicant otherwise qualifies.

## Written screening criteria template

Copy this template, fill in your numbers, and give it to every applicant before they pay a fee.

1. **Application.** Every adult 18 or older applies. Fee: $___ per adult, if your state allows it.
2. **Identity.** A government photo ID for each adult.
3. **Income.** Gross monthly income of at least ___ times the applicant's share of rent. For applicants with rental assistance, the share is the rent minus the assistance payment.
4. **Rental history.** ___ years of verifiable history. No eviction judgment for nonpayment in the last ___ years.
5. **Credit.** We review credit for rent and utility debts. Medical debt does not count.
6. **Criminal history.** We consider only criminal activity in the last ___ years that threatens the health, safety or property of others, as listed in our policy.
7. **Occupancy.** Up to ___ people per bedroom, under local code.
8. **Order.** We review complete applications in the order we receive them.
9. **Decision.** We tell you in writing within ___ business days. If we deny, we tell you the reason.
10. **Equal treatment.** We apply these criteria to every applicant in the same way.

Keep a copy of each decision for at least as long as your state requires. For more on the voucher process, see [Section 8 rent limits by metro](/guides/section-8-rent-limits).

## What is a good net profit margin for a property management company?

A good net profit margin for a residential property management company is 20% or more. The average in 2021 was 11%. The top 25% earned 32%.

These numbers come from the 2022 NARPM Financial Performance Guide. NARPM (the National Association of Residential Property Managers) and the finance firm ProfitCoach ran the study. 153 companies sent data straight from their property management software and QuickBooks. The study covers the years 2019 to 2021. It is the most recent public guide we could read in full. ProfitCoach showed newer benchmarks at the NARPM Broker/Owner conference in 2026 (PMAssist), but we did not find those numbers in public.

| Year | Average margin | Top 25% margin |
|---|---|---|
| 2017 | 6% | 25% |
| 2019 | 6% | 25% |
| 2020 | 8% | 30% |
| 2021 | 11% | 32% |

Source: 2022 NARPM Financial Performance Guide, "The BIG Shift" and "Game of Profit" pages.

Watch for: the study measures property management profit only. It removes maintenance and brokerage divisions. If your books mix them, your number will not compare.

## Why is the benchmark lower than the margin on my books?

The benchmark is lower because the study pays the owner a market salary. Many owners take a small salary and the rest as distributions. That makes the company look more profitable than it is.

The study asked owners what it would cost to hire someone to replace them. Then it set a pay scale by revenue. For 2021, the owner salary was $65,000 at $0 revenue, $89,000 at $500,000 and $110,000 at $1,000,000.

| 2021 measure | Average company |
|---|---|
| Margin as companies reported it | 16% |
| Margin with a market owner salary | 11% |
| Difference | 5 points |

To compare your company, do the same step:

1. **Take your net profit** for the property management division only.
2. **Add back** what you paid yourself as W-2 salary.
3. **Subtract** a market salary for your role at your revenue level.
4. **Divide** by property management revenue.

## How does margin change with door count?

Margin rises with size, but not in a straight line. Very small companies lose money once the owner gets a fair salary.

| Units under management | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–100 | -12% | -20% | -1% |
| 101–250 | 1% | 5% | 4% |
| 251–400 | 12% | 16% | 11% |
| 401–600 | 10% | 13% | 18% |
| 601–800 | 15% | 17% | 12% |
| 801–1,000 | 17% | 22% | 26% |
| 1,001–1,500 | 8% | 6% | 14% |
| 1,501+ | 20% | 15% | 11% |

Source: 2022 NARPM Financial Performance Guide, "How does size impact profitability?"

The study says 801 to 1,000 units "may be a sweet spot". It also found profitable companies at almost every size. Margin rose more steadily with revenue. Companies under $500,000 in revenue averaged 3% in 2021. Companies over $3 million averaged 24%.

What this means for you: under about 250 doors, the company mostly pays the owner a salary. The profit comes later, or from better pricing.

## Which costs eat the most margin?

Labor eats the most margin. In 2021, total labor was 55.6% of revenue for the average company and 43.1% for the most profitable 25%.

| Cost as % of revenue (2021) | Bottom 25% | Average | Top 25% |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net profit | -13.0% | 11.0% | 31.8% |
| Direct labor (owner- and tenant-facing staff) | 45.1% | 38.4% | 29.4% |
| Management labor | 23.4% | 17.2% | 13.7% |
| Payroll taxes and benefits | 5.6% | 5.5% | 4.8% |
| Facilities (office, auto, tech, phone) | 15.7% | 10.5% | 7.4% |
| Marketing | 5.2% | 3.2% | 1.9% |
| Other operating costs | 18.3% | 13.5% | 9.9% |

Source: 2022 NARPM Financial Performance Guide, "Game of Expense Management". The brackets sort companies by profit.

Two more points from the same study:

- **Direct labor efficiency.** The study calls revenue divided by direct labor cost "DLER". It names DLER the most important driver of profit. The 2021 average was 2.90. The top 25% reached 3.96.
- **Offshore staff.** Companies with no non-US team members averaged 9% profit. Companies with 6 or more averaged 16%.

Rent level did not decide the margin. The study found no link between average rent and profit.

## How do I raise my margin?

Raise revenue per door first, then labor efficiency. The study found that 80.9% of the profit gain at the fastest-improving companies came from higher profit per unit. Only 15.8% came from more units.

| Lever | Average (2021) | Top 25% (2021) |
|---|---|---|
| Profit per unit per month | $29 | $78 |
| Revenue per unit per month | $222 | $317 |
| Units per direct team member | 49 | 59 |
| Owner churn per year | 19.5% | 9.6% |

A worked example. You manage 300 doors at the average revenue of $222 per door per month. That is $799,200 a year. At an 11% margin you keep $87,912. Move to 20%, and you keep $159,840 on the same doors.

## Where do property management benchmarks come from?

Most public numbers trace back to one source: the NARPM Accounting Standards and the benchmark studies built on them. NARPM and ProfitCoach ran the first study with data for 2017 and the update with data for 2019 to 2021.

Know the limits before you use them:

- **Single-family focus.** Many benchmarks leave out companies with 20% or more multifamily units.
- **Per-metric top 25%.** Each benchmark is the top 25% for that one metric. Todd Ortscheid notes on PMAssist that the top group changes from metric to metric. No real company wins all of them.
- **Age.** The newest public data is for 2021. Labor costs and software prices have changed since.
- **Self-selection.** Companies that join a benchmark study may run tighter books than the rest.

Use the benchmarks to find your weakest number. Then fix that one first. If owners say your fee is too high while your margin is low, the issue is labor, not price.

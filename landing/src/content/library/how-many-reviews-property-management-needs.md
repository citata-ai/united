BrightLocal asked 1,002 US adults about online reviews for its 2026 survey. 47% said they will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews. Only 9% will use one with 5 or fewer (BrightLocal, 2026). For a property manager, that sets a clear floor. Below 20 reviews, about half of the people who find you leave before they call.

## What the 2026 data says

The numbers that matter most for a review plan, from BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026:

| Finding | Share of consumers |
|---|---|
| Will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews | 47% |
| Will use a business with 5 or fewer reviews | 9% |
| Only care about reviews from the last 3 months | 74% |
| Will only use a business with 4.5+ stars | 31% |
| Care that a review is backed by similar reviews | 56% |
| Value a review posted within the last month | 44% |
| Care that the owner replied to the review | 37% |

BrightLocal also found that Google's share as a review source fell from 83% in 2025 to 71% in 2026, and that AI tools like ChatGPT rose to third place for local business recommendations (BrightLocal, 2026). Google is still first by far.

## Do more reviews beat a higher star rating?

Count and rating work together, and neither wins alone. Google says local results mainly depend on relevance, distance and prominence. Prominence uses facts like how many reviews you have. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking (Google Business Profile Help).

For owners who compare managers, the rating sets the bar. 31% will only use a business with 4.5 stars or more (BrightLocal, 2026). A 4.9 with 8 reviews fails the 20-review test. A 4.2 with 300 reviews fails the 4.5-star test.

Property managers have a special problem. Tenants review you too, and tenant reviews are often about denied applications, deposits and repairs. Do not try to hide them. Reply to each one with facts and care. Then ask your happy owners and tenants for reviews every month, so the full picture shows.

## How does review recency matter?

Recency matters because most people ignore old reviews. 74% of consumers only care about reviews written in the last 3 months (BrightLocal, 2026). A profile with 150 reviews, all from 2022, looks like a business that slowed down.

Set a monthly minimum instead of a total target. Even 4 new reviews a month means every visitor sees about 12 reviews from the last quarter.

## How do I benchmark against local competitors?

Benchmark against the 3 managers that show in your Map Pack for your main search. That is the real competition for the click.

Steps:

1. Search "property management [your city]" in Google, logged out or in a private window.
2. Write down the 3 Map Pack results: name, review count, star rating.
3. Open each profile. Count reviews from the last 90 days.
4. Repeat for 2 nearby areas you serve.
5. Repeat every quarter.

A worked benchmark:

| Manager | Total reviews | Rating | Reviews, last 90 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| Competitor A | 240 | 4.7 | 18 |
| Competitor B | 180 | 4.6 | 9 |
| Competitor C | 95 | 4.8 | 6 |
| You | 60 | 4.7 | 3 |

The example numbers are for the method only. In this case, you trail on count and on recency. To reach Competitor C's total in 12 months, you need (95 − 60) ÷ 12 ≈ 3 more reviews a month, plus what C adds. To match B's pace, you need 3 reviews a month.

Reviews on those sites feed AI answers too. The manager is Fridman Properties, whose owner is CEO of MyRemotely, our partner. ## What review pace should I aim for?

Aim for a pace that keeps at least 10 reviews from the last 3 months on your profile. It should also close the gap to the Map Pack leaders within a year. A simple rule by size:

| Doors managed | Monthly target | Who to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Under 100 | 3–4 | New owners at 60 days, tenants after a fast repair |
| 100–300 | 5–8 | Add move-in tenants and lease renewals |
| 300+ | 10+ | Add vendors where relevant, every happy owner each year |

These targets are our planning rule, not study results.

## How do I ask for reviews the right way?

Ask every client at a good moment, and never pay for a positive review. The FTC published its rule on consumer reviews in August 2024. It bans fake reviews (16 CFR 465.2) and bans incentives conditioned on a review that expresses a set sentiment, positive or negative (16 CFR 465.4). It also bans unfounded legal threats or intimidation to stop or remove a review (16 CFR 465.7).

A safe routine:

- **Ask at a win.** A lease signed, a repair done the same day, a strong year-end statement.
- **Send the direct link** to your Google review form by text or email.
- **Ask everyone,** not only the people you expect to be happy.
- **Reply to every review** within 2 business days, good or bad.
- **Never write reviews** for yourself or ask staff to post them without disclosure.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

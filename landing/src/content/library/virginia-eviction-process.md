Virginia evictions follow the Virginia Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (VRLTA), Title 55.1, Chapter 12, and the unlawful detainer rules in Title 8.01. Here are the key deadlines, read on the official Code of Virginia site on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice for unpaid rent | 14 days to pay | Va. Code § 55.1-1245(F) |
| Notice for a lease breach you can fix | 21 days to fix, lease ends 30 days after notice | § 55.1-1245(A) |
| Notice for a breach you cannot fix | Lease ends 30 days after notice | § 55.1-1245(C) |
| Criminal or willful act that threatens health or safety | Immediate termination | § 55.1-1245(C) |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | 30 days before the next rent due date | § 55.1-1253(A) |
| First court hearing | Within 21 days of filing, 30 days at most | § 8.01-126(B) |
| Service of the summons | At least 10 days before the return date | § 8.01-126(B) |
| Appeal by either party | 10 days after judgment | § 8.01-129 |
| Sheriff's notice before eviction | At least 72 hours | § 8.01-470 |
| Tenant pays all and stops the eviction | Up to 48 hours before the sheriff acts | § 55.1-1250(D) |
| Lockout or utility shutoff | Not allowed | § 55.1-1252 |

## What notice must a Virginia landlord give for unpaid rent?

A Virginia landlord must serve a written 14-day notice before filing for unpaid rent. Va. Code § 55.1-1245(F) says the notice must tell the tenant about the nonpayment. It must also say the lease will end if the tenant does not pay within the 14 days.

Watch for: some older guides still say 5 days. The current text says 14 days. The 5-day notice in § 55.1-1415 applies only to nonresidential property, such as a shop or an office.

The same 14 days apply to a bounced rent check or a rejected electronic payment. In that case, the notice can require payment by cash, cashier's check, certified check or a completed electronic transfer (§ 55.1-1245(F)).

You can add two items to the 14-day notice:

- **Attorney fee notice.** § 55.1-1245(F) lets you include the § 55.1-1202 notice, so you can ask for costs and attorney fees later.
- **Acceptance with reservation.** § 55.1-1250(A) lets you accept partial rent and still evict. Your notice must say so and must quote the statute's exact text.

A change starts on July 1, 2027. The version of § 55.1-1245 that takes effect then adds a payment plan rule. It applies to a landlord who owns more than 4 rental units. If the debt is one month's rent plus late charges or less, the notice must offer a payment plan. The plan runs for 6 months or the rest of the lease, whichever is shorter. You must offer it only once per lease term.

## How long does an eviction take in Virginia?

A Virginia eviction for unpaid rent takes about 6 to 10 weeks when the tenant does not pay, appeal or redeem. That range comes from the statute deadlines, not from court data. Busy courts and sheriffs can take longer.

Here is a worked timeline. Day 0 is the day you serve the 14-day notice.

| Step | Statute limit | Example day |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Serve the 14-day notice | 14 days to pay | Day 0 |
| 2. File the unlawful detainer | After the 14 days end | Day 15 |
| 3. First hearing (return date) | Within 21 days of filing, max 30 | Day 36 |
| 4. Judgment for possession | At the hearing, if uncontested | Day 36 |
| 5. Appeal period ends | 10 days after judgment | Day 46 |
| 6. Sheriff serves notice of eviction | At least 72 hours before | Day 47 |
| 7. Sheriff executes the writ | Within 15 days of receipt, max 30 from issue | Day 50 to 66 |

If the tenant contests the case, the judge can set a trial date. That adds weeks. A redemption tender from a local government or nonprofit can add 10 days (§ 55.1-1250(B)).

## Where do you file an eviction in Virginia, and what does it cost?

You file an unlawful detainer in the general district court for the area where the property is. Under § 8.01-126(B), you give a statement under oath to a magistrate, or to a clerk or judge of the court. The court then issues the summons.

The court will not enter an order of possession without a copy of a proper termination notice in evidence (§ 8.01-126(D)). Bring your signed notice and proof of service.

Two more rules from § 55.1-1204(H) and § 8.01-126(F):

- **Statement of tenant rights.** You cannot file or keep a case for a lease violation before you give the tenant this statement. The Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD) writes it.
- **Amount due.** You can ask on the summons for all amounts due as of the hearing date. Then the court can update the amount at the hearing.

Filing and sheriff fees: we could not read the current fee schedule on the Virginia courts site, so this page gives no amounts. Ask your general district court clerk for the current filing, service and writ fees.

## Can a Virginia landlord evict for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes, for a lease violation, with a written notice. The notice type depends on the breach (§ 55.1-1245):

1. **Remediable breach.** The tenant gets 21 days to fix it. The lease ends no sooner than 30 days after the notice if the tenant does not fix it.
2. **Non-remediable breach.** The lease ends no sooner than 30 days after the notice.
3. **Repeat breach.** If the tenant fixed a breach and then intentionally repeats it, a 30-day notice can refer to the first breach (§ 55.1-1245(E)).
4. **Criminal or willful act that threatens health or safety.** You may end the lease at once. The first hearing must take place within 15 calendar days of service (§ 55.1-1245(C)).

No-cause endings work through the lease term. For a month-to-month tenancy, either side can end it with written notice at least 30 days before the next rent due date (§ 55.1-1253(A)). A landlord with more than 4 units must give a written notice of nonrenewal at least 60 days before a fixed lease ends (§ 55.1-1204(K)).

If the tenant stays after a proper notice, you file the same unlawful detainer.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Virginia?

The sheriff removes the tenant, never the landlord. After the judgment, you ask the clerk for a writ of eviction. § 8.01-129(B) lets the judge issue the writ at once if you ask. But the sheriff cannot evict before the 10-day appeal period ends.

The sheriff must serve a notice with the date and time at least 72 hours before the eviction (§ 8.01-470). The sheriff should act within 15 calendar days of receiving the writ. A writ not executed within 30 days of issue is void (§ 8.01-471). An order of possession stays valid for 180 days.

The tenant can still stop a nonpayment eviction. Under § 55.1-1250(D), the tenant pays all amounts on the summons, plus sheriff fees. The payment must reach you at least 48 hours before the scheduled eviction. You must then tell the sheriff to cancel.

## Is a self-help eviction legal in Virginia?

No. Va. Code § 55.1-1252 bars a landlord from taking possession by cutting an essential service. It also bars keeping the tenant out of the unit without a court order.

The penalty is high. Under § 55.1-1243.1, the tenant can petition the general district court. The first hearing comes within 5 calendar days. If the court finds a willful lockout or shutoff, the tenant recovers:

- actual damages,
- $5,000 or 4 months' rent, whichever is greater,
- reasonable attorney fees.

- [ ] You kept proof of service.
- [ ] The tenant got the DHCD statement of tenant rights.

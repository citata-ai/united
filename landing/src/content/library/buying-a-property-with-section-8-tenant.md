## Is the HAP contract current?

Check first that the HAP contract is current and the PHA is paying. The HAP contract (Housing Assistance Payments contract) is between the seller and the housing authority (PHA). Its term matches the lease term (24 CFR 982.309(b)). It ends if the lease ends, if the PHA ends it, or if the PHA ends the family's assistance.

Ask the seller for 12 months of HAP payment statements or bank deposits. Look for 3 things:

- **Steady payments.** A missing month can mean abatement or a lapsed contract.
- **A HAP of $0.** If the tenant's share covers the full rent, the HAP is $0. The contract ends automatically 180 days after the last payment (24 CFR 982.455).
- **Changes in amount.** A change often follows an income review or a rent change.

The contract does not move to you by itself. NYC HPD's change-of-ownership packet says HPD "will only release payments to the purchaser once property ownership documentation has been supplied and the HAP contract assigned". You must also qualify as an owner under 24 CFR 982.306. So plan for a gap in HAP after closing, and plan who collects it.

## When is the next inspection?

The next inspection is due no later than 2 years after the last one. 24 CFR 982.405(b) requires a PHA to inspect each voucher unit "at least biennially". Small rural PHAs inspect every 3 years. The PHA may inspect more often, and a tenant complaint can trigger a special inspection at any time.

Ask for the last inspection report and the PHA's pass letter. If the last inspection was 20 months ago, expect a new one soon after you close. Budget for repairs before that date, not after.

Watch for: HUD set February 1, 2027 as the date by which voucher PHAs must use the new NSPIRE inspection standard (Federal Register, 2025-09-30). An older building that passed under the old standard may face new items.

## Is the contract rent at or below the payment standard?

The contract rent can be above the payment standard, but it cannot be above the reasonable rent. These are 2 different limits.

| Limit | What it controls | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Reasonable rent | The highest rent to owner the PHA may approve, from comparable unassisted units | 24 CFR 982.507 |
| Payment standard | The maximum subsidy; the family pays any gap | 24 CFR 982.505 |
| Initial lease term | No rent increase during it | 24 CFR 982.309(a)(3) |
| Rent change notice | Owner tells PHA at least 60 days before | 24 CFR 982.308(g)(4) |

A rent far above the payment standard means the tenant pays a large share. That raises your collection risk. A rent far below market may look like upside, but any increase must pass rent reasonableness. Check local numbers in our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) before you count on a rent bump in your model.

## Is there abatement or open fail items?

Ask the seller in writing whether any unit is in abatement or has open deficiencies. Then confirm with the PHA if the seller allows it.

Under 24 CFR 982.404, the owner must fix a life-threatening item within 24 hours and other items within 30 days of PHA notice. If not, the PHA must abate (stop) the HAP. If the unit still fails 60 days after the abatement notice, or a longer PHA period, the PHA must end the HAP contract. The PHA does not pay back abated months.

If you close on a unit in abatement, you inherit that clock. We found no rule that restarts it on a sale. Ask for a price credit or a repair before closing.

## Can I change the tenancy after I buy?

You take the lease as it stands. Tacoma Housing Authority's landlord FAQ says the seller must disclose the voucher tenant to the buyer, and "the new owner must honor the current lease".

You may end the tenancy only on the grounds in 24 CFR 982.310: serious or repeated lease violations, certain law violations, or "other good cause". A "business or economic reason", such as sale of the property, counts as other good cause. But during the initial lease term, you may not use it. After that term, you still need a written notice of the grounds, a copy of any eviction notice to the PHA, and a court action. State and local law can add more limits.

## Due-diligence checklist

Use this list before you remove your inspection contingency.

**Documents from the seller**

- [ ] The lease and the HUD tenancy addendum.
- [ ] The HAP contract, with its start date.
- [ ] 12 months of HAP payment statements.
- [ ] The tenant ledger for the tenant's share.
- [ ] The last PHA inspection report and pass letter.
- [ ] Any open deficiency or abatement notices.
- [ ] The latest rent change notice to the PHA.
- [ ] The security deposit amount and where it is held.

**Checks you run**

- [ ] Confirm the seller is the owner of record and the HAP payee.
- [ ] Compare the rent with the local payment standard and with market comps.
- [ ] Check if the initial lease term has ended.
- [ ] Walk the unit with an inspection checklist (smoke and CO alarms, windows, heat, hot water, paint).
- [ ] Check that you are not debarred and have no conflict under 24 CFR 982.306.
- [ ] Ask the PHA for its change-of-ownership forms and timing.
- [ ] Agree in the contract who keeps HAP paid after closing and how it is returned.

**Model the deal**

To model the income, split the rent into HAP and tenant share. Our [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator) shows the split for a given payment standard and income. Then stress-test 2 cases: one month of abatement, and 60 days of no HAP while the contract moves to you.

| Case | Monthly HAP | Months lost | Income lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Example: assignment delay | $1,200 | 2 | $2,400 |
| Example: one month of abatement | $1,200 | 1 | $1,200 |

These are example numbers. NYC HPD says it may pay up to 60 days of back payments to the buyer, but only when the seller gave notice and the buyer sent documents within 30 days of the sale. Other PHAs set their own rules.

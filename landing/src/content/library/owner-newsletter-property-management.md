An owner opens your email on a Tuesday night. It says rents for 3-bedroom homes in their ZIP rose 2% this year. A new city rental license fee starts in January. The gutters need a clean before November. That took 90 seconds. They forward it to a friend who owns a duplex.

That is the job of an owner newsletter. It is a small, useful update that keeps owners informed, keeps you in mind and brings a referral now and then.

## Which sections do owners read?

Owners read the sections that touch their money or their risk. Use 5 sections, in the same order every month, so owners learn where to look.

| # | Section | Length | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Market number | 2–3 sentences | Median rent on our 3-bed leases this quarter |
| 2 | Law or rule update | 2–4 sentences | New rental license fee, deposit rule, notice change |
| 3 | Cost or maintenance tip | 2–4 sentences | What a furnace service costs now, and why to book it |
| 4 | Company update | 1–2 sentences | New inspector, new portal feature, office hours |
| 5 | One ask | 1 sentence | Refer an owner, leave a review, reply with a question |

Cut anything else. Recipes, holiday trivia and stock photos push the useful parts down.

## The template

```text
Subject: [City] rents: [one fact with number] + [one rule change]
Preheader: Your 2-minute owner update for [Month Year]

Hi [First name],

1. MARKET NUMBER
On the [number] leases we signed in [City] in [quarter], the median rent for a
[3]-bed home was $[amount], [up/down] [x]% from [period] (our lease data).
HUD's FY 2027 Fair Market Rent for a [3]-bed in [metro] is $[amount] (HUD User).

2. RULE UPDATE
[Rule] changes on [date]. What it means for you: [one sentence].
Source: [ordinance, statute or agency page]. We will handle [what] for you.

3. COST TIP
[Season task] costs about $[range] with our vendors this month.
Skipping it can lead to [problem]. Reply "book it" and we will schedule it.

4. FROM OUR TEAM
[One short company update.]

5. ONE ASK
Know an owner who is tired of tenant calls? Reply with their name, or send them
[short link]. [What happens next / referral terms where your state allows them.]

Thank you,
[Name], [Title], [Company]
[Phone] · [Owner portal link]

[Company legal name] · [Valid postal address]
You get this because you are an owner client or asked for our owner updates.
Unsubscribe: [one-click link]
```

## How do I add local market numbers?

Add local market numbers from your own leases first, then from one public source. Your own data is unique, and that is why owners read it. A public source gives context and lets owners check you.

| Source | What it gives | How to cite |
|---|---|---|
| Your lease data | Median rent, days to lease, renewals | "(our lease data, Q3 2026, 41 leases)" |
| HUD Fair Market Rents | Rent standard by metro or county and bedroom count, FY 2027 | "HUD User, FY 2027 FMR" |
| Zillow Observed Rent Index | Typical market rent by region, monthly | "Zillow ZORI, [month]" |

HUD publishes Fair Market Rents by state, county and metro area, with full documentation for FY 2027 (HUD User). Zillow describes ZORI as a smoothed, repeat-rent measure of typical market rent, weighted to the whole rental stock, not only homes listed now (Zillow Research). Our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) shows FY 2027 Fair Market Rents by metro.

Three rules for numbers:

1. **Say the sample.** "41 leases" tells the owner how much to trust the median.
2. **Say the period.** "Q3 2026", not "recently".
3. **Do not cherry-pick.** If rents fell, say so, and say what you do about it.

## Should the newsletter ask for referrals?

Yes, once per issue, in one sentence. Current owners know other owners. In Goodjuju's fall 2026 study of 35 PM companies, referral traffic brought 3.7% of web form leads (Goodjuju, 2026). That count misses many word-of-mouth referrals, because a referred owner often calls or searches your name. Add "Referral from someone" to your "How did you first hear about us?" field to count them.

Make the ask easy:

- **One action.** "Reply with their name" or a short link.
- **Say what happens.** "We call them within 1 business day and keep you posted."
- **Rotate the ask.** Referral one month, a Google review the next, a question the month after.

Check your state's real estate rules before you offer a referral fee or gift.

## How long should it be?

Keep it to 300 to 500 words, about 2 minutes to read. That is our working range, not a study result. Link to longer articles on your site instead of pasting them in. A short email gets read on a phone; a long one gets saved for later and forgotten.

## Rules for every issue

Every issue is a commercial email, so CAN-SPAM applies. The FTC lists the duties: accurate sender details, a subject line that does not mislead, your valid physical postal address, a clear way to opt out, and opt-outs honored within 10 business days. Each email that breaks the law can bring a penalty of up to $53,088 (FTC).

Gmail adds sender rules. All senders to Gmail accounts must set up SPF or DKIM and keep the spam rate below 0.3%. Bulk senders, over 5,000 messages a day to Gmail, must also support one-click unsubscribe and pass DMARC (Google Workspace Admin Help). Most email tools handle the unsubscribe link; ask your IT vendor to set up the DNS records once.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Newsletter checklist

- Same 5 sections, same order, every month.
- One local number with sample, period and source.
- One rule update with its source.
- One ask, rotated each month.
- 300 to 500 words.
- Postal address and one-click unsubscribe in the footer.
- Sent on the same day each month.

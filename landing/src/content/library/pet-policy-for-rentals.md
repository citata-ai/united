## Which pets and how many should a rental allow?

A rental pet policy starts with a list of allowed pet types and a limit per unit. Write both as plain rules, so every applicant gets the same answer. A vague policy ("small pets OK") leads to disputes and uneven decisions.

Decide these 4 points first:

| Decision | Example wording | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Allowed types | "Dogs, cats, caged birds, fish in tanks up to 20 gallons" | Removes guesswork on each request |
| Limit per unit | "Up to 2 pets per unit" | Caps wear on floors and yards |
| Size limit | "Dogs up to 50 lb at adult weight" | Must match your insurance terms |
| Approval | "Each pet needs written approval before move-in" | Stops unlisted pets |

Pick limits that fit the property. A small condo with carpet needs a tighter limit than a house with a fenced yard. Write the same limits for every applicant.

## Which weight, breed and insurance rules should a pet policy have?

Weight and breed limits in a pet policy should follow your insurance policy first. Your landlord policy may exclude claims for some dog breeds. Ask your insurer for the list in writing, and match your policy to it.

Then set the money terms. State law caps what you may charge, and the caps differ a lot:

| State | Pet money rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Colorado | Pet deposit up to $300, refundable. Pet rent up to $35 a month or 1.5% of monthly rent, whichever is greater. In effect from January 1, 2024. | HB23-1068 |
| California | All "security" together is capped at 1 month's rent for most landlords (2 months for some small landlords). A pet deposit counts inside that cap. | Civil Code 1950.5(c) |

California's cap took effect for security collected on or after July 1, 2024, per Civil Code 1950.5(c)(6). The statute defines "security" as any payment or deposit "for any purpose," so a separate pet deposit does not add room above the cap.

Worked example for a Colorado unit at $2,400 a month:

- 1.5% of $2,400 = $36.
- $36 is greater than $35, so the cap is $36 a month in pet rent.
- The pet deposit may not exceed $300.

Many policies also require renters insurance with liability coverage. That protects the tenant and gives you a second source of payment after a dog bite or damage claim.

## What is the federal rule on assistance animals?

An assistance animal is not a pet, so pet limits and pet charges do not apply to it. The Fair Housing Act bans discrimination based on disability, per the U.S. Department of Justice. The HUD rule at 24 CFR 100.204 makes it unlawful to refuse "reasonable accommodations in rules, policies, practices, or services" when a person with a disability needs them.

The rule's own example is a blind applicant with a guide dog in a building with a no-pets policy. Refusing the dog violates the rule.

HUD notice FHEO-2020-01 adds detail. As NLIHC reports, "A housing provider may not exclude service animals or support animals or charge a fee or deposit for them." The notice also lists common household animals, such as a dog, cat, small bird, rabbit or fish.

Write one line in your pet policy: "This policy does not apply to assistance animals. Requests for assistance animals follow our reasonable accommodation process." This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Which pet screening steps should I use?

Pet screening checks the pet and the owner's record with the same steps for every applicant. Use a short written form and keep it in the tenant file.

Pet screening checklist:

- **Pet profile.** Type, breed, adult weight, age, name and a current photo.
- **Vaccines.** A current rabies record from a vet, where local law requires it.
- **License.** The city or county pet license, where required.
- **Spay or neuter.** Ask, if your policy sets a rule on it.
- **Prior landlord.** Ask the last landlord about pet damage or complaints.
- **Meet the pet.** A short meeting shows if the pet is calm and house-trained.
- **Signed addendum.** The tenant signs the pet addendum before the pet moves in.

Do not run these pet steps for an assistance animal. Use your reasonable accommodation process instead.

## Which pet rules and waste cleanup terms should I write?

Pet rules turn the policy into daily conduct. Write each rule so a tenant can tell if they broke it. Each rule also needs a consequence.

House rules to include:

1. **Waste.** Pick up dog waste at once in shared areas and yards. Clean litter boxes and bag the waste.
2. **Leash.** Keep dogs on a leash in shared areas.
3. **Noise.** No barking that disturbs neighbors for a long time.
4. **Damage.** The tenant pays for pet damage beyond normal wear.
5. **Alone time.** No pet left alone in the unit for a long trip without care.
6. **New pets.** Ask in writing before you add or replace a pet.
7. **Breach.** Name the steps: a written warning, then a notice under state law.

Put the rules, the money terms and the pet details in one signed pet addendum. Keep a copy in the lease file and in your property management software.

## Should I allow pets at all?

A clear pet policy gives you control, while a "no pets" rule can push pets into the unit without approval. You still must accept assistance animals under federal law, so "no pets" never means "no animals".

## What did the Miami-Dade Tenant Bill of Rights require?

The Miami-Dade Tenant's Bill of Rights was Ordinance 22-47, adopted by the Board of County Commissioners on May 3, 2022. The county's own Resolution R-236-23 (March 2023) describes it. Its stated goals were to improve housing stability, give residents accurate information about laws that protect tenants, and connect tenants to agencies that can help.

The resolution says the Bill of Rights set out tenant rights to:

- **A place to call home**, free from arbitrary eviction, retaliation and discrimination.
- **Safe and healthy living conditions.**
- **Strong consumer protections** and full and fair access to the courts.

A second county law mattered more for daily operations. Ordinance 22-30, adopted March 15, 2022 and codified as Section 17-03, did two things, per R-236-23:

1. **Rent increase notice.** Landlords in incorporated and unincorporated Miami-Dade who raised rent by 5% or more had to give tenants advance written notice.
2. **Longer month-to-month notice.** It enlarged the notice to end a month-to-month tenancy from 30 days to 60 days, on top of state law.

## What did Florida HB 1417 (2023) preempt?

Florida HB 1417 created Florida Statutes 83.425 and moved the regulation of residential tenancies to the state. It became Chapter 2023-314, effective July 1, 2023, per the Florida Senate bill page.

Section 83.425 says the regulation of residential tenancies, the landlord-tenant relationship and all other matters in the state act "are preempted to the state." It "supersedes any local government regulations" on those matters, including:

| Area | Preempted to the state by 83.425 |
|---|---|
| Tenant screening | Yes |
| Security deposits | Yes |
| Rental applications and their fees | Yes |
| Terms and conditions of leases | Yes |
| Rights and duties of landlord and tenant | Yes |
| Disclosures about the unit, the lease or rights | Yes |
| Fees charged by the landlord | Yes |
| Notice requirements | Yes |

The House final bill analysis (June 30, 2023) explains the change. Before the bill, the state act did not preempt local rules, so cities and counties could add rules that did not conflict with state law. The bill made the preemption express.

Miami-Dade saw this coming. In R-236-23, the commission wrote that if HB 1417 passed, its "fair notice requirements and Tenant's Bill of Rights may be preempted, rendering the protections adopted by this Board ineffective." The commission opposed the bill, but the Legislature passed it.

## Which local notice rules no longer apply in Miami?

The Miami-Dade notice rules in Ordinance 22-30 cover matters that 83.425 now reserves to the state. Notice requirements and lease terms are both on the preempted list. Here is how the main rules compare:

| Rule | Miami-Dade 2022 ordinance | Florida law now |
|---|---|---|
| End a month-to-month tenancy | 60 days (Ord. 22-30) | At least 30 days before the end of a monthly period (83.57(3)) |
| Rent increase of 5% or more | Advance written notice (Ord. 22-30, Sec. 17-03) | No separate state notice rule in the act, per R-236-23 |
| End a year-to-year tenancy | Not changed | At least 60 days (83.57(1)) |
| Max notice a lease may require | Not changed | 60 days from either side (83.575, per the House analysis) |

A note on the state change itself: HB 1417 also raised the state month-to-month notice. The House analysis shows the old minimum was 15 days. Section 83.57(3) now says 30 days.

Watch for: we found no county page that confirms the current status of Section 17-03 and Ordinance 22-47. The county's old Tenant's Bill of Rights web page now returns "page not found." Treat the county rules as likely preempted, and ask a Florida attorney before you rely on them either way.

Worked example: a Miami two-bedroom at $2,564 a month, HUD's FY2027 Fair Market Rent for the metro, up 5.3% from $2,436. A move to $2,692 is a 5% increase. Under the 2022 county rule, that would have needed advance written notice. The state act has no separate rent increase notice rule, per R-236-23. For a month-to-month tenancy, the state notice period to end it is at least 30 days (83.57(3)). See the [Miami Fair Market Rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/miami-fair-market-rent-2027) for other sizes.

## Which Florida state rules still govern Miami rentals?

The Florida Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, Chapter 83 Part II, now sets the core rules for Miami rentals. The ones owners use most:

| Topic | State rule | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Entry for repairs | At least 24 hours' notice, between 7:30 a.m. and 8:00 p.m. | 83.53(2) |
| Emergency entry | Any time to protect or preserve the premises | 83.53(2) |
| Unpaid rent | 3-day written demand, not counting weekends and court holidays | 83.56(3) |
| Month-to-month end | At least 30 days before the end of a monthly period | 83.57(3) |
| Deposit holding | Separate Florida account, or a surety bond, no mixing with your funds | 83.49(1) |
| Deposit notice | Within 30 days, name the bank and state if interest is paid (landlords with 5+ units) | 83.49(2) |
| Deposit return | 15 days with no claim; 30 days for a written claim notice | 83.49(3) |
| Attorney fees | The prevailing party may recover reasonable fees, per the House analysis | 83.48 |

A short checklist for a Miami rental in 2026:

- **Update your lease** to state law: remove county-only notice terms.
- **Use 30 days** to end a month-to-month tenancy, not 15.
- **Cap lease notice terms** at 60 days for either side.
- **Use the exact 3-day notice text** in 83.56(3) for unpaid rent.
- **Send the deposit notice** with the exact 83.49(2)(d) wording.
- **Keep fair housing rules in mind.** Federal fair housing law still applies, and preemption of landlord-tenant matters does not change it.

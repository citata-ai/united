## Where do I find rental comps?

Rental comps are rentals near yours that a renter would see as real options. You find them in 3 places.

| Source | What it gives you | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Zillow Rental Manager | A free Rent Zestimate plus a list of similar advertised rentals you can sort and filter | Asking rents, not signed rents |
| Apartments.com | Free rent comparison reports: beds, property age, size, days on market, average rents | Asking rents, not signed rents |
| HUD Fair Market Rents | Metro-wide rent plus utilities at the 40th percentile of recent movers (24 CFR 888.113) | Not a market price for one unit |
| Your own and local managers' recent leases | Signed rents | Small sample |

Zillow says its Rent Zestimate looks at comparable rental properties and local rates, and that "how much you charge for rent is ultimately up to you." Treat any automated estimate as a starting point.

HUD's rule says Fair Market Rents are set at the 40th percentile rent of standard-quality units occupied by recent movers. So 60% of those units rent for more than the FMR, and a unit in good condition can too. For FMR numbers by metro, see our [Section 8 rent limits](/guides/section-8-rent-limits).

## How many comps do I need?

Use at least 3 comps, and 5 or 6 when the area has them. With 3, one odd listing moves the answer a lot. With 6, you can drop the highest and the lowest and still have 4.

Rules for a good comp:

1. **Same beds and baths.** Do not compare a 2-bed to a 3-bed.
2. **Close by.** The same neighborhood or school zone, or within a short drive.
3. **Similar size.** Within about 20% of your square footage.
4. **Recent.** Listed now, or leased in the last 3 months.
5. **Same type.** House with house, apartment with apartment.

## How do I adjust comps for size, beds and amenities?

Adjust each comp's rent to what it would be if it had your unit's features. Add for what your unit has and the comp lacks. Subtract for what the comp has and yours lacks. Use one set of dollar values for every comp.

**Example adjustment values (your own estimates, not market data):**

- Each 100 sq ft: $50
- In-unit laundry: $50
- Off-street parking spot: $40
- Updated kitchen: $75
- Water and trash included: $60

**Your unit:** 2 bed, 1 bath, 950 sq ft, in-unit laundry, 1 parking spot, older kitchen, tenant pays all utilities.

| Comp | Asking rent | Size adj. | Laundry | Parking | Kitchen | Utilities | Adjusted rent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: 900 sq ft, laundry, no parking | $1,650 | +$25 | $0 | +$40 | $0 | $0 | $1,715 |
| B: 1,000 sq ft, no laundry, parking, updated kitchen | $1,750 | −$25 | +$50 | $0 | −$75 | $0 | $1,700 |
| C: 950 sq ft, laundry, parking, water included | $1,775 | $0 | $0 | $0 | $0 | −$60 | $1,715 |
| D: 1,050 sq ft, laundry, parking | $1,800 | −$50 | $0 | $0 | $0 | $0 | $1,750 |
| E: 925 sq ft, no laundry, no parking | $1,575 | +$13 | +$50 | +$40 | $0 | $0 | $1,678 |

**Result:** the adjusted rents run from $1,678 to $1,750. The middle value (median) is $1,715. A price of $1,695 to $1,725 fits this unit.

## What if the unit gets no calls in two weeks?

If a well-listed unit gets few calls or no applications in 14 days, check the price first. Then check what renters see. Work through this list in order:

1. **Inquiries low?** The price or the photos are the likely issue. Compare your listing to the comps side by side.
2. **Showings, but no applications?** The unit, its condition or the price does not match what renters saw online.
3. **Applications, but none qualify?** Check your screening criteria and the ad's wording.
4. **Still slow at day 21?** Cut the price by a small step, such as 2% to 3%, and refresh the listing.

Keep a log of inquiries, showings and applications by day. You will price the next vacancy faster.

## Should I price a little under market?

Pricing a little under market is often the cheaper choice. One empty month costs a full month of rent. A small discount costs much less over a year.

**Worked example, market rent $1,715:**

| Choice | Monthly rent | Days vacant (your estimate) | Lost rent while vacant | 12-month total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| At market | $1,715 | 30 | $1,715 | $18,865 |
| $50 under | $1,665 | 10 | $555 | $19,425 |

Math: 12-month total = (12 months × rent) − lost rent. At market: $20,580 − $1,715 = $18,865. At $50 under: $19,980 − $555 = $19,425.

The days vacant are your estimate, not data. If $50 under market fills the unit 20 days sooner, it earns $560 more in the first year. A lower price also brings more applicants, which gives you a better choice of tenant.

## What rules apply when I set rent?

Set one rent for the unit and offer it to every applicant. HUD's rule at 24 CFR 100.75 counts different terms based on a protected class as discrimination. Do not quote a different price to different renters for the same unit.

Some states also cap increases after move-in. Washington, for example, caps 2026 rent increases at 9.683%, per its Department of Commerce. A low starting price is hard to fix later in a capped state, so price to the market from day one.

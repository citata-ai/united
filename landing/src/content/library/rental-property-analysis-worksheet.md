## What inputs does a rental analysis need?

A rental analysis needs 4 groups of inputs: what you pay, how you borrow, what the home earns, and what it costs to run. Get each number from a document, not a guess.

| Group | Input | Where to get it |
|-------|-------|-----------------|
| Purchase | Price | Offer or contract |
| Purchase | Closing costs | Lender's loan estimate |
| Purchase | Repairs before the first tenant | Inspection report and contractor quotes |
| Loan | Down payment | Lender quote |
| Loan | Interest rate and term | Lender quote; Freddie Mac's weekly survey as a check |
| Income | Market rent | 3–5 recent leases of similar homes nearby |
| Income | Vacancy rate | Local data; the Census Bureau's national rate as a floor |
| Costs | Property tax | County assessor, at the new assessed value |
| Costs | Insurance | Landlord policy quote |
| Costs | HOA dues and assessments | HOA resale documents |
| Costs | Maintenance | A share of rent |
| Costs | Capital reserve | A share of rent for roof, HVAC, water heater |
| Costs | Management and leasing fees | Manager's fee schedule |
| Costs | Owner-paid utilities | Utility bills from the seller |

Two benchmarks for the first draft:

- **Vacancy.** The Census Bureau reported a national rental vacancy rate of 7.3% for the second quarter of 2026. Your city can be higher or lower.
- **Management.** iPropertyManagement's 2026 survey found an average fee of 8.49% of monthly rent. The average leasing fee was 70.6% of one month's rent when the manager also manages the home.

For maintenance, Baselane reports that spending equaled 8.0% of rent receipts at the median rental property in 2023. It cites an analysis of the Census Rental Housing Finance Survey. Use your own repair history if you have it.

## Which outputs should it show?

The worksheet should show 5 outputs. Each answers a different question.

| Output | Formula | Question it answers |
|--------|---------|---------------------|
| Net operating income (NOI) | Rent collected − operating costs | What does the home earn before the loan? |
| Cash flow | NOI − yearly mortgage payments | What do I keep each year? |
| Cap rate | NOI ÷ price | How does the price compare with similar homes? |
| Cash-on-cash return (CoC) | Cash flow ÷ cash invested | What does my own cash earn? |
| Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) | NOI ÷ yearly mortgage payments | Can the rent carry the loan? |

Operating costs do not include the mortgage payment. Keep the loan out of NOI so you can compare homes with different financing.

## How do I stress-test the numbers?

A stress test changes one input to a worse value and checks what happens to cash flow. Run each case on its own. Here is the same example under 4 bad cases:

| Case | Change | Yearly cash flow | DSCR |
|------|--------|------------------|------|
| Base | None | $2,464 | 1.16 |
| Lower rent | Rent 10% lower, $2,520 | $130 | 1.01 |
| More vacancy | Vacancy 12% | $1,145 | 1.07 |
| Higher rate | Rate 8.40% | $901 | 1.05 |
| Higher fixed costs | Tax $4,200, insurance $2,700 | $864 | 1.06 |

What the test shows: rent is the weak point. A 10% miss on rent wipes out almost all the cash flow. Before you buy, confirm the rent with 3 to 5 signed leases nearby, not with listing prices.

Watch for: the rate case applies only if you buy with a new loan. If you hold a fixed-rate loan, check the rate case at refinance time instead.

What to do next:

1. **Fill in the worksheet** with real quotes for tax, insurance and repairs.
2. **Run the 4 stress cases.** Note the worst cash flow.
3. **Decide your floor.** For example: no deal with a worst case below $0.
4. **Price the management line** with our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## What the worksheet leaves out

The worksheet is a cash view. Taxes add a second layer. IRS Publication 527 says residential rental property is depreciated over 27.5 years, and that deduction can lower the tax on your rent. Appreciation and loan paydown also add to your return over time. Keep those out of the go or no-go decision, and treat them as extra.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

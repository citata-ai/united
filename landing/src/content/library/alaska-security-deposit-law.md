Alaska puts its deposit rules in AS 34.03.070, part of the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act. We read the current text of AS 34.03 on the Alaska Legislature site (akleg.gov) on 2026-10-20. The rules have more parts than in most states: two caps, a pet deposit, two deadlines and a trust account. This table holds each one.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit plus prepaid rent | 2 months' rent | AS 34.03.070(a) |
| Units with rent above $2,000 a month | The whole section does not apply | AS 34.03.070(a) |
| Pet deposit (not a service animal) | Up to 1 month's rent, extra | AS 34.03.070(h) |
| Refund if proper notice given, no damage deductions | 14 days | AS 34.03.070(g) |
| Refund with damage deductions | 30 days | AS 34.03.070(g) |
| Refund if tenant gave no proper notice | 30 days | AS 34.03.070(g) |
| Itemized list | Required, mailed to last known address | AS 34.03.070(b) |
| Where the money is held | Trust account, where practicable | AS 34.03.070(c) |
| Penalty for wilful failure | Up to 2 times the amount withheld | AS 34.03.070(d) |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Alaska?

The maximum is 2 months' periodic rent, and prepaid rent counts toward it. AS 34.03.070(a) says a landlord may not demand or receive prepaid rent or a security deposit, "however denominated", above that amount.

The cap has a rent ceiling. Subsection (a) says "this section" does not apply to units where the rent is more than $2,000 a month. The words cover all of AS 34.03.070, not only the cap. So for those units, the section sets no maximum, no pet limit, no refund deadline and no trust account rule. Your lease terms then carry more weight.

A pet deposit sits on top of the cap. Under AS 34.03.070(h), a landlord may take an extra deposit for a pet that is not a service animal. The pet deposit:

- may not be more than 1 month's rent
- must be accounted for apart from the main deposit
- may cover only damage directly related to the pet

Worked example:

| Monthly rent | Main deposit plus prepaid rent, maximum | Pet deposit, maximum |
|---|---|---|
| $1,500 | $3,000 | $1,500 |
| $2,000 | $4,000 | $2,000 |
| $2,400 | Section does not apply | Section does not apply |

A service animal is not a pet under this section. AS 34.03.070(i) defines a service animal as one trained to do work or tasks for a person with a disability. Do not charge a pet deposit for one.

## How many days does an Alaska landlord have to return a security deposit?

An Alaska landlord has 14 or 30 days. The deadline depends on two facts: did someone give proper notice, and did the landlord deduct for damage? AS 34.03.070(g) sets three cases.

1. **14 days.** The landlord or the tenant gave notice that meets AS 34.03.290, and the landlord makes no damage deduction. The clock starts when the tenancy ends and the tenant delivers possession.
2. **30 days.** Proper notice was given, but the landlord deducts for damage under AS 34.03.120.
3. **30 days.** The tenant did not give proper notice. The clock starts when the tenancy ends, the tenant delivers possession, or the landlord learns the unit is abandoned.

Proper notice under AS 34.03.290 is written notice at least 30 days before the rental due date for a month-to-month tenancy, or at least 14 days for week-to-week.

If the landlord does not know the tenant's mailing address but knows how to reach the tenant, the landlord must make a reasonable effort to deliver the notice and refund.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Alaska?

An Alaska landlord can deduct accrued rent and damages from the tenant's failure to meet AS 34.03.120, the tenant duties section. AS 34.03.070(b) lists these uses.

The section defines damages. Damages mean deterioration of the premises and, if it applies, of the contents. Damages do not include:

- normal wear and tear
- deterioration caused by the landlord's failure to prepare for expected conditions
- deterioration caused by the landlord's failure to meet a landlord duty under the act

AS 34.03.070(i) defines normal wear and tear as deterioration from the intended use, without negligence, carelessness, accident, misuse or abuse by the tenant, the household or guests.

A move-in record helps here. Under AS 34.03.020(e), a landlord may require a signed premises condition statement and a contents inventory. AS 34.03.335 makes a signed statement presumptive evidence of the condition at move-in.

## Does an Alaska landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. AS 34.03.070(b) says the landlord must itemize the accrued rent and damages in a written notice. The landlord mails the notice to the tenant's last known address, with the amount due to the tenant, within the deadline in subsection (g).

Move-out checklist:

- [ ] Record the date the tenant delivered possession.
- [ ] Check whether proper notice was given under AS 34.03.290.
- [ ] Compare the unit to the signed move-in condition statement.
- [ ] List each deduction with the amount and the reason.
- [ ] Leave out normal wear and tear.
- [ ] Mail the list and the refund within 14 or 30 days.
- [ ] Keep proof of the mailing date.

## Must an Alaska landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

Alaska requires a trust account but not interest. AS 34.03.070(c) says all prepaid rent and deposits must go promptly, wherever practicable, into a trust account at a bank, savings and loan association or licensed escrow agent.

The landlord must also give the tenant the terms under which the landlord may keep any part of the deposit. The same subsection sets account rules:

- The landlord may keep prepaid rent and deposits in one account.
- The landlord must keep a separate record for each tenant.
- The landlord must not mix deposits with other funds.
- The landlord must not use one tenant's money to refund, or pay rent or damages for, another tenant.

The section has no interest rule. Whoever holds the landlord's interest when the tenancy ends is bound by the section (AS 34.03.070(f)). If you buy a rental, get the trust records at closing.

## What is the penalty for an Alaska landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

The penalty is up to twice the actual amount withheld. AS 34.03.070(d) gives the tenant this claim if the landlord wilfully fails to itemize and refund as subsection (b) requires.

Worked example: a landlord wilfully keeps $1,800 with no itemized list. The tenant may recover up to $3,600. AS 34.03.350 adds attorney fees for the prevailing party in any case under the act, so a loss costs more than the deposit.

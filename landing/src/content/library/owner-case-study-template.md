## Which results should a case study show?

A case study should show one owner's result in numbers, with a start date and an end date. Owners who read case studies compare their own problem with the story. Vague praise does not help them decide.

Goodjuju, a PM marketing agency, puts one number in each case study headline on its reviews page. Examples there include "~50 → 350+ doors" and "3 owners signed in 1 month". The same pattern works for an owner case study: one before, one after.

Pick metrics an owner cares about:

| Metric | Before example | After example | Where you get it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days vacant | Days empty before you took over | Days to lease after | Your PM software |
| Rent | Old monthly rent | New lease rent | Lease records |
| Collections | Late or missed payments in 12 months | Same period after | Owner ledger |
| Repair cost | A vendor quote the owner had | Your vendor's invoice | Work orders |
| Owner time | Hours a month the owner spent | Hours after | Owner interview |
| Turnover | Tenants per 3 years | Renewals after | Lease records |

Use real numbers from records. Round them only if the owner asks. Write the dates. "Leased in 12 days, March 2026" beats "leased fast".

## How do I get owner consent?

Get consent in writing before you publish anything about an owner. A written form protects both of you and makes the owner part of the story. Ask after a clear win, while the owner is pleased.

Use this consent form. Edit it to fit your company, and check it with a local attorney.

```text
CASE STUDY CONSENT

Owner name: ______________________
Property (as it will appear): [ ] full address  [ ] street only  [ ] city only  [ ] none

I agree that [Company name] may publish a case study about the
management of my rental property.

I agree to publish (check each):
[ ] My full name     [ ] First name only     [ ] No name
[ ] My photo         [ ] Property photos
[ ] These numbers:   ____________________________________
[ ] My quote below, in these exact words:
    "________________________________________________"

[Company name] [did / did not] give me anything for this case study.
If it did, describe: ____________________________________
(For example: a fee discount. This will be shown on the case study.)

I have read the draft dated ____________ and it is true to my experience.

I may withdraw this consent at any time by writing to
[email]. [Company name] will remove the case study within [__] days.

Owner signature: ____________________   Date: __________
Company signature: __________________   Date: __________
```

Send the owner the full draft before they sign. If the owner changes a word in their quote, use their word. Never publish tenant names, tenant photos or tenant details.

## What do FTC endorsement rules require?

FTC endorsement rules require that a case study is true, typical or qualified, and open about any connection. The rules are in the FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255. An owner's quote or story about your service is an endorsement.

| Rule | What the FTC text says | What it means for your case study |
|---|---|---|
| Honest experience | 16 CFR 255.1(a): endorsements "must reflect the honest opinions, findings, beliefs, or experience of the endorser" | Publish only what happened |
| Exact words | 16 CFR 255.1(b): quoted words must be the endorser's exact words and not reworded to distort | Do not edit a quote's meaning |
| Typical results | 16 CFR 255.2(b): if you lack proof the result is typical, disclose the generally expected result | Add your average next to a great result |
| No magic phrase | FTC FAQ: "Results not typical" or "Individual results may vary" do not change the reading | State a real average instead |
| Material connection | 16 CFR 255.5: discounts, free products, payment, family or business ties must be disclosed clearly | Write "We waived one month's fee for this owner" |
| No fake testimonials | 16 CFR 465.2 bans testimonials that misstate whether the person exists, used the service, or what happened to them | Never invent an owner or merge two owners |
| No paid sentiment | 16 CFR 465.4 bans rewards conditioned on a positive or negative review | Do not offer a discount for a 5-star story |

An example of a typical-result line: "Leased in 12 days. Our average for 3-bedroom homes in 2026 was 24 days." The second sentence must come from your own records.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What is the owner case study template?

The template below has 7 parts. It fits in 400 to 700 words on the page. Fill each bracket from records and the owner interview.

```text
TITLE
[Result in numbers] for a [property type] owner in [city]
Example format: "[N] days to lease a [type] in [city]"

SUMMARY (2-3 sentences)
[Owner first name or "An owner"] owned [number] [property type] in [area].
[The problem in one sentence.] After [Company] took over in [month year],
[the main result with a number].

THE OWNER BEFORE
- Properties: [count, type, area]
- Managed by: [self / another manager / none]
- Main problem: [vacancy, late rent, repairs, distance, time]
- Numbers before: [days vacant, rent, collections, hours a month]

WHAT WE DID
1. [Step, with date]
2. [Step, with date]
3. [Step, with date]

THE RESULT
- [Metric]: [before] -> [after], [period]
- [Metric]: [before] -> [after], [period]
- Typical result: [your average for similar homes, with period]

IN THE OWNER'S WORDS
"[Exact quote from the signed consent form]"
- [Name as agreed], [city]

DISCLOSURE
[One of these:]
- "[Company] gave nothing in exchange for this case study."
- "[Company] waived [fee] for this owner. The owner approved this text."
Results depend on the property, the market and the season.

NEXT STEP
[One link: rental analysis form or call booking]
```

## Where should case studies live on the site?

Case studies should live on their own pages, one owner per page, linked from the pages owners read most. A case study buried in a blog list gets few readers.

Use this checklist:

- [ ] **One URL per case study.** For example, `/case-studies/[city]-[property-type]`.
- [ ] **A case study index.** One page that lists all of them, with the result number in each card.
- [ ] **Links from owner pages.** Link the closest case study from each city page and the owner services page.
- [ ] **Links from your fee page.** Owners who read fees look for proof next.
- [ ] **A date on the page.** Show when the result happened and when you last checked it.
- [ ] **Review each year.** The FTC FAQ says old endorsements can stay if the claims are still accurate. The disclosures must match today's typical results.

## What is the average monthly management fee in Los Angeles?

The typical Los Angeles management fee on published price pages is 6% to 10% of monthly rent for a single home. We read 10 local manager pages on 2026-10-19. The lowest rate is 4.5% plus $59 a month. Flat plans run from $150 to $250 a month.

We used only pages where a manager states its own fee. A survey average for all of California does not tell you what an LA manager will put in your contract.

## What leasing and renewal fees do Los Angeles managers charge?

Los Angeles leasing fees run from $0 to one full month of rent on the pages we read. Half of one month is the most common figure. Some managers set a floor: PMI Los Angeles charges 50% of one month or $1,500, whichever is greater.

Renewal fees in Los Angeles are flat on every page that lists one: $75 (HCM), $190 (Welcome Home), $195 (PMI Los Angeles) and $250 (Bell, Ziprent).

Other line items show up on LA fee pages. PMI Los Angeles lists a 10% supervision markup on renovation and maintenance work. Welcome Home lists a $250 cancellation fee and shares tenant late fees 50/50 with the owner. Ask for these lines in writing.

## Which local rules add cost for Los Angeles owners?

The City of Los Angeles bills rental owners per unit each year, and a manager often tracks and pays these bills. The Los Angeles Housing Department (LAHD) billing fee schedule lists three annual fees.

| LAHD annual fee | Per unit, on time | Per unit, late |
|---|---|---|
| Rent Stabilization Ordinance (RSO) | $38.75 | $58.13 |
| Systematic Code Enforcement Program (SCEP) | $67.94 | $135.88 |
| Just Cause Ordinance (JCO) | $31.05 | $46.58 |

The bills are due by the last day of February. A 10-unit RSO building that also pays SCEP owes $1,066.90 a year on time. The same building pays $1,940.10 if both bills are late.

LAHD also bills for inspections and cases. A complaint inspection costs $201.50, or $503.75 if late. A property placed in the Rent Escrow Account Program (REAP) pays $50 per unit each month.

Some LA managers price this compliance work as a higher monthly rate. Others bill it as extra. Ask each manager which RSO, JCO and SCEP tasks the base fee covers.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Must a Los Angeles property manager hold a California license?

Yes, in most cases a Los Angeles property manager needs a California real estate broker license. Business and Professions Code section 10131(b) covers a person who, for pay, leases or rents property for others, solicits tenants or collects rents. That person acts as a real estate broker.

Section 10131.01 exempts some roles. A resident manager of an apartment building, and that manager's employees, do not need the license for that work.

Checklist before you sign:

1. **Get the license number** of the broker who will sign the agreement.
2. **Look it up** on the DRE Public License Lookup.
3. **Check the status** and any listed discipline.
4. **Match the name** on the license to the company on the contract.

## Flat fee or percent: which model is common in Los Angeles?

A percentage of rent is the common model in Los Angeles. Eight of the 10 managers in our table charge a percentage, often with a dollar minimum. Two use a flat monthly fee: Ziprent ($150) and HCM ($199 or $250).

High rents make a flat fee look good. At $2,964 rent, 8% is $237 a month. A $150 flat fee saves $87 a month, or $1,044 a year. But Ziprent's $1,500 leasing fee takes back most of the first year's savings. HCM's leasing fee of 5% of the annual lease value comes to $1,778 at this rent.

HUD's FY2027 fair market rent for a 2-bedroom in the Los Angeles area rose 2.1%, from $2,903 to $2,964. As rents rise, a percentage fee grows with them and a flat fee does not. See all bedroom sizes on the [Los Angeles fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/los-angeles-fair-market-rent-2027).

What to do: ask three managers for the full fee schedule. Put each one into the two-year table above. Then ask who files the LAHD bills and what that costs you.

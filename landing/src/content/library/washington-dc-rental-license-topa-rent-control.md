## Who needs a DC Basic Business License to rent a unit?

A DC owner who rents out housing needs a Basic Business License (BBL) from the Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection (DLCP). DLCP lists separate housing categories: One Family Rental, Two Family Rental, Apartment and others. You apply online in DLCP's BOSS system.

DLCP fees, read on 2026-10-19:

| License | 2-year license | 4-year license |
|---|---|---|
| One Family Rental | $149 | $298 |
| Two Family Rental | $199 | $398 |
| Apartment | Separate fee schedule | Separate fee schedule |

DLCP says it may ask for more papers or proof of inspection before it issues a license.

The license matters for more than the fee. Under D.C. Code 42-3502.08(a)(1), you cannot raise the rent above the base rent unless you are "properly licensed" and the unit is registered. A nonresident owner also needs a registered agent in DC for a rent increase. So an unlicensed unit can be a frozen-rent unit.

## Which DC rentals are under rent control?

DC rent control applies to each rental unit in the District, unless an exemption in D.C. Code 42-3502.05(a) applies. Most owners who are exempt fall into one of these groups:

| Exemption | Rule in 42-3502.05(a) |
|---|---|
| Newer building | Building permit issued after December 31, 1975 |
| New unit in an old building | Certificate of occupancy for housing issued after January 1, 1980 |
| Small owner | 4 or fewer units, owned by up to 4 natural persons with no other DC rental unit |
| Subsidized | Federally or District-owned or subsidized units, and LIHTC units, with limits |
| Co-op unit | Up to 4 natural persons who own no more than 4 DC rental units |

Watch for: the new-building exemption does not apply if the new building replaced a demolished rent-controlled building, unless it has more units than before. The small-owner exemption needs a filed claim of exemption, signed by every person with an interest. If ownership changes, report it to the Rent Administrator within 30 days.

The small-owner rule names "natural persons", which means people. So a property held by a company does not fit the plain text. The code makes one exception for an estate or testamentary trust.

## What is the yearly DC rent increase cap?

For Rent Control Year 2026, from May 1, 2026 to April 30, 2027, the cap is 4.1%. For a registered elderly tenant or tenant with a disability, the cap is 2.1% (DC Office of the Tenant Advocate, 2026-02-02).

How the number works:

1. **The Rental Housing Commission (RHC) publishes CPI-W.** That is the DC-area price index for urban wage earners, for the past calendar year (42-3502.06(b)). For 2026, OTA reports 2.1%.
2. **You may add 2 points for an occupied unit.** 2.1% + 2% = 4.1%. The total may not be more than 10% (42-3502.08(h)(2)(A)).
3. **Elderly and disabled tenants get a lower cap.** It is the lowest of the Social Security COLA (2.8%), CPI-W (2.1%) or 5%. So 2.1% for 2026.
4. **One increase per 12 months.** You cannot apply a new general increase within 12 months of the last one (42-3502.06(b)).
5. **Give 60 days' notice.** The increase starts on the first rent day more than 60 calendar days after notice (42-3509.04(b)).

A worked example for a $2,000 rent-controlled 2-bedroom:

| Tenant | Cap | Highest new rent |
|---|---|---|
| Not elderly, no disability | 4.1% | $2,082 |
| Registered elderly or disabled | 2.1% | $2,042 |

For a sense of the market: HUD's FY2027 fair market rent for a 2-bedroom in the DC metro is $2,438, up 8.5% from $2,246. That rise is far above the 4.1% cap, so in a rent-controlled unit, lawful rent can fall behind the market. See our [Washington DC fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/washington-dc-fair-market-rent-2027).

## Which DC rentals must register with the Rental Accommodations Division?

Every DC housing provider who receives rent registers the property or files a claim of exemption with the Rent Administrator, under D.C. Code 42-3502.05(f). Filing now goes through an online housing provider portal tied to the public rent control database.

The registration asks for:

- [ ] The address and type of the housing accommodation
- [ ] The number of bedrooms in each unit
- [ ] Your business information
- [ ] Your housing business license number, date and a copy
- [ ] Certificate of occupancy dates and numbers, where required
- [ ] For a covered unit: current rent, services and facilities, and any open housing code violations
- [ ] For an exempt unit: the date it became exempt and the rent just before that

A new housing provider files within 30 days after becoming the provider, once the database is complete (42-3502.05(f)(2)). An unregistered building cannot take a rent increase above base rent (42-3502.08(a)(1)(B)).

## What is TOPA and when does it apply to a sale?

TOPA, the Tenant Opportunity to Purchase Act, says that before you sell a housing accommodation, you must give the tenants an opportunity to buy it. The offer must be "a bona fide offer of sale" (D.C. Code 42-3404.02(a)). The same rule applies before a notice to vacate for demolition or to end housing use.

TOPA rights depend on the building size and the owner:

| Building | TOPA rule | Key periods |
|---|---|---|
| Single-family home | Exempt, but you must notify the tenant in writing within 3 days of an offer to buy (42-3404.09(b)) | Full rights for an elderly or disabled tenant who signed by March 31, 2018 and moved in by April 15, 2018 |
| 2 to 4 units, majority owned by a business corporation | Full TOPA | 15 days for a joint statement of interest, at least 90 days to negotiate, at least 90 days to settle (42-3404.10) |
| 2 to 4 units, other owners | Exempt, except the 3-day notice of an offer | None |
| 5 or more units | Full TOPA through a registered tenant organization | 45 days to register, at least 120 days to negotiate, at least 120 days to settle (42-3404.11) |

An offer of sale must state the price and terms, the tenant's rights, and whether a third-party contract exists. On request, you share 2 years of operating costs, the rent roll and a floor plan within 7 days (42-3404.03).

Watch for: a "sale" under TOPA can include the transfer of a majority interest in an LLC that owns the building (42-3404.02b). Transfers to family members and many lender foreclosures are excluded. The DC Council amended these sections in D.C. Law 26-80, dated December 31, 2025, so read the current text before you list.

## What should a DC owner do before the next lease?

Use this order:

1. **Get or renew the BBL.** Pick the right category in BOSS.
2. **Register or claim the exemption.** File in the Rent Administrator portal within 30 days of becoming the provider.
3. **Check rent control status.** Note the permit date, the owner type and the unit count.
4. **Plan the 2026 increase.** Use 4.1%, or 2.1% for registered elderly and disabled tenants, with 60 days' notice.
5. **Plan any sale early.** Map the TOPA periods before you sign with a buyer.

## What should the first email to an owner lead say?

The first email should answer the owner's question, add one useful fact and ask one easy question back. Send it within one business day, from a named person, in plain text. It should read like a note from a manager, not a newsletter.

Keep it under 150 words. Do not attach a brochure. Do not book a call yet unless the owner asked for one.

### Email 1 (day 0 or 1): personal answer

```text
Subject: Your rental on [street name]

Hi [first name],

Thanks for asking about [rental analysis / our fees / management for your home].

[One sentence that answers their question.]

One fact for your area: [one dated, sourced fact about rents,
days to lease, or local rules for their ZIP code].

I'm [name], and I'll be your contact. Quick question so I can help:
is the home empty now, or is a tenant in place?

[Name], [title]
[Company] · [phone]
[Postal address]
Reply "stop" or use this link to opt out: [link]
```

Our first-reply question also helps attribution. Goodjuju Marketing's 2026 study recommends asking every new lead "How did you hear about us?" in the first email. Most of its AI-sourced leads were found that way. Add it as a P.S. if the form did not ask.

## How many emails over how many weeks?

Seven emails over 10 weeks is a good starting plan. Space them out: close together at first, while the owner is still deciding, then further apart.

| # | Day | Topic | Owner question it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 to 1 | Personal answer | "Did a real person read my request?" |
| 2 | 3 | Rent and days to lease for their area | "What will my home earn?" |
| 3 | 7 | Full fee table, all fees listed | "What will this really cost?" |
| 4 | 14 | One owner story, with consent | "Do owners like you trust them?" |
| 5 | 24 | How you handle repairs, late rent, bad tenants | "What happens when things go wrong?" |
| 6 | 40 | How to switch from self-managing or another manager | "Is it hard to start?" |
| 7 | 70 | Check-in and an easy exit | "Can I say no without pressure?" |

Do not judge the sequence by open rates. Mailchimp's benchmark page warns that open rates may be affected by Apple's Mail Privacy Protection. Judge it by replies, calls booked and signed doors.

## Which content moves owners to a call?

Content that removes a fear or a question moves owners to a call. Owners rarely book because of a slogan. They book when they believe the numbers and the people.

### Email 3 (day 7): full fee table

```text
Subject: Every fee, on one page

Hi [first name],

Owners tell us fees are the hardest part to compare. Here is all of ours:

- Monthly management: [x]% of collected rent
- Leasing: [amount or %]
- Lease renewal: [amount]
- Maintenance markup: [none / x%]
- Setup: [amount or none]
- Cancel: [terms]

What is included: [list].
What is not included: [list].

Want me to run these numbers on your rent? Reply with your rent amount.

[Name] · [Company] · [Postal address] · Opt out: [link]
```

### Email 5 (day 24): when things go wrong

```text
Subject: What we do when rent is late

Hi [first name],

Here is our process, step by step:

1. Day [x]: [reminder]
2. Day [x]: [late fee per lease and local law]
3. Day [x]: [notice, as your state allows]
4. You get an update at each step.

Last year, [real number from your records, dated] of our tenants paid late
more than once. [One sentence on what you changed.]

[Name] · [Company] · [Postal address] · Opt out: [link]
```

Use only real numbers from your own records, with a date. If you do not track a number, leave it out.

Tag the links in each email. Google Analytics Help says to always use utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign, for example utm_source=nurture&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=owner-seq-email3. You will see which email sends owners to your site.

## When should I stop the owner nurture sequence?

Stop the sequence when one of four things happens:

1. **The owner signs.** Move them to your onboarding emails.
2. **The owner says no.** Thank them and stop.
3. **The owner opts out.** Remove them at once.
4. **The sequence ends.** After email 7, move silent leads to a monthly or quarterly newsletter, if they agreed to get one.

The law sets limits too. The FTC's CAN-SPAM guide says each commercial email needs a clear and conspicuous way to opt out and your valid physical postal address. You must honor an opt-out within 10 business days. Your opt-out must keep working for at least 30 days after you send. The FTC says each email in violation can bring penalties of up to $53,088. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

### Email 7 (day 70): easy exit

```text
Subject: Should I close your file?

Hi [first name],

I don't want to fill your inbox. If now isn't the time, that's fine.

Reply with one number:
1 – Let's talk this month
2 – Check back in 6 months
3 – Please stop emailing

Either way, thank you for thinking of us.

[Name] · [Company] · [Postal address] · Opt out: [link]
```

The fee email works best when owners can test your numbers. Link to the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) in email 3.

## What does E&O insurance cover for a property manager?

E&O insurance pays when a client says your professional work caused them a financial loss. Insureon describes it as cover for lawsuits over work that was unsatisfactory, negligent, late or never completed. The policy pays legal defense, settlements and judgments, up to the limit.

For a property management company, "client" usually means the owner. But tenants and applicants sue too. Insureon names a tenant discrimination claim and a wrongful eviction claim as two examples a property manager's E&O policy can cover.

Here are common claim types, put in property management terms:

| Claim type (Insureon) | What it looks like in property management |
|---|---|
| Work mistake or oversight | You enter the wrong rent on a renewal and the owner loses 12 months of income |
| Undelivered service | The agreement promised annual inspections and you did none |
| Missed deadline | You miss a notice deadline and the eviction restarts |
| Breach of contract | You spend above the repair limit in the agreement without approval |
| Misrepresentation | Your marketing overstates your track record and an owner relies on it |
| Poor communication | The owner expected monthly statements and got quarterly ones |

The right-hand column holds our examples, not Insureon's. Each insurer words its policy in its own way. Read the insuring clause and ask the broker which of these cases your policy pays.

## What does E&O insurance not cover?

E&O insurance does not cover injuries, damaged property or crimes. Insureon lists these exclusions for a standard E&O policy:

- **Customer injuries and property damage.** A visitor who trips at a showing is a general liability claim.
- **Employee injuries.** That is workers' compensation.
- **Employee discrimination lawsuits.** That needs employment practices liability (EPLI).
- **Illegal or intentional acts.** No policy pays for harm you meant to cause.
- **Damage to your own business property.** That is commercial property cover.
- **Auto accidents.** That is commercial auto, or hired and non-owned auto.
- **Suits you start.** E&O defends you. It does not fund a lawsuit you bring.

Two gaps catch property managers often. First, money that goes missing from a trust account through theft is usually a crime or fidelity matter, not E&O. Second, a data breach of tenant files usually needs a cyber policy. Ask the broker to name the exclusions that touch trust funds and tenant data.

## What does a $1 million E&O policy cost?

A $1 million E&O policy for a property manager costs a median of $83 per month, or $996 per year, according to Insureon. Insureon bases its figures on the median premium for property managers who apply for quotes through it. The median excludes very high and very low quotes.

The typical policy behind that number:

| Item | Insureon median for property managers |
|---|---|
| Premium | $83 per month ($996 per year) |
| Per-claim limit | $1,000,000 |
| Aggregate limit (all claims in the year) | $1,000,000 |
| Deductible | $1,000 |

To see the cost per door, divide the premium by your door count. This is our math on Insureon's median:

| Doors | Annual premium | Cost per door per year | Cost per door per month |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | $996 | $19.92 | $1.66 |
| 150 | $996 | $6.64 | $0.55 |
| 300 | $996 | $3.32 | $0.28 |

Two warnings. The median is for small firms that shop online. A company with 300 doors and a claims history will not pay the median. And premiums often grow with revenue and door count. So use the table to plan, and get real quotes.

## How do limits and deductibles change the price?

Higher limits raise the premium, and a higher deductible lowers it. Insureon names four main price factors for E&O:

1. **Coverage limits and deductible.** A $2 million limit costs more than $1 million. A $5,000 deductible costs less than $1,000.
2. **Claims history.** Past E&O claims make you a higher risk to the insurer.
3. **Type of business and work.** Managing a few single-family homes is a different risk from managing 40 HOAs.
4. **Contract requirements.** Some clients ask for higher limits or set terms that raise the cost.

A simple rule for the deductible: pick an amount you can pay from cash this week without stress. If one $5,000 claim would hurt your payroll, keep the deductible low.

For limits, start from your largest owner contract. If an owner's agreement asks for $2 million in E&O, you need $2 million, or you need to change the agreement.

## Why does "claims-made" matter so much?

E&O is a claims-made policy: the policy must be active both when the incident happens and when the claim is filed. Insureon explains this, and the Colorado Division of Real Estate warns about the same risk for brokers who leave a firm.

Here is how a gap happens:

- In 2026 you make an error on a lease renewal.
- In 2027 you switch insurers, and the new policy has no prior acts cover.
- In 2028 the owner sues over the 2026 error.
- Neither policy pays. The old one ended. The new one does not reach back.

Two tools close the gap:

- **Prior acts (retroactive date).** The new policy covers past work done after a set date. Ask for a retroactive date that matches your first policy.
- **Tail cover.** When you close, sell or leave a group policy, a tail extends the time to report claims on past work. Colorado's Division of Real Estate notes that brokers who leave a firm with a group policy may need to buy tail cover.

If you plan to sell your company, ask the buyer and your broker about tail cover early in the deal.

## Do states require E&O for property managers?

Some states require E&O for real estate licensees, and many property managers work under a broker license. Colorado is a clear example. The Colorado Division of Real Estate states that every active real estate licensee, including licensed companies with more than one broker, must carry an E&O policy that covers all acts that need a license. Colorado runs a group program through Rice Insurance Services. Licensees may buy elsewhere, but then they file a certificate of independent coverage.

Other states have no such rule. Rules change, and limits differ by state. Check your state's real estate commission page before you renew.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## E&O buying checklist

Use this list when you request quotes:

- [ ] Limits match your largest owner contract
- [ ] Deductible is an amount you can pay from cash
- [ ] Retroactive date reaches back to your first policy
- [ ] Policy names property management, not only sales, as a covered service
- [ ] Fair housing and wrongful eviction defense: covered, sub-limited or excluded (get it in writing)
- [ ] Trust fund theft: confirm which policy pays (crime or fidelity, not E&O)
- [ ] Tenant data breach: confirm a cyber policy, not E&O
- [ ] Tail cover price, in case you sell or close

E&O is one policy of several. Insureon's property manager data also lists general liability at $44 per month and a business owner's policy at $212 per month. If you are setting your fees, add insurance to your cost per door.

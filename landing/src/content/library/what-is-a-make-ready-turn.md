## What is a make-ready?

A make-ready is the work that gets a vacant unit ready for the next tenant. Property managers also call it a unit turn, a turnover or a rent-ready. The make-ready starts at move-out and ends when the unit passes a final check and can be shown or handed over.

Most make-readies follow the same 8 steps:

1. **Move-out inspection.** Walk the unit with the move-in report and take photos.
2. **Scope.** List every task: repair, replace, paint, clean.
3. **Approval.** Get owner approval for costs above the agreement limit.
4. **Repairs.** Fix doors, fixtures, appliances, drywall and anything unsafe.
5. **Paint.** Touch up or repaint walls.
6. **Floors.** Clean or replace carpet; clean hard floors.
7. **Clean.** A full clean after all trades finish.
8. **Final check.** Test every light, outlet, appliance, lock and smoke alarm, then sign off.

The order matters. Cleaning before paint wastes the clean. Paint before drywall repair wastes the paint.

## How long does a make-ready take?

A make-ready takes as long as its slowest task and its longest wait. Labor time is often shorter than wait time. A painter may need 1 day, but the next open slot may be 4 days out.

Here is a sample schedule for a 2-bedroom unit with normal wear and a few repairs. It is an example plan, not a benchmark.

| Day | Task | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Tenant moves out; inspection and photos | Manager |
| 1 | Scope and estimate; owner approval if needed | Manager |
| 2 | Repairs: drywall patches, door hardware, faucet | Handyman |
| 3 | Paint walls and trim | Painter |
| 4 | Carpet clean; hard floor clean | Flooring vendor |
| 5 | Full clean | Cleaner |
| 5 | Final check and sign-off; listing photos | Manager |

The plan takes 5 days because each trade starts the day after the last one ends. To shorten it, book vendors before move-out. You know the move-out date from the notice. Use the pre-move-out inspection, where your lease and state law allow one, to scope work early.

Watch for: long waits for parts, such as an appliance or a special flooring. What to do: order long-lead items on day 1, before other work starts.

## What does a make-ready cost?

A make-ready costs the repair and cleaning bills plus the rent lost while the unit is empty. The rent loss is often the bigger part.

For a large-property benchmark, Multifamily Dive (2023-10-11) reported a Zego survey of 630 property managers at communities of 250 units or more. Zego's 2023 Resident Experience Management Report put the average turnover cost at about $3,872. That total includes advertising and marketing, repairs, concessions and lost rent. It is a survey average for large communities, not a price for your unit.

Build your own number with this worked example:

| Cost line | How to calculate | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Lost rent | $1,650 rent ÷ 30 days × 21 days vacant | $1,155 |
| Repairs | Vendor invoices | $450 |
| Paint | Vendor invoice | $600 |
| Carpet clean | Vendor invoice | $180 |
| Full clean | Vendor invoice | $250 |
| Listing and showings | Your cost | $150 |
| Total turn cost | | $2,785 |

The math for lost rent: $1,650 ÷ 30 = $55 per day. 21 days × $55 = $1,155.

Each day saved is $55 back to the owner in this example. Cut 7 days and you save $385, more than double the carpet clean. That is why fast scheduling often matters more than a cheap vendor.

## Make-ready vs renovation: what is the difference?

A make-ready restores the unit to its rent-ready standard. A renovation changes the unit to raise its value or rent. The line matters for the budget, the approval and the tax treatment.

| Feature | Make-ready | Renovation |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Rent the unit as it was | Rent the unit for more |
| Typical work | Patch, paint, clean, small repairs | New kitchen, bath, flooring, layout |
| Time | Days | Weeks |
| Approval | Within the spend limit, or one owner approval | A written budget and bids |
| Owner report | Repairs and maintenance | Often a capital expense (CapEx) |
| Lost rent | Short | Long, so plan it |

Some turns sit in between. Replacing worn carpet in one bedroom is part of the make-ready. Replacing all floors in the unit with a new material is closer to a renovation. Ask the owner's tax adviser how to book it.

## Who approves make-ready costs?

The management agreement decides who approves make-ready costs. Many agreements give the manager a spend limit per job. Work above that limit needs the owner's approval before it starts.

Use a standard turn approval form so the owner can say yes fast:

- [ ] Move-out date and planned rent-ready date
- [ ] Photos of each problem
- [ ] Line items with vendor estimates
- [ ] Items to charge to the past tenant, with the reason
- [ ] Total cost to owner
- [ ] Lost rent per day of delay
- [ ] Owner approval: name, date, method

Show the lost rent per day on the form. It helps the owner see the cost of a slow answer.

Separate tenant damage from normal wear and tear. The owner pays for normal wear and tear. Damage beyond that may come from the past tenant's deposit, with an itemized list and the deadline your state law sets.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do you track make-readies across a portfolio?

Track make-readies with 2 numbers per turn: days vacant and turn cost. Then compare them across units and months.

1. **Days vacant.** Count from move-out to the new lease start.
2. **Days to rent-ready.** Count from move-out to the final sign-off.
3. **Turn cost.** Add vendor bills and lost rent.

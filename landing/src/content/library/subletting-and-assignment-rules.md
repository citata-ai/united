## What is the difference between a sublet and assignment?

A sublet creates an occupancy arrangement beneath the original tenancy. Assignment transfers the tenant's interest in the lease. In either case, identify the actual agreement rather than relying on the title of a form.

A tenant may call a replacement a roommate while planning to leave the property entirely. Another tenant may stay and share the home. Those facts can lead to different legal paths.

| Issue | Sublet | Assignment |
|---|---|---|
| Original lease | Usually remains the main tenancy | Tenant's lease interest transfers |
| Occupancy agreement | Separate sublease commonly used | Assignment and consent documents |
| Original tenant's liability | Usually continues, subject to law | Release needs specific review |
| Landlord review | Consent and any lawful screening | Consent, duties and possible release |
| Main drafting question | Who manages the subtenant relationship? | Who holds each duty after transfer? |

Use the table to identify questions for counsel. It does not decide the consent rule for your property.

Record the proposed occupants, dates and scope of use. Ask whether the original tenant will return. Those details matter more than a label added to a payment memo.

## Can a lease ban subletting?

A lease restriction works only within the applicable law. Do not assume that a prohibition in your form defeats a statutory tenant right.

New York RPP 226-b gives covered tenants in buildings with at least 4 residential units a subletting right subject to advance written consent. The landlord cannot unreasonably withhold that consent. The section defines a request and response process and identifies exceptions. [Read New York's statute](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-B).

Texas uses a different baseline. The Texas State Law Library explains that subletting generally requires the landlord's consent under Property Code section 91.005. Check the lease and governing rule together. [Read the Texas guide](https://guides.sll.texas.gov/landlord-tenant-law/ending-the-lease).

A new occupant is not always a subtenant. New York RPP 235-f separately restricts certain occupancy bans. Review that distinction before treating an added roommate as an unauthorized sublet.

Have counsel review the policy before staff send a rejection. The approved process should tell staff which requests need reasons and which deadlines apply.

## Should I screen a proposed subtenant?

Use the lawful review allowed for the request, with clear criteria and limited data collection. Approval of a sublet and a new direct tenancy are different decisions.

Ask for the details that local law permits and your reviewed policy needs. Identify the proposed person, occupancy period and agreement. Do not demand unrelated private information simply because the request is inconvenient.

If you use a consumer report, follow the applicable consumer reporting process. The FTC explains that an adverse housing decision based partly on a report triggers notice duties. [Read the FTC landlord guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/using-consumer-reports-what-landlords-need-know).

Keep reasons factual. A documented failure to meet lawful criteria differs from a preference for a certain kind of household. Avoid personal judgments about relationships or background.

Do not impose a new application fee before checking local limits. If the law requires a particular request process, use it. A software workflow does not override the consent rule.

## Who owes rent during a sublet?

The original tenant commonly remains responsible under the main lease, but verify the contract and governing law. A subtenant's payment to the tenant does not itself prove the landlord received rent.

New York RPP 226-b expressly preserves the original tenant's obligations when a covered sublet receives consent. Assignment and release need a separate review. Do not apply that state's rule as a nationwide answer.

State who receives payment and who handles maintenance contact. If the landlord accepts a direct payment, record the payer and the account credited. Ask counsel whether the practice affects the legal relationship.

Avoid collecting the same rent from both people. Track charges under the actual agreement rather than opening duplicate rent accounts without a basis.

Explain deposit treatment separately. Money paid between the original tenant and subtenant can create a different obligation from the deposit you hold. Do not settle that private account from your deposit funds without proper authority.

## How do I write and manage consent?

Write consent around the exact arrangement and preserve the original lease. A general message saying everything is fine can leave liability and return rights unclear.

Use this recommended consent checklist:

- **Describe the arrangement.** Identify sublet or assignment based on the actual facts.
- **Name the parties.** Record the original tenant and proposed occupant or assignee.
- **State the dates.** Clarify when the arrangement starts and ends.
- **Address liability.** Explain duties and any express release after legal review.
- **Set communication.** Identify repair contacts, payment handling and notice addresses.
- **Preserve the terms.** Attach approved documents and retain the original lease.
- **Review changes.** Require the lawful process for a later extension or replacement.

Check whether a guarantor must consent to the change. Also check insurance, subsidy and association terms where relevant. These reviews should support the lawful process, not create a hidden veto.

Give the parties the final signed documents and a clear contact for questions. Update system access only after the arrangement is settled. Keep the old record so staff can explain earlier payments.

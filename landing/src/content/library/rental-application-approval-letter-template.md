## What should a rental application approval letter include?

A rental application approval letter should include 7 items: the applicant, the unit, the approval, the lease terms, the move-in funds, the deadline and any conditions. The letter turns a "yes" into a clear next step. A vague "you're approved, call us" letter leads to missed deadlines and an empty unit.

Use this checklist before you send:

- [ ] Applicant names, as on the application
- [ ] Unit address and number
- [ ] Monthly rent and due date
- [ ] Lease start date and lease length
- [ ] Every move-in charge, with a total
- [ ] Deadline to sign the lease and pay, with date and time
- [ ] Any conditions (co-signer, extra deposit) and the reason
- [ ] How to pay (portal, certified funds) and who to contact

### Copy-ready approval letter template

**Subject: Your rental application for [unit address] is approved**

Date: ________

Dear ________,

Thank you for your application for [unit address, unit number]. We are glad to tell you that your application is approved.

**Lease terms**

- Monthly rent: $________, due on the ____ of each month
- Lease start date: ________
- Lease length: ____ months, ending ________

**Move-in funds due before you get keys**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| First month's rent (or prorated rent) | $________ |
| Security deposit | $________ |
| Pet deposit or pet fee (if any) | $________ |
| Other charges listed in the lease | $________ |
| Total due | $________ |

**Next steps**

1. Sign the lease by ________ at ____ (time). We will send it to you by ________.
2. Pay the total above by ________ by ________ (payment method).
3. Book your move-in time and key pickup with ________.

If we do not receive the signed lease and funds by the deadline, we may offer the unit to the next applicant.

**Conditions (delete if none)**

This approval depends on: ________.

If a condition above is based in whole or in part on a consumer report, please read the attached notice about your rights.

Questions? Contact ________ at ________.

Sincerely,
________, [title], [company]

This is a generic template, not a state legal form. Check your local law before you use it.

## How long does the applicant have to sign?

The applicant has as long as your letter says. No federal rule sets the deadline, so put a clear date and time in the letter. A short deadline keeps the unit moving. A deadline that is too short can push out good applicants who need a day to move money.

Pick one rule for your company and use it for every applicant. The same deadline for every applicant is fair and easy to defend. It also follows the spirit of the Fair Housing Act, which bans discrimination based on race or color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status and disability (U.S. Department of Justice).

A sample deadline table:

| When you send the letter | Sign and pay by | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Monday, 10:00 | Wednesday, 17:00 | 2 business days |
| Friday, 15:00 | Tuesday, 17:00 | Weekend does not count |
| Day before a holiday | 2 business days after | Banks may be closed |

Check your state and city rules too. Some places have rules on holding deposits or on how long an applicant may hold a unit. Write those limits into your standard letter.

## What move-in funds should the letter list?

The letter should list every dollar due before move-in, with a total. Surprise charges at key pickup cause disputes and delay the move-in. Each charge in the letter should match a line in the lease.

Here is a worked example of prorated rent. The lease starts on the 20th of a 30-day month. The rent is $1,500.

1. Daily rent: $1,500 ÷ 30 = $50.
2. Days the tenant lives in the unit that month: the 20th to the 30th = 11 days.
3. Prorated rent: 11 × $50 = $550.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Prorated rent (11 days) | $550 |
| Security deposit | $1,500 |
| Pet deposit | $300 |
| Total due before keys | $2,350 |

Some managers collect the first full month instead of prorated rent, then prorate the next month. Either way, say which method you use in the letter. Some states limit the size of a security deposit, so check the cap before you set it.

## Should you include conditions (guarantor, deposit)?

Include conditions in the letter when they apply, and state them plainly. A condition might be a co-signer or guarantor, a larger deposit, or proof of income before keys. The applicant should know the condition before they sign.

A condition can trigger a federal notice. The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) says a landlord takes an "adverse action" when, based on a consumer report, it does any of these:

- Denies the application
- Requires a co-signer
- Requires a deposit not required of other applicants, or a larger deposit
- Charges higher rent than another applicant

The FTC says the notice is required even if the report was only a minor factor. So a conditional approval based on a screening report needs an adverse action notice with the letter.

Under 15 U.S.C. 1681m(a), the notice must include:

1. The name, address and phone number of the consumer reporting agency that supplied the report.
2. A statement that the agency did not make the decision and cannot explain the reasons.
3. The applicant's right to a free copy of the report if they ask within 60 days.
4. The applicant's right to dispute wrong or incomplete information.
5. Any credit score used, with its range, source, date and key factors.

The FTC says oral notice is allowed, but written notice is the best practice. Put the notice on its own page, attached to the approval letter.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What do you do after the applicant signs?

After the applicant signs, confirm receipt of the lease and funds in writing. Then close the listing and tell other applicants in a timely way.

Use this short checklist:

- [ ] Countersign the lease and send a full copy
- [ ] Post the funds to the tenant ledger
- [ ] Book the move-in inspection with photos
- [ ] Send utility and renter's insurance steps
- [ ] Take the listing down
- [ ] Tell other applicants the unit is no longer available
- [ ] Securely dispose of screening reports you no longer need, as the FTC disposal rule requires

The FTC says you must securely dispose of consumer reports when you are done with them. Shred paper. Delete digital files so they cannot be read or rebuilt.

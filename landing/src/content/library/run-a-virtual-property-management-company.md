## Which tasks need people on the ground?

A virtual management company needs local people for work that requires safe physical access. Map those jobs before you move desk work online. Treat local coverage as part of the service, with a named lead and a backup.

Buildium's virtual-company guide describes remote management supported by software and outsourced field work. That is an operating model, not permission to ignore local license rules. Decide which tasks your staff own and which a field partner handles.

Walk through a vacancy from notice to move-in. Someone may need to inspect the home, secure keys, check repair work and document condition. A remote coordinator can book those visits. The coordinator cannot confirm physical facts that nobody checked.

Repeat the exercise for an occupied home. Plan for failed access, a resident who cannot use the portal and an urgent call during staff leave. Do not accept a property solely because its address falls inside an online service map.

Give field partners written scope and access controls. State who may enter, how staff confirm authority, and where photos go. Review missed visits and resident complaints with the same care you apply to employees.

## How do I handle showings and inspections remotely?

Remote coordination works when lawful local staff perform the physical task and return useful evidence. Choose the method after checking state rules, property condition and resident access needs.

Texas offers a clear caution. TREC's unlicensed-assistant guidance says unlicensed assistants cannot show a broker's listings, including opening doors or allowing access. TREC permits clerical scheduling. Do not turn a scheduling job into an unlawful showing assignment.

Use a field-work checklist like this:

- **Confirm authority.** Check who may perform the task in that state.
- **Confirm access.** Verify the appointment and the permitted entry process.
- **Define evidence.** State which rooms, equipment and conditions need records.
- **Protect records.** Keep access codes and resident information out of public folders.
- **Review findings.** Have the responsible manager read the report before closing work.
- **Assign follow-up.** Give each unresolved condition a named person and next action.

These are proposed operating steps, not a substitute for local notice rules. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

For inspections, distinguish a documented observation from a specialist diagnosis. A photo can show damage. It may not establish the cause or the correct repair. Send uncertain issues to the right qualified trade.

## How do I manage a remote team?

Manage a remote team through clear ownership, shared records and planned handoffs. Use an agreed place for work orders, owner decisions and account questions. Personal inboxes should not hold the only copy of a service record.

Assign each open matter a lead and a backup. Make the backup test access before covering a shift. A name in a handbook is not enough if that person cannot reach the phone system or find the lease.

Write short task instructions around the decisions staff face. Explain when to seek broker review, owner approval or specialist help. Keep sensitive access limited to the work each person needs.

Set coverage by the property's service hours, not just the employee's location. Show shift changes in the work queue. Require an explicit handoff for unresolved urgent matters so the next person knows they own them.

Measure output that matters: useful replies, completed visits, clean reconciliations and resolved complaints. Avoid counting logged-in time as proof of service. Staff need time to think, call and document work.

For software selection, compare your workflows with the [AppFolio and Buildium guide](/guides/appfolio-vs-buildium). Ask for a demonstration of permissions, mobile field reports and the process for exporting records.

## What do owners think of no office?

Owners can judge a virtual company by its service plan, but you need to test their concerns directly. Ask prospects what they expect a local office to provide. Then explain how your company meets that need.

Some owners may want a place to bring documents. Others may care about a manager visiting the home. Do not treat those needs as the same objection. Offer a secure document route and a clear plan for local appointments.

Show who handles field work, where the service area ends and how urgent calls reach a person. Describe actual coverage. Do not imply a contractor is an employee or claim an office you do not have.

Check license, registered-office and record-access rules before promoting a company as office-free. A remote service model and a lawful business address are separate questions. Ask your supervising broker to approve the setup.

Keep savings claims tied to your own budget. Removing a lease does not remove field travel, secure equipment, insurance or backup coverage. Build a full cost plan before changing fees.

Let an owner follow a sample maintenance request from report to closure. A clear demonstration can answer more than a long list of software brands.

## How should I test a virtual model?

Test the model on a defined service area before expanding. Choose properties that your field team can reach and keep current service promises during the trial. Record every extra visit and handoff.

Run a simulated software outage. Have staff find emergency contacts, access instructions and the escalation route without their normal dashboard. Repair any gap before relying on remote coverage.

Review the cost of desk work and field work separately. Include contractor travel, failed appointments, training and quality review. A virtual model is useful only if the service still covers its full cost.

Ask residents whether they know how to get help. Ask owners whether they know who is responsible for their property. Use those answers to improve the handoff instructions.

Keep a written decision on what the trial proved and what remains uncertain. Then update your onboarding plan and service area. Browse the [guide library](/guides) for the related systems your remote team must support.

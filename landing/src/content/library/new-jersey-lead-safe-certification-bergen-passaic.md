## Which New Jersey rentals need a lead-safe certificate?

Most New Jersey rentals built before 1978 need a lead-based paint inspection under P.L.2021, c.182. The NJ Department of Community Affairs (DCA) says all single-family, two-family and multiple rental dwellings must be inspected unless an exemption applies. A clean inspection gives you a lead-safe certificate.

| Exempt from the inspection | Per NJ DCA |
|---|---|
| Built in 1978 or later | Yes |
| Single and two-family seasonal rentals, rented under 6 months a year, with no consecutive lease renewals | Yes |
| Certified free of lead-based paint under N.J.A.C. 5:17-3.6(b) | Yes |
| Multiple dwellings registered with DCA for at least 10 years with no outstanding paint violations | Yes |
| A unit with a valid lead-safe certificate | Yes, while it is valid |

Everything else is in scope. That includes a single-family home in Ridgewood or a three-family in Paterson, if built before 1978.

What to do: find the year built on your tax record. If it is before 1978, plan the inspection before the next tenant moves in.

## When must the inspection happen?

The first New Jersey lead inspection was due by July 22, 2024, or earlier at tenant turnover. DCA explains it this way: the law took effect on July 22, 2022, and the first inspection had to happen within two years or at turnover, whichever came first.

After the first inspection, the cycle is:

1. **Every 3 years**, or
2. **At tenant turnover**, whichever is earlier.
3. **No turnover inspection** is needed if you hold a valid lead-safe certificate. A certificate is valid for 2 years.
4. **Expired certificate at turnover:** you need a new inspection.

A worked timeline: you get a lead-safe certificate on 2026-03-01. A tenant leaves on 2027-06-30. The certificate is still valid until 2028-03-01, so no new inspection is needed for that turnover. A second turnover on 2028-08-31 needs a new inspection, because the certificate has expired.

The inspection method depends on your town's data. DCA uses one test: the share of tested children aged 6 or under with a blood lead level of 5 micrograms per deciliter or more. Below 3%, a visual inspection may be used. At 3% or above, dust wipe sampling is required.

## Who does the inspection and what does it cost?

Your municipality must inspect, or hire a certified lead evaluation contractor. It must also let you hire a certified contractor yourself. The statute (N.J.S.A. 52:27D-437.16) says the town charges the owner a fee to cover the cost.

| Cost item | Amount | Source |
|---|---|---|
| State fee per unit inspected | $20 | N.J.S.A. 52:27D-437.16(h) |
| Clifton: first city inspection, 1-bedroom unit | $300, plus $30 per extra bedroom | Clifton Code Ch. 273 |
| Clifton: later visual inspection | $150 for 1 unit, $275 for 2 units on one property | Clifton Code Ch. 273 |
| Paterson: lead inspection by the Division of Health | $300 for the first unit in a 1 to 3-family dwelling, plus $150 per extra unit | Paterson Code, Inspections |
| Private certified contractor | Market price | Ask for 2 quotes |

A worked example in Clifton: a 3-bedroom unit's first city inspection costs $300 + 2 x $30 = $360, plus the $20 state fee, or $380.

If the inspection finds hazards, you must fix them by abatement or lead hazard control. Then you get the certificate.

Penalty: if you fail to comply, the town gives you 30 days to cure. After that, the penalty can reach $1,000 per week until you complete the inspection or start remediation.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Which Bergen and Passaic towns also need a certificate of occupancy at turnover?

Some Bergen and Passaic towns add their own certificate on a change of tenant, on top of the state lead rule. Three examples from town codes:

| Town (county) | Rule at change of occupancy | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| Garfield (Bergen) | File a dwelling certificate with the Zoning Officer no more than 60 days before a change of occupancy. An inspection is required on a new lease or rental, with some exceptions. Proof of lead inspection and remediation is required. | $60 per dwelling unit, including the smoke detector certification; $60 for a return visit |
| Paterson (Passaic) | Get a certificate of inspection from the Division of Health before a tenant takes occupancy. Units are evaluated by dust wipe every 3 years or at turnover. | Lead inspection $300 first unit, $150 each extra; exemption certificate $150 per unit |
| Clifton (Passaic) | Lead-based paint inspection at turnover or every 3 years under Ch. 273, Art. IV | $300 first inspection for a 1-bedroom, plus $30 per extra bedroom |

Paterson exempts owner-occupied single-family homes and premises built after 1980 from its own inspection chapter, if you get a certificate of exemption.

Other towns in both counties may have similar rules. Search your town's code on eCode360, or call the building or housing office before you sign a new lease.

Rent context: HUD's FY2027 fair market rent for a 2-bedroom in the Bergen-Passaic area is $2,282 a month, down 1.8%. A $380 inspection is about 17% of one month's rent. See the [Bergen-Passaic fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/bergen-passaic-fair-market-rent-2027).

Turnover checklist for a Bergen or Passaic owner:

- [ ] Confirm the year built. Before 1978 means the state rule applies.
- [ ] Check the date on your lead-safe certificate.
- [ ] Book the inspection before the move-out, so you can fix hazards in the gap.
- [ ] File your town's dwelling or occupancy certificate, if it has one.
- [ ] Give the new tenant a copy of the lead-safe certificate.
- [ ] Keep the certificate with the tenant's name in your records.

A manager can schedule all of this at each turnover. To compare costs, use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

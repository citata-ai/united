## How long do rental appliances last?

Most major appliances last 9 to 17 years, by the published estimates. The real life of a rental appliance depends on the model, the care and the number of tenants who use it.

Two sources give the range. The Association of Home Appliance Manufacturers (AHAM) published estimates in 2019, which NerdWallet reported in 2023. An older National Association of Home Builders (NAHB) table appears in Fannie Mae's 2008 guide "Becoming a Landlord".

| Appliance | AHAM 2019 (years) | NAHB via Fannie Mae 2008 (years) |
|---|---|---|
| Gas range | 14.2 | 14 (built-in) |
| Electric range | 11.6 | 17 (built-in) |
| Top-freezer refrigerator | 14 | Not listed |
| Side-by-side refrigerator | 11.1 | Not listed |
| Dishwasher | 12 | 10 |
| Dryer | 13.4 | 14 |
| Top-load washer | 11.2 | 13 (automatic washer) |
| Front-load washer | 9.1 | Not listed |
| Gas water heater | Not listed | 11 to 13 |
| Electric water heater | Not listed | 14 |

Use these numbers as a planning guide, not a promise. Write the install date on a label inside each appliance door. Keep the same date in your property records.

## What is the 50% rule for repair vs replace?

The 50% rule says: replace an appliance when the repair costs more than half the price of a new one. In the past, Consumer Reports gave this advice, according to NerdWallet's 2023 report.

Consumer Reports has since refined its advice. NerdWallet reports it used member feedback on 528,000 products bought from 2012 through 2022. Its tools now weigh the purchase price, the age and the repair cost. In general, the more you paid for the appliance, the more likely the tool will advise a repair.

For a rental, add 2 more factors: the age against the expected life, and the cost of downtime for the tenant. Here is a simple 4-step test.

1. **Find the age.** Check the label or your records.
2. **Find the expected life.** Use the table above.
3. **Get the repair quote and a replacement quote.** Include delivery, install and haul-away.
4. **Apply the test below.**

| Age vs expected life | Repair cost vs new | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Under half | Under 50% | Repair |
| Under half | 50% or more | Compare: repair if the model is good and parts are easy to get |
| Over half | Under 25% | Repair, and plan the replacement |
| Over half | 25% to 50% | Lean to replace |
| Any age | 50% or more | Replace |

The 25% line is our own planning rule, not a published standard.

A worked example:

- A 10-year-old dishwasher. Expected life: 12 years (AHAM).
- Repair quote: $280. New unit installed: $700.
- Repair ratio: $280 / $700 = 40%.
- Age ratio: 10 / 12 = 83%, so it is past half its life.
- Result: lean to replace. A $280 repair buys at most about 2 more expected years.

## Must a landlord provide appliances?

In most states, a landlord does not have to provide appliances. But if you do provide them, many states make you maintain them. Read the law in your state.

| State | Rule | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| California | A stove and a refrigerator in good working order are required for leases entered, amended or extended on or after January 1, 2026. A recalled refrigerator does not count. | Civil Code 1941.1(a)(10) and (11) |
| Oregon | Appliances "supplied or required to be supplied by the landlord" must be kept in good repair | ORS 90.320(1)(i) |
| Florida | The statute does not name appliances. It requires heat, running water and hot water in units other than single-family homes and duplexes, unless agreed otherwise in writing | Fla. Stat. 83.51(2)(a) |

California's new rule has more detail. Under Civil Code 1941.1, a landlord must repair or replace a recalled stove or refrigerator within 30 days of notice. The tenant and landlord may agree that the tenant provides and maintains the tenant's own refrigerator, under set conditions. Some housing types are exempt, such as single-room occupancy units and units with shared kitchens.

Your lease matters too. If the lease lists a washer and dryer, the tenant can expect them to work. If you do not want to maintain an appliance, say so in the lease before the tenant signs.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How does the IRS treat appliance costs?

The IRS treats a repair and a replacement differently. IRS Publication 527 says an expense for repairing or maintaining your rental may generally be deducted. It says you must capitalize any expense that improves the property.

A new appliance is a capital asset. Publication 527 puts "appliances, carpeting, and furniture used in a residential rental real estate activity" in the 5-year class under the General Depreciation System. The building itself uses 27.5 years.

This is general information, not tax advice. Ask a tax professional.

## Should I buy home warranties?

A home warranty can help, but only if the contract covers the appliances you have at a fair cost. The California Department of Insurance says "All home warranties contain dozens of exclusions." It adds that buyers often find the exclusions only after a claim is denied. Warranties also have dollar limits on what the company pays.

Compare a home warranty with a repair fund before you buy:

| Question | Home warranty | Your own repair fund |
|---|---|---|
| Who picks the technician? | The warranty company, in most contracts | You |
| Is there a cost per visit? | Often a service fee per call | Only the repair bill |
| Is there a cap? | Often a dollar limit per item | Your fund limit |
| What is excluded? | Read the contract list | Nothing, you decide |
| How fast is service? | Depends on the company | Depends on your vendors |

Before you buy, the California Department of Insurance advises you to check the company's license, read the contract and make sure it covers your major appliances and systems. Do not pay for coverage you do not need, such as a pool you do not have.

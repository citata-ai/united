## Which company facts does AI repeat most?

AI assistants repeat the facts that a landlord needs to pick and call a manager. These are the name, the city, the phone, the website, the rating, the fees and the property types. An AI answer about "best property manager in Denver" is a short list. Each line on that list is a few facts about one company.

The engines take those facts from many places, not only your site. In our city run (our data, 2026-10), ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini answered 150 property manager prompts. Expertise.com was a source in 93 answers. iPropertyManagement.com and Yelp were sources in 50 each. If those 3 sites hold an old phone or an old fee, the answer can repeat it.

SOCi's 2026 Local Visibility Index shows how common this is. Only 68% of business contact data on ChatGPT and Perplexity matched what Google Business Profiles show. SOCi also tells businesses to claim Apple Business Connect and Bing Places, because both feed data that AI assistants use.

The fix is simple to say. Write each fact once, in one sheet. Copy it everywhere.

## What goes in the entity facts sheet template?

The entity facts sheet holds 4 groups of facts. Copy the template below into a shared document. Fill every field. Leave a field empty only if it does not apply.

## How do you write door count and service area?

Write door count as one number, one unit and one date. "312 doors (units) under management, 2026-10-01" is clear. "300+ properties" is not. A building with 12 apartments is 12 doors, not 1 property. Pick the rule and use it on every site.

Do not round up, and do not mix counts. If your site says 312 and Expertise.com says 450, an engine may quote either one. Owners may also ask which number is true.

Write the service area as a list of real places. Name the cities, counties or ZIP codes. "Chicago and the North Shore" is vague. "Chicago, Evanston, Skokie, Wilmette, Oak Park" is exact. Google's guidelines say a service area should not extend farther than about 2 hours of driving from your base. Only list places where you really take new owners.

| Fact | Weak version | Strong version |
|---|---|---|
| Door count | "Hundreds of happy owners" | "312 doors, 2026-10-01" |
| Service area | "Greater Chicagoland" | "Chicago, Evanston, Skokie, Oak Park" |
| Fee | "Low, fair fees" | "8% of collected rent, $0 when vacant" |
| Property types | "All property types" | "Single-family and 2–24 unit buildings" |
| Founded | "Decades of experience" | "Founded 2009" |

## Which rules apply to name, address and phone?

Google Business Profile has strict rules, and other sites copy from it. Use them as the base for every listing.

- **Name.** Google says the name "should reflect your business's real-world name". It bans taglines, phone numbers and service descriptions in the name. So "Acme Property Management", not "Acme Property Management – Best Chicago Rentals".
- **Address.** Google says P.O. boxes and remote mailboxes are not acceptable. A virtual office is not eligible for a Business Profile.
- **Home office.** Google says a service-area business "should hide your business address from customers".
- **Phone.** The number must connect to your business location and be under your direct control. Avoid call-tracking numbers that differ by site, unless the main number is also listed.
- **Website.** Google says to give a site that represents your business location, with no redirects to a landing page.

If you use schema on your site, copy the same facts into it. Google's local business markup requires `name` and `address`. Google's AI features guide says structured data must match the visible text on the page.

## Where do you publish the facts sheet?

Publish the facts sheet in 3 places. Keep the full sheet as an internal document. Put the public parts on your website. Then copy the public parts into each listing.

1. **Internal.** A shared doc or spreadsheet with an owner and a "last checked" date. This is the source of truth.
2. **Your website.** An About page with name, address, phone, founding year, door count, service area and license. A fees page with the full fee table.
3. **Listings.** Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp, Expertise.com, iPropertyManagement.com, BBB and your social profiles.

The listings in step 3 matter most for AI answers in our data. For more on where landlords meet AI answers, read [how landlords use AI to choose a property manager](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai).

## How often do you update the facts sheet?

Check the facts sheet every quarter. Update it the same day when a key fact changes. Use this schedule:

| Trigger | Action | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Every quarter | Check each listing against the sheet | 1–2 hours |
| Every quarter | Update door count and review counts | 15 minutes |
| New phone, address or hours | Change sheet, then every listing | Same day |
| New fee or contract term | Change sheet, site fee page, listings | Same week |
| New owner, merger or rebrand | Update name everywhere, add old name note on site | Same week |

The time estimates are our working numbers, not measured data. After each update, ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity "What is the phone number of <your company>?" and "What does <your company> charge?" Write down any wrong answer.

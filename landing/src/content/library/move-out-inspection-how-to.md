## When do I do the move-out inspection after keys come back?

Do the move-out inspection on the day the keys come back, or the next business day. The deposit clock usually starts when the tenant leaves. Each day you wait gives a new event a chance to blur the record: a leak, a break-in, a vendor's mistake.

Some states set the time. Virginia Code 55.1-1226(G) says that if the tenant asks to be present, the inspection must happen within 72 hours of delivery of possession. New York General Obligations Law 7-108 adds an inspection before move-out, if the tenant asks for one.

Then the deposit deadline runs. The table shows 3 states.

| State | Deposit statement deadline | Pre-move-out inspection | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| New York | Itemized statement and balance within 14 days after the tenant vacates | Tenant may ask for one, no earlier than 2 weeks and no later than 1 week before the end; 48 hours' written notice | GOL 7-108(1-a)(d), (e) |
| Florida | Return within 15 days if no claim; or send a certified-mail notice of claim within 30 days | Not in 83.49 | F.S. 83.49(3)(a) |
| Virginia | Itemized written notice within 45 days after tenancy ends or tenant vacates, whichever is last | Tenant may ask to attend the move-out inspection | Va. Code 55.1-1226 |

The penalties are real. New York says a landlord who misses the 14-day statement forfeits any right to keep part of the deposit. Florida says a landlord who misses the 30-day notice forfeits the right to claim against the deposit.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Should the tenant attend the move-out inspection?

The tenant should be invited to the move-out inspection, and in some states the law requires the offer. A tenant who sees the damage in person argues less later. A tenant who was not invited can say the landlord made it up.

Two state rules show the range:

- **Virginia:** Code 55.1-1226(G) says the landlord must tell the tenant in writing of the right to be present. The landlord must send it when asking the tenant to leave, or within 5 days after the tenant's notice to leave. If the tenant asks in writing, the landlord sets a date and time within 72 hours of delivery of possession.
- **New York:** GOL 7-108(1-a)(d) says the landlord must tell the tenant in writing of the right to ask for an inspection before move-out. After that inspection, the landlord gives an itemized list of proposed deductions. The tenant can fix those items before the tenancy ends.

Where your state has no rule, invite the tenant anyway. Put the invite in writing and keep it in the file. If the tenant does not come, note that in the report.

## How do I compare move-in and move-out photos?

Compare move-in and move-out photos by room, in the same order, from the same angles. The move-in report is your baseline. Without it, most damage claims become one word against another.

Use these steps:

1. **Open the move-in report** and photos on a tablet before you enter.
2. **Walk the same route**: entry, living room, kitchen, each bath, each bedroom, then outside.
3. **Shoot the same angles** as the move-in set. Stand in the same spot for each wide shot.
4. **Take close-ups** of every new mark, with a coin or tape measure for scale.
5. **Turn on the timestamp** in your camera or app.
6. **Test each item**: taps, toilets, burners, oven, fridge, lights, smoke alarms, windows, locks.
7. **Mark each line** as same, wear and tear, or damage.
8. **Write a cost** next to each damage line, with a vendor quote or invoice later.
9. **Sign and date the report.** Ask the tenant to sign if present.

The hard part is the line between wear and tear and damage. New York GOL 7-108 bars any deduction for ordinary wear and tear. Virginia Code 55.1-1226 lets the landlord deduct damages "less reasonable wear and tear." The table gives common examples. Courts decide close cases, so write down age and condition.

| Usually wear and tear | Usually damage |
|---|---|
| Light scuffs on walls from furniture | Holes in walls, large gouges |
| Carpet worn flat in walkways after years of use | Burns, pet stains, tears in carpet |
| Faded paint or blinds from sun | Broken blinds or missing slats |
| Loose door handle from use | Door off its hinges or kicked in |
| Small nail holes from pictures | Unapproved paint colors |

## What do I do with left-behind items?

Do not throw out left-behind items until you check your state's abandoned property rule. Many states require a written notice and a wait before you may dispose of items.

Florida gives a clear example. Florida Statutes 715.104 says the landlord must send written notice to the former tenant and to anyone the landlord thinks owns the items. The notice must describe the items and say where to claim them. The claim date must be at least 10 days after hand delivery, or at least 15 days after mailing.

During the inspection, photograph every item left behind. List each item in the report. Do not move or sell anything until the notice period ends.

## What goes in the move-out report?

The move-out report lists each room, each item, its condition, and the cost of any damage. It is the backbone of the deposit statement. Keep it short, exact and tied to photos.

Use this checklist:

- **Header:** address, unit, tenant names, move-in date, move-out date, inspection date and time.
- **Attendees:** who was there, or a note that the tenant was invited and did not come.
- **Rooms:** one block per room, with each item marked same, wear, or damage.
- **Photo IDs:** a photo number next to each damage line.
- **Costs:** a dollar amount per line, with invoices to follow.
- **Keys:** count of keys, fobs and remotes returned.
- **Meters:** utility readings if the tenant pays utilities.
- **Signatures:** inspector and tenant, if present.

Send the deposit statement with the report attached, before the state deadline. Send it by a method you can prove, such as certified mail in Florida.

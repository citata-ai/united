## Which lease violations need a written notice?

Any lease violation you may act on later needs a written notice. A text that says "please move the car" is fine for a first, small issue. But before you can end a tenancy for a violation, most states require a written notice in a set form and with a set wait time.

Common lease violations:

| Violation | Can the tenant usually cure it? | Example in state law |
|---|---|---|
| Unauthorized pet | Yes | Florida 83.56(2)(b) lists it as curable |
| Unauthorized guest or occupant | Yes | Florida 83.56(2)(b) |
| Parking in the wrong place | Yes | Florida 83.56(2)(b) |
| Dirty or unsanitary unit | Yes | Florida 83.56(2)(b) |
| Noise that keeps recurring | Often no, after a warning | Florida 83.56(2)(a): "continued unreasonable disturbance" |
| Intentional damage to property | Often no | Florida 83.56(2)(a) |
| Unpaid rent | Separate rules | Most states have a separate rent notice |

Unpaid rent follows its own notice law in most states. This article covers non-rent lease rules.

## What is a cure-or-quit notice?

A cure-or-quit notice tells the tenant to fix the violation by a date, or the tenancy ends. "Cure" means fix. The cure period and the form differ by state.

| State | Notice for a curable violation | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Florida | Tenant has 7 days from delivery to fix it, or the landlord may end the lease | Florida Statutes 83.56(2)(b) |
| Washington | Notice to perform the lease term or leave; tenant has 10 days after service | RCW 59.12.030(4) |
| Oregon | Termination date at least 30 days after delivery; notice must describe at least one way to cure and the cure date | ORS 90.392 |
| Texas | At least 3 days' written notice to vacate before filing, unless the lease sets a different period | Property Code 24.005 |

Texas shows why you must read your own state. Its statute sets a notice to vacate, not a cure period. The lease may add a cure step.

Florida law even gives the text. Florida Statutes 83.56(2)(b) sets a form that starts: "You are hereby notified that (cite the noncompliance). Demand is hereby made that you remedy the noncompliance within 7 days." Use your state's form word for word where one exists.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I write a lease violation notice?

Write a lease violation notice that a judge could read and understand with no other context. Oregon ORS 90.392, for example, asks the notice to "specify the acts and omissions constituting the violation" and to describe at least one possible remedy.

Notice checklist:

- [ ] Tenant names and rental address, as on the lease
- [ ] Date of the notice
- [ ] The lease clause, quoted or cited by section number
- [ ] What happened: dates, times, facts you saw or have proof of
- [ ] What the tenant must do to cure it
- [ ] The cure date, counted the way your state law counts
- [ ] What happens if the tenant does not cure it
- [ ] Your name, signature and contact details
- [ ] How you delivered it, with proof

Keep the tone neutral. Write "On October 3 and October 10, a dog was seen in unit 2B. Section 9 of the lease bars pets." Do not write "You keep ignoring the rules."

## How many warnings should a landlord give?

A good default is one informal reminder, then one formal notice. After that, follow your state's rule for repeats. Many states let you skip the cure step for a repeat.

- **Florida.** If the same curable violation "recurs within 12 months after notice, an eviction action may commence without delivering a subsequent notice" (83.56(2)(b)).
- **Oregon.** If the same violation repeats within 6 months of a prior notice, the new notice needs at least 10 days, and the tenant "does not have a right to cure this subsequent violation" (ORS 90.392).

A step list for each violation:

1. **Confirm the facts.** See it yourself, or get a written report with a date.
2. **Check the lease.** Make sure a clause covers the issue.
3. **Send a friendly reminder** for a minor first issue. Keep a copy.
4. **Send the formal notice** in your state's form if the issue goes on.
5. **Check on the cure date.** Inspect with proper entry notice, or ask for proof.
6. **Close or escalate.** If the tenant cured it, write that in the file. If not, talk to a local attorney before you file.

Treat every tenant the same way. The Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. 3604, bars different terms or treatment based on race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status or national origin. A strict policy for one tenant and a loose one for another is a risk.

## How do I document lease violations?

Document lease violations with facts, dates and proof, kept in one tenant file. Good records win disputes. Missing records lose them.

| Record | What to keep | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Photos and video | Date-stamped files | Shoot common areas, not inside the unit without notice |
| Written reports | Neighbor or staff reports, signed and dated | Ask for facts, not opinions |
| Notices | A copy of each notice sent | Save the exact version you delivered |
| Proof of delivery | Certified mail receipt, signed hand delivery note, or app log | Use the delivery method your state allows |
| Replies | Tenant texts, emails, letters | Export them; screenshots can lose the date |
| Outcome | Date cured, or next step | One line per event |

A shared log works well: date, issue, lease clause, action, proof file, next date.

## When should you call in help?

Call a local attorney before you file any eviction for a violation. Notice errors are a common reason a case gets thrown out, and you then start over.

## Which license must a Houston property manager hold, and how do I look it up?

A Houston property manager who manages your rental for a fee must hold a Texas Real Estate Commission (TREC) license. Texas Occupations Code 1101.002 defines a broker as a person who, for a fee, leases real estate for another person. It also names a person who "controls the acceptance or deposit of rent from a resident of a single-family residential real property unit." Section 1101.351 says no person may act as a broker or sales agent without a license.

A licensed sales agent may only work for a sponsoring broker. A business entity that acts as a broker also needs its own license (Section 1101.351(a-1)).

Section 1101.005 lists exceptions. Two matter for owners:

- **An on-site manager of an apartment complex** does not need a license.
- **You or your own employee** can lease your own property without a license.

How to check a manager in 4 steps:

1. **Get names and numbers.** Ask for the agent's license number and the sponsoring broker's name.
2. **Search TREC.** Use the License Holder Search on trec.texas.gov. Look up the person and the broker entity.
3. **Confirm status.** The license must be active, and the broker on the record must match the firm you hire.
4. **Read the history.** TREC publishes disciplinary actions. Ask about any you find.

Watch for: TREC's search page showed an outage notice in one archived copy we read. If the search is down, call TREC at 512-936-3000.

## Which local rules must a Houston manager know?

A good Houston manager knows the Texas Property Code rules that cost owners money when they go wrong. Use these 4 as test questions.

| Rule | What the law says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Floodplain notice | Give a separate written notice at or before lease signing: is the unit in a 100-year floodplain? If you know it flooded in the past 5 years, give a second notice. | Property Code 92.0135 |
| Deposit refund | Refund on or before the 30th day after the tenant leaves | Property Code 92.103 |
| Bad-faith deposit | $100 plus 3 times the amount wrongly kept, plus attorney's fees | Property Code 92.109 |
| Late fees | Only after rent is 2 full days late, and only if in the lease. Safe cap: 12% of rent in buildings of 4 or fewer units, 10% above 4 units | Property Code 92.019 |

The flood rule matters most in Houston. Under 92.0135(f), if you skip the notice and the tenant suffers a substantial flood loss, the tenant may end the lease. Ask the manager to show you the flood notice they use.

Worked example: a Houston two-bedroom at $1,497 a month, HUD's FY2027 Fair Market Rent for the metro. In a fourplex, a reasonable late fee under 92.019 is up to 12%, or $179.64. In a 20-unit building, the cap is 10%, or $149.70. A wrong late fee costs $100 plus 3 times the fee, plus attorney's fees (92.019(c)). See the [Houston Fair Market Rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/houston-fair-market-rent-2027) for other sizes.

Texas cities also may not cap rent outside a declared disaster (Local Government Code 214.902), so a Houston manager sets rent by the market.

## What should a Houston management agreement say?

A Houston management agreement should spell out who does what, who pays what and how you get out. Texas law does not give one required form for it, so read every line. A checklist:

- **Fees.** The monthly fee as a percent or a flat amount, leasing fee, renewal fee, and any markup on repairs.
- **Repair limit.** The dollar amount the manager may spend without your approval.
- **Trust account.** Where rent and deposits are held, and when you get paid each month.
- **Reports.** A monthly owner statement and a year-end statement for taxes.
- **Flood and storm plan.** Who calls the insurer, who secures the unit, and who orders repairs after a storm.
- **Notices.** Who sends the 92.0135 flood notice and keeps the signed copy.
- **Term and exit.** The notice period to end the deal, any cancellation fee, and how files and deposits move to you.
- **License data.** The broker's name and license number on the contract.

Also expect 2 TREC documents. The Information About Brokerage Services (IABS) notice describes how a broker can represent you. Section 1101.558 requires it at the first substantive talk about a specific property, with exceptions such as a residential lease under one year. TREC also requires license holders to post a Consumer Protection Notice.

## Which questions should I ask a Houston manager before I sign?

Ask questions that make the manager show proof, not promises. Twelve to start with:

1. What are your TREC license number and your broker's license number?
2. How many doors do you manage, and how many in my zip code?
3. What was your average days on market last year for homes like mine?
4. Show me a sample monthly owner statement.
5. What is your repair approval limit, and do you mark up vendor bills?
6. Which flood notice form do you use, and who keeps the signed copy?
7. How did you handle your properties after the last major storm?
8. How do you set and collect late fees under Property Code 92.019?
9. How do you return deposits within 30 days, with an itemized list?
10. How do you screen tenants, in writing, the same way for every applicant?
11. What happens to my deposits and files if I end the contract?
12. Can I speak with 2 owners who have used you for over 2 years?

The screening question matters for Fair Housing. A manager should apply one written standard to all applicants.

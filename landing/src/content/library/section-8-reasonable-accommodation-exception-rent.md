## What is a reasonable accommodation exception payment standard?

A reasonable accommodation exception payment standard is a higher subsidy ceiling for one family, because of a disability. The payment standard is the maximum monthly subsidy the PHA uses to compute the HAP (24 CFR 982.505). Most PHAs set it between 90% and 110% of the fair market rent (FMR), the "basic range" in 24 CFR 982.503(c).

24 CFR 982.503(d)(5) adds a path for one family:

> If required as a reasonable accommodation in accordance with 24 CFR part 8 for a person with a disability, the PHA may establish, without HUD approval or prior notification to HUD, an exception payment standard amount for an individual family that does not exceed 120 percent of the applicable FMR.

Here are the 3 tiers side by side:

| Payment standard level | Who decides | HUD approval? | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90% to 110% of FMR | PHA, for the whole area | No | 982.503(c) |
| Above 110% of FMR, for an area | PHA asks HUD with rental market data | Yes | 982.503(d)(4) |
| Up to 120% of FMR, one family, disability accommodation | PHA | No, and no notice | 982.503(d)(5) |
| Above 120% of FMR, one family, disability accommodation | PHA asks HUD | Yes | 982.503(d)(5) |

## What did HOTMA change?

HOTMA mainly clarified the rule and made PHAs explain it. HUD's final rule for the Housing Opportunity Through Modernization Act (HOTMA), published 2024-05-07, moved the text to paragraph (d)(5). HUD wrote that the paragraph "now specifies existing policy that PHAs may establish an exception payment standard of up to 120 percent of the applicable FMR without prior notification to HUD" for a reasonable accommodation.

The rule also changed what families learn. HUD now requires "an explanation of reasonable accommodation exception payment standards" in the PHA's voucher briefing packet. The current 24 CFR 982.301 lists information on "requesting exception payment standards as a reasonable accommodation" among the packet items. Every family must also hear about the PHA's reasonable accommodation process at the oral briefing.

What this means for you: more voucher holders with disabilities may know they can ask for a higher payment standard. You may see more applicants for accessible or costlier units.

## Who asks for the exception, landlord or family?

The family asks. The rule ties the exception to "a reasonable accommodation in accordance with 24 CFR part 8 for a person with a disability", "for an individual family". A reasonable accommodation is a request by or for the person with a disability.

You can still help, within limits:

- **Give facts about the unit.** Accessibility features, floor, elevator, distance to transit or medical care, utilities.
- **Give a firm rent.** State the rent and what it includes in writing.
- **Wait for the PHA.** Do not ask the applicant about the disability. The PHA handles the request and any verification.

Watch for: do not advertise a unit as "only for disability vouchers" or treat applicants differently by disability. Describe the unit's features and let each family decide.

## How high can the exception go?

The exception can go up to 120% of the applicable FMR without HUD approval, and higher with HUD approval. Here is a worked example. The numbers are an example, not a real FMR.

- 2-bedroom FMR: $1,500
- PHA payment standard (100% of FMR): $1,500
- Reasonable accommodation ceiling (120% of FMR): $1,800
- Unit gross rent (rent plus utility allowance): $1,750
- Family adjusted monthly income: $2,000; 30% is $600

| Line | Regular payment standard | RA exception payment standard |
|---|---|---|
| Payment standard | $1,500 | $1,750 (up to $1,800 allowed) |
| HAP: lower of (payment standard − $600) or (gross rent − $600) | $900 | $1,150 |
| Family share: gross rent − HAP | $850 | $600 |
| Family share as % of adjusted income | 42.5% | 30% |
| Passes the 40% rule at move-in? | No | Yes |

The 40% rule comes from 24 CFR 982.508: at move-in, when the gross rent is above the payment standard, the family share may not exceed 40% of adjusted monthly income. In this example, the exception turns a lease the PHA could not approve into one it can.

## Does it change my rent approval?

No, an exception payment standard does not approve your rent. It raises the subsidy ceiling only. The PHA "may not approve a lease until the PHA determines that the initial rent to owner is a reasonable rent" (24 CFR 982.507). It compares your unit with similar unassisted units by location, quality, size, type, age, amenities and the utilities you provide.

So the exception helps when your rent is reasonable but above the normal payment standard. It does not help if your rent is above the market for that unit.

A related tool: the PHA must also approve a higher utility allowance when a household member with a disability needs it as a reasonable accommodation (24 CFR 982.517(e)).

## How long does approval take?

The federal rule text we read sets no deadline for this decision. Each PHA sets its own reasonable accommodation process. So plan for time:

1. **Ask the PHA early** whether the family has asked for an exception payment standard, if the family tells you it has.
2. **Hold the unit in writing** for a set number of days, if you agree to.
3. **Have your RFTA packet ready**, with the rent and utilities clear.
4. **Prepare for inspection** so the exception is not the only open item.
5. **Ask for the PHA's written decision** with the payment standard amount, so you can confirm the HAP.

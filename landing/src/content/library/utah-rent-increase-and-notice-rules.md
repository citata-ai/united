## Utah rent and notice rules at a glance

Utah gives landlords wide room to set rent, but the notice to end a tenancy has firm rules. We read each section below on le.utah.gov on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Rent cap or rent control | None. Local rent and fee control is banned | 57-20-1 |
| Notice for a rent increase | No fixed period in the Utah Code | 57-22-4 (no notice rule) |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | Serve 15 calendar days or more before the period ends | 78B-6-802(1)(b)(i) |
| End a tenancy at will | Not less than 5 calendar days | 78B-6-802(1)(b)(ii) |
| Fixed-term lease ends | No notice needed | 78B-6-802(1)(a) |
| New fee on a month-to-month tenancy | 15-day notice of the charge | 57-22-4(5)(b) |
| Late fee cap | 10% of rent or $75, whichever is greater | 57-22-4(5)(a) |
| Local rules that conflict with the Fit Premises Act | Not allowed | 57-22-7 |

## Does Utah have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

No, Utah has no cap on rent increases. Utah Code 57-20-1 bars local rent control. A county, city or town "may not enact an ordinance or resolution that would control rents or fees on private residential property". The only exception is express approval of the Legislature.

The same section keeps local zoning, building and planning powers. So a city can still enforce its building code. It cannot set the rent.

The ban covers fees too. A city cannot cap an application fee or a pet fee by ordinance. State law still limits some fees, as the next sections show.

## How many days notice does a Utah landlord need to raise the rent?

The Utah Code sets no fixed number of days for a rent increase notice. We checked the Utah Fit Premises Act (Title 57, Chapter 22) and the eviction part of the code (Title 78B, Chapter 6, Part 8).

Lawmakers tried to add one. In the 2026 General Session, H.B. 478 "Residential Rental Modifications" would have amended Utah Code 57-22-4. Its first substitute says the bill "establishes a period of time by which a landlord shall provide a notice of rent increase to a tenant". On 2026-10-19, the current 57-22-4 still shows its 2021 text, with no rent increase notice. So the bill did not change the code.

That does not mean you can raise rent with no warning. Two rules shape the timing in practice:

1. **The lease.** If your lease sets a notice period for rent changes, follow it.
2. **The 15-day rule for month-to-month tenancies.** Many owners give the new rent with at least a 15 calendar day lead. That is the notice that ends the tenancy under 78B-6-802(1)(b)(i). The tenant can then choose to stay at the new rent or leave.

Watch for: a unit with a HUD voucher or other program rent has its own rules. Check the housing authority contract before you change the rent. For HUD rent limits, see our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits).

## Can a Utah landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

No, not unless the lease says so. A fixed-term lease sets the rent for the whole term. Raise the rent at renewal, when the term ends.

Utah Code 78B-6-802(1)(a) says a fixed term ends "without notice" at its end. So you do not owe a notice to end the term. But you should send the renewal offer, with the new rent, well before the end date. That gives the tenant time to decide.

Utah Code 57-22-4(5)(b) also limits fees during a lease. An owner may not charge a fee, fine or other cost:

- greater than the amount agreed in the rental agreement, or
- not in the rental agreement, unless the tenancy is month-to-month and the owner gives a 15-day notice of the charge.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Utah?

A Utah owner must serve the notice 15 calendar days or more before the end of the month or rental period (78B-6-802(1)(b)(i)). The tenancy then ends at the end of that period. For a tenancy at will, the notice is not less than 5 calendar days (78B-6-802(1)(b)(ii)).

Serve the notice in a way the code allows (78B-6-805):

1. **Hand it to the tenant.**
2. **Send it by registered or certified mail** to the residence or the leased property.
3. **Leave it with a person of suitable age and discretion** at the unit, if the tenant is absent.
4. **Post it in a clear place on the unit**, if no such person is there.

Here is a worked example for a month-to-month unit with rent due on the 1st.

| Line | Example |
|---|---|
| Current rent | $1,500 a month |
| New rent | $1,600 a month, from December 1 |
| Increase | $100, or $100 ÷ $1,500 = 6.7% |
| Period ends | November 30 |
| Latest date for 15 calendar days before November 30 | November 15 |
| Safer service date (adds a margin for mail) | November 1 |
| Late fee cap at the new rent | 10% × $1,600 = $160, which is greater than $75, so $160 |

The numbers are a sample. Use your own lease dates. If you mail the notice, count from the day of service, and leave a margin.

## Do cities in Utah add their own rent or notice rules?

No Utah city or county can control rent or fees (57-20-1). Utah Code 57-22-7 adds a second limit. A county or municipality may not adopt a rule that is inconsistent with the Utah Fit Premises Act.

Cities keep some tools. A city may enforce an administrative remedy for a breach of its own ordinance, within the limits of 57-22-7(2). And a city with a good landlord program under Utah Code 10-1-203.5 may run it, within the limits of 57-22-7(3).

So for rent and notice, one set of state rules applies in Salt Lake City, Provo, Ogden and every other Utah city. Check your city for business license and inspection rules, which are not rent rules.

## Checklist before you raise the rent in Utah

- [ ] Read the lease: fixed term or month-to-month, and any notice clause.
- [ ] For a fixed term, wait for renewal. Send the offer early.
- [ ] For month-to-month, serve written notice 15 calendar days or more before the period ends. Earlier is kinder and safer.
- [ ] Use a service method from 78B-6-805 and record the date.
- [ ] Recheck the late fee against the new rent: the cap is 10% or $75, whichever is greater.
- [ ] For any new fee on a month-to-month tenancy, give a 15-day notice of the charge.
- [ ] For a voucher unit, follow the housing authority's process.

## Must Columbus owners register with the Franklin County Auditor?

Yes. Every Columbus owner of residential rental property must file contact details with the Franklin County Auditor under Ohio Revised Code (ORC) 5323.02. The Auditor's rental registration page sets out the rule.

| Item | Franklin County rule |
|---|---|
| Who files | The owner. If several people own it, one files as contact. For a business, a trustee, executor, general partner, member, manager or officer files. |
| When | Within 60 days after notice of a property transfer, or after you get the tax bill |
| Updates | After any change in the information |
| How | The Auditor's online rental registration portal |
| Penalty | A fine of $150 per property per tax year |

The Auditor's page lists no filing fee. The only cost it states is the $150 fine. Questions go to 614-525-4663 or RentalRegistration@franklincountyohio.gov.

A worked example: an owner with 6 single-family rentals who never filed risks $150 x 6 = $900 for one tax year. The fix takes minutes in the portal.

## What is the new City of Columbus rental registry?

Columbus adds its own registry on top of the county filing. City Council passed Ordinance 0923-2026 on April 20, 2026. It creates Columbus Code Chapter 4515, a registry of residential rental properties. A July 2026 summary by the law firm Kegler Brown Hill + Ritter gives the details.

| Item | City rule (Chapter 4515) |
|---|---|
| Start | January 2027; pre-registration October to December 2026 |
| Who registers | The owner, or a local operator (lives within 100 miles of Columbus or holds an Ohio real estate license) |
| Fee | $15 per dwelling unit a year, capped at $1,500 per complex |
| Inspection | Building and Zoning Services inspects common areas and building systems once every three years. Units are inspected only on request. |
| Penalty | $2 per unit per day unregistered, capped at 10% of fees up to 120 days late and 20% after |
| Exempt | Vacant properties, stays under 30 days, dorms, rooming houses with shared kitchens and baths |

City Council says the fee funds rental assistance.

A worked example: a 40-unit building pays 40 x $15 = $600 a year. A 150-unit complex would owe $2,250, but the cap holds it at $1,500.

What to do: if you live more than 100 miles away and have no Ohio license, name a local operator. A property manager with an Ohio real estate license qualifies.

## What does the Columbus pay-to-stay rule mean?

The Columbus pay-to-stay rule gives tenants a defense in a non-payment eviction. Council's 2023 bulletin says Ordinance 2109-2023 amends Chapter 4551. Tenants can now "assert tender of rent as an affirmative defense" to an eviction for non-payment of rent. City Council lists it as passed on July 31, 2023.

Tender means an offer to pay what is owed. So if your tenant offers the rent before the court rules, that offer can defeat or delay your case. Council's Housing For All page says the goal is to give residents time to secure rental assistance up until a court judgment.

Two related rules came with it:

- **Third-party payments (Ordinance 2108-2023).** You may not refuse rent because a person other than the tenant paid it, if the tenant is not in breach.
- **Legal representation (Ordinance 2089-2023).** The city funds lawyers for tenants in eviction court.

What this means for you: file only when you must, and track every payment offer in writing. Ask your attorney how the defense works in Franklin County Municipal Court before you refuse any tender.

## What does the Columbus source-of-income ordinance require?

Columbus bars a landlord from refusing a tenant because of a lawful source of income. Ordinance 0494-2021 passed in March 2021 and took effect on July 1, 2021, per Columbus REALTORS. Covered income includes housing choice vouchers, child support and other lawful payments.

Columbus REALTORS reports that a landlord found guilty can face a first-degree misdemeanor, up to 180 days in jail and a $1,000 fine, citing the city's website. The city says landlords can still screen all applicants the same way and deny those who fail other criteria.

The same 2021 package added two more rules, per Columbus REALTORS and WCBE:

1. **Security deposit installments.** Operators with 5 or more units must let tenants pay the deposit in monthly installments over up to 6 months.
2. **Written receipts.** Landlords must give written receipts for security deposits and rent payments.

Vouchers matter more in 2027. HUD's FY2027 fair market rent for a 2-bedroom in the Columbus area rose 12.0%, to $1,602. That sets the base for voucher payment standards. See the [Columbus fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/columbus-oh-fair-market-rent-2027).

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Does Ohio allow rent control?

No. Ohio does not let Columbus cap rents. HB 430, passed by the General Assembly on June 1, 2022, added a specific preemption of local rent control to Chapter 5321 of the Revised Code. That is the reading of a Vorys law firm summary. Chapter 5321 is the Ohio Landlords and Tenants Law.

Columbus can still set notice and process rules. City Council lists Ordinance 3525-2024, passed December 16, 2024, which it says requires at least 180 days' notice of a monthly rent increase at lease renewal. Plan renewal increases about 6 months ahead.

Compliance checklist for a Columbus owner:

- [ ] File or update the Franklin County Auditor rental registration.
- [ ] Pre-register with the City of Columbus by December 2026.
- [ ] Budget $15 per unit a year for the city fee.
- [ ] Remove any "no vouchers" wording from listings.
- [ ] Offer deposit installments if you run 5 or more units.
- [ ] Give written receipts for every deposit and rent payment.
- [ ] Accept third-party rent when the tenant is not in breach.
- [ ] Send rent increase notices 180 days before renewal.

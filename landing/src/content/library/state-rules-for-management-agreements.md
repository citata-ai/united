## Must the management agreement be in writing?

Writing requirements depend on the state's rules, but Washington and Oregon expressly require written management agreements. Check the official rule before accepting authority to manage. Do not assume a proposal or verbal approval supplies the required agreement.

Washington's WAC 308-124D-215 requires an agreement signed by the owner and designated broker. Oregon's Real Estate Agency says the agreement must be signed and unexpired before management begins. These are state examples, not a complete national survey.

| State example | Written terms to verify | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Washington | Signed management agreement, compensation, fund authority and reporting frequency | WAC 308-124D-215 |
| Oregon | Signed, unexpired agreement; duties, fees, term and termination process | Oregon Real Estate Agency agreement guidance |

Use the table as a starting point for reading the full sources. Each source includes more detail than fits in a summary row. Ask counsel to identify the exact rule for your license and services.

Check who signs for an entity owner. Confirm authority from the relevant records rather than relying only on the person's email signature. Keep the signed version and related approvals accessible to the service team.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Must fees be stated in the agreement?

Fee requirements need a state-specific review, and both examples here require compensation terms. Washington states that the agreement must include the firm's compensation. Oregon's agency guidance includes fees and other compensation, with earning and payment terms.

Build a fee inventory from actual billing records. Include the base charge and work such as leasing, renewal, vacancy oversight and project coordination. Ask counsel which terms and disclosures the applicable rules require.

State the billing base in clear language. Rent billed, rent collected and other receipts can produce different charges. Do not leave the software operator to infer the intended base from a sales brochure.

Separate your fee from money paid to others. An owner should see the manager's compensation and outside costs without guessing. Identify when approval is needed for work beyond the agreed scope.

Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to compare proposed structures. Then check that the contract and sample statement use the same method. A calculator result does not authorize a charge.

Review rejected and waived charges too. Repeated manual exceptions may show that the agreement does not match your service. Fix the terms through the proper process instead of relying on staff memory.

## What must I disclose about markups and affiliates?

Disclosure requirements depend on the state and service relationship. Give counsel the actual ownership links, provider roles and compensation. A generic sentence saying other fees may apply may not answer the relevant rule.

Washington's WAC 308-124D-215 addresses written relationship disclosure, prior fee disclosure and owner permission for other services. Oregon's agency guidance addresses employees and businesses in which the manager has a financial interest. Read each requirement in its own context.

Map your service providers before reviewing the clause. Include a repair company owned by a principal, an employee performing paid field work and any linked billing entity. Use the legal names that appear on invoices.

Prepare a sample owner statement showing the outside bill and manager compensation. Ask whether the owner can tell who did the work and which part the management company earned. Clear reporting should support the contract.

Do not treat disclosure as proof that every arrangement is lawful. Ask counsel to review the arrangement itself as well as its wording. License, agency and other rules may need separate consideration.

Train staff to use the approved disclosure process before work starts. A document added after a complaint does not provide the same process control as a reviewed instruction used in advance.

## How do I find my state's rules?

Find your state's rules through the real estate regulator and the official code publisher. Search for property management agreements, compensation, trust accounts and supervision. Confirm that the rule applies to the license and property work involved.

Save the full source link with a plain note describing what it controls. Record the review date and the person responsible for checking changes. Avoid relying on a template seller's state summary as the final authority.

Read definitions and cross-references before drawing a conclusion. A rule may distinguish a property manager, broker and principal broker. Do not use a term from another state as if it carries the same legal meaning.

Ask the regulator or local counsel when the official source leaves a practical question open. Describe the actual task and relationship. A broad question about whether management is allowed may miss the issue.

Review amendments with the same care as the first agreement. Washington requires signed written modifications. Oregon's agency guidance calls for written, signed and dated amendments when duties or fees change.

Keep older signed terms with the periods they governed. Do not overwrite the agreement archive when introducing a new template. Staff need to know which fee schedule applies to an existing owner.

## How should I check the agreement against daily work?

Check the agreement by following real work from owner instruction to invoice. Select a routine management charge, an outside repair and an additional service. Ask where the agreement grants authority and states compensation.

Compare the contract with sales pages and onboarding materials. Remove promises that operations cannot provide or that conflict with the approved terms. Do not let a salesperson improvise new services without review.

Assign responsibility for contract version control. Staff should be able to find the signed version for each property and identify any later amendment. Restrict edits to the approved template.

Create a short exception register with the property, missing authority, reviewer and next action. Pause the disputed charge or task while the responsible professional reviews it. Keep the owner informed in plain language.

## Which fees go into owner lifetime value?

Owner lifetime value counts every fee an owner pays you, minus the direct cost to serve them. Do not count money that passes through you to vendors, such as a plumber's bill. Count it only if you add a markup, and then count only the markup.

List your recurring fees per door per year:

| Fee type | How to estimate per door per year | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly management fee | Fee % × average monthly rent × 12 × occupancy | 8% × $1,700 × 12 × 0.95 = $1,550 |
| Leasing fee | Fee × leases signed per door per year | $850 × 0.4 = $340 |
| Renewal fee | Fee × renewals per door per year | $200 × 0.6 = $120 |
| Other recurring fees | Inspection, tech or admin fees billed to owners | $60 |
| Gross fee revenue | Sum | $2,070 |
| Direct cost to serve | Staff time, software per door, inspections | −$1,100 |
| Gross margin per door | Revenue − direct cost | $970 |

These are example numbers, not benchmarks. Pull your real fees and costs from your property management software and your profit and loss statement. If you do not know your cost to serve, divide your direct staff and software costs by your door count.

## How does average owner tenure change lifetime value?

Tenure multiplies everything. An owner who stays 8 years is worth twice an owner who stays 4. Small changes in how long owners stay move lifetime value more than a small change in fees.

Find tenure from your own records:

1. Export every owner with a start date and an end date (blank if active).
2. Count owners who left in the last 12 months.
3. Divide by the number of owners at the start of those 12 months. That is your yearly churn rate.
4. Average tenure ≈ 1 ÷ yearly churn rate.

| Yearly owner churn | Approximate average tenure |
|---|---|
| 8% | 12.5 years |
| 10% | 10 years |
| 12% | 8.3 years |
| 15% | 6.7 years |
| 20% | 5 years |

Use your own churn number. Published churn figures for property management vary between sources. Do not borrow one you cannot check.

Split churn by reason when you can: sold the home, moved back in, went to a competitor, you fired them. Only "went to a competitor" is fully in your control. If most churn is sales, a buyer's agent referral program can recover some value.

## How do extra doors from the same owner count?

Extra doors raise lifetime value in two ways: more doors per owner from day one, and doors the owner adds later. Count both.

### Full formula

Owner lifetime value = gross margin per door × average doors per owner × average tenure in years

Add a growth term if your owners add doors over time:

Owner lifetime value = gross margin per door × (starting doors + yearly added doors × tenure ÷ 2) × tenure

### Worked example (example numbers)

- Gross margin per door: $970 a year
- Starting doors per owner: 1.4
- Doors added per owner per year: 0.05
- Tenure: 8.3 years (12% churn)

Doors on average over the tenure: 1.4 + 0.05 × 8.3 ÷ 2 = 1.61

Lifetime value: $970 × 1.61 × 8.3 = $12,962

Round it and use $13,000 in planning. Show the inputs next to the number every time you share it, so no one treats an estimate as a fact.

## How do I use owner lifetime value to set ad spend?

Use lifetime value to set a ceiling on what you pay to sign one owner. Then compare each channel's real cost per signed owner with that ceiling.

1. **Pick a ratio.** There is no industry standard. A common choice is to spend up to one third of lifetime value. With $13,000, that is about $4,300 per signed owner.
2. **Check cash flow.** Gross margin in year 1 is $970 × 1.4 = $1,358. At $4,300 per owner, you earn back the cost in about 3.2 years. If you cannot wait that long, lower the ceiling.
3. **Measure cost per signed owner by channel.** Total spend on the channel ÷ owners signed from it, over at least 3 months.
4. **Move budget to channels below the ceiling.**

| Channel | Spend (3 months) | Signed owners | Cost per signed owner | Under $4,300? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google Ads | $4,500 | 2 | $2,250 | Yes |
| SEO retainer | $6,000 | 3 | $2,000 | Yes |
| Paid directory | $1,500 | 0 | none signed | Watch |

These are example numbers. Read vendor data with care too. Goodjuju Marketing's Traverse case study reports a 38% closing ratio on owner leads; that is one client's claim, not a norm.

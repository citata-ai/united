A rental can show a tax loss even when cash comes in each month. Depreciation, interest and repairs can push Schedule E below zero. The next question is the one in the title: can that loss lower the tax on your salary?

The answer depends on three things. How you take part in the rental, your income, and your filing status.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## What Is a Passive Activity?

A passive activity is a business activity in which you do not materially participate, or a rental activity. IRS Publication 925 treats rental activities as passive in general, with exceptions. A passive loss can usually offset only passive income, such as income from another rental.

So by default, a $10,000 rental loss does not reduce tax on $90,000 of wages. The special allowance below is the main exception for small landlords. A second exception covers real estate professionals.

| Path | Who it fits | Main test (Publication 925) |
|---|---|---|
| Default passive rule | Most investors | Passive loss offsets only passive income |
| $25,000 special allowance | Owners who actively participate | Management decisions, at least 10% interest, income limit |
| Real estate professional | People who work in real estate | More than 750 hours and more than half of work time, plus material participation |

## What Is the $25,000 Special Allowance?

The special allowance lets you deduct up to $25,000 of rental loss from nonpassive income, such as wages. Publication 925 says you or your spouse must actively participate in the rental real estate activity.

Active participation is a lower bar than material participation. Publication 925 lists decisions that count:

- **Approving new tenants.**
- **Deciding on rental terms.**
- **Approving expenses.**
- **Similar management decisions** made in a significant and bona fide sense.

You also need at least 10% of the interests in the activity, by value, for the whole year. Publication 925 says limited partners generally do not actively participate.

Filing status changes the limit. If you are married, file separately and lived apart all year, the allowance is at most $12,500. If you lived with your spouse at any time in the year and file separately, you can't use the allowance at all.

## How Does Income Phase Out the Allowance?

The allowance shrinks as your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) rises. Publication 925 says the $25,000 is reduced by 50% of MAGI above $100,000. At $150,000 or more, the allowance is $0. For married filing separately and living apart, the limits are $50,000 and $75,000.

Here is the math at different MAGI levels:

| MAGI | Excess over $100,000 | Reduction (50%) | Allowance left |
|---|---|---|---|
| $90,000 | $0 | $0 | $25,000 |
| $110,000 | $10,000 | $5,000 | $20,000 |
| $120,000 | $20,000 | $10,000 | $15,000 |
| $140,000 | $40,000 | $20,000 | $5,000 |
| $150,000 | $50,000 | $25,000 | $0 |

MAGI here is not the same as AGI. Publication 925 says you figure it without some items, such as taxable Social Security, deductible IRA contributions, and the passive loss itself. Your tax software or preparer does this step.

## Can You Show One Owner's Numbers?

Yes. This example uses invented numbers for one single filer.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Wages | $120,000 |
| MAGI for this rule | $120,000 |
| Rental loss on Schedule E | $18,000 |
| Allowance at this MAGI (from the table) | $15,000 |
| Loss deducted against wages this year | $15,000 |
| Loss suspended and carried forward | $3,000 |

The owner approves tenants and repairs, so the owner actively participates. The owner gets $15,000 of the loss this year. The other $3,000 waits.

Publication 925 also has a simple example. A single filer with $70,000 in wages, $15,000 of passive income and a $26,000 rental loss can use $15,000 against the passive income. The other $11,000 offsets wages.

## What Happens to Losses I Cannot Use?

Losses you can't use this year carry forward. Publication 925 says disallowed deductions are treated as deductions from the activity for the next tax year. Form 8582 is the form that figures the allowed loss and tracks what carries forward.

Suspended losses are not lost. They can come back in two ways:

1. **Future passive income.** A later year with rental profit, or a second rental with profit, can absorb them.
2. **A full sale.** Publication 925 says passive losses that were not allowed are generally allowed in full in the year you dispose of your entire interest. The sale must be fully taxable, and the buyer must not be related to you.

Keep a simple record each year: the loss, the amount allowed, and the amount carried forward. Your prior-year Form 8582 holds those numbers.

## When Do You Not Need Form 8582?

Some owners can skip the form. Publication 527 says you don't complete Form 8582 if you meet all of these conditions:

- **Only active rentals.** Your only passive activities were rental real estate in which you actively participated.
- **Small loss.** Your overall net loss is $25,000 or less.
- **Low income.** Your MAGI is $100,000 or less.
- **Clean history.** You have no prior-year unallowed losses or passive credits.
- **No limited interests.** You hold no rental interest as a limited partner or as a trust or estate beneficiary.

Married filing separately has lower limits and a living-apart test. If you are not sure, the form is the safe default.

## What Should a Small Landlord Do Now?

Use this checklist before you file:

- **Write down your decisions.** Keep emails where you approved a tenant, a lease term or a repair.
- **Check your ownership share.** Confirm you hold at least 10%.
- **Estimate your MAGI.** Use the phaseout table above.
- **Pull last year's Form 8582.** Carry forward any suspended loss.
- **Plan the sale year.** A full sale can release all suspended losses.

## What is one owner lead worth to a property manager?

One owner lead is worth the margin of the doors it brings, over the years the owner stays, times the chance you sign that owner. Start with a signed owner, not a lead. A signed owner pays you a management fee each month. Some also pay leasing and renewal fees.

Use this formula for the value of one signed owner:

- **Monthly fee per owner** = average rent × average doors per owner × management fee %.
- **Lifetime revenue** = monthly fee per owner × 12 × average years an owner stays.
- **Lifetime margin** = lifetime revenue × your margin %.

Use margin, not revenue. Each new door adds work: inspections, calls, rent collection, bookkeeping. If you skip margin, the math looks twice as good as it is.

Take each input from your own books. Your property management software has rent rolls and owner start dates. If you do not know your fee as a percent, our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) helps you turn flat fees into a percent of rent.

## Example inputs for the calculation

The table below holds example inputs. They are not market averages. Replace each one with your own number.

| Input | Example value | Where to find your number |
|---|---|---|
| Average monthly rent | $1,800 | Rent roll |
| Doors per owner | 1.5 | Doors ÷ owners |
| Management fee | 8% of rent | Management agreement |
| Years an owner stays | 3 | Owner start and end dates |
| Margin | 40% | Profit and loss report |
| Lead-to-signed rate | 25% | CRM: owner leads vs. signed agreements |

With these inputs, one signed owner gives:

1. Monthly fee: $1,800 × 1.5 × 8% = $216.
2. Yearly fee: $216 × 12 = $2,592.
3. Lifetime revenue: $2,592 × 3 = $7,776.
4. Lifetime margin: $7,776 × 40% = $3,110.

So one signed owner is worth about $3,110 in margin. One owner lead is worth 25% of that, about $778.

## What conversion rate should you use for AI search leads?

Use your own lead-to-signed rate for AI search leads until you have 20 or more of them. Public numbers are thin and come from other industries.

The most quoted number comes from Ahrefs. On its own site, AI search sent 0.5% of visitors in the last 30 days but 12.1% of signups. Ahrefs says that is about 23 times the conversion rate of organic search visitors (Ahrefs, 2025-06-16). The author calls it single-site data and doubts the rate will hold as AI search grows.

A wider Ahrefs study of 3,000 sites found AI chatbots sent 0.17% of the average site's visitors (Ahrefs, 2025-02-06). So AI traffic is small. The case for AI search is not volume. The case is that an owner who arrives after an AI answer has often read a short list and already knows your name.

Watch for this trap: many AI visits show up as "Direct" in analytics. Ahrefs makes the same point. Google also puts AI Overviews and AI Mode clicks inside normal search traffic in Search Console (Google Search Central). So the best signal is the owner's own answer to "How did you hear about us?"

## How many AI leads must you win to break even?

Divide the yearly cost of the AI search work by the margin per signed owner. Then divide by your close rate to get leads. With the example margin of $3,110 and a 25% close rate:

| Yearly cost of AI search work | Owners to sign | Owner leads needed |
|---|---|---|
| $6,000 ($500/mo) | 2 | 8 |
| $12,000 ($1,000/mo) | 4 | 16 |
| $18,000 ($1,500/mo) | 6 | 24 |
| $30,000 ($2,500/mo) | 10 | 40 |

Round the owners up. You cannot sign 1.9 owners.

Two notes keep the table honest:

- **Year 1 is slower.** An owner you sign in month 10 pays only 2 months of fees in year 1. Lifetime margin still counts, but cash in year 1 is lower.
- **Cost includes your time.** If you or a staff member spend 4 hours a week on reviews, listings and pages, add that cost.

## What does a 100-door example look like?

A company with 100 doors buys a $1,500/mo AI search program. The yearly cost is $18,000. With the example inputs, the company must sign 6 new owners from AI search to break even. That is 24 owner leads at a 25% close rate, or 2 per month.

Six owners at 1.5 doors each add 9 doors. That is 9% growth on 100 doors. For a small firm, that is a real target, not a sure thing.

Now change one input. If owners stay 5 years instead of 3, lifetime margin per owner goes from $3,110 to $5,184. Break-even drops to 4 owners (18,000 ÷ 5,184 = 3.5, rounded up). Retention moves the result more than most marketing inputs. Fix owner churn before you buy any channel.

## Which leads count as AI search leads?

Count an owner as an AI search lead when the owner says ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot or Google AI told them about you. Also count visits that analytics tags as AI referrals.

Be careful with credit. In our own run of 50 city prompts on ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, Expertise.com was a source in 93 of 150 answers. Yelp and iPropertyManagement were sources in 50 each (our data, 2026-10; full table in our [AI Property Manager Index](/research/ai-property-manager-index-2026)). So an owner may find you through an AI answer that quotes a directory. Your directory profile did part of the work. That still counts as an AI search lead.

A simple monthly log is enough:

- Owner leads this month, by source (owner's own answer).
- AI search leads signed this month.
- Doors added from those owners.
- Running total of margin vs. cost.

Our guide on [how to track AI traffic](/guides/track-ai-traffic-property-management) shows the analytics setup.

## When is AI search not worth it?

AI search work is not worth it when you cannot take on more doors, or when your close rate is very low. Fix the close rate first. If 5% of owner leads sign, a $1,000/mo program needs 80 leads a year to break even with our example margin.

It is also weak when your fees are not public. AI answers repeat facts they can find. A clear fee page helps both the AI answer and the owner who reads it.

We do not promise that AI search pays back. AI answers change from week to week. Do the math with your own numbers, set a 6- or 12-month review date, and decide on data.

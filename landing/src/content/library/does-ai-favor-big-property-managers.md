## How did we test whether AI favors big brands?

We asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude one owner question for each of the 50 most populous US cities. The prompt was: "What is the best property management company in [City, ST]? Give names." That gave 150 answers on 2026-10-19, with 0 failures (our data, H03). We used each vendor's API with web search, not the consumer apps.

For each answer, we logged every company named, its order and every source linked. We marked a company "national" when it is on our fixed list of 39 multi-state brands, such as Real Property Management, Ziprent or HomeRiver Group. All other names count as local.

Limits: one prompt, one day, one run per engine. AI answers change between runs. So read these numbers as a snapshot, not a law. The full city table is in the [AI Property Manager Index 2026](/research/ai-property-manager-index-2026).

## Which brands show up most?

National brands show up in most answers, but each one appears in only some cities. Here are the 7 national brands AI named most (our data, 2026-10-19).

| Brand | Answers (of 150) | Cities (of 50) |
|---|---|---|
| Real Property Management | 49 | 29 |
| Ziprent | 40 | 24 |
| HomeRiver Group | 33 | 21 |
| Evernest | 27 | 15 |
| PMI (Property Management Inc.) | 25 | 18 |
| Keyrenter | 23 | 14 |
| Mynd | 16 | 11 |

Some national names came with a city attached, such as "Real Property Management Albuquerque" or "HomeRiver Group Chicago". In those answers, the AI named a local office of a national brand.

Now the other side. The 150 answers named 821 different companies. Of those, 794 were local. Of all 1,326 names, 1,011 (76%) were local firms and 315 (24%) were national.

## Does each AI engine treat big brands the same way?

No. Each engine mixes national and local names in its own way (our data, 2026-10-19).

| Engine | Names per answer | Answers with a national brand | Share of names that are local |
|---|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT | 5.3 | 66% | 82% |
| Claude | 15.5 | 98% | 78% |
| Gemini | 5.7 | 92% | 67% |

Claude gives long lists, so it nearly always includes a national brand. ChatGPT gives short lists and leans most local. Gemini gives short lists with the highest share of national names.

Who goes first matters, because owners read the top of a list. In 112 of 150 answers, the first company named was local (our count, 2026-10-19). That is about 3 of 4 answers.

## Why do big brands win?

Big brands win on reach: they are present in many cities at once. Three patterns stand out in our data.

1. **Their own sites get cited.** mynd.co was a source in 24 answers, ziprent.com in 22, utopiamanagement.com in 18 and belonghome.com in 13. Few local sites reach that count, because each one serves one city.
2. **They sit on the directories.** Expertise.com was a source in 93 of 150 answers, iPropertyManagement in 50 and Yelp in 50. A brand with many offices can appear on many city lists.
3. **They have many reviews in many places.** Review volume feeds directory picks. Expertise.com says it analyzes the number of review sites that cover a provider, the average score and how much ratings change.

A separate study points the same way for chains. SOCi looked at more than 350,000 locations of 2,751 multi-location brands. ChatGPT recommended 1.2% of those locations, while 35.9% appeared in Google's local 3-pack (Localogy, 2026-01-28). Locations that ChatGPT recommended averaged 4.3 stars. So even big brands are not named for every location.

## Where do small firms win?

Small firms win in their own city. In our test, a local firm was the most named company in 30 of 50 cities (our data, 2026-10-19).

Agreement between engines is a strong sign. In 38 cities, at least one company was named by all 3 engines. In 28 of those cities, a local firm was in that group. Across all cities, 43 local firms and 18 national brands were named by all 3 engines.

Two examples from the data:

- **Chicago.** Landmark Property Management and GC Realty & Development were named by ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude.
- **Albuquerque.** Brickleys Property Solutions and Rent Builder Property Management were named by all 3 engines, next to Real Property Management.

Small firms also win on narrow questions. A national brand page rarely covers one suburb, one building type or one local law in depth. A local page can.

## What can a 50-door firm do?

A 50-door firm can copy what the local winners have: directory presence, reviews and clear city pages. Use this checklist.

- **Find your city's sources.** Run the owner prompt in ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude. Write down every domain each answer cites.
- **Get on the top directories.** Check Expertise.com, Yelp, iPropertyManagement and BBB for your city. Fix or add your profile.
- **Match your facts.** Use the same name, address, phone, cities and services on every site.
- **Ask for honest reviews.** Ask real owners to review you on the sites AI cites. Never pay for reviews.
- **Write one page per city you serve.** Put your services, your fees and your local facts on it.
- **Answer narrow questions.** Publish pages on your local rules and property types that a national page skips.
- **Check again in 6 weeks.** Run the same prompts and compare.

Google says no special markup is needed for AI Overviews. A page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet. So the base work, a crawlable site with clear pages, comes first.

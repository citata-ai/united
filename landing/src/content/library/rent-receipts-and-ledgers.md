## Which rent payments need a receipt?

Receipt requirements depend on where the property sits and how the tenant pays. Build the local rule into your collection process before accepting money.

New York Real Property Law section 235-e requires written receipts for residential rent paid in cash or instruments other than the tenant's personal check. A written request triggers receipts for personal checks too. The law also sets timing and content requirements. [Read New York's receipt statute](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/235-E).

Washington RCW 59.18.063 requires receipts for cash and, on tenant request, for other forms of payment. That on-request rule covers noncash forms such as money orders and cashier's checks. Check the current text for the exact situation. [Read Washington's payment rule](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=59.18.063).

Do not assume an online system removes the legal duty. Check the content and delivery of the confirmation. The tenant needs a record that identifies the payment, not simply a screen saying a transfer started.

Set a process for cash received outside the office. Staff should never depend on memory to issue a receipt after returning from a property visit.

## What goes on a rent receipt?

A useful receipt identifies the payer, property, payment and period. Match the statutory fields first, then add clear accounting detail.

New York section 235-e lists the date, amount, premises, period paid and the receiving person's signature and title. Use those requirements when they apply. Other states can call for different content.

Here is a generic recommended field list, not a completed legal form:

- **Property and unit.** Identify the rental covered by the payment.
- **Tenant and payer.** Distinguish the tenant from someone who pays on the tenant's behalf.
- **Receipt date.** Record when the payment was received.
- **Amount and method.** Note the method without publishing sensitive bank data.
- **Covered period.** State the rent period or account item involved.
- **Reference and recipient.** Link the receipt to the ledger and authorized receiver.
- **Payment status.** Distinguish received funds from a transfer still awaiting settlement.

Avoid a blanket paid-in-full stamp on partial payments. Do not use a receipt to hide disputed charges. If a payment reverses, keep the original receipt and issue a clear account update.

Give the tenant a readable copy through the permitted method. Keep your own copy linked to the source transaction.

## What is a tenant ledger?

A tenant ledger records the charges, payments and credits for a tenancy in date order. The running balance shows what remains after each entry.

| Entry | What to record | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Rent charge | Period and contractual amount | Lease and renewal |
| Payment | Receipt date, amount and method | Receipt and bank settlement |
| Credit | Reason and applicable period | Approved adjustment |
| Reversal | Original reference and reason | Bank or processor notice |
| Refund | Recipient, amount and release date | Payment confirmation |
| Correction | Prior entry and new treatment | Review note |

A ledger is not the same as a bank statement. The statement shows money movement across accounts. The ledger connects that movement to the tenant and obligation.

Keep the security deposit in a distinct record. A deposit receipt should not make the rent balance look paid unless a lawful application actually occurred. Avoid merging different kinds of money into an unexplained total.

If several people pay toward the same lease, preserve payer details while applying your approved allocation method. Do not create extra rent charges just because several payments arrived.

## How do I reconcile the ledger?

Match ledger entries to payment evidence and explain every difference. Do that before using the balance in a notice, court claim or collection referral.

Start with the lease terms. Confirm rent changes and concessions have the correct effective dates. Then compare charges with receipts, bank settlement and processor fees.

Watch for unsettled transfers. An authorization is not the same as cleared funds. Also check whether the processor deducted a fee before sending the deposit. Net bank receipts alone may not equal the tenant's payment amount.

Review returned payments and duplicated entries. A manual receipt can overlap with an imported transaction. Preserve the correction trail instead of deleting whichever entry looks wrong.

Send a tenant account statement when the correction affects their balance. State what changed and attach the relevant proof. A clear explanation can resolve a dispute before the parties spend time on formal demands.

## How long should I keep payment records?

Keep payment records under the applicable tenancy, tax and dispute rules. There is no safe national deletion date for every rental account.

New York section 235-e requires records of cash rent receipts for at least 3 years. The IRS generally uses a 3-year period for ordinary tax records, with longer exceptions. These rules serve different purposes and do not replace local claim periods or preservation duties.

Ask counsel and your tax professional to approve the retention schedule. Keep records longer when a dispute, audit or legal hold requires preservation. Document the reason for the hold in the account file.

Check your software export before changing vendors. Make sure the export includes credits, reversals, receipts and attachment references. A balance-only spreadsheet cannot explain how the balance arose.

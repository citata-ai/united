## Why do case studies matter for AI answers?

A case study is first-hand proof, and AI answers favor exact, sourced facts. Owners ask AI questions like "Which property manager in Tampa fills vacancies fastest?" A generic "We get results" page gives the AI nothing to quote. A sentence like "Our 120 Tampa homes sat vacant 21 days on average in 2025" does.

Research supports this. In the 2023 paper "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization" (Aggarwal et al.), three methods worked best: adding statistics, adding quotes and citing sources. On the authors' test set, they raised a page's visibility in AI answers by 30 to 40%. Keyword stuffing offered "little to no improvement."

Google's guide to helpful content asks a similar question: "Does your content clearly demonstrate first-hand expertise?" A case study is the clearest way to show it.

Watch for: the paper measured a research test set, not property management sites. Treat the 30 to 40% as a direction, not a forecast.

## Which numbers matter to owners?

Owners care about money and time. Pick 3 to 5 numbers from this list and show each one as before, after and period.

| Number | Why an owner cares | How to measure it |
|---|---|---|
| Days vacant per turnover | Each empty day is lost rent | Move-out date to new lease start, average |
| Rent collected by the 5th | Cash flow | Share of rent due that arrived on time |
| Days to lease | Speed of the leasing team | Listing date to signed lease |
| Renewal rate | Fewer turnovers, lower costs | Leases renewed ÷ leases that ended |
| Maintenance cost per door | Net income | Total repair spend ÷ doors, per year |
| Owner retention | Trust in the manager | Owners who stayed ÷ owners at start |

Always add the door count and the dates. "Vacancy fell from 34 to 19 days" means little without "across 46 single-family homes, 2024 vs 2025."

### A worked example

Here is the format with made-up numbers. Use your own, from your software reports.

| Metric (46 homes, Mesa, AZ) | 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Average days vacant per turnover | 34 | 19 | −15 days |
| Rent collected by the 5th | 91% | 97% | +6 points |
| Renewal rate | 58% | 71% | +13 points |

Now the money. Say the average rent is $1,800 per month, about $59 per day ($1,800 × 12 ÷ 365). The owner had 9 turnovers in 2025. 15 fewer vacant days × 9 turnovers × $59 = $7,965 more rent in a year. Show this sum in the case study. Owners and AI both repeat a dollar figure more easily than a percent.

## How do you get owner consent?

Get consent in writing before you publish any name, address, photo or quote. Our own rule at citata.ai: no client page goes live without written consent. Steps:

1. **Ask after a good result.** Send the draft with the numbers, not a blank request.
2. **Show exactly what goes public.** Name, city, door count, quote, photos.
3. **Offer an anonymous version.** "A Mesa owner with 12 homes" still works for AI if the numbers are exact.
4. **Keep private data private.** Do not publish rent rolls, tenant names or exact street addresses.
5. **Save the consent** with the date, in your CRM or email.

Disclose any material connection. The FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR 255.5) say a connection that "might materially affect the weight or credibility of the endorsement" must be disclosed clearly. The Guides name payment and free or discounted products as examples. If the owner got a month free for the quote, say so on the page.

Say what is typical. 16 CFR 255.2(b) covers this. If you cannot back up that a client's result is typical, clearly disclose the generally expected result. A line like "Across all 640 homes we manage, average vacancy was 26 days in 2025" does that and adds a second fact for AI.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What format do AI tools quote?

AI tools quote short pieces of text. Claude's API documentation says each web citation holds "up to 150 characters of the cited content." So write your key result as one sentence of about 150 characters or less.

A page layout that works:

- **Title with the result:** "How a Mesa owner cut vacancy from 34 to 19 days"
- **Summary sentence first:** who, where, how many doors, what changed, which dates
- **Before-and-after table:** 3 to 5 rows, with units
- **What we did:** 3 to 5 numbered steps
- **Owner quote:** one or two sentences, with the owner's name and role, if consented
- **Typical result line:** your average across all doors
- **Date and author:** when the page was written and who wrote it

Google's guide asks, "Do pages carry a byline, where one might be expected?" Put a person's name on the case study, not only the company name.

Look at the market leaders for ideas, not text. Goodjuju Marketing's case study for Best Property Management of Chattanooga leads with one result: 3 to 5 new owners closed a month. It names the owner and quotes her. It has no "before" number for leads, because the owner did not have one. Start tracking now so your case study has a before.

## Where should case studies link?

Link each case study to and from the pages it proves. AI finds pages through links, and Google lists internal links as a best practice for its AI features.

| Link from | Link to | Anchor text example |
|---|---|---|
| Service page (leasing) | Case study | "See how we cut vacancy to 19 days in Mesa" |
| City page (Mesa) | Case study | "A Mesa owner's results" |
| Case study | Service page | "Our leasing service" |
| Case study | Fee page | "What we charge" |
| Home page or results page | All case studies | "Owner results" |

Watch for: a case study page with no links to it. If only a sitemap points to the page, few people and few crawlers will find it.

## What is the vacation home day test?

IRS Topic 415 uses personal-use and fair-rental days to decide whether you used the dwelling as a residence. Personal use must exceed the greater of 14 days or 10% of days rented at a fair rental price.

The word greater matters. So does exceeds. Do not apply the lower threshold or change more than into at least. A calendar error can change how deductions are limited.

This is separate from the minimal rental rule. That rule asks whether a home used as a residence was rented fewer than 15 days. Do not merge the tests into a single rule called the 14-day rule.

| Question | IRS source and test | What to check next |
|---|---|---|
| Is the dwelling used as a residence? | Topic 415: personal use exceeds the greater of 14 days or 10% of fair-rental days | Mixed-use deduction limits |
| Is rental use minimal? | Topic 415: residence rented fewer than 15 days | No rental income reporting or rental deductions under that rule |
| Is the home not a residence under the test? | Publication 527: apply the relevant rental and personal-use treatment | Allocation and other loss limits |

The table is a decision outline. The full IRS guidance also defines personal use and exceptions. Review those definitions before classifying your days.

## Which days count as personal use?

Personal use includes more than your own vacation nights. IRS Topic 415 includes use by owners, certain family use, home exchanges, and below-market use. The facts and exceptions matter.

Topic 415 describes an exception for a family member who uses the property as a main home and pays fair rent. Do not assume every payment by a relative turns the day into a fair-rental day. Record the relationship, use, and payment terms.

Publication 527 discusses days spent working substantially full time on repairs and maintenance. Those can receive different treatment from leisure days. Improving the property is not the same rule, so describe the work accurately.

Record the purpose of each stay when it happens. Save travel records, work notes, invoices, and booking details. A calendar that only says owner visit may not explain what occurred.

Keep vacancy separate from occupancy. Publication 527 says days available but not rented do not count as days of rental use for the mixed-use calculation it describes. An open booking calendar is not proof of paid use.

## Can you show how the residence test works?

Yes. Use the actual fair-rental days to calculate the percentage threshold, then compare personal use with the larger threshold. These examples use hypothetical calendars to show the IRS Topic 415 method.

Assume a home has 100 fair-rental days. Ten percent equals 10 days, so the larger threshold is 14 days. Personal use of 16 days exceeds that threshold, making the home a residence under this test.

Now assume another home has 200 fair-rental days. Ten percent equals 20 days, which is greater than 14. Personal use of 18 days does not exceed the larger threshold.

The second result does not make all expenses deductible. You still need to allocate personal costs and apply the relevant rules. Other tax limits may affect any calculated rental loss.

If your calendar includes overlapping owner and renter use, use the detailed Publication 527 instructions. Do not force the facts into these simplified examples. Give the full calendar to your preparer.

## How do you divide rental and personal expenses?

Divide expenses using the rules that fit the cost and use. IRS Topic 415 generally describes splitting total expenses between rental and personal use based on days used. Publication 527 provides more detailed instructions and worksheets.

Separate direct rental expenses from shared costs. Advertising a rental stay is different from a whole-home insurance premium. A cost used only for personal enjoyment should not enter the rental ledger because the property sometimes earns rent.

Keep interest, property taxes, repairs, utilities, and depreciation identifiable. Different categories can require different review. Ask your preparer to confirm the allocation method rather than applying a blanket percentage to every bill.

The [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) can help you budget a quoted management service. Tax reporting still depends on the actual charge and use. Save the agreement and invoices behind the budget figure.

A cash reserve transfer is not a rental deduction by itself. Furniture or improvements also need proper classification. Publication 527 explains depreciation and improvements separately from ordinary expenses.

## When is rental income unreported under minimal use?

IRS Topic 415 gives a special rule for a dwelling used as a residence and rented fewer than 15 days. Under that rule, you do not report the rental income. You also do not deduct rental expenses.

Both conditions matter. Do not use the rule for every property rented briefly without reviewing residence status. Keep booking and personal-use records even when the rule appears to apply.

The federal income tax rule does not settle local rental permission, lodging taxes, association limits, or insurance. Check those separately before accepting bookings. A tax result is not a rental license.

## How do losses work on mixed-use homes?

A home treated as a residence can face rental deduction limits tied to income. Topic 415 explains that some unused expenses may carry forward, subject to the later year's limits. Publication 527 has the detailed calculation.

If the property is not a residence under this test, other rules can still limit losses. IRS Publication 925 explains passive activity and at-risk restrictions. A negative worksheet result is not proof that wages can absorb the deduction.

Before filing, gather the complete calendar, payment records, personal-use facts, and itemized expenses. Use the [guide library](/guides) to organize related records. Ask your preparer to show the separate residence, allocation, and loss-limit steps.

## What does the law say about wear and tear?

Deposit statutes let a landlord deduct for damage the tenant caused, but not for ordinary wear and tear. Two examples:

- **Virginia (55.1-1226):** the deposit may cover damages from the tenant's breach of duty, "less reasonable wear and tear."
- **Oregon (90.300):** the landlord may claim only the amount reasonably needed to repair damage caused by the tenant, "not including ordinary wear and tear."

Federal voucher rules draw the same line. 24 CFR 982.404 lets a housing agency hold the family responsible for damage "beyond damage resulting from ordinary use."

The tenant's duty in Arizona's 33-1341 shows the other side. The tenant must "not deliberately or negligently destroy, deface, damage, impair or remove any part of the premises." Damage is what breaks that duty. Wear is what normal living does over time.

## Wear vs damage table by item

Use this table as a guide at move-out. Context matters: a 6-month tenancy should show less wear than a 6-year one.

| Item | Normal wear and tear | Damage |
|---|---|---|
| Carpet | Light walkway wear, flattened pile, some fading | Burns, pet urine, large stains, tears, rips |
| Hardwood floor | Light surface scratches, dull finish in traffic paths | Deep gouges, water damage from spills left, pet scratches through finish |
| Vinyl or tile | Minor scuffs, slight wear | Cracked tiles, burns, cuts |
| Paint | Fading, light scuffs, minor marks | Crayon, marker, large gouges, unapproved colors |
| Walls | A few small nail holes | Many holes, large anchors, holes that need patching |
| Doors | Loose handles, worn finish | Holes, broken frames, kicked-in panels |
| Blinds | Faded slats, sun wear | Bent or missing slats, cut cords |
| Windows | Worn seals, minor wear on tracks | Broken glass, torn screens from misuse |
| Kitchen counters | Light wear, minor dulling | Burns, deep cuts, broken edges |
| Appliances | Normal age wear, minor rust | Broken shelves, dents, missing parts |
| Bathroom | Worn caulk, dull grout, light mineral stains | Broken fixtures, cracked toilet, mold from leaks the tenant did not report |
| Tub or shower | Worn finish from normal cleaning | Chips, cracks, stains from dye |
| Light fixtures | Worn switches | Broken globes, missing fixtures |
| Yard | Worn grass in paths | Dead lawn from neglect where the lease required care, holes dug by pets |
| Keys and locks | Worn keys, sticky locks | Lost keys, broken locks, changed locks |

## Is carpet wear normal?

Some carpet wear is always normal. Carpet in use loses pile in walkways and fades near windows. Those changes are wear and tear. Burns, pet urine, tears and stains that do not clean out are damage.

Oregon adds a specific rule. A landlord may deduct for carpet cleaning, beyond normal vacuuming, only if 3 things are true. The cleaning uses a carpet cleaning machine. The carpet was cleaned or replaced before the tenant moved in. And the written lease says the landlord may deduct the cost whether or not the tenant cleans.

## Are nail holes damage?

A few small nail holes are usually wear and tear. Tenants hang pictures in their homes. Many holes, wall anchors, TV mounts and holes that need drywall patching lean toward damage.

What to do: say in the lease what is allowed. For example, "small picture nails allowed; no anchors or mounts without written approval." Then the line is clear at move-out.

## Is faded paint wear and tear?

Yes. Paint fades, scuffs and dulls with age and normal living. That is ordinary wear. Repainting after a long tenancy is a normal cost of owning a rental.

Paint becomes damage when the tenant causes it: crayon on walls, smoke stains, large gouges, or a color the tenant applied without approval.

## How do I charge fairly for old items?

Charge only for the part of an item's life the tenant used up early. A full charge for an old carpet or an old fridge asks the tenant to pay for normal aging.

A simple yardstick is the IRS recovery period. IRS Publication 527, Table 2-1, lists 5 years for carpets, appliances and furniture used in rental property. That is a tax rule, not a deposit rule, but it gives you a clear, documented basis.

**Worked example, carpet:**

- Replacement cost: $2,000.
- Useful life (IRS yardstick): 5 years.
- Age at move-out: 3 years.
- Remaining life: 5 − 3 = 2 years, or 2 ÷ 5 = 40%.
- Fair charge if the tenant ruined it: $2,000 × 40% = $800.
- If the carpet was 5 or more years old: the remaining life is 0, so charge little or nothing for replacement. You may still charge for the cost of removal or cleaning where the law allows.

## How do I prove damage?

Prove damage with 3 things: the start condition, the end condition, and the real cost. Without all 3, a deduction is hard to defend.

1. **Start condition:** the signed move-in report and dated photos.
2. **End condition:** move-out photos from the same angles, taken right after the tenant leaves.
3. **Cost:** invoices, receipts or written quotes. Oregon requires labor charges to be based on a reasonable hourly rate.

Then send the itemized list on time. Virginia gives 45 days. Arizona gives 14 days, excluding weekends and holidays, after the tenancy ends, possession is delivered and the tenant asks. In Arizona, a landlord who does not comply may owe twice the amount wrongfully withheld.

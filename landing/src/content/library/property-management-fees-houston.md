## What leasing and renewal fees do Houston managers charge?

Houston leasing fees run from 50% to 100% of one month's rent on the pages that list one. Mynd and Evernest sit at 50%. Keyrenter Houston charges 75%, ELDA 90%, and Sugarland Property Management and Texas Lone Star's Standard plan charge a full month. Real Property Management Prestige and Texas Lone Star lower the leasing fee on their higher-priced plans.

Renewal fees come in 3 forms:

- **Flat:** $175 to $350 (RPM Prestige, Keyrenter Houston, Evernest, Texas Lone Star).
- **Percent of a month's rent:** 20% (ELDA) or 25% (Mynd, with a $399 floor and a $1,000 cap).
- **None:** Texas Property Management Partners says it charges no renewal fee.

## Which local rules add cost for Houston owners?

Texas flood disclosure adds a step to every Houston lease. Texas Property Code 92.0135 requires a written notice that says whether you know the home is in a 100-year floodplain. If you know it flooded in the past 5 years, a second notice says so. Both go in the lease, an addendum or a separate signed paper, at or before signing.

The cost of skipping it: if a tenant suffers a substantial loss from flooding, the tenant may end the lease with written notice within 30 days. Ask your manager to show you its flood notice form.

Other cost drivers to price in:

- **Make-ready work.** Texas Lone Star charges $100 for make-ready management on its lower plans.
- **Maintenance fees.** Flat Fee Landlord adds a 10% maintenance coordination fee to vendor bills. TexasRenters says it does not mark up maintenance.
- **Eviction work.** RPM Prestige lists $275 plus cost on its base plan. Several firms sell eviction protection.
- **Falling rents.** HUD's FY2027 fair market rent for a Houston-area 2-bedroom fell 4.8%, from $1,573 to $1,497. A percent fee falls with the rent. A flat fee does not. See [Houston fair market rent 2027](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/houston-fair-market-rent-2027).

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Must a Houston property manager hold a Texas license?

Yes, in most cases. Texas Occupations Code 1101.351 says a person may not act as a broker without a license from the Texas Real Estate Commission (TREC). A business entity that acts as a broker needs its own license too.

The definition of broker in section 1101.002 includes anyone who, for pay and for another person:

- Leases real estate, or offers or negotiates to lease it.
- Lists real estate for lease.
- Controls the acceptance or deposit of rent from a resident of a single-family rental.

Section 1101.005 exempts an owner or the owner's employee who leases the owner's own property, and an on-site manager of an apartment complex. So an outside company that leases your house and collects the rent needs a license.

What to do: look up the firm and its broker on TREC's license search, and write the license numbers in your management agreement.

## Flat fee or percent: which model is common in Houston?

Both are common in Houston: 6 of our 11 firms charge a flat fee and 5 charge a percent. That is a sharp contrast with Chicago, where 9 of 10 firms we read use a percent.

| Rent | 8% fee | $99 flat | $129 flat | Cheapest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1,200 | $96 | $99 | $129 | 8% |
| $1,500 | $120 | $99 | $129 | $99 flat |
| $2,000 | $160 | $99 | $129 | $99 flat |
| $2,500 | $200 | $99 | $129 | $99 flat |

Two points to weigh. A flat fee usually stays the same in a vacant month, while a percent of collected rent drops to $0. And flat plans often add more line items, such as make-ready, coordination or inspection fees. Compare the total for a full year, not the monthly headline.

You move out on a Saturday. On Monday, the house is a business. Most of the work in this checklist is easy, but the order matters. A missed insurance call or a lost receipt can cost more than a month of rent.

Use the steps below in order. Rules differ by state and city, so check local law for leases, deposits and entry.

## What Paperwork Changes First?

Three accounts change before a tenant moves in: the loan, the insurance and the utilities.

**Loan.** Read your mortgage for an occupancy clause. Many home loans assume you live in the home for a set time after closing. Call your servicer and ask what the loan says about renting. Keep the answer in writing.

**Insurance.** A homeowners policy is written for a home you live in. The North Carolina Department of Insurance says a dwelling fire policy is typically used for a property that is not your primary residence, including rentals. It also says dwelling policies typically do not include liability coverage, so ask about liability or an umbrella policy. Tell your insurer the date the home becomes a rental.

**Utilities.** Decide which utilities the tenant pays. Put that list in the lease. Keep the accounts you pay, such as water in some cities, in your name.

| Account | Action | Done |
|---|---|---|
| Mortgage | Read the occupancy clause, call the servicer | ☐ |
| Insurance | Switch to a policy for a rented home, add liability | ☐ |
| Utilities | Decide who pays each one, set transfer dates | ☐ |
| HOA, if any | Read rental rules and caps before you list | ☐ |
| Mail | Set mail forwarding to your new address | ☐ |
| Bank | Open a separate account for rent and expenses | ☐ |

## Which Repairs and Safety Items Come First?

Fix safety items before cosmetic ones. A tenant can live with an old kitchen. A tenant should not live with a broken smoke alarm.

Walk the home with this list:

- **Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms.** Test each one. Your state or city sets the rules for type and location.
- **Locks.** Rekey all exterior doors after you move out.
- **Electrical and gas.** Fix exposed wires, loose outlets and any gas smell.
- **Water heater.** Check the age, the relief valve and any leaks.
- **Stairs and rails.** Fix loose rails and broken steps.
- **Lead paint.** The EPA says the lead disclosure rule applies to most housing built before 1978. Landlords must give information about known lead-based paint before the tenant signs.
- **Photos.** Take dated photos of every room. You will need them at move-out.

Keep each receipt. The next sections explain why.

## How Do I Set the Rent?

Set rent from what similar homes nearby rent for now. Find at least 3 rentals with the same bedroom count, size and condition in the same area. Fannie Mae asks lenders to use at least 3 comparable rentals when they estimate rent on a departing residence. That is a sound habit for you too.

A home that sits empty costs money each day. Price near the middle of your comps. If you get few calls in 2 weeks, review the price and photos.

If you plan to accept housing vouchers, compare your rent to the local limits in our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits).

Screen every applicant with the same written criteria. The FTC says that if you deny an applicant based partly on a consumer report, you must give an adverse action notice. The Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. 3604, bans choices based on race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status or national origin.

## What Records Do I Need for Taxes From Day One?

Start the rental file on the day the home is available for rent. IRS Publication 527 says you can deduct ordinary and necessary expenses for managing, conserving or maintaining the property from the time you make it available for rent.

Keep these records:

- **Date the home became available for rent.** An email or listing date works.
- **Fair market value on that date.** An appraisal or a broker's opinion helps.
- **Purchase closing statement** and receipts for past improvements.
- **Every expense.** Publication 527 lists common ones: advertising, cleaning and maintenance, insurance, management fees, mortgage interest, repairs, taxes and utilities.
- **Security deposits.** Publication 527 says a deposit you plan to return is not income. A deposit you keep, or one used as last month's rent, is income.

Use a separate bank account. It makes Schedule E much easier.

## What Is the Cost Basis of a Converted Home?

The basis for depreciation of a converted home is the lesser of two numbers. IRS Publication 527 says it is the lower of the fair market value on the date of conversion, or your adjusted basis on that date. Adjusted basis is your cost plus permanent improvements, minus some deductions such as casualty losses.

Here is an example with invented numbers:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Original cost of the home | $240,000 |
| Plus improvements while you lived there | +$20,000 |
| Adjusted basis on conversion date | $260,000 |
| Fair market value on conversion date | $310,000 |
| Basis for depreciation (lesser of the two) | $260,000 |
| Minus land value (land is not depreciated) | −$60,000 |
| Building basis to depreciate | $200,000 |
| Yearly depreciation over 27.5 years | about $7,273 |

If the value had dropped to $230,000, the basis for depreciation would be $230,000 instead. The lesser-of rule stops you from deducting a loss in value that happened while you lived there.

## Will the Home Sale Exclusion Still Apply?

Possibly, for a limited time. IRS Publication 523 says you must own and use the home as your main home for at least 24 months in the 5 years before the sale. You may exclude up to $250,000 of gain, or $500,000 for a married couple filing jointly. Gain equal to depreciation after May 6, 1997, is not excludable.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## What Is the Last Step Before the Tenant Moves In?

Sign a written lease that fits your state's law, collect the deposit, and do a move-in walk-through with the tenant. Then decide who will run the rental. If you will not manage it yourself, compare costs with the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

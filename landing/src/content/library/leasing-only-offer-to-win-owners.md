## What is a leasing-only offer?

A leasing-only offer is tenant placement without ongoing management. You market the rental, screen applicants, sign the lease and hand the keys and the tenant to the owner. After move-in, the owner collects rent and handles repairs.

Lubin Property Management's fee page states the scope well. Its placement-only service covers advertising, applicant screening, tenant selection, the lease and the deposit. It leaves out rent collection, accounting, owner statements, maintenance and inspections.

The idea behind the offer: some owners will not sign a full management agreement on day one. A leasing job lets them see your work at lower commitment. If the tenant is good and the process is smooth, some of them come back for full management.

## What do leasing-only fees look like?

Leasing-only fees on the pages we read sit at about one month's rent. Full-management plans charge less for the same leasing work. We read three published fee pages on 2026-10-19:

| Company | Leasing-only fee | Leasing fee inside a management plan | Monthly management fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lubin Property Management (Memphis) | One month's rent | Not stated on the page | 8% to 12% of monthly rent |
| Real Property Management Unity | 100% of one month's rent | 75% (Standard), 50% (Premium) | 9.9% (Standard), 11.9% (Premium) |
| Real Property Management Gold (Southern Maryland) | 6.9% of the 1-year lease total | Not stated on the page | From 8.9% of monthly rent |

Note that 6.9% of a 12-month lease equals about 83% of one month's rent. Example: $2,000 × 12 × 6.9% = $1,656.

Baselane's fee page (updated 2026-09-17) quotes outside studies: 70.6% of one month's rent for placement bundled with management, and 81.8% for standalone placement. Baselane says the bundled number comes from Rapid Eye Inspections research and the standalone number from an iPropertyManagement study. Neither sample is stated, so treat them as rough.

The pattern is clear across these pages. The leasing-only price is higher, and the management plan includes a discount on leasing. That gap is your upgrade pitch.

## How do I price the gap so owners upgrade?

Price the gap so an owner can see the upgrade pays back. Here is a worked example with the Real Property Management Unity rates and a $2,000 rent:

| Item | Leasing-only | Premium plan |
|---|---|---|
| Leasing fee | $2,000 (100%) | $1,000 (50%) |
| Management fee, 12 months | $0 | $2,856 (11.9% × $2,000 × 12) |
| Owner cost in year 1 | $2,000 | $3,856 |
| Owner gets | A tenant and a lease | A tenant, a lease, rent collection, repairs, reports |

The owner pays $1,856 more in year 1 for full management, and $1,000 less up front for leasing. Show this table to the owner. Some will decide that 12 months of service for $1,856 is a fair trade. Use our [fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to run the numbers for your own rates.

One common move is an upgrade credit. If the owner signs full management within a set time, you credit part of the leasing-only fee. This is a hypothesis to test, not an industry rule. It turns the leasing job into a trial.

## How many leasing-only owners convert?

No public study we found measures how many leasing-only owners move to full management. So measure your own rate.

Track 4 numbers each quarter:

1. **Leasing-only jobs closed.** Count each signed placement agreement.
2. **Upgrades within 12 months.** Count owners who sign full management after a leasing job.
3. **Upgrade rate.** Divide upgrades by leasing-only jobs.
4. **Time to upgrade.** Note the months from move-in to the management agreement.

Ask each upgraded owner why they switched. Common triggers to listen for: a late payment, a repair at night, a renewal, or a move-out. Put a reminder in your CRM before each of those dates for every leasing-only owner.

If your upgrade rate stays near zero after 4 quarters, leasing-only is a separate product, not a funnel. Price it to earn a profit on its own.

## How do I market leasing-only without cheapening the brand?

Market leasing-only as a defined service with a clear end, not as "management lite". Owners compare price first when you lead with price.

Do:

- **Show both offers on one page.** Put leasing-only next to full management, with what each includes and what it costs.
- **Name the handoff.** List what you hand over at move-in: lease, deposit record, condition report, tenant contact.
- **Show the upgrade path.** One line: "Want us to manage after move-in? Here is how the leasing fee changes."
- **Use the same screening standard.** The same criteria for every applicant, written down. This also keeps you within fair housing rules.

Do not:

- Lead ads with "cheapest" or "lowest fee". It draws owners who leave at the next price cut.
- Hide the management fee. Owners who find it later trust you less.

Where should the offer appear? Most owner leads in Goodjuju's fall 2026 study came from Google Search and the Map Pack (50.6%) and direct or Maps visits (35.6%) (Goodjuju, 2026-10). So put leasing-only on a page Google can find, linked from your main [property management](/property-management) page, and list it as a service on your Google Business Profile.

## What should the agreement say about upgrades?

The leasing-only agreement should state where your work ends and how the owner upgrades. A vague handoff causes most leasing-only disputes. Use this checklist:

- [ ] Scope: marketing, showings, screening, lease signing, deposit collection, move-in report
- [ ] What is not included: rent collection, repairs, renewals, notices, move-out
- [ ] Who holds the security deposit after move-in, and how it moves to the owner
- [ ] Fee, when it is due, and what happens if the tenant leaves early
- [ ] Any tenant placement guarantee and its limits
- [ ] Upgrade terms: how to switch, any leasing-fee credit, and the deadline for the credit
- [ ] Who handles the lease renewal, and the renewal fee if you do
- [ ] Your license and the state rules that apply to leasing for others

Deposit, notice and license rules differ by state. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

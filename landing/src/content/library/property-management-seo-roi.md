## Which numbers go into property management SEO ROI?

SEO ROI needs 4 numbers: SEO cost, owner leads, close rate, and the value of one signed owner. Everything else is detail. Get these 4 right and the answer is simple math.

| Input | Where you get it | Example value |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly SEO cost | Agency invoice, or staff hours × hourly cost | $1,699 |
| Organic owner leads per month | GA4 key event + "how did you hear about us" | 6 |
| Close rate (lead to signed agreement) | Your CRM | 20% |
| Value of one new owner | Average monthly fee × doors × months the owner stays | $6,480 |

The cost line is the easy part. ClearLead Digital lists SEO plans at $1,199, $1,699 and $2,299 a month, all month to month (pricing page, read 2026-10-19). Goodjuju Marketing lists $1,185, $2,292 and $4,460 a month, plus an application-only $7,930 plan (pricing page, read 2026-10-19). If you do SEO in house, count the staff hours, writer fees and tools.

The owner value is the number most companies get wrong. Goodjuju's pricing page uses a default of 1 door × $180 a month × 36 months, or $6,480. That is their assumption, not your number. Use your own average fee, average doors per owner and average owner tenure.

## The SEO ROI formula, worked out

SEO ROI = (fee revenue from organic owners − SEO cost) ÷ SEO cost. Here is one year of a sample company. The numbers are an example, not a benchmark.

1. **Add up the cost.** $1,699 a month × 12 months = $20,388.
2. **Count the leads.** 6 organic owner leads a month × 12 = 72 leads.
3. **Apply the close rate.** 72 × 20% = 14.4, so about 14 new owners.
4. **Value the owners.** 14 × $6,480 = $90,720 in fees over each owner's first 36 months.
5. **Divide.** ($90,720 − $20,388) ÷ $20,388 = 3.45, or 345% ROI.

Two warnings about this math. First, the $90,720 arrives over 3 years, not in year one. Second, a small change in close rate moves the result a lot. At 10% close rate, the same company wins 7 owners, earns $45,360 and gets 122% ROI.

| Close rate | New owners in year 1 | 36-month fee value | ROI on $20,388 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10% | 7 | $45,360 | 122% |
| 20% | 14 | $90,720 | 345% |
| 30% | 21 | $136,080 | 567% |

So before you blame SEO, check your sales follow-up. A slow reply can halve the return of the same traffic.

## How do I credit owner leads to organic search?

Credit owner leads to organic search with 3 records: Google Analytics 4, a UTM-tagged Google Business Profile link, and the lead's own answer. One record alone is not enough.

- **Mark the owner form as a key event.** Google Analytics Help explains how to mark an event, such as a form submit, as a key event. Use one key event per owner form. Keep tenant maintenance and application forms out of it.
- **Tag your Google Business Profile link.** Goodjuju's fall 2026 report notes that Map Pack clicks show as plain "google / organic" in GA4 unless you tag the link. It suggests adding `?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp` to the profile's website link.
- **Ask how the owner first heard about you.** Put a required dropdown on the form and ask again in your first reply. Goodjuju found that GA4 credited 7 leads to AI tools on its own site, but it could trace at least 13.
- **Reconcile once a month.** Put GA4, the form answers and your CRM side by side. Where they disagree, trust the lead's own answer for discovery.

Watch for "Direct" traffic. In the same report, Direct / Maps brought 35.6% of owner leads across 35 companies, but it mixes clients, vendors and untraced visits. Do not credit it to SEO or take it away from SEO without the lead's own answer.

## How long until SEO pays back?

Property management SEO usually takes months, not weeks, to pay back. Goodjuju's pricing FAQ gives its own estimate. It is 2–4 months if the site already ranks well. From a low start in a mid-size market, it is 6–10 months. In the most competitive metros, it is 12–18 months.

Payback month is the month when total fee revenue from organic owners passes total SEO spend. Use monthly fees, not the 36-month value, for this check. This table assumes a slow start: no new owners in months 1–3, then about 1 new owner a month, each paying $180 a month.

| Month | SEO spend to date | New owners to date | Monthly fees now ($180 × owners) | Fees earned to date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | $5,097 | 0 | $0 | $0 |
| 6 | $10,194 | 3 | $540 | $1,080 |
| 12 | $20,388 | 10 | $1,800 | $8,820 |
| 18 | $30,582 | 17 | $3,060 | $23,940 |
| 24 | $40,776 | 24 | $4,320 | $46,800 |

In this example, cash payback comes in month 22, when fees to date ($38,340) pass spend to date ($37,378). That is why you judge SEO over 12 to 24 months. Google Search Central warns that no one can guarantee a #1 ranking. Ask any SEO provider for a realistic estimate and a timeframe in writing.

## How do I compare SEO ROI to Google Ads ROI?

Compare SEO and Google Ads with the same formula, the same owner value and the same time window. Then the only difference is cost and lead count.

Ads stop the day you stop paying. SEO keeps some rankings after the contract ends. So a 1-month view favors ads, and a 24-month view often favors SEO. Neither result is fixed: your market and your site decide it.

Data from one study helps set expectations. In Goodjuju's fall 2026 report, Google Search and the Map Pack brought 50.6% of owner leads. Paid search brought 1.2%, but only 6 of 35 companies ran ads, and the report says to judge ads by your own cost per lead. Use your own numbers before you move budget.

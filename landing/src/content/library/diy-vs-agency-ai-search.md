## Which AI search tasks can your staff do?

Your staff can do most of the base AI search tasks, because they are about facts you already own. GEO (getting cited by AI answers) starts with listings, reviews and clear owner pages. A careful office manager can do these with a checklist.

| Task | Staff can do it? | Skill needed | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fix name, address, phone on every listing | Yes | Care, a sheet | AI reads directories first |
| Complete Expertise.com, Yelp, iPropertyManagement, BBB profiles | Yes | Care | Most cited sources in our data |
| Ask owners for honest reviews | Yes | A routine | Review sites feed AI answers |
| Update Google Business Profile each month | Yes | Low | Google lists it as a best practice |
| Publish a full owner fee table | Yes, with the owner of the firm | Low | Gives fee prompts a number from you |
| Run 10 prompts a month and log results | Yes | Low, a fixed list | Shows if anything changed |
| Write 1 owner article a week | Maybe | Writing, local law checks | Gives AI more pages to cite |
| Add and test schema | Rarely | Technical | Tells engines who you are |
| Check crawler access, robots.txt, CDN rules | Rarely | Technical | Blocks stop all citations |
| Outreach to list pages AI cites | Rarely | Sales, time | Hard to do in spare hours |

In our 50-city test, directories showed up in 134 of 150 AI answers (our data, 2026-10-19). Expertise.com alone was a source in 93. So the first tasks on the list carry real weight, and staff can do them.

## How many hours a month does AI search take?

No public study gives a standard number of hours for a property manager. So treat any hour count as a plan to test, not a fact. The table below is our planning estimate (hypothesis) for a 50 to 500 door firm. Put your own numbers in its place.

| Work | Example hours per month (hypothesis) |
|---|---|
| Listings check and fixes | 2 |
| Review requests | 2 |
| Google Business Profile posts and updates | 1 |
| Monthly prompt check and log | 2 |
| 4 owner articles, written and fact-checked | 12 |
| Total | 19 |

Expect the first month to take longer, because you fix every listing once. After that, the work turns into a monthly routine. Log your real hours for 2 months and update the table.

## What does each option cost?

Compare the full monthly cost of each option. Use the same scope for both, or the numbers do not compare.

Worked calculation for in-house work:

1. **Staff rate.** The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics gives a median pay of $37.87 an hour for market research analysts and marketing specialists (May 2025). Use your own wage plus taxes if you have it.
2. **Hours.** Use the 19 hours from the example above.
3. **Staff cost.** 19 hours × $37.87 = $719.53 a month.
4. **Tool.** Otterly.ai Standard lists $189 a month for 100 prompts (Otterly.ai pricing page, 2026-10-19).
5. **Total.** $719.53 + $189 = $908.53 a month.

Now the options side by side:

| Option | Monthly cost | What you get | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Staff, base tasks only, no tool | 7 hours × $37.87 = $265.09 | Listings, reviews, profile, manual prompt log | BLS; our hour estimate |
| Staff plus tracking tool | $908.53 (example above) | Base tasks, 4 articles, 100 tracked prompts | BLS; Otterly.ai |
| Reporting tool only | $29–$489 (Otterly.ai); $99 (PMW AIO Reporting) | A report, no changes to your site | Vendor pages, 2026-10-19 |
| GEO agency retainer | From about $2,000; some set a $10,000 floor | Strategy, content, schema, outreach, reports | Citant.ai |
| Citata.ai Starter | $750 (first price, hypothesis) | Listings in week 1, owner pages, 4 articles, Monday report | Our offer |

Citant.ai also lists the Profound tracking tool at $99 a month for its Starter plan and $399 for Growth. For the PM-focused agencies and their public prices, see [GEO and AEO agencies for property management compared](/guides/geo-agencies-for-property-management).

## What does an agency add?

An agency adds steady hours, a method and a view across many clients. Staff work often stops when the office gets busy. A retainer keeps a person on the task each week.

What a good agency brings:

- **A baseline.** A prompt list and the results before any change, so you can see what moved.
- **Technical work.** Schema, crawler access and site fixes that office staff rarely do.
- **Content at a steady pace.** Owner articles with checked facts, each week.
- **Outreach.** Contact with the list pages and directories that AI cites.
- **Pattern data.** What worked for other managers in other cities.

What an agency cannot add: your fees, your cities, your door count and your reviews. You still own those facts. Citant.ai notes that a tool alone "does not write, restructure, or place anything." The same is true of an agency that only sends reports.

## How do you decide?

Decide by staff time first, then by scope. Use this table.

| Your situation | Best fit |
|---|---|
| One person can give 5 to 20 steady hours a month | In-house, plus a low-cost tracking tool |
| Nobody has steady hours | Agency or done-for-you service |
| Listings are a mess and you need them fixed fast | Agency for the first 3 months, then in-house |
| You want to test before you spend | In-house base tasks for 2 months, with a prompt log |
| Your site needs schema and technical fixes | Agency or a one-time developer job |

Watch for: a plan that is only a dashboard. Citant.ai warns that "a budget that funds only the instrument produces a dashboard and no movement." Ask each vendor which changes it will make each month.

What to do this week: run the 10-prompt check yourself, fix your 3 most cited listings, and log the date. That gives you a baseline for either path.

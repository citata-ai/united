## Which expenses does IRS Pub 527 list?

IRS Publication 527 lists the most common rental expenses in its chapter on rental expenses. You generally deduct them in the year you pay them, if you use the cash method.

The list, in Pub 527's order, with plain-word examples:

| # | Pub 527 expense | Plain-word example |
|---|-----------------|--------------------|
| 1 | Advertising | Listing fees, signs, photos |
| 2 | Auto and travel | Trips to manage or repair the rental |
| 3 | Cleaning and maintenance | Turnover cleaning, lawn care |
| 4 | Commissions | Leasing commission to an agent |
| 5 | Depreciation | The building cost over 27.5 years |
| 6 | Insurance | Landlord or dwelling policy |
| 7 | Interest (other) | Interest on a credit card used only for the rental |
| 8 | Legal and other professional fees | Lease review, tax preparation for the rental |
| 9 | Local transportation | Mileage to the rental |
| 10 | Management fees | Property manager's monthly fee |
| 11 | Mortgage interest paid to banks | Interest on the rental's loan |
| 12 | Points | Loan points; Pub 527 has special timing rules |
| 13 | Rental payments | Renting equipment for the rental |
| 14 | Repairs | Fixing a leak, replacing a broken window |
| 15 | Taxes | Property tax on the rental |
| 16 | Utilities | Water, trash or power you pay as owner |

Note: Tax Topic 414 says that for tax years 2018 through 2025, expenses are deductible only if incurred in a trade or business, for the production of income, or for managing, conserving or maintaining property held to produce income. A rental held for profit meets that test.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## What is the difference between a repair and an improvement?

A repair keeps the property in its normal working condition. An improvement makes it better, restores it or adapts it to a new use. You deduct a repair. You capitalize and depreciate an improvement.

Pub 527 says an expense is an improvement if it results in a betterment, restores the property or adapts it to a new or different use.

| Repair (deduct this year) | Improvement (depreciate) |
|---------------------------|--------------------------|
| Patch a roof leak | New roof |
| Fix a furnace part | New heating system or furnace |
| Replace a broken window pane | Storm windows |
| Fix a dripping faucet | New water heater |
| Repaint a room | Kitchen modernization |
| Re-secure loose carpet | New wall-to-wall carpeting |
| Fix a section of fence | New fence |

The improvement examples come from Pub 527, Table 1-1. It also lists additions such as a bedroom, bathroom, deck or garage, and items such as a security system, insulation and a septic system.

Two safe harbors can help:

- **De minimis safe harbor.** If you elect it, you can deduct certain low-cost purchases instead of capitalizing them (Pub 527).
- **Routine maintenance safe harbor.** Some costs that look like improvements may still be deductible as routine maintenance (Pub 527).

The IRS's Tangible Property Regulations FAQ has the details. Ask a tax professional before you rely on either.

## Are mortgage interest and property tax deductible?

Yes. Pub 527 says you can deduct mortgage interest you pay on your rental property. Property tax is on the Pub 527 list of common expenses.

What is and is not deductible:

| Payment | Deductible? |
|---------|-------------|
| Mortgage interest on the rental | Yes |
| Mortgage principal | No; it repays the loan |
| Property tax on the rental | Yes |
| Interest on a cash-out refinance used for personal costs | Generally no, for the part not related to rental use (Pub 527) |
| Points on the loan | Yes, under special timing rules (Pub 527) |
| Insurance paid more than 1 year ahead | Only each year's part, in that year (Pub 527) |

Worked example, with made-up numbers:

| Line | Per year |
|------|----------|
| Total mortgage payments | $14,400 |
| Of which interest | $9,600 |
| Of which principal | $4,800 |
| Property tax | $3,600 |
| Deductible interest and tax | $13,200 |

## Can I deduct travel to my rental?

Yes, in most cases, if the trip's main purpose is to run the rental. Pub 527 has two rules.

- **Local transportation.** You may deduct ordinary and necessary local transportation to collect rent or to manage, conserve or maintain the rental. Pub 527 notes that travel between your home and a rental can be nondeductible commuting unless your home is your principal place of business.
- **Travel away from home.** You can deduct ordinary and necessary costs of travel away from home if the main purpose is to collect rent or manage, conserve or maintain the rental. You cannot deduct a trip whose main purpose is to improve the property. That cost goes into depreciation.

The mileage rate: Pub 527 lists a standard mileage rate of 70 cents a mile for 2025.

Worked example: 24 trips of 30 miles each to show, inspect and repair a rental in 2025.

| Line | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Miles (24 x 30) | 720 |
| Rate (2025) | $0.70 |
| Mileage deduction | $504 |

Keep a mileage log: date, miles and purpose of each trip. Pub 527 points to the record rules in Pub 463 for car expenses.

## Which expenses are often missed?

Owners often miss small or rare costs. Check this list each year:

- **Vacant months.** Pub 527 says you may deduct expenses, including depreciation, while a rental held for rent is vacant. You cannot deduct the lost rent.
- **Expenses the tenant paid for you.** These count as income, and you may also deduct them (Pub 527).
- **Pre-rental expenses.** Pub 527 lists them as a topic. Ask a tax professional how they apply to you.
- **Legal and professional fees.** Lease review, eviction filings, and tax preparation for the rental.
- **Leasing and management fees.** Your manager's 1099 and owner statements show them. Estimate your yearly fees with our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## What records must I keep?

Keep a record for every income and expense item, and keep improvement records until you sell. Pub 527 says to separate the costs of repairs and improvements and keep accurate records, because you need the cost of improvements when you sell or depreciate.

Record checklist:

- **Receipts and invoices** for every expense.
- **Bank and card statements** for a separate rental account.
- **Lease, rent ledger and deposit records.**
- **Mileage log.**
- **Closing statement** from the purchase, for basis.
- **Improvement file:** date, cost and what was done.
- **Owner statements and Form 1099s** from your manager.
- **Prior years' Form 4562** for depreciation.

Missouri puts its deposit rules in one statute: RSMo 535.300. We read the text from the Missouri Revisor of Statutes on 2026-10-19. The revisor site did not answer our requests, so we read its page through the Internet Archive copy. That copy shows the version in effect since August 28, 2018.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | 2 months' rent | RSMo 535.300.1 |
| Where to hold it | Federally insured bank, credit union or depository institution | RSMo 535.300.2 |
| Interest | Belongs to the landlord; no interest due to the tenant | RSMo 535.300.2 |
| Return deadline | 30 days after the tenancy ends | RSMo 535.300.3 |
| Itemized list | Required for any amount withheld | RSMo 535.300.3 |
| Allowed deductions | Unpaid rent, damage beyond ordinary wear and tear, short-notice damages | RSMo 535.300.4 |
| Move-out inspection | Written notice of date and time; tenant may attend | RSMo 535.300.5 |
| Penalty | Twice the amount wrongfully withheld | RSMo 535.300.6 |
| Pet deposit | Not a "security deposit" under the statute | RSMo 535.300.8 |
| Local deposit caps | Banned since 2025-08-28 | RSMo 441.043.2(3) |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Missouri?

The maximum security deposit in Missouri is 2 months' rent. RSMo 535.300.1 says a landlord "may not demand or receive" more than that amount.

The name of the charge does not decide the cap. RSMo 535.300.8 defines a security deposit as any money or property, "however denominated", that secures the rental agreement. One exception: money called a deposit for a pet is outside the definition.

Worked example for a $1,400 unit:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Monthly rent | $1,400 |
| Cap (2 × rent) | $2,800 |
| Pet deposit | Not counted toward the cap (RSMo 535.300.8) |

Cities cannot set a lower cap. A 2025 change to RSMo 441.043 took effect on August 28, 2025. It bars any county or city ordinance that "limits the amount of security deposit a landlord may require".

## How many days does a Missouri landlord have to return a security deposit?

A Missouri landlord has 30 days after the tenancy ends. Within that time, RSMo 535.300.3 gives the landlord 2 options:

1. **Return the full deposit.**
2. **Send a written itemized list** of the damages, with the balance of the deposit.

The landlord complies by mailing the list and any payment to the tenant's last known address. Ask the tenant for a forwarding address at move-out. Without one, use the rental address.

Watch for: the 30 days count from the end of the tenancy, not from the day you get the keys back. Put the end date in your file the day the lease ends.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Missouri?

A Missouri landlord may deduct only amounts "reasonably necessary" for 3 reasons in RSMo 535.300.4:

1. **Unpaid rent** due under the rental agreement.
2. **Restoring the unit** to its move-in condition, ordinary wear and tear excepted.
3. **Actual damages from short notice,** when the tenant did not give the notice the law or the lease requires. The landlord must make reasonable efforts to mitigate (reduce) those damages.

Carpet cleaning has its own rule. The lease may set a carpet cleaning fee, and you may withhold the amount you actually spend. The lease must also tell the tenant that he or she may owe actual carpet cleaning costs beyond ordinary wear and tear. Within 30 days of the end of the tenancy, give the tenant a receipt for the actual cost.

If the damage is more than the deposit, you can sue for the rest. RSMo 535.300.7 keeps the landlord's right to recover actual damages above the deposit.

## Does a Missouri landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. A Missouri landlord who keeps any part of the deposit must give a written itemized list of damages (RSMo 535.300.3). Send the balance with the list.

Missouri also gives the tenant a right to see the move-out inspection. RSMo 535.300.5 says the landlord must give reasonable written notice of the inspection date and time. The landlord gives the notice in person or at the tenant's last known address. The inspection must be at a reasonable time, and the tenant has the right to be present.

A move-out checklist that follows the statute:

- [ ] Send written notice of the inspection date and time.
- [ ] Hold the inspection at a reasonable time; let the tenant attend.
- [ ] Take dated photos of each room.
- [ ] Price each repair; leave out ordinary wear and tear.
- [ ] Attach the carpet cleaning receipt, if the lease has a carpet clause.
- [ ] Mail the itemized list and balance within 30 days.

## Must a Missouri landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

A Missouri landlord does not pay interest to the tenant. RSMo 535.300.2 says any interest earned on a security deposit "shall be the property of the landlord".

The same subsection controls where the money goes. The landlord holds each deposit for the tenant in a bank, credit union or depository institution insured by a federal agency. The statute does not use the words "separate account". It does require an insured institution. Housing authorities and other government landlords are exempt from this subsection.

## What is the penalty for a Missouri landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

The penalty is twice the amount wrongfully withheld. RSMo 535.300.6 says the tenant "shall recover as damages" double the amount the landlord kept in violation of the section.

Example: you keep $600 for "general cleaning" with no itemized list, and a court finds the whole $600 wrongful. The tenant recovers $1,200 in damages.

The rule cuts both ways. RSMo 535.300.7 does not let the tenant skip the last month's rent and tell you to keep the deposit. If a tenant does that, you can deduct the unpaid rent at move-out under RSMo 535.300.4.

## What about a tenant who leaves without notice?

A tenant who leaves without notice may trigger Missouri's abandonment rule in RSMo 441.065. The unit counts as abandoned when 4 things are true:

1. You reasonably believe the tenant moved out and does not plan to return.
2. Rent is due and unpaid for 30 days.
3. You post a notice on the unit and mail it by first class and certified mail.
4. The tenant does not pay or answer in writing within 10 days.

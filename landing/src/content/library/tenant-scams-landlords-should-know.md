## How big is the problem?

Scams that involve rentals and real estate are common and costly. The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) 2024 report lists:

| Crime type (IC3 2024) | Complaints | Reported losses |
|---|---|---|
| Real estate | 9,359 | $173,586,820 |
| Overpayment | 2,705 | not shown here |
| Business email compromise | 21,442 | not shown here |

These are national numbers across all victims, not only landlords. They still show the scale. Below are the 4 scams landlords meet most, with the check that stops each one.

## How do overpayment and refund scams work?

In an overpayment scam, a fake applicant sends a check or payment for more than the amount due. Then they ask you to send back the difference. Days or weeks later, the original payment bounces. You lose the refund you sent.

A second version: the applicant pays the correct amount, then backs out and asks for a refund. The original payment was fake.

The FTC says banks must make deposited funds available quickly. Seeing the money in your account does not mean the check is good. The FTC says fake checks can take weeks to be discovered. By then the scammer has your refund, and you owe the bank.

**Warning signs:**

- The applicant has not seen the unit and wants to rent it sight unseen.
- The payment is for more than you asked.
- The applicant asks for a refund by wire, gift card or crypto.
- A "relative" or "employer" is paying for them.

**What to do:** the FBI's advice is direct: do not accept overpayment. If a check is for more than the amount, return it. Do not deposit it. Never send a refund until the original payment has fully cleared and your bank confirms it.

## How do fake identity applications work?

A fake identity application uses stolen or made-up details to pass screening. The scammer may want the unit for illegal use, or may plan never to pay rent.

**Warning signs:**

- The ID photo or name does not match the person at the showing.
- Pay stubs have round numbers, wrong math or a fake employer.
- The applicant pushes to skip the showing or the screening.
- Phone numbers for the employer or past landlord go to the same voice.

**Checks that stop it:**

1. **Meet in person** or by live video. Compare the person with the ID.
2. **Verify income** with the source, not with documents the applicant gives you. Call the employer's main number from its website.
3. **Call past landlords** using a number you find in public records, not the one on the application.
4. **Use a screening service** that checks identity.
5. **Check the county recorder** if a "past landlord" claims to own a property.

Apply the same checks to every applicant. Extra checks for some applicants and not others can break Fair Housing law.

If screening results lead you to deny an applicant, the Fair Credit Reporting Act requires an adverse action notice (15 U.S.C. 1681m).

## How do squatters with fake leases work?

In a squatter scam, a person moves into a vacant home and shows a fake lease when asked to leave. Sometimes a scammer "rents" your vacant home to a real victim with a fake listing, and the victim moves in.

**Prevention:**

- [ ] Check vacant units often. Weekly is a good minimum.
- [ ] Use smart locks or rekey between tenants.
- [ ] Search listing sites for your address. Report fake listings.
- [ ] Post a sign with your contact details only if local practice suggests it helps.
- [ ] Ask neighbors to call you if they see move-in activity.

**Removal rules differ by state.** Florida added a fast path in 2024. Since July 1, 2024, Florida Statute 82.036 lets a property owner ask the sheriff to remove unauthorized occupants. The Volusia County Sheriff lists the conditions. They include: the person entered unlawfully, the owner told them to leave, they are not current or former tenants under a written or oral rental agreement, and no related lawsuit is pending. The owner files a sworn form with ID.

In many other states, you may need to file an eviction or an unlawful detainer case. Do not change locks or remove belongings yourself until you know your state's rule. For tenants, Texas Property Code 92.0081 bars a landlord from keeping a tenant out except by judicial process, with narrow exceptions. A person with a fake lease may claim to be a tenant, so get legal advice before you act.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do fake vendor and payment-change scams work?

In a fake vendor scam, you get an invoice for work no one did, or an email that says a real vendor changed bank details. The email may look exactly like your plumber's or your manager's. Business email compromise, the IC3 category for this kind of email fraud, had 21,442 complaints in 2024.

**Warning signs:**

- An invoice from a vendor you do not know.
- A known vendor asks to change bank details by email.
- The email address is one letter off from the real one.
- Pressure to pay today.

**Checks that stop it:**

1. **Keep an approved vendor list.** Pay only vendors on it.
2. **Match each invoice** to a work order you created.
3. **Confirm bank changes by phone,** using a number you already have, not one in the email.
4. **Use two-step approval** for payments above a set amount.
5. **Turn on two-factor login** for your email and bank.

## What do I do if I get scammed?

Act fast. Each hour matters for money transfers.

1. **Call your bank** and ask them to stop or recall the payment.
2. **Report it** to the FBI at ic3.gov and to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
3. **Report fake listings** to the listing site.
4. **Save all messages,** checks and documents.
5. **Change passwords** if email was involved.

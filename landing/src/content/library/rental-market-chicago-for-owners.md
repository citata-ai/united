## How much did HUD's fair market rent change in Chicago for FY2027?

HUD's fair market rent for a Chicago-area 2-bedroom rose 12.9% for FY2027, from $1,781 to $2,011. That was the second-largest rise among the 50 largest HUD metro areas, after Kansas City (our ranking of HUD's FY2027 data). The new FMRs took effect on 2026-10-01 (Federal Register, 91 FR 56156).

| Unit size | FY2026 FMR | FY2027 FMR | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Studio | $1,480 | $1,678 | +13.4% |
| 1 bedroom | $1,581 | $1,780 | +12.6% |
| 2 bedrooms | $1,781 | $2,011 | +12.9% |
| 3 bedrooms | $2,294 | $2,586 | +12.7% |
| 4 bedrooms | $2,653 | $2,954 | +11.3% |

Source: HUD FY2027 and revised FY2026 FMR files for the Chicago-Joliet-Naperville HUD Metro FMR Area.

The FMR is HUD's estimate of the 40th percentile gross rent (rent plus utilities) paid by recent movers. Housing authorities set voucher payment standards at 90% to 110% of it (24 CFR 982.503). For a 2-bedroom, that range is now $1,810 to $2,212. The full table, with the housing authority, is on our [Chicago fair market rent 2027](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/chicago-fair-market-rent-2027) page.

## Are Chicago rents rising or falling?

Chicago rents are rising over the year and dipping for the season. Apartment List's October 2026 report for the City of Chicago shows these numbers:

| Measure | Chicago city | Illinois | US |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median rent, all sizes | $1,882 | — | $1,388 |
| 1-bedroom median | $1,745 | — | $1,220 |
| 2-bedroom median | $1,900 | — | $1,374 |
| Change, past 12 months | +4.4% | +3.1% | -0.4% |
| Change, September 2026 | -0.8% | — | -0.1% |

Source: Apartment List, Chicago rent report and national rent report, October 2026.

Three points stand out:

- **The year is still strong.** Chicago rents rose 5.8% from January to September 2026. That is slower than the 7.9% rise in the same months of 2025.
- **The city beats the metro.** The metro-wide median is $1,741, and metro rents rose 3.0% in a year. The city is 8.1% above the metro median.
- **The Midwest leads.** Apartment List names Chicago among Midwest markets in the national top 10 for rent growth. The others it names are Detroit, Milwaukee, Kansas City and Cleveland.

The September drop is normal. Apartment List notes that fewer renters move in fall and winter, so owners give small discounts to fill units. Plan your lease end dates for late spring and summer when you can.

## Which local rules affect Chicago rental returns?

The Chicago Residential Landlord and Tenant Ordinance (RLTO) affects returns in 3 ways. Most rentals in the city fall under it. The main exemption is an owner-occupied building with 6 units or fewer.

| Rule | Effect on your return | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit in a separate Illinois bank account, with yearly interest | Admin work. A mistake costs 2 times the deposit plus interest. | 5-12-080 |
| 30, 60 or 120 days' notice before a lease ends or rent rises | A late notice can delay a rent increase by up to 60 days. | 5-12-130(j) |
| Late fee cap: $10 on the first $500 of rent, plus 5% of the rest | On $1,900 rent, the most you can charge is $80. | 5-12-140(h) |

Worked example, a late rent-increase notice: you plan a 4.4% increase on $1,900, or $84 a month. If the notice is defective, the tenant may stay up to 60 days on the old rent. That costs you about $168. The deposit rule costs far more: on a $1,900 deposit, the penalty is $3,800.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What vacancy rate does Chicago have?

We could not verify a Chicago metro vacancy rate. The Census Bureau publishes rental vacancy rates for large metros in its Housing Vacancy Survey. But census.gov refused our requests on 2026-10-19, and we do not quote a number we did not read.

What we can say from Apartment List's national report (2026-09-29):

- The national multifamily vacancy rate fell to 7% in September 2026, after a peak earlier in the year.
- Units took an average of 34 days to lease after listing.
- 33 of 55 large metros now have rents up year over year.

For your own building, your days-on-market is the number that counts. Track it for each turnover. If a unit sits more than 30 days, check the price against the Apartment List median for its size.

## How much entry notice do I give for a showing?

Use the showing rule for the property's jurisdiction, not a notice period copied from a repair policy. Check the lease and any local requirements before booking the visit.

Washington RCW 59.18.150 addresses showings with at least 1 day's notice at a specified time. It also bars excessive showings that unreasonably interfere with tenant enjoyment. Read the full conditions rather than treating the notice as unlimited permission. [Read Washington's entry statute](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=59.18.150).

Florida section 83.53 permits showing access under its listed conditions and bars harassment. Its defined 24-hour reasonable notice rule concerns repairs. Do not label that repair provision a universal Florida showing deadline. [Read Florida's access statute](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0083/Sections/0083.53.html).

Record the applicable rule in the property file. Give leasing staff a reviewed notice method and a contact for access questions. A calendar invitation alone may not meet the required process.

Keep the actual notice and delivery proof. If the visit changes, check whether new notice is needed. Do not assume an earlier canceled visit authorizes a later unannounced entry.

## Can the current tenant refuse showings?

A refusal needs review under the law, lease and facts. Both Washington and Florida address unreasonable withholding of consent, while also limiting landlord access.

Ask why the proposed time does not work. Work shifts, pets, a health concern or a visitor can create a practical conflict. A reasonable reschedule can resolve the issue without a legal dispute.

Distinguish a request for another time from a blanket refusal. Keep the messages and offered alternatives. Do not label the tenant obstructive because they asked who will enter.

If access remains disputed, use the lawful notice and enforcement route with local advice. Do not force a showing by treating it as an emergency. A prospect's tight schedule does not create an emergency.

Avoid threatening the deposit or renewal over an unresolved access request. Review any separate lease issue through its own process. Keep staff messages factual and calm.

## Should I offer the tenant a credit?

A voluntary credit can make an occupied showing plan easier to carry out. Use it only as a clearly documented arrangement, not as a substitute for required rights.

State what the credit covers. It might support agreed visit windows, photography coordination or preparation help. Avoid promising payment for a vague standard such as making the home perfect.

Check the amount against your leasing budget and the tenant's actual effort. There is no universal credit rate. Do not present an invented figure as a local market standard.

Record when the credit posts and whether a canceled visit affects it. Keep the agreement readable. A tenant should not need to guess whether the manager approved the credit.

Offer a practical alternative when a credit does not solve the problem. You may use existing accurate photos, a floor plan or showings after move-out. Compare the likely vacancy cost with the disruption of repeated visits.

Use the [management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to plan leasing costs separately. A budget tool does not establish entry authority.

## How do I keep the unit ready for photos?

Agree on a modest photo plan and protect personal information. A lived-in home does not need to look like a staged vacant listing.

Ask the tenant to move sensitive papers and valuables before the planned session. Do not move or open personal items without permission. Avoid faces, identity documents, medication labels and security details in published images.

Use a shot list focused on the property. Photograph room layout, fixtures and relevant features. Review the images before uploading them to a public listing.

| Preparation task | Manager's role | Tenant coordination |
|---|---|---|
| Access | Check law and give notice | Discuss a workable time |
| Photos | Set the shot list and review privacy | Move personal items voluntarily |
| Pets | Plan safe entry and exit | Agree on the pet arrangement |
| Keys | Keep controlled access | Confirm lock and door instructions |
| Cancellations | Notify everyone promptly | Confirm any new visit |
| Follow-up | Check doors and report issues | Offer a contact for concerns |

The table is a recommended coordination plan. It does not create new tenant cleaning duties.

If photos need major changes, consider waiting until the unit is vacant. Do not misrepresent current condition by hiding defects or using outdated images without context.

## How do I run the visit without causing problems?

Use a named host, a clear time window and a controlled route. Keep prospects with the host while inside the occupied home.

Confirm the appointment before traveling. Cancel promptly when the prospect cannot attend. Repeated no-shows waste the tenant's effort and make future coordination harder.

Ask prospects to avoid touching belongings, opening private storage or taking personal photos. Explain the unit using property facts. Keep the current tenant's personal story out of the sales conversation.

After the visit, check doors and lights within the agreed access plan. Report any incident promptly and preserve the facts. Do not leave a complaint for the tenant to discover later.

Use this recommended closeout checklist:

- **Record the visit.** Note attendance, actual access time and any issue.
- **Update the tenant.** Confirm completion or cancellation.
- **Review images.** Remove personal information before public use.
- **Post credits.** Apply any agreed adjustment with a clear reference.
- **Plan the next step.** Avoid repeat visits that add little value.

Your next step is to agree on the showing plan before advertising appointment slots.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

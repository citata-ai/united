Virginia sets one firm cap on late rent fees and one firm condition: the fee must be in the written lease. Both rules are in Va. Code § 55.1-1204(E), part of the Virginia Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (VRLTA). We read the current text on the official Code of Virginia site on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Late fee needs a written lease term | Required | Va. Code § 55.1-1204(E) |
| Maximum late fee | Lesser of 10% of periodic rent or 10% of remaining balance | § 55.1-1204(E) |
| Grace period, written lease | None set by statute | § 55.1-1204 |
| Rent late date, no written lease | Late if not paid by the 5th | § 55.1-1204(C)(4) |
| Notice for unpaid rent | 14 days to pay | § 55.1-1245(F) |
| Late fees during a payment plan (from July 1, 2027, landlords with more than 4 units) | No new late fees on the plan amount | § 55.1-1245(G), 2027 version |
| Online payment fee | Only if a free payment method exists; capped at the actual third-party cost | § 55.1-1204(J) |

## What is the maximum late fee in Virginia?

The maximum Virginia late fee is 10% of the periodic rent or 10% of the remaining balance due, whichever is less. The exact words of § 55.1-1204(E) are: "No such late charge shall exceed the lesser of 10 percent of the periodic rent or 10 percent of the remaining balance due and owed by the tenant."

The second test matters when the tenant pays part of the rent. A small unpaid balance means a small fee. Here is a worked calculation for 3 cases. The rents are examples.

| Case | Monthly rent | Unpaid balance | 10% of rent | 10% of balance | Maximum fee |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Nothing paid | $1,800 | $1,800 | $180 | $180 | $180 |
| B. Part paid | $1,800 | $300 | $180 | $30 | $30 |
| C. Higher rent, part paid | $2,500 | $1,000 | $250 | $100 | $100 |

How to calculate it:

1. **Find the periodic rent.** Use the monthly rent in the lease.
2. **Find the remaining balance.** Use what the tenant still owes on the late date.
3. **Take 10% of each.** Multiply both numbers by 0.10.
4. **Charge no more than the lower number.** That is your cap for the period.

Watch for: a flat "$100 late fee" in a lease can break the cap. In case B, the cap is $30, not $100.

## Does Virginia law require a grace period before a late fee?

No, not when there is a written lease. § 55.1-1204 does not set a grace period for a written rental agreement. So the lease sets the due date and the day the fee starts.

The rule changes when the landlord offers no written lease. Then § 55.1-1204(C) creates a 12-month tenancy by law. Under it, rent is due on the 1st and is late only if not paid by the 5th. After the 5th, the landlord may charge a late fee "as provided in this chapter".

But § 55.1-1204(E) still says a late charge needs a written lease term. So a landlord with no written lease has a weak basis for any fee. Offer a written lease. § 55.1-1204(B) requires you to offer one anyway.

## Must the late fee be written in the lease in Virginia?

Yes. § 55.1-1204(E) says: "A landlord shall not charge a tenant for late payment of rent unless such charge is provided for in the written rental agreement."

Put these items in the lease:

- [ ] The rent due date.
- [ ] The day the late fee starts.
- [ ] The fee amount or formula.
- [ ] A line that the fee never exceeds the § 55.1-1204(E) cap.
- [ ] How the tenant can pay without a processing fee.

The last item comes from § 55.1-1204(J). You may charge a fee to process a card or online payment only if you also offer a payment method with no fee. That fee cannot exceed your actual out-of-pocket cost from the third-party processor. You must also accept rent by check and money order.

A landlord cannot add a late fee in the middle of a lease alone. § 55.1-1204(I) says a one-sided change is not valid unless both parties consent in writing.

## Can a Virginia landlord charge a daily late fee?

The VRLTA does not name daily late fees. The cap in § 55.1-1204(E) limits the total late charge, not the method. So a daily fee is possible only if the total stays under the cap.

Example: rent is $1,500 and the tenant pays nothing. The cap is $150. A $10 daily fee would reach $150 on day 15. After that, you cannot add more for that period.

This is a reading of the statute text, not a court ruling. If your lease uses a daily fee, ask a Virginia attorney to check it.

## Can a Virginia landlord evict a tenant for unpaid late fees?

The VRLTA defines rent broadly. § 55.1-1200 says "Rent" means "all money, other than a security deposit, owed or paid to the landlord under the rental agreement." A late charge in the lease is money owed under the rental agreement.

So the 14-day nonpayment notice in § 55.1-1245(F) can cover it. Other parts of the VRLTA also treat late charges as part of the debt:

- **Redemption.** To stop a nonpayment case, the tenant must pay rent plus "late charges contracted for in the rental agreement and as provided by law" (§ 55.1-1250(C)).
- **Judgment.** The court may award late charges in the money judgment (§ 55.1-1245(I)).
- **Deposit.** At move-out, you may apply the deposit to accrued rent, "including the reasonable charges for late payment" (§ 55.1-1226(A)).

A judge still checks the amount. A fee above the cap or missing from the lease is at risk in court.

A change starts on July 1, 2027. The 2027 version of § 55.1-1245 makes a landlord with more than 4 units offer a payment plan in some nonpayment notices. During the plan, you cannot add late fees on the plan amount if the tenant pays on time.

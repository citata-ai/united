## How fast must a landlord make repairs?

A landlord must make repairs within the time set by state law, the lease, and any program the unit is in. No federal rule sets one deadline for all private rentals. Most state laws start the clock when the tenant gives written notice. The deadline is shorter for problems that affect health and safety.

| Rule | Deadline | What happens if missed | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arizona, health and safety breach | 5 days after written notice | Tenant may end the lease | ARS 33-1361(A) |
| Arizona, other material breach | 10 days after written notice | Tenant may end the lease | ARS 33-1361(A) |
| Arizona, minor defect | 10 days after written notice, or sooner in an emergency | Tenant may hire a licensed contractor and deduct up to $300 or half a month's rent, whichever is greater | ARS 33-1363 |
| Florida, code or structure duties | 7 days after written notice of intent to withhold | Tenant may raise a rent-withholding defense | Fla. Stat. 83.60(1)(b) |
| Virginia, serious conditions | More than 30 days after notice presumed unreasonable | Tenant may file in court and pay rent into escrow | Va. Code 55.1-1244 |
| Voucher units (federal) | 24 hours if life-threatening, 30 days for others | Housing agency enforcement | 24 CFR 982.404 |

Other states set different periods. Check your state's landlord-tenant act before you set your own targets.

## What is a habitability repair?

A habitability repair keeps the home fit to live in under the law. State statutes list the basics. Arizona's ARS 33-1324 is a clear example. It requires the landlord to:

- Meet building codes that affect health and safety.
- Make all repairs needed to keep the unit "fit and habitable."
- Keep common areas clean and safe.
- Keep electrical, plumbing, heating, cooling and supplied appliances in good working order.
- Supply running water, hot water, and heat or cooling where installed.

Florida's statute 83.51 is similar. It requires code compliance, or where there is no code, good repair of roofs, windows, doors, floors and other structural parts, plus working plumbing. For buildings other than a single-family home or duplex, it adds pest control, locks and keys, garbage removal, heat in winter, and running and hot water.

## What counts as an emergency repair?

An emergency repair is one that threatens life, health or safety, or causes damage that grows by the hour. Treat these as emergencies:

- No heat in cold weather, or no cooling where your state requires it.
- No running water or hot water.
- Gas smell or carbon monoxide alarm.
- Active water leak, flood or sewage backup.
- Electrical hazard: sparks, burning smell, exposed wiring.
- Broken exterior door or lock.
- Fire or smoke alarm that does not work.

For voucher units, federal rules at 24 CFR 982.404 require the owner to fix a life-threatening deficiency within 24 hours of notice. Use the same 24-hour target for every unit. It is fair, and it is simpler to run.

Arizona's ARS 33-1364 gives tenants extra remedies when a landlord deliberately or negligently fails to supply water, gas, electricity, heat, cooling or essential services. One option: the tenant buys the service and deducts the reasonable cost from rent.

## What can a tenant do if repairs wait too long?

A tenant's remedies depend on the state. Common ones are ending the lease, repair and deduct, rent withholding, rent escrow, and suing for damages. Four examples from the statutes:

1. **End the lease (Arizona).** After 5 days for a health and safety breach, or 10 days for other breaches, with written notice.
2. **Repair and deduct (Arizona).** For a minor defect under $300 or half a month's rent, whichever is greater, after 10 days' written notice. The tenant must use a licensed contractor and give an itemized statement.
3. **Withhold rent as a defense (Florida).** After 7 days' written notice naming the problem and the intent to withhold. The court decides how much to reduce the rent.
4. **Rent escrow (Virginia).** The tenant files in court and pays rent into an escrow account. The court may order repairs and decide how to use the money.

Watch for: remedies do not apply to damage the tenant caused. Arizona, for example, bars repair and deduct for a condition caused by the tenant, family members or guests.

## How do I set repair response targets?

Set written targets that beat your state's legal deadline, and share them with tenants. Here is a simple three-tier model:

| Tier | Examples | Respond | Fix target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emergency | No heat, no water, gas smell, flood, broken exterior lock | Within 1 hour | Make safe within 24 hours |
| Urgent | Broken fridge, one toilet out, AC out in heat, pest problem | Same business day | Within 3 business days |
| Routine | Dripping faucet, loose cabinet door, worn weatherstrip | Within 2 business days | Within 14 days |

These are suggested targets, not legal limits. If your state's deadline is shorter, use the state deadline.

**Steps for each request:**

1. **Log the request** with the date, time and the tenant's words.
2. **Assign a tier** and tell the tenant the target date.
3. **Schedule entry** with the notice your state requires.
4. **Record the fix** with photos and the invoice.
5. **Close the ticket** with the tenant in writing.

A clean log protects you if a tenant later says you ignored a repair. If your maintenance queue is too long to run alone, see how [property management](/property-management) handles it.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

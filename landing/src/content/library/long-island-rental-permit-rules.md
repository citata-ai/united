## Does the Town of Hempstead require a rental permit?

Yes. The Town of Hempstead requires a rental occupancy permit before you rent a dwelling unit. The rules are in Town Code Chapter 99, Article II (Rental Dwelling Units). The permit is valid for two years from the date of issue.

| Item | Town of Hempstead rule (Chapter 99) |
|---|---|
| Application fee | $300 per property, nonrefundable, credited to the permit fee once approved |
| Permit fee | $500 for a property with one rental unit, plus $200 for each additional unit |
| Renewal | $450, plus $200 for each additional unit |
| Late | A late charge equal to two times the fee, prorated for the overdue period |
| Exempt | Mother-daughter and senior residence permit units, and units run by a government agency or housing authority |
| Transient rentals | No permit for a transient rental property |

The application goes to the Department of Buildings on the town's form. The town posts the registration and renewal forms on its website.

A worked example: a two-family house with both units rented. Year 1 costs the $500 permit (the $300 application fee is credited to it) plus $200 for the second unit, or $700. The renewal two years later costs $450 + $200 = $650.

Watch for: the Village of Hempstead is a separate place with its own rules. Check which government your address falls under before you file.

## How do the Babylon and Islip rental permit programs work?

Babylon and Islip both use two-year permits, but the process differs. Babylon adds a public hearing. Islip does not require an interior inspection before it issues a permit.

| Item | Town of Babylon | Town of Islip |
|---|---|---|
| Who issues | Rental Permit Review Board (via Planning and Development) | Division of Code Enforcement |
| Term | 2 years, renew before expiry | 2 years, renew |
| Fee | $400 first unit, $50 each additional unit | $500 one-family or one unit in a two-family; $1,000 two-family |
| Hearing | Public hearing on new applications and first-time renewals | None listed |
| Inspection | A building inspector inspects the unit | The application says state law bars the town from requiring an interior inspection before issuing the permit |
| Key documents | Floor plans, survey, certificate of occupancy, ID, tax bill | Notarized form, tax map number, owner's residential address; for new permits, the deed, a floor plan and the survey |

Sources: Babylon's Rental Review Board page and rental permit FAQ; Islip's Code Enforcement page and its Rental Occupancy Permits application.

Two details matter in practice:

- **Babylon:** the Board meets on set dates, so your rental start date depends on the calendar. The town classifies rentals as Class I or Class II.
- **Islip:** a P.O. Box is not accepted as the owner's legal address. Islip also makes the owner tell prospective tenants that the permit is not a town safety certification.

## Do Nassau County villages use rent stabilization (ETPA)?

Some Nassau localities do. The Emergency Tenant Protection Act (ETPA) applies only where a locality adopts it. Then the Nassau County Rent Guidelines Board sets the yearly allowed increases, which NYS Homes and Community Renewal (HCR) publishes.

| Lease start | 1-year lease | 2-year lease |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-01 to 2027-09-30 (Order 61) | 1.50% | 2.50% |
| 2025-10-01 to 2026-09-30 (Order 60) | 2.00% | 3.00% |
| 2024-10-01 to 2025-09-30 (Order 59) | 1.50% | 2.50% |

HCR notes that Order 58 (2023-24) applied only to the Village of Hempstead.

Which buildings ETPA covers: section 5 of the act exempts buildings with fewer than six dwelling units, and buildings completed or substantially rehabilitated on or after January 1, 1974. So a single-family rental or a 4-unit building is outside ETPA, but it still needs the town or village permit.

A worked example: a stabilized unit at $2,689, the HUD FY2027 2-bedroom fair market rent for Nassau-Suffolk, renews for one year from October 2026. The increase is at most 1.50%, or $40.34. See the [Nassau-Suffolk fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/nassau-suffolk-fair-market-rent-2027).

## What happens if a Long Island owner rents without a permit?

Renting without a permit on Long Island can cost far more than the permit. The Town of Hempstead's penalties, in section 99-29 of its code, are a clear example.

| Offense (Hempstead) | Penalty |
|---|---|
| First conviction | Fine of $1,000 to $5,000, up to 15 days in jail, or both |
| Second conviction within 10 years | Fine of $2,500 to $10,000, up to 15 days in jail, or both |
| Late permit | Late charge of two times the fee, prorated |

Babylon's Rental Review Board page states that it is a violation of Town Code to rent any dwelling unit without a valid permit. Islip's application warns that failure to renew may lead to enforcement, including legal proceedings.

Compliance checklist for a Long Island owner:

- [ ] Confirm whether your address is in a town, a village or a city. Each may have its own permit.
- [ ] Gather the deed, survey, floor plan and certificate of occupancy.
- [ ] File before the tenant moves in.
- [ ] Put the two-year renewal date in your calendar.
- [ ] Check whether your building is under ETPA (6 or more units, built before 1974, in an adopting locality).
- [ ] Use the Nassau guideline that matches the lease start date.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

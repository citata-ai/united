## What is a typical yearly rent increase?

The national benchmark is the BLS rent index. The Bureau of Labor Statistics reports that the rent of primary residence index rose 2.8% from September 2025 to September 2026. Over the same 12 months, the all-items CPI-U rose 3.7% and shelter rose 3.2%.

| BLS index, Sept. 2025 to Sept. 2026 | 12-month change |
|---|---|
| Rent of primary residence | 2.8% |
| Owners' equivalent rent | 3.3% |
| Shelter | 3.2% |
| All items (CPI-U) | 3.7% |

The BLS figure is a national average across many markets. Your street can move faster or slower. Use it as a sanity check, not as your price.

## How do I compare to market rent?

Compare the tenant's current rent to what the same unit would rent for today. Pull 3 to 5 active or recent listings near you with the same beds, baths and size. Apartments.com offers free rent comparison reports, and Zillow Rental Manager shows a Rent Zestimate with nearby comps.

Then place the unit in one of 3 cases:

| Case | What it means | Typical move |
|---|---|---|
| Rent is well below market | Tenant pays less than a new renter would | Raise in steps over 2 renewals, not all at once |
| Rent is near market | The unit is priced right | Raise near the local change, or hold flat |
| Rent is above market | A new renter would pay less | Hold flat, or the tenant may leave |

Watch for: a big catch-up raise can push out a tenant who pays on time. Two moderate raises can get you to market with less risk of a move.

## Is there a legal limit on how much I can raise rent?

Some states cap the increase. Two examples with current numbers:

| State | Cap | Notice | Other rules | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oregon | 2027: 10%. 2026: 9.5%. Formula: lesser of 10% or 7% plus CPI | 90 days, written | No raise in the first year; max once per 12 months | Oregon DAS; ORS 90.323 |
| Washington | 2026: 9.683%. Formula: lesser of 10% or 7% plus CPI | 90 days, written | Applies to rental units under RCW 59.18 | WA Commerce; WA Attorney General |

Oregon's ORS 90.323 also says the notice must state the amount of the increase, the new rent and the date it takes effect. Many cities have their own rent rules, so check local law too.

## When is a flat rent better?

A flat renewal is better when the raise you would get is smaller than the risk of a vacancy. That is most likely when:

- The rent is already at or above market.
- The tenant pays on time and takes care of the unit.
- Your market has many empty units, so a vacancy could last.
- The renewal falls in a slow leasing season.

A flat rent is still a choice, not a habit. Check the market again at the next renewal.

## Worked example: increase vs turnover cost

The right raise depends on what one turnover costs you. Here is the math with example inputs. Replace each number with your own.

**Example inputs (not market data):**

- Current rent: $1,800 a month.
- Planned raise: 5%, or $90 a month.
- If the tenant leaves: 1 month vacant, $1,200 to clean, paint and repair, and $300 in listing and screening time.

**Step 1. Value of the raise over 12 months:**
$90 × 12 = $1,080.

**Step 2. Cost of one turnover:**
$1,800 (1 month lost rent) + $1,200 (make-ready) + $300 (leasing) = $3,300.

**Step 3. Compare:**
The raise brings in $1,080 a year. One turnover costs $3,300. The raise only pays off if it does not make the tenant leave. If a 5% raise gives even a 1-in-3 chance that the tenant moves, the expected turnover cost is $3,300 ÷ 3 = $1,100. That is more than the $1,080 the raise brings in.

**Step 4. Try a smaller raise:**
At 3%, the raise is $54 a month, or $648 a year. If that cuts the chance of a move to 1 in 10, the expected cost is $330. The smaller raise wins: $648 − $330 = $318 net, against $1,080 − $1,100 = −$20.

The odds are your judgment, not data. But the math shows why a modest raise for a good tenant often beats a big one.

## What if the tenant pushes back on the raise?

Listen first, then decide with numbers. Ask the tenant what they found for less, and check those listings yourself. If the comps are real, meet in the middle or offer a longer lease at a lower raise. If they are not, explain your comps in a short note. Treat every tenant who asks the same way, and put any change in writing.

## How do I deliver the renewal offer?

Send the renewal offer in writing, well before the notice deadline. A clear offer includes:

1. **The new rent** and the date it starts.
2. **The amount of the raise** in dollars and percent.
3. **The lease term** offered, such as 12 months.
4. **A reply date**, at least 2 weeks before your notice deadline.
5. **Any change** to fees or rules.

In Oregon and Washington, the notice must reach the tenant at least 90 days before the increase. Plan your calendar from that date back.

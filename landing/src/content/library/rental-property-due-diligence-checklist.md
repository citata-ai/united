## Which inspections should I order?

Order a general home inspection on every rental, then add specialty checks where the general report or the age of the home points to risk. Book them on day 1 of your inspection period. Good inspectors fill up fast.

| Inspection | When to order | Why it matters for a rental |
|------------|---------------|-----------------------------|
| General home inspection | Always | Finds the list of repairs to price |
| Sewer camera scan | Older homes, large trees, past backups | A broken main line is a large, urgent bill |
| Roof inspection | The roof is near the end of its life, or the general report flags it | A roof is the largest capital cost on most houses |
| HVAC service check | The system is old or has no service records | No heat or cooling is an urgent tenant call |
| Electrical check | Old panel, fuses, or DIY wiring | Safety risk and insurance issue |
| Lead-based paint check | Homes built before 1978 | Federal disclosure rules apply when you lease |
| Mold or moisture check | Musty smell, stains, wet basement | Health complaints and repair cost |
| Pest inspection | Many lenders and states ask for it | Wood damage is hidden |

Lead paint rules, in plain words. Federal rules call housing built before 1978 "target housing" (40 CFR 745.103). Before a buyer is bound to buy target housing, the seller must give the buyer 10 days to test for lead-based paint (40 CFR 745.110). Both sides can agree in writing to another period. The buyer can waive this in writing.

The rule matters after you close too. When you lease that home, you become the lessor. Under 40 CFR 745.107, you must give the tenant an EPA-approved lead hazard pamphlet and disclose any known lead-based paint before the tenant signs. Ask the seller for every lead report they have.

## How do I check the current leases and rent roll?

Check the leases by matching 3 records: the rent roll, the signed leases and the money in the bank. All 3 must agree.

Ask the seller for:

- **Rent roll.** Unit, tenant name, rent, lease start and end, deposit, balance due.
- **Every signed lease** and every amendment.
- **12 months of bank statements** or a ledger that shows rent deposits.
- **Deposit records.** Where each deposit is held and how much.
- **Tenant estoppel letters.** Each tenant signs to confirm rent, deposit, lease dates and side deals.

Red flags in the tenant file:

1. **Rent roll is higher than deposits.** Some tenants may not pay in full.
2. **All leases signed in the last 60 days.** The seller may have filled units at low rents or with weak screening.
3. **Prepaid rent.** The seller may have collected rent you expect to receive.
4. **Side deals.** "Tenant does yard work for $200 off" lives in emails, not the lease.
5. **Missing deposits.** You may owe the tenant a deposit the seller kept.

Vacancy check. If the home is empty, test the rent with signed leases of similar homes nearby. For context, the Census Bureau put the national rental vacancy rate at 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026.

## What should I check about permits and zoning?

Check permits and zoning to confirm the home can legally be used the way the listing says. A "3-bedroom" with one unpermitted basement bedroom is a 2-bedroom to the city.

| Item | Where to check | What can go wrong |
|------|----------------|-------------------|
| Building permits | City or county permit portal | Unpermitted additions, decks or basement units |
| Zoning | City zoning map | A "duplex" in a single-family zone |
| Rental license or registration | City housing or building department | Some cities require a license and an inspection before you rent |
| Open code violations | City code enforcement records | Fines move to you at closing in some places |
| Short-term rental rules | City code | If your plan depends on short stays |
| Flood zone | FEMA Flood Map Service Center | Flood insurance cost and lender rules |

FEMA calls its Flood Map Service Center the official online source for flood maps under the National Flood Insurance Program. Look up the address before you remove your contingencies.

## How do I confirm taxes, insurance and HOA costs?

Confirm each cost with a document from the source, not the listing sheet. The listing sheet shows the seller's costs, and your costs can be higher.

**Property tax.** Many counties reassess at the sale price. Some give a lower rate or an exemption to owner-occupied homes, and that exemption ends when an investor buys. Ask the county assessor for an estimate at your price.

At closing, IRS Publication 551 says real estate taxes you pay that the seller owed, without reimbursement, become part of your basis. Keep the settlement statement.

**Insurance.** Get a written quote for a landlord or dwelling fire policy, not a homeowner policy. Ask about the roof age, flood and liability limits.

**HOA.** Ask for the HOA's budget, the reserve study, the last 12 months of board minutes, and any approved or planned special assessment. Rules on reserve studies differ by state. Florida is one example. Section 718.112 requires a condo association to complete a structural integrity reserve study at least every 10 years. The rule applies to each building of 3 habitable stories or higher.

Also ask the HOA for its leasing rules. Some limit rentals, set a minimum lease term or cap the number of rented units.

## The full due diligence checklist

Use this list during your inspection period.

**Building**
- [ ] General inspection booked on day 1
- [ ] Sewer, roof and HVAC checks where needed
- [ ] Lead-based paint test or written waiver for pre-1978 homes
- [ ] Repair quotes from 2 contractors for each large item

**Tenants**
- [ ] Rent roll, leases and 12 months of deposits match
- [ ] Tenant estoppel letters signed
- [ ] Deposit amounts confirmed and transfer planned at closing
- [ ] No prepaid rent or side deals left open

**Paperwork**
- [ ] Permits match bedrooms, baths and units
- [ ] Zoning allows the current use
- [ ] Rental license rules confirmed
- [ ] No open code violations
- [ ] Flood zone checked on FEMA

**Costs**
- [ ] Property tax at your price from the assessor
- [ ] Landlord insurance quote in writing
- [ ] HOA budget, reserve study, minutes and assessments read
- [ ] All numbers entered in your deal analysis

If the checks turn up repairs, ask for a price cut or a credit before the period ends. If you plan to hire a manager, add the fee to your numbers with our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

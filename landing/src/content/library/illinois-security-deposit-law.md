Illinois splits its state deposit rules across 2 short acts. The Security Deposit Return Act (765 ILCS 710) covers deductions and deadlines. The Security Deposit Interest Act (765 ILCS 715) covers interest for larger landlords. I read both on the Illinois General Assembly site on 2026-10-20. The latest change in the return act is Public Act 103-224, in force since January 1, 2024.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | No cap in state law | 765 ILCS 710 |
| Itemized statement of damage | Within 30 days after move-out or end of possession, whichever is later | 710/1(a) |
| Paid receipts after an estimate | Within 30 days after the statement | 710/1(a) |
| Full return if no statement | Within 45 days after move-out | 710/1(a) |
| Delivery | In person, postmarked mail, or e-mail to a verified address the tenant gave | 710/1(a) |
| Penalty for refusal or bad faith | 2 times the deposit due, plus costs and attorney fees | 710/1(c) |
| Interest, 25+ units | Required on deposits held more than 6 months | 715/1 |
| Interest payment | Within 30 days after each 12-month period, if $5 or more | 715/2 |
| Penalty for wilful non-payment of interest | Amount equal to the deposit, plus costs and fees | 715/2 |
| Sale of the property | Buyer and seller both liable for the deposit | 710/1.1 |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Illinois?

Illinois state law sets no maximum security deposit. Neither 765 ILCS 710 nor 765 ILCS 715 limits the amount.

City rules can add more. Chicago's Residential Landlord and Tenant Ordinance, Municipal Code chapter 5-12, has its own sections on deposits: 5-12-080 (security deposits), 5-12-081 (interest rate) and 5-12-082 (interest rate notification). I could read only the section list for this article, not the text. If you manage units in Chicago, read those sections in full before you collect a deposit.

## How many days does an Illinois landlord have to return a security deposit?

An Illinois landlord has 30 days to send an itemized statement if the landlord wants to keep any part of the deposit for damage. If the landlord sends no statement, the full deposit is due within 45 days after the tenant vacates (765 ILCS 710/1(a)).

The 30 days start at the later of 2 dates: the day the tenant vacates, or the day the tenant's right of possession ends. Worked example:

| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| Lease ends (right of possession ends) | May 31 |
| Tenant moves out early | May 20 |
| 30-day clock starts (later date) | May 31 |
| Last day for the itemized statement | June 30 |
| Last day for full return if no statement (45 days from move-out) | July 4 |

If the tenant gives no mailing or e-mail address, the landlord is not liable for damages or penalties caused by the missing address.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Illinois?

An Illinois landlord can deduct unpaid rent and the cost to repair or replace damage. The return act says the deposit secures "the payment of rent" or compensation "for damage to the leased premises". For damage, the statement rules in § 1 apply.

Three rules shape damage deductions:

1. **Own labor.** If the landlord does the repair, the landlord may include the reasonable cost of that labor.
2. **Costs set in the lease.** A written lease may name a dollar amount for cleaning, repair or replacement of a component. The landlord may withhold that amount. It must be for damage beyond normal wear and tear and be reasonable to restore the unit to its condition at the start.
3. **Lease reference.** When a deduction uses a lease amount, the statement must cite it and include a copy of that part of the lease.

Other deductions must meet the normal statement and receipt rules.

## Does an Illinois landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

An Illinois landlord must give an itemized statement of the damage and the estimated or actual cost of each item, with paid receipts or copies attached (765 ILCS 710/1(a)). Without it, the landlord may not keep any part of the deposit for property damage.

Two cases need extra steps:

- **Estimates.** If the statement shows estimated costs, send the paid receipts within 30 days after the statement.
- **No receipts through no fault of the landlord.** Send an itemized list of costs, any other evidence of cost, and a verified statement that says why the receipts are not available (710/1(b)).

Delivery: by hand, by postmarked mail to the last known address, or by e-mail to a verified e-mail address the tenant gave.

## Must an Illinois landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

An Illinois landlord with 25 or more units must pay interest. The Security Deposit Interest Act applies to residential property with 25 or more units in one building, or in a complex on contiguous parcels (765 ILCS 715/1).

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Which deposits | Held more than 6 months |
| Rate | The rate the largest Illinois commercial bank (by total assets) paid on minimum-deposit passbook savings on December 31 of the year before the lease began |
| When to pay | Within 30 days after each 12-month rental period, if the interest is $5 or more |
| How | Cash or a credit against rent |
| Exception | Not while the tenant is in default under the lease |
| At move-out | All unpaid interest, whatever the amount |
| Public housing | The act does not apply |

The state acts do not require a separate account. Chicago's ordinance may have its own rules. Check section 5-12-080.

When the property is sold or transferred, the buyer becomes liable for the deposit and statutory interest. The seller stays jointly liable (710/1.1). After a foreclosure sale, the holder or buyer who receives deposits must post a notice on each unit's main door within 21 days (710/1.2).

## What is the penalty for an Illinois landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

An Illinois landlord who refuses to send the itemized statement, or sends it in bad faith, and fails to return the deposit on time owes twice the deposit due. The court adds court costs and reasonable attorney fees (765 ILCS 710/1(c)).

For interest, a landlord who wilfully fails to pay owes an amount equal to the deposit, plus costs and fees (765 ILCS 715/2).

Worked example: a 30-unit building, a $1,600 deposit, no statement sent, deposit kept. If a court finds bad faith, the landlord owes $3,200 plus fees. If the landlord also wilfully skipped the interest, the court can add another $1,600.

## Illinois deposit checklist

- Record the move-out date and the end of possession.
- Send the itemized statement with receipts by day 30.
- Use e-mail only to a verified address the tenant gave.
- Cite and attach the lease clause for any preset charge.
- If you have 25+ units, pay interest every 12 months.
- In Chicago, check chapter 5-12 before you collect a deposit.

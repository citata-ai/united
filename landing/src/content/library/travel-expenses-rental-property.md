## Which trips count as rental business?

A rental trip needs a qualifying rental purpose, not just a stop at your property. IRS Publication 527 allows ordinary and necessary travel away from home for managing, conserving or maintaining a rental. Collecting rental income can also qualify.

Separate local driving from travel away from your tax home. IRS Publication 463 explains tax home and travel rules. An overnight stay does not by itself establish a deductible business trip. Your purpose and the applicable rules still matter.

For local transport, IRS Publication 527 gives a specific warning. Travel between home and a rental generally counts as nondeductible commuting. An exception may apply if your home is your principal place of business. Ask your tax professional whether your facts meet that test.

Use a trip review before recording a deduction:

| Trip purpose | What to record | Tax question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meet a repair contractor | Work order and meeting notes | Rental expense or improvement cost? |
| Inspect a managed rental | Inspection report and reason for travel | Ordinary and necessary business visit? |
| Collect rent | Payment record and trip purpose | Does the route include commuting? |
| Visit family and check the house | Personal itinerary and rental activity | Which costs, if any, qualify? |
| Direct a major renovation | Project scope and travel records | Must costs be capitalized? |

IRS Publication 527 says travel mainly to improve property is not deductible as a current travel expense. Send renovation travel to your preparer with the project records. Do not place it automatically in the ordinary travel account.

## How does the standard mileage rate work?

The standard mileage method uses eligible business miles and the rate for that tax year. The IRS announced a business rate of 72.5 cents per mile for 2026. Do not apply that rate to a different year's driving.

Here is an illustrative calculation. Assume your tax professional confirms that 200 logged miles qualify for the standard mileage method in 2026. The mileage amount is 200 × $0.725 = $145. The miles are assumed; the rate comes from the IRS announcement.

That calculation does not decide whether the trip qualifies. Review business purpose and commuting rules first. Record personal detours separately, even when they occur during a work trip.

IRS Publication 463 explains the standard mileage and actual expense methods. Method choice has eligibility rules, including rules linked to prior vehicle use and depreciation. Do not switch methods only because the current result looks larger.

Under the mileage method, do not also deduct the same vehicle's gas, repairs and depreciation as separate operating costs. Publication 463 explains what the rate replaces and which separate expenses may remain available. Ask your preparer about business parking and tolls.

Keep a vehicle record across the full year. A list of rental addresses does not show how you used the car. Record total use as well as the trips you believe qualify. Give the preparer enough information to review the whole claim.

## What records does the IRS want?

Keep a trip log and evidence that explains the expense when you make it. IRS Publication 463 calls for adequate records or other sufficient evidence. It warns against relying on approximate or estimated amounts.

A useful rental log includes the date, destination, miles and business reason. Attach the related inspection, invoice or meeting note. For travel expenses, keep receipts and the trip itinerary. Record personal activities while the details are clear.

Write a purpose that another person can understand. A note that says property visit tells little. A note that says met plumber to inspect recurring kitchen leak explains the work. Link that note to the repair file.

For a mixed trip, save the full schedule. Identify rental work, personal days and shared costs. IRS Publication 527 requires allocation between rental and nonrental activities. Publication 463 gives further rules for mixed travel. Do not assume a rental stop converts the whole flight or hotel stay into a deduction.

Avoid duplicate claims when your manager pays a cost from your rental account. Match reimbursements to receipts and statements. Mark which person paid and whether you received money back. A receipt can appear in several files while supporting only one actual expense.

Ask your tax professional how long to retain the records for your situation. Keep a backed-up copy with the tax return and supporting schedule. The goal is a claim you can explain from documents, not a year-end guess.

## Can you deduct a trip to look at a new rental?

Do not treat a purchase-search trip as routine maintenance of a rental you already own. The purpose differs, so the tax treatment needs a separate review. Keep acquisition research in its own folder from the start.

IRS Publication 527 explains when pre-rental expenses become deductible for property made available for rent. That guidance does not establish that every search trip is currently deductible. A trip to evaluate a possible purchase needs facts about your activity and the transaction.

Give your preparer the property addresses, trip dates and work performed. State whether you bought a property, abandoned a purchase or continued an existing business. Keep offers, inspection notes and adviser invoices. Let the classification follow the evidence.

If you combine a search trip with existing rental work, divide the activities in your log. Do not assign all costs to the existing rental because it provides an easy expense category. Explain the split and ask your preparer to review it.

Before your next trip, compare travel with local service options. A manager's inspection report may answer the same question without your presence. Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to compare management costs with your own cash spending. Then decide which work actually requires travel.

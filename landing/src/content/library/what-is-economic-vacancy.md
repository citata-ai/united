## What is economic vacancy?

Economic vacancy is the share of a property's potential rent that it did not collect. It answers the owner's real question: of all the rent this building could earn, how much did it miss?

Physical vacancy is simpler. It counts empty units as a share of all units. A 20-unit building with 1 empty unit has 5% physical vacancy. But that same building can miss 12% of its potential rent. The gap between the 2 numbers is where income leaks out.

The national number most people quote is physical. The U.S. Census Bureau rental vacancy rate was 7.3% in Q2 2026, up from 7.0% in Q2 2025 (FRED series RRVRUSQ156N, not seasonally adjusted). That figure counts vacant units for rent. It does not count a tenant who lives in a unit and pays nothing.

## How do you calculate economic vacancy?

You calculate economic vacancy by dividing lost rent by gross potential rent. Gross potential rent (GPR) is the rent you would collect if every unit was leased at market rent and every tenant paid in full.

**Economic vacancy = (gross potential rent − rent collected) ÷ gross potential rent**

A worked example for one month in a 20-unit building. Market rent is $1,500 per unit.

| Line | How | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Gross potential rent | 20 units × $1,500 | $30,000 |
| Empty units (physical vacancy) | 1 unit × $1,500 | − $1,500 |
| Concessions | 2 new leases with half a month free | − $1,500 |
| Loss to lease | 4 units leased at $1,400 vs $1,500 market | − $400 |
| Model or staff unit | 1 unit, no rent | − $1,500 |
| Bad debt and unpaid rent | 1 tenant paid nothing this month | − $1,500 |
| Rent collected | | $23,600 |

Now the 2 vacancy numbers:

- **Physical vacancy:** 1 empty unit ÷ 20 units = 5%.
- **Economic vacancy:** ($30,000 − $23,600) ÷ $30,000 = $6,400 ÷ $30,000 = 21.3%.

The building is 95% full by units but collected 78.7% of its potential rent. That is a big example on purpose. Most months, only some of these lines will hit.

## Why can a full building still lose rent?

A full building can still lose rent because economic vacancy has 5 causes, and only 1 of them is an empty unit. The other 4 happen with a tenant in place, so they never show in a physical vacancy report.

| Cause | What it is | How to spot it |
|---|---|---|
| Physical vacancy | Empty units | Occupancy report |
| Concessions | Free rent or discounts to sign a lease | Lease terms and ledger credits |
| Loss to lease | Leases below current market rent | Rent roll vs market survey |
| Non-revenue units | Models, staff units, units down for repair | Unit status list |
| Delinquency and bad debt | Rent billed but not paid, then written off | Aged receivables report |

Buildium's dictionary entry on gross effective income makes the same split from the other side. It defines income as "Potential Rent – Vacancy Loss – Concessions – Other Adjustments", and lists unpaid rent among the other adjustments.

## What causes economic vacancy (concessions, bad debt, delinquency)?

Economic vacancy comes from 3 big, controllable causes beyond empty units: concessions, delinquency and bad debt, and loss to lease. Each one has a fix that a property manager can own.

1. **Cut concessions with better timing.** Concessions grow when units come up in a slow season. Stagger lease end dates so fewer units turn in winter.
2. **Collect faster.** Delinquency is rent billed but not yet paid. Bad debt is rent you give up on. Send reminders before the due date, apply late fees the same way for each tenant, and follow the lease and local law on notices.
3. **Close the loss to lease at renewal.** Check market rent before each renewal. Raise rent toward market where the lease and local law allow.
4. **Shorten turns.** Every day a unit is down for a make-ready is a day of lost rent. Book vendors before move-out.
5. **Review non-revenue units each year.** A model unit that made sense in lease-up may not be needed now.

A worked fix. In the example above, the tenant who paid nothing costs $1,500 a month. If faster collection gets that tenant to pay in full, economic vacancy drops from 21.3% to ($6,400 − $1,500) ÷ $30,000 = 16.3%. One tenant moves the number by 5 points in a 20-unit building.

Watch for: a manager who reports only occupancy. What to do: ask for collections as a percent of potential rent each month.

## Which vacancy number should an owner report watch?

An owner should watch economic vacancy, or its mirror, collections as a percent of gross potential rent. Physical vacancy tells you how many units are full. Economic vacancy tells you how much money came in.

Ask for both on each monthly owner report:

| Metric | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Physical occupancy | Occupied units ÷ total units | 19 ÷ 20 = 95% |
| Physical vacancy | Empty units ÷ total units | 1 ÷ 20 = 5% |
| Economic occupancy | Rent collected ÷ gross potential rent | $23,600 ÷ $30,000 = 78.7% |
| Economic vacancy | 1 − economic occupancy | 21.3% |

Track the trend over 12 months, not one month. A single bad month can come from one large concession. A rising trend points to a real problem in leasing or collections.

Economic vacancy also feeds the owner's income math. Gross potential rent minus vacancy and credit loss, plus other income, gives effective gross income. Effective gross income minus operating expenses gives net operating income.

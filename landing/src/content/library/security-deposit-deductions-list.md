## Which charges can a landlord take from a security deposit?

A landlord can take 3 kinds of charges from a deposit: unpaid rent, tenant damage beyond ordinary wear and tear, and other charges the lease or statute allows. Virginia's 55.1-1226 lists them plainly. The deposit may be applied "solely" to:

1. Accrued rent, including reasonable late charges in the rental agreement.
2. Damages from the tenant's breach of duty, "less reasonable wear and tear."
3. Other damages or charges as provided in the rental agreement.
4. Actual damages for breach of the rental agreement.

Arizona's 33-1321(D) is similar: rent, charges in the signed lease, and damages from the tenant's breach of duty, subject to the landlord's duty to mitigate. Oregon's 90.300 allows only "the amount reasonably necessary" to cover defaults such as unpaid rent and to repair tenant damage, "not including ordinary wear and tear."

## Deductions checklist: can and cannot

Use this list before you write the itemized notice.

| Charge | Usually can deduct? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Unpaid rent | Yes | Virginia includes reasonable late charges in the lease |
| Late fees in the lease | Yes, in Virginia | Must be in the lease and within any state cap |
| Repair of tenant damage | Yes | Less ordinary wear and tear |
| Cleaning beyond normal use | Often | Oregon sets rules for carpet cleaning |
| Carpet replacement | Partly | Charge for remaining useful life only |
| Unpaid tenant utility bills | In Virginia, with notice | Landlord must give written notice first |
| Lost or unreturned keys | Often, if the lease says so | Charge the real cost |
| Routine re-key between tenants | Usually no | A normal cost of turnover |
| Normal wear and tear | No | Faded paint, light carpet wear, small nail holes |
| Upgrades | No | New paint color you chose, better carpet |
| Fees not in the lease or the law | No | Arizona: any fee not marked nonrefundable in writing is refundable |
| Fee or deposit for an assistance animal | No | HUD and DOJ joint statement; you may still charge for real damage |

## Can I deduct unpaid rent?

Yes. In each state above, unpaid rent is an allowed deduction. Virginia adds that a tenant who leaves before the lease ends, or without proper notice, is liable for actual damages. The landlord must still send the deposit notice within 45 days, but may hold the balance against what the tenant owes.

Watch for: Virginia also says a tenant has no right to an immediate credit of the deposit against unpaid rent when the tenancy ends. Apply it through the itemized notice, on time.

## Can I deduct cleaning?

You can deduct for cleaning beyond normal use in many states. Normal use leaves some dust and wear. Heavy grease, trash left behind, and pet waste go beyond it.

Oregon sets the clearest rules we read:

- **Labor:** any labor charge for cleaning or repairs "must be based on a reasonable hourly rate." The landlord may charge a reasonable rate for their own work.
- **Carpet cleaning:** allowed only if it uses a carpet cleaning machine, the carpet was cleaned or replaced before move-in, and the written lease allows the charge.
- **Repairs not done:** a landlord need not make the repair to claim its cost.

## Can I charge for re-keying?

It depends on the cause and the lease. If the tenant lost keys, kept keys or changed locks, the cost is a fair charge when your lease names it. If you re-key after every tenant as a safety routine, it is part of your turnover cost, like a fresh coat of paint after a long stay.

Write the rule in the lease: "Tenant pays the cost to re-key if any key, fob or remote listed on the move-in report is not returned."

## What needs a receipt?

Keep proof for every deduction: an invoice, a receipt or a written quote. Even where a statute does not say "receipt," you must show the deduction is real and reasonable.

State rules on the itemized notice:

| State | Deadline | Key rule | Penalty if missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arizona | 14 days, excluding weekends and holidays, after the tenancy ends, possession is delivered and the tenant asks | Itemized list by first class mail | Twice the amount wrongfully withheld |
| Florida | 15 days to return with no claim; 30 days to send a claim notice by certified mail or allowed email | Tenant has 15 days to object | Landlord forfeits the right to claim on the deposit |
| Oregon | 31 days after tenancy ends and possession is delivered | Written accounting stating the basis of each claim | Twice the amount withheld without accounting, or in bad faith |
| Virginia | 45 days | Itemized written notice; 15 more days to itemize if damage exceeds the deposit and needs a contractor, with notice | Return of deposit, damages and attorney fees, if willful |

## How do I write the itemized notice?

Write one line per deduction, with the item, the reason, the cost and the proof. Then subtract from the deposit and state the balance.

**Example, $1,800 deposit:**

| Item | Reason | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Unpaid rent, last half month | Rent due, not paid | $900 |
| Bedroom carpet | Pet urine; carpet 3 of 5 years old, 40% life left of $1,500 | $600 |
| Keys | 2 keys not returned, re-key per lease | $95 |
| **Total deductions** | | **$1,595** |
| **Refund due to tenant** | $1,800 − $1,595 | **$205** |

Attach the photos and invoices. Send it by the method your state requires. To learn how move-out work fits into full management, see [property management](/property-management).

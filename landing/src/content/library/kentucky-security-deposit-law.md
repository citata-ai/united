Kentucky's deposit law rests on lists, not deadlines. I read KRS 383.580 on the Kentucky General Assembly site on 2026-10-19. It has no cap and no refund deadline in days. Instead, it rests on a separate account and 2 signed damage lists.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | None set | KRS 383.580 |
| Account | Separate, used only for deposits, at a regulated bank | KRS 383.580(1) |
| Tell the tenant | Location and number of the account | KRS 383.580(1) |
| Move-in damage list | Before the tenant pays; both sign | KRS 383.580(2) |
| Move-out damage list | Landlord inspects and lists; both sign | KRS 383.580(3) |
| Skip account and lists | Landlord may keep nothing | KRS 383.580(4) |
| Tenant leaves owing last month's rent | Apply deposit after 30 days if no demand | KRS 383.580(6) |
| Tenant leaves owing nothing | Notify; keep if no reply in 60 days | KRS 383.580(7) |
| Where it applies | URLTA areas | KRS 383.500 |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Kentucky?

Kentucky sets no maximum security deposit. KRS 383.580 has no cap, and we found no other Kentucky statute with one.

Price the deposit to the risk, and apply it the same way to every applicant. Fair Housing rules still apply to how you set and use deposit amounts.

## Does the Kentucky deposit law apply to your unit?

KRS 383.580 is part of the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, KRS 383.505 to 383.715. KRS 383.500 lets cities, counties and urban-county governments adopt those sections "in their entirety and without amendment."

So the account and list rules bind you where the URLTA is in force. Ask the county clerk or city code office if your area adopted it. You can choose to follow KRS 383.580 everywhere. The lists make deductions easier to prove.

## How many days does a Kentucky landlord have to return a security deposit?

KRS 383.580 sets no fixed refund deadline in days. It sets 2 waiting periods instead:

- **Tenant leaves owing last month's rent.** If the tenant does not pay the last month and does not demand the deposit, the landlord may, "after thirty (30) days, remove the deposit from the account" and apply it to the debt (KRS 383.580(6)).
- **Tenant leaves owing nothing.** The landlord sends notice of the refund amount to the last known address. If the tenant does not reply within 60 days, the landlord may keep it "free from any claim of the tenant" (KRS 383.580(7)).

Worked timeline for a tenant who owes nothing:

| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| Tenant moves out | March 31 |
| Landlord mails notice of $1,000 refund | April 5 |
| No reply by | June 4 (60 days) |
| Landlord may keep the deposit after | June 4 |

Pay promptly when the tenant replies. A fast refund keeps a dispute from starting.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Kentucky?

A Kentucky landlord may charge the deposit for damage on the move-out list and for unpaid rent.

The move-out list must show "any damage to the unit which is the basis for any charge against the security deposit and the estimated dollar cost of repairing such damage" (KRS 383.580(3)). Damage that was on the move-in list existed before the tenant, so do not charge it again.

Unpaid last month's rent may come out of the deposit after 30 days if the tenant does not demand the deposit (KRS 383.580(6)).

## Does a Kentucky landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes, at both ends of the tenancy.

**Move-in list (KRS 383.580(2)).** Before the tenant pays a deposit, give "a comprehensive listing of any then-existing damage" and the estimated repair cost. The tenant may inspect first. Both sign. If the tenant disagrees, the tenant writes and signs a statement of the items in dispute.

**Move-out list (KRS 383.580(3)).** At the end, the landlord inspects and lists all damage that is the basis for a charge, with estimated costs. The tenant may inspect. Both sign, or the tenant signs a written dissent.

The signatures matter. They are "conclusive evidence of the accuracy" of the list, except for hidden defects at move-in. A tenant who will not sign and gives no written dissent may not recover damages in court (KRS 383.580(5)).

A simple list format:

| Room | Item | Condition | Estimated repair cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kitchen | Counter | Burn mark near stove | $180 |
| Bedroom 2 | Wall | 3 holes, 1 inch | $120 |
| Bath | Mirror | Cracked | $60 |

## Must a Kentucky landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

Kentucky requires a separate account but no interest. KRS 383.580(1) says landlords who take deposits before occupancy must deposit them "in an account used only for that purpose." The bank must be regulated by Kentucky or a U.S. agency. Prospective tenants "shall be informed of the location of the separate account and the account number."

KRS 383.580 says nothing about interest. We found no interest rule in the section.

## What is the penalty for a Kentucky landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

The main penalty is loss of the whole deposit. Under KRS 383.580(4), "no landlord shall be entitled to retain any portion of a security deposit" if the deposit was not in a separate account and the move-in and move-out lists were not given.

A tenant who disputes the move-out list may sue in District Court, but only on the items the tenant dissented from in writing (KRS 383.580(5)).

If a landlord unlawfully locks out a tenant and the lease ends, KRS 383.655 requires the return of all prepaid rent, and the tenant may recover up to 3 months' rent plus attorney fees.

## Kentucky deposit checklist

- Open a separate deposit-only account at a regulated bank.
- Tell each applicant the bank and the account number in writing.
- Give a signed move-in damage list before you take the deposit.
- Inspect at move-out and sign a damage list with costs.
- Mail refund notice to the last known address; wait 60 days for a reply.
- Keep both lists and any written dissents.

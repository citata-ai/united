New Mexico puts its deposit rules in one section: NMSA 47-8-18. The legislature last changed it in 1989. We read the current text on NMOneSource, the site of the New Mexico Compilation Commission, on 2026-10-19. The 2025 changes to the Act (Laws 2025, ch. 122) did not touch this section.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit cap, lease under 1 year | 1 month's rent | NMSA 47-8-18(A)(2) |
| Deposit cap, annual lease | No fixed cap; must be "reasonable" | NMSA 47-8-18(A) |
| Interest | Annual, if annual lease and deposit above 1 month's rent | NMSA 47-8-18(A)(1) |
| Last month's prepaid rent | Not counted as the deposit | NMSA 47-8-18(B) |
| Normal wear and tear | No deduction | NMSA 47-8-18(C) |
| Itemized list and balance | Within 30 days of lease end or move-out, whichever is later | NMSA 47-8-18(C) |
| Miss the 30 days | Lose the deposit claim, pay costs and attorney fees | NMSA 47-8-18(D) |
| Bad-faith retention | $250 civil penalty | NMSA 47-8-18(E) |
| Rent paid used for deposit | Not allowed without written agreement | NMSA 47-8-15(G) |
| Separate account | No rule in the statute | NMSA 47-8-18 |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in New Mexico?

The New Mexico deposit cap depends on the lease term. For a rental agreement of less than 1 year, NMSA 47-8-18(A)(2) bars a deposit above 1 month's rent. That covers month-to-month tenancies and a 6-month lease.

For an annual rental agreement, the statute sets no fixed number. NMSA 47-8-18(A) allows a "reasonable" deposit. If the deposit is above 1 month's rent, you owe interest each year (see below).

The courts have read "reasonable" with limits. In Hedicke v. Gunville (2003-NMCA-032), tenants paid $50,000. The Court of Appeals said this was not a security deposit. The court noted most deposits are 1 month's rent, or somewhat more, "but not by a multiple of 32."

Prepaid last month's rent is a separate item. NMSA 47-8-18(B) says the section does not include last month's prepaid rent that the lease requires.

## How many days does a New Mexico landlord have to return a security deposit?

A New Mexico landlord has 30 days. NMSA 47-8-18(C) starts the clock on the later of 2 dates: the end of the rental agreement or the tenant's departure. Within 30 days, you send an itemized written list of deductions and the balance, if any.

Mail counts. The statute says you comply by mailing the statement and any payment to the tenant's last known address.

A worked example:

- Lease ends: June 30.
- Tenant moves out: July 3.
- Later date: July 3.
- Deadline: August 2 (30 days later).

What to do:

1. **Get a forwarding address** at move-out, in writing.
2. **Inspect within days,** with dated photos.
3. **Mail the list** with tracking, and keep a copy.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in New Mexico?

A New Mexico landlord can deduct unpaid rent and damages caused by the tenant's breach. NMSA 47-8-18(C) lists rent, damages from noncompliance with the rental agreement, and damages from breach of the tenant duties in NMSA 47-8-22. The same subsection names nonpayment of rent or utilities, repair work and other legitimate damages.

You may not deduct for normal wear and tear. NMSA 47-8-18(C) says so in plain words.

| Item | Deduct? | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Unpaid rent | Yes | NMSA 47-8-18(C) |
| Unpaid utilities | Yes | NMSA 47-8-18(C) |
| Hole in a wall | Yes, as damage | NMSA 47-8-18(C) |
| Faded paint after years of use | No, normal wear and tear | NMSA 47-8-18(C) |

The deposit does not cap your claim. In Stodgell v. Weissman (2025-NMCA-003), the landlord sent a full itemized list in time. The Court of Appeals let her also seek more damages in court. That right exists only if you meet the 30-day rule.

## Does a New Mexico landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes, a New Mexico landlord must give an itemized list when actual cause exists to keep any part of the deposit. NMSA 47-8-18(C) requires an itemized written list of the deductions and the balance.

Make each line clear:

- [ ] the item or the damage;
- [ ] the cost, with an invoice or a quote;
- [ ] the total deposit, the total deductions and the balance;
- [ ] the date you mailed it, and the address.

One narrow exception exists in case law. In Bruce v. Attaway (1996-NMSC-030), the tenant left without the 30-day notice required by NMSA 47-8-37(B). The Supreme Court let the landlord apply the deposit to the unpaid rent without a written list. Do not count on this. Send the list every time.

## Must a New Mexico landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

A New Mexico landlord must pay interest only in one case. Under NMSA 47-8-18(A)(1), the lease must be annual and the deposit must exceed 1 month's rent. Then you pay the tenant interest each year.

The statute ties the rate to the passbook interest that the federal home loan bank board permits savings and loan associations in New Mexico. It gives no number. Ask a local attorney what rate to use today.

NMSA 47-8-18 has no rule on a separate or trust account. Still, keep clean records. NMSA 47-8-15(G) says rent paid may not go to deposits or damages unless both of you agree in writing.

## What is the penalty for a New Mexico landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

A New Mexico landlord who misses the 30-day list loses 4 rights under NMSA 47-8-18(D):

1. **The right to withhold** any part of the deposit.
2. **The right to counterclaim** in a suit to recover the deposit.
3. **Protection from fees.** You owe court costs and reasonable attorney fees.
4. **The right to sue separately** for damage to the unit.

Bad faith adds more. NMSA 47-8-18(E) sets a $250 civil penalty, payable to the tenant. In Garcia v. Thong (1995-NMSC-030), a landlord gave no itemized list. She lost the right to keep any part of the deposit, and the tenant got attorney fees.

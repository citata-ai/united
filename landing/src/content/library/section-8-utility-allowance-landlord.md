## What is gross rent?

Gross rent is your rent to owner plus the utility allowance. 24 CFR 982.4 defines it as "the sum of the rent to owner plus any utility allowance." The utility allowance covers only utilities the tenant pays. If you pay all utilities, the allowance is $0 and gross rent equals your rent.

Gross rent matters because the PHA uses it in every HAP calculation. Under 24 CFR 982.505(b), the HAP is the lower of:

- the payment standard minus the family's Total Tenant Payment (TTP), or
- the gross rent minus the TTP.

The payment standard comes from HUD's Fair Market Rent, and FMRs are "estimates of rent plus the cost of utilities, except telephone" (24 CFR 888.113(a)). So the cap was built to cover both rent and utilities.

## Should I include utilities in the rent?

Include a utility in the rent when your real cost for it is lower than the PHA's allowance for it. Leave it to the tenant when your real cost is higher. The test is simple because the payment standard caps rent and allowance together.

Two more limits apply either way. Your rent must be reasonable compared with similar unassisted units, and the reasonable rent test counts the utilities you supply (24 CFR 982.507(b)). At move-in, if gross rent is above the payment standard, the family share may not exceed 40% of adjusted monthly income (24 CFR 982.508).

## Worked example: owner-paid vs tenant-paid utilities

All numbers below are made up. The formulas are the federal ones.

**Inputs:** payment standard $1,600 for 2 bedrooms. Family TTP $500. The PHA's allowance for tenant-paid electric, gas and water is $200 a month. Your actual bill for those utilities would be $160 a month.

| Line | Owner pays utilities | Tenant pays utilities |
|---|---|---|
| Utility allowance | $0 | $200 |
| Highest rent with gross rent at the payment standard | $1,600 | $1,400 |
| Gross rent | $1,600 | $1,600 |
| HAP (gross rent − TTP) | $1,100 | $1,100 |
| Tenant rent to owner | $500 | $300 |
| Your utility bills | −$160 | $0 |
| Your net each month | $1,440 | $1,400 |
| Your net each year | $17,280 | $16,800 |

Here, including utilities pays $40 more a month, or $480 a year, because your $160 cost is under the $200 allowance. If your bill were $240, the result would flip: $1,360 against $1,400. The family pays the same total in both cases.

Watch for: when you pay utilities, a cold winter or a leak raises your cost, not the tenant's. Use 12 months of bills, not 1.

## How does the PHA set its utility allowance schedule?

The PHA sets the schedule from typical local costs. Under 24 CFR 982.517(b), the area-wide schedule must reflect "the typical cost of utilities and services paid by energy-conservative households that occupy housing of similar size and type in the same locality," using current rates.

What the schedule must include:

- **Categories:** space heating, air conditioning, cooking, water heating, water, sewer, trash, other electric, refrigerator, range, surcharges and other services (982.517(b)(1)(ii)).
- **Air conditioning:** an allowance when most units in the market have central air or wiring for window units (982.517(b)(1)(iii)).
- **No extras:** no allowance for cable or satellite TV (982.517(b)(1)(iv)). Telephone is also left out (982.517(a)(1)).
- **Unit size:** the allowance for the smaller of the unit leased or the family's voucher size (982.517(d)), unless a reasonable accommodation applies.

Two options can help you. A PHA may keep a separate energy-efficient schedule for buildings that meet LEED or Energy Star standards (982.517(b)(2)(ii)). And if you bill utilities as a flat fee, the PHA may base the allowance on that fee, but only if it is no greater than the schedule amount (982.517(b)(2)(iii)).

## What happens when the utility allowance changes?

When the allowance changes, gross rent changes, and so can the split between the PHA and the family. Under 24 CFR 982.517(c), the PHA must review its schedule each year. It must revise a category when the utility rate has changed 10% or more since the last revision.

Effects for a tenant-paid unit, with rent and payment standard the same:

| Change | Gross rent | Who feels it |
|---|---|---|
| Allowance goes up, gross rent still at or under the payment standard | Up | PHA pays more HAP; the family's rent to you drops |
| Allowance goes up, gross rent goes over the payment standard | Up | Family pays the amount above the payment standard |
| Allowance goes down | Down | PHA pays less HAP; the family's rent to you rises |

Your rent to owner does not change in any row. Your PHA's Administrative Plan says when a new schedule applies to current families, so ask for that page.

## What is a utility reimbursement?

A utility reimbursement happens when the HAP is larger than your rent. 24 CFR 982.514 says the PHA pays you "the lesser of the housing assistance payment or the rent to owner." The rest goes to the family or straight to the utility company.

Example (made up): TTP $50, rent to owner $1,400, allowance $200. Gross rent is $1,600 and HAP is $1,550. The PHA pays you $1,400 and pays $150 to the family or its utility. You still get your full rent, all from the PHA.

## Can I change who pays utilities?

You cannot change who pays utilities with a simple lease edit. 24 CFR 982.308(g)(2)(i) says that if lease terms on utility or appliance duties change, assistance continues only after the PHA approves a new tenancy and signs a new HAP contract. The lease must also state who supplies each utility and appliance (982.308(d)(5)).

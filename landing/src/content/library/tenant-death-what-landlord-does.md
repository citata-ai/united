## Does the lease end at death?

A tenant's death does not create a single national lease-end rule. Check the state statute, lease and occupancy facts. A surviving tenant may still hold rights to the home. Do not treat an occupied unit as vacant because a leaseholder died.

Texas Property Code 92.0162 provides a termination route for the estate representative of a deceased sole occupant. It requires written notice, removal of property under the cited procedure and an inventory if required. Termination takes effect on the later of the 30th day after notice or completion of the conditions. Earlier obligations can remain. Do not extend that rule to a home with surviving occupants.

Open a tenancy file when you receive reliable notice. Record who reported the death, what you verified and whether anyone still lives there. Review the lease names before contacting a turnover vendor. Keep any unverified detail out of resident-wide messages.

If the circumstances suggest an immediate emergency, contact emergency services. Follow the responding authority's directions about access. Do not let a leasing schedule drive the first response. Treat the home and the people involved with care.

## Who can enter and remove belongings?

Access and removal depend on verified authority, not on which family member arrives first. Ask counsel what documents establish that authority locally. Keep a copy of the relevant record and confirm the person's identity before releasing keys.

Washington RCW 59.18.590 allows a sole occupant to designate a person through a separate written authorization. The statute describes powers over access, belongings and refunds. That authority ends when a qualifying estate representative or successor becomes known under the section. Check the actual designation and current authority before relying on it.

Texas Property Code 92.014 also addresses a tenant-designated contact and handling a deceased sole occupant's property. Its conditions matter. Do not treat a normal emergency contact field as an unrestricted right to empty the home.

Use an access log for every approved visit. Record the visitor, purpose, time and staff member present where applicable. Ask for a signed inventory when appropriate under the local process. Keep photographs focused on the property and items needed for the record. Avoid sending private images to unrelated relatives.

## What happens to the deposit?

The deposit needs a lawful accounting and payment to the person entitled to receive it. Do not send a refund to the first person who offers a mailing address. Confirm the payee and authority separately.

Build a ledger that shows rent received, supported charges and proposed deductions. Keep the lease end separate from the date staff first heard about the death. A late report can create confusion about prepaid rent and possession. Ask counsel to resolve that issue before you finalize the account.

Separate estate property from owner property. An appliance may belong to the tenant even if it was present throughout the tenancy. Check the inventory, lease and approval records before removing or selling anything. Do not treat belongings as payment for unpaid rent.

Texas Property Code 92.014 addresses deposit refunds less lawful deductions in its sole-occupant procedure. That state example does not establish a national deposit deadline. Determine the local accounting trigger, allowable charges and refund recipient before you close the file.

Give the authorized person an itemized statement and a contact for questions. Keep delivery evidence. If authority remains disputed, ask counsel how to hold or pay the funds lawfully.

## How do I deal with the estate?

Use a named staff contact and a clear document list for the estate. A grieving family should not need to repeat the same account to every employee. Keep communications calm, factual and limited to tenancy matters.

| Stage | Record to create | Decision to confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Initial report | Verification and occupancy note | Is there an emergency or surviving occupant? |
| Authority check | Identity and authority record | Who can receive information and act? |
| Access | Visit and key log | Who may enter, and on what terms? |
| Removal | Inventory and possession record | Which belongings leave and with whom? |
| Closeout | Rent and deposit statement | What remains owed, and who receives funds? |

Ask the authorized person for a contact address and preferred channel. Confirm agreements in writing after calls. Keep the scope narrow. You need the facts required for the tenancy, not private medical details or family history.

Do not demand personal payment from a relative simply because they contacted you. Keep any debt request tied to the correct legal party and verified obligations. If family members disagree about access or belongings, pause nonessential releases and obtain legal guidance. Do not choose between them based on convenience.

## When can I begin the turnover?

Begin turnover only after you establish lawful possession and complete the required property process. A returned key may not settle every issue. Confirm whether all keys, access cards and possessions have been addressed under the agreement and local law.

Walk through the home with the authorized person when practical. Document condition and remaining items. Record the date you receive possession. Keep that record separate from any later contractor visit so the timeline stays clear.

Protect documents found during cleaning. Do not place financial records or identity papers in an open trash pile. Ask the authorized person how to handle them within the lawful process. Give vendors a narrow work order and no unnecessary details about the death.

Review the incident after closeout. Check whether your emergency contact form matches the state procedure. Confirm that staff know the difference between an emergency contact and an authorized estate contact. Update the operating checklist through counsel before the next tenancy.

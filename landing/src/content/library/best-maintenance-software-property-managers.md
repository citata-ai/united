## When is the PMS work-order module enough?

A property management system's work-order module is enough when your staff can reliably finish the repair process. Buildium, for example, lists work orders, maintenance projects and billing in its plan comparison. A separate subscription should solve a documented gap.

Look at recent unfinished requests. Identify where each stopped. Missing renter details, an unavailable vendor and an unapproved estimate are different problems. Better software does not create contractor capacity by itself.

Write the process from request to invoice. Assign responsibility for intake, urgency review, access, approval and closeout. If no person owns a step, fix that before changing tools.

Keep the existing module when it provides a clear queue and complete records. Add a specialist when the workflow, scheduling or coordination need exceeds what your current setup can support.

## How do Property Meld, Latchel and Lula compare?

Property Meld emphasizes maintenance software, while Latchel and Lula describe services that perform more of the coordination. The difference matters because a software license does not necessarily supply someone to act on every request.

Property Meld's pricing page lists scheduling, communication, approvals and maintenance records. Latchel describes an AI front office that troubleshoots requests, dispatches vendors and keeps a human support path. Lula describes residential maintenance coordination through its contractor network.

Ask each vendor to mark responsibility on your process map. Who contacts the renter? Who confirms the appointment? Who approves extra work? Who pursues a missing invoice?

Test the same missed-visit case in every finalist. Choose based on the next action and its owner. A complete-looking work order is not useful when everyone assumes another person is handling it.

## Which tools offer around-the-clock coordination?

Latchel explicitly describes around-the-clock maintenance intake and troubleshooting. Property Meld offers MAX On-Call separately from its main software plan. Lula lists after-hours services, but you should confirm the exact local service and escalation scope.

Distinguish answering from resolution. A system may capture a message immediately without supplying an available contractor. Ask what happens when the preferred vendor declines or the staff contact does not answer.

Give the vendor your approved emergency rules. Use a simulated request to test escalation. Check the notifications, record of attempts and fallback contact. Do not use a live emergency as a pilot.

Ask who can change the call tree and spending authority. Confirm whether emergency intake, human escalation and dispatch all appear in your quote. Staff should know the service boundary before the first after-hours call.

## How do vendor portals work?

A vendor portal should keep the assignment, access notes, estimate, status and invoice connected. The vendors describe different interfaces and communication methods, so test the actual contractor experience rather than assuming every contractor will use another app.

Invite a preferred vendor to the pilot. Ask them to accept a job, propose a time and upload an estimate. Then change the scope and request approval.

Keep access instructions limited to the people who need them. Ask when the vendor loses access after the work closes. Check whether sensitive renter details appear in general messages.

Confirm how approved invoices reach the accounting system. Test a repeated upload and a rejected bill. Your team should not pay twice because the same invoice arrives by email and through the portal. Choose a clear source for payment status.

## What does each cost per door?

Property Meld publishes a per-unit rate, while the full cost of Latchel and local repair services needs a quote. Property Meld lists Ops at $2.00 per unit monthly with a $200 monthly minimum. It lists MAX On-Call at an additional $1.50 per unit.

Lula says its coordination service has no monthly, annual or setup fee. That does not make repairs free. Ask for the work price, approval limit and treatment of materials or extra scope.

For every option, separate subscriptions from completed-job costs. Add any onboarding, phone, integration and after-hours charges. Compare similar repair demand, not a quiet month against a busy one.

Keep business fees separate from charges passed to owners or renters. Do not assume your software or service contract grants authority to pass a fee on.

## How should you run a maintenance pilot?

Run a contained pilot with ordinary requests and simulated exceptions. Choose properties that reflect your actual vendor and access needs.

- **Intake:** capture the problem, location and supporting photos.
- **Urgency:** route a simulated emergency to the correct contact.
- **Approval:** stop work when the estimate exceeds authority.
- **Scheduling:** record a missed visit and the replacement plan.
- **Completion:** obtain clear evidence of finished work.
- **Billing:** match the invoice to the approved scope.
- **Records:** find the full history from the property record.

Compare staff effort and unresolved work before and during the pilot. Do not treat a vendor's advertised savings as your own result.

A Vermont tenant is 1 month behind on rent. The landlord can end the tenancy in 14 days, but only with the right notice. Then a court case, a writ and a sheriff follow. This page lists each step with its statute, as read on legislature.vermont.gov on 2026-10-19. The statute site says its text includes the actions of the 2026 session.

## What Notice Must a Vermont Landlord Give for Unpaid Rent?

A Vermont landlord gives at least 14 days of actual notice to end a tenancy for unpaid rent (9 V.S.A. 4467(a)). The notice must state the exact termination date (4467(f)).

"Actual notice" has a set meaning in 9 V.S.A. 4451(1). It is written notice, hand-delivered or mailed to the last known address. If you mail it by first-class or certified mail, the law presumes the tenant got it 3 days after mailing.

The tenant can cure. The tenancy does not end if the tenant pays or tenders the rent due through the end of the rental period in which the tenant pays (4467(a)). A partial payment that you accept does not waive your remedies for nonpayment.

Watch for: you can send more than one notice. Under 4467(i), several valid notices on different grounds do not cancel each other. You may rely on all of them in one case.

## Can a Vermont Landlord Evict for a Lease Violation or With No Cause?

A Vermont landlord can end a tenancy for a lease breach or for no cause, but the notice periods differ.

**Lease breach.** For failure to follow a material term of the lease or a tenant duty under chapter 137, give at least 30 days' notice (4467(b)(1)). For criminal activity, illegal drug activity or violence that threatens other residents, give at least 14 days (4467(b)(2)).

**No cause, no written lease.** With monthly rent, give 60 days if the tenant has lived there 2 years or less. Give 90 days after more than 2 years (4467(c)(1)). With weekly rent, give 21 days (4467(c)(2)).

**No cause, written lease.** Give notice at least 30 days before the end of the lease term if the tenancy has lasted 2 years or less. Give 60 days if it has lasted more than 2 years. A written week-to-week lease needs 7 days (4467(e)).

**Shared home.** If you rent a room in your own home with shared kitchen, bath or living room, either party may end it with 15 days' notice (monthly rent) or 7 days (weekly rent) (4467(h)).

Watch for: retaliation. Under 9 V.S.A. 4465(c), a no-cause or breach notice within 90 days after a city or state notice of a health or safety violation is presumed to be retaliation. The tenant can use retaliation as a defense (4465(b)).

## Where Does a Landlord File an Eviction Case in Vermont, and What Does It Cost?

A Vermont landlord files an ejectment action, the state's name for an eviction case, in the Superior Court. 9 V.S.A. 4468 sends the case to 12 V.S.A. chapter 169, subchapter 3. Section 4851 gives the presiding judge of the Superior Court power to issue the writ.

The complaint must describe the property and say the tenant holds it unlawfully. You must attach a copy of the rental agreement, if any, and every termination notice (12 V.S.A. 4852). Either party may ask for a jury trial. Service follows the rules for other civil actions (4853).

The cost: the Vermont Judiciary fee page lists $295 for a civil "Filing" under 32 V.S.A. 1431(b)(1) (read 2026-10-19). A postjudgment motion in the civil division costs $90 (32 V.S.A. 1431(e)). Ask the clerk to confirm the fee before you file. Sheriff service fees are extra, and we did not find them on an official court page.

**Rent into court.** You may ask the court to order the tenant to pay rent into court while the case runs (12 V.S.A. 4853a(a)). The hearing can take place any time after 14 days' notice to the parties (4853a(b)). If the tenant does not pay as ordered, you get judgment for immediate possession (4853a(h)).

## How Long Does an Eviction Take in Vermont?

A Vermont eviction has fixed minimum periods, but no fixed total. The court calendar and any defense set the real length. Here is the minimum path for a nonpayment case:

1. **Serve the notice.** Mail it on day 0. The law presumes receipt on day 3 (4451(1)).
2. **Wait for the termination date.** It must be at least 14 days after actual notice, so day 17 or later (4467(a)).
3. **File the complaint.** File no later than 60 days after the termination date, or the notice fails (4467(k)).
4. **Ask for rent into court.** The hearing can happen after 14 days' notice (4853a(b)).
5. **Get judgment.** The writ of possession issues on the date of judgment, unless the court stays it for good cause (12 V.S.A. 4854).
6. **Sheriff serves the writ.** Possession comes no earlier than 14 days after service (4854), or 7 days if the tenant failed to pay rent into court (4853a(h)).
7. **Handle left property.** You may dispose of property left in the unit 15 days after the writ is served or after you regain possession, whichever is later (12 V.S.A. 4854a(a)(1)).

Worked example, nonpayment, using only the statutory minimums: 3 days for mail + 14 days of notice + 14 days from writ service to possession = 31 days. That leaves out the time from filing to judgment, which depends on the court. Plan for longer.

The tenant can also stop a nonpayment case once in 12 months. Before the writ is executed, the tenant pays into court all rent due through the current rental period, plus interest and costs (12 V.S.A. 4773).

## Who Removes the Tenant After a Judgment in Vermont?

A sheriff removes the tenant in Vermont, not the landlord. The writ of possession directs "any sheriff" to serve the tenant and then put you in possession (12 V.S.A. 4854). You do not move the tenant out yourself.

After the judgment, the court may also award rent due, damages and costs. If a written lease provides for it, the court may award reasonable attorney's fees (4854).

## Is a Self-Help Eviction Legal in Vermont?

A self-help eviction is illegal in Vermont. Under 9 V.S.A. 4463, a landlord may not:

- willfully cut off a utility, except for a short stop for emergency repairs;
- deny the tenant access to the unit except through proper judicial process;
- deny the tenant access to the tenant's own property except through proper judicial process.

A tenant harmed by an illegal eviction can sue for an injunction, damages, costs and reasonable attorney's fees (9 V.S.A. 4464). So the court route is also the cheaper route.

## Checklist Before You File

- [ ] The notice states the exact termination date.
- [ ] The notice period fits the ground: 14, 30, 60 or 90 days.
- [ ] You mailed or hand-delivered the notice, and you kept proof.
- [ ] No health or safety notice from a city or the state in the last 90 days (for a non-rent notice).
- [ ] You will file within 60 days after the termination date.
- [ ] The complaint has the lease and every notice attached.

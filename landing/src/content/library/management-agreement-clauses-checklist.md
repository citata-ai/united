## What are the core clauses of a residential management agreement?

The core clauses of a residential management agreement are the parties, term, services, fees, authority, reserve and trust money. These clauses decide who does what and how money moves. Most owner disputes start in one of them.

Here are all 20 clauses in one checklist. The last column shows who each clause protects most.

| # | Clause | What it states | Protects |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Parties and property | Owner, firm, broker of record, each address and unit | Both |
| 2 | Term and renewal | Start date, end date, auto-renewal terms | Both |
| 3 | Scope of services | Leasing, rent collection, repairs, inspections, reports | Both |
| 4 | Excluded services | Construction management, legal work, tax advice | Manager |
| 5 | Management fee | Rate, base (collected rent or rent due), due date | Both |
| 6 | Other fees | Leasing, renewal, setup, inspection, eviction, markups | Owner |
| 7 | Repair approval limit | Dollar amount per job without owner consent | Both |
| 8 | Emergency authority | Right to act above the limit to protect life or property | Manager |
| 9 | Operating reserve | Amount and purpose of money held for repairs | Both |
| 10 | Trust account | Where owner money and deposits sit, who signs | Owner |
| 11 | Interest on trust money | Who keeps any interest or credits | Owner |
| 12 | Owner draws | When and how the owner gets paid, shortfall rules | Owner |
| 13 | Reports | Type and frequency of statements | Owner |
| 14 | Rent setting and leasing rules | Who sets rent, screening rules, lease form | Both |
| 15 | Fair housing | Manager follows federal, state and local fair housing law | Both |
| 16 | Insurance | Owner's property and liability cover, manager's E&O | Both |
| 17 | Indemnification | Who pays for claims caused by whom | Manager |
| 18 | Termination | Notice, cause, fees at termination | Both |
| 19 | Handover | Keys, files, deposits, final accounting, deadlines | Owner |
| 20 | Disputes and governing law | Mediation, venue, state law, attorney fees | Both |

Buildium's agreement guide covers most of the same ground. It also flags red flags: vague service words like "manager will maintain property", no spending limits, missing termination steps, unlimited indemnity and no data return terms.

## What do state broker laws require in writing?

State broker laws differ, so the minimum content of the agreement differs by state. Arizona gives a clear example in Arizona Revised Statutes 32-2173. It says a property management agreement shall be in "clear, unambiguous language" and shall:

- [ ] State all material terms of the firm's services and duties.
- [ ] Be signed by the owner and the firm's designated broker or an authorized licensee.
- [ ] Specify a beginning and an ending date.
- [ ] Contain cancellation terms that both parties agree to.
- [ ] Provide for how all money collected is handled, including tenant deposits.
- [ ] Specify the type and frequency of status reports.
- [ ] State the amount and purpose of the operating reserve.
- [ ] Provide for the allocation of interest on trust account money.
- [ ] State the terms of all compensation the owner pays.
- [ ] Not be assigned to another licensee without the owner's written consent.

The same statute allows an auto-renewal clause only if the firm sends a reminder notice at least 30 days before the renewal date. It also allows "reasonable liquidated damages or cancellation fees for early termination".

California shows the other side: penalties for what the agreement leaves out. Business and Professions Code 10176(g) allows discipline for any "secret or undisclosed amount of compensation". If you take a markup or a vendor rebate, the agreement must say so.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Which clauses protect the manager?

The clauses that protect the manager are indemnification, insurance, emergency authority, excluded services and the owner's duty to fund repairs. Without them, you can carry the cost of a problem you did not cause.

Write each one with care:

1. **Indemnification.** The owner covers claims from the property's condition. You cover claims from your own negligence. Buildium suggests mutual indemnity with exceptions for gross negligence or intentional misconduct.
2. **Insurance.** The owner keeps property and liability cover. Ask to be named as an additional insured on the owner's policy.
3. **Emergency authority.** You may act above the approval limit to stop damage or protect safety, and tell the owner the same day.
4. **Funding duty.** The owner keeps the reserve at the stated amount. You may pause work when the reserve is empty.
5. **Excluded services.** Large projects, court time and tax work cost extra or sit outside the agreement.

## Which clauses do owners read first?

We found no study that measures which clauses owners read first, so this is our view from fee pages and owner questions. Owners look at money and exit: the fee, the other fees, the approval limit and the termination clause.

Make those 4 clauses easy to read:

- **Fee.** One sentence with the rate and the base. Example: "8% of rent collected each month."
- **Other fees.** A table, not a paragraph.
- **Approval limit.** A dollar amount. Example: "Up to $500 per job without owner approval."
- **Termination.** The notice period and any fee, in plain words.

A clear fee table also helps you in search. Owners and AI answers compare managers on what they can read. Read [how owners choose a property manager with AI](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai) for more.

## How long should the agreement be?

The agreement should be as long as the 20 clauses need and no longer. We found no standard page count. A long agreement with vague words protects no one. A short one with clear amounts and dates protects both sides.

Practical rules:

- **Put amounts in a fee schedule.** Then a fee change updates 1 page, not the whole agreement.
- **Use defined terms.** Define "collected rent", "emergency" and "reserve" once.
- **Match your software.** If the agreement says "markup as a separate line", your statements must show it.
- **Match the termination deadlines to your state.** Arizona, for example, requires a final bank reconciliation within 75 days of termination.
- **Review once a year.** Laws, fees and your services change.

## Should a property manager write local landlord law pages?

Yes. Local landlord law pages show an owner that you know the rules where their property is. They also give AI engines a clear, local source to quote.

All 3 direct competitors we studied put this in their plans. Our client playbook lists "local landlord question clusters with checked facts" as move 6. ClearLead Digital, Property Manager Websites and Goodjuju Marketing all do it (our data, 2026-10-19). The key word is "checked". A law page with one wrong deadline does more harm than no page.

Google sets a high bar for such pages. Its guide to helpful content says topics that "could significantly impact people's lives" get a higher standard, and "Of these aspects, trust is most important." A rent deposit rule touches an owner's money and a tenant's home. Treat each law page that way.

## Which law questions do owners ask?

Owners ask about the rules that cost them money or time. We group them in 6 topics. Each topic gets its own page or its own section, with one rule per heading.

| Topic | Typical owner question | Where the official rule lives |
|---|---|---|
| Security deposits | How much can I collect, and when must I return it? | State statute on the legislature site |
| Notices and eviction | How many days of notice before I can file? | State statute, court self-help pages |
| Entry and repairs | How much notice before I enter the unit? | State statute or city housing code |
| Disclosures | What must I give a tenant before the lease? | Federal rule (EPA for lead paint), state statute |
| Fees and listings | Who pays the broker fee? What fees must I list? | City ordinance, city agency page |
| Fair housing | What can I say in an ad or a screening rule? | Fair Housing Act (Department of Justice), state law |

Start with the topics where your city or state differs from the rest of the country. A national page on deposits already exists many times. A page on your city's rules may not.

## Which official sources should you link?

Link the primary text every time, then explain it in plain words. Primary means the statute, the regulation or the agency that enforces it. Here are 3 examples of what a checked rule looks like.

1. **Federal lead paint disclosure.** The EPA rule covers most housing built before 1978. Before the lease, the landlord gives the pamphlet "Protect Your Family From Lead In Your Home". The landlord also shares known lead information and records, and includes a Lead Warning Statement. Keep a signed copy "for three years after the sale is completed or the lease begins" (EPA). Leases of 100 days or less are exempt.
2. **Fair Housing Act.** The Department of Justice lists the protected classes: race or color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status and disability. Check your state law as well, and link it if it exists.
3. **A local rule that changed.** New York City's FARE Act took effect on June 11, 2025. NYC's consumer agency says: "It is now illegal for landlords to charge real estate broker fees to renters." Landlords and agents must also give tenants a written list of all fees before the lease (NYC DCWP, 2025-06-11).

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Use the government site even when a law firm blog ranks higher. If the official site blocks your browser, find the same text on the state's official code site. Do not quote a rule you only saw in a search snippet.

## How do you write a law page that AI can quote?

Write each rule as one short block that stands alone. AI tools cut pages into small parts, so each part must make sense without the rest of the page.

Use this structure for each rule:

- **Heading as a question.** "How many days does a [state] landlord have to return a deposit?"
- **One-sentence answer.** The number, the unit and the condition.
- **Source line.** Statute section and a link, plus "checked on [date]".
- **One local detail.** What owners in your area get wrong, from your own work.
- **What you do as manager.** Your process, in 2–4 steps.

The local detail is what makes your page worth citing. In our test, how-to prompts often got answers that cite the official source. Of 108 non-brand answers, developers.google.com was cited in 11 and support.google.com in 10 (our data, 2026-10-19). An engine can quote the statute directly. It needs you for the part the statute does not say, such as how your city's court schedules hearings.

For the page format that AI cites in general, see the [AI search checklist for property management websites](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website).

## How do you avoid legal advice risk?

Say what the rule is, not what one reader should do. A rule is general. Advice applies the rule to one owner's facts. Keep to the rule.

Do these 6 things on every law page:

1. **Add the disclaimer line.** "This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney."
2. **Cite the section.** Name the statute or ordinance and link it.
3. **Show the checked-on date.** Put it under each rule, not only at the top.
4. **Name the author.** Google asks: "Is it self-evident to your visitors who authored your content?"
5. **Get one attorney read.** Ask a local landlord attorney to check the page once, and say so if they did.
6. **Keep Fair Housing in mind.** Never write a screening rule or an ad example that prefers or excludes a protected class.

Do not write "you can always" or "you never need to". Laws have exceptions, like the 100-day lease exemption in the EPA rule. Say "the rule covers" and list the main exceptions.

## How often should you update a landlord law page?

Update each page on a schedule and on the day a law changes. A stale page is the main risk of this work.

| Trigger | What to do | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| New law passed in your state or city | Update the page within 1 week; note the effective date | Content lead |
| Effective date arrives | Change the page from "will" to "is"; update the checked-on date | Content lead |
| End of each legislative session | Read the session's housing bills; update pages that changed | Manager or attorney |
| Once a year, same month | Open every linked statute; confirm each number | Content lead |
| A broken official link | Find the new official URL the same week | Web person |

Change the "updated" date only when the content changed. Google lists this as a warning sign. "Are you changing the date of pages to make them seem fresh when the content has not substantially changed?"

## Which law pages should you write first?

Write the 3 pages where your answer differs from a national article. For most managers, that is the deposit rule, the notice rule and one local rule. The local rule can be a broker fee law, a rental registration or a rent cap.

Then link each law page from the city page where it applies. Do you serve Section 8 owners? Then add the voucher rent limits as well. Our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) lists HUD Fair Market Rents by metro.

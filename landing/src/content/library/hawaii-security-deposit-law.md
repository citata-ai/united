Hawaii puts its deposit rules in HRS § 521-44, part of the Residential Landlord-Tenant Code. I read the current text on the Hawaii State Legislature site on 2026-10-20. The deadline is one of the shortest in the US: 14 days.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | 1 month's rent | § 521-44(b) |
| Extra pet deposit | Up to 1 month's rent, only for a tenant with a pet | § 521-44(b) |
| Pet deposit for an assistance animal | Not allowed | § 521-44(b)(1)(B) |
| Money at move-in | Only first month's rent plus the deposit | § 521-44(b) |
| Deposit as last month's rent | Only by written agreement, with 45 days' notice | § 521-44(b) |
| Return or written notice of retention | Within 14 days after the agreement ends | § 521-44(c) |
| Late notice | Landlord must return the full deposit | § 521-44(c) |
| Tenant's suit to recover | Within 1 year after the agreement ends | § 521-44(c) |
| Successor's notice to tenants after a sale | Within 20 days after the transfer | § 521-44(f) |
| Wrongful and wilful retention | Up to 3 times the amount kept | § 521-44(h)(1) |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Hawaii?

A Hawaii landlord may charge a security deposit of no more than 1 month's rent. HRS § 521-44(b) allows one addition: a pet deposit agreed by both sides, for damage by a pet allowed under the lease.

The pet deposit has 3 limits:

- It may not exceed 1 month's rent.
- It may not be charged to a tenant who has no pet.
- It may not be charged for an assistance animal that is a reasonable accommodation under HRS § 515-3.

The same subsection limits move-in money. At the start of a rental agreement, the landlord may not require or receive any money other than the first month's rent and the deposit.

Worked example for a $2,400-a-month unit:

| Item | Allowed amount |
|---|---|
| First month's rent | $2,400 |
| Security deposit, maximum | $2,400 |
| Pet deposit, maximum (tenant with a pet) | $2,400 |
| Total at move-in, maximum | $7,200 |

Application screening fees have their own section, HRS § 521-46.

## How many days does a Hawaii landlord have to return a security deposit?

A Hawaii landlord has 14 days after the rental agreement ends to return the deposit or the balance (HRS § 521-44(c)). If the landlord keeps any part, the written notice of retention is due within the same 14 days.

Proof of timing: the return or notice is presumed on time if mailed to an address the tenant gave the landlord, with proof of mailing, postmarked before midnight of day 14. A signed receipt by the tenant within 14 days also works.

A tenant who wants to sue over a deposit must file within 1 year after the rental agreement ends.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Hawaii?

A Hawaii landlord may use the deposit only for the items in HRS § 521-44(a), "and no others":

1. **Damage and defaults.** Accidental or intentional damage from failure to meet § 521-51, unpaid rent, and keys not returned. The statute names key fobs, parking cards, garage door openers and mailbox keys.
2. **Cleaning.** To return the unit to the condition the tenant received it in.
3. **Wrongful quit.** Damages from a tenant who wrongfully leaves.
4. **Pet damage.** Damage by a pet allowed under the lease.
5. **Utilities.** Money owed for landlord-supplied utilities not included in the rent.

A tenant absent for 20 days or more in a row, without written notice and without paying rent, is deemed to have wrongfully quit (§ 521-44(d)). In that case, the landlord may keep the whole deposit, in addition to other remedies.

## Does a Hawaii landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

A Hawaii landlord must give written notice with "the particulars of and grounds for the retention" (HRS § 521-44(c)). The notice must include written evidence of the costs:

- For repairs: estimates or invoices for material and services.
- For cleaning: receipts for supplies and equipment, or charges for cleaning services.

If the notice and the evidence are not sent within 14 days, the landlord may not keep any part of the deposit. The full amount goes back. The only exception is a tenant who wrongfully quit the unit.

## Must a Hawaii landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

HRS § 521-44 does not require interest or a separate account. The section says the tenant's claim to the deposit is "prior to the claim of any creditor of the landlord, including a trustee in bankruptcy, even if the security deposits are commingled".

When the landlord's interest passes to another person, by sale, death, receivership or otherwise, § 521-44(f) applies:

- The original landlord gives the successor an accounting of each unit's deposit.
- Within 20 days after the transfer, the successor tells each tenant in writing the amount credited.
- If the successor fails, the law presumes a deposit of at least 1 month's rent at the original rate.

## What is the penalty for a Hawaii landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

A Hawaii landlord who wrongfully and wilfully keeps a deposit may owe 3 times the amount kept, plus the cost of suit (HRS § 521-44(h)(1)). The court decides; the word in the statute is "may".

The 3 outcomes in small claims court, under § 521-44(h):

| Court finds | Award |
|---|---|
| Landlord kept money wrongfully and wilfully | Up to 3 times that amount, plus cost of suit |
| Landlord kept money wrongfully | That amount, plus cost of suit |
| Landlord was entitled to keep it | Landlord gets the disputed amount, plus cost of suit |

Either side can file in the small claims division of the district court under chapter 633 (§ 521-44(g)). Neither side may use an attorney in that case, including a salaried employee.

## Hawaii deposit checklist

- Cap the deposit at 1 month's rent. Add a pet deposit only for a real pet.
- Collect nothing else at move-in except first month's rent.
- Diary day 14 from each lease end.
- Send the notice with invoices, estimates or receipts attached.
- Mail it with proof of mailing to the tenant's forwarding address.
- Pass deposit records to a buyer at or before closing.

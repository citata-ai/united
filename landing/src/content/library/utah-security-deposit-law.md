## Utah deposit rules at a glance

Utah's deposit law is short: Utah Code Title 57, Chapter 17, "Residential Renters' Deposits", has 5 sections. We read all 5 on le.utah.gov on 2026-10-19. Section 57-17-3 was last amended in the 2025 General Session, and 57-17-5 in 2023 (effective July 1, 2024).

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | No cap in the chapter | 57-17 (no limit) |
| Non-refundable part | Must be stated in writing when you take the deposit | 57-17-2 |
| Allowed deductions | Rent, damage beyond reasonable wear and tear, contract costs and fees, cleaning | 57-17-3(1) |
| Return deadline | 30 days after the renter vacates and returns possession | 57-17-3(2) |
| Itemized list | Required for any deduction, sent with the balance | 57-17-3(2)(c) |
| Deadline after a renter's demand notice | 5 business days after service | 57-17-3(5) |
| Penalty for missing the demand | Full deposit, full prepaid rent, $100 civil penalty | 57-17-5(1) |
| Attorney fees | To the prevailing party, if the other side acted in bad faith | 57-17-5(2) |
| Interest or separate account | Not required by the chapter | 57-17 (no rule) |
| New owner after a sale | Bound by the deposit rules | 57-17-4 |
| Move-in condition record | Inventory, form or walkthrough before the lease | 57-22-4(6) |

## What is the maximum security deposit in Utah?

Utah has no maximum security deposit. Utah Code Title 57, Chapter 17 sets rules for the return of a deposit, but no section caps the amount. So the lease sets it.

Utah Code 57-22-4(3) adds a disclosure rule before you take money. Before an owner accepts an application fee or other payment, the owner must give a written estimate. It covers the rent and each fixed non-rent expense. The owner must also explain how a prospective renter can get money back.

A non-refundable part needs care. Utah Code 57-17-2 covers a written agreement. Any non-refundable part "must be so stated in writing to the renter at the time the deposit is taken". Put it in the lease or the deposit agreement, with the dollar amount.

## How many days does a Utah landlord have to return a security deposit?

A Utah landlord has 30 days. The clock starts on the day the renter vacates and returns possession to the owner or the owner's agent (Utah Code 57-17-3(2)). Within that time, send:

1. **The balance of the deposit.**
2. **The balance of any prepaid rent.**
3. **A written, itemized list of deductions,** if you made any.

Send them to the renter's last known address by mail or delivery. Or send them electronically, by a means the renter gave you (57-17-3(2)). Ask for a forwarding address and an email at move-out, in writing.

Watch for: the 30 days count from the return of possession, not the lease end date. If the renter leaves early but keeps the keys, record the day the keys come back.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Utah?

Utah Code 57-17-3(1) lists four kinds of deduction. The owner may apply the deposit to:

- **Rent** that is unpaid.
- **Damage to the premises beyond reasonable wear and tear.**
- **Other costs and fees provided for in the contract.**
- **Cleaning of the unit.**

Reasonable wear and tear stays with the owner. Chapter 17 does not define the term, so a judge decides in a dispute. Good records help you show what changed. Utah Code 57-22-4(6) says that before you sign a rental agreement, you must do one of three things:

- give a written inventory of the unit's condition, excluding ordinary wear and tear;
- give the renter a form to record the condition, with reasonable time to return it; or
- offer a walkthrough inspection.

Take dated photos at move-in and at move-out. Keep repair invoices for each deduction.

## Does a Utah landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. Utah Code 57-17-3(2)(c) applies to any deduction from the deposit or prepaid rent. It requires "a written notice that itemizes and explains the reason for each deduction". It goes out with the balance, within the 30 days.

Here is a worked example. The numbers are a sample.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Deposit held | $1,500 |
| Unpaid rent for the last 8 days | −$400 |
| Repair a hole in a bedroom door (invoice attached) | −$250 |
| Cleaning, as allowed in the lease | −$150 |
| Balance due to the renter by day 30 | $700 |

Now the cost of a miss. Say you send nothing in 30 days. The renter serves the 57-17-3(3) notice, and you still send nothing within 5 business days. Under 57-17-5(1), the renter may recover the full $1,500 deposit and a $100 civil penalty: $1,600. That is $900 more than the $700 you owed.

## Must a Utah landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

No. Utah Code Title 57, Chapter 17 has no rule on interest and no rule on a separate or trust account. Check your lease for any promise you made.

A sale does not end your duty. Utah Code 57-17-4 says whoever holds the owner's interest when the tenancy ends is bound by the deposit rules. So when you buy a building, get the deposit list and the funds at closing.

## What is the penalty for a Utah landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

The Utah penalty works in two steps. First, if the owner misses the 30-day duty, the renter may serve a "Tenant's Notice to Provide Deposit Disposition" (57-17-3(3)). The statute prints the form. The renter serves it at the address in the lease (57-17-3(4)). Service is by hand, with a suitable person, by posting, or by registered or certified mail.

Second, the owner has 5 business days after service to send the balance, the prepaid rent and the itemized list (57-17-3(5)). If the owner fails, Utah Code 57-17-5(1) lets the renter recover:

- the full deposit, if the owner did not return the deposit balance on time;
- the full prepaid rent, if the owner did not return that balance on time; and
- a civil penalty of $100.

The renter may also sue to enforce. The court awards costs and attorney fees to the prevailing party if it finds the other side acted in bad faith (57-17-5(2)).

Two limits protect owners. A renter who does not serve the notice gets no relief under 57-17-5 (57-17-5(3)). And either side can still claim other damages (57-17-5(4)).

## Checklist for each Utah move-out

- [ ] Record the date the renter returns possession and the keys.
- [ ] Get a forwarding address and an email in writing.
- [ ] Inspect and photograph the unit the same day.
- [ ] Match each deduction to rent, damage, a lease fee or cleaning.
- [ ] Write an itemized list with a reason for each line.
- [ ] Send the balance and the list by day 30.
- [ ] If a renter's notice arrives, act within 5 business days.

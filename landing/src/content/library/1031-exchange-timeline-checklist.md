## The timeline at a glance

A delayed 1031 exchange has one fixed start and 2 fixed deadlines. The Form 8824 instructions set them: identify within 45 days, receive within 180 days or by your return due date with extensions, whichever is earlier.

Worked timeline, with example dates:

| Day | Date (example) | What must be done |
|-----|----------------|-------------------|
| Before listing | 2026-12 to 2027-01 | Tax advice, intermediary chosen, exchange agreement drafted |
| Contract signed | 2027-01-20 | Sale contract names the exchange and lets you assign it |
| Day 0 | 2027-02-15 | Sale closes; money goes to the intermediary |
| Day 1 to 45 | 2027-02-16 to 2027-04-01 | Tour, make offers, choose targets |
| Day 45 | 2027-04-01 | Signed written identification delivered |
| Day 46 to 180 | 2027-04-02 to 2027-08-14 | Inspections, loan, close |
| Day 180 | 2027-08-14 | Replacement property received |
| Tax filing | By 2028-04-15, or the extended date | File Form 8824 with your 2027 return |

In this example day 180 comes before the 2027 return due date, so day 180 is the deadline.

## What must I do before I list the property?

Before you list, set up the exchange so you never touch the sale money. Most failed exchanges fail here, not on day 45.

IRS Publication 544 describes the qualified intermediary (QI) safe harbor. A QI is a person who is not a disqualified person and who signs a written exchange agreement with you. Under that agreement, the QI acquires the property you give up, transfers it, acquires the replacement and transfers it to you.

Who cannot be your QI. Publication 544 lists disqualified persons. They include your agent at the time of the deal and certain related persons. Some past roles also count. Your employee, attorney, accountant, investment banker or broker, or real estate agent or broker in the 2 years before the transfer counts as your agent. Some routine services are exempt.

Before-listing checklist:

- [ ] Confirm both properties will be held for business or investment.
- [ ] Ask a tax professional to estimate the gain and the deferred tax.
- [ ] Choose a QI. Ask where the money will sit and what bond or insurance covers it.
- [ ] Sign the exchange agreement before the sale closes.
- [ ] Add an exchange cooperation clause to the sale contract.
- [ ] Get a loan pre-approval for the replacement property.
- [ ] Shortlist markets and properties now. 45 days is short.
- [ ] Check the dates: if you close late in the year, day 180 can fall after April 15.

## What happens on day 0?

Day 0 is the day you transfer the property you give up. Both clocks start that day.

At closing:

1. **The QI receives the sale money,** not you. The cash must not pass through your account.
2. **You sign the assignment** of the sale contract to the QI, if your agreement uses one.
3. **You keep the settlement statement.** You will need it for Form 8824.
4. **You write both deadlines** on a calendar with reminders at day 30, 40 and 44.

Why the money matters: Treasury Regulation 1.1031(k)-1 says you are in constructive receipt of money when it is credited to your account or set apart for you. Receipt of the money can turn the exchange into a taxable sale.

The late-year trap. Say your sale closes on 2026-11-16. Day 180 is 2027-05-15. But your 2026 return is due 2027-04-15. Under the "whichever is earlier" rule, you must close by 2027-04-15, unless you file an extension. Ask your tax professional to file it.

## How do I identify replacement property by day 45?

You identify replacement property with a signed, written document sent before the end of day 45. Treasury Regulation 1.1031(k)-1(c) sets the method.

The document must be:

- **Signed by you.**
- **Delivered** by hand, mail, fax or another way, before the end of the 45-day period.
- **Sent to the right person.** That is the person who must transfer the property to you. It can also be another person in the exchange who is not you or a disqualified person. The regulation names an intermediary and an escrow agent as examples.
- **Clear.** Publication 544 says to describe the property clearly, for example with the legal description or street address.

How many properties you can name, under 1.1031(k)-1(c)(4):

| Rule | Limit | Example, $400,000 sale |
|------|-------|------------------------|
| 3-property rule | Up to 3 properties of any value | Name 3 homes at $350,000, $500,000 and $650,000 |
| 200% rule | Any number, if the total value is not more than 200% of what you sold | Name 5 homes that total $800,000 or less |
| 95% rule | If you exceed both, you must receive at least 95% of the total value you named | Name $1.2 million; you must acquire at least $1.14 million of it |

If you name more than the rules allow and do not meet the 95% rule, the regulation treats you as if you named nothing.

Small extras do not count as separate properties. The regulation treats incidental property, such as furniture that normally goes with the house, as part of the larger property. The limit is 15% of the larger property's value.

Day-45 checklist:

- [ ] Offers out on your top choices by about day 30.
- [ ] Backups named in case a deal fails.
- [ ] Count against the 3-property or 200% rule.
- [ ] Sign and deliver the notice to the QI before day 45 ends.
- [ ] Keep proof of delivery.

## What closes by day 180?

By day 180, or the earlier return due date, you must receive the replacement property. It must be property you identified by day 45, or property you received before day 45 ends.

Day 46 to 180 checklist:

- [ ] Inspections and appraisal booked right after identification.
- [ ] Loan locked. Less debt on the new property than the old can create taxable boot.
- [ ] QI wires the exchange money to the closing.
- [ ] Title is in the same taxpayer name as the property you sold.
- [ ] Settlement statement saved.
- [ ] Extension filed if the return due date comes before day 180.

If you miss the deadline, the deal does not qualify as a deferred exchange (Form 8824 instructions). The gain may be taxable in the year you sold. That applies even when the QI causes the miss. A narrow exception applies when a QI defaults because of bankruptcy or receivership.

## After the exchange: file and keep records

File Form 8824 with the return for the year you transferred property in the exchange. Keep the records on both properties. The IRS gives a rule for property you received in a nontaxable exchange. Keep records on the old property and the new one until the period of limitations ends for the year you dispose of the new one.

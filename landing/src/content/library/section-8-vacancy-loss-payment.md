## Does the voucher program pay for vacant days?

The base voucher program does not pay for vacant days. 24 CFR 982.311(a) says HAP (housing assistance payments) "may only be paid to the owner during the lease term, and while the family is residing in the unit".

There is one exception for the move-out month. Under 24 CFR 982.311(d), you may keep the HAP for the month when the family moves out. The PHA may not pay HAP for any month after that. So a family that leaves on the 5th still leaves you that month's HAP. A family that leaves on the 31st does the same.

Everything beyond that is local. Some PHAs pay a vacancy loss amount as a landlord incentive. Others pay nothing.

| Program | Vacancy pay | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Tenant-based voucher, base rule | HAP for the move-out month only | 24 CFR 982.311(d) |
| Project-based voucher (PBV) | Move-out month; PHA may add up to 2 full months | 24 CFR 983.352 |
| PHA landlord incentive programs | Set by the PHA; designs we found pay about one month | PHA policy |

## Which PHAs pay vacancy loss?

PHAs that run landlord incentive programs are the ones that pay vacancy loss. There is no national list. You find out by reading your PHA's landlord page or Administrative Plan.

Moving to Work (MTW) agencies use these incentives most. MTW agencies have HUD approval to change some program rules. In 2021, HUD's Office of Inspector General (OIG) surveyed MTW agencies (Memorandum No. 2021-LA-0803). NLIHC summarized the results:

- 34 of 39 MTW agencies answered the survey.
- 28 of the 34 (82%) used some landlord incentive from 2016-01-01 to 2019-12-31.
- 20 of the 28 (71%) used money incentives.
- Vacancy loss or "re-rent" bonuses were on the list of the most popular money incentives.
- The PHAs rated new landlord bonuses, damage reimbursements and vacancy loss or re-rent bonuses as the most effective money incentives.

One PHA told OIG its re-rent bonus worked because "landlords did not lose rent while waiting for a new tenant to be processed". That is the problem a vacancy payment solves.

## How many days do they cover?

The local designs we found cover about one month. Housing Solutions Lab, a project of the NYU Furman Center, describes 2 common designs. Some PHAs "pay for one month of rent to cover any vacancy caused during lease up". Others "pay the full rent amount during a vacancy caused by turnover from one voucher holder tenant to another". It also cites Marin, which pays one month's rent if a tenant vacates.

For PBV units, the federal rule sets the limit. 24 CFR 983.352 lets the HAP contract include vacancy payments for up to 2 full months after the move-out month. The amount for each month cannot exceed the rent to owner minus what you collected, including the tenant's security deposit. You may not keep the move-out month payment if the PHA finds the vacancy is your fault.

## How do I claim the payment?

You claim a vacancy loss payment on the PHA's own form, under its own rules. The details change by PHA, but claims often need the same proof. Use this checklist:

- [ ] The prior voucher tenant's move-out date, in writing.
- [ ] The prior lease and the prior HAP contract number.
- [ ] Proof the unit was ready to rent: photos, a turnover work order.
- [ ] Proof you listed the unit, with dates.
- [ ] The new lease and new HAP contract, if the program pays only on re-rent to a voucher holder.
- [ ] A record of the security deposit and any amount you kept for unpaid rent.
- [ ] The claim, sent before the PHA deadline.

Watch for: some programs pay only after a new HAP contract is signed for a new voucher family. If you rent the unit to a market-rate tenant, you may not qualify. Read the eligibility rules before you choose a tenant.

## Does vacancy loss apply before the first inspection?

Not under federal rules. HAP starts only when the lease term starts and the family lives in the unit (982.311(a)). Before the first inspection and the HAP contract, no HAP is due.

Some local programs do cover lease-up. The Housing Solutions Lab quote above, "one month of rent to cover any vacancy caused during lease up", describes that design. If your PHA has one, the claim usually needs the RFTA (Request for Tenancy Approval) date, the inspection date and the HAP contract start date. To keep the gap short, have the unit ready before you submit the RFTA.

## Example calculation

Here is how a prorated vacancy payment can work. This is an example, not one PHA's formula. Your PHA's form decides the real numbers.

- Prior contract rent: $1,500 per month
- Prior family moved out: 2026-09-20
- New voucher family moves in: 2026-10-13
- Security deposit kept for unpaid rent: $200

Step 1. The move-out month (September) is covered. You keep the September HAP under 982.311(d).

Step 2. Count vacant days in October: October 1 to October 12 is 12 days.

Step 3. Prorate the rent: $1,500 × 12 ÷ 31 = $580.65.

Step 4. Subtract amounts you already got: $580.65 − $200 = $380.65.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Rent lost, Oct 1–12 | $580.65 |
| Less deposit kept for unpaid rent | −$200.00 |
| Possible vacancy payment | $380.65 |

The deposit step follows the PBV rule in 24 CFR 983.352, which subtracts amounts "available from the tenant's security deposit". A local program may use a similar offset. ## What to do now

1. **Search your PHA's site** for "landlord incentive", "vacancy loss" or "re-rent bonus".
2. **Read the Administrative Plan** chapter on owners or landlord incentives.
3. **Ask the PHA** 3 questions: Do you pay vacancy loss? How many days? What form and deadline?
4. **Plan turnover** so the unit is ready when the notice arrives. The fastest re-rent is the best vacancy payment.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

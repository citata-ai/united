## What columns does a rent roll have?

A rent roll has one row per unit and one column per fact about the lease and the money. Buildium's dictionary lists what most rent rolls cover. That is the property, unit, tenant names, lease dates, monthly rent, deposit held, amount paid, balance owed and lease status.

Core columns:

| Column | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Unit | Unit number or name |
| Tenant | Name on the lease, or "vacant" |
| Lease start | First day of the current lease |
| Lease end | Last day, or "month-to-month" |
| Monthly rent | Contract rent on the lease |
| Deposit held | Security deposit amount |
| Paid to date | Payments in the current period |
| Balance owed | Unpaid rent and charges |
| Status | Current, late, notice given, vacant |

Buildium lists optional columns for more detail: concessions, late fees, parking or utility charges, market rent next to actual rent, vacancy status and tenant contact details. Add market rent if the report goes to a buyer. It shows where rent can grow.

## What does a sample rent roll look like?

Here is a sample rent roll for a 6-unit building on October 31, 2026. The tenants and numbers are an example.

| Unit | Tenant | Lease start | Lease end | Rent | Market rent | Deposit | Balance | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1A | Tenant A | 2026-03-01 | 2027-02-28 | $1,450 | $1,500 | $1,450 | $0 | Current |
| 1B | Tenant B | 2024-07-01 | Month-to-month | $1,300 | $1,500 | $1,300 | $0 | Current |
| 2A | Tenant C | 2026-06-01 | 2027-05-31 | $1,525 | $1,525 | $1,525 | $1,525 | Late |
| 2B | Vacant | | | $0 | $1,500 | $0 | $0 | Vacant since 2026-10-01 |
| 3A | Tenant D | 2025-11-01 | 2026-10-31 | $1,475 | $1,550 | $1,475 | $0 | Notice given |
| 3B | Tenant E | 2026-01-01 | 2026-12-31 | $1,500 | $1,550 | $1,500 | $0 | Current |
| **Total** | 5 of 6 leased | | | **$7,250** | **$9,125** | **$7,250** | **$1,525** | |

From this one table, a reader learns:

- **Occupancy:** 5 of 6 units leased = 83.3%.
- **Rent vs market:** $7,250 in place on 5 units. The same 5 units at market rent = $9,125 − $1,500 = $7,625. So in-place rent is $375 a month below market.
- **Risk:** One tenant owes $1,525. One tenant has given notice. One unit is month-to-month.
- **Deposits:** $7,250 held in trust for 5 tenants.

## Why do buyers and lenders ask for a rent roll?

Buyers and lenders ask for a rent roll to check the income they are paying for or lending against. Buildium calls the rent roll key for investors and appraisers. It also says a lender may request a rent roll as proof of stable income and tenant quality.

The rent roll feeds the price. Income on the rent roll leads to net operating income (NOI), and NOI divided by price gives the cap rate, Buildium's cap rate entry explains. So a rent roll that overstates rent overstates the value.

5 checks a buyer or lender runs on a rent roll:

1. **Match each row to a signed lease.** Rent, dates and names must agree.
2. **Match rent to bank deposits.** 3 to 12 months of deposits should support the total.
3. **Check balances owed.** Large balances mean the income is not as safe as it looks.
4. **Check lease end dates.** Many leases that end in the same month are a vacancy risk.
5. **Check deposits held.** The buyer takes over the duty to return them, so the amounts must be right.

## How often should a property manager update the rent roll?

Update the rent roll each time a lease changes, and review it at least monthly. Buildium advises entering new leases, renewals and move-outs in real time, with a monthly review at minimum. It suggests weekly or even daily review for high-turnover properties.

A simple routine:

- [ ] **Same day:** enter each new lease, renewal, notice and move-out.
- [ ] **Each week:** check late balances and units on notice.
- [ ] **Each month-end:** run the rent roll, compare total rent with rent collected, and attach it to the owner statement.
- [ ] **Before a sale or refinance:** run it on the date the buyer or lender asks for. Check every row against the lease.

In good property management software, the rent roll is a report built from the leases and ledgers, so it updates itself. A spreadsheet rent roll needs a person to update it, and it drifts.

## What is the difference between a rent roll and a rent ledger?

A rent roll shows all units on one date. A rent ledger shows one tenant's charges and payments over time. Buildium puts it this way: the rent roll is a snapshot of current occupancy and lease terms, and the ledger is the payment history for a specific tenant or unit.

| | Rent roll | Rent ledger |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | All units in a property | One tenant or unit |
| Time | One date | Full history |
| Main use | Value, occupancy, sale, loan | Disputes, collections, court |
| Key numbers | Rent, lease dates, balance by unit | Each charge and payment with dates |
| Who asks for it | Owners, buyers, appraisers, lenders | Tenants, attorneys, courts, owners |

Use both. The rent roll shows that Unit 2A owes $1,525. The ledger shows when each charge and payment happened, which you need for a late notice or a court case.

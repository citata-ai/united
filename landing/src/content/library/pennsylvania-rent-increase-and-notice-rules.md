A Pittsburgh owner wants to raise a month-to-month rent from $1,200 to $1,300. How much notice is the law's minimum? The Pennsylvania Landlord and Tenant Act of 1951 has no section on rent increases. It only sets how long a notice to quit must be. So the real rule is in your lease, and the fallback is 15 days. I read the Act's official text on 2026-10-19. This page shows what it says and where city rules add more.

## Does Pennsylvania Have a Cap on Rent Increases or Rent Control?

Pennsylvania state law sets no cap on rent increases. I read the full Landlord and Tenant Act of 1951 for this page. It has no section that limits how much rent may rise. The amount is a term of the lease between landlord and tenant.

Two state rules still touch rent increases:

- **Deposits.** After a tenant has lived in the unit 5 years, a rent increase does not require a higher deposit (Section 511.1(d)).
- **Tenant groups.** Section 205 bars ending or not renewing a lease because a tenant or family member joins a tenants' association.

Voucher units add one step. Under 24 CFR 982.507, the housing authority must find the new rent reasonable before any increase in the rent to owner. See the [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) for how payment standards work.

## How Many Days Notice Does a Pennsylvania Landlord Need to Raise the Rent?

Pennsylvania law sets no separate notice period for a rent increase. The lease sets it. On a month-to-month lease with no notice clause, a landlord changes the rent by ending the current terms and offering new ones. That uses the notice to quit in Section 501(b): 15 days from the date of service.

Use this step list for a month-to-month increase:

1. **Read the lease.** Look for a clause on rent changes or notice periods. If it sets 30 or 60 days, use that.
2. **Pick the effective date.** Start the new rent on the first day of a rental period.
3. **Count back.** Give at least 15 days, or the lease period if longer. Many managers give 30 days so the tenant can plan.
4. **Write the notice.** State the old rent, the new rent and the start date.
5. **Serve it.** Hand it over, leave it at the main building, or post it on the unit (Section 501(f)).
6. **Keep proof.** Save a dated photo or a signed copy.

A worked example: rent rises from $1,200 to $1,300 on December 1. A 15-day notice must be served by November 16. A 30-day notice must be served by November 1. The increase is $100 a month, or 8.3%.

## Can a Pennsylvania Landlord Raise the Rent During a Fixed-Term Lease?

Only if the lease allows it. The Act gives a landlord no right to change the rent in the middle of a fixed term. Until the term ends, the signed lease controls.

At the end of the term, the landlord may offer a renewal at a new rent. If the tenant must leave, Section 501(b) sets the notice to quit. It is 15 days for a lease of 1 year or less and 30 days for a longer lease. Section 501(e) lets the lease set a shorter period or waive the notice.

## How Much Notice Is Needed to End a Month-to-Month Tenancy in Pennsylvania?

A Pennsylvania landlord gives 15 days' notice to end a month-to-month tenancy. Section 501(b) applies the 15-day period to a lease "for an indeterminate time." A lease of 1 year or less gets the same period. The notice runs from the date of service.

| Lease type | Notice to quit | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Month-to-month or no fixed term | 15 days | Section 501(b) |
| Fixed term of 1 year or less | 15 days | Section 501(b) |
| Fixed term over 1 year | 30 days | Section 501(b) |
| Unpaid rent, any lease | 10 days | Section 501(b) |
| Mobile home park space | Different periods | Section 501(c) |

If the tenant stays after the notice ends, the landlord files with the magisterial district judge. The court rules for that step are in 246 Pa. Code Chapter 500.

## Do Cities in Pennsylvania Add Their Own Rent or Notice Rules?

Yes. Philadelphia adds the most. Its Fair Housing Commission enforces Philadelphia Code Chapter 9-804 on unfair rental practices. For leases of less than 1 year, including month-to-month, the Commission says:

- **Good cause.** A landlord may not end or refuse to renew without a good cause reason.
- **Notice.** The landlord gives at least 30 days' written notice with the reasons, by hand or by first-class mail with proof of mailing.
- **Missed notice.** If the notice is not given as required, the lease renews month to month.
- **Rent increases.** A tenant's refusal of a reasonable rent increase can be good cause. The landlord must give notice and a chance to respond.
- **Code citations.** A landlord may not answer a code citation by raising the tenant's rent.

Other Pennsylvania cities may have rental license or inspection rules. Check the city code before you send a notice. If you run units in several cities, a [property manager](/property-management) can track each rule for you.

## Which has higher intent, Google Ads or Facebook Ads?

Google Ads has higher intent for owner leads. A Google search ad shows to an owner who types a need, such as "property manager Tampa". A Facebook ad shows to a person who scrolls a feed and did not ask for anything.

| Factor | Google Ads (search) | Facebook Ads (Meta) |
|---|---|---|
| Who sees the ad | People who search a keyword now | People who match an audience |
| Owner intent | High: the owner has the problem today | Low to medium: the owner may need help later |
| Best use | Capture owners ready to switch or hire | Build awareness, remarketing, owner content |
| Housing rules | Property management is listed as not in scope, but the landing page counts | Rental and real estate service ads go in the Housing category |
| Avg. cost per click | $3.94, property management search (LocaliQ 2026) | $1.27, real estate lead campaigns (WordStream 2026) |
| Avg. cost per lead | $76.71, property management search (LocaliQ 2026) | $13.74, real estate lead campaigns (WordStream 2026) |

A note on the numbers. LocaliQ's figures come from nearly 900 real estate search campaigns from April 2025 to March 2026. WordStream's figures come from over 1,800 Facebook campaigns over the past year. Neither study splits owner leads from tenant leads. WordStream's "Real Estate" group is wider than property management.

## Which costs less per lead?

Facebook Ads cost less per lead. WordStream's 2026 report puts the average real estate lead on Facebook at $13.74. LocaliQ's 2026 report puts the average property management lead on Google search at $76.71.

LocaliQ also shows property management search ads at a 6.66% click-through rate and a 10.24% conversion rate. Its property management cost per lead fell 13.92% year over year, the biggest drop of the real estate groups it tracks.

But cost per lead is the wrong finish line. Count cost per signed owner. Here is a worked example with the averages above and two assumed close rates. The close rates are a hypothesis: use your own CRM numbers.

| Step | Google Ads | Facebook Ads |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly budget | $1,500 | $1,500 |
| Avg. cost per lead | $76.71 | $13.74 |
| Leads per month | 19.6 | 109.2 |
| Assumed close rate (hypothesis) | 10% | 1% |
| Signed owners per month | 1.96 | 1.09 |
| Cost per signed owner | $767 | $1,374 |

The math: budget divided by cost per lead gives leads. Leads times close rate gives owners. If your Facebook close rate is 2% instead of 1%, the cost per signed owner drops to about $687. The answer turns on your close rate, not on the ad price.

## Which closes better?

Search leads usually close better, because the owner asked first. No public study we found gives a close rate by ad platform for property management. So track it yourself.

Goodjuju's 2026 study gives the best public view of paid channels for owner leads. Across 35 PM companies in fall 2026, paid search gave 1.2% of owner leads and social media gave 0.8%. Google Search and the Map Pack gave 50.6% (Goodjuju, 2026-10). The study warns that only 6 of the 35 companies ran paid search. It also counts only form leads on main websites, not calls or Facebook lead forms. So do not read it as "paid ads fail". Read it as "most owner leads in this sample came from Google organic and Maps".

To compare platforms fairly:

1. **Tag every lead with its source.** Use UTM tags on ad links and a source field in your CRM.
2. **Count calls.** Use call tracking numbers for each platform. Many owners call instead of filling a form.
3. **Wait 90 days.** Owners take time to decide. Judge each platform on signed owners after 90 days, not leads in week one.
4. **Compare cost per signed owner.** Divide spend by owners signed, per platform.

## What targeting rules apply to each platform?

Both platforms limit targeting for housing ads in the US. The rules differ, so read each vendor's page.

- **Meta.** Meta's housing category covers ads that promote a housing opportunity or related service. Its examples include rental listings and "real estate and house hunting services". For housing ads, audiences must include all genders, ages are fixed at 18 to 65+, and ZIP code targeting is not available. A city or pin audience in the US gets a 15-mile radius. Lookalike and saved audiences are limited (Meta Business Help).
- **Google.** Google's housing policy bars targeting by gender, age, parental status, marital status and ZIP code in the US and Canada. Radius targeting needs at least 1 km. Google's FAQ lists "property management services" as an example that may not have targeting restricted. But Google reviews the landing page too. If a large part of the page is rental listings, the ad can be flagged.

What to do: send owner ads to an owner page, not to your rental listings. On Meta, choose the Housing category for any ad that promotes rentals. If you are unsure, choose it. Never target or exclude people by a protected class.

## When should a property manager run both?

Run both when Google search ads already bring signed owners at a cost you accept. Then add Facebook Ads for two jobs search cannot do.

| Your situation | Start with | Add |
|---|---|---|
| Under 100 doors, small budget | Google Business Profile and organic search | Google search ads on 5 to 10 owner keywords |
| Search ads sign owners at a cost you accept | Keep Google search ads | Facebook remarketing to owner page visitors |
| Many accidental landlords in your area | Google search ads | Facebook content for owners who may hire later |
| No CRM source tracking | Fix tracking first | Nothing until you can count signed owners |

Remarketing means you show ads to people who already visited your owner pages. These people know your name, so the lead is warmer than a cold feed audience.

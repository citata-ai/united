## What is a door in property management?

A door is one rentable unit under management. Count one door for each home that has its own lease and its own front door.

| Property | Doors |
|---|---|
| Single-family house | 1 |
| Duplex | 2 |
| House with a rented accessory dwelling unit | 2 |
| Fourplex | 4 |
| 24-unit apartment building | 24 |
| 10 condos owned by 10 different owners | 10 |

Doors are not the same as buildings, owners or leases. One owner can have 30 doors. One building can have 200. One door can have a new lease every year. Doors is the stable count.

Software vendors often say "units" for the same thing. AppFolio, for example, prices its plans "per unit" per month on its pricing page. AppFolio also reported 9.1 million total units under management on its platform in its third quarter 2025 results, up 7% year over year.

## Why do property management companies count doors?

Property management companies count doors because almost every cost and every dollar of revenue scales with units. Doors is the one number that ties sales, staffing, software and value together.

- **Revenue.** Management fees are a percent of rent or a flat fee per unit. iPropertyManagement's fee study (updated September 23, 2026) found an average of 8.49% of collected rent, or $101.04 per unit per month for flat-fee plans.
- **Software cost.** Many property management systems charge per unit. AppFolio Core starts at $1.49 per unit per month, with a 50-unit minimum.
- **Staffing.** Work orders, renewals and calls come from units. More doors means more staff hours.
- **Company size.** Door count and fee per door give a quick, fair way to compare 2 companies.

Worked example: you manage 180 doors at an average rent of $1,700 and an 8% fee. Monthly fee revenue is 180 × $1,700 × 0.08 = $24,480. That is $136 per door per month. At $1.49 per unit, software at list price is at least $268 a month, about 1.1% of fee revenue, before any minimum spend.

## How do you count doors in edge cases?

You count doors in edge cases by asking one question: does this space have its own lease and its own rent? If yes, it is a door. Write your rule down, so every report and every quote uses the same count.

| Case | Count as a door? |
|---|---|
| Vacant unit you manage | Yes, it is still under management |
| Room rented by the bed, with separate leases | Count each lease, and say so in reports |
| Garage or parking space leased on its own | Track it apart, not as a door |
| Commercial space in a mixed-use building | Track it apart, as commercial units |
| HOA homes you serve for the association | Track as association units, not rental doors |

Watch for: quotes from software vendors. Ask how they count units before you sign, because a per-unit price depends on that count.

## How many doors does a property manager handle?

The number of doors a property manager handles depends on property type, service level and support staff. There is no legal or industry standard number.

One data point: the National Association of Realtors (NAR) 2018 Member Profile reported that "the typical property manager managed 35 properties in 2017, down from 42 properties in 2016." That figure covers NAR members who do property management and counts properties, not doors. A multifamily property can hold many doors.

For the whole field, the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics counts about 460,400 jobs for property, real estate and community association managers in 2025, with a median pay of $69,990 in May 2025. That group includes commercial and HOA managers too.

## How many doors per employee is normal?

Doors per employee has no single normal value, so the useful benchmark is your own trend. The ratio depends on 4 things.

| Factor | Pushes doors per employee up | Pushes it down |
|---|---|---|
| Property type | Large apartment buildings on one site | Scattered single-family homes |
| Service level | Owner pays for basics only | Full service, frequent inspections |
| Outsourcing | Leasing, maintenance or calls outsourced | All in-house |
| Software and process | Online payments, self-showing, portals | Paper, phone and manual steps |

How to measure it:

1. **Count doors** on the first day of each month.
2. **Count full-time equivalent staff**, including owners who work in the business. Two half-time staff count as 1.
3. **Divide** doors by staff. 180 doors and 4 staff is 45 doors per staff member.
4. **Track it each quarter** with 2 quality checks: average days to close a work order and owner churn.

Watch for: a rising ratio is good only if service holds. If work orders take longer and owners leave, the ratio grew too fast.

## How do property management companies grow door count?

Property management companies grow door count in 4 ways. Each has a different cost and speed.

1. **Win new owners.** Referrals, reviews, local search and now AI answers. Owners who ask ChatGPT or Gemini for a manager get a short list of names.
2. **Grow with current owners.** Owners who buy more rentals add doors with no sales cost.
3. **Buy a book of business.** Buying a smaller company adds doors fast but brings owner churn risk during the handover.
4. **Keep the doors you have.** Losing 15 doors a year means you must win 15 just to stay even.

Worked example: you start the year with 180 doors. You lose 10% of doors to owner sales and churn (18 doors). To reach 220 doors by year end, you need 58 new doors, not 40.

## What is tenant turnover rate?

Tenant turnover rate is the share of a portfolio's units where the tenant moved out during a period. It tells an owner how often they pay the cost of a vacancy. It is one of the most useful numbers a property manager can report, because it ties service quality to money.

Turnover is the opposite of retention. If 30% of tenants move out in a year, 70% of units kept the same tenant.

## How do you calculate turnover rate?

You calculate turnover rate by dividing move-outs in a period by the average number of occupied units, then multiplying by 100.

**Turnover rate = move-outs ÷ average occupied units × 100**

Worked example: you manage 120 units. Over 12 months, 33 tenants moved out. Average occupied units across the year were 114.

33 ÷ 114 × 100 = **28.9%** turnover.

Decide these points once and write them down:

| Question | Pick one rule |
|---|---|
| Do transfers count? | Decide if a tenant who moves to another of your units counts as a move-out |
| Do evictions count? | Usually yes; track them as a separate line too |
| Which units? | Occupied units, or all units including vacant ones |
| Which period? | Calendar year, trailing 12 months, or annualized quarter |

Large owners report both gross and net turnover. Camden Property Trust's fourth quarter 2025 release shows annualized gross turnover of 40% and net turnover of 30% for its same-property portfolio. The release does not define the 2 terms. So when you compare with any benchmark, check how it counts.

## What is a normal turnover rate?

A normal turnover rate depends on property type, market and season, and public data mostly comes from large apartment owners. Here are 2 recent data points.

| Source | Measure | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Camden Property Trust, Q4 2025 (58,759 apartment homes) | Annualized net turnover | 30% (31% in Q4 2024) |
| Camden Property Trust, Q4 2025 | Annualized gross turnover | 40% (41% in Q4 2024) |
| RealPage via LeaseLock, 2024 | Share of renters who renewed at lease expiration | 54.6% |
| RealPage via LeaseLock, Q1 2025 (preliminary) | Same measure | 55.1% |

The 2 sources measure different things. Camden reports move-outs against units. RealPage reports renewals against expiring leases. A 54.6% renewal rate means about 45% of expiring leases ended in a move-out, but not every lease expires each year.

Watch for: seasonal swings. Turnover can change a lot from one quarter to the next. Compare a quarter with the same quarter last year, as Camden does.

## What is a good turnover rate for single-family rentals?

A good turnover rate for single-family rentals is one that falls year over year in your own portfolio. We did not find a verified public benchmark only for single-family homes managed by small and mid-size companies.

How to set your own benchmark:

1. **Measure last year** with the formula above.
2. **Split it by cause:** tenant moved for a job or a purchase, rent increase, service issue, eviction, owner sale.
3. **Target the causes you control:** service issues and badly priced renewals.
4. **Compare like with like:** single-family with single-family, same city, same year.

## What does a tenant turnover cost the owner?

A tenant turnover costs the owner lost rent, make-ready work, a leasing fee and marketing. There is no single verified national average, so build the cost from your own numbers.

Worked example for one turnover on an $1,800 a month rental:

| Cost line | Assumption | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Lost rent | 21 vacant days at $59 a day ($1,800 × 12 ÷ 365) | $1,243 |
| Make-ready | Paint touch-up, deep clean, carpet clean, rekey | $900 |
| Leasing fee | 70.6% of one month's rent (iPropertyManagement average with management) | $1,271 |
| Utilities while vacant | 3 weeks | $96 |
| **Total** | | **$3,510** |

That is almost 2 months of rent for a single move-out. The leasing fee average comes from iPropertyManagement's fee study, updated September 23, 2026. The other lines are example inputs; replace them with your own.

Now scale it. A 100-unit portfolio at 40% turnover has 40 turns a year. At $3,510 each, that is $140,400. Cutting turnover to 30% saves 10 turns, or $35,100 a year for the owners.

## How do property managers reduce turnover?

Property managers reduce turnover by removing the reasons tenants leave that the manager controls. The main levers:

1. **Fix repairs fast.** Track time to first response and time to close. Repair speed is a cause the manager controls.
2. **Start renewals early.** Send the renewal offer 90 to 120 days before the lease ends. It gives tenants time to decide, and gives you time to market if they leave.
3. **Price renewals with data.** Compare the renewal increase with the cost of a turn. A $50 a month smaller increase is $600 a year, far below a $3,510 turnover.
4. **Screen with a clear, fair policy.** A written, consistent policy places tenants who fit the unit and its rules.
5. **Ask why people leave.** A 3-question move-out survey shows the real causes.
6. **Keep up the property.** Preventive maintenance stops the slow problems that wear tenants down.

Owners who compare managers often ask about retention. If you publish your turnover rate and how you measure it, AI answers can quote a clear number.

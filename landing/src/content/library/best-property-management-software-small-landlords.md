## Which tools fit a landlord with a small portfolio?

Avail and TurboTenant deserve a first look when you manage your own rentals. Compare the feature and price conditions rather than assuming free means costless.

| Product or plan | Published software price | Useful work | Cost question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Avail Unlimited | $0 | Listings, applications, leases and rent | Payment and screening charges |
| Avail Unlimited Plus | $9 per unit monthly | Faster payment and customization tools | Total at your unit count |
| TurboTenant Free | $0 monthly | Marketing, screening and rent collection | Renter fees and paid tools |
| TurboTenant Essentials | From $149 annually | Added lease tools | Portfolio price band |
| TurboTenant Pro | From $199 annually | Built-in accounting and added tools | Portfolio price band |
| Buildium Essential | From $62 monthly | Broader property management functions | Full quote and usage |

Sources: [Avail](https://www.avail.com/pricing), [TurboTenant](https://www.turbotenant.com/pricing/) and [Buildium](https://www.buildium.com/pricing/), read 2026-10-19.

Buildium may be worth testing when your work goes beyond a landlord's rent-and-lease process. Do not assume the cheapest landlord tool meets a management company's owner-fund needs.

## Which tools are free?

Avail Unlimited and TurboTenant Free publish ongoing free software offers. Separate software access from paid transactions and optional services.

Avail's pricing page describes listings, screening requests, leases, online rent collection, maintenance and income tracking in its free plan. Its paid plan adds specific speed and customization features.

TurboTenant's pricing page describes a free plan for marketing, screening and online rent collection. It presents Essentials and Pro as paid choices.

A useful free-plan test follows a whole tenancy. Create a listing, receive an application, store a signed agreement and record rent. Then enter a repair and download records.

Ask what changes when you stop using the product. Open the export before relying on the system as your only record store.

Free software can be a sensible fit. The decision depends on whether the included work meets your needs and the charges remain clear to everyone involved.

## Which tools charge per unit and which use bands?

Avail's paid plan uses a per-unit monthly price; TurboTenant publishes annual portfolio bands. Compare the actual total at your size.

Avail Unlimited Plus lists $9 per unit monthly. A worked example with 10 units produces $90 monthly before any other costs. This calculation uses Avail's pricing page read 2026-10-19.

TurboTenant lists Essentials at $149 annually and Pro at $199 annually for its 1–10-unit band. Its next published band has different annual amounts. Do not assume a single flat price covers every portfolio.

Keep annual cash paid separate from a monthly equivalent. A monthly equivalent is a budgeting division; it does not prove monthly billing is available.

Ask what happens when you add a unit midterm. Confirm upgrade timing, prorating and renewal price. Keep any promotion separate from the normal rate.

A unit-rate offer can suit a tiny portfolio. A banded plan can suit another size. Use your actual count rather than a broad cheapest label.

## Which has the best mobile app?

No mobile winner follows from a pricing page alone. Test each candidate on your own phone with the work you perform away from a desk.

Try uploading a repair photo, reading a tenant ledger and finding a signed lease. Check whether you can locate the correct property quickly. Test a weak connection as well as normal office service.

Use a mobile checklist:

- **Access:** login and account recovery work clearly.
- **Documents:** lease files open and remain readable.
- **Maintenance:** photos attach to the correct request.
- **Messages:** the full exchange stays with the tenant.
- **Money:** payment status is clear without unsafe workarounds.
- **Export:** you know which records need a desktop.

A phone-friendly browser and a dedicated app are different experiences. Confirm what the vendor offers and what features work in each.

If you often need a desktop, make sure that fits your day. A good phone demo should reflect the actual task, not just a dashboard.

## Which include screening and leases?

Avail and TurboTenant describe application, screening and lease-related tools, but package scope and fees differ. Verify a complete applicant-to-lease workflow.

Ask which reports are included in screening. A score, full credit report and income check are different outputs. Confirm who pays and when the charge appears.

Use sample records rather than a real applicant during evaluation. Check how the landlord views results and stores the decision record. Avoid sharing private reports through ordinary messages.

For leases, test the template, edits, signatures and final download. A state-specific template does not establish that every clause fits your property or agreement.

Keep a locally reviewed agreement when needed. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Do not ask software to steer applicants by protected traits. Use consistent, approved rental criteria and have a person review unusual cases.

## Which tools help organize records for taxes?

Choose a tool that exports clear income and expense records your tax professional can use. A tax-season label does not prove the records meet your needs.

Avail lists income and expense tracking. TurboTenant's Pro tier describes built-in accounting. Ask your tax professional what detail and format they want before you choose.

Test a rent payment, repair bill, refund and owner contribution. Check that supporting files remain linked. Separate transfers from income so the exported report stays understandable.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

If you later manage money for other owners, review accounting requirements again. Landlord expense tracking and professional trust accounting solve different problems.

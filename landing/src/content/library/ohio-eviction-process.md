## Ohio eviction rules at a glance

Ohio calls an eviction case a "forcible entry and detainer" action. Chapter 1923 of the Ohio Revised Code sets the court steps. Chapter 5321 sets the landlord and tenant duties. Here is the core of both.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice to leave before filing | 3 or more days | 1923.04(A) |
| Notice to fix a health or safety breach | Lease ends 30+ days after receipt | 5321.11 |
| End month-to-month with no cause | 30+ days before the rental date | 5321.17(B) |
| Courts that hear the case | County, municipal or common pleas | 1923.01(A) |
| Time limit to file | 2 years after the cause arises | 1923.01(B) |
| Summons served before trial | At least 7 days | 1923.06(A) |
| Continuance | Max 8 days, unless the parties agree or the tenant posts bond | 1923.08 |
| Writ executed | Within 10 days after the officer receives it | 1923.14(A) |
| Self-help (lockout, utility shutoff) | Illegal | 5321.15 |
| Retaliatory eviction | Illegal; a defense for the tenant | 5321.02 |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What notice must an Ohio landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

An Ohio landlord must give a written notice to leave the premises 3 or more days before filing (Ohio Rev. Code 1923.04(A)). The same notice applies to an eviction for unpaid rent and to a holdover tenant.

The statute allows three ways to deliver it:

- certified mail, return receipt requested;
- handing a written copy to the tenant in person;
- leaving it at the tenant's usual home or at the rental unit.

Every residential notice must carry this text, printed or written so it stands out: "You are being asked to leave the premises. If you do not leave, an eviction action may be initiated against you. If you are in doubt regarding your legal rights and obligations as a tenant, it is recommended that you seek legal assistance."

The Franklin County clerk sheet says the filing party must make sure the forms meet Ohio law, including 1923.04. Use a notice form that quotes this text word for word.

## How long does an eviction take in Ohio?

An Ohio eviction takes about 4 to 5 weeks in a simple, uncontested case in Franklin County. That is our sum of the minimums below: 3 + 1 + 14 to 21 + 10 = 28 to 35 days. It is an estimate, not a court promise. A contested case, a jury demand or an appeal takes longer.

Here is the timeline, with the source for each step:

| Step | Minimum time | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Serve the 3-day notice to leave | 3+ days | 1923.04(A) |
| File the complaint and the notice | Day 4 or later | 1923.04(A) |
| Hearing date set by the clerk | 14 to 21 days after filing (Franklin County) | FCMC Clerk sheet, 07/2025 |
| Summons served before trial | 7+ days | 1923.06(A) |
| Continuance, if granted | Up to 8 days | 1923.08 |
| Officer executes the writ | Within 10 days of receipt | 1923.14(A) |

If the tenant does not appear, the court still tries the case as if the tenant were there (Ohio Rev. Code 1923.07). The judge enters a judgment for restitution of the premises and costs if the complaint is true (1923.09).

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Ohio, and what does it cost?

An Ohio landlord files in the county, municipal or common pleas court that covers the property (Ohio Rev. Code 1923.01(A)). Most residential cases go to the municipal court. The case must start within 2 years after the cause of action arises (1923.01(B)).

Fees differ by court. Franklin County Municipal Court in Columbus lists these on its clerk sheet, revised July 2025:

- $123 for 1 cause of action (eviction only);
- $160 for 2 causes of action (eviction plus a money claim);
- more for over 3 defendants or extra service types.

That court also says only the deeded owner may file without an attorney. LLCs, corporations and trusts need an attorney at every stage. A property manager cannot file in the owner's place without one. Check your own court's clerk page, because rules and fees differ.

You can join a claim for back rent and damages to the eviction trial (Ohio Rev. Code 1923.081). The tenant can counterclaim, and the court may order the tenant to pay rent into court while the case runs (1923.061(B)).

## Can an Ohio landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

An Ohio landlord can evict for a lease violation, and can end some tenancies with no cause. The path depends on the reason.

1. **Health and safety breach.** If a tenant breaks a duty in 5321.05 that materially affects health and safety, give a written notice under 5321.11. It names the problem and ends the lease at least 30 days after receipt, unless the tenant fixes it.
2. **Drug activity.** Under 5321.17(C), a landlord with actual knowledge or reasonable cause must end the tenancy on 3 days' notice.
3. **No cause, month-to-month.** Give notice at least 30 days before the periodic rental date (5321.17(B)). Week-to-week needs 7 days (5321.17(A)).
4. **Holdover.** A tenant who stays after the term ends is subject to an eviction case (1923.02(A)(1)).

After any of these, you still serve the 3-day notice to leave before you file.

Watch for retaliation. Ohio Rev. Code 5321.02 bars an eviction because a tenant complained about code violations or joined a tenant group. The tenant can raise it as a defense.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Ohio?

A court officer removes the tenant, never the landlord. After a judgment for restitution, the court issues a writ of execution at the landlord's request (Ohio Rev. Code 1923.13). The writ goes to a sheriff, police officer, constable or court bailiff.

The officer must execute the writ within 10 days after receiving it (1923.14(A)). If the tenant appeals, gets a stay and files any required bond, the judge orders the officer to stop.

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Ohio?

No. A self-help eviction is illegal in Ohio. Ohio Rev. Code 5321.15(A) bars any act to recover possession outside the court process. The statute names "termination of utilities or services, exclusion from the premises, or threat of any unlawful act."

Section 5321.15(B) also bars seizing a tenant's furniture or other things to collect rent without a court order. A landlord who breaks either rule is liable for all damages plus reasonable attorney fees (5321.15(C)).

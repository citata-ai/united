Florida gives landlords wide room on rent. I read Chapter 83 and the two local price-control sections in the 2026 Florida Statutes on 2026-10-19. The short version: no cap, no local rent control, and a 30-day notice window for month-to-month units.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| State cap on rent increases | None | No section in Chapter 83 |
| Local rent control | Barred for counties, cities and other local bodies | § 125.0103(2), § 166.043(2) |
| Local rules on notice, fees and lease terms | Preempted to the state | § 83.425 |
| Separate rent increase notice | None in Chapter 83 | Chapter 83, Part II |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | At least 30 days before the end of a monthly period | § 83.57(3) |
| End a week-to-week tenancy | At least 7 days before the end of a weekly period | § 83.57(4) |
| End a quarter-to-quarter tenancy | At least 30 days before the end of the quarter | § 83.57(2) |
| End a year-to-year tenancy | At least 60 days before the end of the year | § 83.57(1) |
| Lease clause on notice before a fixed term ends | Between 30 and 60 days | § 83.575(1) |
| Retaliatory rent increase | Banned | § 83.64 |

## Does Florida have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

Florida has no cap on rent increases and no rent control. The state sets no limit in Chapter 83. The legislature also blocks local limits in two parallel sections:

- **Counties:** Fla. Stat. § 125.0103(2).
- **Cities:** Fla. Stat. § 166.043(2).

Both say a municipality, county or other local government "may not adopt or maintain in effect any law, ordinance, rule, or other measure that would have the effect of imposing controls on rents". The history lines show both sections were last amended in 2023 and 2024.

Local governments keep one tool. Subsection (3) of each section lets them use land-use measures, such as inclusionary housing ordinances, to add affordable housing.

## How many days notice does a Florida landlord need to raise the rent?

Florida law sets no separate notice period for a rent increase. I checked the section list of Part II of Chapter 83, and no section deals with rent increase notice.

What does apply is the notice to end a tenancy without a specific term, in § 83.57:

| Tenancy | Minimum notice | Before |
|---|---|---|
| Week to week | 7 days | End of a weekly period |
| Month to month | 30 days | End of a monthly period |
| Quarter to quarter | 30 days | End of a quarterly period |
| Year to year | 60 days | End of an annual period |

Most Florida landlords time a rent change to this window. The tenant then has a choice: accept the new rent or leave at the end of the period. Section 83.57 requires written notice, delivered as § 83.56(4) allows: mail, hand delivery, e-mail under § 83.505, or a copy left at the unit if the tenant is absent.

Worked example: a month-to-month tenant pays on the 1st. To start new rent on July 1, deliver written notice no later than May 31. That gives at least 30 days before the June period ends on June 30.

## Can a Florida landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

A Florida landlord can raise the rent during a fixed-term lease only if the lease allows it. Chapter 83 gives no power to change the rent mid-term. The rent in the lease binds both sides until the term ends.

At the end of the term, the landlord may offer a renewal at a new rent. Section 83.575 controls the notice clauses in fixed-term leases:

- A lease may require notice before the end of the term, but not less than 30 days or more than 60 days, from either side.
- If the lease requires the tenant to give notice, it must also require the landlord to tell the tenant within the same period if the lease will not renew.
- A tenant who stays with permission after the term ends without notice under § 83.57(3) owes 1 extra month's rent (§ 83.575(3)).

If you want room to raise rent mid-term, write the clause into the lease. Many owners prefer 12-month terms with a renewal offer instead.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Florida?

A Florida month-to-month tenancy ends with written notice at least 30 days before the end of a monthly period (§ 83.57(3)). Either the landlord or the tenant can give it. The section history shows its last amendment in chapter 2023-314.

Section 83.46(2) decides the tenancy type when the lease says nothing about duration. If rent is paid monthly, the tenancy is month to month.

Florida has no just-cause rule, so the landlord does not need a reason. One limit applies. Under § 83.64, a landlord may not raise rent in a discriminatory way, or end a tenancy, mainly to retaliate. Examples include a tenant's good-faith complaint to a code agency, joining a tenant group, or using fair housing rights.

## Do cities in Florida add their own rent or notice rules?

Florida cities cannot add their own rent or notice rules. Section 83.425 preempts to the state the "regulation of residential tenancies" and all matters in Part II. The section names, among others:

- Security deposits.
- Application fees and screening.
- Terms and conditions of rental agreements.
- Fees charged by the landlord.
- Disclosures and notice requirements.

Together with §§ 125.0103 and 166.043, this means one statewide rulebook for rent and notice. A rule you see in an old county ordinance may no longer apply. The section history shows § 83.425 came in 2023 (chapter 2023-314).

## Rent increase checklist for Florida managers

1. **Check the lease type.** Fixed term or month to month.
2. **Read the lease clause** on rent changes and renewal notice.
3. **Count back from the end of the rent period.** 30 days for monthly, 60 for yearly.
4. **Deliver in writing** in a way § 83.56(4) allows.
5. **Keep proof** of the delivery date.
6. **Apply the same rule** to every tenant in the same position, to stay clear of § 83.64 and fair housing risk.

## Why should a property manager set up Bing Places?

A property manager should set up Bing Places because Bing data reaches more AI tools than Bing search alone. OpenAI's help page for ChatGPT search says it "sometimes partners with other search providers". The same page links the Microsoft privacy statement as one of those providers. So a query an owner types into ChatGPT can go to Microsoft search.

Microsoft's own blog adds one more link. When it launched the new Bing Places on October 3, 2025, it named "deeper integrations with Bing Maps and Copilot" as a goal.

Watch for: OpenAI does not say that ChatGPT reads Bing Places listings. Local Falcon, a local SEO tool, calls Bing Places "one of the biggest data sources" for ChatGPT. Its post gives no numbers. Treat the link as likely, not proven.

## How do you claim a Bing Places listing?

You claim a Bing Places listing at bing.com/forbusiness. Microsoft moved the tool there from bingplaces.com in October 2025. Current users and their listings moved to the new account on their own.

The steps below come from BrightLocal's guide. That guide dates from 2020, before the redesign, so screen names may differ now.

1. **Sign in** with a Microsoft account.
2. **Search for your business** by phone number, or by name and city.
3. **Claim or create.** Click "Claim Business" if the listing exists. Otherwise, create a new one.
4. **Check name, address and phone.** You cannot edit them until you verify. Bing does not accept a PO Box address.
5. **Place the map pin** on your office.
6. **Pick a category** from Bing's preset list and write a description.
7. **Choose whether to show your address.** Show it only if owners can visit your office.
8. **Add contact details, social links, photos and hours.**
9. **Verify.** Methods include phone, website, email or postcard. Bing decides which methods you see. Email must match your website domain. A postcard takes 5 to 6 business days.
10. **Wait for publishing.** BrightLocal gives 7 to 14 days after verification.

## Can you import from Google Business Profile?

Yes, you can import a verified Google Business Profile into Bing Places, and for most property managers it is the fastest way. Microsoft's October 2025 post says the new import flow is "faster, more reliable, and better at preserving key attributes like business name, hours, and contact details."

BrightLocal's older guide describes the flow this way: choose the Google import, sign in to Google, pick the locations and choose a sync interval (weekly, every 2 weeks or monthly). It says the import skips Bing's own verification when your Google listings are verified.

After the import, check every field. An import copies errors too. If your Google profile has an old phone number, Bing gets it.

| Path | Best for | Verification | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Import from Google | You have a verified Google Business Profile | Usually skipped (BrightLocal) | Copies Google errors; check the sync setting |
| Claim existing listing | Bing already shows your company | Phone, website, email or postcard | Old data from the web; fix name and phone first |
| Create new listing | Bing shows nothing for you | Phone, website, email or postcard | Duplicate listings if one already exists under an old name |

## Which categories fit a property management company?

Pick the category that reads "property management" or the closest one in Bing's preset list. Then make it match the primary category on your Google Business Profile. We could not confirm Bing's full category list from a public Microsoft page, so check the list in your dashboard.

Rules that hold on every platform:

- **One primary category** that says what owners hire you for.
- **Extra categories** only for services you sell, such as real estate if you also sell homes.
- **The same company name everywhere.** No city or keyword added to the name.
- **The same service area** as on your website and Google.

## Does Bing Places change your ranking in ChatGPT?

No public data shows that a Bing Places profile changes your place in a ChatGPT answer. Local Falcon, which recommends the profile, says Bing Places does not set the order. ChatGPT may take basic facts from Bing and still rank other companies higher, based on reviews and other citations.

Our own test cannot answer this either. On 2026-10-19 we asked the "best property management company in <City, ST>" question for 50 cities through the OpenAI API with web search. None of the 150 answers in that test, on any engine, linked a Bing domain (our data, 2026-10-19). A search provider usually points to the pages it found, not to itself, so this does not show that Bing data was unused. The ChatGPT engine cited Expertise.com in 30 of 50 answers, Birdeye reviews in 17 and BBB in 11.

So the honest goal for Bing Places is correct facts. If ChatGPT pulls your phone, hours or address through Microsoft, you want them right.

## What should you check after the listing goes live?

Check the listing on Bing Maps and in AI answers 2 weeks after it publishes. This checklist takes 20 minutes.

- [ ] Search your company name on Bing. The panel shows the right name, phone and website.
- [ ] Search "property management <your city>" on Bing Maps. Your pin is in the right place.
- [ ] Hours and service area match Google and your website.
- [ ] No duplicate listing under an old name or address.
- [ ] Ask ChatGPT "What is the phone number and address of <your company>?" and compare.
- [ ] Ask Microsoft Copilot the same question.
- [ ] Note the date. Repeat each quarter, or after you move or change your phone.

Bing Webmaster Tools is a separate tool for your website, not your listing. It can show how often Copilot cites your pages.

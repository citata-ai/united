## What is a triple net lease?

A triple net lease is a lease where the tenant pays base rent plus 3 property costs: taxes, insurance and maintenance. "NNN" is the short form. Each N stands for one "net", a cost that passes from the landlord to the tenant.

The landlord's income is the base rent, "net" of those 3 costs. So the landlord can plan its income with less risk. The tenant takes the risk that taxes, insurance or upkeep rise.

Tyler Cauble's commercial real estate glossary defines an NNN lease as one where "the tenant pays base rent plus their pro rata share of property taxes" and related costs. "Pro rata share" means the tenant's part of the building, often by square feet.

## What are the 3 "nets"?

The 3 nets are property taxes, building insurance and maintenance. Commercial leases often call the maintenance part common area maintenance (CAM).

| Net | What it covers | How it is billed |
|-----|----------------|------------------|
| Property taxes | Real estate tax on the land and building | Tenant's share, often monthly estimates |
| Building insurance | Property and liability insurance for the building | Tenant's share of the premium |
| Maintenance (CAM) | Common areas: parking lot, landscaping, lighting, cleaning, snow | Monthly estimates, then a yearly reconciliation |

Leases with fewer nets have their own names:

- **Single net (N).** The tenant pays base rent and property taxes.
- **Double net (NN).** The tenant pays base rent, taxes and insurance. Wikipedia notes the landlord keeps some extra maintenance duties.
- **Triple net (NNN).** The tenant pays base rent, taxes, insurance and maintenance.
- **Absolute net.** The tenant pays everything, including roof and structure. Wikipedia notes that in this form "the tenant cannot terminate the lease or seek any rent abatements under any circumstances."

## A worked NNN example

This example uses made-up numbers. A tenant leases 2,000 square feet in a 10,000-square-foot retail building. The tenant's pro rata share is 2,000 / 10,000 = 20%.

| Line | Building total per year | Tenant's 20% share |
|------|------------------------|--------------------|
| Property taxes | $40,000 | $8,000 |
| Building insurance | $10,000 | $2,000 |
| CAM | $25,000 | $5,000 |
| Total nets | $75,000 | $15,000 |
| Base rent ($18 per sq ft) | | $36,000 |
| Tenant's total cost | | $51,000 |

The tenant pays $36,000 in base rent plus $15,000 in nets, so $51,000 a year, or $4,250 a month. If taxes rise 10% next year, the tenant's tax share rises from $8,000 to $8,800. The landlord's base rent stays the same.

Most NNN leases bill the nets as monthly estimates. After the year ends, the landlord compares the estimates with the real costs and bills or credits the difference. This step is the reconciliation.

## NNN lease vs gross lease: what is the difference?

The difference is who pays the building's operating costs. In a gross lease, the landlord pays them out of one rent. In an NNN lease, the tenant pays them on top of base rent.

| Lease type | Tenant pays | Landlord pays |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| Full-service gross | Base rent | Taxes, insurance, CAM, utilities |
| Modified gross | Base rent plus some costs | The rest, as the lease says |
| Double net (NN) | Base rent, taxes, insurance | CAM, roof, structure |
| Triple net (NNN) | Base rent, taxes, insurance, CAM | Roof and structure (usually) |
| Absolute NNN | Everything | Nothing |

Source: Tyler Cauble's lease type table. Terms differ by market and by lease, so the lease text decides.

Trade-off in one line: an NNN lease usually has a lower base rent but a less certain total cost for the tenant. A gross lease is easier for the tenant to budget but usually has a higher rent.

## Are NNN leases used for residential property?

NNN leases are rare in residential rentals. A residential tenant normally pays one monthly rent, sometimes plus utilities. The owner pays property taxes and building insurance and handles most repairs.

Where you may see NNN in a residential portfolio:

- **Mixed-use buildings.** The ground-floor shop may be on an NNN lease, while the apartments above use normal residential leases.
- **Single-tenant commercial units.** A manager who runs apartments may also manage a small retail or office unit.
- **Investment sales.** Listings for single-tenant buildings often advertise an NNN lease. PropertyMetrics notes that NNN leases are common for large single-tenant properties such as national restaurant chains.

Residential tenant protection laws differ from commercial lease rules. Before you move a cost like property tax to a residential tenant, check the law. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What risks does an owner keep in an NNN lease?

An owner keeps 4 main risks in an NNN lease, even though the tenant pays most costs.

1. **Vacancy.** When the tenant leaves, the owner pays all taxes, insurance and maintenance until a new tenant signs. Wikipedia lists the tenant ending the lease early or not renewing as the main landlord risk.
2. **Tenant credit.** The NNN income is only as good as the tenant's ability to pay. A weak tenant can fall behind on the nets after a large reconciliation bill.
3. **Roof and structure.** In a standard NNN lease, the landlord usually keeps these. Tyler Cauble warns that a lease called NNN may really be a double net, which leaves more costs with the owner.
4. **Reconciliation errors.** If the owner does not track and audit CAM costs, the reconciliation can under-bill the tenant or start a dispute.

Owner checklist before you sign or buy an NNN lease:

- **Read the repair clause.** Who pays for the roof, structure, parking lot and HVAC?
- **Check the cap.** Does the lease cap CAM increases? A cap moves risk back to the owner.
- **Check the reconciliation rules.** When is the yearly statement due? Can the tenant audit it?
- **Check the tenant.** Ask for financial statements or a credit report, and check the remaining term.

## How a property manager handles an NNN lease

A property manager handles the billing and paperwork that make an NNN lease work. Typical tasks:

- Send monthly invoices for base rent and estimated nets.
- Track each tax bill, insurance premium and CAM cost.
- Prepare the yearly reconciliation and send it to each tenant.
- Answer tenant audit requests.

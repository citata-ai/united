## Why do backlinks matter for a property management company?

Backlinks matter because Google uses them in local ranking. Google's Business Profile help names 3 local factors: relevance, distance and prominence. It says prominence is based in part on how many websites link to your business. So a link from a local site is a vote that you are a known business in that area.

Links also send visits. Goodjuju Marketing's fall 2026 study of 35 PM companies found referral traffic gave 3.7% of owner leads from 3.0% of traffic. Goodjuju says directories and partner sites send pre-qualified visitors. A good local link can rank you and send an owner.

| Link value | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Ranking | Adds to prominence | Chamber member page links to you |
| Visits | Sends owners who click | Real estate agent's "trusted partners" page |
| Trust | Confirms you are real | NARPM member directory |
| AI answers | Gives AI engines a third-party source | Local news story names your company |

## Which local links matter most for property management?

The best local links come from places owners already trust. Rank each link by 3 questions: is it local, is it real, and would an owner click it? A link that passes all 3 is worth an hour of work.

Here is a starting list, ordered by effort:

1. **Your own partners.** Real estate agents, lenders, title companies, insurance agents and attorneys who refer owners. Ask for a listing on their partner or resources page.
2. **Your chamber of commerce.** Ask if the member directory links to each member's website.
3. **NARPM and state associations.** NARPM runs searchable directories of members and chapters.
4. **Local sponsorships.** A youth sports team, a school event or a charity run often thanks sponsors with a link.
5. **Local news and blogs.** Offer a rent data quote or a local rental market comment.
6. **Vendor pages.** Plumbers, roofers and cleaners you use may list clients or partners.
7. **Helpful tools and guides.** A local rent guide or a fee table that other sites cite on their own.

Watch for: a "local" directory you have never heard of with 5,000 cities and no traffic. Google lists low-quality directory links as link spam.

## Do NARPM and chamber links help local rankings?

NARPM and chamber links help because they are real, local and hard to fake. Both require membership, so they show Google and AI engines that a trade body or a local group knows you.

Their ranking effect on its own is modest, by our reading. A single chamber link will not lift you into the map pack. But these links are cheap if you already pay dues, and they give owners a way to check you. That trust matters more as owners ask AI engines to compare managers.

What to do:

- **Log in to each member profile.** Check that the website field has your current URL with https.
- **Match your name and phone** to your Google Business Profile.
- **Add a short owner description.** Say what you manage and where.
- **Write down each URL** in a link log so you can recheck it every 6 months.

## Does guest posting still work for property management SEO?

Guest posting for links does not work and can hurt. Google's spam policies list "links with optimized anchor text in articles, guest posts, or press releases distributed on other sites" as link spam. The same list names advertorials that pass ranking credit.

A real guest article is different. A piece for a local real estate blog on "what owners should know about the new rental registration rule" helps that blog's readers. If it carries a plain link to your site, as an author bio, that is normal. If you paid for the spot, Google says the link must be qualified.

Google explains how to qualify links:

- **rel="sponsored"** for ads and paid placements. Google prefers this value for paid links.
- **rel="ugc"** for user-generated content such as comments and forum posts.
- **rel="nofollow"** when the other values do not fit.

Google says buying and selling links for ads and sponsorship is normal. It is not a breach as long as the link is marked sponsored or nofollow.

## Which link offers are spam?

Link offers are spam when the link exists only to move rankings. Google's spam policy lists these examples:

| Offer you may get | Google's view |
|---|---|
| "$50 per link on 20 high-DA sites" | Buying links for ranking: link spam |
| "Free post if you link back to us" | Exchanging goods or services for links: link spam |
| "Link to me and I'll link to you" | Excessive link exchanges: link spam |
| "We'll build 500 links a month" | Automated link building: link spam |
| "Add our badge to your footer" | Widely spread footer or template links: link spam |
| "Submit to 300 directories" | Low-quality directory links: link spam |
| "Sponsored post, do-follow link" | Advertorial that passes ranking credit: link spam |

Google also lists a contract or terms of service that requires a link, without letting the other site qualify it. So a vendor or franchise contract that forces a followed link to your site is a risk.

## Step list: a 90-day local link plan

Use this plan if you start from zero. It fits in 2 hours a week.

1. **Week 1: Audit.** List every site that links to you now. Google Search Console shows this under Links.
2. **Week 2: Compare.** Look at the 3 managers in your local map pack. Note the local sites that link to them.
3. **Weeks 3–4: Fix member profiles.** Chamber, NARPM, state association, BBB.
4. **Weeks 5–8: Ask partners.** Email 10 agents, lenders and attorneys you already work with. Ask for a partner page listing.
5. **Weeks 9–10: Sponsor one local cause** you care about. Ask how they thank sponsors online.
6. **Weeks 11–12: Publish one local resource.** A fee table or a local rent guide that others can cite.
7. **Day 90: Recheck.** Count new linking sites and log owner leads with the "How did you first hear about us?" question.

Do not count links by the hundred. Count local sites that would send you an owner. For the site pages those links point to, use the [AI search checklist for property management websites](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website).

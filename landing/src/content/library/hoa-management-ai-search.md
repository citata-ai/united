## How big is the HOA management market?

The HOA market is large, and most of it hires professional help. The Foundation for Community Association Research (FCAR) estimates these numbers for 2025:

| Fact, 2025 | Number |
|---|---|
| Community associations in the US | 373,000 |
| Housing units in associations | 29.6 million |
| Residents in associations | 78.1 million |
| Share of US housing in associations | 35.2% |
| Community association managers | 60,000–65,000 |
| Community association management companies | 9,000–10,000 |
| Self-managed associations | 30–40% |
| Board and committee members | 2,555,000 |

Homeowners associations are about 58–63% of the total, condominiums 35–40% and cooperatives 2–4% (FCAR). So 9,000 to 10,000 firms compete for the boards that do hire a manager.

## What do boards ask AI?

Boards ask AI about cost, control and trust. A board member is a volunteer who signs off on other people's money. Those 2.5 million volunteers give 102.6 million hours a year, by FCAR's estimate.

Typical board prompts, written the way people type them:

- "Best HOA management company in [city] for a 120-home community"
- "How much does HOA management cost per door in [state]"
- "What should an HOA management contract include"
- "How to switch HOA management companies"
- "Self-managed HOA vs management company"
- "What reports should an HOA manager give the board each month"

Each prompt has a buyer stage. The first is a shortlist. The others are research before a vote. A site that answers only the first prompt misses the board in the research stage.

## Do AI answers already name HOA managers?

Yes, and often in the wrong place. We asked ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini "What is the best property management company in [city]?" We did it for the 50 largest US cities: 150 answers (our data, 2026-10-19).

| What we saw | Count |
|---|---|
| Answers that named at least one community association manager | 20 of 150 |
| Cities where that happened | 13 of 50 |
| Answers from Claude / Gemini / ChatGPT | 11 / 6 / 3 |
| Answers that named FirstService Residential | 7 |
| Answers that cited hoaunlimited.com | 8 |

The prompt asked about rental management, not HOAs. So AI engines do not always split the two services. That is a risk and a chance. A rental owner may get an HOA manager. A board may get a rental manager. Clear pages help the AI put your firm in the right answer.

## Which directories cover HOA managers?

HOA managers have their own credential directory, plus the general ones. Use the ones that fit your firm.

1. **CAMICB "Find a CMCA".** CAMICB runs the CMCA program and says it has certified over 25,000 CMCAs. List each credentialed manager and link the profile from your team page.
2. **Your local CAI chapter.** In our test, ChatGPT cited a CAI chapter site (caiaustin.org) in an Austin answer. Check your chapter's member listing.
3. **Google Business Profile.** Use your real name, your service areas and the same phone everywhere.
4. **Review sites.** Yelp and BBB were among the most cited sources in our local tests. Boards read reviews too.
5. **Niche comparison sites.** One Miami answer cited comparehoamanagers.com. Check whether a site like that covers your city.

Watch for: do not list your firm under rental management if you do not manage rentals. A wrong category sends you the wrong leads.

## Which pages do boards need?

Boards need pages that answer their vote questions with facts. Google says AI features need no special markup. Important content must be in text form, and structured data must match the visible text (Google Search Central).

Checklist for an HOA management site:

- [ ] **Service area pages,** one per county or city, with the association types you manage there.
- [ ] **A fee page** with a per-door or per-month range and what it covers.
- [ ] **A sample monthly financial report,** with fake numbers, as a page and a PDF.
- [ ] **A reserves and budget page** that explains your yearly budget process.
- [ ] **A vendor page:** how you bid, approve and pay vendors.
- [ ] **A transition page:** the steps and timeline to switch from another manager.
- [ ] **A team page** with each manager's name and credential, such as CMCA.
- [ ] **A self-managed page** that is honest about when a board does not need you.
- [ ] **An "updated" date** on each page.

The self-managed page matters. FCAR says 30–40% of associations manage themselves. A fair answer to "do we need a manager?" earns a board's trust.

## How is it different from rental management?

HOA management sells to a group, and rental management sells to one owner. That changes every page.

| Topic | Rental management | HOA management |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer | One owner or investor | A volunteer board that votes |
| Main question | "What will I net?" | "Can we trust you with our funds?" |
| Proof | Rent, vacancy, owner reviews | Financial reports, audits, credentials |
| Decision | One person decides | The board decides at a meeting |
| Directories to check | Expertise.com, Yelp, iPropertyManagement (cited most in our data) | CMCA directory, CAI chapter, reviews |

If you do both, split them. Use separate pages, separate headings and separate contact forms. Say on each page which service it covers.

## Where to start

Start with one service area and one board question. Write the fee page and the team page with credentials first. Then ask the 6 board prompts above in ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude, and log who they name.

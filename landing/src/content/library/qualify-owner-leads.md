## Why should a property manager qualify owner leads?

A property manager should qualify owner leads because not every form fill is a real owner who fits your service. The Goodjuju 2026 lead source report counted "completed lead form submissions, not necessarily qualified leads". Goodjuju also had to remove click-to-call taps, double form events and tenant maintenance forms from its lead counts before the numbers made sense.

So your lead count is not your sales pipeline. Qualifying helps you in 3 ways:

1. Your team spends time on owners who can sign.
2. Your cost per signed owner becomes clear.
3. Owners who do not fit get a fast, honest answer.

## Which questions show if an owner lead fits?

Ten questions show fit. Ask the first 4 on the form and the rest on the call.

| # | Question | What a fit looks like | Red flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Where is the property? (ZIP) | Inside your service area | Over an hour from your team |
| 2 | What type of property is it? | A type you manage now | A type you have never managed |
| 3 | How many doors? | At or above your minimum | Below your minimum, no plan to grow |
| 4 | How did you first hear about us? | Any answer, logged | None |
| 5 | What is the current rent and status? | Rent covers your fee floor | Rent too low for your minimum fee |
| 6 | Why do you want a manager now? | Clear reason: time, distance, growth | "Just checking prices" with no timeline |
| 7 | Did you have a manager before? What went wrong? | Clear, fair story | Many managers in a short time |
| 8 | What condition is the property in? | Ready to rent or a clear repair plan | Owner refuses needed safety repairs |
| 9 | Who will approve repairs, and up to what amount? | Owner accepts a repair limit | Owner wants to approve every $50 job |
| 10 | When do you want to start? | Within 90 days | No date |

Fourandhalf makes a good point about the call itself: listen far more than you talk. Ask why the owner is calling today before you ask for the address. The answers to questions 6 and 7 tell you more about fit than the address does.

## Which properties should I decline?

Decline a property when you cannot serve it well or when the owner's terms put you at risk. Common reasons:

- **Outside your area.** Long drives make showings, inspections and repairs slow and costly.
- **Below your fee floor.** If the fee does not cover your cost per door, each new door loses money.
- **Unsafe condition and no repair plan.** You will carry the tenant complaints.
- **A property type you do not know.** Short-term rentals, HOAs and commercial units need other skills.
- **An owner who asks you to discriminate.** This one is not a choice. See below.

Here is a worked example of the fee floor. The numbers are hypothetical.

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Your cost to manage one door per month | $90 |
| Your fee | 8% of collected rent |
| Rent where 8% equals your cost | $90 ÷ 0.08 = $1,125 |
| Lead's rent | $950 |
| Fee at 8% | $950 × 0.08 = $76 |
| Result | $14 per month below cost, unless you add a minimum fee |

A minimum monthly fee fixes this, for example "8% or $95, whichever is more". Check your own numbers in our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## What fair housing rules affect which owners I take?

The federal Fair Housing Act affects every owner you take on. Under 42 U.S.C. 3604, it is unlawful to refuse to rent, or to set different terms, "because of race, color, religion, sex, familial status, or national origin". Other parts of section 3604 cover disability.

Some small owners have a narrow exemption under 42 U.S.C. 3603(b). That exemption has a limit. It applies only if the owner rents "without the use in any manner" of a broker or agent. That includes anyone "in the business of selling or renting dwellings". When an owner hires you, that exemption does not cover the rental.

So decline an owner who asks you to:

- Turn away families with children.
- Rent only to people of one religion, race or national origin.
- Reject an applicant because of a disability, or refuse a reasonable accommodation.

Many states and cities add more protected classes. Illinois is one example. Under 775 ILCS 5/3-102, an owner or agent may not discriminate in a rental because of source of income. The same section adds immigration status and arrest record. Check the law where the property is.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I screen owner leads on the form?

Screen on the form with 4 to 6 short fields. Each extra field lowers the number of people who finish the form, so ask only what changes your next step.

Use these fields:

1. **Name, phone, email.** Required.
2. **Property ZIP code.** Required. It routes the lead or tells you it is out of area.
3. **Property type.** A dropdown: single-family, condo, 2 to 4 units, 5+ units, other.
4. **Number of doors.** A dropdown: 1, 2 to 4, 5 to 20, 21+.
5. **How did you first hear about us?** Required, no pre-selected answer. Goodjuju recommends naming each AI tool (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity) as its own option.
6. **Anything else?** One optional text box.

Keep tenant forms (repairs, rent payment, applications) separate from owner forms. Goodjuju found tenant forms mixed in with owner inquiries in client accounts, which made lead counts wrong.

## How do I say no to an owner lead politely?

Say no within one business day, give one reason, and offer a next step. A fast no keeps your name good with the owner, and owners talk to other owners.

A short template:

> Hi [Name], thank you for asking us about [address]. We only manage [property type] in [area], so we are not the right fit for this property. [Other company or resource] may be able to help. If you buy a property in [area] later, we would be glad to talk.

For an owner who asks you to discriminate, keep it plain:

> We follow federal and state fair housing law on every property we manage, so we cannot accept that condition. If you want to work without it, we are happy to talk.

Log the lost reason in your CRM. After 3 months, count the reasons. If most leads are out of area, your website or Google profile may show a wider area than you serve. Fix the page, not the lead.

If Section 8 owners are a fit for you, link them to our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) in your reply.

## How much should a rent late fee be?

A late fee should be high enough to make rent on time the clear choice, and low enough to pass your state's test. The state test comes first. Here are 4 state rules, from each statute.

| State | Cap or test | Grace period | Lease must state it? | Statute |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minnesota | Max 8% of the overdue rent | Not set | Yes, and when it applies | 504B.177 |
| Virginia | Lesser of 10% of periodic rent or 10% of remaining balance | Not set in this section | Yes | 55.1-1204(E) |
| Oregon | Reasonable flat fee, or a daily or 5-day fee with limits | Rent late only after the 4th day | Yes, with type, amount and dates | 90.260 |
| Arizona | "Reasonable late fee set forth in a written rental agreement" | Not set in this section | Yes | 33-1368(B) |

Other states have their own rules, and some have none. Check yours before you sign a lease.

## Flat fee or percentage?

A flat fee is simpler to explain and to defend. A percentage keeps the fee in line with rent when rent changes. Both can work if they stay under your state's cap.

**Worked example, $1,500 rent:**

| State | Max late fee | Math |
|---|---|---|
| Minnesota | $120 | $1,500 × 8% |
| Virginia, full rent unpaid | $150 | lesser of $150 (10% of rent) or $150 (10% of balance) |
| Virginia, $400 still unpaid | $40 | lesser of $150 or $40 (10% of $400) |

The Virginia rule matters when a tenant pays part of the rent. The cap drops to 10% of what is still owed.

Two Oregon options for the same rent, if $75 is a reasonable flat fee in that market:

- **Flat:** $75, once per rental period.
- **5-day:** 5% of rent ($75) for each 5-day period still late, from day 5, through that period only.

## Is a daily late fee legal?

A daily late fee is legal in some states, with tight limits. Oregon's ORS 90.260 allows a per-day fee from the 5th day of the rental period. Each day's charge may not exceed 6% of a reasonable flat fee, and it stops at the end of that rental period.

**Oregon daily example:** if a reasonable flat fee is $75, the daily cap is $75 × 6% = $4.50 a day.

In Minnesota, the total fee may not exceed 8% of the overdue rent, however you build it. A daily fee that grows past the cap breaks the rule. If your state is silent, a daily fee with no ceiling is the type most likely to be called unreasonable.

## What makes a late fee "reasonable"?

A late fee is reasonable when it fits the real cost of late rent: your time, the bank's charges, and the cost of money you did not get. Oregon's statute defines a reasonable flat amount as "the customary amount charged by landlords for that rental market." Arizona uses the word "reasonable" too, without a number.

Signs a fee may fail the test:

- It is far above what other local landlords charge.
- It grows every day with no limit.
- It is higher than the state cap.
- You charge it on top of a fee for the same late payment.

## Must the lease state the late fee?

Yes. In each of the 4 states above, the late fee must be in a written agreement. Minnesota's statute adds that "the agreement must specify when the late fee will be imposed." Oregon goes further: the lease must state the duty to pay, the type and amount, the rent due date and the date the fee is due.

Copy-ready lease clause (edit for your state):

> Rent is due on the 1st day of each month. If rent is not received in full by 11:59 p.m. on the [day] day of the month, Tenant will pay a late fee of $[amount], once per month. The late fee will not exceed the limit set by [state] law.

## Can I waive a late fee once?

Yes. You can waive a late fee, but do it in writing and say it is a one-time choice. Otherwise a tenant may argue that you changed the rule. Add a non-waiver clause to the lease that says a waiver once is not a waiver later.

Three more rules to keep the fee clean:

1. **Do not take the late fee out of the next rent payment.** Oregon bars a landlord from deducting a past late fee from a later rent payment to make that payment late.
2. **Do not serve a nonpayment notice for the fee alone.** Oregon says nonpayment of a late charge alone is not grounds to end a tenancy for nonpayment of rent. It may be grounds for a for-cause notice.
3. **Apply it the same way to everyone.** Selective waivers can raise fair housing questions.

## Late fees on subsidized tenancies

Late fees on a voucher tenant often have extra rules. Minnesota's statute says a landlord with a housing assistance payments contract must calculate a late fee only on the part of rent the tenant pays. For voucher rent levels, see our [Section 8 rent limits](/guides/section-8-rent-limits).

## Is property management an eligible LSA category?

Property management is not an eligible Local Services Ads (LSA) category in the US, based on Google's own pages. Local Services Ads are the pay-per-lead ads at the top of some Google results, with a "Google Verified" style badge. They sell only to listed categories.

We read Google's US "Getting started with Local Services Ads" page on 2026-10-19. It lists a long set of categories, from plumbers to dentists to restaurants. It has no "property management" or "property manager" entry. The only real estate entries are "Real estate services" and "Real estate lawyer". Some categories are open only in California and Florida, so the list differs by place.

Google's US screening page defines the real estate category as "Real estate agents and brokers". It says: "Real estate agents help buy, sell, or rent property." That describes a service to buyers, sellers and renters. It does not describe managing rentals for owners.

| Question | What Google's pages say (read 2026-10-19) |
|---|---|
| Property management category in the US? | Not listed |
| Nearest category | Real estate services: agents and brokers |
| Who that category serves | People who buy, sell or rent property |
| License check | Each real estate agent in the firm |
| Insurance | Professional liability insurance |
| How you pay | Per valid lead, not per click |

Watch for: agencies that say they can "get you into LSA" as a property manager. Ask them which category they will use, and check it against Google's list for your state.

## What verification does LSA need?

LSA verification is stricter than for search ads. Google screens each business before its ads go live, and the checks depend on category and place.

For real estate agents and brokers in the US, Google's screening page lists these checks:

- **Identity.** Business owner identity verification is required.
- **Background check.** A business check, for select users only. In 21 states, the license process already includes a background check, so Google does not run a separate one.
- **Business registration.** A business and business representative check, for select users only.
- **Insurance.** Professional liability insurance.
- **License.** A license check for each real estate agent in the firm.

Google's "How providers qualify" page adds that Google checks licenses when a business joins and over time. Serious or repeated bad customer feedback can lower a provider's rank or hide it.

To go live, Google's getting-started page also asks for a license or business registration, reviews (by category rules), billing setup and a budget.

## How do LSA leads differ from search ads?

LSA charges per lead, and search ads charge per click. That is the main difference, and it changes who carries the risk.

| Factor | Local Services Ads | Google search ads |
|---|---|---|
| You pay for | A valid lead: call, message or booking | A click to your site |
| Keywords | None: Google matches by category and job type | You choose keywords such as "property management company" |
| Open to property managers | No PM category in the US list | Yes |
| Landing page | Your LSA profile | Your own owner page |
| Verification | Identity, license, insurance checks | Standard advertiser checks |
| Owner targeting | Not possible for owner leads | Possible with owner keywords |

Google's "How leads work" page defines a lead. A phone lead counts when you speak with the customer, or when a missed caller stays on the line over 20 seconds during business hours. A message lead is a text, email or voicemail. A booking lead is open in the US and Canada only.

Google does not charge for some contacts:

- Missed calls under 20 seconds
- Callers who hang up at an automated menu
- Repeat contact from the same number or email within 15 days of a charged lead
- Spam and duplicates
- Unanswered calls outside your ad schedule

LSA is also changing. Google is moving LSA into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals. The first phase started in August 2026 for select US home and storefront service advertisers. Broader groups follow in late 2026 and non-US accounts in 2027. You still pay only for valid leads, and ads still show only on Google Search and Maps.

## What does an LSA lead cost?

Google does not publish a fixed LSA lead price. Its "How leads work" page says prices vary by location, job type, lead type and bidding mode. Message leads usually cost less than phone leads.

For property managers, a better comparison is the search ad price. LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks put the average property management search ad at $3.94 per click and $76.71 per lead. Those figures come from nearly 900 real estate search campaigns from April 2025 to March 2026. They do not split owner leads from tenant leads.

Here is how to set a ceiling on what any owner lead is worth to you. The fee figures are a hypothesis: use your own.

1. **Find your yearly fee per door.** Example: $1,800 rent × 8% × 12 months = $1,728.
2. **Multiply by the average doors per new owner.** Example: 2 doors gives $3,456 a year.
3. **Multiply by your close rate on owner leads.** Example: 10% gives $345.60 per lead.
4. **Keep a margin.** If you want to earn back the cost in year one with room for onboarding, pay well under that per lead.

## What should a property manager run instead?

Run the channels that already bring owner leads: Google organic search, the Map Pack and your Google Business Profile. In Goodjuju's fall 2026 study of 35 PM companies, Google Search and the Map Pack gave 50.6% of owner leads. Direct and Maps gave 35.6%. Paid search gave 1.2% (Goodjuju, 2026-10). The study notes that only 6 of the 35 companies ran paid search, so the paid number is not a benchmark.

A short plan:

- [ ] Keep your Google Business Profile complete, with owner services listed and UTM tags on the website link
- [ ] Run Google search ads on owner keywords, sent to an owner page
- [ ] Track calls and forms per channel, and count signed owners after 90 days
- [ ] Check the LSA eligibility tool for your area twice a year in case Google adds a category

If owner leads fall across all channels, read [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping).

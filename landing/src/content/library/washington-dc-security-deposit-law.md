## DC security deposit rules at a glance

The District of Columbia splits its deposit rules between the D.C. Code and the Housing Regulations. D.C. Code 42-3502.17 points to the Security Deposit Act and 14 DCMR 308 to 311. We read the Code section on the Council's D.C. Law Library and the four regulations on the DC Office of Documents site on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | First full month's rent, charged once | 14 DCMR 308.2 |
| Where to hold it | Interest-bearing escrow account at a DC financial institution | 14 DCMR 308.3 |
| Lease or receipt | States the terms of the deposit | 14 DCMR 308.6 |
| Yearly posting | Where deposits are held and the interest rate for each 6 months | 14 DCMR 308.7 |
| Move-out inspection | Within 3 business days before or after the tenancy ends; 10 days' written notice | 14 DCMR 310 |
| Return or notice to withhold | 45 days after the tenancy ends | 14 DCMR 309.1 |
| Refund balance and itemized statement | 30 days after the notice to withhold | 14 DCMR 309.2 |
| Interest | At least the bank's statement savings rate, reset each January 1 and July 1 | 14 DCMR 311.1 |
| Interest due | When a tenancy of 12 months or more ends | 14 DCMR 311.2 |
| Wrongful withholding | The amount withheld; treble in bad faith | 14 DCMR 309.5 |
| Willful failure to pay interest | Civil fine up to $5,000 per violation | 14 DCMR 311.2 |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in District of Columbia?

A DC landlord may charge a security deposit up to the first full month's rent. 14 DCMR 308.2 sets that cap for any deposit or other payment held as security for the tenant's duties. The landlord may charge it only once.

Two more limits apply:

- **Long-time tenants.** D.C. Code 42-3502.17(a) bars a new deposit from a tenant who lived in the unit on July 17, 1985 and paid none before. This applies in units under the rent stabilization program.
- **Exempt units.** 14 DCMR 308.8 says section 308 does not apply to federally subsidized units or units leased by federal or DC agencies.

**Worked example.** Rent is $2,400 a month. The deposit cap is $2,400. Now add a refundable $300 "pet deposit" held as security for the tenant's duties. 308.2 covers "any security deposit or other payment" held as security, so the total security is $2,700. That is $300 above the cap.

## How many days does a District of Columbia landlord have to return a security deposit?

A DC landlord has 45 days after the tenancy ends. Within that time, 14 DCMR 309.1 requires one of two actions:

1. **Return it all.** Pay the full deposit and the interest due, without a demand from the tenant.
2. **Give notice to withhold.** Tell the tenant in writing that you intend to keep money for costs under the deposit agreement. Deliver it in person or by certified mail to the last known address.

If you choose option 2, you then have 30 days after that notice to act (14 DCMR 309.2). Refund the balance, with unused interest. Send an itemized statement of each repair or other use and its cost.

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 | Tenancy ends |
| By day 45 | Full refund with interest, or written notice to withhold |
| By 30 days after the notice | Refund balance plus itemized statement |

D.C. Code 42-3502.17(e) adds steps. At the end of a tenancy, ask the tenant for a forwarding mail or email address. Within 45 days, notify the tenant in writing of any unpaid amounts claimed, with photographs or other proof. Tell the tenant of the right to dispute. The tenant then has 30 calendar days to dispute, and you must answer in writing within 10 days. Keep proof of the notice for at least 60 days before you send a debt to a collector.

The Code history lists a 2025 amendment, D.C. Law 25-308. Its own applicability note says it depends on budget funding and has not been implemented. The history also lists D.C. Law 26-156 of August 14, 2026. Read the current Code text before you rely on any one subsection.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in District of Columbia?

A DC landlord may deduct only costs "properly incurred under the terms and conditions of the security deposit agreement" (14 DCMR 309.1(2)). D.C. Code 42-3502.17(e)(2)(A) lists the usual items:

- **Unpaid rent.** Rent arrears under the lease.
- **Damage.** Damage beyond ordinary wear and tear.
- **Removal costs.** The cost to remove furniture and items the tenant left at move-out.

You may not deduct for ordinary wear and tear (D.C. Code 42-3502.17(c)(1)). The Code defines it as wear from the intended use of the unit, including breakage or malfunction from age or deteriorated condition. Damage from negligence, carelessness, accident or abuse by the tenant, family or a guest is not ordinary wear and tear.

A promise in the lease to "leave the unit in good repair" does not make the tenant do major repairs. It also does not make the tenant replace obsolete materials or fix defects that are not the tenant's fault (D.C. Code 42-3502.17(c)(2)).

Plan the move-out inspection. Under 14 DCMR 310, you may inspect within 3 days before or after the tenancy ends, not counting weekends and holidays. Give the tenant written notice of the time and date at least 10 days before.

## Does a District of Columbia landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. A DC landlord who keeps any part of the deposit must give an itemized statement. 14 DCMR 309.2 requires a list of the repairs and other uses of the money and the cost of each. The statement goes with the refund of the balance.

Missing the steps is costly. Under 14 DCMR 309.3, a failure to comply with 309.1 and 309.2 is prima facie evidence (proof unless rebutted). It shows the tenant gets the full deposit plus interest. One relief exists. A good-faith but failed try to serve the tenant in person or by certified mail is not a failure to comply (14 DCMR 309.4).

## Must a District of Columbia landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

Yes to both. 14 DCMR 308.3 requires an interest-bearing escrow account held in trust at a DC financial institution with federal or state deposit insurance. The account holds only security deposits. An owner of more than one building may use one account for all of them (14 DCMR 308.5).

Interest rules under 14 DCMR 311:

- **Start date.** Interest starts on the day the tenant pays.
- **Rate.** At least the statement savings rate at the bank holding the account, reset each January 1 and July 1.
- **Who gets it.** All interest goes to the tenant. If you invest at a higher rate, you may keep up to 30% of the excess interest for administrative costs.
- **When due.** At the end of a tenancy of 12 months or more.
- **No pledging.** You may not assign the account or use it as security for a loan.

Each year end, post in the lobby and the rental office where deposits are held and the rate for each 6-month period. At move-out, list for the tenant the rate for each 6-month period of the tenancy (14 DCMR 308.7).

## What is the penalty for a District of Columbia landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

The penalty is the amount withheld, or 3 times that amount in bad faith. 14 DCMR 309.5 defines bad faith as a frivolous or unfounded refusal to return a deposit. The motive must be fraudulent, deceptive, misleading, dishonest or unreasonably self-serving. Simple negligence, bad judgment or an honest belief is not bad faith.

| Violation | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Missed 45-day or 30-day step | Prima facie case for full refund plus interest | 14 DCMR 309.3 |
| Deposit wrongly withheld | Amount withheld | 14 DCMR 309.5(1) |
| Bad-faith withholding | Treble damages | 14 DCMR 309.5(1) |
| Interest not paid | Interest owed; treble in bad faith | 14 DCMR 311.2 |
| Willful failure to pay interest | Civil fine up to $5,000 per violation | 14 DCMR 311.2 |

Tenants may file a complaint with the Office of Administrative Hearings for a deposit not returned or interest not paid (D.C. Code 42-3502.17(b)).

## DC deposit checklist for property managers

- [ ] Deposit is no more than the first full month's rent, and all security payments count.
- [ ] Funds are in an insured, interest-bearing escrow account at a DC institution.
- [ ] Lease or receipt states the deposit terms.
- [ ] Year-end lobby posting shows the bank and the 6-month rates.
- [ ] Move-out inspection notice goes out 10 days ahead.
- [ ] Refund with interest, or notice to withhold, goes out by day 45.
- [ ] Balance and itemized statement go out within 30 days after the notice.

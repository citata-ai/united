## What are the reasons a PHA can end a HAP contract?

A PHA can end a HAP contract (the Housing Assistance Payments contract between you and the housing authority) for 8 main reasons in 24 CFR part 982. Some reasons are about you. Some are about the family or the PHA budget.

| # | Reason | Rule | Who caused it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Repairs not made within the abatement period | 982.404(d)(5) | Owner |
| 2 | Owner breach of the HAP contract | 982.453(a)(1) | Owner |
| 3 | Breach of any other Section 8 HAP contract | 982.453(a)(2) | Owner |
| 4 | Fraud, bribery, drug-related or violent crime | 982.453(a)(3)–(6) | Owner |
| 5 | Unit too small after the family grows | 982.403 | Family size change |
| 6 | Lease ends, or PHA ends the family's assistance | 982.309(b)(2) | Owner, family or PHA |
| 7 | Not enough funding | 982.454 | PHA budget |
| 8 | 180 days after the last payment | 982.455 | Automatic |

Under 24 CFR 982.453(b), the PHA's remedies for an owner breach include more than termination. The PHA can also recover overpayments, abate (stop) payments, or reduce them.

## Does long abatement end the contract?

Yes, long abatement ends the contract. 24 CFR 982.404 sets a clear path for HAP contracts signed or renewed on or after 2024-06-06.

1. **Inspection finds a deficiency.** The PHA tells you in writing.
2. **Cure period.** You have 24 hours for a life-threatening item and 30 days for others, or a PHA-approved extension.
3. **Withholding (optional).** The PHA may hold payments during the cure period. If you fix the items in time, it pays the held amounts.
4. **Abatement.** If you miss the cure period, the PHA must abate the HAP, including held amounts. It tells you and the family that the contract will end in 60 days (or a longer PHA period) if the unit still fails.
5. **Fix within 60 days.** The PHA starts paying again, but it does not pay for the abated time.
6. **No fix within 60 days.** The PHA must end the HAP contract. The family gets at least 90 days to find a new unit.

Some PHAs move faster than 60 days. A Worcester Housing Authority page says that if a unit is in abatement "for more than 30 days", it sends the landlord a termination letter. After that date, the tenant owes the full contract rent if the tenant stays.

Worked example: your contract rent is $1,600 and the HAP is $1,200. You fix a failed item 45 days into abatement. Under the federal rule, you lose about 1.5 months of HAP, about $1,800, and the PHA does not pay it back. At a PHA with a 30-day policy, you may have lost the contract too.

## Can the PHA ban an owner from the program?

Yes, a PHA can refuse to approve new tenancies with an owner. 24 CFR 982.306 lists the cases.

The PHA must refuse if HUD says the owner is debarred, suspended or under a limited denial of participation. When HUD directs, it must also refuse if a Fair Housing action is pending or a court found a violation.

The PHA may refuse, at its discretion, if the owner:

- violated a Section 8 HAP contract before;
- committed fraud, bribery or a corrupt or criminal act in a federal housing program;
- engaged in drug-related or violent crime;
- has a history of HQS failures or of renting units that fail local housing codes;
- has a history of not acting on tenants who threaten others' safety;
- has not paid state or local real estate taxes, fines or assessments.

"Owner" here includes a principal or other interested party (982.306(f)). So the record of a partner or an LLC member can count.

## What owner actions break the HAP contract?

The owner actions that break the HAP contract are in 24 CFR 982.453(a). The main one is failing to keep the unit to HQS (housing quality standards). The others:

- breaking any other obligation in this HAP contract;
- breaking an obligation in any other Section 8 HAP contract;
- fraud, bribery or other corrupt or criminal acts in any federal housing program;
- for HUD-insured or HUD-loan projects, breaking the mortgage or regulatory agreement terms;
- drug-related criminal activity;
- violent criminal activity.

Watch for: you also may not evict a family because the PHA abates your HAP (982.404(d)(3)). An eviction for that reason adds a second problem to the first.

Not every damage leads to abatement. If the PHA finds the tenant or a guest caused damage beyond ordinary use, it may waive your repair duty. Then it may not withhold or abate your HAP (982.404(a)(4)).

## What ends the contract without anyone at fault?

Some HAP contracts end with no breach at all. 24 CFR 982.309(b) says the contract ends when the lease ends, when the PHA ends the contract, or when the PHA ends the family's assistance.

The contract also ends if the family outgrows the unit. Under 24 CFR 982.403, the PHA issues a new voucher. When a suitable unit is found, the contract ends at the end of the month after the month of notice.

Last, 24 CFR 982.455 ends the contract automatically 180 calendar days after the last HAP payment. This often happens when the family's income rises and the HAP drops to $0.

## Is there an appeal?

The federal rules give no formal hearing right to owners. Informal reviews and hearings in part 982 are for applicants and families. 24 CFR 982.306(e) says nothing in the rule gives an owner "any right to participate in the program". Also, the family is not a party to the HAP contract (982.456).

What you can do:

1. **Read the HAP contract.** It sets out the PHA's remedies and notice steps.
2. **Read the PHA Administrative Plan.** Some PHAs offer an owner review.
3. **Send proof fast.** Photos, invoices and a reinspection request can stop an abatement before it reaches termination.
4. **Ask a local attorney** if you believe the PHA broke its own contract.

## Prevention checklist

- [ ] Fix life-threatening items within 24 hours. Keep an on-call vendor.
- [ ] Fix other items within 30 days, or ask for an extension in writing before day 30.
- [ ] Book the reinspection yourself and make sure someone gives access.
- [ ] Send photos if your PHA accepts photo proof.
- [ ] Track each unit's abatement start date and the 60-day (or PHA) deadline.
- [ ] Keep real estate taxes and city fines paid.
- [ ] Keep every HAP contract clean. A breach on one unit can affect all your units.
- [ ] Never end a tenancy because of abatement.

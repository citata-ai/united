## What does days on market mean for a rental?

Days on market (DOM) for a rental is the number of days a unit is listed for rent before a tenant signs a lease. The term comes from home sales, where it counts days from listing to contract. For rentals, the end point is the signed lease.

DOM tells an owner how fast the market accepts the unit at its price. A low DOM means the price and the unit fit demand. A high DOM means something is off: price, condition, marketing or the season.

## What is a normal days on market for a rental?

A normal days on market depends on the city, the season and the unit type. National numbers are only a benchmark. Hemlane, a software company for landlords and managers, publishes monthly medians from its own platform.

| Month (2026) | Median DOM (Hemlane platform) |
|--------------|-------------------------------|
| January | 33 days |
| February | 28 days |
| March | 22 days |
| April | 24 days |
| May | 30 days |
| June | 28 days |

The same June 2026 report shows wide state gaps. Pennsylvania had a median of 15 days, and Mississippi and Alabama had 20. Utah had 111 days, Tennessee 60 and New Jersey 59. Hemlane does not say how it counts DOM, and its data covers only its own users. Treat these numbers as a rough guide.

Market slack also matters. FRED, using Census Bureau data, shows a US rental vacancy rate of 7.3% for Q2 2026. More vacant units means more competition for each tenant.

Best benchmark: your own history. Compare each unit with its own last 3 turnovers and with similar units your manager leased in the same season.

## How do you count days on market for a rental?

You count days on market from the first day the unit is listed to the day a tenant signs the lease. Write down the rules once, so every report counts the same way.

Counting rules:

1. **Start date.** The first day the listing is live on a public site.
2. **End date.** The day the lease is signed by both sides. Do not use the move-in date.
3. **Relists.** If the listing goes down and back up within 30 days, keep counting from the first start date. A reset hides a problem.
4. **Failed applications.** If an approved tenant backs out, the clock keeps running.
5. **Paused listings.** If you pause for repairs, log the pause, but report total days too.

Days on market is not the same as days vacant. Days vacant counts every day the unit earns no rent.

| Phase | Counts in DOM | Counts in days vacant |
|-------|---------------|----------------------|
| Move-out to make-ready done | No | Yes |
| Listing live to lease signed | Yes | Yes |
| Lease signed to move-in | No | Yes |

Worked example, with made-up dates:

- Old tenant moves out: June 1.
- Make-ready done, listing live: June 8.
- Lease signed: June 29.
- New tenant moves in: July 1.

DOM = 21 days (June 8 to June 29). Days vacant = 30 days (June 1 to July 1). At $1,800 per month, rent lost is about $1,800 / 30 x 30 = $1,800. The owner lost a full month, even though DOM looks good.

## What causes long days on market?

Long days on market usually comes from 1 of 6 causes. Check them in this order, because the first ones are the most common and the fastest to fix.

1. **Price above market rent.** The most common cause. Few inquiries in the first 7 days is the main sign.
2. **Weak listing.** Dark or few photos, no floor plan, a short description, or missing facts like pets and parking.
3. **Slow lead response.** Leads that wait a day for an answer often rent somewhere else.
4. **Hard showings.** Only 1 showing time a week, or no self-tour option.
5. **Condition.** Dirty carpet, old paint or a broken item in the photos.
6. **Season.** In the Hemlane 2026 data, the January median was 11 days longer than the March median.

Diagnosis table:

| What you see | Likely cause | What to do |
|-------------|--------------|-----------|
| Few inquiries | Price or listing | Recheck comps; improve photos |
| Inquiries, few showings | Slow replies or hard booking | Answer within hours; add more showing times |
| Showings, no applications | Price or condition | Fix condition items; adjust price |
| Applications, none approved | Screening fit | Review whether the price draws qualified renters |

Fair Housing: change the price, the listing or the process. Do not change who you market to by protected class.

## What does each vacant day cost?

Each vacant day costs about one-thirtieth of the monthly rent, plus utilities and other carrying costs. The math is simple, and it often changes the pricing choice.

| Monthly rent | Cost per vacant day | 14 extra days | 30 extra days |
|-------------|--------------------|---------------|---------------|
| $1,200 | $40 | $560 | $1,200 |
| $1,800 | $60 | $840 | $1,800 |
| $2,500 | $83 | $1,167 | $2,500 |

Compare that with a price cut. Cutting $1,800 rent by $50 costs $600 over 12 months. If the cut saves 14 days, it saves $840. The cut wins.

## How do property managers report days vacant to owners?

Good property managers report both days on market and days vacant for each turnover, with the reason for any delay. The report should let an owner see where the time went.

What to ask for in each turnover report:

- **Move-out date.**
- **Make-ready start and end dates**, with the list of work done.
- **List date and list price**, plus any price changes with dates.
- **Lead count, showing count and application count** by week.
- **Lease signed date and move-in date.**
- **DOM and days vacant**, and the rent lost.

Across a portfolio, ask for the average DOM and days vacant each quarter, compared with the same quarter last year. That trend shows whether the manager's leasing process is getting better.

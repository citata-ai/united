Tennessee has two sets of eviction rules. The Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA), Tenn. Code Title 66, Chapter 28, applies "only in counties having a population of more than seventy-five thousand (75,000), according to the 2010 federal census" (66-28-102(a)). Other counties use Title 66, Chapter 7. Check your county first, because the notices differ.

## What notice must a Tennessee landlord give before filing for unpaid rent?

A Tennessee landlord in a URLTA county gives a written notice that names the breach. The notice says the lease ends if the tenant does not fix the breach within 14 days after receipt (Tenn. Code 66-28-505(a)(2)). The breach can be unpaid rent, repair costs, damages or "any other amount due" under the lease.

Watch for these details in 66-28-505:

- **Repairs by the tenant:** the notice must tell the tenant that you must approve any repair in writing first.
- **Repeat breach:** if the same breach comes back within 6 months, 7 days' written notice is enough.
- **Notice waiver:** a lease can waive the notice for unpaid rent. The waiver must be in 12-point bold type or larger (66-28-505(b)). The waiver does not shorten the 5-day late fee grace period in 66-28-201.

In a non-URLTA county, Tenn. Code 66-7-109(a) sets 14 days' notice for rent that is due and in arrears "upon demand". If the tenant pays before the date in the notice, the lease does not end.

## How long does an eviction take in Tennessee?

A Tennessee eviction for unpaid rent takes at least about 30 days from notice to writ. That number is the sum of the minimums in the statutes. Real cases often take longer.

Here is a worked timeline for a URLTA county. Rent is due on the 1st.

| Step | Day (example) | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Rent due | Day 1 | Lease |
| Grace period ends (the due date counts as day 1) | Day 5 | 66-28-201(d) |
| You deliver the 14-day notice | Day 6 | 66-28-505(a)(2) |
| Notice period ends | Day 20 | 66-28-505(a)(2) |
| You file the detainer warrant; the officer serves it | Day 21 | 29-18-107, 29-18-115 |
| Earliest trial | Day 27 | 29-18-117 |
| Earliest writ of possession | Day 37 | 29-18-126 |

The math: 14 days of notice + 6 days to trial + 10 days to the writ = 30 days. Add the grace period and filing time, and the example reaches day 37. A late service, a postponed trial or an appeal moves every later date.

## Where do you file a Tennessee eviction, and what does it cost?

You file a detainer warrant with the court of general sessions in the county where the property is. Tenn. Code 29-18-107 lets one General Sessions judge decide "all questions of law and fact" in the case.

Service follows Tenn. Code 29-18-115. The officer can serve any adult person in possession of the unit. Certified mail and, in some cases, posting plus publication are also allowed. The officer must tell the tenant the trial date, which cannot be less than 6 days after service (29-18-117).

Filing fees differ by county, and each clerk sets its own schedule. We did not find a current fee schedule on an official court site that we could open and read. Ask your county's General Sessions civil clerk for the detainer warrant fee and the service fee before you file.

## Can a Tennessee landlord evict for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes, with the right notice. The notice depends on the breach and on the county.

| Reason | URLTA county | Non-URLTA county |
|---|---|---|
| Breach you can fix with money or repairs | 14 days to cure (66-28-505(a)(2)) | 14 days for rent or damage (66-7-109(a)) |
| Breach you cannot fix with money | At least 14 days (66-28-505(a)(3)) | 30 days (66-7-109(b)) |
| Violent act, real danger, hazardous condition | 3 days (66-28-517) | 14 days (66-7-109(a)(1)(C)); 3 days for violence or drug activity (66-7-109(d)) |
| No cause, month-to-month | 30 days before the rent date (66-28-512(b)) | Not covered here |
| No cause, week-to-week | 10 days (66-28-512(a)) | Not covered here |

Two more rules matter. Under 66-28-505(f), a false claim of a service or support animal need is a material breach. And when domestic abuse is the cause, 66-28-517 and 66-7-109(e) let you evict only the perpetrator, not the victim or innocent occupants. Some conditions apply, such as an order of protection.

## Who removes the tenant after a Tennessee judgment?

The sheriff or a constable removes the tenant. The writ form in Tenn. Code 29-18-127(a) commands "the sheriff or any constable" to restore you to possession and to report back within 20 days. The writ cannot issue until 10 days after the judgment (29-18-126).

After the officer removes the tenant, 29-18-127(b) tells you what to do with the tenant's things:

1. **Place the property** on the premises or in a clear area away from the entrance.
2. **Keep it away** from the road, at a reasonable distance.
3. **Do not touch it** for 48 hours.
4. **Discard what is left** after the 48 hours.

If the tenant appeals within 10 days (29-18-128), the tenant must post a bond, cash or a letter of credit, or give 2 personal sureties, for 1 year's rent (29-18-130(b)(2)). A 2024 law (Tenn. Acts ch. 755) amended these appeal sections.

## Is a self-help eviction legal in Tennessee?

No. In a URLTA county, Tenn. Code 66-28-511 bars a landlord from taking possession by "willful diminution of services". That includes cutting electric, gas, water or other essential service. The exceptions are abandonment, surrender or a remedy the chapter allows.

The penalty is real. Under 66-28-504, a tenant who is locked out or loses essential service can get back in or end the lease. The tenant can also recover actual damages, punitive damages "when appropriate" and attorney's fees. You must then return all prepaid rent and the security deposit.

One narrow exception exists. If the lease requires the tenant to put utilities in the tenant's name and the tenant does not do so within 3 days of move-in, you may end service that is in your name (66-28-521).

## What is the difference between the FMR and the payment standard?

HUD sets the Fair Market Rent, and the PHA sets the payment standard. They are two steps of one system.

| Term | Who sets it | What it is | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fair Market Rent (FMR) | HUD, every year | A rent estimate for an area and bedroom count | 24 CFR 888.113 |
| Small Area FMR (SAFMR) | HUD | The same estimate by ZIP code | 24 CFR 982.503(a) |
| Payment standard | Each PHA | The maximum monthly subsidy for a unit size | 24 CFR 982.505(a) |
| Gross rent | Owner rent plus utilities | Rent to owner plus the utility allowance | 24 CFR 982.4 |

HUD's Payment Standards guidebook (June 2025) puts it this way: the FMR is "the basis for determining the payment standard amount." The PHA picks a payment standard for each unit size, from zero bedrooms up. Together these make its payment standard schedule.

The family's payment standard is the lower of two amounts (24 CFR 982.505(c)). One is the standard for the voucher size. The other is the standard for the size of the unit it rents. A family with a 2-bedroom voucher that rents a 3-bedroom unit gets the 2-bedroom standard.

## Can a PHA set the payment standard between 90% and 110% of FMR?

Yes. 24 CFR 982.503(c) calls 90% to 110% of the FMR the "basic range." A PHA may set any amount in that range without HUD approval or notice. It can use one percentage for all sizes, or different ones. The rule's own example: 90% for efficiencies, 100% for 1-bedroom units and 110% for larger units.

When new FMRs come out, the PHA must check its schedule. It must revise it "no later than 3 months following the effective date" of the new FMR, if needed to stay in the range (982.503(c)(3)). HUD's guidebook adds that PHAs must review payment standards at least once a year.

Under 90% needs HUD approval (982.503(e)). HUD will not approve it if more than 40% of current families would then pay over 30% of their income. The exception: when it is needed to avoid ending families' assistance.

## What is an exception payment standard?

An exception payment standard is any amount above 110% of the FMR (24 CFR 982.503(d)). There are four paths:

1. **ZIP code, based on SAFMR.** A PHA not in a Small Area FMR area may set a ZIP code standard up to 110% of that ZIP's SAFMR. It must cover the whole ZIP code.
2. **110% to 120%, by notice.** A PHA may go up to 120% of the FMR after it notifies HUD, if it meets a criterion. One criterion: fewer than 75% of families issued vouchers in the last 12 months leased up. Another: more than 40% of families pay over 30% of adjusted income.
3. **Above 110%, by approval.** Otherwise the PHA must ask HUD and show rental market data.
4. **Reasonable accommodation.** For a person with a disability, the PHA may set up to 120% for one family without HUD approval, and more with approval.

For an owner, an exception area can mean a higher HAP in a high-rent ZIP code. Ask your PHA if your ZIP code has one.

## How do I find my PHA's payment standard?

Your PHA publishes it. Look on the landlord page for "payment standards" and "utility allowances." Tacoma Housing Authority, for example, has a page with that name in its landlord menu. If you cannot find it, ask the housing specialist on the RFTA.

When you compare your rent, compare the gross rent: your rent plus the utility allowance for any utilities the tenant pays.

Here is a worked example. The numbers are for illustration only.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| FMR, 2 bedrooms | $1,800 |
| PHA payment standard at 105% | $1,890 |
| Rent to owner | $1,750 |
| Utility allowance (tenant pays power and gas) | $160 |
| Gross rent | $1,910 |
| Total tenant payment (TTP) | $600 |
| HAP: lower of $1,890 − $600 or $1,910 − $600 | $1,290 |
| Family pays the owner: $1,750 − $1,290 | $460 |
| Family also pays utilities (about $160) | Family share $620 |

The gross rent is $20 over the payment standard, so the family pays $20 more than its TTP. At first move-in, the 40% limit checks that share (24 CFR 982.508). Our [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator) runs these steps for your own numbers.

## Does a lower FMR cut my current tenant's subsidy?

Not at once. 24 CFR 982.505(c)(3) gives the PHA a choice. It "may choose not to reduce" the payment standard for a family for as long as it stays in the unit. If the PHA does reduce it:

- [ ] The first cut cannot apply earlier than two years after the decrease takes effect.
- [ ] The family must get at least 12 months' written notice.
- [ ] The new amount is the greater of the amount in the notice or the schedule amount at the end of the 12 months.

Increases move faster. The PHA must use a higher payment standard by the earliest of three dates (982.505(c)(4)). They are a gross rent increase that raises the family share, the family's next reexamination, or one year after the increase.

A large FMR drop also triggers a rent check. If the FMR falls by 10% or more year over year, the PHA must redetermine the reasonable rent (24 CFR 982.507(a)(2)).

## Where are the FY2027 FMRs for my metro?

HUD's FY2027 FMRs took effect on October 1, 2026. HUD published the notice in the Federal Register on September 1, 2026 (91 FR 56156). Our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) lists the FY2027 FMRs for the 50 largest metro areas, 0 to 4 bedrooms, with the change from FY2026.

Remember that the FMR is not your cap. Your PHA's payment standard is. Check both before you set the rent.

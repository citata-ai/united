## Can I require an SSN?

An SSN requirement needs a local legal review and a clear screening purpose. Do not treat your software's required field as the law. Ask what the provider can verify without that field. Explain any limits before you collect an application fee.

California Civil Code 1940.3 bars landlord inquiries about immigration or citizenship status, subject to its exceptions. The statute allows documents needed to verify identity or financial qualifications. Those are different questions. Ask whether the applicant can meet the lease terms, rather than why the applicant lacks a number.

Washington RCW 59.18.257 requires advance screening disclosures, including the information accessed and criteria that could cause denial. Build that notice into your process where the law applies. A policy copied from another state may miss required steps.

Write an alternate review path before an application arrives. State which records you accept and who can approve a decision. Train staff to use the same path for all applicants with the same missing information. Record the actual reason for each decision.

## Can I screen with an ITIN?

An ITIN can help only if your screening provider supports it; it does not replace every use of an SSN. Get a written answer from the provider. Ask whether it uses the number for identity matching, credit retrieval or another check.

The IRS says an ITIN is for federal tax purposes. It does not establish immigration status, authorize work or serve as identification outside the federal tax system. Do not describe an ITIN as a government background clearance. Do not ask applicants to obtain one just for your form.

Check identity through a separate process. Use accepted identity documents and compare names across the application, income records and rental references. Allow a clear way to explain name differences. Do not collect more documents simply because a person's name is unfamiliar.

Ask how the vendor handles a missing credit file. A report with no match needs review. A report with an uncertain match also needs review. Neither should become an automatic finding of bad credit or an undisclosed fee for more searches.

## What other proof of history can I accept?

You can propose a records-based review that measures payment ability and rental conduct. Treat the table below as a suggested process, subject to local rules. It is not a list of documents every applicant must provide.

| Review goal | Possible evidence | How to check it |
|---|---|---|
| Confirm identity | Accepted photo identification | Compare the person, name and application |
| Confirm current income | Pay records or an employment letter | Contact the employer through an independent channel |
| Confirm other lawful income | Benefit or payment records | Review only information needed for qualification |
| Confirm rent history | Rent ledger or payment receipts | Match the address and contact the prior manager |
| Explain missing credit | Applicant statement and supporting records | Record what is missing and what replaces it |

Ask permission before contacting references. Find a manager's business contact independently when possible. A phone number on an application can be wrong without proving fraud. Give the applicant a chance to clarify gaps before you reach a conclusion.

Keep a short verification log. Record the document reviewed, the check made and the result. Avoid storing a complete bank statement when a limited record can answer the question. Protect any sensitive records you keep. Limit access to staff who need them.

## How do I avoid national origin discrimination?

Use the same lawful criteria and comparable evidence standards across applicants. The Fair Housing Act, in 42 USC 3604, prohibits national origin discrimination in covered rentals. A missing SSN does not tell you where someone was born or how they will treat a home.

Do not make assumptions from accents, language, names or the country that issued a document. Keep advertisements and application instructions open to all qualified applicants. If a staff member cannot read a record, arrange a consistent review method. Do not invent a stricter income test for that applicant.

For example, suppose an applicant has verified earnings and prior rent receipts but no matching credit file. Record that the credit file is missing. Then review the alternate evidence under your policy. Do not change the record to say the applicant has unpaid debts.

Give staff a review route for uncertain cases. Ask a manager to check both the evidence and the policy used. Keep the explanation tied to lease requirements. Do not make staff guess about immigration law or collect unrelated personal details.

## How should I close and document the decision?

Close the application with a clear record of the evidence and the decision. If a consumer report helped cause adverse action, the FTC says an FCRA notice is required. That can include denial, higher rent or a required cosigner. The notice identifies the reporting agency and explains dispute and free-report rights. Credit-score use can trigger more disclosures under the FTC guidance.

Use a written notice even where federal law allows another form. Check whether state law requires a specific form or added reasons. A vendor's recommendation does not remove your duty to review what you used.

Keep the application outcome separate from the applicant's sensitive documents. Assign a staff member to handle corrections and disputed matches. Do not allow an old mistaken report to drive a later decision without review.

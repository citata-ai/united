## What is the maximum security deposit in Minnesota?

Minnesota state law sets no maximum security deposit. Minn. Stat. 504B.178 controls how you hold, return and withhold a deposit, but its text names no cap. We read the 2025 Minnesota Statutes on the Revisor's site on 2026-10-19.

The section covers any money that secures the performance of a residential rental agreement. It does not cover a deposit that is only an advance payment of rent (subd. 1). Some cities and subsidy programs set their own limits. Check those before you set the amount.

## How many days does a Minnesota landlord have to return a deposit?

A Minnesota landlord has 3 weeks after the tenancy ends to return the deposit or send a written statement. Minn. Stat. 504B.178, subd. 3 starts the duty after you receive the tenant's mailing address or delivery instructions. You must do one of two things:

1. **Return the deposit** with interest, or
2. **Send a written statement** that shows the specific reason for each amount you keep.

The deadline is 5 days if the tenant leaves because the building was legally condemned, for reasons the tenant did not cause.

Mail counts. If you put the deposit or statement in U.S. first class mail, postage prepaid, with a return address, to the tenant's address, within the deadline, you meet the time rule (subd. 3(b)).

If you sell the building, you have 60 days from the end of your interest to transfer the deposit to the buyer and tell the tenant, or to return it (subd. 5).

## How does Minnesota deposit interest work?

A Minnesota deposit earns 1% simple interest a year. Minn. Stat. 504B.178, subd. 2 says interest runs from the first day of the month after the tenant pays the deposit in full. It stops at the end of the month in which you return the deposit or send the statement, or at a court judgment, if earlier. Interest under $1 is excluded.

Worked example: the tenant paid a $1,500 deposit in full on March 10, 2024. Interest starts April 1, 2024. The lease ends and you mail the return in July 2026, so interest runs to July 31, 2026.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Deposit | $1,500 |
| Interest period | 2024-04-01 to 2026-07-31, 28 months |
| Yearly interest at 1% | $15.00 |
| Interest for 28 months | $15.00 x 28 / 12 = $35.00 |
| Amount due if no deductions | $1,535.00 |

The statute also says you hold the deposit for the tenant, not in a fiduciary capacity under section 82.55. It does not require a separate bank account.

## What can a Minnesota landlord deduct from a deposit?

A Minnesota landlord may keep only amounts "reasonably necessary" for 2 purposes. Minn. Stat. 504B.178, subd. 3(b) lists them:

- **Unpaid money due:** rent, or other funds due to you under the agreement.
- **Repairs:** to restore the unit to its condition at the start of the tenancy, ordinary wear and tear excepted.

If the tenant sues, you carry the burden of proof for each deduction (subd. 3(c)). A lease term that waives this section is void (subd. 10).

The tenant also cannot use the deposit as last month's rent, with some exceptions. One exception is a month-to-month tenancy where no one has served a notice to quit (subd. 8). A tenant who still withholds after written demand can owe a penalty and interest.

## Does a Minnesota landlord need inspections and an itemized list?

Yes. A Minnesota landlord must send a written statement with "the specific reason" for each amount withheld (Minn. Stat. 504B.178, subd. 3). A total with no reasons does not meet this rule.

Since 2024, Minn. Stat. 504B.182 also adds 2 inspection offers. Laws 2023, chapter 52, article 19 created it, effective January 1, 2024, for leases signed on or after that date.

| Step | When | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Initial inspection offer | At the start, or within 14 days of move-in | Tell the tenant they may ask for an inspection |
| Move-out inspection offer | A reasonable time after either side gives notice to end | Tell the tenant in writing they may ask for one and be present |
| Move-out inspection | No earlier than 5 days before the end date, if asked | Inspect so the tenant can fix problems |
| Photo option | Start or end of tenancy, if the tenant agrees | Give written acknowledgment of photos or videos instead |

This matters for money. Subd. 4 of 504B.178 makes you liable for the penalty if you fail to give these inspection notices, or to do an inspection the tenant asked for.

## What is the penalty for keeping a deposit wrongly in Minnesota?

A Minnesota landlord who misses the rules owes the withheld amount twice, plus interest. Minn. Stat. 504B.178, subd. 4 applies if you fail to send the statement in time, fail to transfer or return under subd. 5, or fail the inspection rules. You owe the amount wrongly withheld with interest, plus a penalty equal to the withheld amount and interest.

Bad faith adds more. Subd. 7 allows punitive damages of up to $500 per deposit. If you missed the subd. 3 or subd. 5 duties, the law presumes bad faith. You avoid that presumption only if you return the deposit within 2 weeks after the tenant starts a court action.

Example: you keep $800 of a $1,500 deposit with no statement in 3 weeks. You can owe the $800, plus an $800 penalty, plus interest. If the court finds bad faith, add up to $500.

The tenant may sue, also in conciliation court, in the county of the unit or in the county where you live (subd. 9).

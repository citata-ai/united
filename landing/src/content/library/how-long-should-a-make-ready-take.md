## How long should a make-ready take?

A light make-ready should take about 3 to 5 work days. Heavier work takes longer. Two maintenance vendors publish ranges. Both sell make-ready services, so read their numbers as industry estimates, not a study.

| Turn type | Typical work | Time | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fastest managed turn | All trades back-to-back, one partner | 72 hours | Lula |
| Light (standard) turn | Clean, touch-up paint, small repairs | 3–5 days, if sequenced correctly | Lula |
| Efficient operator, any turn | Good coordination | "The better part of a week" | Lessen |
| Heavy turn | Full paint, flooring, drywall, fixtures | 7–14 days | Lula |
| Mid-size operator without central scheduling | Mixed | 10–20 days "closer to the norm" | Lessen |
| Renovation scope | Kitchen, bath, major systems | 2–4 weeks | Lula |

Lula also says industry averages run 7 to 14 days "because work gets sequenced backwards." Examples are cleaning before paint, or flooring before plumbing.

## What is a normal light turn?

A normal light turn is cleaning, touch-up paint and small fixes, with no flooring or major repair. With trades booked in advance, it fits in one work week.

A day-by-day plan for a light turn:

| Day | Work | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 (move-out) | Inspection, photos, punch list, order parts | Take dated photos before any work |
| Day 1 | Repairs: doors, blinds, fixtures, outlets; re-key | Handyman first, so paint covers patches |
| Day 2 | Patch and paint | Touch-up or a few rooms |
| Day 3 | Carpet clean or floor care | After paint, not before |
| Day 4 | Deep clean | Last trade in the unit |
| Day 5 | Final walk, photos, smoke alarm test, list as rent-ready | Fix any miss the same day |

The order matters more than the speed of each trade. Dirty work comes first. Cleaning comes last.

## How long does a heavy turn take?

A heavy turn takes about 7 to 14 days, per Lula. It adds trades that must follow each other: drywall, full paint, flooring, and often an appliance or fixture swap.

A heavy turn plan has more waiting points:

1. **Day 0:** inspect, scope, and decide repair versus replace for each item.
2. **Days 1–2:** demolition and drywall repair; order flooring and appliances.
3. **Days 3–4:** drywall dries; plumbing and electrical fixes.
4. **Days 5–7:** full paint.
5. **Days 8–10:** flooring install, then trim touch-up.
6. **Days 11–12:** appliances, fixtures, re-key.
7. **Days 13–14:** deep clean and final walk.

Parts are the usual risk. Order flooring and appliances on day 0, not after demolition.

## What slows a turn down?

Most delay comes from waiting, not from work. Lessen calls it "a coordination problem." Lula lists starting the turnover only after move-out and vendor coordination lag among the main causes of long vacancies.

Common causes and fixes:

| Cause | Fix |
|---|---|
| Work starts only after the tenant leaves | Do a pre-move-out inspection and book vendors at notice |
| Vendors booked one at a time | Book all trades for set days in one call |
| Trades in the wrong order | Use a fixed sequence: repair, paint, floors, clean |
| Parts not on site | Order on day 0; keep common parts in stock |
| Late repair-or-replace decisions | Set rules in advance, such as replace carpet past a set age |
| No final check | One person signs off before the unit is listed |

A pre-move-out inspection also helps with deposits. In California, the tenant may ask for an initial inspection no earlier than 2 weeks before move-out, under Civil Code 1950.5(f). You can scope the work at the same visit.

## How do days vacant compare with work days?

Days vacant are longer than make-ready days. Make-ready days run from move-out to rent-ready. Days vacant run from move-out to the next move-in. They add marketing, showings, screening and the lease start date.

Lula cites a US multifamily average of roughly 41 days vacant in 2025, from RentCafe. It says single-family rentals "typically" run 30 to 45 days per turn.

| Measure | Starts | Ends | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Make-ready days | Move-out | Rent-ready | 5 days |
| Marketing and leasing days | Listing goes live | Lease signed | 14 days |
| Gap to move-in | Lease signed | New tenant moves in | 7 days |
| Days vacant | Move-out | New move-in | 26 days |

You cut days vacant most by leasing during the old tenant's notice period. A unit that is pre-leased can have almost zero gap.

## How do I track make-ready time?

Track two numbers for each turn: make-ready days and days vacant. Write the reason for every day over your target.

- [ ] Move-out date and time.
- [ ] Rent-ready date.
- [ ] Listing date and lease-signed date.
- [ ] New move-in date.
- [ ] Each trade's scheduled and actual day.
- [ ] Reason for each delay.

Each vacant day costs one day of rent. On a $1,500 rental, that is about $49 a day. If your turns run long and you lack time to fix the process, our guide to [property management](/property-management) explains what a manager handles.

## Which San Jose units fall under the ARO?

The San Jose Apartment Rent Ordinance (ARO) covers units in older multi-unit buildings. Municipal Code 17.23.167 defines a rent stabilized unit as a rental unit in a guesthouse or a multiple dwelling building with a certificate of occupancy issued on or before 1979-09-07, or a unit that was offered for rent by that date.

The same section excludes:

- **1- and 2-unit buildings.** Single-family homes and duplexes are out.
- **Affordable rental units** under recorded agreements.
- **Short stays.** Hotel, motel or guesthouse rooms rented for less than 30 days.
- **Care and campus housing.** Some hospital, care, shelter and school housing.

Quick test for your property:

| Question | If yes | If no |
|---|---|---|
| Does the building have 3 or more units? | Go to the next question | Not an ARO unit |
| Was the certificate of occupancy issued on or before 1979-09-07? | Likely an ARO unit | Not an ARO unit; check state law |
| Is the unit under a recorded affordability agreement? | Excluded | ARO applies |

Units outside the ARO can still fall under the state cap in California Civil Code 1947.12 and the city's Tenant Protection Ordinance. See the last section.

## What is the 5 percent yearly cap in San Jose?

The San Jose ARO allows an Annual General Increase of no more than 5% of the monthly rent. Municipal Code 17.23.310 also allows only 1 rent increase in any 12-month period. There is no CPI link: the cap is 5% in every year.

| Current rent | Most you can add in 12 months | New rent |
|---|---|---|
| $2,500 | $125.00 | $2,625.00 |
| $3,272 | $163.60 | $3,435.60 |
| $4,000 | $200.00 | $4,200.00 |

$3,272 is HUD's FY2027 2-bedroom fair market rent for the San Jose metro, down 6.1% from $3,483. See the [San Jose fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/san-jose-fair-market-rent-2027).

Rules that change the math:

- **New tenant, new rent.** Section 17.23.300 lets you set the first rent of a new tenancy only if the last tenant left by choice or by a lawful for-cause termination. After a no-cause termination, the rent stays at the old tenant's lawful rent plus allowed raises.
- **Petitions.** A landlord can petition for a fair return increase or a capital improvement pass-through. A tenant can petition against an unlawful increase or for a cut when services drop.
- **State cap does not stack.** California Civil Code 1947.12 exempts housing under a local rent control rule that limits increases below the state cap. The ARO's 5% is that lower limit.

## What is the San Jose Rent Registry?

The San Jose Rent Registry is the city's yearly record of each rent stabilized unit. Under Municipal Code 17.23.900, you must register each covered unit on a city form, each year unless the city's regulations set another interval.

Registry checklist:

- [ ] Register every ARO unit by the city's due date.
- [ ] Report the rent the household actually pays. For a voucher unit, report the tenant's part plus the agency payment. Reporting a different amount is a violation.
- [ ] Give a copy to an adult in the household within 10 days if the city asks you to.
- [ ] Do not file late. A registration more than 30 days past due brings a late fee set in the City Council fee schedule.
- [ ] Register before you raise rent. Section 17.23.310 bars an increase if you have not substantially complied with the city's registration request.

The city also charges a yearly per-unit program fee for ARO and Tenant Protection Ordinance units. The amount changes each fiscal year. We could not read the current fee schedule on the city site, so check it with the Rent Stabilization Program at (408) 975-4480 before you budget.

## What does the San Jose Tenant Protection Ordinance cover?

The San Jose Tenant Protection Ordinance (TPO) covers more units than the ARO. Under Municipal Code 17.23.1230, it applies to rent stabilized units, rental units in any multiple dwelling (except permitted hotels and motels), guesthouse rooms and unpermitted units. Tenants get just cause protection "commencing on the first day of tenancy."

Section 17.23.1250 lists 13 just causes. They include nonpayment, a material violation, substantial damage, nuisance, refusal of access, criminal activity, substantial rehabilitation, Ellis Act removal, owner move-in and an order to vacate.

Notice duties that trip owners up:

1. **State the reason** in the termination notice.
2. **Use the city form** for rent stabilized units.
3. **Send a copy to the city** within 3 days of delivery. Do the same for an unlawful detainer summons and complaint.
4. **Post or serve the TPO notice** in the 3 most common languages.

For a substantial rehabilitation or owner move-in, you must pay relocation equal to the Ellis Act Ordinance base assistance with the notice. The City Council sets that amount by resolution.

## What does the San Jose Ellis Act Ordinance require?

The San Jose Ellis Act Ordinance (Part 11) applies when an owner takes ARO units off the rental market. You must withdraw the whole building, not part of it. Key rules:

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Notice | At least 120 days; 1 year on request for a tenant 62+, disabled, or terminally or catastrophically ill with 1+ year in the unit |
| Relocation | Base assistance per unit in escrow at notice; more for lower-income, senior, disabled or ill tenants and families with a K-12 child |
| Return within 5 years | Former tenants can return; the rent stays at the old lawful rent plus allowed raises |
| Years 5 to 10 | 120 days' notice to the city and tenants before you rent again |
| Recorded memo | Encumbers the property for 10 years |

How San Jose compares with state law:

| Topic | San Jose | State (Civil Code 1946.2 and 1947.12) |
|---|---|---|
| Rent cap | 5% flat, ARO units | 5% + CPI, max 10%, other covered units |
| Just cause starts | Day 1 | After 12 months |
| Registry | Yearly, per unit | None |

## Which City of Sacramento rules apply to a rental owner?

Two City of Sacramento programs apply to most rental owners in the city. Each has its own code chapter, its own fee and its own office. These rules apply inside city limits only, so check that first.

| Program | City Code | Which units | Yearly fee | Main duty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tenant Protection Program (TPP) | Chapter 5.156, Tenant Protection and Relief Act | Apartments and duplexes built before February 1995 | $20 per unit | Register each year, cap rent increases, give just cause |
| Rental Housing Inspection Program (RHIP) | Chapter 8.120, Rental Housing Inspections Code | Most rental units, including houses, condos and duplexes | $25 per unit | Register, pass a city inspection, then self-inspect each year |

So a pre-1995 duplex is in both programs. A rented single-family house is in the inspection program but not in the TPP. The city adopted the Tenant Protection and Relief Act on 2019-08-13.

## What does the Sacramento Tenant Protection and Relief Act cap?

The Sacramento Tenant Protection and Relief Act caps yearly rent increases at 5% plus the change in the California Consumer Price Index (CPI). The city resets the cap each July 1. For 2026-07-01 to 2027-06-30, the cap is 8.6%. The Department of Industrial Relations (DIR) calculated the April 2026 CPI change at 3.6%, and 5% + 3.6% = 8.6%.

The rules that come with the cap:

- **Once every 12 months.** You cannot raise rent more than once in 12 months.
- **No waiver.** A tenant cannot waive the limit.
- **All tenants.** The cap applies no matter how long the tenant has lived in the unit.
- **Penalties.** An increase over the cap, or a second increase within 12 months, can bring administrative penalties up to $25,000.
- **Fair rate of return.** You can petition the city for a hearing examiner to review an increase above the cap. You must give documents that justify it.

Worked example: a covered pre-1995 apartment rents for $2,201, the HUD FY2027 2-bedroom fair market rent for the Sacramento metro. The most you can add from 2026-07-01 is 8.6% × $2,201 = $189.29, for a new rent of $2,390.29. That is far above the HUD figure, which fell 2.4% for FY2027. Price to the local market, not to the cap. See all sizes on the [Sacramento fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/sacramento-fair-market-rent-2027).

The TPP also limits evictions. After a tenant has lived in the unit for more than 12 months, the city says you must offer to renew the agreement and you need a just cause to evict. The city lists failure to pay rent, breach of the agreement, criminal or nuisance activity, failure to give access, and necessary repairs that need the unit empty.

Units outside the TPP, such as houses and condos, can still fall under the state cap and just cause rules in California Civil Code 1947.12 and 1946.2. Those state rules have their own exemptions and stay in effect until 2030-01-01.

## Must Sacramento owners register in the rental housing inventory?

Yes. Owners of TPP-covered units must register them with the city each year and pay the $20 per-unit fee. The city sends the registration packet based on data from the Sacramento County Assessor. The 2026 notice says registration is due within 30 days, and failure to register may bring a penalty that starts at $500.

Registration checklist:

- [ ] Find your Registration File Number (for example, 21-xxxxxx). Each property has its own.
- [ ] Register online once. The city says a mailed copy as well as an online form delays processing.
- [ ] Ask for exemptions each year. Owner-occupied and subsidized units (for example, SHRA, Section 8 or LIHTC) must renew the exemption every year, or the city removes it.
- [ ] Name an authorized agent if a manager handles the mail. The change starts the next cycle, and you stay responsible for the fee.
- [ ] Pay the invoice. It comes in a separate letter.

## How does the Sacramento Rental Housing Inspection Program work?

The Sacramento Rental Housing Inspection Program inspects each registered property once, then moves passing properties to self-inspection. Here are the steps on the city's page.

1. **Register** each rental property with the program.
2. **Get a notice.** The city gives the owner and the tenant 2 weeks' notice of the inspection date.
3. **Pass the inspection.** If the inspector finds violations, you have 30 days to fix them.
4. **Enter self-certification.** If the unit passes, or you fix all items before the 30-day reinspection, the property moves to the Self-Certification Program.
5. **Self-inspect.** Inspect each unit every calendar year and at each change in tenancy. Keep the checklists for 3 years.
6. **Expect spot checks.** The city inspects 10% of self-certified properties at random. A property that fails goes back to yearly city inspections.

Owners who live outside the Sacramento area must name a Local Contact Representative. That person attends inspections and answers notices. Your property manager can fill this role. You must also give each new tenant the Residents Rights form before move-in.

Units that another agency inspects regularly, and rental properties less than 5 years old, can apply for an exemption.

## What fees do Sacramento owners pay each year?

Sacramento owners pay up to 2 yearly per-unit fees, plus inspection fees when the city inspects. Fees from the RHIP page took effect on 2025-07-01.

| Fee | Amount | When |
|---|---|---|
| TPP program fee | $20 per unit | Each year, covered units |
| RHIP program fee | $25 per unit | Each year, all registered units |
| RHIP unit inspection | $180 per unit | Each unit the city inspects |
| RHIP reinspection | $295 | Repairs take more than 30 days |
| RHIP rescheduling | $180 | Missed or late-rescheduled visit |
| Code technology fee | 8% | Added to RHIP fees |
| TPP failure to register | From $500 | Penalty |

Worked example for a pre-1995 fourplex in year 1 of the inspection program:

| Line | Math | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| TPP fee | 4 × $20 | $80.00 |
| RHIP fee | 4 × $25 | $100.00 |
| First city inspection | 4 × $180 | $720.00 |
| Technology fee | 8% × $820 | $65.60 |
| Year 1 total | | $965.60 |
| Later years, self-certified | $80 + $100 + 8% × $100 | $188.00 |

We applied the 8% technology fee to the RHIP fees only. Ask the city how it bills that fee on your invoice.

## What do state license laws say about referral fees?

State license laws often say a licensed broker may pay referral fees only to other licensees. This matters for a property management company because, in many states, the company works under a real estate broker license. A referral program that pays owners or tenants in cash can put that license at risk.

Here are three states, read from the statute text on 2026-10-19:

| State | Rule | Exception | Statute |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | A broker may not pay an unlicensed person for work that needs a license | A licensed salesperson under the broker; a broker of another state | Bus. & Prof. Code 10137 |
| Texas | A broker "may not pay a commission to or otherwise compensate a person directly or indirectly for performing an act of a broker" | A license holder; a broker licensed in another state who does not negotiate in Texas | Occupations Code 1101.651(a) |
| Florida | Discipline for paying "a fee or other compensation, to a person not properly licensed ... for the referral of real estate business, clients, prospects, or customers" | A broker licensed in another state; a tenant finder's fee up to $50 | Statutes 475.25(1)(h), 475.011(13) |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Florida's wording is the widest of the three. It names referrals of "clients, prospects, or customers" directly. It also says "it is immaterial that the person to whom such payment or compensation is given made the referral ... from within this state or elsewhere" (Florida Statutes 475.25(1)(h)).

Other states have their own rules, and the wording differs. Do not copy a program from a company in another state.

## Can I pay a licensed agent?

Yes, in these three states you can pay a licensed agent, but the money must flow through the agent's broker. This is the most common and safest kind of referral fee for a property manager.

- **California.** Bus. & Prof. Code 10137 says a salesperson may accept pay for licensed work only from the broker they work under. A salesperson may pay another licensee only through their own broker (FindLaw, section 10137).
- **Texas.** Occupations Code 1101.651(b) says a sales agent may not accept pay for a real estate deal from anyone except their sponsoring broker. Section 1101.651(c) says a sales agent may pay a commission only through their sponsoring broker.
- **Florida.** Section 475.25(1)(h) lets a Florida broker "pay a referral fee or share a real estate brokerage commission with a broker licensed or registered under the laws of a foreign state," if that broker does not break Florida law.

A worked example: an agent at ABC Realty sends you an owner with 3 rental homes. Your agreement with ABC Realty pays 25% of the first month's management fee per home. You pay ABC Realty, the broker. ABC Realty pays its agent under its own split. The 25% is an example, not a market rate.

## Are gift cards for owners or tenants allowed?

Gift cards may count as payment under these laws, so check with your state license board first. The statute words are broad. Texas says "otherwise compensate a person directly or indirectly." Florida says "a fee or other compensation." Neither text carves out small gifts for owners.

Florida has one clear exception for tenants. Florida Statutes 475.011(13) exempts a property management firm or apartment complex owner "paying a finder's fee or referral fee to an unlicensed person who is a tenant in such apartment complex, provided the value of the fee does not exceed $50 per transaction." The exception names tenants of that complex. It does not name owners.

| Who refers | California | Texas | Florida |
|---|---|---|---|
| Licensed broker in your state | Allowed | Allowed (license holder) | Allowed |
| Broker in another state | Allowed | Allowed, if no negotiation in Texas | Allowed, if no Florida law broken |
| Agent of another broker | Through that broker | Through that broker | Through that broker |
| Unlicensed tenant | Not allowed for licensed work | Not allowed for broker acts | Up to $50 per transaction, tenant of that complex |
| Unlicensed owner or friend | Not allowed for licensed work | Not allowed for broker acts | Not allowed for referrals |

Ways to thank an owner that do not pay the referrer:

- A handwritten thank-you note.
- A donation to a local charity in the community's name, with no link to a specific referral. Ask your board first.
- A better service for all owners, such as a free annual inspection, not tied to referrals.

## How do I write a referral agreement?

Write a referral agreement only with another licensed broker, and put the terms in writing before the first referral. A short agreement prevents most disputes.

Checklist for a broker-to-broker referral agreement:

- [ ] Names and license numbers of both brokers, and the states
- [ ] What counts as a referral: an owner who signs a management agreement within a set number of days
- [ ] The fee: a flat amount, or a percent of a named fee, such as the first month's management fee
- [ ] When the fee is due: after the owner signs, or after the first rent is collected
- [ ] Who gets paid: the broker, never the agent directly
- [ ] What happens if the owner cancels early
- [ ] Disclosure to the owner that a referral fee is paid, if your state or your agreement requires it
- [ ] Signatures and date

Steps to start a program:

1. **Read your state's license law.** Search the license board site for "referral fee" or "compensation to unlicensed persons."
2. **Ask the board or an attorney** about gifts and tenant referrals in your state.
3. **List local brokers** who sell rental homes but do not manage them.
4. **Send each one a short agreement** and a one-page summary of your services and fees.
5. **Track each referral** in your CRM with the broker's name, the date and the fee.

Referral agents often send owners who just bought a rental. Send them a link to your [owner services page](/property-management) and the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator), so the owner arrives with your prices in mind.

## What if my state has no clear rule?

If your state has no clear rule, treat referral pay to unlicensed people as not allowed until the license board says it is. The cost of a mistake is high: California section 10137 lets the commissioner suspend or revoke a license for a breach, and Florida lists the rule in its discipline section, 475.25. Suspension of a broker license can stop a property management company from collecting fees.

Ask the board in writing and keep the reply. A written answer from the regulator is the best proof that you acted in good faith.

## How long do I have to fix a fail item?

You have 24 hours for a life-threatening item and 30 days for anything else. 24 CFR 982.404(a)(3) sets both clocks from the date the PHA notifies you. The PHA may approve a longer period for non-life-threatening items.

HUD's life-threatening list (82 FR 5458) includes gas leaks, exposed wires and blocked exits. Missing or broken smoke and CO detectors and bad venting on fuel-fired appliances are on it too. Your PHA may add items with HUD approval, but it cannot remove HUD's.

What happens next depends on which inspection the unit failed.

| Inspection | What a fail means | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Initial (before move-in) | No approval, no HAP contract, no payment until the unit passes | 24 CFR 982.305 |
| Initial, with the PHA's NLT option | HAP can start if there are no life-threatening items; you fix the rest within 30 days | 24 CFR 982.405(j) |
| Biennial or annual | Payments may be withheld, then must be abated if you miss the deadline | 24 CFR 982.404(d) |
| Complaint (interim) | PHA inspects within 24 hours (life-threatening) or 15 days (other); same repair clocks | 24 CFR 982.405(d) |

NLT means "no life-threatening deficiencies." It is a PHA option, not a right. Your PHA's Administrative Plan says if and when it uses it.

## What should I do the day the report arrives?

Act the same day. Macon Housing Authority tells owners to visit the unit and see what the inspector saw. "The time to ask questions is when the report is received, not after the due date."

Follow these steps:

1. **Read the full list.** Mark each item as life-threatening (24 hours) or other (30 days).
2. **Call the inspector about unclear items.** Macon prints the inspector's name and phone number on its cover letter.
3. **Fix the 24-hour items first.** Do not wait for a reinspection date.
4. **Book trades for the 30-day items.** Leave a margin for parts and access.
5. **Get access in writing.** Tell the tenant the repair dates in writing. Macon says a tenant who changes the locks or misses repair visits does not earn you an extension.
6. **Check every item twice.** Macon calls an overlooked item "the most common source of abatement."
7. **Send proof.** Ask if the PHA accepts photos. Under 24 CFR 982.405(i), it may accept "photographic evidence or other reliable evidence."
8. **Ask for an extension early, if you need one.** Macon wants a written request at least 10 days before the due date. It needs a valid reason, such as severe weather.

## Who must fix tenant-caused fail items?

The PHA decides. Under 24 CFR 982.404(a)(4), if the household or a guest caused damage beyond ordinary use, the PHA "may waive the owner's responsibility" to fix it. If it waives your duty, the HAP "may not be withheld or abated."

The family then has the same clocks: 24 hours for a life-threatening item and 30 days for others (982.404(b)(2)). The family is also responsible if it fails to pay a utility it owes under the lease. The same applies to an appliance it agreed to supply.

Use this checklist to ask for a waiver:

- [ ] Photos of the damage, with dates.
- [ ] Your move-in inspection report showing the item was fine.
- [ ] The lease clause that assigns the utility or appliance to the tenant.
- [ ] A written request to the PHA, sent before the due date.

You can still charge the tenant for the damage under the lease and the security deposit rules (24 CFR 982.313).

## How do I schedule the reinspection?

Each PHA sets its own method. At move-in, the Maricopa County housing authority puts the job on you: "it is the Landlords responsibility to contact the HAMC Inspector to reschedule." For annual inspections, Macon sets the reinspection "on or shortly after the HQS Due Date."

Know the fee rule before you book. The PHA may not charge you for the move-in inspection or the first inspection during the tenancy (24 CFR 982.405(h)). It may charge "a reasonable fee" when a reinspection finds an owner item that you said was fixed, or that was due, still not fixed. You may not pass that fee to the tenant.

## What if the unit fails the reinspection too?

A second fail costs more. At move-in, the Maricopa County housing authority says: "If a unit fails the Re-inspection, the family must pursue a different unit and start the RFTA process over again." You lose the tenant and the time.

During a tenancy, a missed deadline starts the abatement path in 24 CFR 982.404(d):

| Day | Event |
|---|---|
| 0 | Written notice of deficiencies |
| 1 or 30 | Cure deadline (life-threatening or other) |
| After the deadline | PHA must abate HAP, including any withheld amount |
| Up to 60 days after abatement notice | Fix and pass: payments restart, but no pay for the abated period |
| After 60 days, or a longer PHA period | PHA must terminate the HAP contract |

You may not evict the family because the PHA withholds or abates (982.404(d)(3)). The tenant keeps paying only the tenant share.

## Can I appeal an inspection result?

There is no federal appeal right for owners. 24 CFR 982.555 gives the informal hearing right to participant families, for decisions about their assistance. 24 CFR 982.407 says part 982 gives no party other than HUD or the PHA a right to enforce HQS.

What you can do:

- **Ask for a supervisor review.** PHAs must run supervisory quality control inspections (24 CFR 982.405(c)), so a second opinion process often exists.
- **Show proof.** Photos, receipts and a contractor's note often settle a dispute faster than an argument.
- **Ask for the written policy.** The PHA's Administrative Plan sets its inspection policies.

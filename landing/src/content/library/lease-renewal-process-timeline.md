## When do I send the lease renewal offer?

Send the lease renewal offer at least 90 days before the lease ends. Start your own review 120 days out. This gives the tenant time to decide, and it gives you time to meet any state notice rule if the tenant leaves or if you raise the rent.

Some state notice rules are long. Here are 4 examples.

| State | Rule | Notice | Statute |
|---|---|---|---|
| New York | Non-renewal, or renewal with a rent increase of 5% or more | 30, 60 or 90 days, based on how long the tenant has lived there or the lease length | Real Property Law 226-c |
| Oregon | Rent increase after the first year of a tenancy (not week-to-week) | At least 90 days, in writing | ORS 90.323 |
| California | Rent increase in a month-to-month or shorter tenancy | 30 days if 10% or less in 12 months; 90 days if more than 10% | Civil Code 827 |
| Florida | Ending a month-to-month tenancy | At least 30 days before the end of a monthly period | Fla. Stat. 83.57 |

New York sets the notice by the longer of the tenant's time in the unit or the lease term. Under 1 year needs 30 days. 1 to 2 years needs 60 days. Over 2 years, or a lease of 2 years or more, needs 90 days. If you send it late, the tenancy continues on the old terms until the notice period runs out.

Oregon's rule also limits how much and how often you can raise rent. ORS 90.323 allows 1 increase in any 12-month period, capped by a formula in ORS 90.324, with exemptions. A 90-day timeline fits these rules. A 30-day timeline does not.

Rules differ in other states and some cities. Check yours before you set your calendar.

## The 90-day lease renewal timeline

Use this timeline for a 12-month lease. Count back from the lease end date.

| Day before lease end | Task | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 120 | Review the tenant: payment history, complaints, lease breaks, maintenance requests | Renew or not renew decision |
| 110 | Check the market rent for similar units | Proposed rent |
| 100 | Check your state and city notice rules for this tenant | Notice deadline on the calendar |
| 95 | Schedule a pre-renewal inspection with proper entry notice | Inspection date |
| 90 | Send the written renewal offer, or the non-renewal notice | Offer letter sent, proof of delivery |
| 75 | Follow up if no reply | Second notice sent |
| 60 | Reply deadline. Sign the renewal, or start the move-out plan | Signed renewal or move-out date |
| 45 | If leaving: book the move-out inspection and start marketing | Listing live |
| 0 | New term starts, or the tenant moves out | Renewal filed, or turnover starts |

Move the dates earlier if your state requires 90 days for a rent increase. In that case, send the offer at 120 days. Then a "no" still leaves time for any further notice.

## Should I inspect before renewing?

Yes, inspect before you renew. A short inspection shows damage, lease breaks and repair needs while the tenant still lives there. You can then fix issues, add terms to the renewal, or decide not to renew.

Give proper entry notice under your state's rules. Florida (Fla. Stat. 83.53) and Arizona (A.R.S. 33-1343) list inspection as a lawful reason to enter. California's list in Civil Code 1954 is narrower, so tie the visit to repairs or get the tenant's agreement.

Pre-renewal inspection checklist:

- [ ] Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms work.
- [ ] No leaks under sinks, at the water heater or around toilets.
- [ ] HVAC filter state; book service if due.
- [ ] Walls, floors and doors: damage beyond normal wear.
- [ ] Signs of pets or occupants not on the lease.
- [ ] Mold, moisture or pest signs.
- [ ] Photos of each room, dated, for the file.

Use what you find. A tenant who keeps the unit well is a strong renewal. A unit with deferred repairs needs work before the next term, whoever lives there.

## What goes in the renewal offer letter?

The renewal offer letter states the new terms, the reply date and the default if the tenant does not reply. Put it in writing and keep proof of delivery.

Include these items:

1. **The current lease end date.**
2. **The new rent and the date it starts.** Show the old rent next to it.
3. **The new term.** For example, 12 months, or a choice of 6 or 12 months at 2 rents.
4. **Any changed terms.** New pet rules, utility changes, a new fee.
5. **The reply date.** Give a date, not "soon".
6. **How to reply.** A link to sign online, an email address, or a form.
7. **The default.** What happens if the tenant does not reply by the date.
8. **Any notice your state requires.** In New York, the 226-c notice must include or attach the notice under Real Property Law 231-c, about the good cause eviction law.

If the unit was built before 1978, check the lead rule. 40 CFR 745.101(d) exempts a renewal when you already gave the full disclosure and have no new lead information. If you got a new lead report, disclose it.

## How long does the tenant have to answer?

No federal rule sets a reply period for a renewal offer. A 14 to 30 day window is a practical choice. Pick a reply date that still leaves you time to give any notice your state requires if the tenant declines.

A worked example: the lease ends December 31. You send the offer on October 2, 90 days out. You set the reply date at November 1. If the tenant declines, you still have 60 days to market the unit, book the move-out inspection and plan the turnover.

## What if the tenant does not respond?

If the tenant does not respond, the lease and state law decide what happens. Three common outcomes:

- **Month-to-month.** Under some leases and state laws, a tenant who stays after the end date becomes month-to-month. Ending that tenancy then needs notice. Florida requires at least 30 days before the end of a monthly period (Fla. Stat. 83.57).
- **Automatic renewal.** Some leases renew for a full new term unless a party gives notice. In New York, that clause binds the tenant only if the landlord sends a written reminder 15 to 30 days before the tenant's notice deadline (General Obligations Law 5-905).
- **Lease ends.** Some leases simply end. If the tenant stays, you must use your state's legal process. You cannot change locks or remove belongings.

Steps when the reply date passes:

1. Send a second written notice the next day. Restate the offer and the default.
2. Call or text once. Log the attempt.
3. Apply the default in the lease. Do not invent a new one.
4. If you need the unit back, give the notice your state requires and follow the court process if needed.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

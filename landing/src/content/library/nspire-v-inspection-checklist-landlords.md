## When must PHAs switch from HQS to NSPIRE-V?

PHAs must use NSPIRE for voucher units by February 1, 2027. HUD set that date in a Federal Register notice on September 30, 2025 (90 FR 46911). It was the third delay. The first date was October 1, 2023, then October 1, 2024, then October 1, 2025.

NSPIRE stands for National Standards for the Physical Inspection of Real Estate. HUD published the final rule on May 11, 2023 (88 FR 30442). The voucher version is often called NSPIRE-V. It replaces Housing Quality Standards (HQS), the voucher standard from the 1970s.

HUD lets PHAs switch earlier. In its 2025 notice, HUD "continues to encourage PHAs that are ready to implement NSPIRE" to do so.

## What does the inspector check?

The inspector checks the unit, the path out of the building and the systems that serve the unit. Under 24 CFR 5.703(a), a voucher inspection covers only:

- the assisted unit itself;
- the way out from the unit's entry door to the public way;
- common features tied to living there, such as a laundry room or mail room;
- the building systems that serve the unit.

Use this checklist the week before the inspection. Every line comes from 24 CFR 5.703 unless noted.

**Unit: kitchen and bath**

- [ ] Hot and cold running water in the kitchen and the bathroom.
- [ ] A private bathroom with a sink, a tub or shower, and a flush toilet that works.
- [ ] A kitchen area with a sink, a cooking appliance, a refrigerator, and space to prepare and store food.
- [ ] A permanently mounted light fixture in the kitchen and in each bathroom.
- [ ] GFCI protection on any outlet within 6 feet of a water source.

**Unit: rooms and safety**

- [ ] A living room, and at least one bedroom or living/sleeping room for each 2 people.
- [ ] In each habitable room, 2 working outlets, or 1 working outlet and a permanent light.
- [ ] A guardrail at any walking surface with a drop of 30 inches or more.
- [ ] A permanent heating source, in climate zones HUD names by notice.
- [ ] No unvented space heaters that burn gas, oil or kerosene.
- [ ] Smoke alarms and CO alarms as described below.

**Inside common areas and outside**

- [ ] At least 1 working smoke alarm on each level of the building.
- [ ] GFCI outlets within 6 feet of water, and guardrails at drops of 30 inches or more.
- [ ] Clear exits and fire escapes.

**Health hazards (24 CFR 5.703(e))**

- [ ] No signs of mold, pests, garbage, fire hazards or electrical hazards.
- [ ] Lead-based paint rules met for pre-1978 units (24 CFR part 35).

## Which deficiencies are life-threatening (24-hour fix)?

A life-threatening deficiency must be fixed within 24 hours of notice. Other deficiencies get 30 days or a PHA-approved extension (24 CFR 982.404(a)(3)). HUD's NSPIRE standards notice (88 FR 40832) carries over the HOTMA life-threatening list from 2017 and marks these items with a 24-hour voucher timeframe.

| HOTMA life-threatening item (82 FR 5458) | Examples |
|---|---|
| Gas leak or fumes | Leaking fuel line or valve; strong gas odor |
| Electrical hazard | Exposed wires; missing cover plate with wires showing; water near an electrical device |
| Smoke detector | Missing or not working |
| Carbon monoxide detector | Missing or not working |
| Bad venting | Misaligned water heater flue; dryer not vented outside; unvented space heater |
| Blocked exit | Damaged fire escape; items blocking the emergency exit |
| Fire extinguisher (where required) | Missing, discharged or expired |
| Lead paint | Deteriorated paint in a pre-1978 unit for a child under 6 (blocks move-in) |

The 2023 notice adds one item: HUD raised a missing unit entry door to life-threatening. The notice says the door helps residents shelter in place during a fire.

## What are the smoke and carbon monoxide alarm rules?

Smoke alarms must be in four places in the unit, under 24 CFR 5.703(d)(3):

1. **Each level.** At least one working alarm on every level.
2. **Each bedroom.** One inside every bedroom.
3. **Near bedrooms.** One within 21 feet of any bedroom door, measured along the path of travel.
4. **Behind a door.** If a door separates the hall alarm from the living area, add one on the living-area side.

If a hearing-impaired person lives in the unit, the alarms must suit that person. An install that follows NFPA 72 meets the rule. The Maricopa County housing authority also tells owners that alarms must be hard-wired or have a 10-year sealed battery.

Carbon monoxide alarms follow chapters 9 and 11 of the 2018 International Fire Code. When do you need them? HUD's standards notice says: in most cases, when a fuel-burning appliance or fireplace is in or attached to the unit. An attached, unventilated garage also triggers the rule.

These two rules do not wait for 2027. HUD's July 2024 notice (89 FR 55645) says the smoke and CO standards still apply. They carry out the Consolidated Appropriations Acts of 2021 and 2023.

## What fails most often inside the unit?

The Maricopa County housing authority publishes a list of common fail items in its RFTA packet:

| Common fail item (Maricopa) | Quick fix |
|---|---|
| Utilities not on | Turn on water, power and gas before the visit |
| Cracked or missing cover plates | Replace the plates |
| Missing GFCI in kitchen or bath | Install GFCI outlets |
| Missing or dead smoke alarms | Install 10-year sealed or hard-wired alarms |
| No CO alarm where needed | Install per the 2018 IFC |
| Stove knobs or burners not working | Repair or replace |
| Carpet trip hazards | Fix seams and loose edges |
| Water heater without a pressure relief drain line | Add the discharge line |
| Debris inside or outside | Clear it |

## Does my PHA already use NSPIRE-V?

Ask your PHA directly. In 2024, HUD told PHAs that switch early to notify HUD of their date (89 FR 55645). Your PHA's landlord page or Administrative Plan should say which standard it uses. Some PHA forms already name both: Maricopa's landlord certification refers to "HQS/NSPIRE standards".

## What is rental cash flow?

Rental cash flow is the money left each month after the property pays all its bills. It is cash in your bank account, not a tax number.

Cash flow = gross rent + other income − vacancy − operating expenses − reserves − debt service

A positive number means the rental pays you. A negative number means you pay the rental.

Cash flow is not the same as taxable income. Depreciation lowers taxable income but costs no cash that year. Principal payments cost cash but are not a deductible expense. IRS Publication 527 lists the deductible rental expenses.

## Which income lines go in?

All income the property brings in goes on the top lines. Rent is the largest, but not the only one.

| Income line | Example | Notes |
|-------------|---------|-------|
| Base rent | $2,000/month | Use the lease or local comps |
| Pet rent | $25/month | Only if your lease charges it |
| Parking or storage | $50/month | Common in small multifamily |
| Laundry | Coin or card income | Shared machines |
| Utility reimbursements | Water bill the tenant repays | IRS Pub 527 counts expenses a tenant pays for you as rental income |
| Late fees | Unpredictable | Leave out of the plan; count it as a bonus |

Use only income you can expect. Late fees and lease-break fees are not a plan.

## Which expenses do owners forget?

Owners most often forget vacancy, capital items and management. Each one is real money over a year, even in a month you pay nothing.

1. **Vacancy.** A home sits empty between tenants. The Census Bureau put the US rental vacancy rate at 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026. Budget a share of rent every month.
2. **Capital expenditures (capex).** A roof, HVAC, water heater or flooring wears out. You do not pay for it monthly, but it comes. Put a share of rent aside each month.
3. **Management.** iPropertyManagement's survey, updated 2026-09-23, found an average monthly fee of 8.49% of rent. Count it even if you self-manage.

Others owners miss:

- **Leasing fee** each time a new tenant moves in. iPropertyManagement found an average of 70.6% of one month's rent when bundled with management.
- **Turnover costs.** Paint, cleaning and small repairs between tenants.
- **Utilities in a vacant month.** You pay them while the home is empty.
- **Rising tax and insurance.** Both often go up each year.
- **HOA special assessments.**

## What is a cash flow worksheet?

A cash flow worksheet is one page that lists every income and cost line for one property. Fill it in monthly terms.

Worked example for one house, with made-up costs:

| Line | Per month | How to estimate |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| Gross rent | $2,000 | Lease or comps |
| Other income | $25 | Pet rent |
| Vacancy (7%) | −$140 | Local rate, or the Census national rate |
| Effective income | $1,885 | |
| Property tax | −$280 | Tax bill / 12 |
| Insurance | −$120 | Policy / 12 |
| Repairs (5% of rent) | −$100 | Your history or 5% |
| Capex reserve (5% of rent) | −$100 | Age of roof, HVAC, water heater |
| Management (8.49%) | −$170 | Average fee, 2026 |
| Leasing fee reserve | −$59 | 70.6% x $2,000 / 24 months (a new tenant every 2 years) |
| HOA | $0 | HOA statement |
| Utilities paid by owner | −$40 | Water, trash |
| Operating expenses | −$869 | Sum of the costs above |
| Net operating income (NOI) | $1,016 | Effective income − operating expenses |
| Principal and interest | −$850 | Loan statement |
| Monthly cash flow | $166 | NOI − debt service |
| Yearly cash flow | $1,992 | x 12 |

## How do you stress-test cash flow?

You stress-test cash flow by changing one line at a time and watching the result. If one bad month wipes out a year, the margin is thin.

| Test | Change | New monthly cash flow |
|------|--------|-----------------------|
| Base case | None | $166 |
| Vacancy at 10% | −$60 | $106 |
| Insurance up 25% | −$30 | $136 |
| Rent $100 lower | −$85 net of vacancy and management | $81 |
| Water heater fails | −$1,500 once | Year's cash flow falls to $492 |

Ask: if two of these happen in the same year, can you pay from reserves? If not, raise the reserve or pass on the deal.

## What is good monthly cash flow per door?

There is no official figure for good cash flow per door. A door means one rental unit. The right number depends on your price, loan and goals.

Practical tests instead of a rule:

- **Positive after all lines.** Vacancy, capex and management included.
- **Covers one surprise.** A year of cash flow can pay for one large repair.
- **Survives a stress test.** Stays positive at 2 to 3 points more vacancy.
- **Beats your next-best use of the cash.** Compare it with the return on your down payment. See our guide on how to calculate ROI.

An owner who buys for long-term growth may accept thin cash flow. An owner who needs income cannot. Decide which one you are before you buy.

## A monthly cash flow habit

1. **Use a separate bank account** for the rental.
2. **Move the capex and vacancy reserves** to a savings account each month.
3. **Compare actual to plan** each quarter.
4. **Update rent and insurance** at each renewal.
5. **Keep every receipt.** IRS Pub 527 asks you to separate repairs from improvements and keep accurate records.

## Ohio rent and notice rules at a glance

Ohio gives landlords wide freedom on rent, and a short list of notice rules. Here they are in one table, with the statute for each.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Statewide rent cap | None | Chapter 5321 (none set) |
| Local rent control or stabilization | Barred for private rentals | 5321.19(A) |
| Notice to raise rent | No separate period in the statute | Chapter 5321 (none set) |
| End or not renew week-to-week | At least 7 days before the date in the notice | 5321.17(A) |
| End or not renew month-to-month | At least 30 days before the periodic rental date | 5321.17(B) |
| Drug activity termination | 3 days after notice | 5321.17(C) |
| Retaliatory rent increase | Not allowed | 5321.02(A) |
| Rent terms in the lease | Allowed if not against Chapter 5321 | 5321.06 |
| Owner name and address in written lease | Required | 5321.18(A) |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Does Ohio have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

Ohio has no cap on rent increases. No section of Chapter 5321, the Ohio Landlords and Tenants Act, limits how much or how often you raise rent.

Ohio also bars local rent control. Ohio Rev. Code 5321.19(A) covers every political subdivision, such as a city or county. None may adopt or enforce "rent control or rent stabilization." That rule took effect on September 23, 2022, through House Bill 430.

Section 5321.01 defines both terms widely. "Rent control" includes any limit on rent increases or on rent changes between tenancies. "Rent stabilization" means increases of a fixed amount or on a fixed schedule set by a local government.

## How many days notice does an Ohio landlord need to raise the rent?

Chapter 5321 sets no separate notice period for a rent increase. The statute does not say "give X days before a new rent starts." So the lease and the type of tenancy decide the timing.

On a month-to-month tenancy, the 30-day rule in 5321.17(B) is the practical floor. A rent increase is a change of terms. If the tenant refuses it, your fallback is to end the tenancy, and that needs 30 days before a rental date. Most managers give the increase notice on the same timeline, in writing.

Use this step list for a month-to-month increase:

1. **Check the lease.** Look for a clause that sets a notice period for rent changes. Follow it if it is longer.
2. **Check for retaliation risk.** Did the tenant complain about repairs or code issues lately? If yes, write down the cost reason for the increase.
3. **Pick the start date.** Make it a periodic rental date, such as the 1st.
4. **Count back 30 days.** Deliver the notice before that point, with margin.
5. **Write it down.** State the old rent, the new rent and the start date.
6. **Keep proof.** Save a copy and the delivery date.

## Can an Ohio landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

An Ohio landlord can raise the rent during a fixed-term lease only if the lease allows it. Ohio Rev. Code 5321.06 lets landlord and tenant set "any term relating to rent" in the rental agreement. So the rent written in a 12-month lease holds for those 12 months.

If you want room to change rent mid-term, the lease must say so up front. Common forms are a step-up schedule or a set increase at a named date. Without such a clause, wait for the renewal and offer new terms then.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Ohio?

An Ohio month-to-month tenancy ends with notice given at least 30 days before the periodic rental date (Ohio Rev. Code 5321.17(B)). Either the landlord or the tenant can give it. A week-to-week tenancy needs notice at least 7 days before the date in the notice (5321.17(A)).

The 30 days count back from a rental date, not from any day you choose. Here is a worked example:

| Item | Date |
|---|---|
| Rent due | 1st of each month |
| Planned last day of tenancy | March 31 |
| Periodic rental date after the end | April 1 |
| Latest notice date (30 days before April 1) | March 2 |
| Safer target, for delivery delays | February 25 |

One exception is shorter. Section 5321.17(C) covers certain drug offenses by a tenant or guest. A landlord who knows, or has reasonable cause to believe it, must end the tenancy on 3 days' notice.

Section 5321.17(D) adds a limit. These notice periods do not apply when you end a lease for a breach of the lease or of a legal duty. Those cases follow the eviction notice rules instead.

## Do cities in Ohio add their own rent or notice rules?

Cities in Ohio cannot add rent control. Ohio Rev. Code 5321.19(A) also bars any local measure that conflicts with Chapter 5321 or regulates the rights and duties it covers.

Section 5321.19(B) keeps three kinds of local rules in force:

- Local housing, building, health and safety codes.
- Rent rules for housing the city itself owns or runs.
- Voluntary incentive programs, such as tax abatements, tax credit financing, loans or grants tied to housing supply or quality.

So check your city's housing code and any incentive agreement on the property. A unit with a tax abatement or a public loan can carry rent limits in that contract.

## Can an Ohio landlord raise rent after a tenant complains?

An Ohio landlord may not raise rent to retaliate against a tenant (Ohio Rev. Code 5321.02(A)). The rule protects a tenant who:

- complained to a government agency about a code violation that materially affects health and safety;
- complained to the landlord about a breach of the landlord's duties in 5321.04;
- joined other tenants to negotiate with the landlord.

Section 5321.02(C) gives a clear safe path. You may raise rent to reflect the cost of improvements you installed, or higher operating costs. Keep the invoices and tax bills that support the number.

A tenant who proves retaliation can use it as a defense to eviction, end the lease, and recover actual damages plus reasonable attorney fees (5321.02(B)).

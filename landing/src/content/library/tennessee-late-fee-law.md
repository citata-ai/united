Tennessee caps late rent fees in one sentence of its landlord-tenant law. Tenn. Code 66-28-201(d) says any late charge "shall not exceed ten percent (10%) of the amount of rent past due". The same subsection sets a 5-day grace period. A 2013 law (Tenn. Acts ch. 206) last amended 66-28-201.

## What is the maximum late fee a landlord can charge in Tennessee?

The maximum Tennessee late fee is 10% of the rent past due, in a county under the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA). The cap uses the past-due amount, not the full monthly rent. So a tenant who pays part of the rent owes a smaller maximum fee.

Here is a worked calculation for a $1,400 monthly rent:

| Case | Rent past due | 10% cap | Example fee you set | Allowed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tenant pays nothing | $1,400 | $140 | $100 flat | Yes |
| Tenant pays $1,000 | $400 | $40 | $100 flat | No, cap is $40 |
| Tenant pays nothing | $1,400 | $140 | $10 a day for 20 days = $200 | No, cap is $140 |
| Tenant pays nothing | $1,400 | $140 | $50 + $5 a day for 18 days = $140 | Yes, at the cap |

The cap is easy to break with a flat fee and a partial payment. Write your lease term as "the lesser of $X or 10% of the rent past due". Then your fee stays legal in each case.

## Does Tennessee law require a grace period before a late fee?

Yes. In a URLTA county, Tenn. Code 66-28-201(d) gives a 5-day grace period. The period starts on the day the rent is due, and that day counts as day 1.

Use this example. Rent is due on the 1st:

1. **Day 1 (the 1st):** rent is due. Grace period starts.
2. **Day 5 (the 5th):** last day of the grace period.
3. **Day 6 (the 6th):** the earliest day you may charge the fee.

The statute adds one more rule. If the last day of the grace period is a Sunday or a legal holiday, you may not charge the fee if the tenant pays on the next business day. The code defines legal holidays in Tenn. Code 15-1-101.

Watch for: a lease waiver of notice under 66-28-505(b) lets you file sooner for unpaid rent. That waiver still "shall not reduce the tenant's grace period" in 66-28-201.

## Must the late fee be written in the lease in Tennessee?

Tenn. Code 66-28-201(d) does not say in words that the fee must be in the lease. But a late fee is a term of the rental agreement. 66-28-201(a) lets the parties include terms "not prohibited by this chapter", such as rent and the rights of each party. Without a written term, you have no clear basis to charge the fee.

Put these items in your lease:

- [ ] The rent amount and the due date.
- [ ] The grace period: 5 days, with the due date as day 1.
- [ ] The Sunday and holiday rule from 66-28-201(d).
- [ ] The fee as "the lesser of a set amount or 10% of the rent past due".
- [ ] Where and how the tenant pays. Unless the lease says otherwise, rent is payable at the unit (66-28-201(c)).
- [ ] Any notice waiver in 12-point bold type or larger (66-28-505(b)).

The same section also bars a lease term that makes the tenant waive rights under the URLTA (66-28-201(a)). A fee above the cap in the lease does not make the excess legal.

## Can a Tennessee landlord charge a daily late fee?

Yes, but the total must stay within the cap. Tenn. Code 66-28-201(d) applies the 10% limit to "any charge or fee, however described". So a daily fee, a flat fee and an admin charge for late rent all count toward the same 10%.

A simple way to stay safe: set a flat fee below 10% of your lowest rent, or use the "lesser of" wording. If you use a daily fee, stop the charge when the total reaches 10% of the past-due rent. If your software adds the fee for you, check that it stops at the cap.

## Can a Tennessee landlord evict a tenant for unpaid late fees?

The URLTA notice covers more than rent. Tenn. Code 66-28-505(a)(2) applies when the breach is "remediable by the payment of rent, the cost of repairs, damages, or any other amount due to the landlord pursuant to the rental agreement". A late fee in the lease is an amount due under the lease. So you may give a 14-day notice to cure.

The statute does not say how a General Sessions judge treats a case that rests on a late fee alone. A safer path is to file for the unpaid rent and list the late fees in the same claim. Ask a local attorney before you file a case for fees only.

## Do the Tennessee late fee rules apply in every county?

No. The URLTA applies "only in counties having a population of more than seventy-five thousand (75,000), according to the 2010 federal census" (Tenn. Code 66-28-102(a)). In a smaller county, the 10% cap and 5-day grace period in 66-28-201(d) do not apply by statute. There, look to the lease terms and ask a local attorney what limits apply. Check the 2010 census count for your county before you rely on either rule.

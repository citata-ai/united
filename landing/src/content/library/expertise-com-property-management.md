## How often does AI cite Expertise.com for property managers?

AI engines cite Expertise.com more than any other site for property manager questions in our data. On 2026-10-19 we asked "What is the best property management company in <City, ST>? Give names." for the 50 largest US cities. We sent it to 3 engines with live web search: the OpenAI API (ChatGPT model family), the Gemini API with Google Search grounding, and the Claude API (our data, 2026-10-19).

| Engine | Answers | Expertise.com cited | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claude | 50 | 47 | 94% |
| ChatGPT | 50 | 30 | 60% |
| Gemini | 50 | 16 | 32% |
| All 3 | 150 | 93 | 62% |

Expertise.com showed up in 49 of the 50 cities. The next sites were Yelp and iPropertyManagement, with 50 answers each. No other site came close to 93.

Watch for: this is 1 prompt, 1 run, 1 day, through APIs. The consumer apps can give other sources.

## Why do AI engines cite Expertise.com?

Our hypothesis: Expertise.com pages match the question word for word, and they hold the facts an AI answer needs. The page title for Chicago reads "17 Best Chicago, IL Property Management Companies". The owner's prompt asks for the best company in Chicago. The match is close.

The Chicago page also shows, as read on 2026-10-19:

- **A recent date:** "Last updated: October 18, 2026".
- **Counts:** 577 reviewed, 353 curated, 17 top picks.
- **A method line:** it says it scored Chicago companies "on more than 25 variables across five categories".
- **Per company:** name, street address, website link, a short profile, main services and, for some, a license number.

An AI answer needs names, a reason for each name, and a source it can link. One Expertise.com page gives all 3 for one city. That is a likely reason for the high count, but our data shows the link, not the cause.

## How does Expertise.com pick property management companies?

Expertise.com picks companies with its own research, not with an application form. Its selection process page lists 5 steps:

1. **Find potential picks.** It uses "public databases and customer referrals" to build a list of local businesses.
2. **Check accreditations, awards and licenses.**
3. **Analyze review data.** It counts "the number of professional databases reviewing them", the average review score and how much ratings change.
4. **Assess services.** It picks finalists by main area of expertise, range of services and years of experience.
5. **Mystery shop.** Its callers phone each company as potential customers and rate how well they know the subject, how friendly and how professional they are.

The About page names 5 criteria: reputation, qualifications, experience, engagement and professionalism. It says the company has analyzed more than 10 million companies in more than 200 industries. The selection page says it researches more than 60,000 businesses each month.

## Can you pay to be listed on Expertise.com?

You cannot pay to get picked, but you can pay to advertise after you are picked. The selection page says a provider that passed the review "may choose to advertise on the page to increase their visibility". The Chicago page shows a "Featured Provider" block at the top.

You also cannot ask for a review in this category. The About page says review requests are open only in the Legal and Home Improvement categories. Most listed companies come from the internal research process.

So for a property manager, the path is indirect: make your company look strong on the signals the process reads.

## What should you do if you are not on the Expertise.com list?

Improve the signals Expertise.com says it checks, then fix the other sites AI cites. This is a checklist, not a promise. Expertise.com does not publish its weights.

| Signal Expertise.com names | What you can check this month |
|---|---|
| Reviews on several sites | You have real reviews on Google, Yelp and at least 1 more site, and you reply to them |
| Licenses and awards | Your broker or property manager license number is on your website |
| Years of experience | Your site states the year you started |
| Services | Your site lists each service: leasing, screening, rent collection, maintenance, evictions |
| Engagement | A person answers the phone in business hours and calls back the same day |
| Professionalism | Your phone script answers fee and contract questions clearly |

What to do next:

1. **Open your city's page** on Expertise.com and read the profiles of listed companies. Note what they show that you do not.
2. **Fix your own website facts** so a researcher finds them fast: license, start year, services, service area. The [AI search checklist](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website) covers the page format.
3. **Fix your Yelp page and Google Business Profile.** They were the next most cited sources in our data.
4. **Answer the phone well.** The mystery-shop step is the one you control fully.
5. **Check the list again** after its next update. The Chicago page showed an update on October 18, 2026.

## What should you do if you are on the list?

If you are on the list, check that every fact is right. AI engines can repeat an old phone number or a wrong service from the page. Compare your Expertise.com profile with your website and your Google Business Profile, and use the "Update my listings" section on the Expertise.com About page to fix errors.

Then test: ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude the "best property manager in <your city>" question. See whether the answer names you and links Expertise.com. Our method is in [How to Track AI Traffic and AI Mentions](/guides/track-ai-traffic-property-management).

## How do renters use AI to find apartments?

Renters use AI tools to build a short list, compare communities and check neighborhoods. Two 2026 surveys measure it.

| Survey | Who | AI use | Top uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rentals.ca, Spring 2026 | 1,194 Canadian renters, May 5 to June 14 | 28.5% used ChatGPT, Gemini or similar | Find listings or neighborhoods 57.7%, compare rents 45.5%, read lease terms 40.2% |
| J Turner Research, July 2026 | Apartment renters (country and sample size not stated on the page) | 45.9% use AI for search or review analysis | Lists that fit needs 42.3%, compare communities 40.0%, research neighborhoods 36.6% |

The two numbers measure different things. Rentals.ca asks about the search itself. J Turner also counts review analysis. Both say the same thing: a large minority of renters now asks AI before it asks a leasing office.

The AI answer also shapes the tour. J Turner found that 62.3% of AI users were less likely to tour after an unfavorable AI-made summary. Only 16.8% were more likely to tour because the summary had specific facts.

## Which sources do AI tools use for apartments?

AI tools use listing sites first, then property websites. Peek tracked AI apartment answers for about 5.5 months for its AI Discovery Black Book, 2nd edition.

| Engine | Share of citations from listing sites (ILSs) |
|---|---|
| Perplexity | 81% |
| Gemini | 73% |
| ChatGPT | 65% |
| Claude | 55% |
| All engines | about 68% |

Peek names property websites as the second most-cited source. It also reports that Claude and ChatGPT lean more on well-built property sites than before.

This pattern differs from owner searches. When a landlord asks for a property manager, AI cites directories such as Expertise.com, Yelp and iPropertyManagement. In our test of 150 such answers, Expertise.com was a source in 93 (our data, 2026-10-19). The renter side and the owner side need different source lists. Our page on [landlord vs renter questions](/property-management) explains the split.

## Do ILS listings feed AI answers?

ILS listings feed most AI apartment answers. Peek found that the Zillow network (Zillow, Trulia, Redfin, Realtor.com) held 45% of ILS citations, down from 57% in March. CoStar sites held 38%. Together, Zillow and CoStar cover about 84% of listing-site citations.

So the ILS feed is your first AI surface. Check these points on each listing:

- **Same name** as the property site and Google Business Profile.
- **Current rents** by floor plan, not a stale "from" price.
- **All fees:** application, admin, amenity, pet and parking.
- **Pet rules:** types, limits, deposits and monthly pet rent.
- **Photos and floor plans** that match the current units.
- **Review replies** on the ILS, since reviews shape AI summaries.

## What property facts must be correct?

Every fact a renter filters on must be correct and the same everywhere. AI tools join facts from many sources. When the ILS, the site and Google disagree, the AI may skip you or show the wrong rent.

Schema.org has a type for this: ApartmentComplex. Its own properties map to the facts renters ask about.

| Fact | Where it must match | Schema.org property |
|---|---|---|
| Community name and address | ILS, site, Google Business Profile | name, address |
| Total units | Site, ILS | numberOfAccommodationUnits |
| Units available now | Site, ILS | numberOfAvailableAccommodationUnits |
| Bedrooms per plan | Site, ILS | numberOfBedrooms, accommodationFloorPlan |
| Pet rules | Site, ILS, FAQ | petsAllowed |
| Tour booking | Site, Google Business Profile | tourBookingPage |
| Amenities | Site, ILS | amenityFeature |

Peek found a visibility lift for properties with strong pricing and commute content. The lift shrank since March: from 46% to 30% for pricing, and from 35% to 26% for commute. Publish both anyway; renters ask for them.

Fair Housing applies to AI-facing copy too. Describe the property, the rules and the area facts. Do not describe who "should" live there.

## How should a multifamily manager set up Google Business Profile?

Each apartment community gets one Google Business Profile. Google's guidelines say one profile per location and a website that represents that location. Link each profile to the community page, not to the company home page.

The name must match the real-world name on the sign and the site. Google does not allow taglines, phone numbers or service descriptions in the name field.

## How do you track renter leads from AI?

You track renter leads from AI in 4 steps. No single check is complete, so use all 4.

1. **Add "AI assistant (ChatGPT, Gemini, other)"** to the lead source list on every guest card and online form.
2. **Watch referral visits** from AI tools in your analytics. Our [guide to tracking AI traffic](/guides/track-ai-traffic-property-management) shows the setup.
3. **Run 10 to 20 renter prompts** each month, for example "2-bedroom apartments under $1,800 near downtown Tampa with parking". Note which communities and sources the answer names.
4. **Compare** the AI lead count with tours and leases, not only with clicks.

Many AI answers end with no click. The renter may visit the ILS listing or call you directly. That is why the guest card question matters most.

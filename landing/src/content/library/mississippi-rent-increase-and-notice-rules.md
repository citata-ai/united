Mississippi gives landlords a lot of room on rent. No state law caps an increase, and cities may not set rent control on their own. The real limits come from three places: your lease, the notice rules for ending a tenancy, and a ban on retaliation. Here they are in one table.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Statewide rent cap | None in the statutes we read | Title 89, Chapter 8 |
| City rent control | Cities may not regulate private residential rent without prior legislative approval | Miss. Code 21-17-5(2)(h) |
| Rent increase notice | No specific day count in the Act | Title 89, Chapter 8 |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | At least 30 days' written notice | 89-8-19(3) |
| End a week-to-week tenancy | At least 7 days' written notice | 89-8-19(2) |
| No fixed term in the agreement | Week-to-week if rent is weekly, otherwise month-to-month | 89-8-19(1) |
| Health or safety violation | No notice to terminate required | 89-8-19(4) |
| Fixed-term lease | Ends on the date stated | 89-8-17(1) |
| Increase after a lease expires | Allowed if retaliation is not the dominant purpose | 89-8-17(2) |

## Does Mississippi have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

Mississippi has no statewide cap on rent increases in the statutes we read. The Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, Title 89, Chapter 8, does not limit how much you may raise the rent.

Cities also cannot add rent control on their own. Miss. Code 21-17-5 gives cities their home-rule powers. Subsection (2)(h) says that section does not let a city, "without prior legislative approval, regulate, directly or indirectly, the amount of rent charged for leasing private residential property in which the municipality does not have a property interest."

Two limits still apply:

- **Program rules.** A Housing Choice Voucher contract or a tax-credit property sets its own rent rules. The state law does not override them. For voucher rent math, see our [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator).
- **Your lease.** If the lease sets the rent for a term, that rent holds for the term.

## How many days notice does a Mississippi landlord need to raise the rent?

The Mississippi Residential Landlord and Tenant Act sets no specific notice period for a rent increase. We read sections 89-8-13, 89-8-17 and 89-8-19, which hold the Act's notice periods. None of them names a day count for a rent change.

So how do you raise the rent on a month-to-month tenant? The safe path uses the notice that ends the tenancy. Under 89-8-19(3), you may end a month-to-month tenancy with written notice at least 30 days before the end date. You can then offer a new agreement at the new rent.

Step list for a month-to-month increase:

1. **Check the lease.** Your lease may set its own notice period for changes. Follow it if it is longer.
2. **Pick the effective date.** Use the first day of a rental period at least 30 days out.
3. **Write the notice.** State the current rent, the new rent and the effective date. Offer the new agreement.
4. **Deliver it in writing.** Section 89-8-19 requires a written notice. Keep proof of the delivery date.
5. **Record the business reason.** A written reason helps if the tenant claims retaliation.

Worked example: you deliver the notice on 2026-11-01 for a tenant who pays on the 1st. The termination date must be at least 30 days later. The first rent date that meets that rule is 2026-12-01, which is exactly 30 days out. To leave a margin, you can pick 2027-01-01.

## Can a Mississippi landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

A Mississippi landlord cannot raise the rent during a fixed-term lease unless the lease allows it. A rental agreement with a definite term expires on the date it states (89-8-17(1)). Until then, the agreed rent applies.

Section 89-8-17(2) speaks to the end of the term. After the lease expires, the landlord may demand an increase in rent. One condition applies: the increase must not have retaliation as its dominant purpose. Section 89-8-17(2) ties that to tenant actions the chapter allows, after the landlord got written notice of each condition.

If you want the right to raise rent mid-term, the lease must say so in clear words. Ask a local attorney to review that clause.

## How much notice ends a month-to-month tenancy in Mississippi?

A Mississippi month-to-month tenancy ends on written notice given at least 30 days before the termination date. The rule is 89-8-19(3), and it applies to the landlord and the tenant alike.

| Tenancy type | Who gives notice | Minimum notice | Statute |
|---|---|---|---|
| Month-to-month | Landlord or tenant | 30 days, written | 89-8-19(3) |
| Week-to-week | Landlord or tenant | 7 days, written | 89-8-19(2) |
| Fixed term | No notice needed to end on the stated date | Ends on the lease date | 89-8-17(1) |
| Any tenancy, health or safety violation | Either side | No notice to terminate required | 89-8-19(4) |

When does a tenancy count as month-to-month? Under 89-8-19(1), a tenancy with no definite term is week-to-week if the tenant pays weekly rent. In all other cases, it is month-to-month.

If a month-to-month tenant does not leave after the notice period, section 89-8-31(b) lets you file an eviction for failing to vacate. Section 89-8-33 requires you to attach a copy of the written termination notice. So keep that notice clean and dated.

## Do cities in Mississippi add their own rent or notice rules?

Mississippi cities cannot add rent control without prior legislative approval under 21-17-5(2)(h). We found no state statute that lets a city change the notice periods in 89-8-19. We did not check every city code. If you own in Jackson, Gulfport, Hattiesburg or another city, read the local code for rules on matters other than rent amounts, such as rental registration or inspections.

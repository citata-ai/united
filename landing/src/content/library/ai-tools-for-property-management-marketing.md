## Which AI tools help with property management marketing?

Five kinds of AI tools cover most marketing work in a property management company: a chat assistant, a design tool, a website chat agent, the AI inside your ad platform, and the AI inside your property management software. You do not need all five. Most teams with 50 to 500 doors start with one chat assistant and one design tool.

Here are the list prices we read on each vendor's page on 2026-10-19.

| Tool | Main marketing use | Free plan | Paid plan we would start with | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT | Drafts, edits, ad copy, report summaries | Yes | Plus, $20/month | OpenAI Help Center |
| Claude | Long drafts, edits, report summaries, files | Yes, $0 | Pro, $20/month or $17/month billed yearly ($200 up front) | claude.com/pricing |
| Gemini | Drafts, Google Docs and Gmail help | Yes, $0 | Google AI Pro, $19.99/month | gemini.google |
| Canva | Social posts, flyers, ad images | Yes, $0/year | Pro, $144/year for one person | canva.com/pricing |
| Tidio (Lyro AI agent) | Website chat that answers visitors | Yes, $0/month | Starter from $24.17/month, 100 billable conversations; Lyro from $32.50/month | tidio.com/pricing |
| Claude Team | Shared workspace for 2+ staff | No | $20/seat/month billed yearly, $25 monthly | claude.com/pricing |

Prices change often. Check the vendor page on the day you buy.

## Which tools write and edit content?

The chat assistants (ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini) write and edit content. For marketing they all do the same core jobs: draft a blog post from your outline, rewrite an owner email, shorten a page, and turn notes into an FAQ.

Pick by where your team already works:

- **Google Workspace team.** Gemini sits in Gmail and Docs, so it has the least friction.
- **Long documents.** Claude and ChatGPT both read long files, such as a management agreement or a year of owner reviews.
- **Shared prompts and files.** A team plan (Claude Team or ChatGPT Business) keeps prompts and files in one place.

Watch for: an assistant does not know your fees, your city rules or your vacancy rate. Google's own guidance says generative models "don't retrieve facts, but predict a likely sequence of words," so output may contain errors (Google Search Central). Give the assistant your facts in the prompt. Then check every number it writes.

## Which tools help with ads and reports?

A design tool and a chat assistant cover most ad and report work. Canva Pro makes ad images, social posts and postcards from a template. A chat assistant drafts headline and description variants for Google Ads or Meta ads.

For reports, paste your monthly numbers into the assistant and ask for a one-page summary. Here is a worked example with numbers from a made-up month:

| Metric | September | October | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website visitors | 2,400 | 2,600 | +8% |
| Owner form leads | 22 | 24 | +9% |
| Calls from website | 31 | 28 | -10% |
| Signed doors | 3 | 5 | +67% |

The assistant turns this into 5 plain sentences in seconds. Your job is to check the math (24 / 2,600 = 0.9% form conversion) and add the reason a number moved. For a benchmark, Goodjuju's fall 2026 report across 35 companies shows an average owner-lead conversion rate of 0.9% (Goodjuju, 2026). Compare your rate with that, not with a guess.

## Which tools answer leads?

Website chat agents answer leads. Tidio's Lyro is one example: it answers visitor questions from content you give it, and its pricing page shows plans by number of AI conversations. Your property management software may also include an AI leasing assistant for tenant questions.

Owner leads need care. An owner who asks "what is your fee?" should get your real fee and a fast handoff to a person. Use this setup checklist:

- **Feed it your fee table.** Paste your exact fees into the chat tool's knowledge. No fee table, no fee answers.
- **Route owners to a person.** Any message with "manage my property" or "my house" goes to a staff member within minutes.
- **Log every chat in your CRM.** A chat lead that is not in the CRM is a lost lead.
- **Separate tenant and owner paths.** Tenants ask about showings and repairs; owners ask about fees and contracts.
- **Turn off promises.** The bot must not promise rent amounts, vacancy times or guarantees.

## What should a person still check?

A person must check facts, fees, law and Fair Housing risk in every AI output before it goes public. AI saves drafting time, not review time.

| Output | What the person checks | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Blog post | Every number, every law, every local fact | Models predict words; they do not check facts |
| Ad copy | Fees, claims, targeting, wording | A housing ad must not steer by a protected class |
| Owner email | Tone, fees, names | A wrong fee breaks trust with an owner |
| Chat answers | Weekly sample of 20 chats | Bots drift as your content changes |
| Report | Math and the reason behind each change | AI cannot know why a number moved |
| Images | People, homes, text in the image | AI images can show a home you do not manage |

Google says it is "critical to manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content for accuracy and trustworthiness before publishing" (Google Search Central). The same rule makes sense for ads, emails and chat.

## How do I pick my first AI tool?

Pick the one task that takes your team the most hours each week, then buy the one tool for that task. For most PM teams that task is writing: owner emails, listing copy and blog posts. One chat assistant at about $20 a month covers it.

Run a 30-day test. Count the hours before and after, and count the errors a person found. If the tool saves 5 hours a month and the person finds no serious errors, keep it.

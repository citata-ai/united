## Does the City of St. Louis require an occupancy permit for each new tenant?

Yes. The City of St. Louis requires a Certificate of Inspection, which owners often call an occupancy permit, before a rental unit is occupied. The City's Building Division says 100% of property in St. Louis is in a Housing Conservation District, so the rule covers the whole city. Ordinance 71835, effective 2024-04-26, sets the rules in City Code Chapter 25.56.

When you need a certificate:

| Event | Certificate needed? |
|---|---|
| A vacant rental unit gets a new tenant | Yes, before move-in |
| A complete change of occupancy in a rental unit | Yes |
| 3 years since the last inspection of a rental unit | Yes, reinspection |
| Sale of an occupied building with no certificate in the last 12 months | Yes |
| Owner-occupied property | No, exempt |

A certificate is valid for 12 months. So if a tenant leaves and a new one moves in within 12 months of a clean inspection, you do not need a new inspection.

Ordinance 71835 counts a home occupied by the owner's child, grandchild, parent, sibling, parent-in-law or grandparent as owner-occupied. A duplex where the owner lives in one unit also counts as owner-occupied. Short-term rentals fall under a separate chapter.

## How do you get a St. Louis Certificate of Inspection?

You get a St. Louis Certificate of Inspection by applying, paying and passing an inspection. The steps on the City's page:

1. **Apply when the unit is ready.** Apply online at stlcitypermits.com or in person at City Hall, 1200 Market, Room 425.
2. **Pick a date.** The earliest inspection is 3 business days after you apply. The latest is 15 days after.
3. **Turn on utilities.** Water and electric must work at the inspection.
4. **Pass the inspection.** The inspector checks the building and sets the unit's occupancy load.
5. **Fix any violations within 30 days.** The City may extend this for weather or other specific causes.

Fees on the City page and in Ordinance 71835:

| Item | Fee |
|---|---|
| Application, vacant unit | $120 |
| Application, unit already occupied without a current certificate | $200 |
| Each more unit at the same address, same date | $65 |
| Card payment online | 3.5% processing fee |

Worked example: a 4-unit building turns over 2 units on the same day. You pay $120 + $65 = $185 for both. If one tenant already moved in, that unit costs $200 instead of $120.

Do not pass the fee to the applicant. Ordinance 71835 makes it unlawful for an owner or agent to require a prospective occupant to apply or pay for the certificate.

## What does the St. Louis inspection check?

The St. Louis inspection checks minimum interior code items and exterior standards under the International Property Maintenance Code. Exhibit A in the City's ordinance file lists the interior checklist:

| Area | What the inspector looks for |
|---|---|
| Sanitation | No trash, debris, rodents or insects |
| Walls and ceilings | No missing or loose plaster; fire assemblies kept up |
| Entry doors | Working locks, doors that latch, self-closers on fire doors |
| Structure | No rotten or broken floors or joists |
| Safety | Enough exits, sound stairs and handrails, working smoke detectors |
| Plumbing | No missing or leaking fixtures; a vented water heater with a relief valve |
| Electrical | Cover plates, working outlets, right-sized breakers, no bare wires |
| Mechanical | A working, vented heating unit |
| Space | Occupancy load and bedroom rules |

The space rules set the occupancy load. Each unit needs at least 150 square feet of habitable floor area for the first occupant plus 100 for each more. Each bedroom needs 70 square feet for the first occupant plus 50 for each more, and the largest bedroom holds at most 2. Every bedroom needs an operable window. The City page also notes that no more than 3 unrelated people may live in one dwelling.

Pre-inspection checklist:

- [ ] Test every smoke detector.
- [ ] Replace missing or cracked outlet and switch covers.
- [ ] Fix loose handrails and broken stair treads.
- [ ] Check the water heater's relief valve and vent.
- [ ] Make sure every bedroom window opens.

## What is the St. Louis rental registration?

The St. Louis rental registration is a new duty in Ordinance 71835. When you apply for a Certificate of Inspection, you will also register the rental unit, and the City will list it in a public database. The fee rules took effect at once in 2024. The registry parts take effect 60 days after the Board of Aldermen adopts a resolution that the system is technically ready, or on 2027-07-01, whichever comes first.

What the registration asks for:

- **Owner details.** Full legal name, phone, mailing address and email. For an LLC, the managing member; for a corporation, an officer.
- **Address** of each rental unit.
- **A local agent** if you do not live in the City. The agent must be an adult who lives within 100 miles of the unit or works at a business office in the City. Your property manager can serve.
- **Updates** each time you apply again.

Penalties: a violation of the chapter can bring a fine of up to $500, up to 90 days in jail, or both. Each day counts as a separate violation.

## How do St. Louis County rental rules differ?

St. Louis County rules differ because the County is a separate government. In unincorporated St. Louis County, and in towns that contract with the County, a safety and health inspection is required at each occupant change. The County's application form adds these points:

- **No move-in before the permit.** The application is not a permit, and the home may not be occupied until the County issues one.
- **Tenant names.** You must give the names of all occupants before the permit is issued, within 120 days of the approved inspection.
- **90 days to fix.** If you do not correct deficiencies within 90 days, the application lapses and you pay again.
- **Fee.** The fee must come with the application and is not refundable. The form does not print the amount; call 314-615-4100.

If the property is inside a town, ask the town whether it runs its own permit or contracts with the County.

Missouri law limits both governments. RSMo 441.043 bars any city or county from regulating the amount of rent, and since 2025-08-28 from limiting security deposits.

## Washington DC rent increase rules at a glance

DC rent control sets a yearly cap, a 60-day notice and a 12-month gap between raises. We read each rule on the official DC Code site (code.dccouncil.gov) and the 2026 caps on the Office of the Tenant Advocate site, both on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute or source |
|---|---|---|
| Cap, most rent-controlled units, May 1, 2026 to April 30, 2027 | 4.1% (CPI-W + 2%) | 42-3502.08(h)(2)(A); OTA |
| Cap, registered elderly or disabled tenant, same year | 2.1% | 42-3502.24(a); OTA |
| Highest cap the formula allows | 10% | 42-3502.06(b), 42-3502.08(h)(2)(A) |
| Notice before an increase takes effect | First rent day more than 60 days after notice | 42-3509.04(b) |
| Time between increases | A full 12 months | 42-3502.08(g) |
| Increase during a fixed-term lease that sets rent | Not allowed | 42-3502.08(e) |
| Vacancy increase | 10%, or 20% after a tenancy over 10 years | 42-3502.13(a) |
| Landlord ends a month-to-month tenancy by notice | Not allowed | 42-3505.54(a), 42-3505.01(a) |
| Tenant ends a month-to-month tenancy | 30-day written notice | 42-3505.54(a) |

## Does Washington DC have rent control?

Washington DC has rent control, which the DC Code calls the Rent Stabilization Program. Each year the Rental Housing Commission sets an "adjustment of general applicability" equal to the CPI-W change for the Washington area, up to 10% (D.C. Code 42-3502.06(b)). CPI-W is the consumer price index for wage earners.

For an occupied unit, the increase may not exceed that CPI-W figure plus 2%, and never more than 10% in total (42-3502.08(h)(2)(A)). For an elderly tenant or a tenant with a disability, the cap is the lowest of CPI-W, the Social Security cost-of-living raise, or 5% (42-3502.24(a)).

Here are the published caps from the DC Office of the Tenant Advocate (OTA):

| Rent Control Year | Dates | Most units | Elderly or disabled |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | May 1, 2025 to April 30, 2026 | 4.8% | 2.5% |
| 2026 | May 1, 2026 to April 30, 2027 | 4.1% | 2.1% |

For 2026, CPI-W was 2.1% and the Social Security raise was 2.8%, so the elderly cap is 2.1% (OTA, 2026-02-02). The tenant gets the lower cap only after registering elderly or disability status with the Rent Administrator.

## Which DC rentals are exempt from rent control?

Many DC rentals are exempt from the caps. D.C. Code 42-3502.05(a) lists the exemptions. The main ones:

- **Newer buildings.** A building permit issued after December 31, 1975, or a new unit with a certificate of occupancy after January 1, 1980.
- **Small owners.** 4 or fewer units, owned by no more than 4 natural persons who hold no interest in any other DC rental unit.
- **Subsidized units.** Federal or DC owned or subsidized housing, and Low-Income Housing Tax Credit units.

The small-owner exemption is not automatic. You must file a claim of exemption with the Rent Administrator. You must also report a change of ownership within 30 days (42-3502.05(a)(3)(C)). Before a new lease on an exempt unit, the tenant must get a written notice that rent increases are not regulated (42-3502.05(d)).

## How many days notice does a DC landlord need to raise the rent?

A DC rent increase takes effect only on the first regular rent day more than 60 calendar days after you give notice (D.C. Code 42-3509.04(b)). D.C. Law 25-65, effective November 28, 2023, last amended this section.

Worked example for a rent-controlled unit. The rent is $2,000, due on the 1st. The tenant is not registered as elderly or disabled.

| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Current rent | $2,000 |
| 2026 cap | 4.1% |
| Highest new rent | $2,000 x 1.041 = $2,082 |
| Notice delivered | June 10, 2026 |
| 60 days later | August 9, 2026 |
| First rent day after that | September 1, 2026 |
| Earliest effective date | September 1, 2026, if the last increase was before September 1, 2025 |

For a registered elderly tenant at the same rent, the cap is 2.1%: $2,000 x 1.021 = $2,042.

Your notice must show the current rent, the new rent and the utilities included (42-3502.08(f)(1)). It must also carry a summary of tenant rights and a list of help sources the Mayor publishes (42-3502.08(f)(2)).

## What must be true before a DC landlord can raise a rent-controlled rent?

A DC landlord can raise a rent-controlled rent only if the conditions in D.C. Code 42-3502.08(a)(1) are met. Use this checklist:

- [ ] The unit and common areas are in substantial compliance with the housing code.
- [ ] The building is registered under 42-3502.05.
- [ ] You hold the license the law requires.
- [ ] A manager other than the owner is registered where the rules require it.
- [ ] The notice meets 42-3509.04 (the 60-day rule).
- [ ] You told the tenant in writing about the elderly and disability cap and how to register.
- [ ] A nonresident owner has a registered agent.
- [ ] 12 full months have passed since the last increase (42-3502.08(g)).

## Can a DC landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

A DC landlord cannot raise the rent during a fixed-term lease that sets the rent. D.C. Code 42-3502.08(e) says no rent adjustment applies to a unit with a valid written lease that sets the rent for its term. Plan your increase for the renewal date, and send the notice at least 60 days before.

When a unit becomes vacant, 42-3502.13(a) allows a one-time increase. It is 10% if the last tenant stayed 10 years or less, and 20% if longer. The new lease must disclose the starting rent and the last 3 years of increases on the Rent Administrator's form (42-3502.13(d)).

## How much notice ends a month-to-month tenancy in DC?

A DC landlord cannot end a residential month-to-month tenancy with a notice alone. D.C. Code 42-3505.54(a) and 42-3202(b) give the 30-day notice right only to the tenant. The tenant's notice ends on the first day of the first month at least 30 days later.

Under 42-3505.01(a)(1), a tenant who keeps paying cannot be evicted just because the lease expired. You need a reason in that section, such as a lease violation or personal use. See our [guides](/guides) for more owner topics.

Watch for: a lease may not make the tenant give more than 30 days' notice to leave. The exception is when the lease gives the tenant a rent increase notice at least 30 days longer than that (42-3505.54(b)).

## Do parts of DC add their own rent or notice rules?

Washington DC has no separate cities or counties. One set of DC Code rules applies across the District. The Rental Housing Commission sets the yearly cap, and the Rent Administrator at the Department of Housing and Community Development handles registration and exemptions.

If you own rent-controlled units in DC, the 12-month clock and the 60-day notice need a calendar per unit. See how a [property manager](/property-management) handles rent changes.

Delaware calls an eviction an "action for summary possession." The rules sit in Title 25 of the Delaware Code, chapters 51 to 57. I read them on Delaware Code Online on 2026-10-19. In 2026 the rules changed: the rent demand went from 5 days to 7, and tenants got a right of redemption.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Demand for unpaid rent | At least 7 days to pay, sent after the late-fee date | § 5502(a) |
| Lease violation notice | At least 7 days to fix the breach | § 5513(a) |
| Harm or threat of irreparable harm | No cure period; immediate termination on notice | § 5513(b) |
| End of a fixed-term lease | At least 60 days' written notice before it expires | § 5106(c) |
| End of a month-to-month lease | At least 60 days, starting the 1st of the next month | § 5106(d) |
| Court | Justice of the Peace Court in the county | § 5701 |
| Mediation | Required before trial in most cases | § 5702A(c) |
| Trial delay | Not more than 10 days without consent of all parties | § 5710 |
| Appeal | Within 5 days after judgment; no writ in that time | § 5717 |
| Writ of possession | Constable or sheriff; at least 24 hours' notice; sunrise to sunset | § 5715(b) |
| Right of redemption | Tenant pays all amounts due before the writ is carried out | § 5716A |
| Illegal lockout | Treble damages or 3 × daily rent per day, whichever is greater | § 5313 |

## What notice must a Delaware landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

A Delaware landlord must send a written demand that gives the tenant at least 7 days to pay before filing (§ 5502(a)). You may send it only after the late-fee date. That date is at least 5 days after rent is due (§ 5501(d)).

The Delaware Courts Right of Redemption page explains the 2026 change. SS 1 to SB 116 raised the demand period from 5 days to 7. It also says landlords may no longer terminate the lease when the tenant does not cure. Instead, the landlord may file for possession.

Payment rules in § 5502:

- **Before you file.** If the tenant, or someone for the tenant, pays all rent due, you may not file on that non-payment.
- **After you file.** If all rent due is paid, you may not continue that non-payment action.
- **No refusal.** You may not refuse rent paid by or for the tenant. Before judgment, you may limit payment to the methods in the lease. After judgment, you may require a certified check or money order.

Delaware tenants with household income at or below 200% of the federal poverty guidelines have a right to legal representation (§ 5602). The state phases it in through June 30, 2028 (§ 5607). With the rent demand, you must give the tenant the approved information about that right (§ 5606(3)). Landlords with 3 or fewer units and no attorney or authorized agent are outside it.

## How long does an eviction take in Delaware?

A Delaware non-payment eviction takes at least 18 days of fixed waiting periods, plus the time the court needs to serve, mediate and try the case. The 18 days is my own sum of the minimum periods in the statute below. Business days make the first step longer.

| Step | Minimum time | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Late-fee date passes | 5 days after rent is due (weekends and holidays excluded) | §§ 5501(d), 5112 |
| 2. Rent demand runs | 7 days | § 5502(a) |
| 3. File, serve, mediate, try | Set by the court; mediation at least 48 hours before trial | §§ 5704, 5702A |
| 4. Appeal period | 5 days after judgment | § 5717(d) |
| 5. Writ served | At least 24 hours' notice | § 5715(b) |

Two things can make it longer. A tenant may appeal and post a bond, which stays the writ. A tenant can also pay in full under the right of redemption, which ends a non-payment case.

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Delaware, and what does it cost?

A Delaware landlord files a complaint for possession in the Justice of the Peace Court that hears civil cases in the county where the unit is (§§ 5701, 5704). The court serves the complaint with a notice of the hearing time and place.

Steps in court:

1. **File the complaint.** Chapter 57 lists what it must contain (§§ 5707, 5708).
2. **Mediation.** The court schedules mediation between filing and trial (§ 5702A(c)). Trial cannot start until you have mediated, unless the tenant does not take part within 15 calendar days after service.
3. **Answer and trial.** The tenant may answer orally or in writing at the hearing (§ 5709). The court may adjourn the trial, but not for more than 10 days unless all parties agree (§ 5710).
4. **Judgment.** The winner gets the costs of the proceeding (§ 5711).

Lease clauses that make the tenant pay your attorney fees are not enforceable in Delaware (§ 5111).

Cost: the Justice of the Peace Court charges a filing fee and a fee to serve the writ. I could not confirm the current amounts on the court's live fee page, so I leave them out. Ask the clerk before you file.

## Can a Delaware landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes, with the right notice. Section 5702 lists the grounds for summary possession, including holding over after the lease ends, unpaid rent, and breach of a lawful obligation.

| Reason | Notice | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Material breach of a rule or covenant | Written notice with at least 7 days to fix it | § 5513(a) |
| Same breach again within 1 year | You may rely on the first notice | § 5513(a)(1) |
| Breach that causes or threatens irreparable harm | Immediate termination on notice | § 5513(b) |
| End of a fixed-term lease (no cause) | At least 60 days' written notice before expiry | § 5106(c) |
| Month-to-month (no cause) | At least 60 days, from the 1st of the next month | § 5106(d) |

For irreparable harm, you can ask the court for a forthwith summons to speed up the case (§ 5115). Those cases also skip the mediation rule (§ 5702A(c)(2)).

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Delaware?

A constable or the county sheriff removes the tenant. After a final judgment for you, and after the appeal period, the court issues a writ of possession to the constable or sheriff (§ 5715(a)). You must tell the constable to act (§ 5715(c)).

The officer gives at least 24 hours' notice to the people inside. The writ is carried out between sunrise and sunset (§ 5715(b)).

If the tenant leaves property behind, you may remove and store it for 7 days at the tenant's expense (§ 5715(e)). Manufactured homes have a 30-day storage rule.

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Delaware?

No. A tenant removed or kept out "except under color of a valid court order" may get back in or end the lease (§ 5313). The tenant may also recover treble damages, or 3 times the daily rent for each day out, whichever is greater, plus court costs.

Example: rent is $1,500 a month, about $50 a day. A 10-day lockout costs at least 3 × $50 × 10 = $1,500, before any proven damages.

Do not shut off utilities either. If the landlord fails to supply heat, water, hot water or electricity for 48 hours after notice, § 5308 lets the tenant end the lease or keep 2/3 of the daily rent. The tenant may also find substitute housing, and the landlord pays part of the extra cost.

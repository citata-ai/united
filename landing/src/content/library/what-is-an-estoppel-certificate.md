## What is an estoppel certificate?

An estoppel certificate is a signed statement in which a tenant confirms the facts of a lease on a given date. Attorney Robert D. Mitchell describes it as "a statement by a party having an interest in property that describes that interest." The tenant signs. The buyer or lender relies on it.

"Estoppel" is a legal word. It means a person is stopped from later saying something different from what they stated before. So a tenant who signs that the rent is $2,000 cannot later claim the landlord agreed to $1,800.

Estoppel certificates are most common in commercial leases. They also show up when someone buys or refinances an apartment building.

## Why do buyers and lenders ask for estoppel certificates?

Buyers and lenders ask for estoppel certificates to check the lease facts and to lock them in. The California Lawyers Association article names 2 purposes. The certificate gives a buyer or lender accurate information. It also assures them that the tenant will not later take inconsistent positions.

Why that matters to the buyer:

| What the buyer fears | What the certificate fixes |
|---------------------|---------------------------|
| A side deal that lowers rent | Tenant confirms the rent and that the lease is not changed |
| A bigger deposit than the seller shows | Tenant confirms the deposit amount |
| A hidden renewal or purchase option | Tenant lists all options |
| A dispute with the old landlord | Tenant states any defaults or claims |
| Prepaid rent the buyer will not receive | Tenant states the date rent is paid through |

A lender has the same concerns. The rent is the income that repays the loan.

## What does an estoppel certificate confirm?

An estoppel certificate confirms the main facts of the lease as of the date the tenant signs. The California Lawyers Association and Robert D. Mitchell list items like these:

- **Lease documents.** The lease and every amendment, listed by date.
- **Key dates.** Start date, any renewal, and end date.
- **Rent.** Current monthly rent and the date rent is paid through.
- **Deposit.** Amount paid and how it may be applied.
- **Options.** Renewal, extension or purchase rights, with notice deadlines.
- **Defaults.** Whether either side is in breach.
- **Claims and offsets.** Any money the tenant says the landlord owes.
- **Transfers.** Any sublease or assignment.
- **Landlord work.** Whether promised improvements are done and allowances paid.

Template (generic, not a legal form; check local law and the lease before use):

```text
TENANT ESTOPPEL CERTIFICATE

Tenant: ______________________   Premises: ______________________
Landlord: ____________________   Date of this certificate: ________

1. The lease is dated ________ and has these amendments: ________________.
2. The lease is in full force and is not changed except as listed in item 1.
3. Lease start date: ________   Lease end date: ________
4. Current monthly rent: $________   Rent is paid through: ________
5. Security deposit held by Landlord: $________
6. Options (renewal, extension, purchase): ______________________
7. Defaults by Landlord or Tenant known to Tenant: [ ] None  [ ] List: ________
8. Claims or offsets against Landlord: [ ] None  [ ] List: ________
9. Subleases or assignments: [ ] None  [ ] List: ________

Tenant signature: ______________________   Name and title: ______________
```

## Why a signed certificate can bind the tenant

A signed certificate can bind the tenant even when it is wrong. That is the key risk. Robert D. Mitchell writes that the statements "may actually be inaccurate and binding upon the tenant."

The California Lawyers Association article describes the case of Plaza Freeway v. First Mountain Bank. A tenant's lease had unclear start and end dates. When the building sold, the tenant signed a certificate with specific dates. Years later, the tenant gave notice to renew. The landlord said the notice was late, based on the certificate dates. The trial court found the true end date was about 8 months later. The Court of Appeal reversed and held the tenant to its certificate. The court noted that "Lenders and buyers rely upon the certificates in finalizing loans and purchases."

Lesson: read every line. Fix errors before you sign. A wrong date can cost a tenant its renewal right.

## Can a tenant refuse to sign one?

A tenant is generally not required to sign an estoppel certificate unless the lease says so. Robert D. Mitchell notes that "a tenant is not required to complete and sign an estoppel agreement" without a lease duty. Most commercial leases include that duty.

Typical lease terms:

| Term | Example from legal articles |
|------|---------------------------|
| Deadline | "within 10 days after request of Landlord" (California Lawyers Association sample); 15 days written notice (Mitchell sample) |
| If the tenant is silent | Statements are treated as true ("silence is consent") |
| Or | Landlord may sign as the tenant's attorney-in-fact |
| Or | A money penalty, such as 1 month's rent, or liability for the landlord's damages |

If the lease requires a certificate and the tenant does not return it, Mitchell says this can be a breach of the lease. The tenant can still correct wrong facts. Signing a certificate the tenant knows is wrong is not the duty. Returning a correct one is.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How a property manager handles estoppel requests

A property manager often prepares estoppel certificates for the owner during a sale or refinance. The manager has the lease file and the ledger, so it can fill most lines fast.

Steps:

1. **Pull the lease file.** Lease, amendments, notices and the tenant ledger.
2. **Pre-fill the form** from the records, not from memory.
3. **Match the ledger.** Rent, paid-through date and deposit must match the books.
4. **Send to each tenant** with the lease deadline stated in the cover note.
5. **Track returns** in a list, by tenant and date.
6. **Flag every change a tenant makes.** A tenant edit can show a side deal or a dispute the buyer must know about.
7. **Give the owner a summary** of open items before closing.

Owners selling a building should start this work early. Ask your manager how long it takes for your number of leases. To see what a manager covers, read our [property management](/property-management) page.

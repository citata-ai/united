## What is a turnkey rental property?

A turnkey rental property is a home that a company buys, renovates and often rents out, then sells to an investor ready to earn rent. "Turnkey" means you turn the key and it works. The buyer does not manage a rehab or find the first tenant.

A typical turnkey deal has 4 parts:

| Part | Who does it | What the buyer gets |
|------|------------|---------------------|
| Find the property | Turnkey provider | A home in a market the provider knows |
| Renovate | Provider's crew or contractors | Updated systems and finishes |
| Lease up | Provider or its manager | A tenant and a signed lease |
| Manage | Provider's manager or a partner | Monthly rent and statements |

Turnkey rentals attract out-of-state buyers, because the buyer may never see the home in person. That is also where most of the risk comes from.

## Who manages a turnkey rental?

A turnkey rental is often managed by the provider itself or by a manager it partners with. This is convenient. It is also a conflict of interest, because the same company that sold you the home now reports on how well it performs.

Your choices:

- **Use the provider's manager.** Fast to start. Ask how the manager is paid and whether it also earns on repairs.
- **Use an independent local manager.** More work to set up, but the manager has no stake in the sale price.
- **Self-manage from far away.** Possible with software and local vendors, but hard without local help.

Questions to ask before you sign:

1. Is the manager a separate company? Who owns it?
2. Can I change managers without a fee? What notice does the agreement require?
3. Does the manager hold a real estate license where the state requires one?
4. Who picks the repair vendors, and does the manager add a markup?
5. Who holds the security deposit, and in which account?

## What are the risks of turnkey providers?

The main risks of turnkey providers are a high price, a weak tenant, hidden repairs and a manager who is not independent. Each one can turn a good-looking deal into a loss.

| Risk | How it shows up | How to check |
|------|----------------|--------------|
| Price above market value | The provider's comps are other turnkey sales | Get your own appraisal and pull sold comps |
| Rent that is not real | A new lease at a high rent, or a tenant placed just for the sale | Ask for the application, pay history and a copy of the lease |
| Cosmetic rehab | New paint and floors hide old systems | Hire your own inspector; ask for permits |
| Weak market | Low price, but high vacancy and slow rent growth | Check vacancy, crime and job data for the area |
| Conflicted manager | The seller's manager reports good news | Use an independent manager or audit the reports |
| Rent guarantee that ends | A "guaranteed rent" for 6 or 12 months, then nothing | Read who pays after the guarantee ends |

Regulators have pursued schemes built on these risks. In 2008, the New Jersey Attorney General sued 36 defendants in mortgage fraud cases that used inflated sale prices. One defendant allegedly recruited buyers with no money down and promised that rental income would cover the mortgage costs. These were allegations in a lawsuit, not proof against any turnkey provider today. They show the pattern to watch for.

Also watch the marketing of your vacant unit. The Federal Trade Commission warns that scammers copy real rental listings and repost them with their own contact details. Search your address online during each vacancy.

## A worked example: check the numbers

Use your own numbers, not the provider's pro forma. This example uses made-up numbers for a turnkey house.

| Line | Provider's sheet | Your check |
|------|-----------------|-----------|
| Price | $160,000 | Appraisal: $145,000 |
| Monthly rent | $1,450 | Market comps: $1,325 |
| Yearly rent | $17,400 | $15,900 |
| Vacancy | 0% ($0) | 6% (−$954) |
| Repairs and reserves | −$600 ($50 a month) | −$1,800 ($150 a month) |
| Management fee | 8% (−$1,392) | 10% (−$1,495) |
| Taxes and insurance | −$2,500 | −$2,500 |
| Yearly NOI (before mortgage) | $12,908 | $9,151 |
| Cap rate (NOI / price) | 8.1% | 6.3% |

The same house looks like an 8.1% deal on the provider's sheet and 6.3% on yours. The gap comes from the price, the rent and the cost lines. Ask for the source of every number.

## Is a turnkey rental passive income?

A turnkey rental is less work, but it is not fully passive. The owner still makes decisions: approve large repairs, review statements, choose whether to renew a lease, and decide when to sell.

Time an owner still spends, even with a manager:

- **Each month:** read the owner statement and check rent against the rent roll.
- **Each repair over your limit:** approve or decline.
- **Each turnover:** approve the make-ready budget and the list price.
- **Each year:** review insurance, property tax and the manager's results.

For tax, "passive" has its own meaning. IRS Publication 925 says "A rental activity is a passive activity even if you materially participated in that activity", unless you qualify as a real estate professional. It also allows up to $25,000 of rental loss for owners who actively participate. That amount phases out as modified adjusted gross income rises above $100,000 and is generally gone at $150,000. This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current rules or ask a tax professional.

## Turnkey rental checklist before you buy

1. **Get your own appraisal**, not one the provider arranges.
2. **Pull sold comps** for similar homes that are not turnkey sales.
3. **Hire your own inspector**, and ask for the rehab scope and permits.
4. **Verify the tenant:** application, lease, move-in date and 6 to 12 months of pay history if any.
5. **Check market rent** with independent comps.
6. **Check the area:** vacancy, jobs, schools and insurance cost.
7. **Read the purchase contract** for any rule that ties you to the provider's manager.
8. **Read the management agreement:** fees, term, cancellation and repair limits.
9. **Check licenses** of the provider and manager in the state's lookup.
10. **Search court records** for the company and its owners.
11. **Talk to 3 past buyers** whom you find yourself, not only the provider's references.
12. **Visit once**, or pay a local agent for a video walk-through.

North Dakota rent rules are short. Chapter 47-16 of the North Dakota Century Code holds all of them. I read the official chapter text on 2026-10-19, through the Internet Archive copy of the ndlegis.gov PDF captured 2026-10-03, because ndlegis.gov did not answer from our network.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## North Dakota rent and notice rules at a glance

The table below sums up each rule, its limit or deadline, and the statute that sets it.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Statewide rent cap | None | No statute sets one |
| Local rent control | Banned | N.D. Cent. Code 47-16-02.1 |
| Rent change, month-to-month lease | Written notice at least 30 days before the month ends | 47-16-07 |
| Tenant exit after a rent change | At least 25 days' notice, effective at month end | 47-16-15(3) |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | At least 1 calendar month's written notice | 47-16-15(2) |
| Lease converted to month-to-month | End on the last day of a month, 1 calendar month's notice | 47-16-15(5) |
| Auto-renewal clause, lease of 2 months or more | Written notice of the clause at least 30 days before expiry | 47-16-06.1 |
| Notice longer than 1 month for the tenant | Tenant must initial the clause | 47-16-15(4) |

## Does North Dakota have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

North Dakota has no cap on rent increases and no rent control. No state statute limits how much a landlord can raise the rent. Section 47-16-02.1 also stops local rules: a political subdivision "may not enact, maintain, or enforce" an ordinance that controls rent for private residential or commercial property.

The ban has one carve-out. A city or county can still manage rent on residential property that it owns in fee title. That covers public property, not private rentals.

The market sets the amount. The statute sets the timing and the form of the notice.

## How many days notice does a North Dakota landlord need to raise the rent?

A North Dakota landlord needs at least 30 days' written notice before the end of the month to raise rent on a month-to-month lease. Section 47-16-07 says the change takes effect "at the expiration of the month". If the tenant stays after that month, the new rent becomes part of the lease.

The statute is flexible on delivery. Notice "may be served in any reasonable manner which actually informs the tenant". Keep proof that the tenant got it, such as a signed receipt or an email reply.

### Worked example: the latest date to send notice

You want a new rent to start on January 1. The month that ends is December, which ends on December 31.

| Step | Date |
|---|---|
| Month that ends | December 31 |
| Count back 30 days | December 1 |
| Latest safe date for written notice | December 1 or earlier |
| New rent due | January 1 |

Send the notice a few days early. A late notice pushes the change to the end of the next month.

The tenant can respond by leaving. Section 47-16-15(3) lets the tenant end the lease at the end of the month with at least 25 days' notice after a change under section 47-16-07.

## Can a North Dakota landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

Section 47-16-07 gives a notice right for month-to-month leases only. Chapter 47-16 gives no separate right to change the rent in the middle of a fixed term. For a fixed-term lease, the signed lease controls. If the lease has no clause that permits a rent change, plan the new rent for the renewal.

Two rules affect the renewal:

1. **Holdover.** Under section 47-16-06, a residential tenant who stays after the lease ends, while the landlord takes rent, is presumed to hold a month-to-month tenancy. Then the 30-day rule in section 47-16-07 applies.
2. **Automatic renewal.** For a lease of 2 months or more, section 47-16-06.1 says a landlord cannot enforce an auto-renewal clause without written notice of it. The notice goes by hand or first-class mail, at least 30 days before the lease expires. Without that notice, the lease converts to month-to-month.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in North Dakota?

Either side can end a North Dakota month-to-month tenancy with at least 1 calendar month's written notice. Section 47-16-15(2) allows the notice "at any time". The parties can agree in writing to a longer period or a different notice time. Rent is due up to and including the date the tenancy ends.

Section 47-16-15 adds 2 rules for landlords who write long notice clauses:

- **Initial the clause.** If a lease asks the tenant to give more than 1 month's notice from the end of a month, the lease must state it and give space for the tenant's initials. If the tenant did not initial it, the tenant can leave on the last day of a month with 1 calendar month's notice.
- **Converted leases.** When a lease converts to month-to-month under section 47-16-06 or 47-16-06.1, either side can end it on the last day of a month with 1 calendar month's notice.

### Checklist before you send a rent or end notice

- [ ] Lease type confirmed: month-to-month, fixed term, or converted.
- [ ] Notice is in writing.
- [ ] Date is at least 30 days before the month ends (rent change).
- [ ] Or at least 1 calendar month ahead (end of tenancy).
- [ ] Auto-renewal notice sent 30 days before expiry, if the lease has that clause.
- [ ] Long tenant notice clauses carry the tenant's initials.
- [ ] Proof of delivery kept in the tenant file.

## Do cities in North Dakota add their own rent or notice rules?

North Dakota cities cannot add rent control. Section 47-16-02.1 bans any local ordinance that controls the amount of rent. That covers Fargo, Bismarck, Grand Forks, Minot and every other political subdivision.

The state statute does not bar every local housing rule. I did not find or verify any local notice ordinance for this guide. Check with the city before you rely on state rules alone. Fair housing rules apply to every rent change: set increases the same way for every tenant in the same situation.

## What should a North Dakota property manager do with these rules?

Rent changes in North Dakota fail on timing, not on amount. Three steps cover most of the risk:

1. **Track lease type** for each unit, because the notice rule depends on it.
2. **Set alerts** 45 days before each month-end change and each lease expiry.
3. **Use one written template** for rent notices, with a clear start date and new amount.

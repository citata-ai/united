## What work does a lease renewal include?

A lease renewal includes 4 to 6 tasks that take real staff time. When you list them, the fee stops looking like a charge for a signature.

Good Life Property Management, a San Diego manager, describes its renewal steps on its fee page: it contacts the tenant before the lease ends, runs a rental survey to set the new rent, and drafts the renewal at that rent. Northpoint Asset Management adds a renewal negotiation, updated lease language for current law, and a documented mid-lease inspection.

Here is a full renewal task list you can put in your management agreement:

- **Check the lease end date** 90 to 120 days ahead, so you have time to act.
- **Run a rent survey** with 3 to 5 nearby comparable rentals.
- **Send the owner a recommendation**: the new rent, the term, and any changes.
- **Talk with the tenant** about the offer and their plans.
- **Inspect the unit**, inside, with photos.
- **Draft and sign** the renewal or the extension addendum.
- **Update the software**: new rent, new dates, new charges.

Owner-side writers see it the other way. Landager (April 2026) calls the renewal fee an "autopilot charge" and says much of the work is now automatic in software. That view is common with owners. Your answer is the task list, done and shown in the owner portal.

Fee guides give wider ranges. Northpoint says most managers charge $200 to $500, or 10% to 25% of one month's rent. Home365 says $100 to $500 flat, or 25% to 50% of one month's rent. Both note that some managers waive the fee.

Watch for: these are published list prices. A written quote can differ, so confirm before you use them in a sales talk.

## Flat renewal fee or percent of rent?

A flat renewal fee is the better default for most companies. The work takes about the same time on a $1,400 unit and a $3,000 unit. A percent of rent grows with rent, but your cost does not.

| Factor | Flat fee ($150–$300) | Percent of rent (10%–50% of 1 month) |
|---|---|---|
| Matches your cost | Yes, the work is the same per lease | No, it grows with rent |
| Easy to explain | Yes, one number | Harder, owners must calculate |
| Owner pushback | Lower | Higher on high-rent homes |
| Revenue on a $3,000 home | $150–$300 | $300–$1,500 |
| Revenue on a $1,200 home | $150–$300 | $120–$600 |

Landager tells owners to reject a percent-based renewal fee outright and ask for a flat cap. If you sell to owners who read guides like that, a flat fee removes one objection before the first call.

A third option is to include renewals in a premium plan. Legacy does this on its Complete plan. That turns the renewal into a reason to pick the higher tier.

## How do owners react to renewal fees?

Owners accept a renewal fee when it is clear in the agreement and tied to a visible result. They push back when it looks like a charge for an e-signature.

Three things change the reaction:

1. **Show the result.** Send the owner the rent survey and the signed renewal. Good Life says it keeps an 85% renewal rate across its portfolio and ties that to its renewal process.
2. **Name the alternative.** Northpoint puts it plainly: a renewal skipped costs a full turnover. That means vacancy, make-ready work and a new leasing fee.
3. **Cap it.** A flat fee with no surprises is easier to defend than a percent.

Do not hide the fee in the fine print. An owner who finds a surprise fee at month 12 is an owner who starts to look for a new manager.

## Can a renewal fee replace a leasing fee in revenue?

No. A renewal fee is much smaller than a leasing fee, so it cannot replace it per event. But renewals protect the larger revenue line: your monthly management fee.

Here is a worked example for 100 doors. The inputs are examples, not market data: $2,000 rent, a 50% leasing fee (the low end of the 50% to 100% range in the Northpoint and Home365 guides), and a $195 renewal fee.

| Scenario | Renewals | Turnovers | Renewal revenue | Leasing revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60% of leases renew | 60 | 40 | 60 × $195 = $11,700 | 40 × $1,000 = $40,000 |
| 75% of leases renew | 75 | 25 | 75 × $195 = $14,625 | 25 × $1,000 = $25,000 |

Fee revenue goes down when more tenants renew. Your cost also goes down: 15 fewer turnovers means 15 fewer listings, showing rounds and move-in inspections. Each turnover also puts a door at risk. An owner who sits through a long vacancy may blame you and leave.

What to do: model your own numbers. Use your real renewal rate and real leasing fee. Then price the renewal so that it covers the staff hours of the task list above, plus margin. ## How to add a renewal fee to your agreement

Add a renewal fee in your next agreement update, not in the middle of a term. Change the agreement in writing and get the owner's signature.

1. **Write the task list** into the agreement, next to the fee.
2. **Pick a flat amount** that covers the staff time.
3. **Decide the plan rule**: charged on every plan, or included in your top plan.
4. **Tell current owners** 60 days before the change, with the reason.
5. **Report each renewal** in the owner statement, with the rent survey attached.

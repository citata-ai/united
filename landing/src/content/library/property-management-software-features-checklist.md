## Which accounting features are must-have?

Require accounting features that explain money and control its movement. Choose the exact acceptance checks with the person responsible for your books.

The vendors' own [AppFolio](https://www.appfolio.com/pricing), [Buildium](https://www.buildium.com/pricing/), [Rentvine](https://www.rentvine.com/pricing) and [DoorLoop](https://www.doorloop.com/pricing) pages describe broad accounting functions. Read 2026-10-19, those labels do not establish equal scope.

Use this core checklist:

| Required result | Test |
|---|---|
| Explain cash | Reconcile a period with outstanding items |
| Explain client funds | Trace owner and property detail |
| Control payments | Separate preparation from approval |
| Correct errors | Reverse an entry with clear history |
| Hold funds | Show reserves and relevant liabilities |
| Retain evidence | Export transactions and source documents |

Rentvine's [accounting page](https://www.rentvine.com/features/trust-accounting) explicitly describes native three-way reconciliation. Ask it to prove that result. Ask other vendors to show the complete equivalent process rather than only bank matching.

Do not select on an audit-ready label. Your own setup, records and review process need their own checks.

## Which owner portal features matter most?

An owner portal should make financial and property information understandable without exposing another owner's records. Test the owner's view, not only the staff screen.

Start with statements, documents, contributions and distributions. Ask an owner-side user to find the latest statement and explain a repair charge. Check whether the supporting invoice is easy to locate.

Then test an owner with several properties. Can the owner see a combined result and property detail? Ask how shared ownership appears.

Use an access checklist:

- **Invitation:** the owner can enter through a clear process.
- **Permissions:** the owner sees only authorized records.
- **Reports:** statement periods and balances are clear.
- **Documents:** shared files remain readable.
- **Payments:** contributions and distributions have visible status.
- **Help:** the owner knows where to ask questions.

A dedicated owner app is optional unless your service needs it. A phone-friendly portal may be enough, but test that experience.

Do not promise real-time visibility if staff or vendors update records manually. Set the owner expectation to match the actual process.

## Which maintenance features save useful work?

Maintenance features matter when they keep the request, decision, vendor and invoice connected. Measure completed work rather than the number of automated messages.

Ask the vendor to handle a request with photos and incomplete information. Check how staff ask follow-up questions. Then assign an approved vendor.

Your required workflow should name the next action and responsible person. A status label without ownership can leave work stalled.

Use this maintenance checklist:

| Step | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Intake | Correct tenant, property and problem |
| Review | Urgency and missing details recorded |
| Approval | Spending authority and decision |
| Dispatch | Vendor receives scope and access |
| Completion | Notes, photos and invoice attached |
| Accounting | Bill reaches the right property and owner |

Check how urgent cases reach staff. Keep your emergency instructions clear during any automation trial.

Optional tools include advanced inspections, routing and outside contact services. Buy them only after testing the gap in the core process.

Ask who supports a partner tool when the connection fails. Included work and paid outside work should remain separate in the quote.

## Which leasing features do you need?

Leasing software should move a prospect into a complete tenancy without losing the record of decisions and documents. The required functions depend on how your team leases.

Start with listing data, inquiries, applications, screening access, signatures and renewal tracking. Ask which actions the core plan includes.

Buildium and AppFolio describe leasing in their entry offers. DoorLoop and Rentvine also describe applications and digital leasing. Test the actual plan rather than assuming every advanced feature is included.

Use a realistic case. Change listing availability, receive a question, schedule a tour and create the agreement. Check whether the correct rent and dates reach the final document.

Use sample records in the demo. Keep private applicant information out of sales tests. Apply your approved housing criteria consistently.

Showing automation and listing distribution can be optional when your current process already works. If you need a connection, test a real status change and failure recovery.

Do not treat a lease-template label as approval for every local agreement. Ask a qualified local adviser to review the document when needed.

## Which AI features are worth paying for?

AI is worth paying for when a measured result improves your work after review and correction. A generated draft or reply count does not prove completed work.

AppFolio lists Realm-X tools by plan. Rentvine describes an assistant and maintenance functions. DoorLoop's current plans add different levels of agent work. Ask what is live and included.

Start with a narrow task such as drafting a message or extracting invoice fields. Show the source beside the output. Keep approval on actions that change charges or payments during evaluation.

Track these items:

- **Completion:** the task stays finished after review.
- **Time:** preparation, checking and correction.
- **Accuracy:** wrong facts and missing details.
- **Escalation:** unusual cases reach the right person.
- **Price:** platform, usage and required services.

Label your expected labor value as a planning assumption. Do not treat a vendor's savings claim as your own result.

AI for internal work differs from visibility in public AI answers. Read [how owners choose a manager with AI](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai) for that separate subject.

## How do you separate required and optional features?

A required feature supports a task your business must complete; an optional feature improves a process that already works. Write the task before assigning the label.

Give each requirement an owner and acceptance result. Mark the offered plan, add-on and manual work needed. Leave unresolved gaps visible.

Keep the checklist short enough that daily users can review it. A long list of untested checkmarks adds little value.

Compare total cost only after the required tasks pass. A feature-rich offer can still fail a basic export or approval requirement.

Use the [AppFolio and Buildium guide](/guides/appfolio-vs-buildium) to see how plan access can differ. Use the [fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to check affordability.

Ask finalists to complete the same workflow. Choose the product with clear records, usable controls and a price your current business can support.

## What is the yearly cap in Montgomery County?

The Montgomery County rent cap for regulated units is 5.2% from July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027. The Department of Housing and Community Affairs (DHCA) sets it each year from the CPI-U for the Washington-Arlington-Alexandria area. The formula is CPI-U plus 3%, or a flat 6%, whichever is lower.

| Year (July 1 to June 30) | CPI-U | Maximum rent increase allowance |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-27 | 2.2% | 5.2% |
| 2025-26 | 2.7% | 5.7% |
| 2024-25 | 3.3% | 6% |

Source: DHCA, "Rent Stabilization Increases and Limitations."

Rules that shape the math, per DHCA and Bill 15-23:

1. **Once every 12 months.** Increases happen only at lease renewal or a new lease. Rent stays fixed through a multi-year lease.
2. **90 days' notice.** For both regulated and exempt units, give written notice at least 90 days ahead, by US mail or in person with a signed receipt.
3. **Banking.** If you raise less than the allowance, the rest is banked for that unit and does not expire.
4. **10% ceiling.** With banked rent, the total increase may not exceed 10% of current rent.

A worked example: a regulated 2-bedroom at $2,438, the HUD FY2027 fair market rent for the Washington metro, can rise by 5.2%, or $126.78 a month. If you skipped last year's 5.7%, you could add banked rent, but the total stops at 10%, or $243.80. See the [Washington, DC fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/washington-dc-fair-market-rent-2027).

Rent stabilization does not apply in the Cities of Gaithersburg, Rockville and Takoma Park, or the Towns of Barnesville and Laytonsville, per DHCA. Owners there should contact the town or city.

## Which buildings are exempt?

Montgomery County rent stabilization covers county-licensed rental units that are at least 23 years old, unless an exemption applies. DHCA measures age from the "Year Built" on the Maryland SDAT record. A unit turns 23 on January 1 of the 23rd year after that date.

Section 29-60 in Bill 15-23 lists the exemptions. The main ones for private owners:

| Exempt from the cap | Bill 15-23, section 29-60(a) |
|---|---|
| A newly built unit offered for rent for less than 23 years | (1) |
| An accessory dwelling unit | (10) |
| A building built for only 2 units, one of which the owner lives in as a principal residence | (9) |
| An owner-occupied group house | (4) |
| A unit under a government agreement that restricts it to low and moderate income tenants | (11) |
| A unit in a substantially renovated building, renovated within the prior 23 years and in code compliance, under DHCA rules | (12) |
| A unit of a landlord who owns 2 or fewer rental units in the county and is a natural person, or a decedent's trust or estate | (13) |

The list also covers health care facilities, shelters, religious facilities, hotels, dorms and assisted living.

Watch for: the small-owner exemption counts all your units in the county. A person who owns 3 condos is not exempt, even for the first one. An LLC is not a natural person.

## Can I raise rent more than the cap?

Yes, in two cases, with DHCA approval. Bill 15-23 allows:

- **Fair return.** A landlord may apply to raise rent above the allowance. The Director must grant it if the increase is needed for a fair return, under DHCA rules.
- **Capital improvement surcharge.** For permanent structural changes, not ordinary repairs. If the work affects only some units, the surcharge is split among them, spread over at least 60 months, and capped at 15% of base rent. It ends once the cost is recovered.

Properties that DHCA designates as "troubled" or "at risk" face a stricter limit tied to habitability costs.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Must owners get a Montgomery County rental license?

Yes. Every Montgomery County rental needs a DHCA rental housing license each year, and you must have it before you rent or advertise. The license year runs July 1 to June 30. Renewal payments are due by July 31, even if the invoice did not reach you.

| FY27 license (from 2026-07-01) | Fee |
|---|---|
| Single-family detached house, townhouse, duplex, quadraplex | $130 per unit or structure |
| Apartment complex unit | $82 |
| Condominium and other Class I units | $73 |
| Accessory dwelling unit (Class III, new) | $125 license + $260 filing + $230 sign = $615 |
| Short-term rental | $500 |

Source: DHCA, "Rental Housing License Fees." Fees are not prorated. Card payments carry a 2.3% processor fee.

Renting without a license is a class A violation. DHCA lists a $500 civil fine for a first offense and $750 for repeat offenses. You also cannot sue a tenant for unpaid rent in the District Court of Maryland without a license.

## How does it compare with Prince George's County rules?

Prince George's County has its own law, the Permanent Rent Stabilization and Protection Act (PRSA). Its cap is a bit higher this year, and its exemption date is fixed.

| Item | Montgomery County | Prince George's County |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-27 cap, most units | 5.2% | 5.7% |
| Senior housing | Same rule | 2.7% for age-restricted senior housing |
| Formula | CPI-U + 3%, max 6% | CPI-U + 3%, max 6% (senior: CPI-U, max 4.5%) |
| New construction exemption | Units offered for rent under 23 years | Units completed on or after 2000-01-01 |
| Agency | DHCA | DPIE |

Source: Prince George's County DPIE PRSA page. The county says it is still drafting regulations, so check for updates.

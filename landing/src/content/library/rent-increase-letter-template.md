## How much notice do you need before a rent increase?

The notice period depends on the state, the type of tenancy and, in some states, the size of the increase. Check the rule before you set the date in the letter. Count from the day the tenant receives the notice, so use a delivery method that proves the date.

| State | Notice rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| California (month-to-month) | 30 days if the increase is 10% or less over 12 months; 90 days if more than 10% | Civil Code 827 |
| Washington | At least 90 days, in writing | Washington Attorney General |
| New York (increase of 5% or more) | 30 days if under 1 year of occupancy; 60 days for 1–2 years; 90 days for 2+ years | Real Property Law 226-C |
| Oregon | Cap of 10% for 2027 under ORS 90.323; check ORS for the notice period | Oregon OEA |

California Civil Code 827 lets you serve the notice in person or by mail under the Code of Civil Procedure. The California Attorney General adds that a call, text or email is not enough. In New York, Real Property Law 226-C sets the 30, 60 or 90 days. It applies to a renewal offer with an increase of 5% or more.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Copy-ready rent increase letter template

Copy the letter below. Fill in each bracket. Delete any line that does not apply.

> [Date]
>
> [Tenant full name(s)]
> [Property address, unit]
>
> **Notice of rent change**
>
> Dear [Tenant name],
>
> Thank you for renting [property address] with us. This letter is written notice of a change to your monthly rent.
>
> - Current monthly rent: $[amount]
> - New monthly rent: $[amount]
> - Change: $[amount] per month ([percent]%)
> - New rent starts: [date]
>
> All other terms of your lease stay the same, unless a new lease says otherwise.
>
> Why the rent changes: [one or two sentences, for example "Property tax went up by $[amount] and insurance by $[amount] this year."]
>
> Your options:
>
> 1. Do nothing. The new rent starts on [date] and you pay it the same way as now.
> 2. Sign the attached renewal by [date] to keep the new rent for [12] months.
> 3. If you plan to move, tell us in writing by [date], as your lease requires.
>
> Questions? Call or email [manager name] at [phone] or [email]. We answer within [1 business day].
>
> Sincerely,
> [Name, title]
> [Company name, license number if required]
> [Mailing address]
>
> Delivered by: [in person / first-class mail / certified mail] on [date].

This is a generic template, not a state legal form. Some cities require their own form or extra text. Check your local law before you use it.

## How do you explain a rent increase to a good tenant?

Tell a good tenant the amount, the reason and the choice, in that order. A tenant who stays saves the turnover cost: vacancy, cleaning, paint and leasing fees. A clear letter is part of that saving.

Use these 5 rules:

1. **Lead with the number.** Put the old rent, the new rent and the date in the first lines.
2. **Give one real reason.** Name the cost that changed. Do not say "market conditions" unless you can show the comps.
3. **Offer a choice.** A 12-month renewal at the new rent, or month-to-month at a higher rent, if your law allows.
4. **Keep the tone the same for every tenant.** Use the same letter and the same rules for all units. The Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. 3604(b), forbids different rental terms based on race, color, religion, sex, familial status or national origin. Section 3604(f) adds disability.
5. **Call first for long-term tenants.** A 2-minute call before the letter arrives prevents most angry replies.

Here is a worked example. A tenant pays $1,800. You raise it by 4%:

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Increase amount | $1,800 × 4% | $72 per month |
| New rent | $1,800 + $72 | $1,872 |
| Extra rent in 12 months | $72 × 12 | $864 |

If the tenant leaves and the unit sits empty for one month, you lose $1,872 of rent. That is more than double the $864 the increase brings in one year. So price the increase with the turnover cost in mind.

## Can you raise rent during a fixed-term lease?

Read the lease first. A fixed-term lease usually sets the rent for the full term. If the lease has no clause that allows a change, plan the increase for the renewal date.

Some states add rules on top of the lease. The Washington Attorney General says a landlord may not raise rent at all during the first 12 months of a tenancy. That rule applies to both month-to-month and fixed-term leases.

For a month-to-month tenancy, the notice rules in the table above apply to each increase. Send the letter so the tenant gets it before the notice period starts to run.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What local rent caps should you check?

Check state caps, then city rules, before you choose the amount. A letter with an increase above the cap can be void. In some places it can also lead to a claim.

| Place | Cap | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| California (statewide) | 5% plus the change in cost of living, or 10%, whichever is lower, over 12 months | Tenant Protection Act; some units are exempt, local rules can be stricter (California AG) |
| Oregon | 10% for 2027; 9.5% for 2026 | Lesser of 10% or 7% plus CPI, for tenancies under ORS 90.323 (Oregon OEA) |
| Washington | 9.683% for 2026 | Lesser of 10% or 7% plus CPI, over 12 months (Washington AG) |

The California Attorney General lists exempt units. Examples include some single-family homes not owned by a corporation or REIT, and units with a certificate of occupancy from the past 15 years. Section 8 voucher tenancies in market-rate housing are not exempt.

If you manage voucher units, the housing authority must also approve the new rent. The [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator) and the guide to [Section 8 rent limits](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) help you check the numbers first.

## What should you do after you send the letter?

Record the notice, then follow up. Log the date, the method and a copy of the letter on the tenant file. Set a reminder 2 weeks before the new rent starts. If the tenant has not replied, call once to confirm they know the new amount.

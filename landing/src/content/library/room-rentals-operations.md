## One lease or a lease per room?

Choose the lease structure only after you confirm the property's lawful use. A shared whole-home lease and separate room leases create different management questions. Have local counsel review the arrangement rather than swapping the unit number for a bedroom label.

In a room-lease model, identify the private room and shared areas. State which rent and other obligations belong to that tenant. Explain whether the owner can lease other rooms to new occupants. Avoid promising a permanent household composition that your operating model cannot maintain.

In a whole-home arrangement, ask counsel how responsibility, replacement tenants and releases work. A departing roommate should not be removed from your records merely because another roommate sends a message. Get the correct agreement and signatures before changing the lease file.

Texas Property Code 92.010 sets adult occupancy limits with stated exceptions. That rule does not itself approve a rooming-house use. Check local building and use rules separately. In Washington, RCW 59.18.060 includes duties for shared areas. Different laws answer different parts of the operating problem.

Before advertising, confirm the approved room count and safety layout with the relevant local authority. Do not count a storage area as a bedroom merely because a bed fits.

## How do I split utilities?

Split utilities only through a lawful written method that tenants can understand before signing. Start with the actual meters and provider accounts. A billing formula cannot correct an unclear service arrangement.

List every service and identify the account holder. State whether rent includes it or a separate charge applies. Explain the calculation and supporting records. Ask counsel to check state utility allocation and disclosure rules before you use a ratio or submeter system.

| Billing question | Decision to write down |
|---|---|
| Which services are shared? | Name each service and account arrangement |
| How is cost divided? | State the lawful formula and billing period |
| What happens during vacancy? | Explain the owner's share without shifting it by surprise |
| How are errors handled? | Give a contact and correction process |
| How are final bills closed? | Match the lawful charge to the tenancy period |

Do not make one tenant the informal collector for everyone unless you have reviewed and agreed to the arrangement. That can turn a provider bill into a roommate debt dispute. Keep the owner's records clear enough to show each charge.

Provide a way to review an unusually high bill. Check leaks, billing errors and changes in service before accusing a tenant of waste. Record the cause and any correction. Avoid changing the method after the bill arrives.

## How do I handle conflict between roommates?

Handle roommate complaints through a consistent fact-gathering process. Ask what happened, where, when and who observed it. Separate a preference disagreement from a safety issue or an alleged lease breach.

Give each affected person a chance to respond. Keep reports private where practical. Do not forward a complaint thread to the whole household without considering what information it contains. Share only what is needed to address the issue fairly.

Write a clear distinction between house expectations and enforceable lease terms. A preference for a spotless kitchen may need discussion. A documented hazard may need a prompt maintenance response. Do not issue a legal notice merely because a roommate requests it.

The Fair Housing Act, in 42 USC 3604, prohibits discrimination in covered rentals. Apply that duty when handling complaints and replacement applications. Avoid assumptions based on national origin, family status or another protected trait. Seek local advice about any claimed exemption rather than assuming all shared homes are exempt.

For disputed charges or repeated conduct, keep a dated record of observations and responses. Ask counsel which notice process applies before taking enforcement action. Do not allow a group vote to replace that process.

## Which house rules go in the lease?

House rules should address observable conduct and shared use in clear terms. Explain the rule before the tenant signs. Avoid rules that depend on staff judgments about whether someone fits the household.

Include cleaning duties, waste disposal, kitchen use, storage and access to shared facilities. State how guests and overnight visits work, subject to local law. Explain smoking, noise and parking terms if they apply. Keep any fee or charge tied to lawful, reviewed language.

Define private space and keys. A shared front door does not make a tenant's room an open-access area. Have counsel check entry notice, locks and safety requirements. Give maintenance staff a map so work in common space does not lead to an unplanned room entry.

Washington RCW 59.18.060 requires covered landlords to keep common areas reasonably clean, sanitary and safe under its terms. A tenant cleaning schedule does not settle the owner's duties. Inspect shared systems through a lawful process and maintain a clear repair route.

Give every tenant the same current rules. Keep signed versions with each lease. Record approved exceptions and changes. Do not let a handwritten kitchen sign silently replace the agreement.

## How do I manage applications and room turnover?

Manage each room's records separately while keeping shared-property issues in a common file. Track the tenant, room, rent, deposit, access devices and move-in condition. Keep shared bills and maintenance records linked to the affected rooms.

Use written screening criteria. The FTC explains that consumer-report use can require adverse action notices. Apply the process to room applicants when applicable. Do not skip the review because another resident recommends the person.

At turnover, document the room and any relevant common-area condition. Do not divide unexplained damage among all tenants just because responsibility is uncertain. Review the evidence, agreements and local law before charging anyone.

Plan showings with current residents' rights in mind. Explain the route and affected areas. Avoid asking an outgoing tenant to provide access for strangers without a reviewed arrangement. Keep the listing accurate about shared amenities and existing service limits.

Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to include management costs in the owner's budget. Start with a room map and a sample lease file. Confirm that staff can explain every private right, shared duty and billing step from those records.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

The 2021 Rental Housing Finance Survey counted 19.3 million rental properties in the US. About 86% held only one rental unit. Individual investors owned about 70% of all rental properties. Only about 22% of small properties with 1 to 4 units had professional management (HUD and Census, 2022).

So most owners you could serve are individuals with one or two homes, managing on their own. They are not all good clients for you. An ideal owner profile is the tool that tells your marketing which ones to chase.

## What makes an owner a good fit?

A good-fit owner has a property you can manage well, goals you can meet and a budget that covers your real cost. Fit is about the match, not about the owner as a person.

Traits of a good-fit owner for a typical single-family manager:

| Trait | Good fit | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Inside your service area | Drive time eats margin |
| Property type | The type you manage most | Your systems and vendors fit |
| Rent level | At or above your minimum fee threshold | A percent fee on a low rent may not cover your cost |
| Condition | Rent-ready, or owner accepts a make-ready plan | Fewer disputes and vacancies |
| Goal | Hands-off income, long hold | Stays for years |
| Repairs | Agrees to a repair approval limit | You can act fast on small jobs |
| Fees | Read your fee page before the call | No surprise at signing |
| Doors | One now, more likely later | Grows with you |

Use your own data to set each line. Pull your last 20 owners who stayed longer than 2 years. Write down what they had in common. That is the start of your profile.

## Which owner traits predict churn?

Owner traits that predict churn are the ones that clash with how you work. No public study gives churn rates by owner type, so treat these as warning signs from practice, not as statistics:

1. **Price shopping only.** The first question is "What is your lowest fee?" and nothing else.
2. **Repair refusal.** "Never spend more than $100 without calling me," on every job.
3. **Control.** Wants to approve each tenant, each vendor and each rent change.
4. **Short plan.** Plans to sell in 6 months, or wants management only until a sale.
5. **Below-standard property.** Will not fix safety items before you list it.
6. **Fair Housing red flags.** Asks you to avoid families with children, or to steer renters by any protected class.

The last sign is not just a churn risk. HUD's rule at 24 CFR 100.75 covers what anyone says to agents, brokers and employees. It bans a stated preference or limit on renters by race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status or national origin (eCFR). If an owner asks for this, decline the request, and decline the account if they insist.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I write an ideal owner profile?

Write the profile as one page that your whole team and your marketing vendor can use. Fill in this template:

```text
IDEAL OWNER PROFILE — [Company name] — [date]

Who:            Individual owners and small LLCs with 1–10 doors
Where:          [Cities / ZIP codes / max drive time from office]
Property:       [Single-family, condo, 2–4 units; year built; HOA ok?]
Rent range:     $[min] to $[max] per month
Goal:           Long-term hold; wants hands-off income
Repair limit:   Accepts $[amount] approval limit for routine repairs
Fees:           Accepts [fee %] or $[flat] + [leasing fee]
Source:         [Top 3 sources of our best owners today]
Words they use: [Phrases from calls: "tired of tenant calls", "moved out of state"]
Top 3 fears:    [Vacancy, bad tenant, repair cost]
Not a fit:      [Short-term rental, commercial, outside area, refuses repairs]
Red flags:      Lowest-fee-only, no-repair, any Fair Housing request
```

Add one line about value. Goodjuju's pricing page uses a default of $180 per door per month over 36 months, about $6,480 in fees from one door (Goodjuju pricing). Use your own fee. Run it through the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to get your number.

Then write a short "who we are not for" line. Owners respect a clear no more than a vague yes.

## How do I filter bad-fit owners on my website?

Filter bad-fit owners on your website by saying your terms in public before the first call. Every fact you publish saves a call that would end in no.

Website filters that work:

| Filter | Where | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Full fee table | Fee or pricing page | Price-only shoppers self-select out |
| Service area map or list | Service area page | Out-of-area owners stop before calling |
| Minimum standards | Owner FAQ | Owners who refuse repairs see it early |
| "Who we are not for" section | Owner page | Clear no for short-term rentals or commercial |
| 2 lead form questions | Owner form | "How many rentals do you own?" and "Property address" |
| How we screen tenants | Owner FAQ | States your written, equal criteria |

Keep the form short. Two filter questions are enough. Goodjuju's study recommends one more: "How did you first hear about us?", with each source named (Goodjuju, 2026). It tells you which channels bring your ideal owners.

## How do I use the profile in marketing?

Use the profile to pick channels, write copy and judge leads. Three habits:

- **Channel.** Put money where your best owners came from. Check the "first heard" answers each quarter.
- **Copy.** Use the words from your calls. If good owners say "I moved out of state", write a page for out-of-state owners.
- **Scoring.** Score every lead against the profile: 0 to 3 fit points. Call 3-point leads first, the same day.

Review the profile every 6 months. Your best owners change as your company grows. For how owners compare managers today, read [how owners choose a property manager with AI](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai).

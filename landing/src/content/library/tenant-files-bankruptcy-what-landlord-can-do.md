## What is the automatic stay?

The automatic stay is a legal pause on specified actions against the debtor and property. It can affect pending cases, demands and enforcement when a tenant files bankruptcy.

U.S. Courts explains that filing generally stops most collection actions without a separate judicial order. Its Chapter 7 guide includes lawsuits, garnishments and payment demands among the affected actions. Exceptions and limits still matter. [Read the court's Chapter 7 guide](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics).

Treat a bankruptcy notice as a task for immediate review. Do not wait for a paper letter if staff already have credible filing details. Ask for the court, case number, filing date and attorney contact.

Stop account automation that may conflict with the stay. Review collection emails, texts, portal alerts and outside agency instructions. Record which systems you paused and who checked them.

Continue ordinary maintenance communication. Keep that channel separate from debt demands. A repair response should not carry an attached message asking for payment of the old balance.

## Can I still evict the tenant?

You need a case-specific review before starting or continuing eviction. The automatic stay may apply, but federal law has limited exceptions and a process to request relief.

Under 11 USC 362(b)(22) and related provisions, a pre-filing judgment for possession can change the stay analysis. Section 362 also addresses specified endangerment and illegal drug situations. Those provisions have conditions and procedures; they are not permission to bypass review. [Read section 362](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/362).

Send counsel the notice, service proof, complaint and every court order. Identify exactly where the eviction stood at filing. A demand, a filed case and a possession judgment are different events.

Do not instruct enforcement staff based on a general summary of an exception. Ask counsel to confirm which action is allowed and what order or certification supports it.

Keep the state court and bankruptcy files connected in your records. Save the actual relief order when the bankruptcy court grants one. Check its scope and effective terms before restarting any action.

## What happens to unpaid rent?

Separate the account at the filing date and let counsel classify the claim. A single balance can hide different legal treatment.

Create a clean ledger with charge periods, payments, credits and disputed items. Keep security deposit money in its own record. Do not apply it to old rent without advice on the bankruptcy and state rules.

| Account issue | Information counsel needs | Manager's next task |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-filing arrears | Charge dates and credits | Preserve support and pause demands |
| Later rent | Rental periods and receipts | Track separately |
| Security deposit | Account, terms and amount held | Preserve funds pending advice |
| Possession case | Filing dates and orders | Confirm stay status |
| Bankruptcy notices | Docket and service details | Calendar court instructions |

U.S. Courts explains that unsecured claims may require a proof of claim when distributions are available. Follow the actual case notice and current court rules. Do not copy a deadline from an old guide.

A debt balance and a right to possession are separate questions. A change to the rent claim does not by itself tell staff when they can take keys.

## How do Chapter 7 and Chapter 13 differ?

Chapter 7 generally involves liquidation of nonexempt assets, while Chapter 13 uses a court-approved repayment plan for eligible individuals. U.S. Courts explains these distinct structures in its bankruptcy basics guides.

For the landlord, the practical task is to identify the chapter and then read the case instructions. Do not assume that a repayment plan will pay the full rent debt. Do not assume that a Chapter 7 filing ends the tenancy on its own.

Lease treatment also involves 11 USC 365, which governs assumption and rejection of unexpired leases. Bankruptcy-triggered termination clauses face limits under that section. Get counsel to review the lease rather than enforcing a clause merely because the tenant filed. [Read section 365](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/365).

Chapter 13 can also raise issues for someone who shares a consumer debt. U.S. Courts describes a separate co-debtor stay. Refer guarantor and roommate collection questions to counsel before sending demands. [Read the Chapter 13 guide](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics).

## What should the landlord do next?

Use a controlled case file and a named decision owner. Staff should have clear instructions for communication, payments and court dates.

Follow this recommended checklist:

- **Confirm the filing.** Record the court, case number and filing date.
- **Pause affected actions.** Review messages, outside collections and enforcement steps.
- **Preserve the record.** Export the lease, ledger, notices and orders.
- **Split the account.** Distinguish earlier debt from later rent and deposit funds.
- **Seek legal review.** Ask about the stay, exceptions, claims and lease treatment.
- **Follow orders.** Restart actions only within the reviewed authority.
- **Update staff.** Give maintenance and accounting separate, clear instructions.

Keep receiving documents in the same folder so a new order does not get lost in email. Save the date each instruction reached the manager.

If the tenant contacts staff directly, use a calm response that identifies the proper account contact. Avoid promises about debt discharge or possession. Those decisions belong to the legal process.

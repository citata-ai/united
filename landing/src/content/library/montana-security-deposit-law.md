Montana puts its deposit rules in one short chapter: MCA Title 70, chapter 25, "Residential Tenants' Security Deposits". We read each section in the Montana Code Annotated 2025 on the Montana Legislature site on 2026-10-20. The chapter has no cap, but it has two refund clocks and a cleaning notice step that trips up many landlords.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | No cap in the statute | MCA 70-25 (no limit section) |
| Fee "for cleaning and damages" | Presumed to be a deposit | MCA 70-25-101(4) |
| Move-in condition statement | Required, signed by landlord or agent | MCA 70-25-206 |
| Pre-move-out inspection | Within 1 week before the end, if either side asks | MCA 70-25-201(2) |
| Cleaning notice before a cleaning deduction | Written; tenant gets 24 hours | MCA 70-25-201(3) |
| Refund, no deductions | 10 days | MCA 70-25-202(1)(b) |
| Refund with deductions and written list | 30 days | MCA 70-25-202(1)(a) |
| No list sent | Landlord forfeits damage and cleaning deductions | MCA 70-25-203 |
| Wrongful withholding | Amount withheld, attorney fees at court's discretion | MCA 70-25-204 |
| Interest or separate account | Not required by the chapter | MCA 70-25 |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Montana?

Montana sets no maximum security deposit. No section in MCA Title 70, chapter 25 limits the amount, so the lease and the local market decide it.

The chapter does say what counts as a deposit. MCA 70-25-101(4) defines a security deposit as value given to secure rent or to pay for damage and cleaning. Any deposit the lease requires the tenant to keep in place is presumed to be a security deposit. And "a fee or charge for cleaning and damages, no matter how designated, is presumed to be a security deposit."

So a "non-refundable cleaning fee" is a risk in Montana. The statute presumes it is a deposit, and deposit refund rules then apply to it.

Cities cannot add their own deposit rules. MCA 7-1-111(13) denies self-government cities any power to add to or change the deposit law in chapter 25.

## How many days does a Montana landlord have to return a security deposit?

A Montana landlord has 10 days or 30 days, based on whether it deducts anything. MCA 70-25-202 sets both clocks.

- **10 days.** If, after inspection, there is no damage, no cleaning needed and no unpaid rent, and the tenant can show no unpaid utilities, the landlord returns the full deposit within 10 days (70-25-202(1)(b)).
- **30 days.** If the landlord deducts, it sends a written list of rent due and damage and cleaning charges within 30 days after the tenancy ends or after surrender and acceptance, whichever comes first. The refund of the balance goes with the list (70-25-202(1)(a)).

The landlord may pay by electronic transfer, cash, check or other forms, or mail it to the tenant's new address. If the tenant gives no new address, the landlord mails it to the last-known address. Mailing to the last-known address is not a wrongful withholding when the tenant gave no new address, but the landlord stays liable for the money (70-25-202(1)(c)).

One exception: the section does not apply when the landlord ended the lease through an action for possession under 70-24-427 and has a claim pending in court (70-25-202(2)).

### Worked example

Rent is $1,400. The deposit is $1,400. The tenant moves out on June 30 and owes $200 in rent. The tenant also left a broken blind ($60).

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Deposit held | $1,400 |
| Unpaid rent | −$200 |
| Damage: broken blind | −$60 |
| Refund due with written list | $1,140 |
| Deadline | 30 days after the tenancy ends (July 30) |

If the unit were clean, undamaged and paid up, the full $1,400 would be due within 10 days.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Montana?

MCA 70-25-201(1) lists the allowed deductions. A landlord may deduct:

1. Damage caused by the tenant.
2. Unpaid rent, late charges and utilities.
3. Penalties due under lease terms, and other money owed at the time of deduction.
4. Rent owed under 70-24-441(3) after a notice to end the tenancy.
5. Actual cleaning expenses, with a reasonable charge for the landlord's own labor.

MCA 70-25-201(4) bans any deduction for another purpose.

"Damage" excludes normal wear. MCA 70-25-101(2) defines damage as tangible loss, injury or deterioration caused by acts or omissions of the tenant, the tenant's family, licensees or invitees. "Cleaning expenses" are costs not caused by normal wear (70-25-101(1)).

### The cleaning notice step

Cleaning deductions have their own procedure. MCA 70-25-201(3) says:

- The landlord may not charge for routine cyclical maintenance it noted at move-in, unless tenant negligence forced it.
- The landlord must first give written notice. The notice names the cleaning not done and what the tenant must do to restore the move-in condition.
- After delivery of the notice under 70-24-108, the tenant has 24 hours to do the cleaning. Notice by certified mail counts as served 3 days after mailing.
- A tenant who leaves without notice frees the landlord from this step. The landlord may also leave the notice in the unit and tell the tenant by email, phone or text.

## Does a Montana landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. MCA 70-25-202(1)(a) requires a written list of any rent due and any damage and cleaning charges. The list must follow the cleaning steps in 70-25-201 first.

The penalty for skipping the list is severe. Under MCA 70-25-203, a landlord who fails to give the list "shall forfeit all rights to withhold any portion of the security deposit for the damages or cleaning charges."

The move-in statement matters too. MCA 70-25-206 requires a landlord who takes a deposit to give a separate written statement of the unit's condition when the lease starts. It must be signed by the landlord or agent. Without it, the landlord cannot recover for damage or cleaning unless it proves, by clear and convincing evidence, that the tenant caused the damage during this tenancy.

Checklist for a Montana move-out:

- [ ] Signed move-in condition statement on file (70-25-206)
- [ ] Inspection offered within 1 week before move-out (70-25-201(2))
- [ ] Written cleaning notice sent, 24 hours given (70-25-201(3))
- [ ] Refund in 10 days if nothing is owed
- [ ] Written list plus balance within 30 days if you deduct
- [ ] Proof of delivery kept (70-24-108)

## Must a Montana landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

No. MCA Title 70, chapter 25 has six sections in part 2, and none requires interest or a separate account. The chapter covers deductions, the list, forfeiture, lawsuits, the tenant's address and the move-in statement.

Other rules can still apply. A lease, a subsidy contract or a manager's license rules may set how deposit money is held. We did not review those rules for this page, so check them for your own setup.

## What is the penalty for a Montana landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

A landlord who wrongfully withholds a deposit owes the tenant the amount wrongfully withheld (MCA 70-25-204(1)). The court may award attorney fees to the winning side. The landlord carries the burden of proof for damage the tenant caused.

Montana has no double or triple damages rule in this chapter. The cost risk comes from attorney fees and from forfeiture under 70-25-203.

The tenant must wait before suing. MCA 70-25-204(2) bars a suit until one of these happens: the landlord sends a written denial, 30 days pass after the tenancy ends, 30 days pass after surrender and acceptance, or 10 days pass after the landlord says nothing is owed.

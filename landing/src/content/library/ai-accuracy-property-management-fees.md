## Which fee numbers does AI give?

AI engines give a monthly fee of 8% to 12% of rent and a leasing fee of 50% to 100% of one month's rent. We tested this on 2026-10-19.

The test: 1 prompt, 3 cities, 3 engines, 1 run each. The prompt was "How much does a property management company charge in [city] for a single-family rental? Give the monthly management fee and the leasing fee." Each engine had live web search on. The models were gpt-6-luna (ChatGPT), gemini-3-flash (Gemini) and claude-sonnet-5-5 (Claude).

| City | Engine | Monthly fee | Leasing fee | Sources cited |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicago | ChatGPT | 8–10% (7–12% published) | 50–100% | 1 |
| Chicago | Gemini | 8–12%, or $100–$150 flat | 50–100% | 6 |
| Chicago | Claude | 8–10% (7–12% published) | 50–100% | 6 |
| Houston | ChatGPT | 8–12% | 50–100% | 1 |
| Houston | Gemini | 8–12%, or $70–$200+ flat | 50–100% | 7 |
| Houston | Claude | 8–12% | 50–100% | 8 |
| Phoenix | ChatGPT | 8–10% (8.7% average) | 50% typical | 1 |
| Phoenix | Gemini | 8–11% (6–12% wider) | 50–100% | 4 |
| Phoenix | Claude | 8–10% (8.7% average) | 50–100% | 8 |

All 9 answers agree on the core range. This is a small sample: 9 answers on 1 day. AI answers change from run to run.

## Where do the AI fee numbers come from?

The AI fee numbers come from fee guides on manager websites and from a few research sites. We counted the cited domains in all 9 answers.

| Source domain | Answers that cited it | Type |
|---|---|---|
| home365.co | 5 | Manager with fee guides by city |
| ipropertymanagement.com | 4 | Research and directory site |
| turbotenant.com | 2 | Landlord software |
| flatfeelandlord.com | 2 | Manager |
| 21 other domains | 1 or 2 each | Mostly local managers |

Two patterns stand out:

- **ChatGPT used 1 source per answer.** In Chicago it cited only home365.co. In Houston it cited only one local manager's pricing page. One page set the whole answer.
- **Local managers with a fee page got cited.** Sites like keyrenterchicagometro.com and atlasmanagementtx.com appeared because they publish fees as text.

For a property manager, that second point is the useful one. A fee page with real numbers is a page AI can quote.

## How far off are the AI fee numbers?

The AI fee ranges are close to the published averages, but they are too wide to plan with, and some come from old pages. We checked each answer against the iPropertyManagement fee study (722 branches, updated 2026-09-23) and the All Property Management guide.

| Fee | AI answers | Checked source | Match? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly fee, US | 8–12% | 8.49% average (iPropertyManagement); 8–12% typical (All Property Management) | Yes |
| Monthly fee, Texas | 8–12% | 8.87% average (iPropertyManagement) | Yes, wide |
| Monthly fee, Arizona | 8–10% or 8–11% | 8.92% average (iPropertyManagement) | Yes |
| Leasing fee with management, US | 50–100% | 70.6% average (iPropertyManagement) | Yes, wide |
| Leasing fee with management, Arizona | 50% typical (ChatGPT) | 53.9% average (iPropertyManagement) | Yes |
| Flat renewal fee | $150–$400 | $231.77 average (iPropertyManagement) | Yes, wide |

Three problems showed up:

1. **Old data.** ChatGPT and Claude both quoted an 8.7% Phoenix average. That number comes from an iPropertyManagement Phoenix page last updated on 2024-01-04, based on 18 companies. Claude said the study was older. ChatGPT did not.
2. **Wide ranges.** A range of 8% to 12% sounds exact, but it is not.
3. **Source conflicts.** Even the main source is not fully consistent. The iPropertyManagement page gives a $211.92 average renewal fee in its summary and $231.77 in its body.

Worked example, on a $2,000 monthly rent:

| Item | Low end | High end | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly fee, 8% vs 12% | $160 | $240 | $80 a month |
| Monthly fee over 12 months | $1,920 | $2,880 | $960 a year |
| Leasing fee, 50% vs 100% | $1,000 | $2,000 | $1,000 per new tenant |

An owner who reads "8% to 12%" can be off by $960 a year in either direction. You can run your own numbers in our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## What do AI fee answers leave out?

AI fee answers leave out the extra fees that change the real cost. Gemini and Claude listed renewal fees, setup fees and maintenance markups in most answers. ChatGPT named them in 2 of 3 answers, only as items to check.

What AI rarely says:

- **Collected or scheduled rent.** Only 3 of 9 answers (Gemini in Houston and Phoenix, Claude in Phoenix) said the fee should apply to rent collected, not rent due. That one term changes what you pay in a vacant month.
- **Minimum fees.** Two Chicago answers named a $125 monthly minimum as an example. Most answers did not mention minimums.
- **Your fee.** No answer named a fee for a specific company unless that company published it as text.

## How do you correct AI fee answers about your company?

You correct AI fee answers by publishing your own fees in a form AI can read and quote. You cannot edit the answer. You can change the pages it reads.

Checklist:

1. **Publish a fee table as HTML text.** Not a PDF, not an image. List the monthly fee, leasing fee, renewal fee, setup fee, minimum fee and maintenance markup.
2. **Date the table.** Write "Fees as of 2026-10-01". AI quoted a 2024 page because nothing newer answered the question as well.
3. **Say collected or scheduled rent.** Owners ask about it, and few pages say it.
4. **Match your listings.** Use the same fee numbers on your Google Business Profile description and your directory profiles. ClearLead Digital says AI tools report an 8% to 12% fee from pages like its own fee guide.
5. **Ask the question each month.** Run "How much does a property manager charge in [your city]?" in ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude. Log the numbers and the sources.

Watch for: a vendor who says it can make ChatGPT quote your fee. No one controls the answer. You control your pages and listings.

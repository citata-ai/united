Your best owner list is already public. Every county keeps a record of each parcel, who owns it and where the tax bill goes. When the tax bill goes to a different address than the house, the house is often a rental.

The market is large. In the 2021 Rental Housing Finance Survey, individual investors owned 70% of rental properties with 1 to 4 units, or 15.9 million properties. Only about 22% of those small properties had professional management (HUD and Census, 2022). Most of your future clients are on a county list today.

## How do I use county property records?

Use county property records by downloading the parcel file from your assessor or recorder and filtering it for likely rentals. Many large counties publish this data as open data. Others sell it or show it one parcel at a time.

Cook County, Illinois is a good example. Its assessor publishes a "Parcel Addresses" dataset with the property (situs) address and the mailing address of each parcel. It is used to mail assessment notices. A March 2026 update added owner and taxpayer names. The data is updated semi-monthly (Data.gov, Cook County Assessor).

Steps for any county:

1. **Find the data.** Search "[county] assessor parcel data download" or "[county] open data property".
2. **Download the fields you need.** Parcel ID, property address, owner name, mailing address, property class, last sale date.
3. **Keep residential classes only.** Single-family, condo, 2–4 units. Drop commercial, vacant land and exempt parcels.
4. **Keep your service area.** Filter by city, township or ZIP code.
5. **Save the source and the date.** You will need both when you clean the list later.

Watch for one limit. Cook County's assessor says owner names and mailing addresses are only intermittently updated in its source data. It points users to its Parcel Sales dataset for owner and seller names (Data.gov, Cook County Assessor). Check recent sales before you mail.

## What is an absentee owner list?

An absentee owner list is a list of parcels where the owner's mailing address is not the property address. The owner lives somewhere else, so the home is likely a rental, a second home, a vacant home or an inherited home.

A worked filter on a sample county file:

| Step | Filter | Parcels left (example) |
|---|---|---|
| Start | All parcels in 3 ZIP codes | 30,000 |
| 1 | Residential classes only | 22,000 |
| 2 | Mailing address ≠ property address | 5,500 |
| 3 | Remove owners with a business mailing address you already manage | 5,300 |
| 4 | Remove parcels sold in the last 90 days (check with sales data) | 5,100 |
| 5 | Split: in-state vs out-of-state mailing address | 4,000 / 1,100 |

The numbers are an example of the math, not real county data. Your own file gives your real counts.

Split the list by owner type before you write the message:

- **Out-of-state owners.** They need remote proof: reports, a portal, a local team.
- **Owners with 2 or more parcels.** Group by owner name and mailing address. These owners are worth a letter, not a postcard.
- **LLC owners.** Often investors with a business plan. Lead with numbers.
- **Recent inheritance or a recent move.** Often accidental landlords who need simple help.

## Can I find landlords from rental listings?

Yes. Owners who list a home for rent themselves are self-managing right now. You see them on rental sites, local classifieds and yard signs with a personal phone number.

Rules for this source:

1. **Read each site's terms.** Many listing sites limit copying or bulk use of their data.
2. **Contact by the method the listing offers.** A reply to the listing is a direct, personal contact.
3. **Do not pose as a renter.** Say who you are in the first sentence.
4. **Offer help, not pressure.** "If the place sits empty longer than you want, I can share what similar homes rent for."

These owners are warm. They feel the cost of a vacancy this month.

## What data rules apply?

Data rules depend on how you contact the owner and where the data came from. The main federal rules:

| Rule | What it covers | Key duty |
|---|---|---|
| Telemarketing Sales Rule, Do Not Call (FTC) | Sales calls to consumers | Check the National Do Not Call Registry at least every 31 days |
| Established business relationship (FTC) | Past clients and inquiries | You may call for 18 months after a purchase, or 3 months after an inquiry, unless told to stop |
| CAN-SPAM Act (FTC) | Commercial email | Postal address in each email, clear opt-out, honor opt-outs within 10 business days |
| Driver's Privacy Protection Act (18 U.S.C. 2721) | State motor vehicle records | Bulk marketing use only with the person's express consent |

The FTC says a company that calls again after a stop request may face a fine of up to $53,088 (FTC, Do Not Call Q&A). Skip tracing (buying phone numbers and emails for a name and address) does not remove any of these duties. Many states add their own telemarketing and privacy rules.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I keep the list clean?

Keep the list clean with a quarterly update and a stop list that never expires. A stale list wastes postage and annoys owners who already sold.

Quarterly steps:

- Download the new parcel and sales files.
- Remove parcels sold since the last pull.
- Merge owners with the same name and mailing address.
- Remove every owner who asked you to stop, by name and address.
- Tag owners who already became leads or clients.

Then send. A list without a clear offer does nothing.

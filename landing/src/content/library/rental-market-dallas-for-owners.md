## How much did HUD's fair market rent change in Dallas for FY2027?

HUD's fair market rent for a Dallas-area 2-bedroom fell 6.4% for FY2027, from $1,931 to $1,807. That is the biggest 2-bedroom drop among the 50 largest HUD metro areas, ahead of San Jose (-6.1%) and San Diego (-5.9%) (our ranking of HUD's FY2027 data). The new FMRs took effect on 2026-10-01 (Federal Register, 91 FR 56156).

| Unit size | FY2026 FMR | FY2027 FMR | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Studio | $1,582 | $1,491 | -5.8% |
| 1 bedroom | $1,648 | $1,536 | -6.8% |
| 2 bedrooms | $1,931 | $1,807 | -6.4% |
| 3 bedrooms | $2,431 | $2,275 | -6.4% |
| 4 bedrooms | $3,091 | $2,892 | -6.4% |

Source: HUD FY2027 and revised FY2026 FMR files for the Dallas, TX HUD Metro FMR Area (Dallas, Collin, Denton, Ellis, Kaufman, Rockwall and other counties).

What it means for a voucher owner: the payment standard range (90% to 110% of FMR) for a 2-bedroom moved from $1,738–$2,124 to $1,626–$1,988. But a housing authority cannot cut a current family's payment standard sooner than 2 years after the decrease takes effect, and the family must get 12 months' notice (24 CFR 982.505(c)(3)). The full table is on our [Dallas fair market rent 2027](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/dallas-fair-market-rent-2027) page.

Dallas rents are falling slowly over the year. Apartment List's October 2026 report for the City of Dallas shows:

| Median rent, all sizes | $1,324 | $1,415 | — | $1,388 |
| 1-bedroom median | $1,209 | — | — | $1,220 |
| 2-bedroom median | $1,432 | — | — | $1,374 |
| Change, past 12 months | -1.5% | -2.0% | -2.1% | -0.4% |
| Change, September 2026 | -0.3% | — | — | -0.1% |

Source: Apartment List, Dallas rent report and national rent report, October 2026.

Three points stand out:

- **The city is below the metro.** The Dallas city median is 6.4% lower than the metro median.
- **The year is flat, not falling fast.** Dallas rents rose 1.4% from January to September 2026, close to the 1.6% rise in the same months of 2025.
- **The Sun Belt is turning.** Apartment List says rent declines in the Sun Belt markets of the recent building boom are now smaller than at the start of the year.

What to do: price a vacant unit at the current median for its size, not last year's rent. A Dallas 2-bedroom priced at $1,500 sits 4.7% above the city median of $1,432. Expect a longer lease-up at that price.

## Which local rules affect Dallas rental returns?

Texas and Dallas rules affect returns through registration, deposits and late fees. Here are the main ones, with the source for each:

| Rule | What it says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rent control | Only in a disaster, with the governor's approval | Tex. Local Gov't Code 214.902 |
| Single-family registration | Yearly online registration for homes, duplexes and condos: $74 fee from 2025-10-01, plus an owner self-inspection checklist | City of Dallas Code Compliance |
| City inspection | At least once every 5 years, no more than once a year (complaints aside) | Dallas rental registration FAQ |
| Deposit refund | Within 30 days after the tenant surrenders the home | Tex. Prop. Code 92.103 |
| Deposit penalty | Bad faith: $100 + 3 times the amount wrongly kept + attorney's fees | Tex. Prop. Code 92.109 |
| Late fee | Only after rent is 2 full days late; "reasonable" is up to 12% of rent in a building of 4 units or fewer, 10% in larger buildings | Tex. Prop. Code 92.019 |
| Flood notice | Written floodplain and flood-history notice in or with the lease | Tex. Prop. Code 92.0135 |

Worked example, late fee: on a $1,432 rent in a single-family home, a late fee of up to 12%, or $171.84, counts as reasonable under 92.019. Charge more than the law allows and you owe $100 plus 3 times the fee, plus attorney's fees.

Worked example, deposit: you keep $500 of a deposit without a written itemized list by day 30. The law presumes bad faith (92.109(d)). The tenant can claim $100 + 3 × $500 = $1,600, plus attorney's fees.

The Dallas FAQ also notes an exemption: a home with a homestead exemption in the last 2 years does not need to register. Call 214-671-RENT for details.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What vacancy rate does Dallas have?

We could not verify a Dallas metro vacancy rate. The Census Bureau publishes metro rental vacancy rates in its Housing Vacancy Survey, but census.gov refused our requests on 2026-10-19. We do not quote a number we did not read.

What we can say from Apartment List's national report (2026-09-29): the national multifamily vacancy rate fell to 7% in September 2026, and units took 34 days on average to lease. In Dallas, with rents down 1.5% in a year, plan for a lease-up at least that long.

Track your own number. For each turnover, log the list date, the price, every price cut and the lease date. After 2 or 3 turnovers you have a better guide than any metro average.

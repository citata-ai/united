A manager in Los Angeles wants to raise a $2,500 rent to $2,800 next month. That is a 12% increase. Under California law, the notice alone must be 90 days, and the increase may be above the statewide cap. This page sets out the rules from the current statute text on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, read on 2026-10-19.

## Does California Have a Cap on Rent Increases or Rent Control?

Yes. California has a statewide cap in Civil Code 1947.12. Over any 12-month period, an owner may not raise the gross rent more than 5% plus the percentage change in the cost of living, or 10%, whichever is lower. The base is the lowest gross rent charged at any time in the 12 months before the increase. The cost of living is the regional CPI-U from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, with an April-to-April change.

**Worked example.** The lowest rent in the past 12 months was $2,500. Assume the regional CPI change is 3.0% (an example, not a published figure).

| Step | Math | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 5% + CPI | 5% + 3.0% | 8.0% |
| Compare to 10% | 8.0% is lower | 8.0% applies |
| Maximum new rent | $2,500 × 1.08 | $2,700 |

If CPI were 6.0%, 5% + 6.0% = 11%, so the 10% ceiling applies: $2,750.

The cap does not apply to every unit. Exemptions in 1947.12(d) include:

- [ ] Housing with a certificate of occupancy issued within the previous 15 years (not mobilehomes).
- [ ] A single-family home or condo that can be sold apart from other units, if the owner is not a REIT, a corporation or an LLC with a corporate member, and the tenant got the exact written exemption notice in the statute.
- [ ] A duplex where the owner lived in one unit at the start of the tenancy and still lives there.
- [ ] Deed-restricted affordable housing and school dormitories.
- [ ] Units under a local rent control law with a lower cap.

An owner who collects rent above the cap owes the excess. If the owner acted willfully, a court may award up to 3 times the excess (1947.12(k)). A tenant cannot waive these rights (1947.12(l)).

## How Many Days' Notice Does a California Landlord Need to Raise the Rent?

A California landlord needs 30 or 90 days' written notice for a periodic tenancy. Civil Code 827(b) sets the rule by the size of the increase, counting all increases in the prior 12 months:

- **10% or less:** at least 30 days before the increase takes effect.
- **More than 10%:** at least 90 days before the increase takes effect.

The landlord delivers the notice in person or by mail under Code of Civil Procedure 1013. Mail adds time to the notice period. If a recertification of tenant income or family size causes the increase, 30 days applies. If another law or a regulatory agreement requires a longer notice, the longer period applies (827(c)). The rent cap section also points to Section 827 for notice (1947.12(e)).

## Can a California Landlord Raise the Rent During a Fixed-Term Lease?

Civil Code 827 covers week-to-week, month-to-month and other periodic tenancies. It does not give a landlord a right to change the rent in the middle of a fixed-term lease. In a fixed-term lease, the lease terms control the rent until the term ends. When the lease ends or renews, the 1947.12 cap applies to the new rent for the same tenant.

The cap has one more lease rule. Rent discounts and concessions must appear as separate items in the lease. When the owner computes the lowest rent in the past 12 months, accepted discounts do not count (1947.12(a)(1)).

## How Much Notice Is Needed to End a Month-to-Month Tenancy in California?

The notice depends on how long the tenant has lived in the unit. Under Civil Code 1946.1:

| Tenant has lived in the unit | Owner's notice |
|---|---|
| Less than 1 year | At least 30 days |
| 1 year or more | At least 60 days |
| Owner sold a separate unit to a person who will live there (all conditions in 1946.1(d)) | At least 30 days |

The tenant's own notice equals the rental period, for example 30 days for a monthly tenancy. The owner may not charge a fee to serve the notice (1946.1(i)).

Notice is not the only test. After a tenant has lived in a covered unit for 12 months, Civil Code 1946.2 requires just cause, stated in the notice. A no-fault reason, such as owner move-in, also needs relocation help equal to 1 month's rent, or a waiver of the last month's rent.

## Do Cities in California Add Their Own Rent or Notice Rules?

Yes. Local rent control and just-cause laws can be stricter than state law. The statewide cap does not apply to a unit under a valid local ordinance that limits increases to less than the state cap (1947.12(d)(3)). For just cause, 1946.2(i) lets a stricter local ordinance replace the state rule.

State law also limits local rent control. Civil Code 1954.52 lets an owner set the rent for units with a certificate of occupancy issued after February 1, 1995, and for most units that can be sold apart from others, such as single-family homes and condos.

## Does my mortgage allow short-term rentals?

Your signed loan terms and actual use determine the mortgage questions you must resolve before hosting.

Find the note, mortgage or deed of trust, occupancy documents, and any riders. Read restrictions on occupancy, rental use, and transfers. Ask the servicer which provision applies to your proposed use. Keep the written answer with your loan file.

Fannie Mae's Selling Guide separates principal residences, second homes, and investment properties. That guide helps explain lender categories. It does not replace your signed documents or amend an existing loan. A general website answer cannot grant permission for a specific house.

Describe the proposed use accurately. Explain whether you stay in the home, rent a room, or offer the whole home. State whether hosting is occasional or part of a broader change in occupancy. Do not ask only whether Airbnb is allowed without explaining the plan.

| Check | Document or contact | Evidence to keep |
|---|---|---|
| Loan use | Signed terms and servicer | Written clarification |
| Local hosting | City or county office | Current rule and permit record |
| Association | Recorded terms and association | Applicable rental provisions |
| Insurance | Agent and policy | Confirmed rental coverage |
| Tax | Tax professional | Booking and personal-use records |

Resolve each area separately. A city permit does not change your lender's contract. A lender answer does not settle a condominium restriction.

## What do lenders count as rental use?

Lender categories depend on occupancy and underwriting rules, rather than the booking platform's name.

Fannie Mae describes a principal residence as the property the borrower occupies as a primary residence. Its second-home requirements include owner occupancy for part of the year and exclusive owner control. The guide also addresses rental income within those requirements.

The second-home section says identified rental income can be compatible with delivery as a second home under stated conditions. The income cannot serve as qualifying income, and all other second-home requirements must remain satisfied. That is a qualified underwriting rule, not blanket hosting approval.

The guide also limits agreements that give a management firm control over second-home occupancy. Send a proposed management contract to the lender if that condition may matter. Ask for an answer tied to the exact contract terms.

Avoid changing the facts to fit the loan category you prefer. If the home becomes a full-time rental, explain that change. Ask what action the current documents require and what options exist. Do not assume a later refinance will solve an earlier breach.

Keep your hosting plan and occupancy evidence consistent across the lender, insurer, and local registration. Conflicting descriptions create avoidable disputes. Review the file again when the pattern of use changes.

## What if I only rent a room?

Room rental still needs a review of your loan, insurance, local rules, and household arrangements.

A room within your residence differs from handing over the entire house. The difference can matter, but it does not create automatic permission. Tell each relevant party that you remain on site and describe shared areas.

Check access, locks, parking, waste, and emergency instructions. Decide which spaces guests may use. Remove personal records and protect access to mail and financial documents. Set a contact process for problems without relying on informal expectations.

Ask your insurer about paying guests in an owner-occupied home. Travelers' landlord page describes rental-property protection, but the right policy for room hosting needs an individual review. Give the agent the actual booking pattern and services. Ask how guest injury, property damage, and lost income would work.

IRS Publication 527 discusses renting part of a property and dividing relevant expenses. Keep a record of the areas rented and the periods of use. Personal costs do not become rental costs just because a guest stays in a room.

Before setting prices, write a budget for extra cleaning, utilities, supplies, insurance, and wear. Include your own time. A gross booking total does not show what remains for the mortgage after those costs.

## What are the main risks of hosting with a mortgage?

The main risk is a mismatch between your actual rental use and the terms governing the home.

A mismatch can involve the loan, local permission, association terms, or insurance. The available remedy depends on the document and jurisdiction. This guide does not claim that every host faces the same penalty. Obtain advice on the exact terms before you accept bookings.

Cash flow is another risk. Your debt payment remains due when guest revenue falls. Model an empty period, a refund, and an urgent repair. Use assumptions you can fund without new bookings. Separate mortgage affordability from optimistic occupancy forecasts.

Plan for a hosting pause. Ask who handles guests if the home needs repairs or permission remains unresolved. Read cancellation and management terms before signing. Keep a cash reserve for obligations that survive a paused listing.

The [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) can help test one part of the budget. Add platform charges, utilities, furnishing costs, and guest service work separately. Compare that result with a lawful long-term lease if hosting proves impractical.

Before launch, assemble a short permission file. Include lender clarification, current local instructions, association terms, insurance confirmation, and a tax record plan. Start only after the unresolved items have clear answers from the right source.

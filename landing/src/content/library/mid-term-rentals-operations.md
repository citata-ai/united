## What lease works for a mid-term stay?

Use a lease built for the actual length and lawful use of the stay. Do not replace a residential lease with a booking receipt merely because the home has furniture. Confirm which tenancy, lodging, licensing and local use rules apply.

New York City's host guidance defines short-term rentals as stays shorter than 30 days. Its restrictions on whole-home visitor rentals apply to that category in permanent residential buildings. That threshold does not establish every rule for a longer stay or another city.

Florida Statute 83.42 excludes specified transient occupancy from its residential tenancy part. The exclusion depends on the statutory category and facts. A label on an advertisement does not settle the classification. Have local counsel review the proposed arrangement before you offer it.

Set the start date, end date and any lawful extension method in writing. State the notice process, payment schedule and move-out duties. Explain what happens when the renter requests more time. Do not accept an extension casually if it conflicts with the next signed lease.

Keep required disclosures in the signing process. The EPA describes federal lead disclosure duties for most pre-1978 housing and stated exceptions. Check those terms for the exact lease; do not assume every furnished stay is exempt.

## Who are mid-term renters?

Plan the service around practical needs rather than a preferred personal profile. Possible uses include a temporary work assignment, relocation or a gap between homes. These are examples for planning, not a measured demand forecast or a basis to exclude other qualified renters.

Ask about occupancy dates, furnishings, parking, internet and billing needs. Use the same lawful screening criteria across applicants. Avoid turning a target use into a protected-class preference in advertisements. Describe the home and the service plainly.

Verify the actual leaseholder and payer. A company may reimburse an employee without becoming a party to the lease. An employer letter may confirm an assignment without guaranteeing rent. Get any guarantee reviewed and signed rather than assuming a business name provides payment security.

The FTC says consumer-report use can trigger FCRA duties, including adverse action notice requirements. Keep that process in place for shorter leases too. A furnished home does not make a consumer report exempt from the ordinary rules.

Give the renter a clear contact for repairs and questions. Someone arriving from another city may need more instructions about building access and services. Provide those instructions before arrival without collecting unrelated personal details.

## How often do I turn the unit?

Turn the unit after each tenancy, with the scope based on actual condition. Plan from signed dates and the work needed. Do not assume a furnished home can always accept another renter immediately.

Use a turnover board that shows inspection, cleaning, repairs and inventory checks. Assign an owner to every unfinished item. Confirm completion before releasing access. A clean kitchen does not mean furniture, linen and internet are ready.

| Turnover area | What to check | Record to keep |
|---|---|---|
| Condition | Floors, walls and fixtures | Dated inspection and photos |
| Furniture | Missing items, damage and stability | Updated inventory |
| Housewares | Working items and complete sets | Restock list |
| Utilities | Active service and account status | Provider confirmation |
| Access | Keys, codes and building instructions | Access change record |
| Cleaning | Agreed standard throughout the home | Cleaner completion note |

Keep a repair buffer based on your own operating record. Track why a reset took longer than planned. Separate waiting for parts from active labor. Use those records to set future availability, rather than claiming an industry-standard turn time without evidence.

Confirm lawful possession before work begins. If the renter remains beyond the planned departure, use the correct local process. Do not treat the booking calendar as permission to remove belongings.

## How do utilities and furniture work?

Utilities and furniture need separate lease terms and separate cost records. List exactly what the rent includes. Explain internet, water, power, gas and any other service that matters for the property.

If you propose a utility allowance or excess-use charge, get local advice on billing rules. State the method before signing. Do not create a surprise charge because a bill was higher than expected. Keep records that support any lawful amount you pass through.

Use a furniture inventory with condition notes. Identify larger items and list the housewares provided. Avoid a vague promise that the kitchen is fully stocked. Give the renter a way to report missing or damaged items soon after arrival.

Plan repair and replacement responsibility. An owner-supplied item can fail through age or a defect. Do not presume every broken item proves tenant misuse. Compare the condition record, service history and facts before proposing a charge.

Keep spare essentials where staff can reach them. Track replacements in the property account so the rent decision reflects actual costs. Do not rely on the purchase price of furniture alone when evaluating the service.

## How should I price and manage the stay?

Price from the owner's full cost and the actual available period. Separate base housing costs, service costs and vacancy risk. A higher advertised monthly rent does not prove a higher return.

Build a property worksheet using your own estimates and quotes. Include utilities, internet, furniture replacement, cleaning, repairs, platform charges and management. Label assumptions clearly. Update them with actual records after each completed tenancy.

Compare calendar rent with collected rent. A gap between leases reduces collected income even if each signed lease has a high rate. Record cancellations and extension requests separately. Review whether your minimum stay and scheduling policy leave avoidable empty periods.

Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to assess management costs as part of that worksheet. Do not use it as a forecast of local furnished-rental demand.

Before advertising, complete a sample arrival and departure review. Check that the lease, inventory, utility terms and access instructions agree. Ask a staff member unfamiliar with the property to follow the instructions. Fix unclear steps before the first renter arrives.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

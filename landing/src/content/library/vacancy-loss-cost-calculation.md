## How do I calculate vacancy loss?

Vacancy loss is the rent you did not collect because the home was empty. The formula is simple.

Vacancy loss = monthly rent x months vacant

Vacancy rate = vacancy loss / potential gross rent for the year

Worked example for a $2,000 house:

| Months vacant | Vacancy loss | Vacancy rate |
|---------------|--------------|--------------|
| 0.5 (2 weeks) | $1,000 | 4.2% |
| 1 | $2,000 | 8.3% |
| 1.5 | $3,000 | 12.5% |
| 2 | $4,000 | 16.7% |

Potential gross rent here is $24,000 ($2,000 x 12). Each month empty takes 8.3 points off the year.

Daily cost is useful during a vacancy: $2,000 x 12 / 365 = about $66 per day.

## What carrying costs continue in a vacancy?

Carrying costs are the bills you pay whether a tenant is there or not. They make the real cost of a vacancy larger than the lost rent.

Worked example, with made-up costs for the same house:

| Carrying cost | Per month | Paid while vacant? |
|---------------|-----------|--------------------|
| Mortgage (principal and interest) | $1,150 | Yes |
| Property tax | $300 | Yes |
| Insurance | $120 | Yes |
| HOA | $0 | Yes, if any |
| Utilities (now on the owner) | $150 | Yes |
| Lawn or snow care | $60 | Yes |
| Total carrying costs | $1,780 | |

A month empty: you lose $2,000 of rent and still pay $1,780. Cash out of your pocket that month is $1,780, compared with a month of $220 positive cash flow before reserves when rented.

Turnover adds one-time costs on top:

- **Cleaning and paint.**
- **Small repairs** found at move-out.
- **Lock change.**
- **Leasing fee** if you use a manager. iPropertyManagement's 2026 average is 70.6% of one month's rent with management.
- **Listing and photo costs** if you self-manage.

Watch for: insurance terms in a long vacancy. The North Carolina Department of Insurance notes that a DP-2 dwelling policy covers some water damage only if the building was not vacant for 60 days before the loss. Ask your insurer how your policy treats a vacant home.

Tax note: IRS Publication 527 says you may deduct ordinary and necessary expenses, including depreciation, while a rental held for rent is vacant. You cannot deduct the rent you did not collect.

## What vacancy rate should I plan for?

Plan with your local vacancy rate if you can find it. As a national reference, the Census Bureau reported a US rental vacancy rate of 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026.

The Census release is dated July 28, 2026. It says the rate was not statistically different from the second quarter of 2025 (7.0%). It was about the same as the first quarter of 2026 (7.3%).

How to get a local number:

1. **Ask 2 local managers** how many days their homes like yours sat empty last year.
2. **Watch listings** for homes like yours. Note how long each stays on the market.
3. **Check your own history** if you have rented before.

How to use the rate in a budget:

| Vacancy rate | Months empty per year | Monthly budget line on $2,000 rent |
|--------------|-----------------------|------------------------------------|
| 4% | About 0.5 | $80 |
| 7.3% (US, Q2 2026) | About 0.9 | $146 |
| 10% | 1.2 | $200 |

Put the budget line in your cash flow every month, even in months the home is rented.

## Is a lower rent with a faster lease better?

Often, yes. A small rent cut costs a little each month. A week of vacancy costs a lot at once.

Compare the two for the first year of the lease:

| Option | Rent | Days vacant | Rent collected in 12 months from today |
|--------|------|-------------|----------------------------------------|
| A: hold the price | $2,000 | 30 | $22,000 (11 months) |
| B: cut $50 | $1,950 | 14 | $22,490 (about 11.5 months) |
| C: cut $100 | $1,900 | 7 | $22,360 (about 11.8 months) |

In this made-up example, option B wins. It fills 16 days sooner and collects $490 more in year one. Option C fills fastest but the larger cut gives back most of the gain.

Break-even test:

Days saved needed = (rent cut x 12) / (rent / 30)

For a $50 cut on $2,000 rent: ($50 x 12) / ($2,000 / 30) = $600 / $66.67 = 9 days.

If a $50 cut fills the home more than 9 days sooner, it pays for itself in the first year. The cut also lowers your base for future rent increases, so weigh that too.

## How to cut vacancy before it starts

The cheapest vacancy is the one that never starts. Most of the work happens in the last 90 days of a lease, while rent still comes in. A short checklist keeps the gap between tenants to days, not weeks.

- **Start the renewal talk 60 to 90 days before the lease ends.**
- **Price from current comps,** not last year's rent.
- **List before move-out,** if the lease and your state's rules allow showings.
- **Line up cleaning and paint** for the day after move-out.
- **Fix small items during the tenancy,** so turnover is short.

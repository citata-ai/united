## What are the most common rental repair requests?

The most common rental repair requests involve water, heat, appliances, electrical and the roof. These are also the systems the law says a landlord must keep working. A short list of repair types drives most of the calls a landlord gets.

We did not find a national public count of rental repair tickets by type. So this page groups repairs by the systems that state habitability laws name. Two examples:

- **California:** Civil Code 1941.1 says a unit is untenantable if it lacks waterproofing, working plumbing and gas, hot and cold water, heat, safe electrical, and floors and stairs in good repair. Since January 1, 2026, the list also includes a working stove for leases signed, amended or extended on or after that date.
- **Florida:** Florida Statutes 83.51 says the landlord must follow housing codes. Where no code applies, the landlord must keep the roof, windows, doors, floors and structure in good repair and the plumbing in working order.

So the repair calls you should expect most are these:

1. **Leaks and clogs:** faucets, toilets, drains, garbage disposals.
2. **Heat and cooling faults:** no heat, no cooling, thermostat or filter issues.
3. **Appliance failures:** refrigerator, stove, dishwasher, washer and dryer.
4. **Water heater problems:** no hot water, leaks, pilot or element failures.
5. **Electrical issues:** dead outlets, tripped breakers, fixtures.
6. **Roof and window leaks:** often after a storm.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How much do common rental repairs cost?

Most common rental repairs cost a few hundred dollars. HVAC and roof work can cost thousands. The table uses HomeAdvisor's cost pages, read on 2026-10-19. HomeAdvisor shows some ranges by the reader's location, so your local numbers may differ.

| Repair | Typical range | Average | Notes from the source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plumber visit | $182–$501 | $341 | Trip fees $50–$300 |
| Appliance repair | $108–$252 | $180 | Labor $50–$150/hour plus parts; gas about 20% higher |
| Garbage disposal repair | $140–$310 | not used | Plumber labor $50–$120/hour |
| HVAC repair | $130–$2,000 | $350 | Low end $100, high end $3,000 |
| Water heater repair | $228–$1,017 | not used | Tank flush about $200 |
| Roof repair | $395–$1,968 | not used | Full range $150–$8,000 |

Sources: HomeAdvisor cost pages for each repair (see sources below).

Two lessons stand out. First, the trip fee is a large share of a small job. A $50 to $300 trip fee on a $182 plumber visit changes the math. Second, HVAC and roof repairs have very wide ranges. One bad month can cost more than a year of small fixes.

## How much does an appliance repair cost?

An appliance repair costs $108 to $252, with an average of $180, per HomeAdvisor's 2026 page. Labor runs $50 to $150 an hour plus parts. HomeAdvisor says gas models cost about 20% more to repair than electric ones.

Before you approve an appliance repair, ask three questions:

- **How old is the unit?** An old appliance with a costly repair may be worth replacing.
- **Is it under warranty?** Check the receipt or the model number with the maker.
- **Is the part in stock?** A two-week wait for a part means two weeks without a fridge for your tenant.

## Which rental repairs can I prevent?

You can prevent many water and HVAC repairs with seasonal checks. Small leaks and dirty filters turn into large bills when nobody looks.

The EPA's WaterSense program says the average household's leaks waste more than 9,300 gallons of water a year. It says 9% of homes have leaks that waste 50 gallons or more per day. The most common causes are worn toilet flappers, dripping faucets and other leaking valves. The EPA also says fixing easy leaks can save about 10% on water bills.

Use this seasonal checklist at each unit:

- [ ] Look under every sink for drips or water stains
- [ ] Check toilets for running water and loose bases
- [ ] Test the water heater relief valve area for leaks
- [ ] Change or check HVAC filters
- [ ] Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms
- [ ] Clear gutters and look at the roof after storms
- [ ] Check caulk around tubs, showers and windows
- [ ] Ask the tenant about small issues they have not reported

Give notice before each check, as your state's entry law asks. Tell tenants to report small leaks early. A tenant who reports a drip saves you a ceiling repair.

## How do I keep repair costs under control?

Keep repair costs down with a vendor list, clear limits and good records.

1. **Line up vendors before you need them.** A plumber you already know answers faster and charges no surprise rates.
2. **Ask for written quotes on big jobs.** Get two or three quotes for HVAC and roof work, where ranges are wide.
3. **Bundle small jobs.** One visit for three small fixes avoids three trip fees.
4. **Track every repair.** Log the date, unit, problem, vendor and cost. After a year, you have your own budget data.
5. **Know what the tenant pays.** Florida Statutes 83.51 says the landlord is not responsible for conditions the tenant caused by a negligent or wrongful act. Your lease should say how you bill tenant-caused damage.

## What are typical seller closing costs?

Seller closing costs are the fees and taxes taken out of your sale price at closing. They vary by state, by contract and by what you negotiate with your agent and the buyer.

The main lines:

| Cost | Who sets it | Notes |
|------|-------------|-------|
| Listing and buyer agent commission | You negotiate it with the agents | Ask for the rate in writing before you sign a listing |
| State and local transfer tax | State and local law | Some states charge none; others charge a percent |
| Title insurance and escrow or attorney fees | Local custom and the contract | Split rules differ by area |
| Prorated property tax | The tax calendar | You pay your share up to the closing date |
| Repairs after inspection | Your talks with the buyer | Often a credit instead of work |
| Seller concessions | Your talks with the buyer | Credits toward the buyer's closing costs |
| Loan payoff | Your lender | Not a cost, but it reduces your cash |
| Moving and prep | You | Cleaning, staging, storage |

Transfer tax example: Washington. The Washington Department of Revenue says all sales of real property in the state owe real estate excise tax (REET). The exception is a sale with a specific exemption. Usually the seller pays. For sales from January 1, 2027, the state rate is graduated:

| Part of the selling price | State REET rate |
|---------------------------|-----------------|
| Up to $551,000 | 1.1% |
| Over $551,000 up to $1,551,000 | 1.28% |
| Over $1,551,000 up to $3,051,000 | 2.75% |
| Over $3,051,000 | 3.0% |

On a $400,000 sale, the state part is $4,400. Check local add-ons and your own state's rules.

Taxes on the gain. IRS Publication 523 says selling expenses, including sales commissions, reduce your gain. If you owned and lived in the home 2 of the last 5 years, you may exclude up to $250,000 of gain. The limit is $500,000 if married filing jointly.

## What does it cost to get a house ready to rent?

Getting a house ready to rent costs the repairs and safety items a tenant needs, plus the first leasing costs. A home you lived in well is often cheap to prepare. A tired one is not.

Make-ready checklist with cost notes:

| Item | Cost source |
|------|-------------|
| Paint and deep clean | Contractor quotes |
| Repairs from a pre-rental walk-through | Contractor quotes |
| Rekey or replace locks | Locksmith quote |
| Smoke and CO alarms | Store price; check local code |
| City rental license or inspection | City fee schedule |
| Landlord insurance policy | Insurer quote |
| Manager setup fee | iPropertyManagement 2026 average: $185.24 |
| Leasing fee for the first tenant | iPropertyManagement 2026 average: 70.6% of one month's rent with management; 81.8% for leasing-only |
| Empty weeks while you list | Days empty × daily rent |

IRS Publication 527 sets the start date for deductions. You can deduct ordinary and necessary rental costs from the time you make the home available for rent. Repairs before that date and improvements follow other rules. Ask a tax professional.

## What are the first-year costs of a rental?

First-year rental costs are the running costs plus the one-time start costs. Rent offsets them, so the real question is net cash after a year.

Running costs, with benchmarks:

- **Management:** iPropertyManagement's 2026 survey found an average of 8.49% of monthly rent.
- **Maintenance:** Baselane reports maintenance equaled 8.0% of rent receipts at the median rental in 2023, citing a Census survey analysis.
- **Vacancy:** the Census Bureau reported a national rental vacancy rate of 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026.
- **Insurance, property tax and HOA:** from your bills; tax can rise if you lose an owner-occupant exemption.
- **Mortgage:** your current payment, if you keep the loan.

## How do I put both in one table?

Put both paths in one table by writing what you would have in cash after 12 months. Here is an example with made-up house numbers. The fee rates use the sources above.

Assumptions: value $400,000, loan $220,000, rent $2,500 a month, mortgage payment $1,650, tax $4,800, insurance $1,600. Commission and title amounts are examples, not quotes.

**Path A: sell now**

| Line | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Sale price | $400,000 |
| Commission (example: 5%) | −$20,000 |
| Washington state REET at 1.1% (if in Washington) | −$4,400 |
| Title, escrow and fees (example) | −$3,000 |
| Repairs or credits (example) | −$4,000 |
| Loan payoff | −$220,000 |
| Cash at closing, before income tax | $148,600 |

**Path B: rent for 12 months**

| Line | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Rent, 12 months | $30,000 |
| Vacancy at 7.3% | −$2,190 |
| Management at 8.49% of rent collected | −$2,361 |
| Leasing fee at 70.6% of one month | −$1,765 |
| Setup fee | −$185 |
| Maintenance at 8% of rent collected | −$2,225 |
| Make-ready (example) | −$3,000 |
| Property tax | −$4,800 |
| Insurance | −$1,600 |
| Mortgage payments | −$19,800 |
| Net cash for the year | −$7,926 |
| Loan principal paid (example) | +$3,000 of equity |

How to read the example: in year 1, the rental costs about $7,900 in cash, mostly from one-time start costs and a high mortgage payment. You still own the house. Path B only wins if the house's value, the later years' cash flow and your tax position make up for it. Path A gives you $148,600 now, minus any tax.

Put your own numbers in each line. For the management lines, use our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## What changes the answer

- **Your mortgage rate.** A low fixed rate makes Path B cheaper to hold.
- **The 2-of-5-year window.** Publication 523 counts use as your home in the 5 years before sale. If you rent first, you may still sell within the window and keep the exclusion, except for gain equal to depreciation after May 6, 1997.
- **Your time.** Self-managing cuts the management line but costs hours.
- **Repairs.** A home that needs a lot of work may cost more to rent than to sell as-is.
- **Local law.** Rent rules, licensing and transfer taxes differ by state and city.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

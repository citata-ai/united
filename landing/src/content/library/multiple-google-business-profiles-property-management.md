## What does Google require for a second property management location?

Google requires a real location with its own staff for each Business Profile. Google's guidelines say a service business with different locations, separate service areas and separate staff at each location is allowed one profile for each location. The guidelines also say not to create more than one page for each location, in one account or many.

Use this test before you create a second profile:

| Question | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|
| Is there a physical office at this address in the company name? | Next question | Not eligible |
| Do your staff work there during posted hours? | Next question | Not eligible |
| If owners visit, is there permanent signage? | Show the address | Hide the address (service-area) |
| Does this office serve a different area than your first office? | Eligible for its own profile | One profile with a wider service area |
| Can you prove it with a lease, utility bill or license at that address? | Ready to verify | Wait until you can |

Keep names the same across locations. Google's chain guidelines say all locations in a country should use the same name and the same category for the same service. "Smith Property Management" in each city, not "Smith Property Management Naperville".

## Are virtual offices allowed for a property management Google profile?

No. Google's guidelines say a business that rents a physical mailing address but does not operate from it, a virtual office, is not eligible for a Business Profile. P.O. boxes and mailboxes at remote locations are not acceptable either.

Coworking spaces are a gray zone with clear rules. Google says a business can list an office at a coworking space only if three things are true:

1. The office keeps clear signage.
2. It receives customers there during business hours.
3. Your own staff work there during business hours.

A hot desk you book once a week does not meet that bar. If Google asks for video verification, you need to show signage and proof that you run the business there. Google's appeal page lists the evidence that helps: business registration, a business license, tax certificates and utility bills with the same name and address.

## What happens with duplicate Google Business Profiles?

Duplicate profiles confuse Google and owners, and they break the rules. Google's guidelines say there should be only one profile per business, because duplicates cause problems with how information displays on Maps and Search. Google also reserves the right to suspend profiles that break its guidelines.

Duplicates often appear by accident in property management:

- A past employee created a profile years ago with an old address.
- A brokerage and a management arm share one office but have two profiles under different names.
- A manager made a personal profile with the company name.
- An agency created a new profile instead of claiming the old one.

Fix them in this order. Find every profile with your name or phone. Claim the ones you can. Ask Google support to merge or remove duplicates at the same address. Never create a new profile to replace a suspended one; Google's appeal page says not to create a new profile for the same business.

### Can individual managers have profiles?

Google allows profiles for individual practitioners and names real estate agents as an example. The practitioner must be public-facing and reachable at the verified location during stated hours. Google says support staff should not have their own profiles, and sales or lead generation agents are not eligible. If one licensed broker is the only public-facing person, Google suggests one shared profile in the format "[company]: [name]".

## How do I rank in a city with no property management office?

Use a service area, a real city page and local proof. A Business Profile in a city where you have no staffed office is not an option. Google's local ranking help page names three factors: relevance, distance and prominence. You can raise relevance and prominence in a city; you cannot change distance.

1. **Add the city to your service area.** Google allows up to 20 service areas, within about 2 hours of driving from your base.
2. **Build one owner page for the city.** Name the neighborhoods you serve, the doors you manage there, local rent data, the local rules owners must follow, and a review from an owner in that city.
3. **Ask owners in that city for reviews.** Same honest request as everyone else; no rewards.
4. **Get listed where that city's owners look.** Local landlord groups, the city's chamber, directories that AI tools cite for that city.
5. **Open a real office when the doors justify it.** Then create a second profile with its own staff and verify it.

### Common mistakes to avoid

- **A second profile at a staff member's home.** It is not a staffed office that receives customers.
- **One profile per city with the same phone number.** Google's guidelines ask for a phone number that connects to the individual location.
- **City names added to the business name.** Google's guidelines do not allow unnecessary information in the name.
- **A service area that covers the whole state.** Keep it within about 2 hours of driving from your base.

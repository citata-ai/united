## What is a homestead exemption?

A residence homestead exemption reduces tax for an owner who meets the local ownership and use rules. It is not a national rental tax category. A benefit on your current bill may depend on living in the home.

The Texas Comptroller explains that its general residence homestead exemption removes part of a qualifying property's value from taxation. The owner must have an ownership interest and use the property as a principal residence. Other benefits have their own conditions.

Florida Revenue says renting a home can cause loss of the homestead exemption. Its guidance describes a limited short-term rental provision under Florida law. Do not assume that keeping the deed in your name preserves every owner benefit.

These states illustrate different local systems. Neither example decides the result for a home elsewhere. Ask the office that assesses your property to identify the benefits currently on your account.

## Do you have to report the change of use?

Ask the assessor how to report a move and rental conversion. Notice duties, forms, and effective dates differ. Keep the written response and proof of anything you submit.

Give the office your move-out date, lease start date, expected rental term, and whether you retain personal use. Mention another principal residence if you have one. Incomplete facts can produce an estimate that does not fit your case.

Texas Comptroller guidance says an applicant must state that they do not claim another residence homestead exemption in or outside Texas. That requirement makes the new home's status relevant. Check both properties before assuming benefits can overlap.

Florida Revenue's guidance says a qualifying owner can rent after January 1 and keep the exemption for that year under its stated short-term conditions. It references more than 30 rental days per calendar year for 2 consecutive years. Ask the property appraiser to review the full rule before relying on that exception.

Those dates are Florida-specific. They do not provide a grace period for other states. Check local advice before promising a lease based on a presumed tax saving.

## How much can the property tax bill rise?

The bill can rise by the value of lost benefits and any other applicable assessment or rate changes. There is no sound national percentage for a rental conversion. Ask for a calculation using the property's actual account.

Separate taxable value from tax owed. A smaller exemption changes the base to which the rate applies. It does not mean the tax bill rises by the full exemption amount.

Here is a hypothetical calculation, not a Texas or Florida tax estimate. Assume taxable value rises by $50,000 because a benefit ends. Assume the applicable combined rate is 2% and no other rule changes.

The additional annual tax is $50,000 multiplied by 2%, or $1,000. That equals about $83.33 per month. Real bills can involve different taxing units, exemptions, caps, and rate changes.

Replace both assumptions with the assessor's estimate. Ask whether any assessment limit or tax classification also changes. Review the result again when the actual bill arrives.

If the lender pays tax through escrow, update the cash budget for the expected bill. An old escrow payment can lag behind a tax change. Confirm the timing with the servicer rather than treating escrow as a fixed operating cost.

## Is property tax deductible on a rental?

Qualifying rental property tax generally is a rental expense under IRS Publication 527. The federal tax treatment is separate from local eligibility for a homestead benefit. Losing an exemption does not make the larger bill cost-free.

Publication 527 distinguishes property taxes from some assessments for local improvements. A special assessment may need different treatment. Save the bill detail rather than giving your preparer only the total payment.

If you use the home personally or rent only part, expense allocation may apply. Record the rental start date and the use of each part. Ask a tax professional to match the cost to the right period and use.

A deduction reduces taxable income when allowed. It does not refund the whole tax payment. Keep cash planning separate from a possible tax benefit, especially when rental loss limits may apply.

## How should the new tax estimate affect your rental decision?

Use the revised estimate before deciding whether rent covers the home. Combine it with rental insurance, management, repairs, vacancy, and loan payments. Compare full costs with supported local rent.

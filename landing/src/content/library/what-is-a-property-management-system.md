## What modules does a PMS have?

A property management system links operating records so staff can complete work without rebuilding the same facts. The exact modules and limits depend on the product and plan.

Buildium's product page describes property accounting, leasing, maintenance, portals, and reporting. AppFolio's pricing page lists similar operating areas across its packages. These are examples of the category, not proof that every PMS has equal depth.

| Module | Main record | Practical use |
|---|---|---|
| Property and unit records | Address, unit, owner, status | Find the right property context |
| Leasing | Applicant, lease, dates, documents | Track occupancy and agreements |
| Payments | Charge, receipt, refund, status | Explain a balance |
| Accounting | Ledger and financial reports | Review funds and results |
| Maintenance | Request, work order, invoice | Track work from report to completion |
| Communication | Message and recipient history | Keep a clear service record |
| Portals | Approved user access | Let owners and residents complete tasks |

Ask which records share a stable link. A suite can contain many modules while still requiring repeated entry between them.

Test a complete task rather than a feature name. A rent payment should reach the right lease, ledger, and report with a clear status.

## How is a PMS different from accounting software?

Accounting software focuses on financial records, while a PMS also manages property work and relationships. Some PMS products include substantial accounting tools; others need another system for parts of the books.

A ledger records the financial effect of work. A maintenance workflow also needs the request, assigned vendor, access instructions, progress, and completion evidence. Both views matter, but they answer different questions.

Decide which system owns each accounting record before adding a connection. Sending transactions to another ledger without a clear plan can create duplicate or conflicting entries.

Ask your accountant to inspect required reports. Confirm property and owner detail, opening balances, transfers, corrections, and exports. Do not assume that a bank feed proves reconciliation or trust-accounting support.

Use a sample refund and returned payment in the demo. These can reveal whether staff can trace a correction across the operating and financial records.

If your main need is expense tracking for personal rentals, a focused accounting tool may be enough. If staff manage leases, owners, and maintenance, assess the wider workflow too.

## Who uses a property management system?

Owners, management companies, accounting staff, leasing teams, and maintenance staff use different parts of a PMS. Residents and rental owners may use limited portals linked to the same records.

Access should match the job. A vendor needs the assigned work, while an owner needs approved property reports. Neither needs unrestricted access to the whole company account.

Rentec Direct distinguishes its landlord and manager editions on its pricing page. That illustrates why the same vendor name can cover different operating needs.

Association managers also need workflows that differ from rental management. Dues, board approvals, and community requests should not depend on confusing rental workarounds.

Commercial managers may need lease terms and recoverable-expense workflows beyond a basic residential rent record. Yardi's Voyager Commercial page describes lease administration for office, retail, and industrial properties.

Write the required user roles before choosing a system. Ask a future user from each role to complete a sample task. The people who use the software daily should help test it.

## How is a PMS priced?

PMS pricing can use a base fee, a per-unit charge, plan tiers, or a custom quote. Read the current vendor pricing page and request the full cost for your workflow.

AppFolio's pricing page uses per-unit starting rates and minimums. Rentec Direct's pricing page uses editions and unit-sensitive pricing. Enterprise products may require a written proposal instead of a public subscription rate.

A published price is only the starting point. Check setup, imports, training, screening, payments, signing, messages, and partner services. Ask which charges your business pays and which a resident may see.

Record the quote date, billing term, counted units, included users, and required modules. That makes later comparisons clearer. Do not mix annual rates with monthly rates without labeling the terms.

Treat migration as a project. Old records need review, mapping, and a checked opening position. A low subscription does not remove that work.

Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) as a planning aid for your service costs. Put the quoted software cost into your own budget before signing.

## What are the main PMS vendors in the US?

Buildium, AppFolio, Rentec Direct, Yardi, and RealPage are useful starting names for a shortlist. This is not a market-share ranking or a claim that they fit every portfolio.

The vendors' own pages show different product scopes. RealPage describes OneSite for multifamily operations. Yardi offers a commercial platform. Smaller owner needs and association work deserve their own evaluation.

Choose by the hardest task your team must complete. A product that handles the normal payment flow may still struggle with your owner reporting, board approval, or commercial lease process.

Use this first-demo checklist:

- **Name the task:** Describe the current problem and the record it should produce.
- **Trace a record:** Follow a lease, payment, or request through the relevant modules.
- **Check access:** Confirm that each role sees the correct information.
- **Correct an error:** Inspect the original record and the change history.
- **Review reports:** Ask the accountant and operating lead to check the output.
- **Confirm price:** Get the required modules and fees in writing.
- **Test the exit:** Export records your team can inspect and reuse.

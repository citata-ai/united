Most cities have a Facebook group where small landlords trade advice. A typical post: "Tenant is 10 days late. What notice do I give?" The landlord asking that has 1 to 5 homes and no manager. Some of them will hire one within a year. If your answers are the clearest in the group, they know your name when that day comes.

The numbers keep this in scale. In the Goodjuju Marketing fall 2026 study of 35 property management companies, social media gave 0.8% of owner leads from 4.3% of traffic. Facebook groups are a trust channel. Run them on a time budget.

## How do I join without breaking group rules?

Join as yourself, read the rules, and disclose your business before you post. Group admins control the group. Facebook's help center shows the tools they use:

- membership questions to screen new members;
- approval of members before they join;
- approval of posts before they show;
- group experts, a badge for trusted members.

So the admin decides what you can post. Break a rule and the admin removes you.

Meta's own spam policy applies too. It restricts posting "at very high frequencies," and it may restrict accounts at lower frequencies "when other indicators of Spam (e.g., posting repetitive content)" are present. It also bans buying or selling engagement such as likes, shares and clicks.

Before your first post:

- [ ] Read the group rules and pinned posts
- [ ] Answer the membership questions honestly, including your job
- [ ] Check if business posts are allowed, and on which days
- [ ] Add your role to your profile intro: "Owner, [Company] Property Management"
- [ ] Watch the group for 1 to 2 weeks before you answer

If the rules ban business owners, respect that and leave.

## Which answers build trust?

Answers that solve the exact problem with a fact, a source and a next step build trust. Landlords in these groups get many opinions. A clear answer with a source stands out.

| Question | Weak answer | Strong answer |
|---|---|---|
| "Tenant is late. What now?" | "Evict them." | Names your state's notice and its statute, the steps, and "check your lease for a grace period" |
| "What should I charge for rent?" | "Check Zillow." | 3 recent leases near the address, with beds and dates, and a range |
| "Is 10% too much for a manager?" | "We charge less, call us." | What 10% covers in your market, the extra fees to ask about, and a sample yearly cost |
| "Should I let a tenant paint?" | "Never." | Options: written approval, color list, deposit terms |

Rules for each answer:

1. **Answer first** in 1 to 2 sentences.
2. **Cite the rule** for any legal point, and say laws differ by state and city.
3. **Show experience:** "We see this about twice a month."
4. **Disclose:** "I run a property management company here."
5. **No link** unless the rules allow it and the reader needs it.

Never give advice that steers a landlord to pick or reject tenants by a protected class. If a post does that, do not join in. Fair Housing rules apply to advice too.

### Worked example: a time budget

| Task | Time per week |
|---|---|
| Read 2 local groups | 30 minutes |
| Write 4 strong answers | 60 minutes |
| Note repeat questions for your website | 10 minutes |
| Total | 100 minutes |

Over 6 months that is about 43 hours. If those hours bring 2 owners with 2 doors each at $150 a month per door, that is $600 a month in new fees. Our [fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) helps you set your own numbers.

## Should I start my own group?

Start your own group only if you will run it every week for a year. An empty group hurts trust more than no group.

Steps from Facebook's help center: create the group, pick public or private, invite people, then add a cover photo and description. Facebook says it recommends "that group admins share any commercial or business affiliations in the group." So say clearly in the description: "Run by [Company] Property Management."

A good own-group format:

- **Name:** "[City] Landlords: Rules and Q&A", not your company name.
- **Rules:** no spam, no tenant shaming, no Fair Housing violations, business posts on one day.
- **Weekly post:** one local rule or rent fact each week.
- **Monthly live Q&A:** 30 minutes, answers only.
- **Guest experts:** a local attorney or CPA once a quarter.

Invite your current owners first. They seed real questions.

## How do I move from group help to a call?

Move to a call with an offer that fits the question, never with a cold pitch. Fourandhalf, a property management marketing agency, advises keeping the same message across channels, so the group answer and your site should say the same thing.

Paths that work:

1. **The landlord asks you directly.** Reply in private with a short answer and offer a 15-minute call.
2. **A rent question.** Offer a free rental analysis for their address.
3. **A burnout post** ("I'm done with this"). Reply in public with useful options. Send a private note only if they invite it.
4. **A repeat question.** Write a page on your site that answers it fully. Share it when the group allows links.

Ask every new owner, "Where did you first hear of us?" Write the answer in your CRM. After 6 months, compare owners from groups with the 43 hours spent. Our [property management page](/property-management) lists what we help managers measure.

## What signs show a rental no longer works?

A rental no longer works when the money, the time, or the risk stops fitting your life. One sign is a reason to look. Three signs together are a reason to run the numbers.

| Sign | What to look at |
|------|-----------------|
| Cash flow is near zero or negative | 12 months of statements, not one bad month |
| Large capital items are due | Roof, HVAC, sewer line, foundation quotes |
| Rents in the area have stalled | Recent leases for similar homes |
| Vacancy is rising | Days empty per year, compared with the past |
| You dread the calls | Time spent per month, stress, health |
| Problems per lease are rising | Late payments, repair requests, turnover |
| Local rules are adding cost | Rental licensing, inspections, rent limits |
| The equity is large and earns little | The return on equity test below |
| Your goals have changed | Retirement, a move, a child's tuition |

For context on the market, the Census Bureau put the national rental vacancy rate at 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026. FHFA reported house prices up 2.6% from July 2025 to July 2026. Your local numbers matter more than these national ones.

## What is the return on equity test?

The return on equity test compares what your rental earns with the cash you would net if you sold. It answers one question: is the money inside this house working hard enough?

The formula:

```text
Return on equity = (yearly cash flow + yearly loan paydown + expected appreciation)
                   ÷ (sale price − selling costs − loan balance − taxes due on sale)
```

Worked example, with example numbers. The appreciation line uses FHFA's 12-month national rate as a placeholder.

| Line | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Estimated sale price | $450,000 |
| Selling costs (example: 6%) | −$27,000 |
| Loan balance | −$150,000 |
| Estimated tax due on sale (from your tax preparer; example) | −$30,000 |
| Net equity you could move | $243,000 |
| Yearly cash flow after all costs | $6,000 |
| Yearly principal paydown | $4,800 |
| Appreciation at 2.6% of $450,000 | $11,700 |
| Total yearly return | $22,500 |
| Return on equity, all three parts | 9.3% |
| Return on equity, cash flow only | 2.5% |

How to read it: the house earns 9.3% on $243,000 if the 2.6% price growth holds. Only 2.5% of that arrives as cash. If you need income, that cash number is the one that matters.

## How do I compare equity returns with a new investment?

Compare the rental's return on equity with the return of a real option, for the same money and similar risk. Write both side by side.

| Question | Keep the rental | Sell and move the money |
|----------|-----------------|-------------------------|
| Yearly return on $243,000 | 9.3% total, 2.5% cash (example) | The expected return of the new option |
| Hours per month | Your real number | Usually fewer |
| Concentration risk | One house, one street | Depends on the option |
| Taxes paid now | None | Sale taxes, unless you exchange |
| Liquidity | Low | Depends on the option |
| Upkeep ahead | Roof, HVAC, turnover | None, or the new property's |

Three common choices:

1. **Keep and fix.** Raise rent to market at renewal, cut a cost, or refinance. Then re-run the test.
2. **Sell and exchange.** Move the equity into a property that earns more, through a 1031 exchange.
3. **Sell and exit.** Pay the tax and move the money into a non-real-estate option, or pay off debt.

If the main pain is time, not money, a fourth choice is to hire a manager. Price that with our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) and re-run the cash flow line.

## How do taxes change the timing?

Taxes change the timing because the same sale can cost very different amounts in different years or structures. Get a tax estimate before you list.

The main federal rules, from IRS sources:

| Rule | What it says | Timing effect |
|------|--------------|---------------|
| Long-term vs short-term (Topic 409) | Gain on property held more than 1 year is long-term | Hold at least 1 year and 1 day |
| Capital gain rates (Topic 409) | Most net capital gain is taxed at no more than 15% for most people; 0% or 20% apply at some income levels | A low-income year can mean a lower rate |
| Depreciation recapture (Topic 409) | Unrecaptured section 1250 gain is taxed at a maximum 25% rate | Applies whenever you sell, except in a 1031 exchange |
| Net investment income tax (Topic 559) | 3.8% on the lesser of net investment income or MAGI above $200,000 single, $250,000 married filing jointly | A sale in a lower-income year may avoid part of it |
| 1031 exchange (Form 8824 instructions) | Identify a replacement within 45 days and receive it within 180 days, or by the return due date if earlier | You must plan before you list |
| Home sale exclusion (Publication 523) | Up to $250,000, or $500,000 married filing jointly, if you owned and lived in the home 2 of the last 5 years | A former home has a window after you move out |

The former-home window. Publication 523 counts ownership and use in the 5 years before the sale. Say you lived in the house 2 years and then rented it out. You may still meet the test if you sell within about 3 years of moving out. The exclusion does not cover gain equal to depreciation claimed after May 6, 1997.

Example: you moved out on 2024-06-30 after 2 years as your home. A sale that closes by 2027-06-30 can still meet the 2-of-5-year use test, if the other rules are met.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## A sell-or-keep checklist

- [ ] Get a market value estimate from 2 sources.
- [ ] Get your loan payoff amount.
- [ ] Ask a tax professional for the tax due on a sale this year and next year.
- [ ] Price the large repairs due in the next 3 years.
- [ ] Run the return on equity test, total and cash only.
- [ ] Write down one real option for the money and its expected return.
- [ ] Check your own time and stress, in hours per month.
- [ ] If you might exchange, line up a qualified intermediary before you list.
- [ ] If the house was your home, check the Publication 523 dates.
- [ ] Check your lease end date. Many buyers want a vacant home or a lease they can take over.

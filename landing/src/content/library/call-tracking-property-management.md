An owner sees your postcard, searches your name and calls the number on your Google listing. Your report says "Google". The postcard gets no credit, and next quarter you cut it. Call tracking fixes that gap. It gives each source its own number and logs every call against it.

Phone calls matter in property management because many owners do not fill in forms. Goodjuju's 2026 lead study counted form leads only and said so in its method notes. It removed call-tracking events from the form counts because they inflated the totals (Goodjuju, 2026). So a form-only report misses part of your owner demand.

## How does call tracking with dynamic numbers work?

Call tracking with dynamic numbers works by swapping the phone number on your website based on how the visitor arrived. A small script reads the visit source, such as Google Ads, organic search or an email link. It then shows a number from a pool tied to that source. When the owner calls, the software forwards the call to your office and logs the source, the page and the time.

There are two types of tracking number:

| Type | Where it goes | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Static number | One per offline source: postcard, yard sign, radio, a directory | Calls from that one source |
| Dynamic number (DNI) | Website, swapped by script per visit | Source, campaign, keyword, landing page |

DNI means dynamic number insertion: the script that swaps the number. A static number is cheaper and simpler. Use one per postcard batch or sign design. Use DNI on the website, where one page gets visits from many sources.

## Does call tracking hurt local SEO?

Call tracking can hurt local SEO when it puts many different numbers on your public listings. Google, Yelp, Bing and the directories compare your name, address and phone number across the web. Mixed numbers make your business facts less clear.

Google's Business Profile guidelines ask you to represent your business as it is known in the real world (Google Business Profile Help). Follow these rules to keep your listings clean:

1. **Keep one main local number.** Put it on your Google Business Profile, Yelp, Bing and every directory.
2. **Keep the main number in your site code.** Let DNI swap only the number that visitors see, not the number in your footer HTML or schema markup.
3. **Use static tracking numbers only offline.** A postcard or a sign is a closed channel, so a separate number there does not reach the directories.
4. **Tag your Google Business Profile link.** Add UTM tags (tracking codes in a link) to the website button. Goodjuju recommends this so Map Pack visits show as their own line in GA4 (Goodjuju, 2026).

## Can I record calls for training?

You can record calls for training, but the consent rule depends on your state. Federal law allows a person to record a call when that person is a party to the call or one party consents (18 U.S.C. 2511(2)(d)). Some states set a stricter rule.

California is one example. Penal Code 632 makes it a crime to record a confidential call without the consent of all parties. The fine is up to $2,500 per violation (California Penal Code 632). If you take calls from owners in more than one state, apply the strictest rule you may meet.

The safe setup:

- Play a short notice before the call connects: "This call may be recorded for quality and training."
- Store recordings with access limits. A call can hold an owner's address, rent and loan facts.
- Delete old recordings on a fixed schedule.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I report calls next to form leads?

Report calls in a separate column from form leads, by source. Mixed counts hide what works. Goodjuju's setup guide says to track phone calls apart from form leads and to keep tenant forms out of the owner count (Goodjuju, 2026).

A simple monthly table:

| Source | Owner form leads | Owner calls (60+ sec) | Signed doors |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search and Map Pack | | | |
| Google Ads | | | |
| Postcards (static number) | | | |
| Referral and directories | | | |
| AI tools (by answer to "how did you hear") | | | |

Filter calls by length. A 10-second call is often a wrong number or a tenant. Tag each owner call by hand in the first month. Then you learn which length and which source mean a real owner.

AI answers add one more gap. An owner can read your number in a ChatGPT answer and call without a visit. No tracking number catches that. Ask every caller how they first heard about you, and log the answer. Our guide on [tracking AI traffic](/guides/track-ai-traffic-property-management) shows the rest of the setup.

## Checklist before you turn it on

- One main local number on every public listing.
- DNI script on the website, main number kept in the HTML.
- One static number per offline campaign.
- Recording notice set for the strictest state you serve.
- Calls and form leads in separate report columns.
- "How did you first hear about us?" asked on every owner call.

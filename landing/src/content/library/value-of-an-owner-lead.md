## How do I calculate revenue per door per year?

Revenue per door is your monthly fee per door times 12, plus other fees you earn on that door each year. Start with the management fee, because it repeats every month.

Example inputs, from public data:

| Input | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Median gross rent, US | $1,413 a month | U.S. Census Bureau, QuickFacts 2020–2024 |
| Typical management fee | 8 to 12% of rent | All Property Management |
| Fee used here | 10% | midpoint |
| Fee per door per month | $141.30 | $1,413 × 10% |
| Fee per door per year | $1,696 | $141.30 × 12 |

Then add the fees you earn on top. All Property Management says some managers charge a tenant placement fee of about 50% of the first month's rent. At $1,413 rent, one placement is about $707. Add it only as often as a door really turns over.

Property Manager Websites (PMW) uses $2,200 per door per year in its own worksheet. That is a fee plus extra programs. Your number may be higher or lower. The [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) gives you a starting figure in 1 minute.

## How much is one owner worth over time?

One owner's lifetime value is revenue per door × doors per owner × years the owner stays. PMW calls this LTV (lifetime value) and uses the same 3 inputs.

| Input | Example |
|---|---|
| Revenue per door per year | $1,696 |
| Doors per owner | 1.2 |
| Years the owner stays | 4 |
| Lifetime value | $1,696 × 1.2 × 4 = $8,139 |

Get doors per owner from your software: total doors divided by total owners. Get tenure from your churn: if 25% of owners leave each year, the average owner stays about 4 years. The 1.2 and the 4 are PMW's example inputs, not industry averages.

## What close rate should I use?

Use your own close rate: signed owners divided by owner leads, over the last 6 to 12 months. Count only owner leads. A tenant asking about a showing is not an owner lead.

We did not find a public close-rate benchmark with a clear method. PMW's series uses example rates only, such as 40% for paid leads and 60% for referrals. Those are teaching numbers. So run the math at 3 rates and see how much the answer moves.

| Close rate | Value of one lead ($8,139 × rate) |
|---|---|
| 10% | $814 |
| 20% | $1,628 |
| 30% | $2,442 |

The close rate moves lead value more than any other input. If you lift it from 10% to 20%, every lead you already pay for doubles in value. Speed of reply and a clear fee table are the usual places to start.

## How much can I pay per lead and still profit?

You can pay up to your cost cap per owner times your close rate. PMW suggests a lifetime value to acquisition cost ratio of 4:1. So the cap per signed owner is $8,139 ÷ 4 = $2,035.

| Close rate | Max cost per lead at 4:1 |
|---|---|
| 10% | $203 |
| 20% | $407 |
| 30% | $610 |

Compare that cap with what leads cost. PMW's worksheet says its PPC (pay per click) clients see $250 to $350 per qualified lead. WordStream's 2026 benchmarks put the median real estate cost per lead in Google and Microsoft Ads at $102.51. Real estate there is a broad category, not only property management.

So, at a 10% close rate, a $250 ad lead loses money against a 4:1 target. At 20%, it fits. The channel is the same. Your close rate decides.

Watch for: these figures use revenue, not profit. If your profit margin is 25%, profit per owner is about $2,035, not $8,139. For a strict cap, run the math on profit.

## How does lead value change with door count per owner?

Lead value rises in a straight line with doors per owner. The cost to win the owner stays about the same. That is why many managers target investors with several doors.

| Doors per owner | Lifetime value (4 years) | Lead value at 20% | Max cost per lead at 4:1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $6,782 | $1,356 | $339 |
| 2 | $13,565 | $2,713 | $678 |
| 4 | $27,130 | $5,426 | $1,357 |
| 10 | $67,824 | $13,565 | $3,391 |

Use this table to set different targets by lead type. A one-door accidental landlord and a 10-door investor are not the same lead. Tag lead size in your CRM on the first call.

## How to use lead value this week

1. **Pull 3 numbers** from your software: doors, owners, and owners lost in the last 12 months.
2. **Pull 1 number** from your CRM: signed owners ÷ owner leads.
3. **Run the math** in the tables above with your own inputs.
4. **Compare** your max cost per lead with what each channel costs you now.
5. **Cut or fix** any channel above the cap. Often the fix is faster follow-up, not less spend.

Lead value also tells you what a new source is worth. AI answers now bring a small share of owner leads. See [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping) for what changed in search.

## South Carolina Eviction Rules at a Glance

A South Carolina eviction runs through 2 chapters of Title 27. Chapter 40 sets the notices. Chapter 37 sets the court steps. I read both on the South Carolina Legislature site on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Unpaid rent notice | 5 days after rent is due | S.C. Code 27-40-710(B) |
| Lease clause as notice | Allowed if conspicuous | 27-40-710(B), 27-37-10(B) |
| Lease violation notice | 14 days to fix or leave | 27-40-710(A) |
| Health or safety breach | 14 days after written notice | 27-40-720(b) |
| End month-to-month tenancy | 30 days written notice | 27-40-770(b) |
| Tenant response to rule | 10 days after service | 27-37-20 |
| Writ after verdict for landlord | Within 5 days | 27-37-100 |
| Time to vacate after writ | 24 hours | 27-37-160 |
| Appeal bond to stay ejectment | Within 5 days of notice of appeal | 27-37-130 |
| Belongings left on a public street | Removed after 48 hours (business days) | 27-40-710(D) |
| Self-help lockout | Banned | 27-40-760, 27-40-660 |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What Notice Must a South Carolina Landlord Give for Unpaid Rent?

A South Carolina landlord gives 5 days written notice for unpaid rent. Under S.C. Code 27-40-710(B), if the tenant does not pay within 5 days of the due date, the landlord may end the lease. The notice must say the rent is unpaid and that the lease will end if the rent is not paid.

The lease can replace that notice. S.C. Code 27-40-710(B) says a conspicuous clause in the written lease satisfies the notice rule. The statute gives sample text that starts "IF YOU DO NOT PAY YOUR RENT ON TIME". The clause then says the tenant gets no other notice. S.C. Code 27-37-10(B) repeats this rule for ejectment cases.

Two more points from 27-40-710(B):

- **One notice per lease term.** After you give one such notice in a term, you do not need to give it again in that term.
- **The clause carries over.** It also covers a month-to-month tenancy that follows the original term.

What to do: put the statutory clause in bold in every residential lease. It saves 5 days on each later case.

## How Long Does an Eviction Take in South Carolina?

An uncontested South Carolina eviction often takes about 3 to 4 weeks from the missed rent date. That is my estimate from the statutory day counts. Court calendars and service delays change it.

Worked timeline for unpaid rent due on the 1st, with no lease clause:

| Day | Step | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rent due and unpaid | 27-40-310(c) |
| 1 | Landlord delivers 5-day written notice | 27-40-710(B) |
| 7 | Landlord applies to the magistrate for a rule to vacate or show cause | 27-37-20 |
| 8 to 10 | Rule served on the tenant | 27-37-30 |
| 18 to 20 | 10-day response period ends | 27-37-20 |
| 19 to 22 | No appearance: magistrate issues warrant of ejectment | 27-37-40 |
| 21 to 25 | Officer serves writ; tenant has 24 hours | 27-37-160 |

With the lease clause, you can file on day 6 or 7 without a separate notice. If the tenant contests, the magistrate hears the case "forthwith" under 27-37-60. Either side may ask for a jury under 27-37-80.

Service can slow the case. Under 27-37-30(C), after 2 failed service tries, the rule can be posted and mailed through the court clerk. Then service is complete 10 days after mailing, and the 10-day response period starts on day 11.

## Where Does a Landlord File an Eviction in South Carolina?

A South Carolina landlord files with a magistrate who has jurisdiction over the property. S.C. Code 27-37-20 tells the magistrate to issue a written rule. The rule orders the tenant to leave, or to appear and show cause within 10 days after service.

S.C. Code 27-40-710(C) adds 2 useful facts:

- **No bond.** The landlord may get an eviction in magistrate's or circuit court without posting bond.
- **Managers can file.** A licensed broker-in-charge or licensed property manager may complete a form writ of eviction and present facts for the landlord. No separate charge may be made for this service.

Cost: S.C. Code 8-21-1010(A)(9) sets a magistrate fee of $20 for landlord proceedings against a tenant. That covers notices to quit, eviction orders and recovery of rents. Service by mail through the clerk adds the fee in 8-21-1010(A)(14), which is $5. Ask the magistrate's office for the full cost, including constable or sheriff service.

A claim for back rent in magistrate court is limited to $7,500 under S.C. Code 22-3-10(1). For a larger claim, use circuit court.

## Can a South Carolina Landlord Evict for a Lease Violation or With No Cause?

Yes, for both, with the right notice. The notice depends on the reason.

| Reason | Notice | Can the tenant fix it? | Statute |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unpaid rent | 5 days | Yes, by paying in the 5 days | 27-40-710(B) |
| Illegal activity on the premises | Written notice under the same subsection as rent | No cure period in the text | 27-40-710(B), 27-40-540 |
| Other lease breach | 14 days | Yes, if fixed in 14 days or started in good faith | 27-40-710(A) |
| Health or safety breach | 14 days, or sooner in an emergency | Yes | 27-40-720(b) |
| End of month-to-month tenancy | 30 days | Not a breach | 27-40-770(b) |
| Holdover after the lease ends | None stated | No | 27-40-770(c) |

South Carolina has no just-cause rule for the end of a lease. A landlord may end a month-to-month tenancy with 30 days written notice, or let a fixed term run out.

The landlord may not evict to retaliate. S.C. Code 27-40-910 bars an action for possession after a protected complaint. That means a complaint to a housing code agency, or to the landlord about a violation of the Act. Exceptions in 27-40-910(c) include a tenant-caused code violation or a real breach under 27-40-710 or 27-40-720.

## Who Removes the Tenant After a Judgment in South Carolina?

A constable or deputy sheriff removes the tenant in South Carolina. The landlord does not. S.C. Code 27-37-100 says the magistrate issues the writ within 5 days after a verdict for the landlord.

Under S.C. Code 27-37-160, the officer goes to the unit and gives the occupants 24 hours to leave. If the unit looks occupied and no one answers, the officer posts the writ on the door. After 24 hours, only a deputy sheriff, not a constable, may enter by force. The officer may grant a delay for an ill or elderly tenant.

Belongings follow S.C. Code 27-40-710(D). Items put on a public street are removed by city or county officials after 48 hours, not counting weekends and holidays. The eviction notice must tell the tenant this rule.

An appeal does not stop the eviction by itself. Under 27-37-130, the tenant must post a bond within 5 days after service of the notice of appeal. Under 27-40-800(b), the tenant can also stay execution by signing an undertaking to pay rent as it comes due.

## Is a Self-Help Eviction Legal in South Carolina?

No. S.C. Code 27-40-760 bars a landlord from taking possession except as the Act allows. That includes cutting essential services, such as water, heat or power, to force a tenant out.

The penalty is in S.C. Code 27-40-660. A tenant who is locked out, or whose essential services are cut on purpose, may recover possession or end the lease. The tenant may also recover 3 months' rent or twice actual damages, whichever is greater, plus attorney's fees.

## Which phrases risk a fair housing complaint?

Phrases that describe a kind of person, not the home, risk a fair housing complaint. 24 CFR 100.75 makes it unlawful to publish a rental ad that "indicates any preference, limitation or discrimination" because of race, color, religion, sex, handicap (disability), familial status or national origin. The rule covers signs, flyers, applications and spoken statements too.

The rule also names 4 forms of a bad ad (100.75(c)):

1. **Words, photos or symbols** that show a unit is or is not open to a group.
2. **Telling agents or staff** you prefer or do not want a group.
3. **Picking media or places** so a group does not hear about the unit.
4. **Refusing ads or charging more** because of a protected class.

Here is a swap table. The left column shows phrases we built from those 7 protected classes. Each one describes a person. The right column describes the home.

| Risky phrase | Protected class at risk | Safer phrase |
|---|---|---|
| "Adults only", "no kids" | Familial status | "2-bedroom, 850 sq ft" |
| "Perfect for a single professional" | Familial status, sex | "Quiet street, 1 parking space" |
| "Ideal for a couple" | Familial status | "1 bedroom, walk-in closet" |
| "Must be able to climb stairs" | Disability | "Third floor, no elevator" |
| "English speakers only" | National origin | "Application in English and Spanish" |
| "Christian home" | Religion | Leave it out |
| "Great for seniors" | Familial status | "Single-level unit, grab bars in bath" |

The safe pattern: state facts about the unit and let each renter decide. "Third floor, no elevator" tells a fact. "Must be able to climb stairs" sets a rule about a person.

## Can I say "No Section 8"?

In many places, "No Section 8" is against the law. Federal fair housing rules do not list source of income as a protected class. But many states and cities do. The Poverty & Race Research Action Council (PRRAC) keeps a list of these laws. Its March 2026 update estimates that "over 57% of voucher holders are now covered". PRRAC notes 7 states added since December 2018: New York, California, Colorado, Rhode Island, Maryland, Virginia and Illinois.

Where a source-of-income law applies, an ad that refuses vouchers is evidence of a violation. Phrases like "no programs" or "no housing assistance" carry the same risk. Check your state and city law before you post.

## Should I state the rent and the voucher size?

Yes, state the rent, and state the bedroom count. Leave the voucher size out. A voucher holder needs 3 facts to see if your unit fits:

- **Rent:** the monthly rent you ask.
- **Utilities:** which ones you pay and which ones the tenant pays. The PHA uses a utility allowance for tenant-paid utilities.
- **Bedrooms:** the unit size.

Do not write "3-bedroom voucher only" or "for a family of 4". The family's voucher size is a PHA decision, and occupancy wording can touch familial status. Write the unit size and let the family check its voucher.

Watch for: the rent in the ad is your ask, not a promise of approval. Under 24 CFR 982.507, the PHA must find the rent reasonable against similar unassisted units. ## Ad template for a voucher-friendly listing

Copy this template. Replace each bracket. Keep each line about the home or the terms.

```text
[2]-Bedroom, [1]-Bath [Apartment / House] for Rent — [Neighborhood], [City]

Rent: $[1,450] per month
Deposit: $[1,450]
Available: [2026-11-01]
Lease: [12 months]

Utilities: Owner pays [water, sewer, trash]. Tenant pays [electric, gas].
Size: [850] sq ft, [second] floor, [no elevator / elevator].
Features: [Gas stove, refrigerator, in-unit washer/dryer, central air].
Parking: [1 off-street space].
Pets: [Pets considered / No pets].
Transit: [0.3 miles to the #8 bus].

Housing Choice Vouchers accepted. We follow the PHA's inspection
and approval steps and can complete the RFTA packet fast.

How to apply: [link or phone]. Screening criteria: [income rule,
rental history rule, application fee if allowed] — the same for
every applicant.

Equal Housing Opportunity.
```

Why each part is there:

- **Rent, deposit and utilities first.** Voucher holders filter on these.
- **Floor and elevator as facts.** A renter with a disability can decide; you set no rule about people.
- **"The same for every applicant."** Uniform screening criteria protect you.
- **"Equal Housing Opportunity."** A short, common closing line.

## Where do I list for voucher holders?

List on at least 3 kinds of channel: your PHA's landlord list, a voucher listing site, and a general rental site. Using broad channels also helps you meet 24 CFR 100.75(c)(3), which bans picking media to keep a group from learning about the unit.

| Channel | What we verified (2026-10-19) |
|---|---|
| PHA landlord list | Many PHAs publish or share a vacancy list. Ask your PHA how to add a unit. |
| GoSection8 | gosection8.com now redirects to AffordableHousing.com (our check, 2026-10-19). |
| AffordableHousing.com | Free plan: "Free unlimited listings (with ads)". Premium and Enterprise plans add boosts. |
| General rental sites | Reach renters who do not search voucher sites. Put "Housing Choice Vouchers accepted" in the text. |

Before you post, run this 5-point check:

- [ ] Every line describes the unit, the terms or how to apply.
- [ ] No word, photo or symbol suggests who should live there.
- [ ] Rent, deposit and utilities are clear.
- [ ] The ad says vouchers are accepted.
- [ ] The screening criteria are the same for every applicant.

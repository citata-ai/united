## Why does the services page matter so much?

The services page is where an owner decides if you do what they need, at a price they accept. Most owners reach it from Google. In Goodjuju Marketing's fall 2026 study of 35 property management companies, Google Search and the Map Pack brought 50.6% of owner leads. Direct and Maps brought another 35.6%.

So the owner who lands on your services page is already shopping. They have 2 or 3 tabs open. The page with the clearest answer gets the call.

Our own work shows how often this page is weak. When we rebuilt one Chicago-area manager's site in October 2026, the old site had 29 city pages. Two of them, Palatine and Skokie, were 96% identical. Section 8, a service owners ask about, had 1 FAQ answer. The site belongs to Fridman Properties, a related party: its owner is CEO of MyRemotely, our partner. Details are in the [Fridman Properties case study](/case-studies/fridman-properties).

## Which services should each page list?

List every service the fee covers, in the words owners use. Owners do not search "asset stewardship". They search "rent collection" and "who handles repairs".

Use this checklist for full management. Keep each line only if you do it.

- [ ] Rental pricing and a free rental analysis
- [ ] Marketing the vacancy and showings
- [ ] Tenant screening (state the criteria you apply to every applicant)
- [ ] Lease signing and move-in inspection
- [ ] Rent collection and late-rent follow-up
- [ ] Maintenance requests, after-hours line and vendor work
- [ ] Routine inspections (state how often)
- [ ] Owner statements, year-end tax forms and an owner portal
- [ ] Lease renewals and rent changes
- [ ] Move-out, security deposit return and turnover
- [ ] Evictions (state who pays the attorney)
- [ ] Section 8 and Housing Choice Voucher work, if you do it

Screening text needs care. Describe the same written criteria for every applicant. Never describe a preferred kind of tenant.

## How do I show what is in the fee?

Show what is in the fee with one table: service, included or extra, and the extra cost. Owners trust a page that names the extras before they ask.

| Item | Included in the monthly fee? | Extra cost |
|---|---|---|
| Rent collection and owner statements | Yes | — |
| Maintenance coordination | Yes | Repair cost at vendor price; state any markup |
| Tenant placement | No | [X]% of first month's rent, or $[X] |
| Lease renewal | No | $[X] |
| Routine inspection | Yes, [X] per year | $[X] for extra visits |
| Eviction filing | No | Attorney and court fees at cost |
| Vacancy | No monthly fee while vacant? | State yes or no |

If your fee changes by property type or size, show a range and the 2 or 3 things that move it. A blank cell reads like a hidden fee. Owners can test your numbers with our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## Should leasing-only have its own page?

Leasing-only should have its own page if you sell it. It is a different service with a different fee and a different owner. The full-management owner wants peace of mind. The leasing-only owner wants a good tenant fast and plans to self-manage after.

On one shared page, both owners see a fee table that does not fit them. On 2 pages, each owner sees one price, one process and one call to action. Link the 2 pages to each other with one line: "Want us to manage after move-in? See full management."

## Which questions should the page answer?

The page should answer the questions owners ask on the first call. Write each answer in 1 to 3 sentences, with a number where you have one.

1. **How much do you charge, and what is included?**
2. **Is there a contract term, and how do I cancel?**
3. **How fast do you place a tenant, on average?**
4. **How do you screen tenants?** (Your written criteria, the same for everyone.)
5. **Who approves repairs, and up to what dollar amount?**
6. **When do I get paid each month?**
7. **Do you work with Section 8 and voucher tenants?**
8. **Which areas do you serve?**

Clear question-and-answer text also helps AI answers, which quote short, direct passages. One change matters here: Google Search Central says the FAQ rich result stopped showing in Google Search on May 7, 2026. Keep the FAQ section for readers. Do not add FAQ markup to chase a search feature. For your business name, address and hours, Google still documents LocalBusiness structured data.

## The services page template

Copy this outline, fill every bracket, and delete any line you cannot back with a fact.

```text
H1: Property Management Services in [City or Area]

Intro (2–3 sentences):
We manage [number] [single-family homes / small multifamily] for owners in [areas].
Our fee is [X]% of collected rent [or $X per door], with no fee while the home is vacant [if true].
Here is what that fee covers, what costs extra, and how to start.

H2: What Our Full Management Covers
- Rent collection: [how and when owners get paid]
- Maintenance: [24/7 line? approval limit $X]
- Inspections: [X per year, with photos]
- Owner reporting: [monthly statement, portal, year-end forms]
- [Other services you deliver]

H2: What the Fee Includes and What Costs Extra
[Fee table: item | included? | extra cost]

H2: How We Place a Tenant
[Steps: pricing, marketing, showings, written screening criteria, lease, move-in]

H2: Leasing-Only Service
[2 sentences + link to the leasing-only page]

H2: Areas We Serve
[List of cities or ZIP codes, each linked to its own city page]

H2: What Owners Say
[2–3 real reviews with name and source, with permission]

H2: Questions Owners Ask
[6–8 questions from the list, 1–3 sentences each]

Call to action:
Get a free rental analysis for your property. [Button]
Or call [phone] and ask for [name].
```

Google's helpful content guidance asks whether a page shows first-hand expertise and gives a complete description of its topic. A filled template with your real numbers does both. A page full of brackets and claims without numbers does neither.

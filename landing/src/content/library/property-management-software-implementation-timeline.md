## What happens in each phase of onboarding?

A workable implementation plan moves through data, accounting, staff practice and a controlled cutover. Treat the schedule below as our planning template, not a vendor guarantee.

| Phase | Main work | Exit test |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | List records, services and people | Written responsibilities approved |
| Cleanup | Fix duplicates and unclear balances | Source records ready |
| Sample import | Map fields and links | Sample reports agree |
| Training | Practise each role's tasks | Staff complete work unaided |
| Cutover | Move approved live work | Payments and access verified |
| First close | Check reports and exceptions | Reviewer approves balances |

Buildium's onboarding page describes data migration and a tailored training plan. Its templates page says the team schedules an import and reconciliation call after receiving completed files. That sequence makes data readiness a real dependency.

Choose a coordinator inside your company. The vendor can import records, but your team must explain unclear contracts, balances and ownership. Write down who approves the result.

## How long does data migration take?

Migration time depends on the scope and quality of your source data. Do not borrow a duration from another company's sales story and treat it as your deadline.

Ask the vendor to review an export before estimating the work. Include properties, units, owners, residents, leases, vendors and open balances. Add documents and historical reports separately so nothing disappears into a vague promise.

Decide how much history must remain searchable in the new system. Some records may need an archive instead of a structured import. Define how staff will access that archive.

Test difficult cases early. Include shared owners, tenant credits and unpaid invoices. Check links between records, not only whether names imported. Keep a mismatch list with an owner for each correction. The sample is ready when a reviewer can trace the result back to source reports without unexplained differences.

## How long does staff training take?

Training is complete when each role can do its required work correctly. A session on the calendar does not establish readiness.

Buildium describes tailored training and self-guided courses. Ask any vendor to connect its training plan with the tasks your team performs. Focus first on collections, bills, statements and maintenance handoffs.

Give staff practice records and a clear expected result. Ask the bookkeeper to reconcile, the leasing person to create a lease and operations to close a work order. Inspect the record after each exercise.

Include exceptions in practice. A returned payment, corrected invoice or wrong property assignment should have a known response. Staff need to know when to stop and ask for help. Save short instructions for those cases. Keep training work separate from live records so practice does not create real charges or messages.

## What slows an implementation down?

Unclear data and changing decisions slow implementation. Software setup cannot resolve facts that nobody owns.

A common planning risk is an unexplained opening balance. Another is a custom report whose purpose exists only in someone's memory. These are risks to check in your process, not measured vendor failure rates.

Use this dependency checklist:

- Confirm the final chart of accounts.
- Assign owners to unclear balances.
- Gather signed agreements and leases.
- Identify custom fields and reports.
- Verify bank and payment setup.
- Decide the archive scope.
- Approve staff and portal permissions.
- Confirm who sends cutover instructions.

Keep the scope stable once the sample passes. New features can wait if they do not affect the safe move. Record any change that alters training, data or payment work. Update the launch date when an exit test remains unmet instead of treating the date as proof of readiness.

## What does implementation cost?

Implementation cost includes vendor charges and your team's work. Separate migration, training and temporary overlap from recurring subscriptions.

Propertyware's pricing page lists an implementation fee of twice the monthly subscription. Rentvine describes a one-time setup fee without publishing its amount on the page reviewed. Those offers need different calculations, not a shared assumed setup price.

Ask each vendor to price the same scope. State the history, documents, bank accounts, custom reports and training needed. Request exclusions in writing. A low setup fee means little if staff must rebuild essential records afterward.

Estimate internal work from assigned tasks and your own costs. Label it as a planning estimate. Include time for export checks, balance approval, staff practice and resident support during cutover. Keep a separate allowance for unresolved exceptions rather than hiding them inside a vague contingency number.

## How should you set the go-live date?

Set the date after the sample import and readiness checks establish a workable path. Keep a fallback route if a required check fails.

Confirm opening balances before moving collections. Test automatic payment settings and clarify whether residents need new setup. Explain the old and new payment routes without conflicting instructions.

Check owner and resident permissions before sending invitations. Save approved old reports and confirm archive access. Keep support contacts and escalation responsibilities visible to staff.

Review the first close against the approved sample. Resolve unexpected balances before adding more automation.

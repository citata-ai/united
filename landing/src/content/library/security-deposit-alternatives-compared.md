## How does each product work?

Product structure determines the protection offered and what residents may still owe. Do not call every option deposit insurance.

Rhino's published guide describes a bond and required claim records. Request a current contract before relying on that historical description. Verify the provider, address and coverage period.

LeaseLock describes Zero Deposit as lease insurance and states that it does not use subrogation, meaning insurer recovery from residents. Read the actual policy and confirm exclusions.

Obligo's tenant terms describe billing authorization and several plans. Some can involve a deposit as well as fees. Its terms make residents responsible for permitted charges within the agreed authorization.

Ask each provider to explain an ordinary move-out and a disputed charge. Keep the answer tied to the current signed form rather than a broad marketing statement. Request a plain explanation that your staff can repeat accurately.

## Who pays and how much?

Request a complete quote for the exact program. The reviewed materials do not establish comparable current prices.

List resident fees, any refundable deposit, owner costs and recurring payments. Ask whether amounts change at renewal. Keep required and optional charges separate.

Obligo defines service fees as nonrefundable. That does not mean every payment under every plan is a nonrefundable fee. Its deposit-backed options need a separate explanation.

Do not compare recurring fees with cash deposits as if both are permanently spent. A deposit and a service fee have different purposes and outcomes. Explain what may return to the resident and what cannot.

Build a bill preview from proposed enrollment and lease documents. Ask the provider to confirm each line. Include cancellation and renewal scenarios so staff can explain changes without guessing.

## What happens when you file a claim?

Claims require evidence and follow the current contract. A coverage amount is not a promise to pay every submitted charge.

Rhino's historical guide requests a signed lease, full ledger and damage records. Use that as a documentation prompt, not as a current deadline source.

Ask each provider for filing steps, review rules and an appeal route. Check normal wear, unpaid rent, disputed charges and later resident payments. Do not assume identical coverage.

Use this claim-readiness checklist:

- Current policy or authorization.
- Signed lease and approved changes.
- Full charge and payment history.
- Move-in and move-out records.
- Dated photographs and invoices.
- Required resident notices.
- Submission receipt and status.
- Later payments and corrections.

Assign a person to track deadlines. Keep the current contract and legal requirements beside the task record. A reminder in the PMS cannot establish the correct filing rule by itself.

## Which products work with your PMS?

Require confirmation of the exact PMS, plan and workflow. An invitation link may not handle charges, renewals or claims.

Ask for enrollment from sample lease data. Change a move-out date and inspect the result. Check where fees appear in ledgers and owner reports.

Test a failed update. Staff should see the error and know who resolves it. Name the final source for resident, coverage and charge information.

Ask what happens when a property changes managers or leaves the program. Confirm access to an open claim and supporting documents. Do not assume an export includes every attachment.

Write down manual steps and assign them. A connection helps only when staff understand its limits. Keep a task open until the destination record and required evidence are correct. Do not count a directory listing as a complete test.

## Do state laws allow deposit alternatives?

Legal treatment depends on product and location. A national vendor page does not settle a property's requirements.

Florida section 83.491 permits a landlord fee-in-lieu option with written terms and disclosures. It includes a tenant option to pay a deposit instead. Paying the fee does not remove lease obligations.

The section also addresses claims, notices and duplicate recovery. Ask local counsel which provisions apply to the proposed arrangement. Do not assume every bond or insurance product follows the same subsection.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney. Preserve approved wording and current forms with property records. Review changes before editing advertisements, leases or billing. A product name alone cannot tell staff which legal procedure to follow after move-out.

## How should a landlord decide?

Choose after you can explain cost, liability and the claim route clearly. Keep a cash-deposit option in the comparison where applicable.

Test the contract against a disputed move-out, failed payment and renewal. Ask who handles each exception. Record remaining work and its owner.

Do not promise easier leasing or reduced losses without evidence from your own operation. Treat expected gains as hypotheses. Review complaints and corrections alongside enrollment.

Check the first completed move-out against the original service explanation. Fix unclear records and staff instructions before expanding the program. Keep current contracts accessible to authorized staff.

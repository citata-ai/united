## What should a maintenance request policy cover?

A maintenance request policy covers 5 topics: emergencies, how to submit, response times, tenant duties and entry. Tenants read it once at move-in and again when something breaks. So write it short, in plain words, with phone numbers at the top.

The policy does 3 jobs for you:

- **It sets expectations.** The tenant knows what "fast" means before the sink leaks.
- **It creates a record.** Dated requests show when you got notice, which matters under repair laws.
- **It sorts the work.** Emergencies go to the on-call line, and routine items go to the queue.

## What counts as an emergency repair?

An emergency repair is a problem that puts people at risk or will cause serious damage within hours. Oregon law uses a similar test for emergency entry: a repair problem "likely to cause serious damage to the premises" if not fixed now (ORS 90.322).

Your state's habitability list is a good base for the emergency list. California Civil Code 1941.1 lists the basics a rental must have, including weatherproofing, working plumbing and gas, hot and cold running water, heat, safe electrical systems and working locks. Florida Statutes 83.51 requires functioning heat in winter, running water and hot water, among other duties.

| Priority | Examples | Target response |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency | Fire, gas smell, flooding, sewage backup, no heat in freezing weather, no water, exterior door will not lock, live electrical hazard | On-site or made safe within hours, 24/7 |
| Urgent | No hot water, refrigerator or stove down, only toilet not working, AC down in extreme heat, active roof leak | Within 24 to 48 hours |
| Routine | Dripping faucet, slow drain, loose cabinet door, one burner out | Within 3 to 7 business days |
| Cosmetic | Paint touch-up, minor scuffs | Next turnover or scheduled visit |

The response times in this table are our suggested targets, not legal rules. Set times you can meet with your vendors.

## How should tenants submit requests?

Tenants should submit non-emergency requests in writing, through one channel. One channel means nothing gets lost in a personal text thread.

1. **Use the portal or email** for all routine and urgent requests.
2. **Add photos or a short video** of the problem.
3. **Give a time window** when you may enter, and say if a pet is in the unit.
4. **Call the emergency line** for emergencies, then submit a written request too.
5. **Get a ticket number** and keep it.

Watch for: repair-and-deduct rules start from notice. In California, a tenant may repair a habitability problem and deduct the cost after giving notice and waiting a reasonable time. The law presumes 30 days is reasonable, caps the cost at one month's rent, and allows it twice in 12 months (Cal. Civ. Code 1942). A logged request date protects both sides.

## Which repairs are the tenant's job?

The tenant's job is to keep the unit clean, use systems with care and pay for damage they cause. Florida Statutes 83.52 lists tenant duties, for example:

- Keep the part of the premises they use clean and sanitary.
- Remove garbage in a clean and sanitary way.
- Keep plumbing fixtures clean and sanitary.
- Use electrical, plumbing, heating and air systems in a reasonable way.
- Not damage or remove the landlord's property, and not let guests do so.

Florida 83.51 also says the landlord is not responsible for conditions caused by the tenant, family members or guests. Your lease can assign small tasks to the tenant, such as light bulbs, smoke alarm batteries or HVAC filters. Name them in the policy so there is no surprise.

## When can the manager enter to repair?

The manager can enter to repair after giving the notice your state requires, or at any time in an emergency. Here are 3 examples.

- **California:** written notice, with 24 hours presumed reasonable; the notice states date, approximate time and purpose (Cal. Civ. Code 1954).
- **Florida:** at least 24 hours' notice for repairs, between 7:30 a.m. and 8:00 p.m. (Fla. Stat. 83.53).
- **Oregon:** a tenant's written repair request lets the landlord enter for that repair without more notice; the permission ends after 7 days unless work is in progress (ORS 90.322).

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Maintenance request policy template

Copy this text, fill in the brackets, and check it against your state and city law before use.

> **Maintenance Request Policy**
>
> Property: [address]. Manager: [company name].
> Emergency line (24/7): [phone]. Portal: [link]. Email: [address].
>
> **1. Emergencies.** Call [phone] right away for: fire (call 911 first), gas smell (leave and call the gas company first), flooding or a burst pipe, sewage backup, no heat when it is below [temperature], no water, an exterior door or window that will not lock, or a live electrical hazard. After the call, also submit a written request.
>
> **2. All other requests.** Submit through [portal or email]. Describe the problem, add a photo, and give times we may enter. Tell us about pets in the unit.
>
> **3. Our response times.** Emergency: we respond within [X] hours and make the problem safe. Urgent: within [24–48] hours. Routine: within [3–7] business days. We will tell you if parts or a vendor delay the work.
>
> **4. Your part.** Keep the unit clean, use appliances and systems with care, and report leaks, mold or pests as soon as you see them. You must pay for damage caused by you, your household or your guests. You handle: [light bulbs / smoke alarm batteries / HVAC filters every X months].
>
> **5. Entry for repairs.** We will give you [24 hours / state notice] before we enter, unless it is an emergency. A written request from you gives us permission to enter for that repair at the times you list.
>
> **6. What not to do.** Do not hire your own vendor or change locks without our written OK, unless state law allows it.
>
> **7. After-hours non-emergencies.** Requests sent after [time] or on weekends go into the queue for the next business day.
>
> Tenant signature: ________ Date: ________

## How do you show response times to owners?

Show owners your average time to first response and time to close, by priority, each month. The same log that protects you with tenants proves your service to owners.

Worked example: in 1 month you get 40 requests: 2 emergencies, 8 urgent, 30 routine. Your log shows the 2 emergencies made safe in 3 and 5 hours, urgent items closed in 1.5 days on average, and routine items in 4.2 days. Put those 3 numbers at the top of the owner statement.

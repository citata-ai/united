## What percent of revenue do property management companies spend on marketing?

Property management marketing budgets usually sit at 7 to 10% of management revenue, in the advice we found. Property Manager Websites (PMW), an agency for property managers, gives these ranges:

| Situation | Share of management revenue |
|---|---|
| Steady growth | 7 to 10% |
| Small firm, first year of marketing | 7 to 10%, start with SEO and the website |
| Fast growth or a new market | 10 to 15% |

Watch for: these are an agency's ranges, not a survey of property managers. We found no public survey with a clean number for our industry. Use the range as a first check, then test it against your door goal below.

To use the range, you need your revenue per door. Here is one example. The U.S. Census Bureau puts the national median gross rent at $1,413 a month (QuickFacts, 2020–2024). All Property Management says a typical residential fee is 8 to 12% of monthly rent. At 10%, one door brings $141.30 a month, or about $1,696 a year in management fees.

Your numbers will differ. Put your own fee and rents into the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) first.

## What does a starter budget look like for 100 doors?

A 100-door company at $1,696 per door earns about $169,560 a year in management fees. Here is the budget at each PMW range.

| Share | Per year | Per month |
|---|---|---|
| 7% | $11,869 | $989 |
| 10% | $16,956 | $1,413 |
| 15% | $25,434 | $2,120 |

So a 100-door company that wants steady growth has about $1,000 to $1,400 a month to spend. That money must cover software, ads, content and review tools.

## How do I set a budget from a door growth goal?

A door goal gives a better budget than a flat percent, because it ties spend to results. You need 4 numbers:

1. **Door goal.** How many new doors you want this year.
2. **Doors per owner.** Total doors divided by total owners. PMW's worksheet uses 1.2 as its example.
3. **Close rate.** Signed owners divided by owner leads. Take it from your CRM.
4. **Most you can pay per owner.** PMW's worksheet sets a target of 10 to 15% of an owner's lifetime value.

Worked example, with 30 new doors as the goal:

| Step | Math | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Owners to sign | 30 doors ÷ 1.2 doors per owner | 25 owners |
| Leads needed | 25 owners ÷ 20% close rate | 125 leads a year, about 10 a month |
| Value of one owner | $1,696 × 1.2 doors × 4 years | $8,139 |
| Cost cap per owner | 10% of $8,139 | $814 |
| Yearly budget ceiling | 25 owners × $814 | $20,350 a year, about $1,696 a month |

The 20% close rate and 4-year tenure are example inputs, not benchmarks. Use your own. If your close rate is 10%, you need 250 leads, not 125. That one number can double the budget you need.

Compare the two answers. The percent rule said $989 to $1,413 a month. The door goal says up to $1,696 a month. When the door goal costs more than the percent rule allows, lower the goal or raise your close rate first.

## How should I split budget between SEO, ads and referrals?

PMW suggests this split of the marketing budget:

| Channel | Share | At $1,200 a month |
|---|---|---|
| SEO and content | 25 to 35% | $300 to $420 |
| Paid ads (PPC, pay per click) | 20 to 30% | $240 to $360 |
| CRM, automation and follow-up | 10 to 15% | $120 to $180 |
| Reviews and reputation | 5 to 10% | $60 to $120 |
| Website and tests | the rest | $120 to $480 |

Check the split against where owner leads really come from. The Goodjuju 2026 lead source report tracked 35 companies and 2,525 owner leads. In fall 2026, Google search and the Map Pack brought 50.6% of leads, and direct or Maps visits brought 35.6%. Referral sites brought 3.7%, paid search 1.2% and AI tools 3.4%.

Watch for: only 6 of the 35 companies in that study ran paid search. All of them work with an SEO agency. So the study says little about what ads can do, and it may overstate SEO.

Referrals from agents and past clients often cost little cash. They cost time instead. Put a few hours a month on the plan, even if the dollar line is small.

## How much should I spend to test Google Ads?

A Google Ads test needs enough budget to bring enough leads to judge. PMW's worksheet says its clients see $250 to $350 per qualified PPC lead, with campaigns from $500 to more than $10,000 a month.

At $250 a lead, $1,000 a month buys about 4 leads. That is too few to judge a close rate. $2,500 a month buys about 10 leads.

Google Ads can spend up to twice your daily budget on one day. Over a month, it charges no more than 30.4 times the daily budget (Google Ads Help). So a $50 daily budget means up to about $1,520 a month.

## How often should I review the marketing budget?

Review the budget each month against signed doors, not traffic. PMW warns against vanity numbers such as impressions and raw visits. Track 4 numbers per channel:

- [ ] Spend
- [ ] Owner leads (forms and calls, not tenant leads)
- [ ] Signed owners and doors
- [ ] Cost per signed door

Move money each quarter toward the channel with the lowest cost per signed door. Keep SEO funded through slow months, because it takes time to pay back.

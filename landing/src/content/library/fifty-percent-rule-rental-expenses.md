## What is the 50% rule?

The 50% rule is a rule of thumb: about half of a rental's gross rent goes to operating costs. The other half pays the mortgage and, if anything is left, gives you cash flow. It is not an IRS rule or a lender rule.

The math in 3 lines:

```text
Gross yearly rent × 50% = estimated operating costs
Gross yearly rent − operating costs = estimated NOI
NOI − yearly mortgage payments = estimated cash flow
```

Worked example, with example numbers and a rate from Freddie Mac's survey (7.40% average 30-year fixed on 2026-10-15):

| Line | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Rent | $2,000 a month, $24,000 a year |
| 50% for operating costs | $12,000 |
| Estimated NOI | $12,000 |
| Mortgage: $180,000 at 7.40%, 30 years | $14,955 a year |
| Estimated cash flow | −$2,955 a year |

The screen says no at this price and rate. That is its job: to save you a full analysis on a deal that cannot work.

## Which expenses does it include?

The 50% includes every operating cost of the rental, but not the mortgage. Use this list to check what you are counting.

| Included in the 50% | Not included |
|---------------------|--------------|
| Property tax | Mortgage principal |
| Insurance | Mortgage interest |
| Repairs and maintenance | Your income tax |
| Capital reserve (roof, HVAC, appliances) | Depreciation (a tax deduction, not cash) |
| Management and leasing fees | Purchase closing costs |
| Vacancy and unpaid rent | Large upgrades you choose to make |
| Owner-paid utilities | |
| HOA dues | |
| Legal, accounting, advertising | |

Benchmarks for some lines:

- **Management:** iPropertyManagement's 2026 survey found an average of 8.49% of monthly rent.
- **Vacancy:** the Census Bureau reported a national rental vacancy rate of 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026.
- **Maintenance:** Baselane reports maintenance spending equaled 8.0% of rent receipts at the median rental property in 2023.

These 3 lines alone add up to about 24% of rent. Tax, insurance and reserves often take much of the rest.

## How do real expense ratios compare?

Real expense ratios range from about a third of rent to well over half. Baselane's 2026 roundup of studies shows the spread. Each source measures a different set of properties, so compare with care.

| Source (as reported by Baselane) | Property type | Expense measure |
|----------------------------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Novogradac | Market-rate properties | Typical 35% to 45% of rental income |
| Novogradac 2024 LIHTC dataset | Affordable housing (tax credit) | 61.8% of rental income, up from 53.8% in 2016 |
| NAA analysis of 2024 data | Apartments | 27 cents of each rent dollar to operating costs, 10 to property tax, 7 to payroll, 1 to capital and reserves: 45 cents in total |
| Census Rental Housing Finance Survey (2023) | One-unit rentals | $8,670 mean total operating expenses per unit |
| RapidEye analysis of the same survey | All rentals | $4,328 median operating expenses per unit, excluding property tax |

What the $8,670 means for a house: at $2,000 a month in rent, $8,670 is 36% of $24,000. At $1,200 a month, it is 60% of $14,400. The same dollar costs make a very different ratio on a low-rent home.

Baselane also reports that the same survey found 82.9% of rental properties have one unit. So the one-unit figure is the closest match for most individual owners.

## When is the rule wrong for my house?

The 50% rule is wrong when your house has costs far from the average. That is often. Here are the common cases.

| Your house | Ratio likely | Why |
|------------|--------------|-----|
| New build, under warranty | Below 50% | Low repairs for years |
| 1950s house with original systems | Above 50% | Repairs and capital items |
| High-tax county | Above 50% | Tax alone can take a large share of rent |
| Coastal or storm area | Above 50% | Insurance cost |
| Condo with high HOA dues | Above 50% | Dues cover items you would pay anyway, but in one large line |
| Low-rent home | Above 50% | Fixed costs are a larger share of a small rent |
| Tenant pays all utilities | Lower | Removes a line |
| You self-manage | Lower in cash | But your time has a cost |

Watch for: the rule hides the line that hurts. A deal can pass at 50% while a $6,000 tax bill and a 30-year-old roof wait inside it.

## How to replace the 50% with real numbers

Use the rule to screen. Then replace it with a line-by-line list before you make an offer.

1. **Get the tax bill** at your purchase price from the county assessor.
2. **Get a landlord insurance quote.**
3. **Read the HOA budget** and dues, if any.
4. **Price management** with your manager's fee schedule. Our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) shows the yearly cost.
5. **Set maintenance** at a share of rent, adjusted for age.
6. **Set a capital reserve** item by item: roof, HVAC, water heater, flooring.
7. **Set vacancy** with local data.
8. **Add the rest:** utilities you pay, legal, accounting, advertising.
9. **Divide the total by gross rent.** That is your real ratio.

Then compare. If your real ratio is 42%, the 50% rule was too harsh. If it is 58%, the rule was too kind, and the deal is weaker than it looked.

At tax time, IRS Publication 527 lists the common deductible rental expenses, from advertising and cleaning to taxes and utilities. Track each line in your books, so next year's ratio uses your own data.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

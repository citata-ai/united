## What Should Happen on the First Day?

The first day should make the role, support and safety routes clear before the employee handles live accounts. Give the person a usable plan and a named trainer.

Buildium's onboarding guide recommends clear performance standards, policies, team introductions and gradual induction. Its article is a practitioner's account, not a legal or national training standard. The plan below is our suggested approach for a property management team.

Explain who the company serves and what the employee owns. Show the service scope, contact routes and approval limits. Introduce the broker, accounting lead and maintenance lead where relevant. Make it clear where the new hire can ask questions without guessing.

Set up the access the role needs. Use individual accounts and the company's approved security process. Avoid shared passwords or access to unrelated owner and resident files. Show how to report suspicious messages or payment requests.

Give the employee the written schedule and coverage expectations. Explain breaks, leave, after-hours duties and backup. Complete employment and policy steps with qualified support. Do not replace onboarding with an instruction to watch whoever is free.

## Which Software Training Comes First?

Train systems that protect records and guide daily work before advanced features. The employee should learn the approved process before learning shortcuts.

Start with finding the correct owner, property, resident and agreement. Show how to confirm identity before changing a record. Practice adding a factual note and locating the next action. Explain which records need approval before a change.

Use a training environment or approved sample records where possible. If live data is needed, limit access and supervise the task. Do not use real resident details in an outside training tool without approved handling.

This schedule is a proposed plan, not a research-based deadline. Adjust it for the role and demonstrated skills.

| Proposed stage | Main work | Evidence before moving on |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–30 | Systems, policies and observed tasks | Correct records and escalation |
| Days 31–60 | Limited work under review | Accurate completion and clear notes |
| Days 61–90 | Broader tasks with targeted checks | Sound decisions within authority |
| After day 90 | Continued coaching and review | Stable quality across real cases |

Train payment and bank-change controls separately from ordinary data entry. A software lesson does not authorize a person to move money. Have the responsible lead approve those duties after specific training.

## When Can the New Hire Talk to Owners?

Let the new hire speak with owners when the task, authority and support are clear. Supervised conversation can come before independent responsibility for an account.

Begin with listening to approved calls or joining a planned introduction. Explain what the owner knows and what the company promised. Ask the new hire to write a draft update, then review it for accuracy and scope.

Use role practice for difficult situations. Include a delayed repair, an unclear statement and a request outside authority. Check whether the employee recognizes the limit and seeks help. A confident answer is not useful if it is wrong.

Tell owners who handles their account and who provides backup. Do not silently transfer responsibility to a person still in training. Give the employee a way to obtain help during a call and a process for later correction.

Avoid scripts that hide uncertainty. A useful response can say the employee will confirm the ledger and return with a verified answer. Review the promised follow-up afterward. Reliable next steps matter more than memorized language.

## How Do You Check Progress at the End of the Plan?

Check completed work, judgment and escalation rather than attendance alone. Use the same role standards you explained at the start.

Review actual tasks with the employee. Ask them to show the source record, decision and next action. Include work that went well and work that needed correction. Discuss why the correction occurred and whether the employee can now handle the case.

Separate missing knowledge from unclear process. If experienced staff give conflicting instructions, fix the instructions before blaming the new hire. If access prevents correct work, adjust the system with the right approval.

Use a written review with practical outcomes. Record which duties the person may perform alone, which need review and what training comes next. Give each remaining item a trainer and a clear check.

Ask what work differs from the job description. A mismatch may show a hiring or staffing problem. Review workload and support, especially if the new hire has become the default person for unfinished tasks. Keep training active after the initial plan ends.

## What Should the Onboarding Checklist Contain?

Use a checklist that records evidence and approval, not only whether someone watched a lesson. Keep it short enough for trainers to maintain.

- Confirm role duties, reporting lines and the trainer.
- Set up approved access and explain security reporting.
- Review service scope and fair housing escalation routes.
- Demonstrate records, work orders and owner updates.
- Practice common tasks under supervision.
- Approve spending, signing and payment duties separately.
- Record demonstrated skills and remaining training needs.

Check local licensing and employment requirements before assigning restricted duties. General training does not give an unlicensed employee authority to perform every task. The broker and local advisers should review the actual role.

Ask the trainer to keep short examples of correct work. These give the employee a clear reference when a new situation resembles a previous task. Remove personal details from shared training examples.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney. Use our [property management guides](/guides) for related workflows. Before the next hire starts, assign a trainer and prepare the first supervised task.

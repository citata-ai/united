## Which areas does LA County DCBA rent stabilization cover?

LA County rent stabilization covers rentals in unincorporated areas of Los Angeles County, the places outside any city. The Department of Consumer and Business Affairs (DCBA) runs the program. The law is the Rent Stabilization and Tenant Protections Ordinance (RSTPO), Chapter 8.52 of the County Code, in effect since April 1, 2020.

A city inside the county, such as the City of Los Angeles, has its own rules instead. So the first step is to check whether your address is in a city or in an unincorporated area.

The RSTPO sorts units into two groups, per DCBA:

| Group | Which units | What applies |
|---|---|---|
| Fully covered | 2 or more rental units on one property (regardless of permit status), with a certificate of occupancy on or before 1995-02-01, plus accessory dwelling units on such properties; also mobilehomes rented by the mobilehome owner | Rent cap and just cause eviction protection |
| Partially covered | Other rental units, such as newer buildings and single units | Just cause eviction protection only |

DCBA's registry FAQ gives examples of fully covered units: apartment complexes and duplexes in unincorporated areas with an old enough certificate of occupancy.

## What is the yearly allowed rent increase in unincorporated LA County?

The yearly allowed increase in unincorporated LA County is 1.919% from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027 for general fully covered units. Since January 1, 2025, the formula is 60% of the change in average CPI over the 12 months ending in September, with a 3% ceiling.

| Period | General | Eligible small property landlord | Luxury unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-07-01 to 2027-06-30 | 1.919% | 2.919% | 3.919% |
| 2025-07-01 to 2026-06-30 | 1.930% | 2.930% | 3.930% |
| 2025-01-01 to 2025-06-30 | 2.565% | 3.565% | 4.565% |
| Ceiling under the formula | 3% | 4% | 5% |

Source: DCBA Rent Stabilization Bulletin, revised 2026-03-02.

DCBA lists three ways to be a small property landlord:

- You get a Homeowners' Property Tax Exemption on the property.
- You own or control one rental property with no more than 10 units.
- You own or control no more than 3 rental properties with 10 units or fewer in total, counting property outside the county.

Small property landlords must self-certify to DCBA.

A luxury unit has 2 bedrooms or fewer and sits in a structure with 25 or more units. It also rented for at least $4,000 a month as of September 11, 2018.

A worked example: a fully covered 2-bedroom renting at $2,964, the HUD FY2027 fair market rent for the Los Angeles area, may rise by 1.919%, or $56.88 a month. A small property landlord may charge 2.919%, or $86.52. See the [Los Angeles fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/los-angeles-fair-market-rent-2027) for other sizes.

Before any increase, DCBA lists these conditions:

1. **Notice:** at least 30 days' written notice under California Civil Code section 827.
2. **Timing:** at least 12 consecutive months since the last increase, with exceptions.
3. **Registration:** each fully covered unit registered with DCBA and fees paid, and the property in compliance with the law.
4. **Tenant rights notice:** tenants have received the Notice of Tenant Rights.
5. **Status in the notice:** if you claim small landlord or luxury status, say so in the increase notice.

## Must owners register rentals with LA County?

Yes. Owners in unincorporated LA County must register every rental unit and mobilehome space with DCBA each year. The DCBA 2024-25 registry FAQ sets the deadline at September 30.

| Unit type | Yearly registry fee |
|---|---|
| Fully covered rental unit | $90 |
| Partially covered rental unit (just cause only) | $30 |
| Mobilehome space | $90 |

Up to 50% of the fee for a fully covered unit or a mobilehome space may pass to the tenant. The tenant pays it in 12 equal monthly installments, unless agreed otherwise. Penalties and late fees may not pass to tenants.

A worked example: a 6-unit fully covered building pays 6 x $90 = $540 a year. You may pass up to $270 to tenants, or $45 per unit, which is $3.75 a month per unit.

The cost of skipping it is higher. The bulletin says a landlord may increase rent only after the unit is registered and fees are current. A missed registration can cost you the whole year's increase.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How does the county ordinance differ from the City of LA RSO?

The county RSTPO and the City of LA Rent Stabilization Ordinance (RSO) are separate laws for separate places. If your rental is inside the City of Los Angeles, the RSO applies, not the county law.

| Item | LA County RSTPO (unincorporated) | City of LA RSO |
|---|---|---|
| Agency | DCBA | Los Angeles Housing Department (LAHD) |
| Building date | Certificate of occupancy on or before 1995-02-01 | Generally first built on or before 1978-10-01 |
| Building size | 2 or more units on the property | Per LAHD coverage rules |
| Increase | 60% of CPI, 3% ceiling (1.919% in 2026-27) | LAHD's yearly allowable rent increase |
| Frequency | Once every 12 months | Once every 12 months |

LAHD also made two changes on February 2, 2026. Landlords can no longer add an extra percentage for utilities. The extra 10% for an added dependent is gone too.

Checklist for an owner in unincorporated LA County:

- [ ] Confirm the address is outside city limits.
- [ ] Find the certificate of occupancy date and count the units on the property.
- [ ] Register every unit and pay by September 30.
- [ ] Check small property landlord status and self-certify if it applies.
- [ ] Give the Notice of Tenant Rights.
- [ ] Send 30 days' written notice before each yearly increase.

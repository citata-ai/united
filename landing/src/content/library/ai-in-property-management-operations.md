## How many property management companies use AI today?

About one in three property management companies uses AI today. AppFolio's 2025 Property Management Benchmark Report surveyed more than 2,000 professionals. The National Apartment Association summary of that report gives these numbers:

| Measure | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Use AI now | 21% | 34% |
| Plan to adopt or use AI tools | 17% | 28% |
| No plans to use AI | 51% | 37% |

The same summary says AI users expect more growth. 83% of current AI users expect revenue to go up, compared with 71% of those with no plans to use AI. That is a link, not proof that AI causes growth. Companies that buy new tools may also be the ones that grow for other reasons.

AI also ships inside the software many managers already pay for. ## Which property management tasks can AI do today?

AI does well on tasks with many similar inputs, a clear format and a person who checks the output. It does badly on tasks that need judgment about people, money owed or the law. Here is our task map for a residential property management company:

| Task | AI role today | Person's role | Risk level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sort the shared inbox (leasing, maintenance, accounting) | Tag and route | Spot-check tags weekly | Low |
| Leasing inquiry replies (price, availability, showing times) | Draft or send from approved answers | Own the answer bank, review edge cases | Medium |
| Tour scheduling | Book from the calendar | Handle no-shows and changes | Low |
| Maintenance request intake | Ask for photos, sort by urgency, route to a vendor | Confirm emergencies, approve spend | Medium |
| Owner statement notes and monthly summaries | Draft plain-language summary | Check every number against the ledger | Medium |
| Rent reminders | Send on schedule | Set rules, handle disputes | Low |
| Lease and notice drafting | Fill a template | Attorney-approved template, person signs off | High |
| Applicant screening decision | Summarize against written criteria | Decide and send required notices | High |
| Collections and eviction steps | None beyond reminders | All decisions | High |

The pattern: let AI do the first 80% of the typing. Keep the decision with a person.

## Can AI handle leasing inquiries?

AI can answer most routine leasing inquiries if it answers only from a set of approved facts. Price, deposit, availability date, pet rules, parking and showing times fit this model. Build an answer bank first. Then let the tool draft or send replies only from that bank.

Watch the questions that look simple but are not:

- **"Is this a good area for kids?"** An answer about families or schools can steer by familial status.
- **"Do you allow service animals?"** That is a reasonable accommodation question. Route it to a trained person.
- **"Who lives in the building?"** Never describe current residents.

The Fair Housing Act, at 42 U.S.C. § 3604(c), bans any notice, statement or advertisement that shows a preference based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status or national origin. HUD's rule at 24 CFR 100.75 says this applies to written and oral statements by anyone in the rental business. A chatbot reply is a written statement from your company. Rentvine makes the same point in plain terms: add human review checkpoints before AI-generated messages go out.

## Can AI triage maintenance requests?

Yes. Maintenance intake is one of the best fits for AI. The tool can ask for photos, ask follow-up questions, rank the request by urgency and suggest a vendor. Rentvine suggests using AI to rank requests by urgency or frequency so the team can set priorities.

Set hard rules that the tool cannot override:

1. **Gas smell, fire, flooding, no heat in winter, sewage backup:** the tool tells the tenant to call the emergency line and pages a person at once.
2. **Spend approval:** the tool never approves work above the owner's repair limit in the management agreement.
3. **Habitability items:** a person reviews any request that could become a code or habitability issue.

Then review the tool's urgency tags each week for the first month. Count how many emergencies it tagged as routine. That number should be zero.

## What are the Fair Housing and data risks?

The two main risks are discrimination claims and data leaks. Neither risk moves to the vendor when you buy a tool.

**Fair Housing.** 42 U.S.C. § 3604 makes it unlawful to refuse to rent, or to set different terms, because of a protected class. The statute does not care if a person or a model made the call. An AI screening score trained on biased data can produce a pattern of denials. Rentvine's advice is to keep a person on the final decision and audit outcomes for patterns.

**Credit reports.** When you deny or set worse terms based in whole or in part on a consumer report, 15 U.S.C. § 1681m requires an adverse action notice. The notice names the reporting agency and tells the applicant of their rights. An AI tool that reads a screening report does not remove that duty.

**Data.** Owner bank details, tenant Social Security numbers and screening reports are sensitive. Rentvine notes that human error, such as sending files to the wrong address, causes more breaches than AI does. Still, follow these rules:

- [ ] Use AI inside your property management software, or on a business plan with a written data agreement
- [ ] Never paste full screening reports or ID numbers into a consumer chatbot
- [ ] Limit AI tool access by role, the same as your accounting access
- [ ] Keep a log of which tools touch tenant data

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I measure time saved?

Measure time saved with a before-and-after log, not with the vendor's estimate. A simple method:

1. **Pick one task.** For example, maintenance intake.
2. **Log for two weeks before.** Each person notes minutes per request.
3. **Turn on the tool.** Give the team one week to learn it.
4. **Log for two weeks after.** Include minutes spent fixing AI mistakes.
5. **Compare minutes per unit of work.**

A worked example with made-up numbers: your team handles 240 requests a month at 9 minutes each. That is 36 hours. After AI intake, each request takes 4 minutes, plus 2 minutes of review on 1 in 4 requests. That is 960 + 120 = 1,080 minutes, or 18 hours. You save 18 hours a month on that one task. If your real log shows less, trust the log.

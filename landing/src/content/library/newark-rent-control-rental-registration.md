## Which Newark rentals fall under rent control?

Most Newark rentals fall under rent control. Chapter 19:2 of the Newark City Code defines a dwelling as any building or structure rented or offered for rent to one or more tenants, whether occupied or vacant. The chapter applies unless an exemption fits.

| Exempt from Newark rent control | Chapter 19:2 |
|---|---|
| Public housing | Exemption (a) |
| Hotel or motel space rented day to day to transients | Exemption (b) |
| Space rented for commercial use | Exemption (c) |
| Newly constructed multiple dwellings | 19:2-18.1: for the mortgage amortization period or 30 years after completion, whichever is less |
| Vacant dwellings rebuilt at large cost | 19:2-18.2: vacant at least 18 months, with Board approval; exempt for 5 years |
| Units in federal or state rental rehab programs with Section 8 subsidies | Exempt during the program; rents may not exceed HUD fair market rent |
| Units under a contract where a government agency regulates rent | Exempt during the contract period |

The new construction exemption is not automatic. Before any tenant moves in, the landlord must apply to the Rent Control Board for a certification, with a copy of the Certificate of Occupancy.

What to do: if your building is new, file for the exemption before you sign the first lease. If it is older, assume rent control applies.

## What yearly increase does Newark allow?

Newark allows a yearly increase equal to the CPI change, with a hard ceiling of 4%. Section 19:2-3.1 sets the rule at the end of a lease or of a periodic tenancy.

How to compute the cap, step by step:

1. **Pick the index:** CPI-U for the New York-northern New Jersey-Long Island area, from the Bureau of Labor Statistics.
2. **Find two months:** the month 15 months before the increase, and the month 3 months before.
3. **Compute the change** between those two index values.
4. **Apply the ceiling:** if the change is above 4%, use 4%.
5. **Check limits:** no more than that percentage in any 12 months, even if tenants or owners change.

A worked example: a 2-bedroom rents for $2,233, the HUD FY2027 fair market rent for the Newark area. If the CPI change is 3%, the most you can add is $66.99. If the CPI change is 5%, the 4% ceiling applies, so the most is $89.32. See the [Newark fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/newark-fair-market-rent-2027) for other sizes.

Before you raise rent, three conditions must hold:

- **Registration:** you meet the registration rules in section 19:2-9.8.
- **Compliance:** the dwelling is in substantial compliance with codes.
- **Notice:** you give the Rent Regulation Officer and the tenant written notice at least 30 days ahead.

The Board can grant more through hardship increases, major new improvement surcharges, and tax or utility surcharges. Even then, the Board will not grant increases over 25% in any one year for any tenant.

## How does the Newark Office of Rent Control register units?

Newark registers rental buildings under section 19:2-9.8 of the city code. Registration is the key that unlocks every increase: the code bars annual increases, surcharges and hardship increases while registration is not in order. Chapter 19:2 sets the fees.

| Registration item (Chapter 19:2 fees) | Amount |
|---|---|
| Annual registration | $50 plus $10 per dwelling unit |
| Late registration, per year late | $100 plus $20 per unit |
| Late fee if not registered 90 days before an increase application | $100 (waived if registered within 90 days after taking title) |
| Inspection | $50 per unit; reinspection $50 |
| Failure to renew every three years or upon change of tenant | $100 per unit until updated |

There is no inspection fee for owner-occupied units.

A worked example: a 6-unit building pays $50 + 6 x $10 = $110 a year to register. Two years late, it owes the regular fees plus 2 x ($100 + 6 x $20) = $440 in late fees.

The city also inspects each rental unit at least once every three years or on a change in occupancy. Inspections check zoning, health, property maintenance, fire safety and, for pre-1978 dwellings, lead-based paint. New Jersey's statewide lead law also requires a lead inspection at turnover or every three years for most pre-1978 rentals, per NJ DCA.

Penalties: the Rent Control Board can impose up to $500 per violation after a hearing. In Municipal Court, a violation can bring a fine of $100 to $2,000, up to 90 days in jail, or both, counted per housing space.

## What does the New Jersey Anti-Eviction Act require?

The New Jersey Anti-Eviction Act requires good cause to evict or to refuse to renew a lease. NJ DCA's Truth in Renting guide sums it up: no residential landlord may evict or fail to renew a lease, written or oral, without good cause (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.1 and related sections).

Key points from the DCA guide:

- **Coverage:** most residential rentals, including single-family homes, apartment buildings and rooming houses.
- **Exceptions:** the law may not apply to owner-occupied premises with two or fewer rental units. It does not cover transient hotel or seasonal guests.
- **Notices:** each cause except non-payment needs a written Notice to Quit with details. Some causes need a Notice to Cease first.
- **Court:** the tenant moves only after a Judgment for Possession and a warrant for removal.
- **Rent increases:** to evict for non-payment of an increased rent, you must first serve a valid notice to quit and notice of rent increase.

In Newark, this means rent control and the Anti-Eviction Act work together. You cannot push a tenant out to reset the rent.

Compliance checklist for a Newark owner:

- [ ] File the registration statement and pay the annual fee.
- [ ] Update registration at each change of tenant.
- [ ] Compute each increase from the CPI-U window, capped at 4%.
- [ ] Send 30 days' written notice to the tenant and the Rent Regulation Officer.
- [ ] Book the lead inspection at turnover for pre-1978 units.
- [ ] Keep records of every notice and payment.

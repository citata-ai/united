## How do G2 and Capterra collect reviews?

Check each review site's own collection and verification rules before using its labels as evidence. A verified label can address reviewer checks without proving the product fits your work.

G2's Community Guidelines describe automated filtering followed by manual review. G2 also says it labels incentivized reviews and permits incentives for honest feedback. Read the label beside the individual review.

Our attempts to fetch Capterra's verification and guideline pages were blocked during this source check. We do not present its internal collection or moderation process as verified here. Read the current policy directly before relying on a specific Capterra label.

Ask the same questions of either site. What does the label mean? Does it identify the reviewer, product use, or submission source? What does it leave unverified?

## Why do ratings differ by site?

Treat different ratings as a reason to inspect the underlying reviews, not to average them into a universal answer. The displayed score can hide important differences in the reviewed work.

Compare the population and time period before comparing scores. Ask whether reviewers describe self-managed rentals, association work, commercial leases, or management for outside owners.

Check whether the person reviewed the current plan. A complaint about a missing feature may concern a lower package or an earlier release. A glowing review may cover a task your team never uses.

Read detailed positive and negative accounts. Generic praise gives fewer testable details than a clear description of a completed task. A specific complaint still needs verification.

Do not infer fraud simply because a review is brief, anonymous, or positive. G2's guidelines note that vague wording alone does not establish inauthenticity.

Record the issue, source, date, and relevance in your buying notes. Keep uncertain claims marked as questions until you can test them.

## How do you spot vendor-written comparisons?

Check the publisher, commercial interest, links, and source dates before treating a comparison as independent advice. A useful seller article can still favor the product it sells.

Look for a product signup as the main next step. Check whether the comparison links to each competitor's current pricing and explains plan limits. Note unsupported claims about support or performance.

Do not copy a price from another vendor's blog. Buildium and MagicDoor publish their own pricing pages. Use those pages for their current terms, and keep the date you read them.

Watch for mismatched scope. A free owner tool and a manager suite may solve different jobs. A low starting rate may depend on billing term, unit floor, or missing services.

Treat best and easiest as conclusions that need a stated method. Ask what tasks the writer tested and whether they disclosed the proposed plan.

Use the article to build questions, then verify the answers with primary sources. Keep editorial claims separate from your own observed results.

## What can Reddit threads show that review sites do not?

Use Reddit threads to find detailed operating questions, then verify the context and behavior yourself. An account of a difficult task is useful even when it cannot establish a general failure rate.

Look for the exact process. Did a user describe a returned payment, a migration gap, a failed export, or a support handoff? Turn that description into a reproducible test.

Ask whether replies identify the same product and plan. Similar names, old interfaces, and different account settings can make apparently matching stories refer to different situations.

Do not treat repeated complaints as a measured share of customers. A discussion thread is not a controlled sample. This guide does not report review scores or defect rates.

Check the author's disclosed relationship to the software. A seller or consultant can provide useful detail, but the commercial interest still matters.

Avoid putting tenant or owner records into a public thread. Describe a test case with invented details. Bring private evidence to the approved vendor support route.

## What questions should you ask customer references?

Ask references to describe work that matches your portfolio and proposed setup. A friendly recommendation alone does not answer an accounting or migration question.

Use a structured reference call:

- **Match the business:** Ask about property types, staff roles, and management model.
- **Match the product:** Confirm plan, modules, integrations, and implementation path.
- **Ask for a task:** Request a description of the last close, owner handoff, or major correction.
- **Ask about gaps:** Identify work that still happens outside the platform.
- **Check support:** Ask how a specific issue reached resolution and who owned the handoff.
- **Check migration:** Ask what data needed repair and which checks mattered before go-live.
- **Check changes:** Ask what the team would test earlier if choosing again.
- **Confirm your test:** Turn the most relevant answer into an acceptance step for your own demo.

Do not ask a reference to share private records. A clear process description can still help you design a safe sample test.

Keep the reference result beside the vendor response and your observed test. If they conflict, resolve the difference before signing.

## Where do owner leads drop out of the funnel?

Owner leads drop out at 5 points between the form and the signed agreement. Fourandhalf calls this a leaky sales funnel. Many leads go in at the top, few signed owners come out. Your job is to find the stage where they fall out.

| Stage | What should happen | Common leak |
|---|---|---|
| 1. First reply | You reach the owner by phone or text | Reply takes hours, or only an email goes out |
| 2. First real talk | Two-way talk about the owner's goals | The call is a pitch, not a conversation |
| 3. Fit check | Property, area and goals fit your service | No clear fit rules, so time goes to poor fits |
| 4. Proposal | Fee sheet and agreement go out fast | Proposal is late, vague or has hidden fees |
| 5. Follow-up | Planned touches until a yes or no | One email, then silence |

Some leads were never real leads. The Goodjuju 2026 lead source report had to remove click-to-call taps, double form events and tenant maintenance forms from its client lead counts. Clean your lead count first. Otherwise your close rate looks worse than it is.

## Is slow response the main cause?

Slow response is the most common leak for web leads. The best-known data comes from the InsideSales.com and MIT Lead Response Management study, presented in 2007 by Dave Elkington and Professor James Oldroyd. It used 3 years of data from 6 companies, more than 15,000 web leads and more than 100,000 call attempts.

The study found:

- The odds to contact a lead dropped 100 times when the first call came at 30 minutes instead of 5.
- The odds to qualify a lead dropped 21 times over the same gap.
- From 5 minutes to 10 minutes, the odds to qualify dropped 4 times.

Two limits apply. The study measured contact and qualification, not signed contracts. And the 6 companies were not property managers. InsideSales.com also sold lead response software. Still, the direction is clear. Owners often send the same form to 2 or 3 managers. The first one who calls back gets the first real talk.

What to do:

1. Send an instant text and email that says who will call and when.
2. Call within 5 minutes during business hours.
3. After hours, give a booking link for the next morning.
4. Route each lead to one named person, with a backup.

## Do unclear fees stop owners?

Unclear fees stop many owners before they ever call. Owners compare managers online first. If your site says only "contact us for pricing", the owner moves to a site that shows a number.

AI answers make this more important. The second annual PM Trends study, with Rentvine as a sponsor, surveyed 500 small landlords through Harris Poll. It reports that 75% plan to use AI to find their next property manager. An AI answer can only repeat the fees it can find. A clear fee table on your site gives it something true to repeat.

A good fee table lists every fee, not just the monthly one:

| Fee | Example format |
|---|---|
| Monthly management | 8% of collected rent, $95 minimum |
| Leasing | 50% of first month's rent |
| Lease renewal | $150 flat |
| Setup | $0 |
| Maintenance markup | None, or 10% on vendor bills |
| Vacancy fee | $0 |
| Cancel terms | 30 days' notice, no cancel fee |

These numbers are examples, not benchmarks. Use your own. Our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) helps owners compare a percent fee with a flat fee.

## Why does a good call still end with "I'll think about it"?

A good call ends with "I'll think about it" when the talk was about you, not the owner. Fourandhalf describes the typical call. The manager asks for the address and explains fees and services. Then a contract goes out, with a follow-up a week later. Its advice is to listen 80% of the time and talk 20%.

Ask questions like these before you mention a fee:

- Why are we talking today?
- What went wrong with your last tenant or manager?
- Is this your only rental, or do you plan to buy more?
- What would a good year with a manager look like for you?

Then connect each service to the answer. An owner who lost 2 months of rent to a bad tenant cares about screening, not about your portal.

## How do I find my own leak?

Find your leak with 90 days of counts. Put every owner lead in a CRM with a stage and a date. Then count how many leads reach each stage.

Here is a worked example with hypothetical numbers:

| Stage | Leads | Rate from the stage before |
|---|---|---|
| Leads (clean count) | 100 | — |
| Contacted | 62 | 62% |
| Qualified | 40 | 65% |
| Proposal sent | 30 | 75% |
| Signed | 12 | 40% |

Overall, 12 of 100 leads signed. The biggest drop is at "Contacted": 38 leads never had a real talk. That is a speed and follow-up problem, not a marketing problem. More leads would not fix it.

If the biggest drop is at "Signed", look at your proposal and fees. If it is at "Qualified", look at where your leads come from and what your website promises.

Track lead source on every record too. Goodjuju found that GA4 missed about 4 in 10 of the AI leads it could trace on its own site. Ask "How did you first hear about us?" on the form and in the first reply.

Next step: pull your last 90 days of owner leads tonight and put them in the 5 stages. The stage with the biggest drop is where you start. For why there may be fewer leads to begin with, see [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping).

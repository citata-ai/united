## What does a door cost through acquisition?

A bought door costs a multiple of the fee revenue it brings, and the multiple depends on size, margin and market. There is no public price list. The numbers below are rules of thumb and one report of closed deals.

| Source | Measure | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Fourandhalf, 2014 | Price per door | 8 to 12 times the monthly fee |
| PMAssist, 2025 (older rule of thumb) | Price per door | About $1,000, now called out of date |
| PMAssist, 2025 (newer rule of thumb) | Price per business | 1 to 1.5 times revenue |
| PMAssist, 2025 (report of 89 closed deals) | Size effect | About 20% higher multiples above about 350 doors |
| PMAssist, 2025 | Highest price in the report | One roll-up deal above $8,000 per door |

PMAssist is a newsletter by Todd Ortscheid. The 2025 post summarizes a report by Peter Lohmann, built from 89 closed deals over 5 years. The sellers chose to submit their data. The author warns about that self-selection. Over two-thirds of the buyers were roll-ups, large firms that buy many small ones.

Acquisition has costs beyond the price:

- **Legal and due diligence fees.** Fourandhalf names legal fees as a cost.
- **Owner time.** You run the purchase and the transfer.
- **Lost owners.** Some owners leave when the manager changes. Fourandhalf says most deals include some kind of guarantee for accounts that do not transfer.

## What does a door cost through Google Ads or SEO?

A marketed door costs your marketing spend divided by the doors you sign. Fourandhalf's 2014 estimate for Google Ads was $100 to $500 per new property. Their example used $400 per door, so 50 doors cost $20,000.

That number is 12 years old. Ad prices have changed, so build your own figure. Here is the formula:

Cost per door = monthly spend ÷ (leads per month × close rate × doors per owner)

Worked example (hypothetical inputs; use your own):

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Monthly marketing spend | $2,000 |
| Owner leads per month | 20 |
| Close rate, lead to signed agreement | 15% |
| Average doors per new owner | 1.5 |
| Doors signed per month | 4.5 |
| Cost per door | about $444 |

SEO has a different shape. You pay for months before the leads come. Then the cost per door falls, because the pages keep working. Paid ads stop the day you stop paying.

The channel mix matters too. In Goodjuju's fall 2026 study of 35 property management companies, Google Search and the Map Pack brought 50.6% of owner form leads. Paid search brought 1.2%, but only 6 of the 35 companies ran ads. Goodjuju says strong paid campaigns can do better, so judge ads by your own cost per lead.

## Which costs less per door: buying or marketing?

Marketing costs less per door in most examples, but you pay with time. Here is the Fourandhalf 2014 example as a side-by-side, with their numbers.

| Item | Buy a 50-door book | Market for 50 doors |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly fee per door | $100 | $100 |
| Price per door | $1,000 (10 × monthly fee) | $400 (Google Ads) |
| Total cost for 50 doors | $50,000 | $20,000 |
| Time to get the doors | At closing | Months, at the pace of your lead flow |
| Owner lifetime left | Mixed: some owners near the end | Full: new owners at the start |
| Sales work | None | Calls, proposals, follow-up |

In this example, marketing costs $30,000 less. But the bought doors earn fees from day one. The marketed doors arrive one by one. Fourandhalf calls the marketing path "a measured, timed release approach."

Now test the break-even. Each bought door at $1,000 and a $100 fee pays back its price in 10 months of fees, before your costs. A marketed door at $400 pays back in 4 months. Both pay back fast if the owner stays. The real risk is churn.

## Which doors stay longer?

New doors from marketing usually stay longer than bought doors, because they start at the beginning of the client relationship. Fourandhalf makes this point: an acquired owner "could be anywhere in their lifecycle." Some may leave in a few months.

Change of manager is a natural exit point. The owner did not choose you. They chose the old company. So expect some loss after the transfer.

How to protect a purchase:

1. **Ask for a retention guarantee.** Pay part of the price only for doors that stay 6 or 12 months.
2. **Check each owner's tenure.** List how long each owner has been with the seller.
3. **Meet the top owners before closing.** Start with the owners who pay the most fees.
4. **Check the agreements.** Find out if the management agreements can be assigned to you.
5. **Plan the first 90 days.** Call every owner in week 1. Send the first statement on time.

Do not use one churn number from the web as a fact. Track your own: owners lost per year ÷ owners at the start of the year.

## When does each path make sense?

Each path fits a different goal and a different cash position. Use this checklist.

Buy a portfolio when:

- You need scale fast, for example to pass about 350 doors, where PMAssist's summary shows higher sale multiples.
- You want to enter a new city with a base of owners.
- You can finance the price and absorb some owner loss.
- You have staff to run the transfer.

Market for doors when:

- You can wait 12 to 24 months to grow.
- Cash is tight and you prefer a monthly spend.
- You want owners who chose you and match your fee and service.
- You can answer leads fast and close them.

Many companies do both. They buy a small book in a new area, then market there to grow it.

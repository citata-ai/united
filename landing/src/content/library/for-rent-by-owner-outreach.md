## Who are for-rent-by-owner landlords?

For-rent-by-owner (FRBO) landlords are owners who list and lease their rentals themselves. They are the largest group of possible clients for a property manager. HousingWire, reporting the 2024 Rental Housing Finance Survey from HUD and the Census Bureau, says individual investors own about 59% of US rental properties. Those properties hold about 32% of rental units.

So most rental properties belong to individuals, and most of them hold few units. Many of these owners manage the rental themselves. When a listing does not lease, or a tenant leaves a mess, some of them start to look for help.

## Where do FRBO landlords list?

FRBO landlords list where tenants search. Fourandhalf, in a talk with Tenant Turner CEO James Barrett, names the main platforms. Zillow (with Trulia and HotPads) has long been the largest. Facebook Marketplace now shows feeds from Zumper and Apartment List. Craigslist has faded.

| Platform | What the source says | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Zillow, Trulia, HotPads | 28% of tenant leads for managers (Tenant Turner via Fourandhalf) | Most FRBO listings in many markets |
| Zumper and Facebook Marketplace | 26% of tenant leads (Tenant Turner via Fourandhalf) | Close to Zillow in tenant lead share |
| craigslist | Faded, per Fourandhalf | Still used by some owners; strict terms |
| Yard signs | No data | Visible in your area, phone number on the sign |

The Tenant Turner numbers are national and refer to tenant leads, not to owners. Fourandhalf notes that markets differ.

Use these platforms to learn, not to harvest. Watch the FRBO listings in your area each week. Note how long each one stays up, the rent asked and the quality of the photos. That tells you which owners may need help, and what to say.

## What are the rules for contacting FRBO landlords?

The rules depend on the channel and on where you got the contact details. Many FRBO owners use a personal cell phone, so treat them as consumers.

| Channel | Main rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Reply through the listing site | Follow the site's terms | Site terms of use |
| Text to a cell | Commercial texts need written consent | FCC |
| Phone call | Scrub against the Do Not Call Registry every 31 days; call 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. | FTC |
| Email | Honest subject, postal address, opt-out honored in 10 business days | FTC, CAN-SPAM |
| Contact data taken from craigslist | $25 per unsolicited email, $500 per unsolicited text or call | craigslist terms of use |
| Postal mail | Not covered by the call, text and email rules above | FCC, FTC |

The craigslist terms are strict. They ban collecting user information. They set liquidated damages of $25 for each unsolicited email and $500 for each unsolicited text or call made with craigslist user data. Other listing sites have their own terms, so read them first.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What first message is helpful, not pushy?

A helpful first message gives the owner one useful fact about their own rental and asks nothing big. The owner did not ask to hear from you. So the message must earn a reply.

Good first messages share three traits:

1. **Specific.** Name the street or the unit type, not "your property."
2. **Useful.** Give a fact the owner can act on, such as a rent range from a public source.
3. **Small ask.** Offer a free rent check or a short guide, not a full management contract.

A sample postal letter or listing-site reply:

> Hi, I saw your 3-bedroom on Oak Street. I run a small property management company 10 minutes away. If it helps, I can send a free rent check for your unit, with the 5 closest listings. No obligation and no follow-up calls unless you ask. — Name, license number, phone, website

Things to avoid:

- **Fake tenant inquiries.** Never pose as a renter to start a talk. Craigslist's terms set $1,000 in damages for misrepresenting your identity.
- **Pressure.** No "your listing is failing." Owners hear that as an insult.
- **Long pitches.** Keep the first message under 80 words.

## When is the best time to reach out?

The best time to reach out is when the owner feels the pain, not when they first post. An owner who just listed expects to lease fast. An owner whose listing has sat for some weeks, or who relisted at a lower rent, is more open to help.

Signals to watch (our suggestion, not data):

- [ ] The listing has been up longer than similar units nearby.
- [ ] The owner cut the rent once or twice.
- [ ] The same unit appears again a few months later, a sign of turnover.
- [ ] The photos are poor, or the description lacks key facts.
- [ ] The owner lives out of state, per public property records.

Also be easy to find when the owner starts to search, on Google or in an AI assistant. Your site should answer fees and services in plain words. See [how owners choose a property manager with AI](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai).

## Which offer works for FRBO owners?

The offer that works for FRBO owners is a small first step that solves their current problem. A self-managing owner likes control. Full management can feel like too big a jump.

| Offer | Owner's problem | Your upside |
|---|---|---|
| Free rent check | "Is my rent right?" | Start a conversation, show local knowledge |
| Leasing only | "I can't find a tenant." | Fee now, trust for full management later |
| Showing service | "I can't attend every showing." | Small fee, many owner contacts |
| Tenant screening help | "I fear a bad tenant." | Shows your process |
| Free owner guide | "What are the local rules?" | Email opt-in with consent |

Price the offer in a clear way. Use our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to compare leasing-only and full-service fees for one owner.

Track each FRBO owner in your CRM with the source and date of first contact. Ask on each call how they heard about you. After 6 months, you will know which offer and which channel brought owners who signed.

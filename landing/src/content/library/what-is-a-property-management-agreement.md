## What does a property management agreement include?

A property management agreement includes the manager's duties, authority, fees, term, cancellation terms and money rules. It is the one document that answers "who decides, who pays, and who is responsible" for a managed rental.

Some states write the minimum contents into law. Arizona's ARS 32-2173 is a clear example. It says a property management agreement must:

1. State the material terms of the services and the firm's duties to the owner.
2. Be signed by the owner and by the firm's designated broker or an authorized licensee.
3. Have a start date and an end date.
4. Have cancellation terms that both sides accept.
5. Say how the firm handles all money it collects, tenant deposits included.
6. Set the type and frequency of reports to the owner.
7. State the amount and purpose of any operating reserve.
8. Say how interest on trust account money is handled.
9. Set the manager's compensation terms.
10. Forbid assignment to another licensee without the owner's express written consent.

Even if your property is not in Arizona, this list is a good checklist. A clause that is missing is a question to ask before you sign.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What fees does a management agreement list?

A management agreement lists every fee the owner can be charged, with the amount and the trigger for each. A fee that is not in the agreement is a fee you can question.

Typical fee lines, with US averages from iPropertyManagement's 722-branch sample (updated 2026-09-23):

| Fee | What triggers it | Average in the sample |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly management fee | Each month of management | 8.49% of rent (range 3.75% to 14%) |
| Flat monthly fee (instead of %) | Each month, per unit | $101.04 per unit |
| Leasing (tenant placement) fee | A new tenant signs | 70.6% of one month's rent |
| Lease renewal fee (flat) | A tenant renews | $231.77 |
| Setup fee | New contract | $185.24 |
| Inspection fee | Each inspection | $106.72 |

Check the base of the percentage fee. All Property Management notes that contracts may charge on "rental value", "rent due" or "rent collected". A fee on rent collected means the manager earns nothing when the tenant does not pay. That gives the manager a reason to collect.

## How long does a property management agreement last?

Most agreements run for a fixed term, often 12 months, and then renew. Arizona law requires both a start date and an end date in the agreement. Read 3 things in the term clause:

- **Start date.** The day the manager takes over, not the day you sign.
- **End date and renewal.** Does it renew on its own? For how long?
- **Notice to stop renewal.** How many days before the end date you must say no.

A long term with an automatic renewal and a short notice window can lock you in for a second year. Put the notice date in your calendar on the day you sign.

## How do you cancel a property management agreement?

You cancel through the cancellation clause: give the written notice it requires, pay any fee it sets, and collect your records and funds. Arizona's ARS 32-2173 requires cancellation terms that both sides accept, so the clause should exist in every agreement there.

Cancellation checklist:

- [ ] Read the notice period (for example, 30 or 60 days) and the delivery method.
- [ ] Check for an early termination fee and how it is calculated.
- [ ] Check the "tenant placed by manager" clause. Some agreements charge a fee if you keep a tenant the manager found.
- [ ] Ask for the security deposits, the tenant ledgers and the owner balance in writing.
- [ ] Ask for keys, leases, vendor contacts and open work orders.
- [ ] Get the final owner statement.

## Who is liable under a management agreement?

The agreement splits risk between the owner and the manager. It does not remove the owner's duties as landlord. Look for 4 clauses:

1. **Indemnity.** Who pays if a third party sues. Many agreements make the owner cover claims, except for the manager's own negligence. Read the exceptions.
2. **Insurance.** The owner's landlord policy and the manager's errors and omissions policy. Ask to see both.
3. **Authority limits.** The repair amount the manager may spend without your approval. A low limit protects your cash. A limit that is too low slows urgent repairs.
4. **Trust funds.** Who holds rent and deposits. In California, 10 CCR 2835 prohibits commingling, with small exceptions such as up to $200 of broker money for bank fees.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What clauses should an owner check before signing?

Check the clauses that change your money, your control and your exit. Use this table as a final pass:

| Clause | Question to ask | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Fees | Is every fee listed, with the base? | "Other fees as needed" |
| Fee base | Rent collected or rent due? | Fee on rent due for vacant months |
| Repair limit | What can the manager spend alone? | No limit, or no emergency rule |
| Maintenance markup | Is a markup on vendor bills disclosed? | No mention, but bills look high |
| Reserve | How much, and for what? | Reserve with no amount |
| Reports | What report, and how often? | No report schedule |
| Owner draw | Which day, and how? | No fixed date |
| Term and renewal | When can I stop renewal? | Short notice window |
| Cancellation | Notice and fee? | Fee equal to months of management |
| Assignment | Can they sell my contract? | No consent required |

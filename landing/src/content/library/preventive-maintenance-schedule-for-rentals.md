## Which preventive maintenance tasks should a rental get each month?

Monthly preventive maintenance for a rental covers air filters, smoke alarms and leaks. These 3 checks take minutes and catch the problems that cost the most later. ENERGY STAR advises that you "inspect, clean, or change air filters once a month" for central air, furnaces and heat pumps.

Monthly checklist:

- [ ] **Air filter.** Check it. Change it if it looks gray or clogged.
- [ ] **Smoke alarms.** Test each alarm. The U.S. Fire Administration says to "Test the alarm monthly."
- [ ] **CO alarms.** Test each carbon monoxide alarm, where the unit has one.
- [ ] **Leaks.** Look under sinks, around toilets and at the water heater base.
- [ ] **Drains.** Run each drain and check for slow flow.
- [ ] **Outside.** Walk the exterior. Look for trash, broken lights and trip hazards.

In an occupied unit, the tenant often does the first 4 checks. Put that duty in the lease, and give the tenant an easy way to report a problem.

## Which tasks belong in spring and fall?

Spring and fall preventive maintenance centers on the heating and cooling system and the roof drainage. ENERGY STAR advises a cooling check in spring and a heating check in fall, because contractors get busy once peak season starts.

| Season | Task | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Spring | HVAC cooling tune-up by a licensed tech | Owner |
| Spring | Clean the AC coils and check the condensate drain | Owner or tech |
| Spring | Clean gutters and downspouts | Owner |
| Spring | Check exterior paint, caulk and window seals | Owner |
| Spring | Check irrigation and hose bibs for leaks | Owner |
| Fall | Furnace or heat pump heating tune-up | Owner or tech |
| Fall | Clean gutters after leaves drop | Owner |
| Fall | Shut off and drain outdoor faucets in cold climates | Owner or tenant |
| Fall | Check weatherstripping on doors and windows | Owner |
| Fall | Test the heat in each room | Tenant reports, owner fixes |

ENERGY STAR lists the work a tune-up should cover: tighten electrical connections, lubricate moving parts, check system controls, clean the AC coils, check refrigerant and the blower. It also says a clogged condensate drain "can cause indoor water damage."

## Which tasks happen once a year?

Yearly preventive maintenance covers the items that wear slowly but fail at high cost. Do these during a vacant turn if one falls in the year. Otherwise, schedule a yearly visit with the tenant's notice.

Yearly checklist:

- [ ] **Dryer vent.** Clean the full vent run, not only the lint screen.
- [ ] **Alarm batteries.** Replace 9-volt and hardwired backup batteries. The U.S. Fire Administration says "at least once a year."
- [ ] **Alarm age.** Replace any smoke alarm 10 years past its manufacture date.
- [ ] **Water heater.** Check for rust, leaks and a working pressure relief valve. Follow the maker's manual for flushing.
- [ ] **Roof.** Look for missing shingles, flashing gaps and soft spots.
- [ ] **Plumbing.** Check supply lines and shutoff valves under sinks and toilets.
- [ ] **Pest check.** Look for droppings, nests and entry gaps.
- [ ] **Fire extinguisher.** Check the gauge and the date, where one is present.
- [ ] **Unit inspection.** Walk the full unit and take dated photos.

The dryer vent task matters more than it looks. A U.S. Fire Administration report on 2002–2004 data counted about 12,700 clothes dryer fires a year in residential buildings, with 15 deaths and $88 million in property loss. It named "failure to clean" as the leading factor.

## Which tasks can the tenant do?

Tenants can do simple, low-risk tasks if the lease assigns them in writing. Keep any task that needs a license, a ladder on a roof, gas work or electrical work with the owner.

| Task | Tenant | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Change air filter | Yes, if the lease says so | Supplies filters or checks |
| Test smoke and CO alarms | Yes | Replaces alarms and batteries |
| Report leaks and slow drains | Yes, at once | Repairs |
| Clean lint screen after each load | Yes | Cleans the vent run yearly |
| Keep drains clear of grease | Yes | Clears main line clogs |
| HVAC tune-up | No | Yes |
| Roof and gutter work | No | Yes |
| Gas and electrical work | No | Yes |

A filter subscription shipped to the unit makes the filter task easier for the tenant. A short note in the welcome packet tells the tenant which size to buy.

## What does preventive maintenance save?

Preventive maintenance saves money on water, energy and emergency repairs. The best numbers come from federal sources.

- **Water.** EPA WaterSense says household leaks waste nearly 1 trillion gallons a year in the U.S. An average home's leaks can waste more than 9,300 gallons a year. Fixing easy leaks can cut water bills by about 10%.
- **Energy.** ENERGY STAR says dirty filters raise energy costs and can shorten equipment life.
- **Fire.** USFA names failure to clean as the leading factor in residential dryer fires.

Worked example for a 20-unit portfolio where the owner pays water. Assume a water bill of $60 a unit each month (your own number goes here). A 10% cut on that bill, per EPA's estimate, is $6 a unit each month. That is $120 a month across 20 units, or $1,440 a year, from leak checks alone.

## How do I keep the schedule running?

A schedule works only when each task has a date and an owner. Put each recurring task in your property management software as a work order that repeats. Close each one with a photo and a note.

## How do I schedule showings?

Schedule showings in set time slots, not one by one on demand. Slots save you trips and make visitors commit.

A simple schedule for a new listing:

| Slot | Example time | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Weekday evening 1 | Tuesday 5:30–6:30 p.m. | After work hours |
| Weekday evening 2 | Thursday 5:30–6:30 p.m. | A second chance in the same week |
| Weekend block | Saturday 10:00 a.m.–12:00 p.m. | Most flexible for visitors |

Booking steps:

1. **Use one calendar or tool.** Every inquiry books a slot the same way.
2. **Ask three questions first.** Move-in date, number of occupants, and whether they have read the screening criteria.
3. **Send the criteria with the booking.** Visitors who do not meet them can cancel early.
4. **Confirm the day before.** Ask visitors to reply "yes" to keep the slot.
5. **Ask for ID at the door.** It protects you and the current tenant.

Ask the same questions of everyone. Do not ask about children, religion, national origin or disability.

## What do I bring to a showing?

Bring a showing kit so you can answer questions and move visitors to apply. Prepare the unit the day before.

The showing kit:

- [ ] Printed screening criteria and the application link (a QR code works).
- [ ] A one-page fact sheet: rent, deposit, fees, move-in date, lease term, utilities, pet policy, parking.
- [ ] The scam warning: the only ways you take payments.
- [ ] A sign-in sheet or a list of booked visitors.
- [ ] Keys, a flashlight, and a tape measure.
- [ ] Your phone, charged.

The unit check, the day before:

- [ ] Clean, with lights on and blinds open.
- [ ] Every bulb and smoke alarm works.
- [ ] No strong smells; open windows if needed.
- [ ] Repairs done, or a written note of the repair date.
- [ ] Thermostat at a comfortable setting.

In Texas, Property Code 92.3515 requires printed tenant selection criteria to be available when you give out an application. Bring them to every showing in every state.

## Should I do group open houses or one-on-one showings?

Use group showings when interest is high, and one-on-one showings when it is low. Both work if you treat every visitor the same.

| Factor | Group showing | One-on-one showing |
|---|---|---|
| Best when | Many inquiries in the first week | Few inquiries, or a high-end unit |
| Your time | One trip for many visitors | One trip per visitor |
| Same facts for all | Easy: one talk for everyone | Needs a script |
| Visitor questions | Less private | More time for each person |
| Safety | More people around | Ask for ID and tell someone where you are |
| Occupied unit | Fewer entries for the tenant | More entries |

For an occupied unit, follow your state's entry rules. California Civil Code 1954 lets a landlord enter to "exhibit the dwelling unit to prospective or actual... tenants." It requires reasonable written notice, presumed to be 24 hours, during normal business hours. Florida Statutes 83.53 says a tenant "shall not unreasonably withhold consent" for showings to prospective tenants. In New York, the Attorney General says entry needs the tenant's consent for routine purposes or must follow the lease.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What should I say and not say at a showing?

Talk about the home, not about the people. Describe features, rules and costs. Never comment on who would fit.

The Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. 3604(c), bars statements that indicate "any preference, limitation, or discrimination" based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status or national origin. HUD's rule, 24 CFR 100.75(b), says this covers "all written or oral notices or statements." Section 3604(d) also bars telling someone a unit is not available when it is.

| Say this | Not this |
|---|---|
| "The unit has 2 bedrooms and 1 bath." | "Perfect for a young couple." |
| "The building has a quiet-hours rule from 10 p.m." | "This is a quiet building, not good for kids." |
| "The nearest bus stop is 2 blocks away." | "Lots of people like you live here." |
| "Here are the screening criteria we use for every applicant." | "I don't think you would qualify." |
| "Applications are reviewed in the order they are complete." | "I'll call you if the others fall through." |
| "We consider requests for reasonable accommodations." | "We don't allow animals, no exceptions." |

Give the same fact sheet and the same answers to everyone. If you do not know an answer, write it down and send it to all visitors.

## How do I follow up after a showing?

Follow up with every visitor within a day, using the same message. Speed turns visitors into applicants, and the same message keeps you fair.

Sample follow-up text:

```text
Thank you for visiting [address] today.
Apply here: [link]. Application fee: $[amount] per adult.
Our screening criteria: [link].
We will review complete applications starting [date].
Questions? Reply to this message or call [number].
We never ask for payment by wire transfer, gift card or crypto.
```

Then:

1. **Log each visitor** and whether they applied.
2. **Review applications in order** of completion, using your written criteria.
3. **Tell everyone the result.** Send approvals, and adverse action notices where a screening report played a part.
4. **Close the listing** when the lease is signed, so copies lose their target.

The FTC says renters should be wary of anyone who will not show a unit. A real showing and a clear follow-up help honest renters trust you.

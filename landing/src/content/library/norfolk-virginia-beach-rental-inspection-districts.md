## What is a Virginia rental inspection district?

A Virginia rental inspection district is an area where a city may inspect rental homes for Building Code compliance. Va. Code 36-105.1:1 is the only state law that lets a Virginia city run a rental inspection program. Norfolk and Virginia Beach follow the same section.

The city council must find 3 things before it creates a district:

1. **A need** to protect the health, safety and welfare of the people who live in rental homes there.
2. **A condition:** the rental homes are blighted or deteriorating, or need inspection to prevent decline, based on their number, age and condition.
3. **A purpose:** inspection is necessary to keep safe, decent and sanitary living conditions.

Before it adopts the ordinance, the council must hold a public hearing, with notice in a newspaper once a week for 2 weeks in a row. The section also says it does not authorize "one or more locality-wide rental inspection districts." So the district must cover only the areas that meet the test.

A city can also cover a single rental outside a district, but only after a separate finding that the unit is blighted, deteriorating or has Building Code violations.

## What does a Virginia rental inspection require from an owner?

A Virginia rental inspection ordinance can require 4 things from an owner in a district. The state section limits each one.

| Duty | What Va. Code 36-105.1:1 allows | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Tell the city it is a rental | Written notice to the building department | No registration rule and no fee for this notice; at least 60 days to comply; penalty up to $50 |
| Initial inspection | Building Code items that affect safe, decent and sanitary conditions | None stated beyond the code |
| Follow-up inspections | Until the unit complies | As many as needed to fix violations |
| Periodic inspection | Once each calendar year at most | Exemption for at least 4 years after a clean inspection |

Other rules that protect owners:

- **4-year exemption.** A unit that passes with no violations gets an exemption for at least 4 years. A unit with a certificate of occupancy from the last 4 years gets the same. The city can revoke it if the unit later breaks the code, and may inspect again after a sale.
- **Sampling for large buildings.** In a multifamily development of more than 10 units, the city inspects only a sample of 2 units up to 10% of the units. It can charge for no more than 10 units, unless it finds violations.
- **Fees.** The city council may set a per-unit fee for initial, follow-up and periodic inspections.
- **Professional management.** The council may exempt units managed by a licensed real estate professional, a property manager or managing agent, or a publicly traded owner that manages its own units.

Worked example: a 40-unit complex in a district passes its first inspection. The city may inspect 2 to 4 units (10% of 40 is 4), and it may charge for at most 10 units. After the clean result, the complex is exempt for at least 4 years.

## Which Norfolk areas are in a district?

We could not confirm a current list of Norfolk rental inspection districts at a primary source, so we do not name areas here. Each district must come from a city ordinance after a public hearing, so the list is in the Norfolk City Code and in council records.

What we did confirm: the City of Norfolk runs Rent Ready Norfolk, a voluntary program for landlords (City of Norfolk, 2024). It offers:

- **A free quality assessment** of a unit's interior, exterior and common areas before a renter moves in.
- **Workshops** on the Virginia Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (VRLTA), fair housing and maintenance.
- **Free listings** of certified units on the city's property search site.
- **A Landlord-Tenant Relations Officer** for rental disputes and advice.

Contact Rent Ready Norfolk at 757-664-RENT or rentready@norfolk.gov. For code enforcement, contact Neighborhood Quality through Norfolk Cares at 757-664-6510.

If you rent to a voucher holder, the Norfolk Redevelopment and Housing Authority (NRHA) inspects the unit before the lease signing and each year. That inspection is separate from any city program.

## Does Virginia Beach inspect rentals?

We could not confirm whether Virginia Beach has adopted a rental inspection district, so we do not state that it has one. If it inspects long-term rentals, it must follow the same Va. Code 36-105.1:1 limits: districts based on findings, no citywide district, and inspections no more than once a year.

How to check any parcel in Norfolk or Virginia Beach:

- [ ] Ask the city building or code department: "Is this parcel in a rental inspection district?"
- [ ] Ask for the ordinance number and the fee schedule.
- [ ] Ask whether a professional manager gets the exemption in Va. Code 36-105.1:1(G)(2).
- [ ] Keep the clean inspection report. It starts the 4-year exemption.

## What does the Virginia VRLTA require on deposits?

The Virginia Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (VRLTA) caps security deposits at 2 months' periodic rent. Va. Code 55.1-1226 also sets the return rules.

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Cap | No more than 2 months' periodic rent |
| Allowed deductions | Accrued rent and lease late charges, damage beyond reasonable wear and tear, other lease charges, actual damages for breach |
| Deadline | Itemized written notice and any refund within 45 days after the tenancy ends or the tenant moves out, whichever is later |
| More than 1 tenant | One check payable to all tenants, unless all agree otherwise in writing |
| No forwarding address | Hold the deposit; after 1 year past the 45-day period you may send it to the State Treasurer as unclaimed property |

Worked example: HUD's FY2027 fair market rent for a 2-bedroom in the Virginia Beach-Norfolk-Newport News area is $1,667, down 2.7% from $1,713. At that rent, the deposit cap is $3,334.

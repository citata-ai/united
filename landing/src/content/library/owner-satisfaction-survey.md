## Which questions should the owner survey ask?

Ask about the owner's experience with the work your company delivers. Keep the survey short enough that the owner can explain the main issue without completing a long form.

Bain's Net Promoter System uses a recommendation question on a 0–10 scale. Use that question consistently if you want a Net Promoter Score, or NPS. Add service questions to learn what needs action.

Use this complete generic template. Replace the bracketed fields with checked contact and company details. Have your adviser check local privacy and other applicable rules before using it.

```text
Owner Service Survey

Company: [Name]
Review period: [Period]
Reply contact: [Name and approved contact route]

We use this feedback to improve our management service.
Please avoid including resident personal information in your comments.

How likely are you to recommend our management service
to another rental property owner?
Choose a score from 0, not at all likely, to 10, extremely likely.
Score: [ ]

What is the main reason for that score?
[Open response]

Which part of our service currently works best for you?
[Open response]

Which part needs the most improvement?
Choose any that apply:
- Financial reports or explanations
- Repair quality, timing or cost
- Leasing updates
- Response to questions
- Approval requests or funding process
- Other: [Explain]

Tell us about the most important unresolved issue.
[Open response, or write none]

What should we change first?
[Open response]

May [service contact] contact you about your feedback?
[Yes / No]
Preferred approved contact route: [ ]

For urgent property issues, use [staffed urgent route].
This survey does not replace the urgent reporting process.
```

Do not ask residents' personal details in an owner survey. Link a reported case internally through an approved identifier. Store the feedback where the service team can act without giving unnecessary access to account data.

## How often should I send the survey?

Choose a routine survey schedule your team can support with follow-up. Keep event feedback separate from the general relationship survey.

A suggested approach is a regular relationship review plus a short case check after significant work. This is an operating choice, not a verified industry cadence. Avoid sending a fresh general survey every time a single work order closes.

Record the survey date and period covered. Keep the same core question and scale when comparing results. A wording change can make an apparent improvement hard to interpret.

Invite the full intended owner group, including owners with open concerns. Excluding dissatisfied owners makes the score less useful. Keep a record of who received the survey and which addresses failed.

Decide how to handle owners with several properties. A relationship survey can count an owner once, while a property service review may need separate records. State your unit of measurement before calculating results.

Send a clear reminder through the approved channel if you choose to use one. Do not pressure owners to raise a score or tie support to survey participation. The useful output is an honest issue you can address.

Keep a staffed route for urgent concerns outside the survey. Owners should not have to wait for a feedback cycle to report a serious problem.

## What is a good owner NPS?

Use your own consistent results and response coverage before judging a score. This guide does not claim a verified property management owner NPS benchmark.

Bain defines promoters as scores of 9 or 10 and detractors as scores from 0 through 6. NPS equals the percentage of promoters minus the percentage of detractors. Scores of 7 or 8 count in total responses but not either subtraction group.

For an invented example, assume 20 valid owner responses: 12 promoters, 5 passives and 3 detractors. Promoters are 60% and detractors are 15%. The example NPS is 45.

That is a score, not a percentage of owners who will stay. It does not show the views of owners who did not respond. Report the invited owner count, response count and calculation beside the result.

Do not let a small score change replace reading the comments. A few responses can change a small group's result materially. Inspect which owners replied and whether the service mix changed.

Keep the relationship score separate from a score about a single repair. They answer different questions. Use the same definition when you review the next period.

## How should I follow up on low scores?

Follow up by checking the stated issue, confirming the facts and assigning a next action. A low score is an invitation to understand a problem, not proof that the owner is unreasonable.

Ask the owner which event most affected the score. Review the related statement, repair or message history before replying with an explanation. Distinguish a missing explanation from unfinished service.

Assign a person who can act within clear authority. Give the owner a realistic next update. If the issue needs a decision from the broker or another adviser, explain that route.

Record the concern, agreed action and follow-up status. Do not mark a case resolved merely because someone sent a reply. Check whether the promised work happened.

Review positive comments too. They can show which service practices owners value and which staff actions work well. Do not publish a comment or turn it into an endorsement without the needed permission.

If feedback concerns fees, prepare the actual agreement and calculation. The [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) can help explain arithmetic, but the signed terms govern the charge.

## How can I turn feedback into a service change?

Group feedback by the work that needs improvement, then choose a change with a named owner. Keep specific cases open until their own actions finish.

A repeated complaint about statements might call for a clearer payout explanation. Repair complaints may need separate checks for cost, quality and delay. Do not label every problem communication and stop there.

Write what will change and how the team will inspect the result. Keep the next survey comparable so you can assess the response. Avoid promising that the change will produce a specific score or retention result.

Tell owners when their feedback led to a practical change, using accurate facts and no private case details. Ask local advisers about privacy and record handling where needed.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Choose a service contact and a follow-up process before you send the first survey. Collecting feedback creates work that someone must own.

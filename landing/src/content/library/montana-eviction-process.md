Montana gives a landlord 3 notice clocks and one fast court track. Here is the whole process, from the 2025 Montana Code Annotated (MCA) and the court self-help packet, read on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice for unpaid rent | 3 days after written notice | MCA 70-24-422(2) |
| Notice for most other lease breaches | 14 days; the tenant can cure | MCA 70-24-422(1)(a), (1)(d) |
| Unauthorized pet or person, verbal abuse | 3 days | MCA 70-24-422(1)(b), (c), (f) |
| Damage to the premises, or risk of damage or injury | 3 days | MCA 70-24-422(3), (4) |
| Same breach again within 6 months | 5 days | MCA 70-24-422(1)(e) |
| No-cause end, month-to-month | 30 days' written notice | MCA 70-24-441(2) |
| No-cause end, week-to-week | 7 days' written notice | MCA 70-24-441(1) |
| Notice by certified mail | Served 3 days after mailing | MCA 70-24-108(1)(d) |
| Tenant's answer | 5 business days after service | MCA 70-24-429(4) |
| Hearing on possession | Within 10 business days of appearance or answer date | MCA 70-24-427(2)(a) |
| Court ruling | Within 5 days after the hearing | MCA 70-24-427(4) |
| Sheriff executes writ of assistance | Within 5 business days of receipt | MCA 70-24-427(4) |
| Lockout or utility shutoff | Not allowed | MCA 70-24-428 |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What notice must a Montana landlord give for unpaid rent?

A Montana landlord gives a 3-day written notice for unpaid rent. MCA 70-24-422(2) says the notice must state the nonpayment and your intent to end the rental agreement if the tenant does not pay within that period. If the tenant does not pay in 3 days, you may terminate the agreement, and the tenant must move out.

Delivery matters, because the clock starts when the tenant gets the notice. MCA 70-24-108 accepts these methods:

- **Hand delivery** to the tenant.
- **Mail with a certificate of mailing, or certified mail,** to the address the tenant named or the last-known address. Service counts as made 3 days after the date of mailing.
- **E-mail** to an address the tenant gave in the rental agreement. Notice is complete only on a read receipt or a reply that is not automatic.

Watch for: if you mail the 3-day notice, add 3 days for delivery. A mailed rent notice takes at least 6 days, not 3.

## Can a Montana landlord evict for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes, a Montana landlord can evict for a lease violation, and can end a periodic tenancy with no cause. The lease-violation notice is in MCA 70-24-422(1). It must name the acts or omissions, say the agreement will end, and give a move-out date.

For most breaches, the notice period is 14 days. If the tenant can fix the breach by repairs, by paying damages or with your written approval, and does so before the date, the agreement does not end. Some breaches have a 3-day period: an unauthorized pet, unauthorized persons living in the unit, and verbal abuse of the landlord. If the same breach comes back within 6 months of an earlier notice, you may end the agreement on 5 days' notice.

MCA 70-24-422(3) and (4) add two more 3-day cases. The first is when the tenant destroys or damages the premises. The second is when the tenant creates a reasonable risk of damage to the premises or injury to neighbors.

A no-cause end works only for periodic tenancies. MCA 70-24-441 lets the landlord or the tenant end a month-to-month tenancy with at least 30 days' written notice. A week-to-week tenancy needs 7 days. MCA 70-24-422(7) says you can choose the 30-day route instead of a for-cause notice.

One limit applies to every notice. MCA 70-24-431 bars a landlord from bringing, or threatening, an action for possession to retaliate against a tenant. Protected acts include a housing code complaint to an agency, a written repair complaint to you, or joining a tenants' union. A complaint in the 6 months before your action creates a presumption of retaliation. The bar does not apply if the tenant is in default in rent.

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Montana, and what does it cost?

A Montana landlord files an action for possession in a court in the county, city or town where the rental is. The court self-help packet "Asking the Court to Evict Your Tenant" (Montana Supreme Court Commission on Self-Represented Litigants and Montana Legal Services Association, updated 2023-10-11) lists the options:

1. **Justice court,** city court or municipal court (city and municipal courts are not in all areas).
2. **District court.** The packet warns this route may take longer.

The packet lists the forms: Complaint for Possession, Summons, Request to Serve Documents, Order Setting Hearing, and Judgment. You must name each adult tenant you want to remove. You may not hand the papers to the tenant yourself. The sheriff or a process server serves them.

The packet also warns that a corporation or LLC cannot file on its own. Under Rule 2(a) of the Montana Justice and City Court Civil Rules, a business entity must sue through an attorney.

Fees differ by court, and the statutes I read do not set one state fee. One example: the Madison County Justice Court page lists a $50 filing fee and a $30 answer fee for the tenant. You also pay a service fee for each document served on each defendant. Ask your local clerk for the current amounts. If you cannot pay, the packet points you to an Affidavit of Inability to Pay.

If you win, MCA 70-24-442 lets the court award reasonable attorney fees and costs to the prevailing party.

## How long does an eviction take in Montana?

A Montana eviction runs on short court deadlines once you file. Here are the steps with each statutory clock.

| Step | Clock | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Notice delivered in hand | 3, 5, 14 or 30 days, by reason | MCA 70-24-422, 70-24-441 |
| 2. File complaint, sheriff serves tenant | No set time; depends on the sheriff | Court packet |
| 3. Tenant answers | 5 business days, not counting the day of service | MCA 70-24-429(4) |
| 4. Hearing | Within 10 business days of appearance or answer date | MCA 70-24-427(2)(a) |
| 5. Ruling and writs | Within 5 days of the hearing; writs issue at once | MCA 70-24-427(4) |
| 6. Sheriff executes writ of assistance | Within 5 business days of receipt | MCA 70-24-427(4) |

A worked example for unpaid rent with the notice handed over on a Monday: 3 days of notice, about 7 calendar days to answer, up to about 14 calendar days to the hearing, up to 5 days to rule, and about 7 calendar days for the sheriff. That is roughly 5 weeks plus the time to serve the papers. If the tenant does not answer in 5 business days, the packet says you may ask for a default judgment, which is faster.

Some cases move faster. If you ended the tenancy because the tenant put the premises or neighbors at risk (MCA 70-24-321(3)), the hearing must be within 5 business days. A separate hearing for damages must be held within 45 days after the court decides possession and rent (MCA 70-24-427(2)(b)). A jury trial or an appeal to district court adds time.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Montana?

The sheriff removes the tenant after a Montana judgment for possession. MCA 70-24-427(4) says the court must issue a writ of possession and a writ of assistance immediately when it grants your claim. The sheriff executes the writ of assistance within 5 business days of receiving it, or at a time you and the sheriff agree on.

A tenant who holds over can cost the tenant money. Under MCA 70-24-429, if you ended a month-to-month tenancy with a lawful 30-day notice and the tenant stays, the holdover counts as purposeful. You may recover up to 3 months' rent or treble damages, whichever is greater.

## Is a self-help eviction legal in Montana?

No, a self-help eviction is not legal in Montana. MCA 70-24-428 says a landlord may not take possession of the unit except as the chapter allows, or after abandonment or surrender. That includes cutting heat, running water, hot water, electricity, gas or other essential services on purpose.

The penalty is steep. Under MCA 70-24-411, a tenant who is locked out or loses services can recover possession or end the agreement. In both cases, the tenant can recover up to 3 months' rent or treble damages, whichever is greater.

What to do: keep a copy of each notice, the delivery proof and the lease. The judge expects you to show how you gave notice and why.

Montana gives landlords wide freedom on rent. We read the Montana Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (MCA Title 70, chapter 24) and the local-power limits in MCA 7-1-111, in the Montana Code Annotated 2025, on 2026-10-20. The state sets no rent cap, blocks local rent control, and uses short, clear notice periods.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| State cap on rent increases | None | MCA Title 70, ch. 24 (no cap) |
| Local rent control | Denied to self-government cities | MCA 7-1-111(26) |
| Local landlord rules beyond state law | Denied | MCA 7-1-111(13) |
| Rent-increase notice | No set period in the Act; 30 days is common practice | MCA 70-24-441 by analogy |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | At least 30 days, in writing | MCA 70-24-441(2) |
| End a week-to-week tenancy | At least 7 days, in writing | MCA 70-24-441(1) |
| Lease ends with no notice and no default extension | Becomes month-to-month | MCA 70-24-205 |
| Retaliatory rent increase | Banned; 6-month presumption | MCA 70-24-431 |
| Notice by certified mail | Served 3 days after mailing | MCA 70-24-108(1)(d) |

## Does Montana have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

Montana has no cap on rent increases and no rent control. No section in MCA Title 70, chapter 24 limits how much a landlord may raise the rent.

The state also blocks local rent control. MCA 7-1-111(26) denies a local government with self-government powers "any power to control the amount of rent charged for private residential or commercial property." Private residential property does not include property in which the local government has an interest, directly or through a housing authority.

MCA 7-1-111(13) goes further. It denies self-government units any power to license landlords or regulate them "beyond what is provided in" Title 70, chapters 24, 25 and 33. So a city cannot add its own rent-notice rule.

Other limits still apply. Federal fair housing law bars rent terms that differ by protected class. Subsidized units, such as Housing Choice Voucher units, follow their program contract.

## How many days notice does a Montana landlord need to raise the rent?

The Montana Residential Landlord and Tenant Act does not set a rent-increase notice period. We found no section in chapter 24 that names one.

The practical rule comes from the termination notice. On a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord can end the tenancy with at least 30 days' written notice (MCA 70-24-441(2)). Most Montana landlords and managers give the same 30 days in writing for a rent change. On a week-to-week tenancy, the matching period is 7 days (70-24-441(1)).

MCA 70-24-201(2)(a) adds that, unless the lease says otherwise, the tenant pays "the rental value ... as determined by the landlord." The lease can add a longer notice period, and then the lease controls.

### Worked example

A month-to-month tenant pays $1,250 on the 1st. You want $1,325 from August 1.

1. Write the notice: new rent, start date, your name and contact.
2. Count back 30 days from August 1: deliver by July 2 at the latest.
3. If you mail by certified mail, add 3 days: mail by June 29 (MCA 70-24-108(1)(d)).
4. Keep proof of delivery.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Old rent | $1,250 |
| New rent | $1,325 |
| Increase | $75 (6%) |
| Latest hand delivery | July 2 |
| Latest certified mailing | June 29 |

Giving notice a few days early costs nothing and avoids a dispute over the count.

## Can a Montana landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

A Montana landlord can raise the rent during a fixed-term lease only if the lease allows it. MCA 70-24-201(1) lets the landlord and tenant put rent and term in the rental agreement. That agreed rent binds both sides for the term.

When the term ends, the landlord can offer a renewal at a new rent. If the lease sets no default extension and neither side gives a 30-day written notice before the end date, the tenancy continues month to month (MCA 70-24-205). After that, the 30-day practice above applies.

Montana also limits damages for early termination. Under MCA 70-24-201(2)(f), unless the lease says otherwise, a party who ends the lease without cause before it expires owes damages of up to 1 month's rent.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Montana?

A Montana month-to-month tenancy ends with at least 30 days' written notice. MCA 70-24-441(2) lets either the landlord or the tenant give that notice "at any time during the tenancy." The notice names the end date.

Other points from the statute:

- **Week-to-week.** At least 7 days' written notice before the end date (70-24-441(1)).
- **No need to match the rent date.** The tenancy ends on the named date, not at the end of a rent period. Rent is apportioned day by day unless the parties agreed otherwise (70-24-441(3)).
- **Delivery.** Hand delivery, certified mail or a certificate of mailing, or email to an address in the lease (70-24-108). Email notice is complete only on a read receipt or a non-automatic reply.

A landlord may not use a rent increase to punish a tenant. MCA 70-24-431 bars a rent increase after the tenant complains to a housing agency, complains in writing about a repair duty, or joins a tenant union. A complaint within 6 months before the increase creates a rebuttable presumption of retaliation, unless the complaint came after notice of the increase.

## Do cities in Montana add their own rent or notice rules?

No. Montana cities cannot add rent or notice rules for private rentals. MCA 7-1-111(13)(b) denies self-government units any power to "deviate from or add to the exclusive application" of the Residential Landlord and Tenant Act and the security deposit law. MCA 7-1-111(26) separately denies rent control.

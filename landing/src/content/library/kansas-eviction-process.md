Kansas splits its eviction rules between 2 parts of the statutes. I read both on the Kansas Revisor of Statutes site on 2026-10-19. The Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (K.S.A. 58-2540 and on) sets the notices. Chapter 61, article 38 (K.S.A. 61-3801 to 61-3808) sets the court case.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice for unpaid rent | 3 days (3 x 24 hours); +2 days if mailed | K.S.A. 58-2564(b) |
| Notice for a lease breach | 14 days to fix; lease ends 30+ days after receipt | K.S.A. 58-2564(a) |
| Same breach again | 30 days' notice, no cure right | K.S.A. 58-2564(a) |
| Notice to leave before filing | 3 days; +2 days if mailed | K.S.A. 61-3803 |
| Month-to-month tenancy | 30 days, ending on a rent-paying date | K.S.A. 58-2570(b) |
| Appearance date in summons | 3 to 14 days after the summons issues | K.S.A. 61-3805 |
| Trial, if needed | Within 14 days after the appearance date | K.S.A. 61-3807 |
| Writ of restitution | Executed within 14 days after the officer gets it | K.S.A. 61-3808 |
| Lockout or utility cutoff | Banned; up to 1.5 months' rent or damages | K.S.A. 58-2563, 58-2569 |

## What notice must a Kansas landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

A Kansas landlord gives a written 3-day notice for unpaid rent. K.S.A. 58-2564(b) lets the landlord end the agreement if the tenant does not pay "within three days" after written notice of nonpayment and of the intent to terminate.

The statute counts the 3 days as "three consecutive 24-hour periods." The clock starts when you deliver or post the notice:

- **Hand it** to the tenant or to a person over 12 who lives there.
- **Post it** in a conspicuous place on the unit.
- **Mail it.** Allow 2 more days from the mailing date.

K.S.A. 61-3803 also requires a notice to leave the premises at least 3 days before you file. It says this notice "may be combined" with a notice under the landlord and tenant act. One combined notice saves time. Weekends and holidays count in the 3 days under 61-3803.

## How long does an eviction take in Kansas?

A Kansas eviction for unpaid rent can take from under 2 weeks to about 6 weeks, by the statute clocks. Here is a worked example:

| Step | Days | Running total |
|---|---|---|
| 3-day notice, posted | 3 | 3 |
| File petition; summons sets appearance | 3 to 14 | 6 to 17 |
| Trial if the tenant contests | Within 14 more | up to 31 |
| Writ executed after officer gets it | Within 14 | up to 45 |

Most uncontested cases end at the appearance date, so the real time is often shorter. A tenant who asks for a continuance must post a bond for rent and damages (K.S.A. 61-3807(b)).

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Kansas, and what does it cost?

A Kansas landlord files in the district court, under the code of civil procedure for limited actions (K.S.A. 61-3801 to 61-3808). File in the county where the unit is.

The petition must describe the unit and say why you seek possession (K.S.A. 61-3804). You may add a claim for rent due, or sue for the rent later in a separate case.

The docket fee is not in the article 38 sections we read. Ask the district court clerk for the current fee before you file. The sheriff's fee to serve the summons and the writ is also extra.

## Can a Kansas landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes to both, with the right notice.

**Lease violation.** Under K.S.A. 58-2564(a), send a written notice that names the breach. The lease ends on a date at least 30 days after receipt if the tenant does not fix the breach within 14 days. If the tenant starts a good-faith fix in time, the lease does not end. If the same or a similar breach happens again later, a new 30-day notice ends the lease with no chance to fix.

**No cause.** For a month-to-month tenancy, the notice must name a rent-paying date at least 30 days after the tenant receives it (K.S.A. 58-2570(b)). For week-to-week, the notice is 7 days (K.S.A. 58-2570(a)). For a fixed term, the lease ends on its date.

**Holdover.** If the tenant stays after the lease ends, the landlord may file for possession. If the holdover is willful and not in good faith, the landlord may recover up to 1.5 months' rent or 1.5 times actual damages, whichever is greater (K.S.A. 58-2570(c)).

**Retaliation.** K.S.A. 58-2572 bars retaliation after a code complaint, a repair complaint or tenant union activity. The landlord may still file if the tenant is in default in rent.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Kansas?

The person named in the writ of restitution removes the tenant, most often the sheriff. If the court enters judgment for possession, it issues the writ at the landlord's request (K.S.A. 61-3808(a)).

The writ "shall be executed within 14 days" after the person named in it receives it (K.S.A. 61-3808(b)). That person may use "such reasonable force as is necessary." If an appeal stays the case, the officer must stop at once (K.S.A. 61-3808(c)).

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Kansas?

No. K.S.A. 58-2569 says a landlord may not take possession "by action or otherwise, including willful diminution of services" such as electric, gas or water. The only exceptions are abandonment, surrender, or what the act allows.

If you lock out a tenant or cut a utility, the tenant may get back in or end the lease. In either case, the tenant can recover the greater of 1.5 months' rent or actual damages (K.S.A. 58-2563). On $1,000 rent, that is at least $1,500.

## Kansas eviction checklist

- Serve a 3-day pay notice, combined with the 61-3803 notice to leave.
- Add 2 days if you mail it.
- For a breach, give 14 days to fix and 30 days to end.
- File the petition in district court with a clear description of the unit.
- Attend the appearance date, 3 to 14 days after the summons.
- Ask for the writ and let the sheriff execute it.
- Never lock out or cut utilities.

Illinois has banned local rent control since 1997. The Rent Control Preemption Act, 50 ILCS 825, came from Public Act 90-313. I read it and the notice sections of the Eviction Act on the Illinois General Assembly site on 2026-10-20. The planner's note named 735 ILCS 5/9-207 for the month-to-month notice. That is correct: 30 days, in subsection (b).

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| State cap on rent increases | None in the statutes I read | 50 ILCS 825; 735 ILCS 5 Art. IX |
| Local rent control | Barred for all local governments | 50 ILCS 825/5 |
| Home rule units (such as Chicago) | Also barred from controlling rent | 50 ILCS 825/10 |
| Separate rent increase notice | None in the state statutes I read | Not set by statute |

## Does Illinois have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

Illinois has no cap on rent increases and no local rent control. The Rent Control Preemption Act sets 2 rules:

- **All local governments.** 50 ILCS 825/5(a) says a unit of local government "shall not enact, maintain, or enforce an ordinance or resolution that would have the effect of controlling the amount of rent charged for leasing private residential or commercial property".
- **Home rule units.** 50 ILCS 825/10 says a home rule unit "may not regulate or control the amount of rent". It expressly limits home rule powers under the Illinois Constitution.

One power stays local. A local government can still manage residential property in which it has a property interest (825/5(b)).

## How many days notice does an Illinois landlord need to raise the rent?

Illinois state statutes set no separate notice period for a rent increase. I read Article IX of the Code of Civil Procedure and the Landlord and Tenant Act (765 ILCS 705) for this article and found none.

In practice, the notice period comes from the rules to end a tenancy. A landlord who wants new rent on a month-to-month unit gives 30 days' written notice under 735 ILCS 5/9-207(b). The tenant can then accept the new rent or move out.

Worked example: rent is due on the 1st. You want the new rent to start on September 1. Serve written notice by August 1 at the latest. If you mail it, use certified or registered mail with a return receipt (9-211), and mail it earlier so it arrives in time.

Local ordinances may add notice rules. Chicago's chapter 5-12 is the main one. I read only its section list, so I make no claim here about its notice periods. Check the full text before a rent change in Chicago.

## Can an Illinois landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

An Illinois landlord can raise the rent during a fixed-term lease only if the lease allows it. No Illinois statute gives a landlord the right to change rent during the term. The lease binds both sides until it ends.

At the end of the term, the landlord can offer a renewal at a new rent. If the tenant holds over without a special agreement, 9-207 applies: 30 days' written notice for a term under 1 year, 7 days for week to week. A year-to-year tenancy needs 60 days under 9-205.

Write a clear rent change clause if you want the option during the term. A clause that names the new amount and the notice period is easier to enforce than a vague one.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Illinois?

An Illinois month-to-month tenancy ends with 30 days' written notice from the landlord (735 ILCS 5/9-207(b)). The section covers "any term less than one year, other than tenancy from week to week". After the 30 days, the landlord may file an eviction or ejectment action.

| Tenancy type | Notice | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Week to week | 7 days | 9-207(a) |
| Month to month and other terms under 1 year | 30 days | 9-207(b) |
| Year to year | 60 days, at the end of the year | 9-205 |
| Lease on a unit in foreclosure, taken over by a lender or buyer | 90 days | 9-207.5 |

State law has no just-cause rule for these tenancies. Local ordinances can add rules. Read them for each city where you manage.

## Do cities in Illinois add their own rent or notice rules?

Illinois cities can add their own landlord-tenant rules, but not rent control. Chicago's Residential Landlord and Tenant Ordinance, Municipal Code chapter 5-12, is the best-known example. Its section list includes:

- 5-12-080 Security deposits, 5-12-081 interest rate, 5-12-082 interest rate notification.
- 5-12-130 Landlord remedies.
- 5-12-140 Rental agreement.
- 5-12-150 Prohibition on retaliatory conduct by landlord.
- 5-12-160 Prohibition on interruption of tenant occupancy by landlord.
- 5-12-170 Summary of ordinance attached to rental agreement.

None of these can cap the amount of rent, because 50 ILCS 825/10 bars home rule units from that. Other Illinois cities may have their own ordinances. Check each one with a local attorney.

## Rent increase checklist for Illinois managers

1. **Check the lease type.** Fixed term or periodic.
2. **Read the lease** for a rent change clause.
3. **Count back 30 days** from the new rent date for month to month.
4. **Serve in writing** by a method in 9-211.
5. **Check the city.** In Chicago, read chapter 5-12 first.
6. **Treat tenants alike.** Apply the same rule to every tenant in the same position.

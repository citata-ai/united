Owners ask the same questions before they hire a manager. What should my home rent for? How long will it sit empty? Is the market getting weaker? Property Manager Websites lists these exact questions in a June 2026 post on market data. A monthly report answers them before the owner calls you. And it answers them with numbers only you have.

## Which data goes into a monthly rental market report?

A useful rental market report has three layers: your own data, one public benchmark and a short meaning for owners. Your own data is the part no rival can copy.

| Layer | Metric | Source | Why owners care |
|---|---|---|---|
| Your data | Median signed rent, by bedroom count and area | Your property management software | What their home could rent for |
| Your data | Median days on market | Listing date to lease date | How long rent stops |
| Your data | Active listings at month end | Your listings | Competition for renters |
| Your data | Share of listings with a price cut | Your listings | Is pricing too high |
| Your data | Renewal rate | Your leases | How often tenants stay |
| Public benchmark | Typical market rent and its trend | Zillow Observed Rent Index | Is the area going up or down |
| Public benchmark | Government rent baseline | HUD Fair Market Rents | Voucher rents, a floor check |
| Meaning | 3 short points for owners | You | What to do now |

Keep it to one page. Owners skim.

### Worked example: one report row

Last month you signed 14 three-bedroom leases in one area. You sort the 14 rents and take the middle (median): $2,050. The same month last year: $1,975.

- Change: ($2,050 − $1,975) ÷ $1,975 = +3.8%.
- Median days on market: 21, down from 26.
- Meaning for owners: "3-bed homes leased faster and for 3.8% more than last October. Price within 2% of our estimate to lease in about 3 weeks."

Use the median, not the average. One luxury lease can pull an average far from the typical rent. If a row has fewer than 5 leases, merge months or areas before you publish it.

## Where can I get local rent data?

Your property management software is the first source. Export last month's leases and listings, with dates and rents. Then add one or two free public sources:

1. **Zillow Observed Rent Index (ZORI).** Zillow Research describes it as "a smoothed measure of the typical observed market rate rent across a given region." It is published for all homes, single-family and multi-family. The CSV files update monthly.
2. **HUD Fair Market Rents.** A yearly government rent level by metro and bedroom count. See our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) for FY2027 numbers.
3. **Census American Community Survey.** Table B25064 gives median gross rent. The 2024 1-year estimate for the US is $1,487 (±$3). It is yearly and lags, so use it for context, not for this month.
4. **BLS rent index.** The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes "rent of primary residence" each month for each area with CPI data. It shows trend, not dollar rent.

Name each source and its date in the report. Owners trust a number more when they can check it.

## How do I share the report?

Share each report in three places, from one master version.

- **Web page.** Publish the full report on your site at a stable URL, with the month in the title. A web page can rank in search and can be cited by AI answers. Keep past months as an archive.
- **Email.** Send a short summary to owners and owner leads who opted in. Link to the web page for details.
- **Social and Google Business Profile.** Post one chart or one number with a link.

Email has legal rules. The FTC's CAN-SPAM compliance guide requires:

- [ ] A subject line that matches the content
- [ ] Your valid physical postal address in the email
- [ ] A clear way to opt out of future marketing email
- [ ] Opt-outs honored within 10 business days
- [ ] Opt-out link that works for at least 30 days after sending

The FTC says each violating email can cost up to $53,088. Most email tools handle the address and the opt-out link if you set them up.

Fair Housing applies too. Describe homes and markets, never the people who live in an area. Do not suggest who "fits" a neighborhood.

## How do I turn report readers into owner leads?

End each report with one offer that follows from the data. The best offer is a free rental analysis for the reader's own address. The reader just saw your area numbers and now wants their own.

Step list:

1. **One call to action.** "Get a rental analysis for your home." One button, at the top and the end.
2. **Short form.** Address, beds, baths, email. Nothing more.
3. **Fast reply.** Send the analysis within 1 business day, signed by a person.
4. **Tag every link.** Use UTM tags (tracking labels in a link) such as `utm_source=market-report` so Google Analytics shows report leads as their own source.
5. **Count results each month.** Readers, clicks, analysis requests, calls, signed owners.

| Month | Email opens | Web visits | Analysis requests | Signed owners |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Example | 420 | 310 | 6 | 1 |

## How much notice is required for non-renewal?

The notice required for non-renewal is set by your state law and your lease, and you must meet the longer of the two. State periods depend on the kind of tenancy and, in some states, on how long the tenant has lived there.

| State | Situation | Landlord notice | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Florida | Month-to-month | At least 30 days before the end of a period | Fla. Stat. 83.57 |
| Florida | Week-to-week | At least 7 days | Fla. Stat. 83.57 |
| Florida | Year-to-year | At least 60 days | Fla. Stat. 83.57 |
| California | Periodic, tenant there 1 year or more | At least 60 days | Cal. Civ. Code 1946.1 |
| California | Periodic, tenant there under 1 year | At least 30 days | Cal. Civ. Code 1946.1 |
| Oregon | First year of occupancy, no cause | At least 30 days | ORS 90.427 |
| Oregon | After first year, qualifying landlord reason | At least 90 days, plus 1 month's rent in most cases | ORS 90.427 |

Florida 83.57 covers tenancies with no fixed end. For a fixed-term lease in Florida, read the lease for its notice clause.

Watch for: a fixed-term lease can turn into a month-to-month tenancy when it ends. In Oregon, a fixed term that ends after the first year becomes month-to-month unless a new term is signed, the tenant leaves, or the landlord has a qualifying reason (ORS 90.427). So "the lease just ends" is not always true.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Do you have to give a reason?

You have to give a reason in some states, and you must never use an illegal one. In states without a just-cause law, a landlord can often decline to renew with proper notice. In states with one, the reason is required and goes in the notice.

**California.** After a tenant has lawfully lived in a unit for 12 months, the Tenant Protection Act (Civ. Code 1946.2) requires just cause to end the tenancy for covered units. At-fault causes include nonpayment and material lease breaches. No-fault causes include owner move-in, withdrawing the unit from the market, and substantial remodeling. The notice must state the cause. No-fault cases need relocation help of 1 month's rent, or a written waiver of the last month's rent. The statute has exemptions, for example some single-family homes owned by individuals when the tenant got the required exemption notice, and housing with a certificate of occupancy issued within 15 years. The section is in effect until January 1, 2030.

**Oregon.** After the first year, ORS 90.427 allows termination only for a tenant cause or a qualifying landlord reason. Qualifying reasons include demolition, major repairs that make the unit unsafe to live in, owner or family move-in, and sale to a buyer who will live there. The notice must state the reason and facts and give at least 90 days. The landlord must also pay 1 month's rent, unless the landlord owns an interest in 4 or fewer units.

**Every state.** The federal Fair Housing Act bars refusing to rent because of race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status or national origin (42 U.S.C. 3604). Non-renewal is part of renting. Many states also ban retaliation. California bars a landlord from ending a tenancy, raising rent or cutting services for 180 days after a good-faith repair complaint, in most cases (Civ. Code 1942.5).

What to do: write down the business reason in your file before you send the notice, even in a state that does not require one in the letter. Use the same rules for every tenant.

## What move-out steps should the notice list?

The notice should list every step the tenant must take to move out and get the deposit back. Clear steps cut disputes at the end.

- [ ] The exact date and time to return possession
- [ ] Where and how to return all keys, fobs, remotes and parking passes
- [ ] A request for a forwarding address in writing
- [ ] The cleaning standard and the move-out checklist
- [ ] How to book a pre-move-out inspection, if your state gives that right
- [ ] Utility transfer: which accounts the tenant must close
- [ ] The deposit return deadline under your state law
- [ ] Who to contact with questions

California (Civ. Code 1950.5) and New York (Gen. Oblig. Law 7-108) both give tenants a right to a pre-move-out inspection. If you manage there, put the request steps in the notice.

## Notice of non-renewal template

Copy this text, fill in the brackets, and check it against your state and city law before use.

> **Notice of Non-Renewal of Lease**
>
> Date: [date]
> To: [all tenant names on the lease]
> Address: [street, unit, city, state, ZIP]
>
> Your lease for the address above [ends on (date) / is a month-to-month tenancy]. This letter is written notice that [landlord or company name] will not renew or extend it. Your tenancy will end at 11:59 p.m. on **[termination date]**. This date gives you at least [number] days' notice, as [state statute and lease section] require.
>
> [If your state requires a reason:] The reason for this notice is: [state the legal cause and facts, for example: "The owner, (name), will move into the unit as a primary residence."]
>
> [If relocation help applies:] You are entitled to relocation help of [amount]. We will [pay it by (date) / waive rent for (month)] as the law requires.
>
> **Before you move out, please:**
>
> 1. Return all keys, fobs and remotes to [place] by [date and time].
> 2. Send us your forwarding address in writing.
> 3. Clean the unit to the standard in the attached move-out checklist.
> 4. Contact us to book a pre-move-out inspection, if you want one. [Add state-required wording.]
> 5. Close or transfer utilities in your name as of [date].
>
> We will return your deposit, or send an itemized statement of deductions, within [number] days after you move out, as [state statute] requires.
>
> Thank you for renting with us. Contact [name] at [phone] or [email] with any questions.
>
> [Name], [title], [company]
>
> Delivery method: [hand / certified mail / posted and mailed] on [date] by [name]

## How do you send it and keep proof?

Send the notice in a way your state allows and keep proof of the date. California 1946.1 allows service under Code of Civil Procedure 1162 or by certified or registered mail, and bans a fee for delivering it. Florida 83.57 refers to the delivery methods in 83.56(4).

Worked example: a California tenant has lived in a covered unit for 3 years on a month-to-month tenancy. The owner's daughter will move in. You need at least 60 days' notice, a statement of the cause with the daughter's name and relationship, and 1 month's rent in relocation help or a waiver of the last month. If rent is $2,400, plan for $2,400 in relocation cost.

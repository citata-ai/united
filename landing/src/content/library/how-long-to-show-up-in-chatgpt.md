## What changes show up fast?

Changes to access and to listing data show up fast. These are the fixes where you control the switch, and the engine picks them up on its next visit.

| Change | How fast | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Allow OAI-SearchBot in robots.txt | About 24 hours for ChatGPT search to adjust | OpenAI crawler docs |
| Edit Google Business Profile | Usually up to 10 minutes to review, sometimes up to 30 days | Google Business Profile Help |
| Tell Bing about a changed URL with IndexNow | Sent at once; no indexing timeline promised | IndexNow FAQ |
| Ask Google to recrawl a page | A few days to a few weeks | Google Search Central |

The first row is the most common quick win. Check it first: a security plugin or a CDN bot rule can block AI crawlers without your team knowing. OpenAI states that sites that block OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers. If you fix that block, ChatGPT can start to read your pages within a day.

The second row matters for local answers. Google says Business Profile data feeds its wider search experience. A wrong phone or an old address there can reach AI answers. Fix it once, and the fix flows out.

Note the limit on IndexNow. The IndexNow FAQ says "Submitting a URL does not guarantee immediate indexing." It tells Bing and other engines that a page changed. They still decide when to crawl.

## What takes months?

The slow parts are the ones other people control: reviews, mentions and editorial lists. AI answers about property managers cite those sources often. In our [AI Property Manager Index](/research/ai-property-manager-index-2026), Expertise.com was a source in 93 of 150 answers, and Yelp and iPropertyManagement in 50 each (our data, 2026-10).

You cannot speed up an editor. You can only make it easy for them to include you and give them correct facts. Here is what to expect:

- **Owner reviews.** You earn them one owner at a time. A steady ask after each good event (a lease signed, a repair closed) builds a review base over months.
- **Directory and best-of lists.** Some update on a fixed cycle. iPropertyManagement's Chicago page, for example, showed "Last Updated: August 7, 2026" when we read it.
- **Mentions in local press and forums.** These come from real work: a quote in a local news story, a helpful answer in a Reddit thread.
- **New pages ranking for sub-questions.** A page must be crawled, indexed and then chosen over other pages. That can take weeks to months.

## How does it compare to SEO timelines?

AI search and SEO share the slow parts, so the timelines overlap. Goodjuju Marketing, a property management agency, gives these SEO ranges. They come from its own client work, not from an audited study:

| Starting point | Goodjuju's estimate for SEO |
|---|---|
| Decent site foundation | Real movement in about 2–4 months |
| Low baseline | About 6–10 months |
| Competitive metro | About 12–18 months |

AI search adds one fast lane on top. Listing fixes and crawl access can change AI answers before rankings move. But it adds no shortcut for trust. If no other site names you, an AI engine has little reason to name you either.

So plan for both clocks. Expect the fast fixes to register in days or weeks. Expect the mention and review work to take a season or more.

## How do you know it is working?

You know it is working when the share of AI answers that name you goes up over several months. One answer means little. SparkToro found under a 1 in 100 chance that ChatGPT or Google's AI gives the same brand list twice (published January 28, 2026). Use shares, not ranks.

Track these 4 signals each month:

1. **Prompt share.** Run the same 20 owner prompts in ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude. Count the answers that name you.
2. **Citations.** Bing Webmaster Tools has an AI Performance report. It shows how often Copilot and Bing AI summaries cite your pages and the "grounding queries" behind them.
3. **AI visits.** Count visits from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai and gemini.google.com in your analytics.
4. **Lead source.** Ask "How did you hear about us?" on your owner form, with "ChatGPT or another AI" as a choice.

Start with a baseline before you change anything. We did this for our own site. On 2026-10-18, we ran 20 prompts in ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, twice each. citata.ai was named in 0 of 108 non-brand answers. That is our point A. Every later report compares to it.

## What does a realistic first 90 days look like?

A realistic first 90 days fixes the fast items in month 1 and starts the slow ones at the same time. This plan is a hypothesis built from the timelines above, not a promise:

| Month | Work | First signs to look for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline prompt run. Fix robots.txt, Business Profile, Yelp, Expertise.com and iPropertyManagement facts. | Correct facts in AI answers that already name you |
| 2 | Publish city pages and a full fee page. Start owner review asks. | Pages indexed in Google and Bing; first AI crawler visits in logs |
| 3 | Second prompt run. Reply to reviews. Ask local list editors to check your data. | First citations of your own pages; change in prompt share |

If month 3 shows no change in prompt share, check the basics again. Is the page indexed? Is the fact on the page in plain text? Does any other site name you?

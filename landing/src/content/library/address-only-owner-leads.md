## What is an address-only owner lead?

An address-only owner lead is a form entry with a street, city and ZIP code, and nothing else. It comes most often from a rental analysis widget: the owner wants to know what the house would rent for, but does not want a sales call yet.

Many managers delete these entries. That is a mistake. Property Manager Websites (PMW) reports that across 100 of its customers, 47% of 2,147 address-only entries turned into full contact leads after follow-up (PMW, 2025-08-18). PMW sells the tool that captures these leads, so treat the number as vendor data. Still, it shows the order of size: about half of these people will talk to you if you reach them well.

The address tells you a lot already. It gives you the property type, the size, the tax record, and the owner of record. That is more than many full leads give you.

## How do I find the owner from an address?

Start with free public records, then pay for data only if you need it. Use this order.

| Step | Source | What you get | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | County assessor parcel search | Owner of record, mailing address, parcel number, building facts | Free |
| 2 | County recorder or clerk | Deed, sale date, LLC or trust name | Free or small fee per copy |
| 3 | State business registry (if an LLC owns it) | Registered agent and manager names | Free |
| 4 | Skip trace vendor | Phone numbers and emails tied to the owner | Paid per record |
| 5 | Your own CRM | Past contact with the same owner or address | Free |

Most county assessors have an address search. For example, the Maricopa County Assessor in Arizona lets anyone search parcels and addresses, and it administers over 1.8 million parcels and accounts (Maricopa County Assessor site). Look for the mailing address field. If the mailing address is not the property address, the owner is likely an absentee owner. That is your best prospect.

Watch for: some states let people hide their names in assessor and recorder records. The Maricopa site lists a 2026 Arizona change that lets eligible people ask for broader limits on their identifying information. If the record is hidden, mail to the property and stop there.

## Is skip tracing legal for this?

Skip tracing (buying contact data tied to a name) is legal, but what you do with the number has rules. Three federal rules matter most. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

| Rule | What it says | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| FTC Telemarketing Sales Rule, Do Not Call | Sellers must not call numbers on the National Do Not Call Registry, and must update call lists at least every 31 days | Scrub every skip-traced number before you call |
| TSR inquiry exception | An inquiry creates a business relationship for 3 months from the date of the inquiry | The owner asked for a rental analysis, so a live return call within 3 months is likely covered, if you can show the inquiry |
| FCC robotext and robocall rules | Autodialed or prerecorded calls and texts to a cell need consent; commercial texts need written consent | Do not put a skip-traced number into an automated text drip |
| Driver's Privacy Protection Act, 18 U.S.C. 2721(b)(12) | Bulk use of motor vehicle records for marketing needs the person's express consent | Do not use any data product built from DMV records for outreach |

The FTC sets civil penalties of up to $53,088 for each Do Not Call violation (FTC TSR guide). States add their own rules. Some states keep a separate do-not-call list or limit call hours, so check your state attorney general's site.

What to do: keep a record of the form entry with its date and time. That record is your proof of the inquiry. Without it, the 3-month exception does not help you.

## What first message works?

The first message should give the owner what they asked for: a rent number. Do not lead with your fees or your company story.

Use this checklist for the first touch:

- **Mail a one-page rental analysis.** Send it to the property and to the owner's mailing address from the assessor record. Include a rent range, 3 comps and a QR code to the full report.
- **Name the address, not the person.** Write "Rent estimate for 1420 W Elm St." The owner did not give a name, so do not act as if they did.
- **Say how you got the address.** One line: "You asked for a rent estimate on our site on October 12."
- **Give one next step.** "Call or text this number for the full report" works better than five options.
- **Add a tracked phone number.** A call-tracking number tells you which letters worked.
- **Offer a way out.** "Reply STOP or call to be removed." This builds trust and keeps your list clean.

If you do get a phone number, make the first call a live call, not a robocall. Keep it short: "You asked for a rent estimate on our site. I have it ready. Where should I send it?"

## How fast should I respond?

Respond the same day, and call within minutes when you have a number. All Property Management (APM), a lead marketplace owned by Buildium, tells its paying managers that "the property owner should receive a phone call within minutes" and that a marketing package should go out within an hour (APM best practices page). APM also says the first company to reach a lead often becomes the default choice.

An address-only lead moves slower than a full lead. PMW suggests a separate track for 60 to 90 days or more. Here is a simple timeline you can copy:

| Day | Action | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Look up owner; send letter with rent range | Mail |
| 0 | If a number exists and is not on the DNC list: one live call | Phone |
| 7 | Second letter with the full report link | Mail |
| 14 | Retargeting ad to site visitors, if you run them | Ads |
| 30 | Short postcard with one new comp | Mail |
| 60 | Final letter: "Still thinking about renting it out?" | Mail |
| 90 | Move to a quarterly newsletter if they opted in | Email |

## How do I measure this lead source?

Count address-only leads as their own source in your CRM. Track four numbers each month: entries, owners found, owners who replied, and signed doors. If you sign 1 door from 30 entries, you know what the source is worth.

Compare it with your other sources. Goodjuju's fall 2026 lead source report (35 companies, 2,525 leads) shows Google search and the Map Pack bring about half of all form leads. Your rental analysis form likely sits on those pages, so address-only leads are part of your search results too.

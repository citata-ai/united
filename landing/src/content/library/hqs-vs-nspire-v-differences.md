## What does NSPIRE-V add that HQS did not have?

NSPIRE adds specific safety items and a severity scale. HQS, the old rule at 24 CFR 982.401, set general performance requirements for 13 areas, such as sanitary facilities and thermal environment. HUD's 2023 standards notice (88 FR 40832) says HQS was "developed in the 1970s." NSPIRE, at 24 CFR 5.703, names exact items instead.

The voucher version of NSPIRE is often called NSPIRE-V. Here are the main differences, from the 2023 text of 982.401 and the current 5.703:

| Topic | HQS (982.401, 2023 text) | NSPIRE (5.703) |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | 13 areas, each with performance and acceptability criteria | Inside, outside and unit, plus health and safety hazards |
| Smoke alarms | One on each level, including basements | Each level, each bedroom, within 21 feet of bedroom doors, and on the living-area side of a separating door |
| Carbon monoxide | Unit "free from dangerous levels" of CO | Must meet HUD's CO detection standard (2018 International Fire Code) |
| GFCI outlets | No GFCI rule in the text | Required within 6 feet of a water source, inside and outside |
| Guardrails | General "danger of tripping and falling" rule for stairs and porches | Required at any drop of 30 inches or more |
| Outlets | 2 in the living room and each bedroom; a fixed light can count as 1 | 2 outlets, or 1 outlet and a permanent light, in all habitable rooms |
| Heat | A safe heating system adequate for the climate | A permanent heat source in climate zones HUD names |
| Severity | Pass or fail; the 2017 HOTMA list flags life-threatening items | Each deficiency rated life-threatening, severe, moderate or low (5.705(a)(1)) |
| Local codes | HUD-approved variations | Local codes are not part of the voucher pass decision, except approved variations (5.703(f)(3)) |

## Are there new outside and site items?

Yes, but for voucher units the outside scope is limited. HQS had a "site and neighborhood" standard. It covered dangers like flooding, sewage hazards and excessive trash. NSPIRE has an "outside" area. It covers the building site and exterior parts: fencing, walkways, lighting, roofs, walls, windows, fire escapes and parking lots.

For voucher units, 24 CFR 5.703(a) limits the check to:

- the unit itself;
- the way out from the unit's entry door to the public way;
- common features related to living there, such as a laundry room or mail room;
- the systems equipment that serves the unit.

The outside area must have GFCI outlets within 6 feet of water and guardrails at drops of 30 inches or more (5.703(c)). HUD's 2023 notice also raised a missing unit entry door to life-threatening.

## Do pass and fail rules change?

The scoring language changes, but the repair clocks for vouchers stay the same. NSPIRE rates each deficiency in one of four levels under 24 CFR 5.705(a)(1). HUD's 2023 notice renamed the old levels: "Severe Non-Life-Threatening" became "Severe," "Standard" became "Moderate," and "Advisory" became "Low."

For voucher units, the regulation still sets two clocks (24 CFR 982.404(a)(3)):

| Deficiency | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Life-threatening | 24 hours from notice |
| All others | 30 days, or a PHA-approved extension |

HUD's notice says NSPIRE marks the voucher life-threatening items "with a 24-hour HCV Correction Timeframe." It also keeps the 2017 HOTMA life-threatening list. A PHA may add items with HUD approval, but it cannot remove HUD's.

## Which rules stay the same?

Most of the core stays. Both rules require these items:

- [ ] A bathroom usable in privacy, with a flush toilet, a sink and a tub or shower.
- [ ] Hot and cold running water in the kitchen and bathroom.
- [ ] A kitchen area with a sink, a cooking appliance and a refrigerator.
- [ ] A permanent light fixture in the kitchen and each bathroom.
- [ ] At least one bedroom or living/sleeping room for each two people.
- [ ] No unvented space heaters that burn gas, oil or kerosene.
- [ ] Compliance with the federal lead-based paint rules in 24 CFR part 35.
- [ ] A unit free of pests and serious structural defects.

The inspection schedule also stays. Voucher units are inspected at least every two years, and every three years for small rural PHAs (24 CFR 982.405(b), referenced in 5.705(c)(5)). HUD can still approve local variations under 5.705(a)(3).

## What should I fix before my first NSPIRE-V inspection?

Fix the items that are new or more specific. Two of them already apply. HUD's July 2024 notice says the smoke alarm and CO standards apply now. They carry out laws passed in 2021 and 2023. NLIHC's summary of that notice says the same.

Use this list, in order of risk:

1. **Smoke alarms.** Add one in every bedroom and within 21 feet of each bedroom door.
2. **CO alarms.** Install them if there is a fuel-burning appliance, a fireplace or an attached garage.
3. **GFCI outlets.** Replace any outlet within 6 feet of a sink, tub or other water source.
4. **Guardrails.** Add or repair rails at porches, decks, lofts and stairs with a drop of 30 inches or more.
5. **Outlets.** Make sure every habitable room has 2 working outlets, or 1 and a permanent light.
6. **Entry door.** Make sure the unit entry door is in place and works.
7. **Egress.** Clear exits, fire escapes and bedroom windows used as exits.

Ask your PHA when it switches. HUD's September 2025 notice sets February 1, 2027 as the latest date, and lets PHAs switch earlier.

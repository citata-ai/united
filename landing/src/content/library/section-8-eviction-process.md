## What is "good cause" under the HAP contract?

Good cause is the federal limit on ending a voucher tenancy during the lease. 42 U.S.C. 1437f(o)(7)(C) and 24 CFR 982.310(a) allow three grounds only:

1. **Serious or repeated lease violations.** The rule names "failure to pay rent or other amounts due under the lease" as an example.
2. **Violation of law.** A federal, state or local law that puts duties on the tenant about the use of the unit.
3. **Other good cause.** Defined by examples in 982.310(d).

The examples of other good cause depend on the lease term:

| Example of other good cause (982.310(d)) | First lease term | After the first term |
|---|---|---|
| Disturbance of neighbors, property damage, or housekeeping that damages the unit | Yes | Yes |
| Family refuses a new lease or revision | No | Yes |
| Owner wants the unit for personal or family use | No | Yes |
| Business or economic reason: sale, renovation, higher rent | No | Yes |

In the first term, other good cause "must be something the family did or failed to do" (tenancy addendum, section 8(d)).

Criminal activity is a separate ground. The lease must allow eviction for drug-related criminal activity on or near the premises, and for criminal activity that threatens other residents or neighbors (982.310(c)). You do not need an arrest or conviction. You must decide the person did it (982.310(c)(3)).

Two protections limit you. Under 982.310(h)(4), any eviction must follow fair housing rules and the Violence Against Women Act protections in 24 CFR part 5, subpart L. The addendum says an incident of domestic violence against a victim is not good cause to evict that victim.

## Must I notify the PHA when I file?

Yes. Federal rules add two notice duties on top of your state's notices:

- [ ] **Notice of grounds to the tenant.** A written notice that names the grounds, at or before the start of the eviction case (982.310(e)(1)). You may combine it with your state eviction notice.
- [ ] **Copy to the PHA.** The owner "must give the PHA a copy of any owner eviction notice" (982.310(e)(2)). The addendum says at the same time you notify the tenant.
- [ ] **Court action only.** "The owner may only evict the tenant from the unit by instituting a court action" (982.310(f)). No lockouts.

An "eviction notice" means a notice to vacate, or the complaint or first filing in court (982.310(e)(2)(i)). So send the PHA a copy at each stage. PHAs ask for this too. Tacoma Housing Authority: "Any notices to the tenant should be shared with THA." Santa Cruz County asks owners to "send copies of any notices or lease violations to the Housing Authority."

## Can I evict for the PHA's late payment?

No. 24 CFR 982.310(b) says the family "is not responsible for payment of the portion of the rent to owner covered by the housing assistance payment." A PHA's failure to pay "is not a violation of the lease." You "may not terminate the tenancy of the family for nonpayment of the PHA housing assistance payment."

The same applies to abatement after a failed inspection. Under 24 CFR 982.404(d)(3), an owner may not end the tenancy because the PHA withholds or abates payments.

You can evict for the tenant's own unpaid share. That is a serious lease violation under 982.310(a)(1).

| Unpaid amount | Grounds to evict? |
|---|---|
| Tenant share of rent | Yes, as a lease violation |
| PHA share (HAP), paid late | No |
| PHA share withheld or abated after a failed inspection | No |
| Damage charges the tenant owes under the lease | Yes, if your lease and state law allow |

## What happens to the HAP during eviction?

The PHA keeps paying while the family stays. The rule is 24 CFR 982.311(b). Once the owner "has commenced the process to evict the tenant," and the family still lives there, the PHA "must continue to make housing assistance payments." It pays until the owner has a court judgment or other process that allows the eviction. The PHA may keep paying until the family moves out or is evicted.

Then the money stops. The HAP contract ends when the lease ends or the family moves out. The PHA cannot pay for any month after the move-out month, but you keep the move-out month's payment (982.311(d)).

## Does state eviction law still apply?

Yes. Federal rules add to state law. They do not replace it. 42 U.S.C. 1437f(o)(7)(E) says any termination must follow a written notice of grounds, "and any relief shall be consistent with applicable State and local law." The tenancy addendum adds that its examples of other good cause "do not preempt any State or local laws to the contrary."

So you follow your state's notice periods, court steps and any local just-cause rules. The Santa Cruz County housing authority tells owners to "consult an attorney or apartment association before taking action." It notes that California law can require up to 90 days' notice in some cases.

Use this order of steps:

1. **Check the grounds.** Match the facts to 982.310 and your state law.
2. **Write the notice of grounds.** Name the lease clause or law and the facts.
3. **Serve the tenant and copy the PHA.** Keep proof of both.
4. **File in court** if the tenant does not cure or leave.
5. **Send the PHA the complaint** when you file.
6. **Keep collecting only the tenant share.** The PHA pays its share until judgment.
7. **Tell the PHA the move-out date** as soon as you know it.

You may prefer to leave the program instead. The Santa Cruz County housing authority says a landlord "can leave the program at the end of any lease term." State notice rules still apply.

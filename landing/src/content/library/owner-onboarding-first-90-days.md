## Why do the first 90 days decide if an owner stays?

The first 90 days form the owner's first opinion of your company. Rent Manager cites consultant and professor Michael Watkins on this point: the first quarter with a new client matters most. Clients with a bad first impression are more likely to leave than clients with a good one.

For a property manager, the first impression is the onboarding. A lost key, a late first statement or a surprise invoice in month 1 costs more trust than the same mistake in year 3. So treat onboarding as a fixed process with dates, not as a set of tasks you do when you have time.

Rent Manager lists four things an owner must know at the end of onboarding:

- The next step.
- Who to contact with a question or a problem, and how.
- How much they pay you.
- Which services you give, and when.

The checklist below turns those four points into dated tasks.

## What happens in week 1?

Week 1 is for documents, accounts and access. Do these tasks before you market the unit or take over the tenant.

| Day | Task | Owner gives you | You give the owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Countersign the management agreement | Signed agreement | Copy of the agreement, fee schedule |
| 1 | Send the owner portal invite | Email address | Portal login steps |
| 1–2 | Collect the tax form | IRS Form W-9 | Nothing yet |
| 1–2 | Set up the payout account | Bank details for ACH | Payout date in writing |
| 1–3 | Collect property records | Keys, codes, appliance list, warranties, HOA rules | Key receipt |
| 1–3 | Collect lease and deposit records (if tenant in place) | Current lease, ledger, deposit amount and where it is held | Tenant transfer plan |
| 1–3 | Collect lead records (if built before 1978) | Any lead-based paint reports | Lead disclosure plan |
| 2–5 | Confirm insurance | Landlord policy declarations page | Request to add you as additional insured, if your agreement needs it |
| 3–5 | Do the move-in or takeover inspection | Access | Photo report |
| 5 | First owner call | 20 minutes | Answers to the four Rent Manager points |

The W-9 matters for year-end. The IRS says you use Form W-9 to give your correct taxpayer identification number to the party that must report payments to the IRS. Get it before the first payout, not in January. This is general information, not tax advice. Ask a tax professional.

The lead records matter for older homes. The EPA says that before a renter signs a lease for housing built before 1978, the landlord must disclose known lead-based paint information, give the EPA pamphlet "Protect Your Family From Lead In Your Home", and include a lead warning statement in the lease. The EPA also says agents share this duty with the lessor. Keep the signed disclosures for 3 years after the lease starts. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## When should the first owner call happen?

The first owner call should happen within 3 to 5 business days of the signed agreement. A call, not an email. The goal is to confirm the rules before the first repair or the first statement tests them.

Use the same agenda for each new owner:

1. **Confirm the contact.** Give one name, one email and one phone number. Say when that person answers.
2. **Confirm the repair limit.** Say the dollar amount you may spend without approval. Say what counts as an emergency.
3. **Confirm the money dates.** Say the date of the first statement and the first payout. Say why the first payout can be lower: reserves, prorated rent, or setup costs.
4. **Confirm the goal.** Ask if the owner wants cash flow, long-term value or a sale in 2 to 3 years. Write it in the owner record.
5. **Confirm the next step.** End with one task and one date.

Then plan two more calls. Call 2 comes 2 business days after the first statement. Call 3 comes at day 90. Put both on the calendar during call 1.

## What should the welcome packet include?

The welcome packet should fit on 2 to 4 pages. Rent Manager gives a clear rule: do not overdo it, and tell owners only what they need to know. Rent Manager also suggests a short reference guide with links to every site and form the owner may need later.

Welcome packet checklist:

- [ ] One named contact, with phone, email and office hours
- [ ] Fee schedule in plain words, with one worked example on the owner's own rent
- [ ] Repair approval limit and the emergency rule
- [ ] Statement date, payout date and reserve amount
- [ ] Owner portal login steps, with a screenshot
- [ ] How to report a problem, and the answer time you promise
- [ ] What the owner must keep doing: insurance, HOA dues, property tax (if not paid by you)
- [ ] How to end the agreement, in one sentence, with the notice period

Add the worked fee example because owners compare total cost, not the headline rate. You can build the example with the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## How do I set expectations on repairs and statements?

Set repair and statement rules in writing in week 1, then repeat them on call 1. Rent Manager says accurate expectations are better than high expectations that you do not meet.

Repairs: state the approval limit as a dollar number. State the emergency rule: what you fix at once, at any cost, to stop damage or keep the home safe. State your vendor markup, if any. An owner who learns about a markup from an invoice will not trust the next one.

Statements: state the date, the format and the first-month exceptions. Month 1 often shows a prorated rent, a setup fee and a reserve. Mark each line on the first statement, or call before the owner opens it.

| Topic | Write it as | Not as |
|---|---|---|
| Repair limit | "We fix repairs up to $300 without a call." | "We handle minor repairs." |
| Emergency | "Water leak, no heat, no power, broken lock: we fix at once." | "Urgent issues are handled promptly." |
| Statement | "Statement on the 10th, payout on the 12th." | "Monthly statements are provided." |
| Reserve | "We keep $250 in reserve for repairs." | "A reserve may apply." |

The dollar amounts in this table are examples. Use your own numbers from your management agreement.

## What does a 90-day timeline look like?

A 90-day timeline has 4 checkpoints. Each one has a task you can mark done.

| Checkpoint | Day | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | 1–7 | Agreement, W-9, bank details, keys, records and inspection report are in the system |
| First call | 3–5 | Owner confirmed contact, repair limit, money dates and goal |
| First statement | 30–45 | Statement sent on the promised date, call 2 done, every first-month line explained |
| Review | 90 | Call 3 done: what went well, what to change, a review or referral ask if the owner is happy |

At day 90, ask the owner one question: "What one thing would you change?" Write the answer down. If 3 owners in a row name the same thing, fix the process, not the owner.

## Where does onboarding fit in owner growth?

Onboarding keeps the owners you win. Winning them is a separate job. If owner leads drop, read [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping). A strong onboarding process does not replace marketing, but it gives you happy owners who refer others.

What to do this week: write your welcome packet on 2 pages, put the three owner calls in your calendar template, and test the checklist on your next new owner.

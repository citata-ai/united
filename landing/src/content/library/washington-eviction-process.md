## Washington eviction rules at a glance

Washington calls an eviction an "unlawful detainer" case. The rules sit in chapter 59.12 RCW and the Residential Landlord-Tenant Act, chapter 59.18 RCW. We read each section on the Washington State Legislature site on 2026-10-19.

| Step or rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Just cause | Required for most evictions and non-renewals | RCW 59.18.650(1) |
| Unpaid rent notice | 14 days to pay or vacate, set form | RCW 59.12.030(3), 59.18.057 |
| Lease violation notice | At least 10 days to fix | RCW 59.18.650(2)(b) |
| Waste, nuisance, unlawful activity | 3-day notice to quit | RCW 59.18.650(2)(c) |
| Owner move-in or sale of a single-family home | 90 days' notice | RCW 59.18.650(2)(d)-(e) |
| End of qualifying fixed term | 60 days' notice | RCW 59.18.650(1)(b)-(c) |
| Court | Superior court of the county | RCW 59.18.370 |
| Filing fee | $45 | RCW 36.18.020(2)(a) |
| Show cause hearing | 7 to 30 days after service of the order | RCW 59.18.370 |
| Removal | Sheriff, under a writ of restitution | RCW 59.18.312, 59.18.370 |
| Lockout or utility shutoff | Unlawful | RCW 59.18.290, 59.18.300 |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What notice must a Washington landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

A Washington landlord must serve a 14-day notice to pay or vacate. RCW 59.12.030(3) sets 14 days after service for tenancies under chapter 59.18 RCW. You may serve the notice at any time after the rent is due.

The notice must follow the form in RCW 59.18.057. That form lists:

- Monthly rent due, by month.
- Utilities due, by month.
- Other recurring or periodic charges in the lease, by month.
- The total amount due.
- Where and how the tenant pays.
- Phone numbers and web links for legal aid, the Attorney General's site and dispute resolution centers.

The notice must also say that a payment goes first to the total amount due. RCW 59.18.283 adds a matching rule: you must apply each payment to rent before late fees, damages or legal costs. Possession may not depend on payment of any amount other than rent, except as RCW 59.18.410 allows.

Late fees on their own do not support a 14-day notice. Use the statute's form word for word, then fill in only the amounts.

## How long does an eviction take in Washington?

A Washington eviction for unpaid rent takes at least 21 days from the notice to the hearing. The statute sets the minimums: 14 days for the notice, then 7 to 30 days from service of the show cause order to the hearing. If you file and serve on day 15, the hearing falls between about day 22 and day 45. Court calendars, contested cases and the sheriff's schedule add time.

| Phase | Statutory time | Running total |
|---|---|---|
| 14-day notice | 14 days after service (RCW 59.12.030(3)) | Day 14 |
| File the summons and complaint; ask for a show cause order | Your choice; day 15 at the earliest | Day 15 |
| Show cause hearing | 7 to 30 days after service of the order (RCW 59.18.370) | Day 22 to 45 |
| Writ of restitution to the sheriff | After the court grants it | Varies by county |
| Tenant may ask for storage of property | Within 3 days after service of the writ (RCW 59.18.312) | Varies |

Count the hearing window from the date the order is served on the tenant, not the filing date.

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Washington, and what does it cost?

A Washington landlord files in the superior court of the county where the rental is. RCW 59.18.370 lets the landlord, at filing or later, ask that court for an order to show cause. The order says why a writ of restitution should not issue.

The filing fee is $45. RCW 36.18.020(2)(a) sets a $45 case initiating fee for an unlawful detainer action under chapter 59.18 or 59.20 RCW. Other civil cases pay $200. Service of process and the sheriff's fees are extra.

The summons must follow the form in RCW 59.18.365. It warns the tenant of the response deadline. It also lists the Eviction Defense Screening Line, because the state may appoint a lawyer for a qualifying low-income tenant.

Attorneys' fees have limits. Under RCW 59.18.290(3), the court may not award landlord attorneys' fees after a default. It also may not award them when the rent judgment is 2 months' rent or $1,200 or less, whichever is greater.

## Can a Washington landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

A Washington landlord may evict for a lease violation, but not for no cause in most cases. RCW 59.18.650(1)(a) bars eviction, refusal to continue a tenancy, or ending a periodic tenancy except for the causes in subsection (2). Common causes:

1. **Unpaid rent.** After the 14-day notice ((2)(a)).
2. **Breach of a material term.** After a notice of at least 10 days to fix it ((2)(b)).
3. **Waste, nuisance or unlawful activity.** After a 3-day notice to quit ((2)(c)).
4. **Owner or family move-in.** With at least 90 days' notice, when no equal unit is vacant in the building ((2)(d)).
5. **Sale of a single-family home.** With at least 90 days' notice and a real listing within 30 days after move-out ((2)(e)).
6. **Demolition, substantial rehab or change of use.** With the 120-day notice of RCW 59.18.200(2)(c) ((2)(f)).
7. **Uninhabitable, condemned unit.** With at least 30 days' notice ((2)(h)).

The no-cause exception is narrow. Under RCW 59.18.650(1)(b)-(c), you may end a tenancy at the end of its first term without cause only in some cases. One case is a first lease of 6 to 12 months that then rolls to month-to-month. The other is a fixed term of 12 months or more, or back-to-back terms of 6 months or more, that was never month-to-month. In both, you need at least 60 days' written notice before the term ends.

Every cause notice must state the facts that support it, so the tenant can respond (RCW 59.18.650(6)). A removal that breaks this section costs the greater of actual damages or 3 times the monthly rent, plus fees (RCW 59.18.650(4)).

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Washington?

The county sheriff removes the tenant. RCW 59.18.370 says that if the tenant fails to show cause, the court may order the sheriff to restore possession. The sheriff serves the writ of restitution and gives the tenant a notice about personal property (RCW 59.18.312(5)).

After the sheriff executes the writ, the landlord takes the tenant's property found on the premises. If the tenant asks in writing within 3 days after service of the writ, the landlord must store it. The tenant gets the property back after paying the actual or reasonable moving and storage costs, whichever is less.

Before a sale of property worth more than $250, you must notify the tenant and wait 30 days. For $250 or less, wait 7 days. Do not sell personal papers, family pictures or keepsakes in that case (RCW 59.18.312(3)).

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Washington?

No. A self-help eviction is illegal in Washington. RCW 59.18.290(1) makes it unlawful for a landlord to remove or exclude a tenant except under a court order. The tenant may recover possession or end the lease, and collect actual damages, costs and attorneys' fees.

Utility shutoffs are also illegal. RCW 59.18.300 bars a landlord from causing an intentional stop of water, heat, electricity or gas. A short stop for needed repairs is the one exception. The landlord may owe actual damages plus up to $100 for each day or part of a day without service.

## Washington eviction checklist for landlords

- [ ] Confirm the cause is on the RCW 59.18.650 list.
- [ ] Use the exact RCW 59.18.057 form for a 14-day notice.
- [ ] Serve the notice as RCW 59.12.040 requires and keep proof.
- [ ] Apply every payment to rent first.
- [ ] File in superior court with the RCW 59.18.365 summons; budget $45 plus service.
- [ ] Ask for the show cause hearing 7 to 30 days out.
- [ ] Give the writ to the sheriff; never change locks yourself.
- [ ] Track the 3-day storage request window and the property sale notices.

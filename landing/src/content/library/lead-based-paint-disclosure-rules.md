## Which rental homes need a lead-based paint disclosure?

A lead-based paint disclosure is needed for most rental homes built before 1978. The federal rule is 40 CFR Part 745, Subpart F. EPA and HUD enforce it together. The rule calls these homes "target housing" (40 CFR 745.103).

Some homes and some leases are outside the rule. Use this table to check a unit.

| Situation | Disclosure needed? | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| House or apartment built before 1978 | Yes | 40 CFR 745.103 |
| Home built in 1978 or later | No | 40 CFR 745.103 |
| 0-bedroom unit: studio, efficiency, dorm, single room rental | No | 40 CFR 745.103 |
| Housing for the elderly or people with disabilities | No, unless a child under 6 lives or will live there | 40 CFR 745.103 |
| Lease of 100 days or less, with no renewal or extension possible | No | 40 CFR 745.101(c) |
| Unit found lead-based paint free by a certified inspector | No | 40 CFR 745.101(b) |
| Renewal, full disclosure given before, no new information | No | 40 CFR 745.101(d) |
| Sublease of a pre-1978 unit | Yes | 40 CFR 745.101 |

Watch for the 100-day exemption. A short lease with an option to renew or extend does not qualify. A vacation rental with a fixed end date does.

## What must a landlord do before the tenant signs?

A landlord must finish 4 tasks before the tenant is bound by the lease (40 CFR 745.107). If the tenant made an offer first, you finish the tasks before you accept it. Then the tenant can review the facts and change the offer.

1. **Give the pamphlet.** Hand over the EPA pamphlet "Protect Your Family From Lead in Your Home" (EPA #747-K-94-001), or a state version that EPA approved.
2. **Disclose what you know.** Tell the tenant about any known lead-based paint or lead hazards. Include where the paint is, how you know, and the state of the painted surfaces.
3. **Hand over the records.** Give the tenant any lead reports you have. In a multifamily building, this includes reports on common areas. It also includes reports on other units if they came from a building-wide check.
4. **Attach the signed form.** Add the Lead Warning Statement and the disclosure to the lease. Everyone signs and dates it.

The landlord does not have to test the unit. 40 CFR 745.107 says the rule does not create a duty to inspect or to remove lead. EPA says the same on its disclosure page: a renter can ask for a test, but the rule does not require one.

Buyers get a 10-day chance to test for lead (40 CFR 745.110). Tenants do not. That right applies to sales only.

## What goes on the lead disclosure form?

The lead disclosure form must hold 6 parts for a lease (40 CFR 745.113(b)). Write it in the same language as the lease. A Spanish lease needs a Spanish form.

- **Lead Warning Statement.** Use the exact text in the rule. It starts: "Housing built before 1978 may contain lead-based paint."
- **Landlord statement.** Disclose known lead or lead hazards, or state that you know of none.
- **List of records.** List each lead report you gave the tenant. If you have none, say so.
- **Tenant receipt.** The tenant confirms receipt of the disclosure, the records and the pamphlet.
- **Agent statement.** If an agent is involved, the agent confirms they told the landlord about these duties.
- **Signatures and dates.** The landlord, any agent and each tenant sign and date.

EPA publishes sample lessor disclosure forms in English and Spanish on its disclosure rule page. If you use your own form, check that it holds all 6 parts.

Electronic signatures are allowed. EPA says the tenant must consent to electronic access. You must also explain the right to paper copies and how to get and keep the records.

## Who is responsible: owner or property manager?

The owner and the property manager are both responsible. Under 40 CFR 745.115, an agent must tell the owner about the duties. The agent must then make sure the owner did each task, or do the tasks for the owner.

An agent who told the owner about the duties is not liable for lead facts the owner hid from the agent (40 CFR 745.115(b)). So a property manager should ask each owner in writing about known lead and lead reports. Keep that answer in the owner file. If you manage pre-1978 doors, add the question to your owner onboarding form.

## How long do I keep the signed form?

Keep the signed lead disclosure for at least 3 years from the start of the lease. 40 CFR 745.113(c) puts this duty on the landlord and on any agent. EPA's page repeats the 3-year rule.

A simple file plan:

- Save the signed form as a PDF in the tenant file and in the property file.
- Name it with the unit, the tenant and the lease start date.
- Set the earliest delete date at 3 years after the lease start. You may keep it longer.
- Keep each lead report in the property file with no end date. You must give it to every future tenant.

## What are the penalties for no lead disclosure?

The penalties for no lead disclosure come from 3 places. 40 CFR 745.118 lists them.

| Penalty | Who acts | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Civil penalty under 42 U.S.C. 4852d(b)(5) | Federal agencies | Up to $22,263 per violation, assessed on or after 2025-01-08 (40 CFR 19.4) |
| Civil penalty under TSCA section 16 (15 U.S.C. 2615) | EPA | Up to $49,772 per violation, assessed on or after 2025-01-08 (40 CFR 19.4) |
| Damages to the tenant | Tenant, in court | 3 times actual damages, plus court costs and attorney fees (40 CFR 745.118) |

The penalty applies per violation. A landlord with many units and no forms can face many violations. Agencies also can get a court order to stop the violation.

## Do states add their own lead rules?

States and cities can add their own lead rules. 40 CFR 745.119 says the federal rule does not remove any duty under state or local law. So a federal form alone may not be enough in your city. Check your state health department and your city housing code before you lease a pre-1978 unit.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Lead disclosure checklist for each new lease

Use this checklist for each new lease of a pre-1978 unit.

- [ ] Confirm the year built from county records.
- [ ] Check the exemptions: 0-bedroom, elderly or disabled housing, 100 days or less, certified lead-free.
- [ ] Ask the owner in writing about known lead and lead reports.
- [ ] Give the EPA pamphlet before the tenant signs.
- [ ] Give copies of all lead reports, including common areas.
- [ ] Attach the disclosure form with the exact Lead Warning Statement.
- [ ] Get signatures and dates from the landlord, the agent and each adult tenant.
- [ ] Save the signed form for at least 3 years.
- [ ] At renewal, disclose again if you got new lead information.

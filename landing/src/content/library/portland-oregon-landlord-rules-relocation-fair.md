## What is Oregon's yearly rent increase cap?

Oregon's yearly rent increase cap is 7% plus CPI or 10%, whichever is lower (ORS 90.324). The Oregon Department of Administrative Services (DAS) publishes the number by September 30 each year. For 2027, DAS set the maximum at 10%. For 2026, it was 9.5%.

The rules that come with the cap (ORS 90.323):

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| First year | No increase during the first year of a tenancy |
| How often | Once in any 12-month period |
| Notice | At least 90 days' written notice, for tenancies other than week-to-week |
| What the notice says | The increase, the new rent, the start date, and the facts for any exemption |
| Exemption | Units with a first certificate of occupancy less than 15 years before the notice; some regulated affordable housing |
| Penalty | 3 months' rent plus the tenant's actual damages |

Worked example, 2027: the rent is $1,900. The most you can add is 10%, or $190, for a new rent of $2,090. Send the notice at least 90 days before the start date. In Portland, a 10% increase also triggers the city's relocation rule, covered next.

For context, HUD's FY2027 fair market rent for a Portland-area 2-bedroom is $1,878, down 2.3% from $1,922. See [Portland fair market rent 2027](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/portland-or-fair-market-rent-2027).

## When does a Portland owner owe relocation assistance?

A Portland owner owes relocation assistance in 2 cases under Portland City Code 30.01.085:

1. **You end the tenancy without cause or for a qualifying landlord reason.** That includes a decision not to renew a lease. You must give at least 90 days' written notice, or the lease's period if it is longer. You pay at least 45 days before the end date.
2. **You raise the rent by 10% or more in a rolling 12 months.** If the tenant asks in writing within 45 days of your notice, you pay within 31 days. The tenant then has 6 months to repay it and stay, or to give notice and move.

| Unit | Relocation assistance |
|---|---|
| Studio or single room (SRO) | $2,900 |
| 1 bedroom | $3,300 |
| 2 bedrooms | $4,200 |
| 3 bedrooms or more | $4,500 |

The amounts apply per unit, not per tenant. A tenant can get it once per tenancy per unit. You must include a description of the tenant's rights and the amount with each notice and payment. You must also tell the Portland Housing Bureau (PHB) about each payment within 30 days.

Notice rule for smaller increases: for any increase of 5% or more in 12 months, Portland requires 90 days' written notice, or the lease's period if longer.

Exemptions exist, but only if you file an exemption form with PHB and get its acknowledgement letter. They include, among others:

- Week-to-week tenancies.
- A tenant who shares the unit with the landlord.
- One unit of a duplex where the landlord lives in the other.
- An ADU when the owner lives on the site.
- A landlord's own home rented while away for up to 3 years, or on active military duty.
- Ending a tenancy so an immediate family member can move in.

Worked example: you plan a 10% increase on a 2-bedroom at $1,900, or $190 a month. If the tenant asks, you pay $4,200 within 31 days. If the tenant then moves out, you also carry a turnover. If the tenant stays, the tenant repays the $4,200. An increase of 9.9% ($188) does not trigger the payment.

## What does Portland's FAIR ordinance require for screening?

Portland's FAIR rules, in City Code 30.01.086, set how you advertise, accept and judge applications. The main steps:

1. **Post notice 72 hours ahead.** Before you take applications, publish when you will start, the screening factors (if you charge a screening fee), and whether the unit is accessible.
2. **Process in order of receipt.** Accept, conditionally accept or deny completed applications in the order you receive them.
3. **Give accessible units priority.** In the first 8 hours, an application from a household with a member who has a mobility disability goes first for an accessible unit.
4. **Cap the income test.** You may require monthly income of no more than 2.5 times the rent when the rent is below the PHB limit for 80% of median income. At or above that limit, the cap is 2 times the rent.
5. **Count all income.** Include wages, non-government rent help and public benefits. Reduce the rent by any government rent subsidy before you do the math.
6. **Limit guarantor demands.** A friend or family guarantor cannot be required to earn more than 3 times the rent.
7. **Keep a record.** Within 5 business days of a request, give an applicant the date and time you got their application.

Worked income test: rent of $1,878 below the 80% limit. The highest income you can require is 2.5 × $1,878 = $4,695 a month. If the tenant has a voucher that pays $1,200, you test on $678, so the cap is $1,695.

## What are Portland's security deposit rules?

Portland's security deposit rules, in City Code 30.01.087, cap the amount and control where you keep it.

| Situation | Most you can collect |
|---|---|
| No last month's rent | 1 month's rent as deposit |
| You take last month's rent | Last month's rent plus half a month's deposit |
| Conditional approval after screening | Half a month more, payable in up to 3 installments |

Where to keep it: within 2 weeks, deposit the funds in an account at a secure financial institution, separate from your personal and business accounts. If the account earns interest, the interest belongs to the tenant. You may keep up to 5% of that interest for admin costs.

## Must Portland owners register rentals?

Yes. Portland City Code 7.02.890 requires every owner of residential rental property in the City of Portland to register it each year. You file Schedule R with the City of Portland and Multnomah County Business Tax Return, generally by April 15 (City of Portland Revenue Division).

| Tax year | Fee per unit |
|---|---|
| 2025 | $70 |
| 2024 | $70 |
| 2023 | $70 |
| 2022 | $65 |
| 2019–2021 | $60 |

Late payment brings penalties and interest. Some parties are exempt from the fee. Check that the address is inside the city on Portland Maps: the jurisdiction must say "Portland".

Checklist for a Portland owner:

- [ ] Schedule R and $70 per unit by April 15.
- [ ] Rent notices 90 days ahead, under the 2027 cap of 10%.
- [ ] Relocation funds ready for any no-cause end or 10%+ increase.
- [ ] FAIR-compliant ad, intake log and income test.
- [ ] Deposits in a separate account within 2 weeks.

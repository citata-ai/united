Goodjuju studied 35 property management companies from July 8 to October 5, 2026. Social media sent 4.3% of their website visitors but only 0.8% of their owner form leads. Its conversion rate was 0.2%, against 1.2% for Google Search (Goodjuju, 2026). Goodjuju's verdict: social is still not a lead channel for property management.

That is one agency's client data, and it counts form leads on the main website only. Ads that send clicks to Meta lead forms or separate landing pages are not fully in it. So the honest answer is: Meta ads can work, but you must prove it with your own numbers.

## Why run Meta ads at all?

Meta ads reach owners who do not search for a manager yet. About 22% of small rental properties (1 to 4 units) had professional management in the 2021 Rental Housing Finance Survey (HUD and Census, 2022). The other 78% did not. Many of those owners will never type "property manager near me" until something goes wrong. An ad can reach them earlier.

Good reasons to run Meta ads:

- **Retargeting.** Show an owner offer to people who read your fee page last week.
- **Awareness in a new area.** You opened a new office and nobody knows your name.
- **Recruiting and brand.** Goodjuju says social still helps brand, recruiting and staying in front of existing clients (Goodjuju, 2026).

Weak reasons: "everyone is on Facebook" or "our competitor posts every day".

## Which audiences reach rental owners?

The audiences that reach owners are your own data plus broad local reach with owner-only copy. Demographic targeting is not the tool here.

If Meta treats your campaign as housing, the rules are strict. For US campaigns in the housing category, Meta removes age, gender and ZIP code targeting, audience exclusions, lookalike audiences and saved audiences. City or pin targets include a 15-mile radius (Meta Business Help Center).

Audience options, from best to weakest:

| Audience | How to build it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Website visitors to owner pages | Meta Pixel on your owner pages | Highest intent; small size |
| Past owner leads | Upload a list of people who asked for a quote | Use only contacts who gave you their details |
| Engaged users | People who watched your owner videos | Cheap; mixed intent |
| Broad local | City or region, with owner-only copy | Large; copy does the filtering |

Write the ad so renters skip it. "Own a rental in [metro]? See what it should rent for." A renter has no reason to click.

## Which ad offers work on Meta?

Offers that give the owner something useful before a call work best. A plain "Call us for management" ad asks for trust you have not earned yet.

| Offer | What the owner gets | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Free rent estimate | A rent range for their address | Address, contact, intent |
| Vacancy cost check | What one empty month costs them | A money conversation |
| Fee comparison | Your fees next to the local average | A shopper who compares |
| Owner guide | Local law and cost checklist | An email for your newsletter |
| Short video | You, explaining one owner mistake | Retargeting audience |

Keep one offer per ad. Use a real photo of your team or a property you manage, with the owner's consent.

## How do Meta lead forms compare to the website?

Meta lead forms collect the lead inside Facebook or Instagram, and website pages collect it on your site. Each has a trade-off:

| Factor | Meta lead form | Website landing page |
|---|---|---|
| Effort for the owner | Low; the form opens inside the app | Higher; the owner must type |
| Lead volume | Usually higher (hypothesis) | Usually lower (hypothesis) |
| Lead quality | Often lower; people tap by mistake | Often higher |
| Tracking with other sources | Separate export or CRM sync | Same GA4 and CRM as all leads |
| Speed to reply | Needs a CRM sync, or leads wait | Same flow as other forms |

If you use Meta lead forms, add one question that filters, such as "How many rentals do you own?" Then call every lead within one business day. A lead form without fast follow-up wastes the spend.

## What does a Meta owner lead cost?

We found no reliable public benchmark for the cost of an owner lead from Meta. Agency blog numbers vary and rarely show their method. Compute your own numbers instead.

Worked example with your own data:

| Line | Example | Your numbers |
|---|---|---|
| Ad spend, 90 days | $1,500 | |
| Owner leads | 15 | |
| Cost per lead | $1,500 ÷ 15 = $100 | |
| Signed doors | 2 | |
| Cost per signed door | $1,500 ÷ 2 = $750 | |
| Value of one door (36 months × $150 fee) | $5,400 | |

The example numbers are for the math only. If your cost per signed door is well below the value of a door, the channel pays. Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to find your door value.

Run the test for at least 90 days. Owners often take months to decide, so a 2-week test says little.

## How do I track Meta leads correctly?

Track Meta leads by source, all the way to the signed door. Goodjuju's setup guide gives the base steps (Goodjuju, 2026):

1. Add UTM tags to every ad link: `utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=paid&utm_campaign=owner-rent-estimate`.
2. Save the UTM tags in hidden form fields with each lead.
3. Ask "How did you first hear about us?" with "Facebook / Instagram" as an option.
4. Count each owner once. Keep tenant forms out of the count.

Goodjuju also saw an odd effect on its own site. GA4's "(data not available)" bucket spiked when it started Facebook ads, and that bucket brought no leads. Do not count that bucket as a win for any channel.

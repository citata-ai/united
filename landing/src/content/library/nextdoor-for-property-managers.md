Someone posts on Nextdoor: "We're moving for work. Can anyone recommend a property manager to rent out our house?" Within an hour, 12 neighbors reply with names. If one of your current owners names you, you start that call with trust. That is the real value of Nextdoor for a property manager. The page, posts and ads only support it.

## How does a Nextdoor Business Page work?

A Nextdoor Business Page is a free local profile that neighbors can find, follow and recommend. Nextdoor's small business site, read on 2026-10-20, lists four tools:

| Tool | Cost | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Business Page | Free | Your profile; shows when neighbors look for a business type |
| Business Posts | Free | Posts in the neighborhood newsfeed |
| Recommendations | Free | Neighbors recommend you; builds reputation |
| Ads | Paid, daily budget | Reach more neighbors in chosen areas |

Nextdoor's investor site describes the network as "over 110 million verified neighbors." It is a feed built around real addresses. That fits a property manager: your clients and your homes sit in specific neighborhoods.

Set up the page like this:

1. **Claim the free page** with your exact business name, as on Google.
2. **Set your service area** to the neighborhoods where you manage homes.
3. **Write a 3-line description:** who you serve, which areas, your fee model.
4. **Add photos:** team, office, a real managed home with owner consent.
5. **Link to your owner page**, with a UTM tag (a tracking label in a link) so Google Analytics shows Nextdoor visits.

## Do neighbors recommend property managers on Nextdoor?

Yes. Recommendations are the core of Nextdoor for local services. Nextdoor says "1 in 3 neighbors have been influenced by a recommendation on Nextdoor." Its business page quotes a Houston real estate firm: "If you trust your neighbors, you trust the people they recommend."

You cannot buy recommendations. You can earn them and ask for them fairly:

- [ ] Ask happy owners who live in your area if they use Nextdoor
- [ ] Send them a direct link to your page
- [ ] Ask every owner the same way; do not pick only fans
- [ ] Never offer a discount or gift for a recommendation
- [ ] Thank recommenders in public, without client details

Watch threads where a neighbor asks for a manager. Reply once, as yourself, with one useful fact and your page. Do not reply to every thread. Neighbors notice.

Tenants also post on Nextdoor. If a tenant complains about your company, reply once, calmly, and move the talk to private messages. Never share lease or payment details in public.

## Are Nextdoor ads worth testing?

Nextdoor ads are worth a small, tracked test, not a big budget. Nextdoor's budget guide lists three recommended plans:

- $3 per day
- $5 per day
- $10 per day

It says you are "charged upfront your daily budget times 31 days," then the same amount every 31 days until you cancel. You can also set a custom budget and duration. Billing options may have changed since the guide was written; check Ads Manager before you start.

### Worked example: a 62-day test

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Daily budget | $5 |
| Charge per 31 days | $155 |
| Test length | 62 days |
| Total cost | $310 |
| Owner leads from the ad (example) | 2 |
| Cost per owner lead | $155 |

The leads number is an example. Compare your real cost per owner lead with your other channels. In the Goodjuju Marketing fall 2026 study of 35 property management companies, social media gave 0.8% of owner leads and Google Search and the Map Pack gave 50.6%. Nextdoor is a social channel, so start small.

Ad rules: target by area, never by who lives there. Fair Housing rules apply to ads for rentals and to any copy about neighborhoods.

## What should a property manager post on Nextdoor?

Post local facts that help homeowners, not sales pitches. Free Business Posts go into the neighborhood feed, so treat each one as a note to neighbors.

Post ideas:

1. **Rent trend:** "3-bed homes in our area leased in a median of 21 days last month." Use your own data.
2. **Rule change:** a new city or state rental rule, with a link to the official source.
3. **Seasonal checklist:** winter pipe checks, smoke alarm tests, gutter cleaning.
4. **Free event:** an owner Q&A at a library or café.
5. **Accidental landlords:** "Moving for work? 5 things to decide before you rent out your home."

Posting rhythm: 2 posts a month is enough. Too many business posts read as spam.

Track Nextdoor for 90 days: page views, recommendations, clicks to your site and owners who say "I saw you on Nextdoor." If owners do not mention it, keep the free page and recommendations, and stop the ads.

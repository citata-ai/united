## Franchise or independent: how do they compare?

A franchise sells you a system and a brand for a fee. An independent company builds its own system and keeps its revenue. This table puts the main trade-offs side by side. The points come from Buildium's franchise post and the FTC Franchise Rule.

| Factor | Franchise | Independent |
|---|---|---|
| Start-up system | Ready: processes, training, brand guide | You build it |
| Brand | Franchisor's brand and its reputation | Your own name, from zero |
| Marketing | Franchisor marketing or tools, usually for a fee | You pick channels and pay for them |
| Software | Franchisor's tech stack | You pick |
| Ongoing fees | Yes: Buildium lists marketing, software, training and renewal fees | None to a franchisor |
| Rules | You must follow franchisor rules | You make the rules |
| Contract | Buildium: can last 10 years | No franchise contract |
| Exit | Set by Item 17 of the FDD | Sell or close on your own terms |
| Pre-sale disclosure | FDD at least 14 days before you sign (16 CFR 436.2) | Not applicable |

## What does a franchise give me?

A franchise gives you a working playbook on day 1. Buildium lists what you get: set best practices for operations, marketing, branding, and the tenant and owner experience. You get training in rent and fee collection, marketing, accounting, and compliance. The franchisor also sends a brand guide and rules for all franchisees.

Buildium names one more benefit. Most problems you meet will not be new to the franchisor, so it may already have a fix. That helps most in the parts of the job where you are weak. If you are strong at owner service but weak at marketing, the franchise fills the gap.

A franchise also gives you a ready software stack. Buildium notes this is a plus for a new manager, and a minus for a manager who already prefers other tools.

## What does a franchise cost over 5 years?

A franchise costs the initial fee, the initial investment, and the ongoing fees for each year of the term. Do not trust one number from a blog or a franchise directory. Third-party summaries of the same brand often conflict. Use the franchisor's current Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD).

The FTC Franchise Rule fixes where each cost lives in the FDD (16 CFR 436.5):

- **Item 5: Initial fees.** The initial fees and when they are refundable.
- **Item 6: Other fees.** A table of all other fees you pay to the franchisor, with any formula.
- **Item 7: Estimated initial investment.** A table titled "Your estimated initial investment".
- **Item 19: Financial performance representations.** Only if the franchisor chooses to give them.
- **Item 20: Outlets and franchisee information.** Outlet counts for the last 3 fiscal years.

5-year cost model. Fill each cell from the FDD. The example numbers are made up to show the math. They are not any brand's fees.

| Line | Where to find it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Initial franchise fee | Item 5 | $60,000 |
| Other start-up costs | Item 7 | $40,000 |
| Revenue, years 1–5 | Your plan | $1,500,000 total |
| Royalty: rate × revenue | Item 6 | 7% × $1,500,000 = $105,000 |
| Marketing fee: rate × revenue | Item 6 | 2% × $1,500,000 = $30,000 |
| Tech fee: monthly × 60 | Item 6 | $100 × 60 = $6,000 |
| 5-year total paid for the franchise | Sum | $241,000 |

Now ask: would an independent company need $241,000 more in marketing, software, training and lost months to reach the same revenue? If yes, the franchise may be worth it. If no, start independent.

Buildium gives one data point for scale: one property management franchise recommends that candidates have $92,000 to $266,000 available to start. An independent start can cost as low as $2,000 if you bootstrap, per Buildium.

## How much freedom do I lose?

You lose the right to make your own rules. Buildium is direct: as a franchisee, you will not have the freedom to set your own rules, and you must follow the franchisor's rules "to a T".

Freedom you give up, per Buildium:

- **Process.** You run the franchisor's procedures, even when your local market differs.
- **Marketing.** You may have little say in how or where you market. You pay for it, often as a percent of revenue.
- **Suppliers.** Buildium lists penalty fees for using unapproved suppliers and for marketing outside your territory.
- **Software.** You switch to the franchise's system.
- **Reputation.** The brand's reputation is yours, good or bad.

Buildium also warns about local fit. If your market does not match the franchisor's system, you can end up with gaps in service or rules that make no sense for your tenants and owners.

## Can I leave a franchise later?

You can leave only on the terms in your contract. Item 17 of the FDD must show those terms in a table titled "The franchise relationship" (16 CFR 436.5). Read these rows first:

- [ ] Length of the term and renewal conditions
- [ ] Termination by you, and termination by the franchisor
- [ ] Transfer: can you sell the business, and who must approve
- [ ] The franchisor's right of first refusal and option to buy your business
- [ ] Non-competition rules during the term
- [ ] Non-competition rules after the franchise ends or is terminated

The last row decides if you can keep managing your doors under your own name after you leave. Ask a franchise attorney to read it with you. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I check a franchise before I sign?

Use the 14 days the law gives you. Under 16 CFR 436.2, the franchisor must give you its current disclosure document at least 14 calendar days before you sign a binding agreement or pay. If the franchisor changes the agreement on its own, you must get the revised agreement at least 7 calendar days before you sign it.

Step list:

1. Get the FDD and mark the day you got it.
2. Fill the 5-year cost model from Items 5, 6 and 7.
3. Read Item 20 and call 3 to 5 current and former franchisees.
4. Read Item 19, if it exists. Compare its numbers to your own plan.
5. Read Item 17 with an attorney.
6. Price the independent path for the same 5 years.

## Which trades does a landlord need on call?

A landlord needs a short list of trusted vendors before the first emergency. A burst pipe at 2 a.m. is the wrong time to search for a plumber. Build the list when nothing is broken.

| Trade | Typical rental jobs | Urgency |
|---|---|---|
| Plumber | Leaks, clogs, water heaters, burst pipes | Often an emergency |
| HVAC technician | No heat, no cooling, furnace service | Emergency in extreme weather |
| Electrician | Outages, breakers, outlets, panels | Emergency if unsafe |
| Locksmith | Lockouts, rekeys at turnover | Same day |
| Appliance repair | Fridge, stove, washer, dryer | 1 to 3 days |
| Handyman | Small repairs, turnover punch lists | Routine |
| Pest control | Roaches, mice, bed bugs | Fast, before it spreads |
| Cleaner | Turnover cleaning | Routine |
| Water damage company | Floods, mold after leaks | Emergency |

For each trade, keep a first choice and a backup. A vendor who is busy on the day you call is no help.

Where to find candidates:

- **Other landlords and property managers** in your area. Ask who they call, and who they stopped calling.
- **Local landlord groups and trade associations.**
- **Supply houses.** Plumbing and HVAC supply counters know which trades pay on time and come back to fix mistakes.
- **Reviews.** The FTC advises searching the company name with words like "scam," "review" or "complaint," and reading reviews with care.

## What should I check: license, insurance, W-9?

Check four things before the first job: license, insurance, tax form, and lead-safe status for older homes. The FTC's advice is direct: "Consider only contractors who are licensed and insured."

Vendor vetting checklist:

- [ ] **License.** Look up the license on the state or county lookup site. Confirm the name, the trade and an active status.
- [ ] **Insurance.** Ask for a certificate of insurance. Check the dates. Ask to be named as a certificate holder.
- [ ] **W-9.** The IRS says Form W-9 is used to "provide your correct Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) to the person who is required to file an information return."
- [ ] **Lead-safe certification** for homes built before 1978 (see below).
- [ ] **References.** Two other landlords or managers who used the vendor in the last year.
- [ ] **Written contract or work order** for each job.
- [ ] **Payment terms** in writing.

The W-9 matters at tax time. The IRS instructions for Form 1099-NEC say that for tax years after 2025, the minimum reporting threshold rose to $2,000. The same instructions say to "enter nonemployee compensation (NEC) of $2,000 or more." Whether you must file depends on your situation. This is general information, not tax advice. Ask a tax professional.

Lead paint is a federal rule for older rentals. The EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) rule applies to homes built before 1978. The EPA says anyone paid to do work that disturbs painted surfaces in those homes must be "certified in lead-safe work practices." The rule also applies if you rent out all or part of your home. Ask each painter, handyman and contractor for the firm's RRP certificate.

## How do I compare contractor quotes?

Compare contractor quotes only when each quote covers the same written scope. The FTC advises getting several written estimates, and says each should describe the work, the materials, the completion date and the price. It also says not to pick the lowest bid by default.

Worked example: a water heater swap in a rental.

| Line | Quote A | Quote B | Quote C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tank, 40 gallon | Included | Included | Included |
| Labor | Included | Included | Included |
| Permit | Included | Not listed | Included |
| Haul away old tank | Included | Not listed | Extra |
| Warranty on labor | 1 year | 30 days | 1 year |
| Start date | 2 days | Same day | 5 days |
| License verified | Yes | No | Yes |
| Total price | Middle | Lowest | Highest |

Quote B looks cheapest, but it has no permit, no haul away and a short labor warranty, and the license is not verified. Ask each vendor to price the missing lines. Then compare again.

Payment rules from the FTC:

- Do not pay the full amount up front. Some states limit the size of a down payment.
- Do not make the final payment until the work is done and you have checked it.
- Avoid a contractor who accepts only cash.

FTC red flags: a contractor who knocks on your door because they are "in the area", who offers "left over" materials, who pushes for a fast decision, or who asks you to get the permits.

## Should I use a handyman or a licensed trade?

Use a handyman for small, simple work your state allows without a license. Use a licensed trade for plumbing, electrical, gas, HVAC, roofing and structural work. The line between the two is set by state law, and it differs a lot.

| State | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Florida | Exempts work "of a casual, minor, or inconsequential nature" under $2,500 total, with limits; the exemption does not apply if a larger job is split to dodge the law | Florida Statutes 489.103(9) |
| North Carolina | A general contractor license applies where "the cost of the undertaking is forty thousand dollars ($40,000) or more" | G.S. 87-1 |

Even under a dollar limit, many states and cities require a licensed trade for electrical, plumbing and gas work. Check your state license board and your city permit office before you hire.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I keep good vendors?

Good vendors stay with landlords who make the work easy. Three habits help:

1. **Pay on time.** Set a payment term, such as 15 days after the invoice, and keep it.
2. **Send clear work orders.** Include the address, the tenant's contact, the problem, photos and entry rules.
3. **Give steady work.** A vendor who gets 10 jobs a year from you answers your call first.

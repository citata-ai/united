## Why do companies look beyond Rent Manager?

A company should look beyond Rent Manager when a specific task remains costly or hard to control. Growth alone does not show that the software no longer fits.

Rent Manager's bundle page lists accounting, reporting and custom workflows. A replacement needs to meet the parts your team actually uses. Do not discard a useful custom report because a different dashboard looks simpler.

Collect examples of extra work from your own staff. Ask the bookkeeper, maintenance lead and leasing team to show where they leave the system. Label each issue as a configuration need, training gap or missing function.

Ask the current vendor to address that list before you price a move. The answer gives you a baseline. If training fixes the problem, you may avoid a difficult migration. If a limit remains, bring the same example to every new vendor and require a working demonstration.

## Which alternatives have simpler setup?

Setup is simpler when the scope is clear and the data is clean. A claim about fast onboarding does not prove either condition for your portfolio.

Ask each alternative to show its import template. Compare the required fields with your exports. Check how the system connects owners to properties and tenants to leases. A flat contact list does not preserve those relationships.

Use a difficult sample rather than a perfect empty unit. Include an unpaid bill, a tenant credit and an owner reserve. Ask the vendor to show who resolves each mismatch. Request the same test from the team that will handle your real migration.

Training is part of setup. Ask each role to complete a task without the presenter taking over. A system can be easy to navigate while still needing careful accounting instruction. Keep that distinction in your notes and budget staff time for practice.

## How do prices compare?

Rent Manager now asks buyers for a quote. Do not use old per-unit figures as a current offer. The current vendor page also separates add-on services from bundles.

Ask for the subscription, setup, training and support scope in writing. Include connected products and communication usage. Ask what happens to pricing when your unit count changes. Request an explanation of cancellation and archive access.

For each alternative, price the same door count and workload. Use your own payment history to estimate transaction costs. Record assumptions about annual billing and optional features beside the totals.

A lower subscription can still produce more work outside the system. Add the cost of report cleanup, duplicate entry and extra tools using your own records. Keep that estimate separate from vendor charges. The [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) helps connect those costs to the fee you charge owners.

## Which alternatives preserve accounting depth?

Accounting depth means the system supports your actual close, corrections and reporting. It does not mean the vendor has the longest feature list.

Prepare a test close from a sample property. Include cleared and uncleared payments, tenant credits, deposits and owner funds held for future work. Ask for a bank reconciliation and an owner statement from the same records.

Then change an entry. Check the audit record, approval and revised report. Your bookkeeper should be able to explain the change without rebuilding the calculation elsewhere. Ask whether the system supports the reporting basis you use.

Pay attention to reports built over years in Rent Manager. List the data fields, filters and totals each report needs. Ask the new vendor to reproduce the report before signing. If a report cannot move, decide who will replace it and how the replacement will be checked.

## How do you migrate off Rent Manager?

Move through a controlled export, sample import and accounting approval. Do not start by cancelling the old account or moving payment links.

Use this migration checklist:

- Export core records and preserve their identifiers.
- Save the chart of accounts and mapping notes.
- Record tenant, owner, vendor and deposit balances.
- Save bank reconciliations and reports used at close.
- Archive agreements, leases, invoices and inspection files.
- List custom fields, workflows and connected services.
- Confirm which history the new vendor will import.
- Assign a person to approve each exception.

Run the import on a sample portfolio. Compare record counts and trace balances back to the source. Check access permissions before inviting owners or residents. A correct balance shown to the wrong owner is still a failed migration.

Test collection settings separately. Confirm whether automatic payment instructions need new consent or setup. Ask how returned payments from the old processor will appear after cutover. Keep a named owner for that follow-up work.

## When is staying the better choice?

Stay when the improvement remains unproven or the move would remove needed controls. You can still change reports, training or connected services while keeping the core system.

Write a short decision note with the problem, tested result and total cost. Include any task that still needs manual work. Do not let a promised future feature count as a passed test.

If you do move, compare the first close with your sample. Review owner questions and unresolved balances before adding more tools.

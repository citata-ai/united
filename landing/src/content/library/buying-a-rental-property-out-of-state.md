## How do I pick an out-of-state market?

Pick an out-of-state market by scoring a short list of cities on the same 6 factors. Start with data, then visit your top 1 or 2 if you can.

| Factor | What to check | Source |
|--------|---------------|--------|
| Rent to price | Monthly rent of a typical home ÷ its price | Local listings and leases |
| Vacancy | Rental vacancy rate for the state or metro | Census Bureau quarterly vacancy tables |
| Price trend | House price change over 1 and 5 years | FHFA House Price Index |
| Landlord law | Notice periods, deposit rules, rent limits, eviction time | State statutes |
| Costs | Property tax rate, insurance cost, HOA share | County assessor, insurance quotes |
| Jobs and people | Employers, population trend | State labor and census data |

National numbers set the baseline. The Census Bureau reported a national rental vacancy rate of 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026, and it publishes rates by state and metro. FHFA reported US house prices up 2.6% from July 2025 to July 2026. A market far from these numbers needs a reason you can explain.

Landlord law differs a lot between states. Two examples:

- **Oregon (ORS 90.323).** No increase in the first year of a tenancy. The landlord must give 90 days' written notice, and may raise rent once in any 12 months, up to a state maximum.
- **Washington (RCW 59.18.700).** No increase in the first 12 months of a tenancy. In any 12-month period, the cap is 7% plus CPI or 10%, whichever is less.

A state without such caps is not "better". It is different. Put the rules into your numbers.

## How do I check a property I cannot visit?

Check a remote property through people who work for you, not for the seller. The seller's agent and the seller's contractor are paid when the deal closes.

Your remote check, step by step:

1. **Hire your own inspector.** Pick one from reviews and the state license list. Ask for a live video call during the inspection.
2. **Add specialty checks.** Sewer camera, roof and HVAC where the report points to risk.
3. **Get a rent opinion from a manager** you plan to hire. Ask for 3 recent leases of similar homes.
4. **Walk the street by video.** Ask your agent or manager to film the block, day and night.
5. **Check permits online.** Match bedrooms and units with city permit records.
6. **Check the flood zone.** FEMA's Flood Map Service Center is the official source for flood maps under the National Flood Insurance Program.
7. **Get insurance quotes** before you remove contingencies.
8. **Use remote closing tools** your title company offers, and confirm wire instructions by phone.

Watch for: wire fraud. Always call the title company at a number you found yourself before you send money.

## What is turnkey property and what are its risks?

A turnkey property is a rental sold repaired and ready to rent, often with a tenant and a manager already in place. It saves time. It also puts one company on both sides of your deal.

| Turnkey promise | Risk | What to do |
|-----------------|------|------------|
| "Fully renovated" | Cosmetic work over old systems | Your own inspector checks roof, HVAC, plumbing and electrical |
| "Tenant in place" | Weak screening or a short first lease | Ask for the application, lease and payment history |
| "Projected rent" | Rent set to sell the house | Get a rent opinion from an unrelated manager |
| "In-house management" | No one checks repair bills | Ask for the full fee schedule; keep the right to switch |
| "Guaranteed rent" | Guarantee ends when the company changes or fails | Read the terms; treat it as a bonus, not a basis |
| Price above local sales | You pay for the convenience | Compare with recent sales of similar homes nearby |

A turnkey deal can still be good. Just price it as if the promises fail, and see if it still works.

## Which local team do I need?

You need 5 people on the ground before you close. Vet the manager first, because a good manager can recommend the others.

| Role | Job | How to vet |
|------|-----|------------|
| Property manager | Leasing, rent, repairs, law compliance | License, fee schedule, sample owner statement, reviews |
| Inspector | Pre-purchase inspection | State license, sample report |
| Buyer's agent | Finds and negotiates the deal | Investor clients, rental comps |
| Insurance agent | Landlord policy, flood if needed | Quotes from 2 carriers |
| Contractor or handyman | Repairs and turnovers | References, license where required |

Licensing differs by state. Texas Occupations Code 1101.002 counts a person who controls the acceptance or deposit of rent from a resident of a single-family rental as a broker. Section 1101.351 says a person may not act as a broker without a license. Check your target state's real estate commission.

Budget for the manager. iPropertyManagement's 2026 survey found an average fee of 8.49% of monthly rent. Managers that also manage the home charged an average leasing fee of 70.6% of one month's rent. ## Financing and tax notes for out-of-state owners

**Loan limits.** Fannie Mae's eligibility matrix (2026-08-05) allows up to 85% loan-to-value on a 1-unit investment purchase and 75% on 2 to 4 units. Plan for at least 15% to 25% down, plus closing costs and reserves.

**Travel.** IRS Publication 527 lets you deduct ordinary and necessary travel in some cases. The main purpose must be to collect rent or to manage, conserve or maintain the rental. You cannot deduct travel whose main purpose is to improve the property. You must split costs between rental and personal parts of a trip.

**State taxes.** The state where the property sits may tax the rental income. You may need to file a nonresident return there. Ask a tax professional in that state.

This is general information, not legal or tax advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney or tax professional.

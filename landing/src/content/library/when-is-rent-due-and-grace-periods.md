## Is rent due on the 1st by law?

Rent is due on the date the lease sets, not on a date set by national law. Most leases pick the 1st because many tenancies start on the 1st of a month. A lease can pick the 5th, the 15th or any other day.

Write the due date in plain words: "Rent of $1,800 is due on the 1st day of each month." Add where and how to pay. A clear clause prevents most arguments.

Some states require the due date to appear in the lease before you can charge a late fee. Oregon ORS 90.260 is an example. It says the written rental agreement must state the date rent is due and the date late charges become due.

## What is a grace period?

A grace period is the time after the due date before a late fee may start. Some leases offer one by choice. Some states require one by law.

| State | Earliest a late fee may start | Fee limit | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| New York | Rent not paid within 5 days of the due date | $50 or 5% of monthly rent, whichever is less | RPL 238-a(2) |
| Texas | Rent unpaid 2 full days after the due date | Presumed reasonable up to 12% (4 units or fewer) or 10% (more than 4) | Prop. Code 92.019 |
| Oregon | Rent not received by the 4th day of the rental period | Reasonable flat fee, capped daily fee, or 5% per 5-day period | ORS 90.260 |

**Worked example (Texas):** rent of $1,800 is due on the 1st. Two full days after the 1st end at midnight on the 3rd. So the earliest a late fee may apply is the 4th. For a building of 4 units or fewer, a fee up to 12%, or $216, is presumed reasonable. The fee must be in the written lease.

**Worked example (New York):** rent of $1,800 is due on the 1st. The fee may apply only if rent is not paid within 5 days. The cap is the lesser of $50 or 5% ($90), so the cap is $50.

In states with no grace period in statute, your lease sets the rule. Many leases add 3 to 5 days anyway. Pick one rule and apply it to every tenant.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Does a grace period make rent not late?

A grace period does not move the due date. Rent is still due on the 1st. The grace period only delays the late fee.

The difference matters for 3 reasons:

1. **Notices.** In some states you may serve a notice to pay rent after the due date, even inside the fee grace period. In others, you must wait. Check your state's eviction notice rules.
2. **Records.** Log the actual payment date, even when no fee applies. A tenant who pays on the 4th every month is a pattern you want to see.
3. **Lease terms.** Your lease may treat repeated late payment as a breach. A payment inside the fee grace period can still count as late for that clause, if the lease says so.

Tell tenants the difference in plain words: "Rent is due on the 1st. A late fee applies if we receive it after the 5th."

## What if the due date falls on a weekend?

If the due date falls on a weekend or holiday, follow what the lease says. The state statutes above do not move the rent due date for weekends. Write a weekend rule into your lease so both sides know.

Two common lease rules:

- **No change:** rent is due on the date, even on a Sunday. Online payments make this workable.
- **Next business day:** rent paid on the next business day is on time.

State law can still count weekends in other steps. In California, Code of Civil Procedure 1161(2) says the 3-day notice to pay rent or quit excludes Saturdays, Sundays and other judicial holidays. A notice served on a Friday does not run out on Monday.

If you take ACH or bank payments, plan for processing time. A payment started on a Saturday may not settle until Monday or Tuesday.

## How do I pick a due date and grace period?

Pick a due date and grace period that fit your tenants and your own bills. A due date that most tenants can meet is better than a strict date that most tenants miss.

Use this checklist:

- [ ] Check your state's late fee statute for a minimum grace period.
- [ ] Check city rules. Some cities add their own.
- [ ] Ask tenants when they get paid. Consider a due date after payday.
- [ ] Line up the due date with your mortgage date, with a few days of margin.
- [ ] Write the due date, grace period, fee amount, payment methods and weekend rule into the lease.
- [ ] Apply the same rule to every tenant in the same property.

## What should the lease clause say?

The lease clause should name the amount, the due date, the grace period, the fee, and the methods. A sample (check it against your state's law):

> Rent of $[amount] is due on the [1st] day of each month. If we do not receive rent by 11:59 p.m. on the [5th] day of the month, a late fee of $[amount] is due. If the due date falls on a weekend or legal holiday, rent received on the next business day is on time. You may pay by [methods].

Fill each bracket with a value that fits your state's limits. Ask a local attorney to review the clause once. Then use it on every lease.

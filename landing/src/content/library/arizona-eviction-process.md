An Arizona rent case can reach a writ in about 2 weeks on paper. The Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (Title 33, Chapter 10) sets the notices. Title 12, Chapter 8, Article 4 sets the court steps. We read each section on the Arizona Legislature site on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice for unpaid rent | 5 calendar days to pay | A.R.S. 33-1368(B) |
| Notice for a lease breach | 10 days to cure | A.R.S. 33-1368(A) |
| Notice for a health or safety breach | 5 days to cure | A.R.S. 33-1368(A) |
| Repeat breach of the same kind | 10 days' notice, no cure | A.R.S. 33-1368(A) |
| Material and irreparable breach | Immediate termination notice | A.R.S. 33-1368(A) |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | 30 days before the rental date | A.R.S. 33-1375(B) |
| Trial date | 3 to 6 days after the summons | A.R.S. 33-1377(B) |
| Trial for an irreparable breach | By the 3rd day after filing | A.R.S. 33-1377(E) |
| Earliest writ of restitution | 5 calendar days after judgment | A.R.S. 12-1178(C) |
| Appeal from justice court | 5 calendar days after judgment | A.R.S. 12-1179(A) |
| Self-help eviction | Not allowed | A.R.S. 33-1374 |

## What notice must an Arizona landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

An Arizona landlord must give a 5-day written notice for unpaid rent. A.R.S. 33-1368(B) says the notice must state the nonpayment and that the landlord will end the lease if the tenant does not pay within that time. Subsection G says "days" means calendar days.

How you deliver the notice matters. Under A.R.S. 33-1313(B), a tenant receives notice when it is handed to the tenant or sent by registered or certified mail. A mailed notice counts as received on the day the tenant gets it, or 5 days after mailing, whichever comes first.

A tenant can pay to stop the case. A.R.S. 33-1368(B) sets 3 stages:

1. **Before filing.** All past-due rent plus a reasonable late fee written in the lease reinstates the lease.
2. **After filing.** The tenant must also pay attorney fees and court costs.
3. **After judgment.** Reinstatement is up to the landlord.

A tenant may not withhold rent for a reason the act does not allow, per the same subsection.

## How long does an eviction take in Arizona?

An Arizona eviction for unpaid rent takes about 2 to 3 weeks by the statute minimums. Here is a worked timeline with a notice handed to the tenant on day 0:

| Day | Step | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Deliver the 5-day notice by hand | A.R.S. 33-1368(B) |
| 6 | Rent still unpaid: file the special detainer complaint; summons issues the same day | A.R.S. 33-1377(B) |
| 9 to 12 | Trial, 3 to 6 days after the summons | A.R.S. 33-1377(B) |
| 14 to 17 | Earliest writ of restitution, 5 calendar days after judgment | A.R.S. 12-1178(C) |

This is our calculation from the statute minimums, not a court average. Real cases often take longer. A notice sent by certified mail can add up to 5 days. For good cause, the court may postpone the trial up to 3 days in justice court or 5 days in superior court (A.R.S. 33-1377(C)). An appeal with a supersedeas bond (a bond that pauses the judgment) can stay the writ (A.R.S. 12-1179).

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Arizona, and what does it cost?

An Arizona landlord files the complaint with a justice of the peace or with the clerk of the superior court. A.R.S. 12-1175(A) names both courts. The complaint must be in writing and under oath. It must describe the premises and state the facts that give the landlord the right to possession.

A.R.S. 33-1377(B) sets the service rules. The summons must be served at least 2 days before the trial. If personal service fails, the landlord may post the summons on the main entrance and send it by certified mail the same day. The tenant then counts as served 3 days after mailing.

Two more court rules help landlords. A.R.S. 12-1175(D) says no court may require a mandatory form, if the notice or pleading meets the statute. A.R.S. 12-1176 lets either side ask for a jury.

Filing fees: each court sets them. We could not open an official fee schedule on 2026-10-19, so we do not give a number. Check the fee page of the justice court for the property's precinct before you file.

## Can an Arizona landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes, for a lease violation, with the right notice. A.R.S. 33-1368(A) sets 4 tracks:

- **Material breach.** 10 days' written notice. The lease ends if the tenant does not fix the breach in 10 days.
- **Health and safety breach** of A.R.S. 33-1341. 5 days' notice to cure.
- **Second breach of the same kind** in the lease term. 10 days' notice, then a special detainer case. No new cure right.
- **Material and irreparable breach.** For example, an illegal weapon discharge, assault or a threat on the premises. Notice for immediate termination, and a trial by the 3rd day after filing. If the court finds the breach, it orders restitution in 12 to 24 hours (A.R.S. 33-1377(E)).

Material falsification on the rental application is also a breach. Falsified criminal or eviction records cannot be cured.

No-cause: Arizona has no statewide just-cause rule in these sections. A month-to-month tenancy ends with 30 days' written notice before the rental date (A.R.S. 33-1375(B)). A week-to-week tenancy needs 10 days. If the tenant stays after that, the landlord can sue for possession. A willful holdover can cost the tenant up to 2 months' rent or twice actual damages, whichever is greater (A.R.S. 33-1375(C)).

Watch for retaliation. A.R.S. 33-1381 bars a possession case filed to punish a tenant for a code complaint, a repair complaint or joining a tenant union. A complaint within 6 months before the landlord's action creates a presumption of retaliation. Default in rent is an exception.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Arizona?

A sheriff or constable removes the tenant. A.R.S. 12-1181(B) says the writ of restitution "shall be executed by the sheriff or constable." The writ cannot issue until 5 calendar days after judgment (A.R.S. 12-1178(C)).

After the writ, the tenant must stay out. A.R.S. 12-1178(D) makes it criminal trespass in the third degree to stay or return without the owner's permission. On the day after the writ is executed, the landlord may cut landlord-provided utilities, but only a person the utility authorizes may disconnect them (A.R.S. 33-1368(D)). The landlord must then follow A.R.S. 33-1370 for the tenant's property.

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Arizona?

No. A.R.S. 33-1374 says a landlord may not take possession by force, by removing the tenant's things, or by cutting electric, gas, water or other essential service. The exceptions are abandonment, surrender, or a step the act allows.

The penalty is steep. Under A.R.S. 33-1367, the tenant may recover possession or end the lease. In both cases, the tenant can recover up to 2 months' rent or twice actual damages, whichever is greater.

## What credit score do most landlords want?

No law sets a minimum credit score for renting. Each landlord sets its own floor. Many set it somewhere in the 600s.

A useful reference is the FICO scale. myFICO says FICO Scores range from 300 to 850. It calls 670 or above good, 740 or above very good, and 800 or above exceptional.

Markets with high rents often ask for more. Insurent, a lease guarantor company, says some landlords ask for income of more than 40 times the monthly rent and a credit score above 700. It describes this for the markets it serves, not as a national rule.

| Score range (FICO) | myFICO label | A common landlord response |
|---|---|---|
| 800–850 | Exceptional | Approve if income and history pass |
| 740–799 | Very good | Approve if income and history pass |
| 670–739 | Good | Approve if income and history pass |
| 580–669 | Below good | Review the report items; consider a guarantor |
| Below 580 | Poor | Review the report items; decide by written policy |

The right column is a sample policy, not a rule. Write your own and use it for every applicant.

## Why is the score only one input?

The score is only one input because it measures loan habits, not rent habits. myFICO says payment history makes up 35% of a FICO Score, amounts owed 30%, length of history 15%, new credit 10% and credit mix 10%. None of those is rent.

The CFPB has found problems in tenant screening reports. Its reports on the industry say prior rent payment history is overwhelmingly not reflected in screening reports. Industry estimates put rent payment coverage at only 1.7% to 2.3% of U.S. renters. The CFPB also found renters filed more than 16,000 complaints about wrong information on their reports.

So read the report, not only the number. Look at:

- **Payment history:** are late payments recent or old?
- **Collections:** what type, how old, paid or not?
- **Balances:** is debt rising fast?
- **Errors:** does anything look like it belongs to someone else?

Pair the report with rent history and income. A tenant with a 640 score and 4 years of on-time rent may be a better risk than one with 720 and no rent history.

## Should medical debt count?

Many landlords exclude medical collections from their credit criteria. A medical bill often comes from illness or an insurance gap, not from a choice. It says little about whether a tenant pays rent.

If you exclude it, write the rule down: "Medical collections are not counted." Then apply it to every applicant. Some screening services let you filter medical collections out of the decision. Ask yours.

Check state and local law too. Some places limit how landlords may use certain debts or records. Rules in this area change often.

## How do I weigh thin credit files?

Weigh a thin file with other proof, not with the missing score. A thin file means the applicant has few or no credit accounts. It is common for young adults and people new to the U.S.

Ask for evidence that shows the same thing a score tries to show: that the person pays bills on time.

1. **Rent records.** Bank statements or a rent ledger from a past landlord.
2. **Income proof.** Pay stubs, an offer letter or bank deposits.
3. **Landlord reference.** A call to the last landlord, with written questions.
4. **Utility or phone bills** paid on time.
5. **A guarantor,** if your policy allows one.

Apply the same thin-file path to every applicant with a thin file. Do not ask for more documents from some groups than others. That can break Fair Housing law.

## Can a higher deposit offset low credit?

A higher deposit can offset low credit only within your state's deposit cap. Many states cap deposits.

California is an example. Civil Code 1950.5(c) says most landlords may not take a deposit of more than one month's rent. A landlord who is a natural person, or an LLC of natural persons, with no more than 2 properties and 4 units, may take up to two months' rent. Service members are an exception to the small-landlord rule.

Other options where a deposit cap is tight:

- **Guarantor or co-signer** who passes your criteria.
- **Lease guarantee company** that backs the lease for a fee the tenant pays.
- **Shorter first lease** with renewal based on payment record.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What must I do if credit leads to a denial?

If a credit report leads you to deny an applicant, or to set worse terms such as a higher deposit, you must send an adverse action notice. The Fair Credit Reporting Act, 15 U.S.C. 1681m(a), requires it.

The notice must include:

- [ ] A statement of the adverse action.
- [ ] The credit score you used, if any, and key factors.
- [ ] The name, address and phone number of the reporting company, including a toll-free number for national companies.
- [ ] A statement that the reporting company did not make the decision.
- [ ] The applicant's right to a free copy of the report within 60 days and to dispute errors.

Most screening services provide a template. Use it every time.

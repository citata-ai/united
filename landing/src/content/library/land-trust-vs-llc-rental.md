## What privacy does a land trust give?

A land trust can place title in a trustee's name, but privacy depends on the state and transaction. Do not buy a structure based on a promise of complete anonymity. Ask what each public record and required disclosure will show.

Start with the basic distinction:

| Question | Land trust | LLC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main legal role | Holds title through a trustee under an agreement | Operates as a state-created entity |
| Who acts? | Trustee or authorized direction holder under the documents | Authorized manager or member under the documents |
| Public record question | What does the recorded instrument disclose? | What do formation and property records disclose? |
| Liability question | Which state provisions and duties apply? | Which entity shield and personal obligations apply? |
| Tax question | How is this trust treated? | What classification or election applies? |
| Ongoing task | Maintain trust and title records | Maintain entity and operating records |

Florida's Land Trust Act, section 689.071, provides for title held by a trustee under a recorded instrument. Illinois's Land Trust Beneficial Interest Disclosure Act also defines a trustee-held title arrangement. Read the local text rather than using an online form from another state.

Illinois provides a useful privacy limit. Its disclosure act requires identification of beneficiaries in specified applications to state or local government. A trustee's name on a deed therefore does not establish privacy in every related transaction.

Before forming a trust, ask counsel to map the records. Include the deed, loan application, insurance application and required local filings. Keep lawful disclosure separate from your wish to reduce casual public searches.

## What liability protection does an LLC give?

An LLC can separate company obligations from personal liability under the applicable statute. It is not a promise that every claim stops at the entity. Ask counsel to distinguish company debts, your own acts and obligations you personally sign.

For example, Florida Statutes section 605.0304 says a member or manager is not personally liable for company obligations solely because of that status. The phrase solely because matters. Do not read it as immunity for every action connected with a rental.

A land trust is not automatically the opposite. Florida section 689.071 limits liability based solely on beneficiary status, subject to the statute and agreements. That is why a blanket statement that land trusts have no protection can mislead an owner.

Ask an attorney to review your actual risks. Identify who owns the property, signs contracts, directs repairs and gives guarantees. Request a clear explanation of which assets remain exposed under the proposed arrangement.

Coordinate insurance with the legal structure. Give the agent the proposed owner, trustee and operating party. Ask which names and interests the policy should show. A deed change does not by itself confirm that your insurance follows the new arrangement.

Keep operating records that match the structure you choose. Use clear contracts and traceable payments. Ask your accountant and attorney how to manage accounts and owner contributions. Do not rely on the formation certificate as your complete operating plan.

## Do owners combine land trusts and LLCs?

A combined structure may be possible, but each part needs a stated purpose. Do not add entities merely because a diagram appears protective. Ask what practical benefit the extra arrangement provides for your specific rental.

Florida section 689.071 includes an entity within its definition of a beneficiary. That supports considering an LLC beneficiary under Florida's framework. It does not establish that every state, lender or insurer will accept the same arrangement.

Request a plain ownership map from counsel. It should identify the deed holder, beneficiary, direction holder and person authorized to sign the lease. Add the bank account holder and insured parties. Make sure your rental manager can follow the map.

Ask the lender to review the proposed transfer before documents are recorded. Provide the actual plan, not a vague description of a trust. Have counsel check loan provisions, consent requirements and any relevant legal exception. Do not assume a trust label resolves those issues.

Review taxes separately. The IRS says a single-member LLC generally is disregarded for federal income tax unless it elects corporate treatment. That rule has exceptions for employment and certain excise taxes. It does not determine the treatment of every trust arrangement.

Compare annual administrative work as well as setup cost. Ask for a written list of filings, trustee duties, account tasks and professional fees. A structure that nobody maintains can create confusion when you refinance, sell or change managers.

## Which states use land trusts?

Florida and Illinois provide concrete statutory examples, but their rules should not be treated as identical. Florida has its Land Trust Act. Illinois has a land trust disclosure statute with its own definition and required disclosures.

Avoid a national list that treats every trust as an Illinois-style land trust. The words land trust can describe different legal arrangements. Ask a lawyer in the property's state to identify the relevant law and accepted title practice.

Bring the current deed, loan, insurance, leases and existing entity documents to the review. State your main goal in a sentence. It may be ownership privacy, succession, shared ownership or liability planning. Ask which tool addresses that goal with the least unnecessary work.

Request written answers about transfer taxes, recording, property tax treatment and local registration before signing. These are questions to resolve for the transaction, not costs that every owner will owe. Ask who will complete each required step.

Then update the rental management records to match the approved structure. Confirm the authorized signer, payment recipient and emergency decision contact.

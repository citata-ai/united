## Can I use my own lease?

Yes. In most cases you must use your own standard lease. 24 CFR 982.308(b)(2) says that if you use a standard lease form for unassisted tenants, the voucher lease "must be in such standard form (plus the HUD-prescribed tenancy addendum)." If you do not have a standard form, you may use another lease, such as a PHA model lease.

The statute says the same. 42 U.S.C. 1437f(o)(7)(B) requires a lease in "a standard form used in the locality" with terms that apply generally to unassisted tenants. In the HAP contract you certify that your lease meets this rule.

The Santa Cruz County housing authority puts it simply: "The landlord uses his/her own lease with a Housing Authority-provided Tenancy Addendum."

Every lease must state five items (24 CFR 982.308(d)):

- [ ] The names of the owner and the tenant.
- [ ] The unit: address, unit number and any other ID.
- [ ] The term: the first term and any renewal terms.
- [ ] The monthly rent to owner.
- [ ] Which utilities and appliances the owner supplies, and which the family supplies.

The PHA may review the lease for compliance with state and local law. It may refuse the tenancy if the lease does not comply (982.308(c)). For pre-1978 units, sign the lead-based paint disclosure with the lease (982.305(b)(1)(ii)).

## What is the HUD tenancy addendum?

The tenancy addendum is HUD's set of required lease terms for the voucher program. It is form HUD-52641-A, and it is also Part C of the HAP contract. The edition we read (8/2009, posted by the Santa Cruz County housing authority) has 17 sections. Ask your PHA for its current version.

| Addendum section | What it says, in short |
|---|---|
| 2. Lease | The tenant can enforce the addendum; it controls in a conflict |
| 3. Use of unit | Only PHA-approved members live there; no sublease or assignment |
| 4. Rent to owner | No increase in the first term; never above reasonable rent or your unassisted rents |
| 5. Family payment | The tenant pays only its share; PHA late payment is not a lease violation |
| 6. Other fees | No charges for meals, services or items free to other tenants |
| 7. Maintenance | Owner maintains to HUD standards; not responsible for tenant damage |
| 8. Termination by owner | Grounds, good cause, notices, eviction by court only |
| 9-11. HAP link, PHA termination, move-out | The lease ends if the HAP contract or assistance ends |
| 12. Security deposit | Allowed; itemize deductions and refund the rest |
| 13. Discrimination | No discrimination on protected grounds |
| 15. Changes | Changes in writing; 60 days' notice to the PHA for rent changes |

## Which lease terms are not allowed?

Any term that conflicts with the addendum or the program rules is not allowed. Common problem clauses:

1. **Rent increase in the first term.** Barred by 24 CFR 982.309(a)(3) and addendum section 4(b).
2. **Tenant pays the PHA share if the PHA is late.** Barred. The tenant "is not responsible" for the HAP portion (addendum 5(d)).
3. **Extra fees for normal items.** Barred if the item is customarily in the rent or free to unassisted tenants (24 CFR 982.510(c)).
4. **Required meals or services.** The lease may not require them, and nonpayment cannot be grounds to evict (982.510(b)).
5. **Self-help eviction.** The owner may only evict by court action (addendum 8(f)).
6. **No-cause ending in the first term.** Other good cause in the first term must be "something the family did or failed to do" (addendum 8(d)(1)).
7. **Higher deposit than for others.** The PHA may bar a deposit above market practice or above your unassisted tenants' deposits (addendum 12(a)).

Some terms are required, not just allowed. Under 24 CFR 982.310(c), the lease must let you end the tenancy for drug-related and certain other criminal activity. The addendum includes these grounds.

## How long must the first lease term be?

The first term must be at least one year. 24 CFR 982.309(a) and 42 U.S.C. 1437f(o)(7)(A) set the rule. The PHA may approve a shorter first term if both are true:

- the shorter term "would improve housing opportunities for the tenant"; and
- it "is the prevailing local market practice."

After the first term, the lease decides renewals. The Santa Cruz County housing authority says month-to-month leases "are acceptable." Ask your PHA about its policy.

Some changes restart the approval process. Under 24 CFR 982.308(g)(2), assistance stops unless the PHA approves a new tenancy and signs a new HAP contract if you change:

| Change | New HAP contract needed? |
|---|---|
| Who pays utilities or supplies appliances | Yes |
| The lease term | Yes |
| The family moves to another unit, even in the same building | Yes |
| The rent amount | No, but 60 days' notice and a rent check |
| Other agreed lease changes | No, but in writing with a copy to the PHA |

## Which clauses win if the lease and addendum conflict?

The addendum wins. 24 CFR 982.308(f)(2) says the addendum must be added "word-for-word" to your lease. It says "the terms of the tenancy addendum shall prevail over any other provisions of the lease." The tenant "shall have the right to enforce the tenancy addendum against the owner."

The addendum's own section 14 says the same. Its requirements control over "any other provisions of the lease or any other agreement between the owner and the tenant." Section 15(a) adds that the owner and tenant "may not make any change in the tenancy addendum."

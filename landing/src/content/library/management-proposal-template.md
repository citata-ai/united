An owner gets three proposals in one week. One is a 20-page brochure with the fee on page 17. One is an email with "8% plus leasing". One is 6 pages: their home, their rent range with sources, every fee in one table and a total for the first year. The third one is easy to say yes to.

A proposal is not a brochure. It is a decision page for one owner and one property.

## Which sections does a proposal need?

A property management proposal needs 9 sections, in this order. Each one answers a question the owner will ask.

| # | Section | Owner question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your goals and the property | Did they listen to me? |
| 2 | Rental analysis | What will it rent for? |
| 3 | What we do | What do I get? |
| 4 | Fees and first-year total | What will it cost me? |
| 5 | Guarantees and cancel terms | What if it goes wrong? |
| 6 | Tenant screening standards | Who will live in my home? |
| 7 | Sample owner report | What will I see each month? |
| 8 | Proof | Who else trusts you? |
| 9 | Next steps | What happens if I say yes? |

Keep it to 4 to 8 pages. Owners read the fee table and the rent number first.

## How do I show fees clearly?

Show fees clearly by putting every fee in one table and adding a first-year total for this owner's rent. A fee on page 17 makes an owner doubt every other page.

A worked first-year total for a $2,000 rent:

| Fee | Terms | First year |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly fee | 8% of $2,000 × 12 | $1,920 |
| Leasing fee | 50% of one month's rent | $1,000 |
| Setup fee | One-time | $150 |
| Renewal fee | Not in year 1 | $0 |
| Estimated total | | $3,070 |

The rent and fee levels are an example for the math only. Run your own fees through the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) and paste the result.

Show the total next to the rent. For context, the 2021 Rental Housing Finance Survey found a national median rent receipt of $1,000 per unit per month. The median operating expense was $380 per unit per month (HUD and Census, 2022). Owners think in net income, so help them see it.

## Should I add a rental analysis?

Yes. The rental analysis is often the part that wins the account, because it gives value before the owner pays you. Make it credible:

- Use 3 to 5 comparable rentals from the last 90 days, with beds, baths and days on market.
- Add one public benchmark. HUD publishes Fair Market Rents by metro and county (HUD User). Zillow publishes the Zillow Observed Rent Index, a typical market rent by region (Zillow Research). Our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) shows FY 2027 Fair Market Rents by metro.
- Give a range, not one number, and say why.
- Name your sources and dates.

Do not overstate the rent to win the deal. A high number that sits vacant loses the owner in month 2.

## How fast should I send it?

Send the proposal within one business day of the walk-through. That is our working rule, not a study result. Owners often talk to several managers in the same week, and the first clear proposal sets the frame for the others.

To send fast, keep the template ready, with your fee table, screening text and sample report filled in. After the walk-through, you only add the property, the goals and the rental analysis.

## Rules to check before you send

- **Fair Housing.** HUD's rule at 24 CFR 100.75 covers written statements and documents used for the rental of a dwelling. Describe your screening criteria, never the kind of renter you prefer (eCFR, 24 CFR 100.75).
- **Guarantees.** FTC guides at 16 CFR Part 239 say to disclose material conditions and to perform guarantees promptly and fully.
- **State rules.** License numbers, trust account rules and required disclosures differ by state.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

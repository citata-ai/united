## What is preventive maintenance for a rental?

Preventive maintenance for a rental is work you schedule before something breaks. It covers heating and cooling, roof and gutters, plumbing, the water heater, and life-safety devices. The goal is fewer emergency calls, longer equipment life and safer homes for tenants.

Preventive work has 3 parts:

- **A calendar.** Each task has a month and an interval.
- **A log.** Each task has a date, a person and a photo.
- **A report.** The owner sees what you did and what you found.

## Which tasks happen every month or quarter?

Monthly and quarterly tasks are short and cover safety and airflow. You can assign some to the tenant in the lease and check them at visits.

| Interval | Task | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | Test smoke alarms | U.S. Fire Administration |
| Monthly or as recommended | Clean or replace furnace and AC filters | DOE Energy Saver Guide |
| Every 3 months | Drain a quart from the water heater tank to remove sediment | DOE Energy Saver Guide |
| Yearly | Replace 9-volt smoke alarm batteries and hardwired backup batteries | U.S. Fire Administration |
| Every 10 years | Replace the whole smoke alarm | U.S. Fire Administration |

The DOE Energy Saver Guide also suggests a water heater setting of 120°F, "a temperature considered safe for most of the population." It notes most storage water heaters last 10 to 15 years. Add the install date to your asset log, so you can plan the replacement cost before it fails.

## Spring checklist

Spring work repairs winter damage and gets cooling ready.

- [ ] Service the air conditioner before the first hot week; clear plants and trash from the outdoor unit
- [ ] Replace the HVAC filter
- [ ] Inspect the roof for missing or lifted shingles and damaged flashing
- [ ] Clean gutters and downspouts; make sure water flows away from the foundation
- [ ] Look for water stains on ceilings and in the attic
- [ ] Turn on outdoor hose bibs and check for leaks from freeze damage
- [ ] Check the irrigation system, if any
- [ ] Contact 811 a few business days before any digging, such as planting or fence posts

The 811 service is free. It marks buried utility lines. The 811 Before You Dig site says damage to buried utilities costs the U.S. about $30 billion a year, and not calling 811 first is the top cause.

## Summer checklist

Summer work keeps cooling running and the outside sound.

- [ ] Check the AC condensate drain line for clogs and leaks
- [ ] Replace or check the HVAC filter again during peak use
- [ ] Trim trees and shrubs away from the roof, siding and AC unit
- [ ] Check exterior paint, caulk and wood trim for rot or gaps
- [ ] Inspect decks, rails and steps for loose boards and fasteners
- [ ] Look for pest entry points around pipes and vents
- [ ] Clean the dryer vent from the dryer to the outside hood

The U.S. Fire Administration reports that failure to clean was the leading factor in home clothes dryer fires from 2018 to 2020, at 31%. A yearly vent cleaning is a small cost against that risk.

## Fall checklist

Fall work gets heating ready and protects the house from water and cold.

- [ ] Service the furnace or heat pump before the first cold night
- [ ] Replace the HVAC filter
- [ ] Clean gutters again after leaves fall
- [ ] Test smoke and CO alarms; replace any alarm past its date
- [ ] Have a pro inspect the chimney and fireplace, if the home has one
- [ ] Shut off and drain outdoor hose bibs; add covers
- [ ] Seal gaps around windows and doors
- [ ] Check the sump pump, if the home has one

The Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) says to put CO alarms on every level and outside sleeping areas. Fall is the best time to check them, because heating systems start to run.

## Winter checklist

Winter work prevents frozen pipes and heat loss.

- [ ] Check pipe insulation in crawl spaces, garages and outside walls
- [ ] Tell tenants how to find the main water shut-off
- [ ] Give tenants your rule for heat when they travel (for example, never below a set temperature)
- [ ] Check for ice dams and roof leaks after storms
- [ ] Clear snow and ice from walks and steps, or confirm the tenant's duty in the lease
- [ ] Replace the HVAC filter
- [ ] Check the water heater for leaks and drain a quart

Watch for: the lease must say who does snow removal and filter changes. If the lease is silent, assume you do them.

## Which HVAC, roof and plumbing checks go in each season?

The core rule is simple: service each system before its peak season. Here is the calendar in one table.

| System | Spring | Summer | Fall | Winter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cooling | Service, clear unit | Drain line, filter | None | None |
| Heating | None | None | Service, filter | Filter, check heat |
| Roof and gutters | Inspect, clean | Trim trees | Clean after leaves | Check for ice dams |
| Plumbing | Hose bibs on, leaks | Pest gaps at pipes | Hose bibs off | Pipe insulation |
| Water heater | Drain a quart | Drain a quart | Drain a quart | Drain a quart |
| Alarms | Monthly test | Monthly test | Full check, dates | Monthly test |

## How do you show preventive maintenance to owners?

Show preventive maintenance to owners with a seasonal report and a cost per door. Owners see the bill for prevention. They do not see the leak that did not happen, unless you show it.

Use this format for each property, 4 times a year:

1. **Tasks done:** a list with dates and who did them.
2. **Photos:** filter, gutters, outdoor unit, any finding.
3. **Findings:** each issue with a priority and a cost estimate.
4. **Approvals:** repairs above your approval limit.
5. **Next season:** what is planned.

Worked example: you manage 40 single-family homes. Use example prices: spring HVAC service at $95 per home and gutter cleaning at $150 per home. Put in your own vendor prices. That is $245 per door, or $9,800 for the season across 40 doors. In the report, put the spring cost next to the number of AC calls last summer and the average cost of each call. The owner can then judge the value with real numbers from your own records.

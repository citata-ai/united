## Which license must a Tampa property manager hold, and how do I look it up?

A Tampa property manager who rents homes for others for pay must hold an active Florida real estate license. Florida Statutes 475.01 defines a broker as a person who, for another and for compensation, "rents, or offers, attempts or agrees to" negotiate the rental of real property. The definition also covers anyone who advertises rental property lists.

The stakes are high. Florida Statutes 475.42 says a person may not operate as a broker or sales associate without a valid, current, active license. A violation is a third-degree felony. A sales associate may work only for the broker who is registered as the employer.

Florida Statutes 475.011 lists exemptions. Three matter for owners:

- **You, leasing your own property.** An owner that leases its own real property is exempt.
- **Apartment leasing staff.** A salaried employee in the on-site rental office of an apartment community.
- **Condo or co-op managers.** A salaried manager who rents units for terms of 1 year or less.

How to check a manager in 4 steps:

1. **Ask for both license numbers:** the person's and the broker's.
2. **Search the Florida DBPR license database** at myfloridalicense.com.
3. **Confirm "active" status.** Florida law requires an active license, not only a valid one.
4. **Match the employer.** The sales associate's registered broker must be the firm you sign with.

## Which local rules must a Tampa manager know?

The rules that most often cost Tampa owners money are the state deposit rules in Florida Statutes 83.49. A good manager can explain each one without notes.

| Rule | What the law says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| How to hold the deposit | Separate non-interest account in a Florida bank, separate interest account with interest to the tenant, or a surety bond plus 5% simple interest | 83.49(1) |
| Interest option | At least 75% of the account's average rate, or 5% simple interest a year, the landlord's choice | 83.49(1)(b) |
| No mixing | Deposit money may not be mixed with the landlord's funds or pledged | 83.49(1) |
| Deposit notice | Within 30 days of receipt, name the bank and state if interest is paid. Not required for landlords with fewer than 5 units | 83.49(2) |
| No claim | Return the deposit within 15 days after the lease ends | 83.49(3)(a) |
| Claim | Send written notice by certified mail or e-mail within 30 days. The tenant has 15 days to object | 83.49(3)(a) |

The deposit notice rule applies to a manager who rents your units if the landlord side has 5 or more units. Ask the manager to show you the notice template they use, with the exact disclosure text from 83.49(2)(d).

Worked example: a Tampa two-bedroom at $1,917 a month, HUD's FY2027 Fair Market Rent for the metro, down 3.0% from FY2026. With a deposit of one month's rent in an account where the landlord elects 5% simple interest, the tenant earns $1,917 × 5% = $95.85 a year. ## What should a Tampa management agreement say?

A Tampa management agreement should spell out each duty and each fee. Florida law does not give one required form, so read the draft line by line. A checklist:

- **Fees.** The monthly fee, leasing fee, renewal fee, and any markup on repairs.
- **Deposits.** Which of the 3 methods in 83.49(1) the firm uses, and in whose name.
- **Owner money.** When rent reaches you each month, and how big the reserve is.
- **Reports.** A monthly statement and a year-end statement for taxes.
- **Repair limit.** The normal approval limit and a separate emergency limit.
- **Term and exit.** The notice period, any cancellation fee, and how deposits and files move to you.
- **License data.** The broker's name and license number in the contract.

Add a written hurricane plan. Tampa sits on the Gulf coast, and a storm plan agreed in calm weather saves calls in a crisis:

1. **Before a storm.** Who secures the home, clears the yard and shuts off what needs it.
2. **Tenant notice.** Who tells tenants about plans and contacts.
3. **After a storm.** Who inspects, within how many days, and with photos.
4. **Emergency spend.** The dollar amount the manager may spend without your approval.
5. **Insurance.** Who files the claim and meets the adjuster.
6. **Your contact.** How the manager reaches you if power and phones fail.

## Which questions should I ask a Tampa manager before I sign?

Ask questions that need a document or a number. Twelve to start with:

1. What are your license number and your broker's license number?
2. How many doors do you manage in Hillsborough and Pinellas counties?
3. How do you hold tenant deposits under 83.49, and in which bank?
4. Show me your 30-day deposit notice template.
5. What was your average days on market last year for homes like mine?
6. Show me a sample monthly owner statement.
7. What is your repair approval limit, and do you mark up vendor bills?
8. What did you do for your owners after the last major storm?
9. Who files insurance claims, and do you charge for it?
10. How do you screen applicants with one written standard for all?
11. What happens to deposits and files if I end the contract?
12. Can I call 2 owners who have used you for more than 2 years?

The screening question matters for Fair Housing. One written standard for everyone protects you and the applicant.

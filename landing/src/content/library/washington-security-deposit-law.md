## Washington security deposit rules at a glance

Washington puts its deposit rules in the Residential Landlord-Tenant Act, chapter 59.18 RCW. The table sums up each rule. We read every section on the Washington State Legislature site on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | No dollar cap | Chapter 59.18 RCW |
| Written lease | Required before you take any deposit | RCW 59.18.260(1)-(2) |
| Move-in checklist | Signed and dated by both sides; tenant gets a copy | RCW 59.18.260(2)-(3) |
| Where to hold it | Trust account at a Washington bank or licensed escrow agent | RCW 59.18.270 |
| Receipt and bank notice | Written receipt plus name and address of the bank | RCW 59.18.270 |
| Installments | Tenant may ask in writing if deposits and fees exceed 25% of first month's rent | RCW 59.18.610 |
| Holding fee cap | 25% of first month's rent | RCW 59.18.610(4)(b) |
| Return deadline | 30 days after move-out | RCW 59.18.280(1)(a) |
| Itemized statement | Full and specific, with invoices or estimates | RCW 59.18.280(1)(a)-(b) |
| Late statement | Liable for the full deposit | RCW 59.18.280(2) |
| Intentional refusal | Up to 2 times the deposit | RCW 59.18.280(2) |
| Lawsuit for more than the deposit | Within 3 years (leases from 2023-07-23) | RCW 59.18.280(3)(c) |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Washington?

Washington sets no maximum security deposit. Chapter 59.18 RCW does not name a dollar or a month limit. But the state limits how and when you collect the money.

First, you need a written rental agreement. The lease must state the terms under which you may keep all or part of the deposit (RCW 59.18.260(1)). Second, you need a written checklist at the start of the tenancy. It must describe the condition and cleanliness of walls, paint, carpets, floors, furniture and appliances. Both sides sign and date it, and the tenant gets a copy (RCW 59.18.260(2)-(3)).

Third, the tenant may ask to pay in installments (RCW 59.18.610). This right applies when the deposits and nonrefundable fees total more than 25% of the first full month's rent, or when you require last month's rent at move-in. For a lease of 3 months or longer, the tenant may pay in 3 equal monthly parts. For shorter terms, 2 parts. You may not charge a fee or interest for the plan. A refusal costs one month's rent as a penalty, plus attorneys' fees.

**Worked example.** Rent is $2,000. You ask for a $1,000 deposit and a $200 cleaning fee. That is $1,200, which is 60% of the first month's rent. The 25% line is $500. The tenant may ask in writing to pay the $1,200 in 3 parts of $400 on a 12-month lease.

A holding fee is separate. Under RCW 59.18.610(4)(b), you may not ask for a fee to hold a unit above 25% of the first month's rent.

## How many days does a Washington landlord have to return a security deposit?

A Washington landlord has 30 days. The clock starts when the rental agreement ends and the tenant moves out. If the tenant abandons the unit, the clock starts when you learn of the abandonment (RCW 59.18.280(1)(a)).

Within those 30 days you must send three things together:

1. **The statement.** A full and specific statement of the basis for each deduction.
2. **The proof.** Copies of estimates or paid invoices for damage charges.
3. **The refund.** Any balance due to the tenant.

You comply if you hand these to the tenant or mail them first-class to the last known address within the 30 days. Put the mailing date on your calendar on the day the tenant hands back the keys.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Washington?

A Washington landlord may deduct only on the terms written in the lease. The lease must say that you may keep the deposit for damage the tenant is responsible for (RCW 59.18.260(1)). Unpaid rent and other charges owed are also valid uses (RCW 59.18.280(4)).

RCW 59.18.280(1)(c) bans four kinds of deductions:

- **Ordinary wear.** Wear from ordinary use of the premises.
- **Routine carpet cleaning.** Unless you document carpet wear beyond ordinary use.
- **Items not on the checklist.** Repair or replacement of fixtures, appliances or furniture whose condition the move-in checklist did not reasonably record.
- **Full replacement for partial damage.** If only part of an item is damaged, you may charge only for that part.

When your own staff do the repair, you must also list the time spent and the hourly rate. For materials, attach a bill, receipt or vendor price list (RCW 59.18.280(1)(b)).

## Does a Washington landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. A Washington landlord must give a "full and specific statement" for each amount kept (RCW 59.18.280(1)(a)). For damage, the statement must include copies of estimates or invoices.

The documentation rules do not apply when you keep money only for rent or other charges owed, not for damage (RCW 59.18.280(4)). Even then, send the statement and any refund within 30 days.

Damage charges without that proof carry another risk. RCW 59.18.280(3)(b) says you may not charge them to the tenant, report them to a credit bureau or screening service, or send them to collections.

## Must a Washington landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

A Washington landlord must keep the deposit in a trust account but need not pay interest. RCW 59.18.270 has these rules:

- **Trust account.** Deposit the money promptly in a trust account used only for tenant deposits, at a financial institution or licensed escrow agent in Washington.
- **Interest.** The landlord keeps the interest, unless the parties agree otherwise in writing.
- **Receipt.** Give the tenant a written receipt.
- **Bank notice.** Tell the tenant in writing the name, address and location of the bank, and any later change.
- **Sale of the building.** Move the deposits to the new owner's trust account. The new owner must tell the tenants.
- **Foreclosure.** If the deposit does not pass to the new owner, the old owner must refund it right away or face up to 2 times the deposit.

Nonrefundable fees follow their own rule. RCW 59.18.285 says a nonrefundable fee may not be called a deposit. The lease must be in writing and say clearly that the fee is nonrefundable. If it does not, the fee becomes a refundable deposit.

## What is the penalty for a Washington landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

The penalty starts at the full deposit. If you miss the 30-day statement, the documentation or the refund, you owe the tenant the full amount (RCW 59.18.280(2)). In the tenant's lawsuit, you also lose the right to raise any claim or defense for keeping the money. There are two exceptions: events beyond your control, or abandonment by the tenant.

For an intentional refusal, the court may award up to 2 times the deposit. The winning party also recovers court costs and a reasonable attorneys' fee.

| Mistake | Result | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| No written lease or checklist | Liable for the deposit, plus fees | RCW 59.18.260(5) |
| Statement or refund after day 30 | Liable for the full deposit | RCW 59.18.280(2) |
| Intentional refusal | Up to 2 times the deposit | RCW 59.18.280(2) |
| Nonrefundable fee not in writing | Liable for the fee | RCW 59.18.285 |
| Refused installment plan | One month's rent penalty | RCW 59.18.610(5) |

## Washington deposit checklist for property managers

Use this list on every Washington unit:

- [ ] Written lease states the terms for keeping the deposit.
- [ ] Move-in checklist covers walls, floors, furniture and appliances, signed and dated by both sides.
- [ ] Tenant has a copy of the checklist.
- [ ] Deposit is in a Washington trust account; tenant has a receipt and the bank's name and address.
- [ ] Any nonrefundable fee is marked "nonrefundable" in the written lease.
- [ ] Installment plan offered in writing when deposits and fees exceed 25% of the first month's rent.
- [ ] Move-out statement, invoices and refund mailed by day 30.

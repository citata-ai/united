## What is Ask Maps?

Ask Maps is a conversational AI feature in the Google Maps app. You tap "Ask Maps", type a question, and get an AI answer with a map of places. Google announced it on 2026-03-12, in a post by Miriam Daniel, VP and GM of Google Maps.

Facts from Google's own pages:

| Fact | What Google says |
|---|---|
| Launch | 2026-03-12, rolling out in the US and India |
| Devices | Android and iOS first, desktop "coming soon" (at launch) |
| Data | More than 300 million places, reviews from more than 500 million contributors |
| Personal | Answers use places the person searched for or saved in Maps |
| Actions | Book a table, save a place, share it, get directions |
| Accuracy | "AI-powered answers can sometimes be incorrect" (Maps Help) |

Google's example questions are long and specific. One is about a public tennis court with lights that is open tonight. That matters for property managers. An owner can now ask Maps a full question, such as "Who manages single-family rentals near me and answers fast?"

## What does Ask Maps pull from a Business Profile?

Google has not published which Business Profile fields Ask Maps uses. The launch post talks about places, reviews and tips from the Maps community. It does not mention Business Profiles or ads by name.

What Google does document is how local results work in general. Its Business Profile help page names 3 factors:

1. **Relevance.** How well a profile matches what the person searched for. Google asks for complete, specific business details.
2. **Distance.** How far the business is from the person, or from the place in the query.
3. **Prominence.** How well known the business is. Google names links from other sites and the number of reviews as inputs.

For AI features in Search, Google adds one more line: check that your Business Profile information is up to date. Google says there are no extra technical requirements and no special schema for AI features.

So the honest answer is this. Ask Maps reads the same place data that powers Maps. A complete, correct profile is the base. Anything more specific is a guess until Google says more.

## Do review words change the Ask Maps answer?

Google has not said that the words in reviews change Ask Maps answers. It says Maps "analyzes information" from places, including reviews. It does not give weights.

There is a fair hypothesis, though. Ask Maps takes long questions with details, like "with lights on" or "without a long line". To answer those, the model needs text that holds those details. Star ratings alone do not say "fast repairs" or "clear owner statements".

What to do (hypothesis, not a Google rule):

- **Ask for specific reviews.** "If you are happy, say what we did for you" gets more detail than "Please leave 5 stars".
- **Ask owners and tenants both.** Owners write about statements, fees and vacancy. Tenants write about repairs and response time.
- **Do not script or reward reviews.** Google's review policy bans incentives and asking only happy customers.

## How is Maps AI different from AI Mode?

Ask Maps and AI Mode both use Gemini, but they answer from different places and for different jobs.

| | Ask Maps | AI Mode |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Google Maps app | Google Search |
| Best for | "Which place near me..." | Any question, including research |
| Main data Google names | Places, reviews, Maps community tips | Web content, through "query fan-out" (many searches at once) |
| Your page that matters most | Business Profile and reviews | Your website and pages about you |
| Personal input | Saved and searched places | Search context |
| Accuracy note | "Can sometimes be incorrect" | "Doesn't always get it right" |

For a property manager, the two overlap. An owner may ask AI Mode "how much does property management cost in Denver" and then ask Maps "who are the best managers near me". You need both your site and your profile to be right.

## What should a property manager update first?

Update the facts Google already documents, in this order. Each step takes under an hour for one office.

1. **Name.** Use the name on your sign, site and letterhead. Google's guidelines ban extra information in the name, such as service words or a location, and can suspend the profile.
2. **Primary category.** Pick the one category closest to your core work. Add others only if true.
3. **Service area.** List the cities and ZIP codes you serve. Google says the area should not go farther than about 2 hours of driving from your base.
4. **Hours, phone and website.** Match the website footer word for word.
5. **Description and services.** Say what you manage: single-family, small multifamily, HOA, Section 8. Name the fee model in plain words.
6. **Photos.** Add your office, team and real properties you manage, with owner consent.
7. **Reviews.** Ask every owner and tenant at a natural point: lease signed, repair done, first statement sent. Reply to each review with facts, never with private data.
8. **Website.** Make one page per city you serve, with fees and services in plain text. Use our [AI search checklist for property management websites](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website).

Watch for: the profile is not the only source. In our 50-city test, AI engines cited directories such as Expertise.com in 93 of 150 answers (our data, 2026-10-19). See the full data in the [AI Property Manager Index 2026](/research/ai-property-manager-index-2026).

## How do you check what Ask Maps says about you?

Ask Maps changes by person, place and day, so check it the same way each month. Use the Google Maps app on a phone.

1. Stand in, or search near, the area you serve.
2. Ask 5 fixed questions, such as "best property manager for a rental house near me".
3. Save a screenshot of each answer and the sources it links.
4. Note who is named and in what order.
5. Repeat on the same day next month.

Google's own help page tells users to check important facts in more than one place and to ask several versions of a question. Do the same when you test. One answer is a sample, not a rank.

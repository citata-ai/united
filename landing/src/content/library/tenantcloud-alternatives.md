## Why might you leave TenantCloud?

Leave when a specific need remains unmet after checking the right plan and setup. Do not start with the assumption that growth always requires another vendor.

TenantCloud's pricing page separates plans by features and lease allowances. A unit count alone can mislead you if the active lease allowance is lower. Read the full capacity table, then check how your account uses it.

Write down your own reason for looking. It might involve owner reports, bank reconciliation, team access or a manual task. These are possible decision factors, not claims about why all TenantCloud users leave.

Ask TenantCloud whether an upgrade or configuration change solves the problem. Price that option beside a move. Keep support, training and data cleanup in the comparison. If the current system can meet the task, staying may require less work than moving to a cheaper headline plan.

## Which alternatives are free?

Stessa Essentials and Landlord Studio GO provide free starting plans, according to their own pricing pages. Free access still comes with a scope that you need to check.

Stessa's pricing page centers on rental investment records and reports. Landlord Studio's page presents a free GO tier beside paid plans. Neither label proves that the free tier can replace your current owner accounting process.

Test the actual task you need. Import a sample expense, attach a receipt and export a report. Check whether the plan supports the records, users and documents you need. Ask what happens when you reach a limit.

Separate software price from payment and service charges. A free account can still involve tenant-paid transactions or paid features. Read the payment terms before asking residents to use a new route. Keep a copy of the plan details with your decision notes so the team knows what the account includes.

## Which alternatives scale beyond a small portfolio?

Test a larger portfolio against accounting and team needs, not only the number of units a vendor says it accepts. More records do not guarantee more control.

For a hypothetical portfolio above 100 units, include Buildium and Yardi Breeze in the demo list. Ask TenantCloud for a Business quote with enough active leases. Use the same owner structure, bank accounts and reports in each request.

Bring a shared owner case and a home that has expenses while vacant. Test an owner contribution, a tenant credit and an unpaid bill. Ask for the report that explains each balance. A basic income total will not answer those questions.

Check staff access before adding a team. Ask who can change payment details, issue refunds and export resident data. Test how the account works when someone leaves. A growing company needs clear access rules as well as room for more leases.

## How do prices compare?

Compare plans that support the same tasks and capacity. A free bookkeeping plan and a full property management plan serve different needs, even when both record rent.

Start a quote sheet with subscription, setup, payments, screening, signatures and support. Add the cost of any separate tool needed for maintenance or owner reports. Use your recent activity to estimate transaction volume.

Annual billing can reduce the displayed monthly equivalent while requiring a longer commitment. Record the amount due, the contract term and the renewal rules. Keep promotional prices separate from the rate you expect after renewal.

Do not guess a larger portfolio price from the entry figure. Ask for a written quote and confirm the billable unit or lease definition. Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to see how the confirmed monthly cost fits your business. An investor managing personal rentals should instead compare the cost with their own bookkeeping workload.

## How do you move your data?

Move records through a sample import before changing rent collection. The import must preserve useful links between people, properties, leases and balances.

Use this export checklist:

- Save owner, property, unit, tenant and vendor records.
- Export rent balances, credits, deposits and unpaid bills.
- Preserve receipts, agreements and signed leases.
- Save reports that explain the closing balances.
- List recurring charges and automatic reminders.
- Confirm whether payment instructions need new setup.
- Record what the new tool cannot import.

Check the sample against the old system. Trace an expense to its receipt and a statement balance to its ledger. Confirm dates, property names and ownership. Correct the mapping before importing the rest.

Keep the old account available until you approve the archive and opening balances. Test resident and owner access with sample accounts. Do not send portal invitations until permissions and payment settings pass review.

## How do you make the final choice?

Choose the smallest plan that completes your required work and leaves clear records. Do not buy extra tools before the core accounting test passes.

Give the same task to every vendor. Record the steps, missing fields and outside work. Ask support to explain an error rather than only watch a sales demo.

If you manage rentals for other owners, require a clear owner statement and separate client balances. If you manage your own rentals, focus on the reports and records your bookkeeper needs.

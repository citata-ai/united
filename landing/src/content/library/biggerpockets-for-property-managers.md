An investor on the BiggerPockets forums wrote that finding a good property manager "seems like one of the more difficult things a real estate investor has to do." That thread is a sales lead in plain sight. BiggerPockets is where many small investors go to learn, compare and ask. This guide shows how a property manager can show up there in the right way.

## How does the BiggerPockets property manager finder work?

The BiggerPockets finder matches an investor with property managers in 3 steps. Its page, read on 2026-10-20, says:

1. **Enter your market.** The investor names the US area where they own or buy rentals.
2. **Share key details.** A few questions about property type and strategy.
3. **See your matches.** The investor requests contact "in one click" from managers they like.

BiggerPockets says the tool is "fast, commitment-free, and trusted by 135k+ investors." It says it only recommends managers who are experienced in the investor's property type and rental strategy and "have a proven track record."

For managers, the same page says: "Stand out to our network of 3M+ investors by getting featured on BiggerPockets." It gives no price. Ask BiggerPockets for the cost, the lead volume in your market, and whether a lead goes to you alone or to several managers.

There is also a business directory. It lists property managers with the states they serve, star ratings and review counts. One Arizona manager on the page we read showed 4.7 stars from 222 reviews.

| BiggerPockets feature | What it does | Cost to you |
|---|---|---|
| Find a Property Manager | Matches investors to managers by market and strategy | Featured placement: price not public |
| Business directory | Profile with states, reviews and description | Ask BiggerPockets |
| Forums | Investors ask questions; you answer | Free account |
| Pro membership | Investor tools and perks | $39/month or $390/year |

## Should I answer forum questions?

Yes. Forum answers are the cheapest way to build trust on BiggerPockets, if you help and do not sell. Fourandhalf, a property management marketing agency, notes the marketplace section is "the only place you can solicit and sell services." So keep sales out of forum threads.

Threads worth watching:

- "Looking for a property manager in [your city]"
- "Is 10% a fair management fee?"
- "My property manager charges a markup on repairs"
- "Should I self-manage or hire a manager?"
- Out-of-state investors asking about your market

A strong forum answer:

1. **Answers the exact question first,** with numbers.
2. **Shares what you see in your market,** labeled as your view.
3. **Says who you are:** "I manage 180 single-family homes in Columbus."
4. **Stops.** No "DM me." Investors click your profile if the answer helps.

The top forum answers we read listed what to check in a manager: talk to current owners, read reviews on Google, Facebook or Yelp, and ask how long it takes to rent a vacant home. Have proof ready for each of those questions.

## What profile details matter?

Investors compare managers on fees, fit and proof. Your BiggerPockets profile should answer those in the first screen.

Profile checklist:

- [ ] Company name exactly as on your Google Business Profile and website
- [ ] Markets and ZIP codes you serve
- [ ] Property types: single-family, small multifamily, short-term
- [ ] Door count and years in business
- [ ] Full fee list: management %, leasing, renewal, maintenance markup, cancellation
- [ ] Average days on market for the last 12 months, from your own data
- [ ] Broker license number, if your state requires one
- [ ] Link to your owner page, not your home page
- [ ] A real photo of the team

Investors on BiggerPockets think in returns. Show your fees as a yearly cost on a sample home. Our [fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) can do that math.

### Worked example: a fee line for your profile

A $1,600 rental. You charge 8% a month and a 50% leasing fee.

- Management: $1,600 × 8% × 12 = $1,536 a year.
- Leasing: $1,600 × 50% = $800, once per new tenant.
- Year 1 with one placement: $2,336.

Put a line like that on your profile. Investors trust a manager who shows the full cost up front.

## What are the costs?

The direct costs are small; the main cost is your time.

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Free account and forum answers | $0 |
| Pro membership (optional, investor tools) | $39/month or $390/year |
| Featured property manager listing | Not published; ask BiggerPockets |
| Time: 5 forum answers a week | About 1.5 hours a week |

Watch the competition too. On 2026-08-17 BiggerPockets named Mynd its "preferred full-service property management partner." Pro members save $500 for every home they onboard with Mynd, up to $10,000. A local manager wins against that with local proof: days on market, owner references and fast answers.

Track the channel for 6 months. Ask every new owner where they heard of you. If BiggerPockets brings even 2 investor owners with 3 doors each, compare those doors with the hours spent.

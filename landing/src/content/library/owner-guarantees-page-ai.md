## Why do guarantee terms matter in AI answers?

Guarantee terms matter because owners ask AI to compare managers, and AI repeats what your page says. An owner may ask: "Which Tampa property managers let me cancel without a fee?" The engine reads the pages it finds and writes a short answer. If your terms are vague, the answer is vague, or wrong.

We studied the clients of the 3 direct competitors in our market (31 confirmed clients, 2026-10-19). All 3 agencies build a full owner fee table and an owner protections section for their clients. Our own playbook now does the same. The work is common. What is rare is a check that AI repeats the terms right.

## Which guarantees do owners compare?

Owners compare four kinds of guarantee, and the terms differ a lot between companies. We read two public guarantee pages on 2026-10-19 to show the range.

| Guarantee | Pensacola Realty Masters | RexMont Property Management |
|---|---|---|
| Leasing time | Not leased in 30 days: 1 month of management free | Not leased in 30 days from "Rent-Ready": placement fee cut by 50% |
| Tenant stays | Tenant leaves before 9 months: new tenant at no charge | Tenant leaves in first 12 months: placement fee waived on the next lease |
| Pet damage | Up to $1,000 above the deposit, approved pets | Up to $1,000 per tenancy above deposits, claim within 30 days of move-out |
| Cancel | "Cancel your agreement at any time with no penalties" | 9-month initial term, then 30 days' written notice, no penalty |
| Limits shown | Few | Many: owner-caused delays pause the clock, a total cap on all guarantees |

The two pages show a real trade-off. The short page is easy to read but leaves out most limits. The long page lists the limits, so a reader and an AI engine can see the full deal.

An AI answer will compress either page to one line. With the short page, the line may promise more than the contract gives. With the long page, the line is more likely to be true.

## How do you word each guarantee?

Word each guarantee as five facts in one block: trigger, window, cap, remedy and limits. Keep the facts together, so a chunk of the page holds the full promise.

Use this template for each guarantee:

1. **Name:** "30-Day Leasing Guarantee."
2. **Trigger:** "If we do not sign a lease within 30 days..."
3. **Start of the clock:** "...from the day the home is ready, photographed and listed at our recommended rent."
4. **Remedy:** "...we cut our placement fee by 50%."
5. **Cap:** "The cut cannot exceed $X."
6. **Main limits:** "The clock stops if you raise the rent or limit showings."
7. **Source line:** "Full terms: section 4 of the management agreement, updated 2026-10-01."

Rules for the wording:

- **Use numbers, not adjectives.** "30 days", not "fast". "$1,000", not "generous".
- **Put the limit in the same sentence or row as the promise.** A limit in a footnote gets lost.
- **Name the remedy in dollars or fees.** "One month of management free" is clear. "We make it right" is not.
- **Use one name per guarantee.** Do not call it a "leasing guarantee" on one page and a "rental promise" on another.

## How do you word cancel terms?

Cancel terms need five facts: the term, the notice, the fee, what stays payable and when guarantees end.

| Fact | Weak wording | Clear wording |
|---|---|---|
| Term | "Flexible" | "Initial term 9 months, then month to month" |
| Notice | "Easy to leave" | "30 days' written notice, by email" |
| Fee to cancel | "No hassle" | "No cancel fee" or "Cancel fee: $X" |
| What stays payable | Not stated | "Fees already earned stay payable" |
| Lease in place | Not stated | "The tenant's lease stays in force. We transfer the deposit and records within X days." |

Fill the X values from your own contract and your state law. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What do the FTC guides say about guarantees?

The FTC guides on guarantee advertising give a useful bar, even though they are written for products. 16 CFR 239.3 covers ads that mention a satisfaction guarantee. Such an ad should disclose its material limits with "clarity and prominence". 16 CFR 239.5 says a seller should advertise a guarantee only if it promptly and fully honors it.

For a property manager, the lesson is simple. Show the limits where you show the promise. Do not publish a guarantee your team cannot pay out.

## Should terms be on one page?

Yes, put all guarantees and cancel terms on one page, then link to it. One page gives AI one clear source, and gives you one place to update.

Checklist for the page:

- **One URL,** such as /owner-guarantees. Plain HTML text, not a PDF or an image.
- **One H2 per guarantee,** with the five facts in the first two sentences.
- **A summary table** at the top: guarantee, window, cap, remedy.
- **The same numbers** on the fee page, the city pages and the agreement.
- **An "updated" date** that changes only when a term changes.
- **Links** from the fee page, each city page and the contact page.
- **Structured data,** if you add it, that matches the visible text. Google says this in its guide to AI features.

## How do you check that AI repeats the terms right?

Ask the engines directly, then compare each answer to your page. Do this before and after each change.

1. **Write 5 prompts.** "What guarantees does [Company] offer owners?", "Can I cancel [Company] without a fee?", and 3 comparison prompts for your city.
2. **Run them** in ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude. Save the answer, the date and the sources.
3. **Score each fact.** Right, wrong, missing or invented.
4. **Fix the source.** A wrong fact often comes from an old page, a directory profile or a review. Fix your page first, then the listing.
5. **Run again in 4 weeks.** Count the right facts again.

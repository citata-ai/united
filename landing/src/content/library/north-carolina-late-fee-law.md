North Carolina puts its late fee rule in one statute. G.S. 42-46 gives the cap, the wait and the limits in one section. We read the official text on ncleg.gov on 2026-10-19. The section's history lists amendments as late as 2025, so check the current text before you sign a new lease.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Late fee, monthly rent | $15 or 5% of monthly rent, whichever is greater | G.S. 42-46(a)(1) |
| Late fee, weekly rent | $4 or 5% of weekly rent, whichever is greater | G.S. 42-46(a)(2) |
| Earliest fee date | Rent 5 calendar days or more late | G.S. 42-46(a) |
| How often | 1 time for each late payment | G.S. 42-46(b) |
| Water or sewer charges | No late fee for those charges | G.S. 42-46(d) |
| Subsidized rent | Fee on the tenant's share only | G.S. 42-46(h)(5) |
| Complaint-filing fee | $15 or 5% of monthly rent, whichever is greater | G.S. 42-46(e) |
| Court-appearance fee | 10% of monthly rent | G.S. 42-46(f) |
| Second trial fee | Up to 12% of monthly rent | G.S. 42-46(g) |
| Attorney fees in the lease | Up to 15% of the amount owed | G.S. 42-46(i)(3) |
| Contrary lease terms | Void | G.S. 42-46(h)(4) |

## What is the maximum late fee a landlord can charge in North Carolina?

The maximum late fee for monthly rent in North Carolina is $15 or 5% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater. G.S. 42-46(a)(1) sets that cap. For weekly rent, G.S. 42-46(a)(2) sets $4 or 5% of the weekly rent.

The $15 floor matters only for rents of $300 or less. Above $300, 5% is larger. Here is the cap at common rent levels:

| Monthly rent | 5% of rent | Maximum late fee |
|---|---|---|
| $250 | $12.50 | $15.00 |
| $300 | $15.00 | $15.00 |
| $900 | $45.00 | $45.00 |
| $1,200 | $60.00 | $60.00 |
| $1,650 | $82.50 | $82.50 |
| $2,400 | $120.00 | $120.00 |

Worked example: rent is $1,350 a month, due on the 1st. 5% of $1,350 is $67.50. That is more than $15, so the cap is $67.50. A lease fee of $75 would go over the cap.

Watch for: if a housing authority pays part of the rent, use the tenant's share. G.S. 42-46(h)(5) says the subsidy is not included. A tenant who pays $400 of a $1,400 rent has a cap of $20, not $70. ## Does North Carolina law require a grace period before a late fee?

Yes, North Carolina law has a built-in grace period of 5 calendar days. G.S. 42-46(a) lets you charge the fee "only if any rental payment is five calendar days or more late". The first day counts from the day after the rent was due.

For rent due on the 1st:

1. **The 2nd** is day 1 late.
2. **The 6th** is day 5 late. The fee may apply from this day.
3. **Weekends and holidays** count. The statute says calendar days.

So a lease that charges a fee on the 2nd, 3rd, 4th or 5th breaks the rule. Set the fee date to the 6th or later.

## Must the late fee be written in the lease in North Carolina?

Yes, the late fee should be in the lease in North Carolina. G.S. 42-46(a) says "the parties may agree to a late fee" in a rental agreement with a fixed rent due date. With no agreement, the statute gives you no fee to charge.

The eviction-related fees go further. Subsections (e), (f) and (g) each say "pursuant to a written lease". An oral lease does not support those fees.

A lease late fee clause should state:

- [ ] The rent due date.
- [ ] The day the fee starts (day 5 late or after).
- [ ] The fee amount, at or under the cap.
- [ ] That the fee applies one time per late payment.
- [ ] That subsidized rent uses the tenant's share.

## Can a North Carolina landlord charge a daily late fee?

No, a North Carolina landlord cannot charge a daily late fee. G.S. 42-46(b) says a late fee "may be imposed only one time for each late rental payment". A fee of $5 a day is a new fee each day, so it breaks that rule.

G.S. 42-46(h)(4) says any lease term contrary to the section is void and unenforceable. A daily fee clause does not hold up, even if the tenant signed it.

Two more limits apply:

1. **No fee for water or sewer charges.** G.S. 42-46(d) bars a late fee for unpaid water or sewer service billed under G.S. 62-110(g).
2. **No fee stacking into later rent.** G.S. 42-46(b) bars taking a late fee out of a later rent payment to put that rent in default.

## Can a North Carolina landlord evict a tenant for unpaid late fees?

A North Carolina landlord cannot use a late fee to push the next rent payment into default. G.S. 42-46(b) bars deducting an unpaid late fee from a later rent payment for that purpose. So if the tenant pays full rent, apply it to rent.

G.S. 42-3 ties the 10-day forfeiture to "all past-due rent". It does not name late fees. Base an eviction notice on unpaid rent, or on a lease breach under G.S. 42-26(a)(2).

The statute does let a written lease add one administrative fee when you file for eviction. G.S. 42-46(h)(1) says you may keep only one of these for each complaint:

| Fee | When it applies | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| Complaint-filing fee | Tenant cures, you dismiss before judgment | $15 or 5% of monthly rent |
| Court-appearance fee | You win in small claims court | 10% of monthly rent |
| Second trial fee | You win again after the tenant appeals | 12% of monthly rent |

G.S. 42-46(h)(2) adds a limit. You may not deduct an earned administrative fee from later rent. You also may not call an unpaid fee a default for a later eviction.

You may also recover court filing fees, service costs and lease-based attorney fees up to 15% of the amount owed (G.S. 42-46(i)). G.S. 42-51(a)(8) lets you take any fee allowed by G.S. 42-46 from the security deposit at move-out.

If a lease requires renters insurance and the tenant gives no proof within 3 business days of your request, G.S. 42-46(l) lets you buy it. You may charge the actual cost plus up to $50 a year.

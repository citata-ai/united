Missouri says little about rent increases and a lot about who may not regulate them. We read RSMo 441.043, 441.050, 441.060 and 441.120 from the Missouri Revisor of Statutes on 2026-10-19. The revisor site did not answer our requests, so we read its pages through Internet Archive copies.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| State rent cap | None in the sections we read | — |
| Local rent control | Banned for private rentals | RSMo 441.043.1 |
| Rent increase notice | No statute found | — |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | One month's written notice, ending on a rent-paying date | RSMo 441.060.4 |
| Oral lease | Treated as month to month | RSMo 441.060.3 |
| End a year-to-year tenancy | Written notice at least 60 days before the year ends | RSMo 441.050 |
| Mobile home lot (tenant owns the home) | At least 60 days from the next rent due date | RSMo 441.060.4(2) |
| Local source-of-income, screening, deposit cap or first-refusal rules | Banned since 2025-08-28 | RSMo 441.043.2 |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Does Missouri have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

Missouri has no rent control. RSMo 441.043.1 covers every county and city, charter or not. None may "enact, maintain, or enforce" an ordinance that regulates the amount of rent for private rentals. The ban covers single-family, multi-unit residential and commercial property.

We found no state cap either. None of the landlord-tenant sections we read limits how much a landlord may raise the rent.

Subsection 3 keeps 3 narrow powers for local government:

1. **Its own property.** A city, county or its housing authority may regulate property it owns.
2. **Voluntary deals.** A city may sign voluntary agreements with owners that limit rent on subsidized rentals.
3. **CDBG-funded units.** A city may restrict rent on properties assisted with community development block grant funds.

If your unit has a subsidy contract, the contract can limit rent even though state law does not.

## How many days' notice does a Missouri landlord need to raise the rent?

We found no Missouri statute that sets a notice period for a rent increase. RSMo 441.060 sets notice to end a tenancy, not to change the rent.

For a month-to-month tenant, the end-of-tenancy notice is the practical floor. The tenant can end the tenancy with the same one month's written notice. So a fair practice is to give the rent notice at least one full rental period ahead, in writing. This is our suggestion, not a statute.

Worked example for a lease with rent due on the 1st:

| Step | Date |
|---|---|
| Tenant receives your written notice | March 10 |
| One month after receipt | April 10 |
| First rent-paying date after that | May 1 |
| Earliest date the new rent or the move-out takes effect | May 1 |

If the tenant receives the notice on March 1, April 1 is one month after receipt. Then April 1 can work. A notice received after the 1st pushes the date out by a full month.

## Can a Missouri landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

A fixed-term lease sets the rent for its term, and Missouri law holds parties to written leases. RSMo 441.030 says a tenant shall not violate the conditions of a written lease. The written rent is one of those conditions. We found no statute that lets either side change it mid-term.

The lease itself may allow a change. Some leases include an escalation clause, a set increase on a set date. If yours has none, wait for the term to end.

Put any renewal in writing. RSMo 441.120.1 says that when a tenant holds over after a written lease, oral evidence cannot prove a renewal or a new deal. The tenant's right to stay, and your right to the new rent, must be established by a written contract.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Missouri?

One month's written notice ends a Missouri month-to-month tenancy. RSMo 441.060.4(1) says the notice must name a termination date on a "periodic rent-paying date" at least one month after the other party receives it. Either the landlord or the tenant may give it.

Other tenancy types:

- **Tenancy at will, by sufferance, or under one year:** one month's written notice to vacate (RSMo 441.060.1).
- **Oral lease:** treated as month to month, ended with one month's written notice (RSMo 441.060.3). Farm leases are an exception.
- **Year to year:** written notice at least 60 days before the end of the year (RSMo 441.050).
- **Mobile home lot, tenant owns the home:** the landlord's notice gives at least 60 days from the next rent due date (RSMo 441.060.4(2)).

If the tenant stays after the notice period, the tenant holds over. That is "unlawful detainer" under RSMo 534.030, and you file in court. You may not lock the tenant out.

## Do cities in Missouri add their own rent or notice rules?

Missouri cities cannot add rent caps, and since 2025 they cannot add several other rental rules. House Bill 595 and 343 amended RSMo 441.043, in effect August 28, 2025. Subsection 2 now bars any county or city ordinance that:

1. stops a landlord from refusing a renter because the renter's income includes a federal or other housing assistance program;
2. limits a landlord's use of income tests, credit, eviction or damage history, or criminal history under the landlord's own standard criteria;
3. limits the security deposit a landlord may require;
4. gives tenants an automatic right of first refusal.

One local power remains. Subsection 4 lets a city bar discrimination against a tenant only because the tenant receives veterans' benefits.

Watch for: state preemption does not remove federal fair housing law. Screen every applicant with the same written criteria. If you take housing choice vouchers, our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) shows the local fair market rents.

Local building, licensing and inspection codes may still apply. Ask your city before you rent a unit.

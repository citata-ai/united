## Self-manage vs hire at a glance

Self-managing saves the fee and costs you time and risk. Hiring a manager costs the fee and saves you time and some risk.

| Factor | Self-manage | Hire a manager |
|--------|-------------|----------------|
| Monthly cost | $0 in fees | Average 8.49% of rent (iPropertyManagement, 2026) |
| New tenant cost | Your ads and time | Leasing fee, average 70.6% of one month's rent |
| Your hours | All of them | Approvals and reviews |
| Night and weekend calls | You | The manager |
| Legal know-how | You learn it | The manager's process |
| Vendor network | You build it | The manager's list |
| Control | Full | Shared, by the agreement |
| Best for | Owners close by, with time | Owners far away, busy, or with several doors |

## How many hours per year does one rental take?

One rental takes few hours in a quiet year and many in a turnover year. No official survey measures owner hours well, so estimate yours with a worksheet.

The hours below are made-up examples for one house. Change them to fit you.

| Task | Quiet year | Turnover year |
|------|------------|---------------|
| Rent and bookkeeping | 12 | 12 |
| Tenant calls and repairs | 15 | 15 |
| Renewal | 2 | 0 |
| Move-out and deposit | 0 | 5 |
| Make-ready | 0 | 15 |
| Listing, inquiries, showings | 0 | 18 |
| Screening and lease | 0 | 6 |
| Taxes and records | 6 | 6 |
| Total | 35 hours | 77 hours |

Turnover years drive the total. The US rental vacancy rate was 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026 (Census Bureau). Each turnover is also a vacancy risk.

## What does a property manager cost per month?

A property manager costs about 8% to 10% of collected rent per month, plus one-time fees. iPropertyManagement's survey of 80 metros, updated 2026-09-23, gives these averages:

| Fee | 2026 average | On a $2,000 house |
|-----|--------------|-------------------|
| Monthly fee | 8.49% of rent | $170/month |
| Flat-fee alternative | $101.04/month | $101/month |
| Leasing with management | 70.6% of 1 month | $1,412 per new tenant |
| Setup | $185.24 | $185 once |
| Renewal | $211.92 | $212 per renewal |

One house, one year: about $2,250 in a renewal year and $3,635 in a year with a new tenant. Use our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) for your rent.

## What tasks can I keep and what can I hand off?

You can split the work. Many owners hand off the tasks that need local presence or legal care, and keep the ones that need judgment.

| Task | Keep it if | Hand it off if |
|------|------------|----------------|
| Rent setting | You track local comps | You do not know the market |
| Marketing and showings | You live close and have evenings free | You live far away or work long hours |
| Tenant screening | You have written criteria and know the rules | You are not sure of fair housing and credit report rules |
| Lease | You have a lease written for your state | You do not |
| Rent collection | You use an online payment tool | You want someone to enforce late rent |
| Repairs | You have reliable vendors | You cannot take night calls |
| Move-out and deposit | You know your state's deadline | You do not |
| Bookkeeping | You keep a separate account | You want monthly statements made for you |

Screening has legal steps. The FTC says that if you deny an applicant based partly or fully on a consumer report, you must give an adverse action notice with the reporting agency's contact details and the applicant's dispute rights. A self-managing owner must do this too.

Partial options:

- **Leasing only.** A manager finds and places the tenant. iPropertyManagement found an average of 81.8% of one month's rent for placement-only service.
- **Full service with a high approval limit.** You approve repairs above a set amount.

## When does a manager pay for itself?

A manager pays for itself when its fee is less than the value of your time plus the money it saves or earns you. Do the math in two parts.

Part 1, your time:

Break-even hourly rate = yearly management cost / hours you would spend

- Quiet year: $2,250 / 35 = $64 per hour.
- Turnover year: $3,635 / 77 = $47 per hour.

Part 2, money the manager may add or save:

| Lever | Value on a $2,000 house |
|-------|-------------------------|
| Lease 1 week faster | About $460 |
| Rent $50/month closer to market | $600 a year |
| One tenant stays a year longer | Saves a leasing fee, make-ready and vacancy |

Do not assume these gains. Ask each manager for its average days to lease and renewal rate, in writing.

Management fees are a deductible rental expense. IRS Publication 527 lists management fees among the common rental expenses, so the after-tax cost is lower.

## How does distance change the answer?

Distance is often the deciding factor. A leak at 2 a.m. needs someone at the door, not a phone call.

| Distance to the rental | Common choice |
|------------------------|---------------|
| Under 30 minutes | Self-manage is practical |
| 30 to 60 minutes | Self-manage with a local handyman on call |
| 1 to 3 hours | Leasing-only plus a local repair contact, or full service |
| Another state | Full-service manager in most cases |

Out-of-state owners also face rules they may not know: the deposit return deadline, notice periods and local license or inspection rules. A local manager works with those every day.

## A 5-question decision test

1. **Can you reach the home in under an hour?**
2. **Do you have 5 or more free weekday hours in a turnover month?**
3. **Do you have written screening criteria and a state-specific lease?**
4. **Do you have a plumber and a handyman you trust?**
5. **Would one bad tenant hurt your finances badly?**

Three or more "no" answers to 1 through 4, or a "yes" to 5, point to a manager. See [property management](/property-management) for how owners compare managers, and [how owners choose a property manager with AI](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai).

This is general information, not legal or tax advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney or a tax professional.

New Mexico cut its late fee cap in half in 2025. Laws 2025, chapter 122, changed NMSA 47-8-15(D) from 10% to 5% of the rent. The change took effect on June 20, 2025. We read the current text on NMOneSource, the site of the New Mexico Compilation Commission, on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |

| Late fee cap | 5% of the rent for each rental period in default | NMSA 47-8-15(D) |
| Base for the fee | Rent only; no deposits, other fees or utilities | NMSA 47-8-15(D) |
| Lease term | Rental agreement must provide for a late fee | NMSA 47-8-15(D) |

## What is the maximum late fee a landlord can charge in New Mexico?

The maximum New Mexico late fee is 5% of the rent for each rental period the tenant is in default. NMSA 47-8-15(D) sets this cap. Before June 20, 2025, the cap was 10%.

The 2025 law also fixed the base. The statute now says late fees are calculated only on rent. The rent figure may not include deposits, additional fees or utilities.

Here is the math on 3 common rents:

| Monthly rent | Cap at 5% | Old cap at 10% (before 2025-06-20) |
|---|---|---|
| $900 | $45 | $90 |
| $1,200 | $60 | $120 |
| $1,800 | $90 | $180 |

Watch for: rent plus a $75 pet fee plus $50 for water is not the base. On $1,200 rent with those charges, the cap stays $60, not 5% of $1,325.

What to do:

1. **Check every lease form.** Any late fee above 5% of rent conflicts with the statute.
2. **Check your software.** If it takes a percent of the full balance, set the base to rent only.
3. **Read old leases too.** NMSA 47-8-16 says a rental agreement may not waive rights under the law.

## Does New Mexico law require a grace period before a late fee?

New Mexico law does not require a grace period before a late fee. NMSA 47-8-15 has no grace period rule. Under NMSA 47-8-15(B), rent is payable without demand or notice at the time and place the parties agreed.

A lease can still give a grace period. A short grace period can cut disputes over a payment that is a day late. If your lease has a grace period, follow it each month.

## Must the late fee be written in the lease in New Mexico?

Yes, a New Mexico late fee must come from the rental agreement. NMSA 47-8-15(D) opens with a condition: "If the rental agreement provides for the charging of a late fee". With no lease term, you have no right to the fee.

The 2025 law added 2 more fee rules that touch late fees:

- **Listings.** NMSA 47-8-19.1 says your published listing must disclose all fees and charges, itemized and easy to find.
- **Fee increases.** NMSA 47-8-19.4 lets you raise a lease fee with written notice. Give it at least 60 days before the periodic rental date. For a fixed term, it is 60 days before the end of the term.

Notice is also part of each fee. To assess a late fee, NMSA 47-8-15(D) requires notice no later than the last day of the next rental period. For example, take rent due March 1 that the tenant pays late. March is the period in default, and April is the next period. Your notice deadline is April 30.

Watch for: in Hedicke v. Gunville (2003-NMCA-032), the landlords gave no notice of late fees in the months they wanted to collect. The Court of Appeals agreed they could not collect those fees.

## Can a New Mexico landlord charge a daily late fee?

A New Mexico landlord can charge a daily late fee only if the total stays within the cap. NMSA 47-8-15(D) limits the late fee to 5% of the rent for each rental period in default. The statute does not ban a daily structure. It caps the sum.

A worked example:

- Monthly rent: $1,400.
- Cap for the period: 5% x $1,400 = $70.
- Lease fee: $10 per day late.
- The fee reaches $70 on day 7. It must stop there for that period.

A flat fee is simpler. One fee of 5% of rent, or less, avoids day counts and errors.

## Can a New Mexico landlord evict a tenant for unpaid late fees?

The New Mexico 3-day notice in NMSA 47-8-33(D) covers rent. The statute does not say a late fee is rent. NMSA 47-8-3 defines rent as payments for use of a dwelling unit or premises. NMSA 47-8-15(D) treats the late fee as a charge calculated on rent, not as rent.

That gap matters. A safe practice:

1. **Keep the 3-day notice to rent.** List the unpaid rent, and show the late fee as a separate line.
2. **Treat a late fee as a lease term.** Repeated unpaid fees may be a material noncompliance under NMSA 47-8-33(A). That path needs a 7-day notice with the dates and facts.
3. **Follow the rent payment rule.** NMSA 47-8-15(G) says rent paid may not go to deposits or damages unless both of you agree in writing.

Before you file a case based only on fees, ask a local attorney. The court can grant relief only on the grounds in your written notice (NMSA 47-8-33(G)).

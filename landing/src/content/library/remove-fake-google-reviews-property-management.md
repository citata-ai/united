A property manager gets a 1-star review from a name nobody knows. No lease, no work order, no call. The first wish is to delete it. Google will not do that because the review hurts. Google removes it only if it breaks a written policy. This guide shows which reviews qualify, how to flag them, and what to do when Google says no.

The stakes are real. In the BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, 77% of consumers said negative reviews make them less likely to use a business. Owners who look for a manager read the same stars.

## Which Google reviews break Google policy?

A Google review breaks policy when it fits one of the prohibited content types on the Google Maps policy page. Disagreement is not one of them. Google's help page says: "Do not report a review just because you disagree with it or dislike it."

These are the policy types that fit property management reviews most often:

| Policy type | What Google says it covers | Property management example |
|---|---|---|
| Fake engagement | Content not based on a real experience, paid or incentivized reviews, several accounts for one person | A reviewer who never rented, owned or applied with you |
| Rating manipulation | Incentivized or biased reviews, unusual posting patterns, reviews written despite a conflict of interest | 5 one-star reviews in 2 days from new accounts; a review from a competitor's staff |
| Off-topic | Content not based on an experience at that place, general political or social rants | A rant about rent prices in the city, not about your company |
| Harassment | Specific threats, doxxing | A review that names a staff member and threatens harm |
| Personal information | Personal details posted without consent | A tenant's phone number or unit number in the text |
| Offensive content | Attacks on people, unproven claims of crime or unethical acts | "The owner steals deposits" with no event described |
| Advertising and solicitation | Phone numbers, emails, links to other sites | A review that sends readers to another manager |
| Impersonation | Posing as another person or group | A reviewer who claims to be your employee and is not |

Source: Google Maps Help, Prohibited and restricted content (read 2026-10-20). Google also notes that business contact details and the names of public-facing staff are allowed. So a tenant who names your leasing agent does not break the personal information rule.

## Which reviews will Google keep up?

Google keeps a review up when it describes a real experience, even if it is harsh, one-sided or wrong in your view. Google says it "doesn't get involved in conflict between businesses and customers."

Watch for these cases. They feel unfair, but they usually stay:

- **A tenant you evicted.** The tenant had a real experience with you.
- **An applicant you denied.** The applicant dealt with your company, so the review is on topic.
- **A wrong account of events.** Google does not judge who is right.
- **A review of a former owner's building.** If the reviewer lived there under your management, it stays.

For these, the reply is your tool, not the flag. More on that below.

## How do I flag a Google review?

You flag a Google review from your Business Profile or from the Google Reviews Management Tool. The second path lets you track status and appeal.

**Path 1: Business Profile**

1. Open your Business Profile.
2. Select Read reviews.
3. Select the Report icon next to the review.
4. Choose a reason, for example Spam or Conflict of interest.
5. Select Send report.

**Path 2: Reviews Management Tool**

1. Open the Reviews Management Tool and confirm the account that owns the profile.
2. Select your business, then Continue.
3. Select Report a new review for removal.
4. Select Report next to the review and choose a reason.
5. Select Submit.

Source: Google Business Profile Help (read 2026-10-20). Pick the reason that matches the policy type in the table. A clear reason helps more than a long story.

## What evidence should I keep before I flag?

Keep a short record for each review before you flag it. You need it for the appeal, and maybe for an attorney.

- Screenshot of the review with date, name and star count.
- A search of your property management software for the reviewer's name, phone and address. Note "no match" if none.
- The date of any related lease, application or work order.
- For a pattern: a list of all reviews in the burst, with times.
- For a conflict of interest: the public fact that links the reviewer to a competitor or a former employee.

Do not post private tenant data in your reply or your report to show the person is wrong. That can break the same personal information rule.

## What if Google rejects my request?

If Google rejects your request, the status reads "Report reviewed - no policy violation" and you get one appeal. Google says the first check typically takes several days.

The statuses in the Reviews Management Tool:

| Status | What it means | Your next step |
|---|---|---|
| Decision pending | Google has not checked yet | Wait |
| Report reviewed - no policy violation | Google found no violation | Send the one-time appeal |
| Escalated - check your email | Google is checking the appeal | Watch your email for the final decision |

To appeal, open the tool, select "Check the status of a review I reported previously and appeal options", then "Appeal eligible reviews". You can select up to 10 reviews. Add the facts from your evidence record to the form.

If the appeal fails, the review stays. Then reply in public. In the same BrightLocal survey, 89% of consumers expect owners to respond to reviews, and 50% say templated replies make them unlikely to choose a business.

A good reply to a review you cannot match:

> We searched our records and found no lease, application or work order under this name. We want to understand what happened. Please call our office at the number on this profile and ask for the manager. — Dana, Office Manager

Keep it short. State facts. Do not accuse the reviewer of lying.

## Can I take legal action for a fake review?

Legal action is possible in some cases, but the rules differ by state. Defamation (a false statement of fact that harms you) is state law. An opinion such as "worst manager ever" is usually not a statement of fact.

Two federal points matter before you act:

- **Google's legal form.** Google has a separate form to report content "that violates the law or your rights." You give the exact URL and explain why the content is unlawful. Google says it should be your step after you flag in the product.
- **The FTC rule on reviews.** The FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule took effect on October 21, 2024. Its review suppression section bans knowingly false accusations, intimidation and unfounded legal threats used to stop or remove reviews. So a threat letter to a real tenant with a real complaint can put you at risk.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I keep fake reviews from moving my rating?

The best defense is a steady flow of real reviews. One fake 1-star review moves a profile with 8 reviews far more than a profile with 120.

The math, with made-up numbers:

| Profile | Reviews before | Average before | After one 1-star | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small | 8 at 4.8 | 4.80 | (38.4 + 1) / 9 = 4.38 | -0.42 |
| Large | 120 at 4.8 | 4.80 | (576 + 1) / 121 = 4.77 | -0.03 |

BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 31% of consumers use only businesses with 4.5 stars or more. The small profile drops below that line with one review. Ask every owner and tenant for a review after a real event, such as a finished repair or a lease signing. Google bans incentives and selective asks for only happy customers, so ask everyone the same way.

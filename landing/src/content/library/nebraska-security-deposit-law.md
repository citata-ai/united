Nebraska puts almost all of its deposit rules in one section: Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1416. That section is part of the Nebraska Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act. We read the 2025 text on Justia on 2026-10-19. The official site, nebraskalegislature.gov, did not answer from our network that day.

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Nebraska?

The maximum security deposit in Nebraska is 1 month's periodic rent. Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1416(1) says a landlord may not demand or receive security "in an amount or value in excess of one month's periodic rent". The words "however denominated" matter. A fee with another name can still count as security.

The same subsection allows a pet deposit. The pet deposit may not exceed 1/4 of 1 month's periodic rent. The statute says the landlord may ask for it "when appropriate".

Here is a worked example for a unit at $1,200 a month:

| Item | Calculation | Maximum |
|---|---|---|
| Security deposit | 1 × $1,200 | $1,200 |
| Pet deposit | $1,200 ÷ 4 | $300 |
| Total upfront security | $1,200 + $300 | $1,500 |

One exception applies. The cap does not apply to housing agencies under the Nebraska Housing Agency Act (76-1416(1)).

## How many days does a Nebraska landlord have to return a security deposit?

A Nebraska landlord has 14 days after the tenancy ends to return the deposit balance. Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1416(2) sets the rule. The balance and a written itemization "shall be delivered or mailed to the tenant within fourteen days" after the tenancy ends.

The Nebraska Supreme Court read this deadline in Hilliard v. Robertson (1997). The court said the 14 days is the time the landlord has to return the deposit. It is not a time limit for the tenant to ask for it. Justia lists this note under the statute.

The statute also covers a tenant who leaves no address:

1. **Mail it anyway.** If the tenant gives no address or instructions, send the balance and the itemization by first-class mail to the last-known mailing address.
2. **Watch for returns.** If the mail comes back as undeliverable, the balance counts as abandoned property.
3. **Track uncashed balances.** If the balance stays outstanding for 1 year, it also counts as abandoned property.
4. **Report it.** Report and pay abandoned balances to the State Treasurer under the Uniform Disposition of Unclaimed Property Act.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Nebraska?

A Nebraska landlord may deduct unpaid rent and damages from the tenant's noncompliance. Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1416(2) lets the landlord apply the deposit and prepaid rent to two things:

- rent the tenant owes;
- damages from the tenant's failure to comply with the rental agreement or section 76-1421.

Section 76-1421 lists the tenant's duties. At move-out, the tenant must leave the unit "in as clean condition, excepting ordinary wear and tear, as when the tenancy commenced". The tenant must also not deliberately or negligently damage or remove any part of the premises.

So normal wear and tear is not a tenant duty under 76-1421(2). A clear move-in record helps you show what changed.

The statute has one more limit, in 76-1416(4). Sometimes a government order removes the tenant because the unit is unfit. If the landlord's negligence or neglect caused that, the tenant is not liable for the related damages.

## Does a Nebraska landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes, a Nebraska landlord must give a written itemization of every amount kept. Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1416(2) requires "a written itemization" with the balance, within the same 14 days.

A simple move-out checklist:

- [ ] Write down the date the tenancy ended. Day 14 is your deadline.
- [ ] Compare the unit with your move-in record and photos.
- [ ] List each deduction with the reason and the amount.
- [ ] Separate unpaid rent from repair costs.
- [ ] Leave out ordinary wear and tear.
- [ ] Deliver or mail the balance and the list together.
- [ ] Keep a copy and proof of mailing.

## Must a Nebraska landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1416 does not require interest on a deposit. The section also does not require a separate or escrow account. We found no such rule in the text of 76-1416 that we read.

Other rules can still apply to you. Your lease may promise interest. A city or a subsidy program may set its own terms. Check those before you decide where to hold deposits.

One related rule concerns sale of the building. Under 76-1416(5), the holder of the landlord's interest at the end of the tenancy is bound by the section. If you buy a building, you take on the duty to return deposits for current tenants.

## What is the penalty for a Nebraska landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

The penalty for a Nebraska landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly has two levels. Both come from Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1416(3).

| Level | When it applies | What the tenant may recover |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | Landlord fails to comply with 76-1416(2) | The property and money due, court costs, reasonable attorney's fees |
| Added | Failure is willful and not in good faith | Basic recovery, plus the lesser of 1 month's rent or 2 times the deposit |

A worked example: the rent is $1,200 and the deposit is $1,000. Two times the deposit is $2,000. One month's rent is $1,200. The lesser amount is $1,200. A court could award that $1,200 as liquidated damages, on top of the money due, costs and fees.

Attorney's fees are a real cost. Lomack v. Kohl-Watts (2004) is a Nebraska Court of Appeals case. It says a tenant who wins under 76-1416(2) gets reasonable attorney fees as a matter of right. In Black v. Brooks (2013), the Nebraska Supreme Court said these fees include pro bono work. Justia lists both notes under the statute.

The section history on Justia ends with Laws 2021, LB532. We found no 2024 to 2026 change to 76-1416 in the text we read.

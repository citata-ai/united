## Where can cameras go?

Camera placement should follow a written purpose and a local privacy review. Start with the event you need to document. A camera aimed at a package drop serves a different purpose from a camera tracking every visitor.

Use the smallest view that meets the approved need. Check the image during daylight and darkness. Look for views through windows or open doors. Adjust the angle or use privacy masks where needed. Do not assume that a common hallway cannot reveal private activity.

Avoid cameras in bedrooms, bathrooms and private living areas. Do not install hidden cameras. These are operating limits for the policy proposed here, not a claim that every state uses the same camera statute. Ask local counsel to review the actual placement, recording method and tenancy.

For platform rentals, check the platform as well as the law. Airbnb's device policy prohibits cameras monitoring indoor home spaces. That includes shared indoor spaces in private-room listings. Its policy has a narrow Japan exception. Do not apply that exception to a US rental.

## Can cameras record audio?

Audio recording needs its own legal review because sound can capture protected conversations outside the intended camera view. Disable microphones until counsel confirms a lawful setup. Check the device, mobile app and cloud settings. Some settings may change after an update or a device reset.

California Penal Code 632 prohibits intentional recording or eavesdropping on confidential communications without all-party consent, subject to its terms. Washington RCW 9.73.030 generally requires all participants' consent for private conversations, with stated exceptions. These examples do not make audio lawful in every other state.

A sign that says cameras operate does not answer every consent question. A conversation may happen near a doorway without the speakers noticing a sign. A visitor may never see the lease disclosure. Ask counsel how the rule applies to those people.

Choose video-only equipment when sound is unnecessary. Test the exported recording to confirm that no audio track remains. Record that check in the installation file. Repeat the check after a replacement or a settings change. Tell staff never to turn audio on for convenience.

## Must I tell tenants?

Tell tenants what the system does before it starts, and check the legal disclosure rules for the property. Use clear written language. Avoid vague statements about monitoring that hide the actual location or purpose.

Include the following information in your proposed camera notice:

| Notice item | What to explain |
|---|---|
| Purpose | The security problem the camera addresses |
| Location | Each approved camera and the area it views |
| Sound | Whether audio is disabled |
| Access | Which staff roles can see recordings |
| Retention | Your approved deletion and incident-hold process |
| Contact | How tenants can raise a privacy concern |

For Airbnb listings, the platform requires disclosure of exterior cameras under the host's control. Airbnb also bars exterior views into spaces with greater privacy expectations. Check the current policy before changing a listing or device.

Give residents a practical route to report a bad angle or unexpected recording. Treat the report as a system issue to check. Keep a record of the review and any change. Do not dismiss a concern simply because the camera sits on owner-controlled property.

## Can I use footage for lease violations?

Footage can support a review only after you check lawful collection, context and the lease term involved. Preserve the original file before staff annotate or shorten a copy. Keep the date, device identifier and export record together.

Do not describe a person as a tenant based only on clothing or a distant image. A visitor carrying a bag does not prove unauthorized occupancy. A person entering a unit does not prove a paid short-term booking. Look for independent evidence and ask for an explanation.

Keep an incident log that separates observation from inference. For example, record that a door remained open during the clip. Do not turn that observation into a claim that someone disabled security. Use plain descriptions that another reviewer can assess.

Have counsel review contested enforcement before serving a legal notice. California Penal Code 632 also limits use of unlawfully obtained confidential recordings as evidence. The cost of a camera system does not make its recordings admissible. Do not threaten to publish footage to make a tenant comply.

## How should I control access and storage?

Control footage as a sensitive property record, with named access and a clear deletion process. Avoid a shared account that former staff can still use. Use separate staff accounts where the system supports them. Remove access when duties change.

Choose the retention period through a documented business and legal review. There is no universal retention term in this guide. Keep relevant footage when a claim or preservation duty requires it. Do not let routine deletion erase an incident file under active review.

Test whether you can export an original clip without altering it. Check whether the provider can access recordings and whether cloud storage forms part of the service. Ask how the provider handles deletion and account closure. Store the answers with the contract.

Train vendors who work near cameras. A cleaner may cover a lens by mistake. A technician may reset sound settings. Provide a change log so the manager knows when the system needs another privacy check.

Start with a site map, a proposed camera view and a written notice. Have the local review resolve those concrete details before installation. Our [property management resources](/property-management) can help you organize the wider operating process.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

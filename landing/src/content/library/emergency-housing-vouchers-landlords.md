## Are the rules the same as a regular HCV?

Mostly yes, from the owner's side. A special-purpose voucher is still rental help that follows the family. The Terner Center at UC Berkeley says EHVs are "a type of tenant-based assistance where participants contribute 30 percent of their adjusted monthly income to rent and utilities, and the voucher covers the rest". That is the same split as a regular Housing Choice Voucher (HCV).

The differences sit around the edges. Here are the 3 programs side by side, from the sources we read.

| Point | EHV | Mainstream | FYI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who it serves | People who are homeless, at risk of homelessness, or fleeing domestic violence, sexual assault or trafficking | Non-elderly people with disabilities who are homeless, at risk, leaving an institution or at risk of one | Youth 18 to 24 who left foster care, or will within 90 days, and are homeless or at risk |
| Who refers | Continuums of Care (CoCs) | Supportive service providers and Coordinated Entry (Sonoma County) | Public Child Welfare Agency (Sonoma County) |
| Time limit | Program ends 2026-12-31; leased families move to TPVs | Not stated on the page we read | 36 months, plus up to 24 more if eligible (Sonoma County) |
| Extra money | $3,500 service fee per allocated voucher | Not stated on the page we read | Not stated on the page we read |
| Payment standard | Could be up to 120% of fair market rent | Ask your PHA | Ask your PHA |

Sources: Terner Center brief (December 2025) for EHV; Sonoma County Housing Authority pages for Mainstream and FYI.

For you, the steps look like any voucher lease: an application, a Request for Tenancy Approval, an inspection, a lease with the tenancy addendum and a HAP contract. Ask your PHA if any step differs for the program.

Watch for: you still screen each applicant with your normal written criteria. Apply those criteria the same way to every applicant.

## Do these vouchers come with landlord incentives?

Some did, mainly EHV. The Terner Center says the EHV program gave PHAs "$3,500 per allocated voucher" in service fees. PHAs could use them for "security deposits, housing search assistance, landlord engagement, and other move-in needs".

How PHAs spent the fees varied:

- About 90% of PHAs used the service fees to help EHV holders lease homes.
- The Housing Authority of Santa Barbara County, where the median rent is about $2,200, spent about $3,100 of the $3,500 per EHV on landlord incentives.
- PHAs in low-cost markets often used the fees for furniture and move-in costs instead.

The Terner Center also says "HUD has begun reclaiming PHAs' remaining service fees". So do not count on an EHV incentive now. Ask your PHA what is still on offer.

For Mainstream and FYI, the pages we read did not list owner incentives. Some PHAs run general landlord incentive programs that cover all voucher types. Check your PHA's landlord page.

## Who refers the tenant?

A partner agency refers the tenant, not the regular waiting list. This is the biggest difference from a regular HCV.

- **EHV:** the Terner Center says referrals "are made through Continuums of Care (CoCs), rather than through PHA waitlists". A CoC is the local network that plans homeless services.
- **Mainstream:** Sonoma County Housing Authority says "all referrals to this program come from supportive service providers and Coordinated Entry".
- **FYI:** Sonoma County says eligible youth "are referred for rental assistance by the PCWA", the Public Child Welfare Agency. Youth also get supportive services through a partner agency.

What this means for you: a case manager or housing navigator often helps the tenant find and apply for your unit. Ask for their contact, with the tenant's consent. A named contact helps when paperwork stalls.

## What happens when EHV funding ends?

EHV ends on 2026-12-31, but most leased EHV households keep their help. HUD Notice PIH 2026-25 gives a Tenant Protection Voucher (TPV) to every EHV family under lease on 2026-09-30. Sacramento Housing and Redevelopment Agency (SHRA) and Home Forward date the notice 2026-09-29. NAHRO, the housing agency trade group, says the conversion must be done "by the end of December 2026".

The timeline from our sources:

1. **2021.** HUD created EHV under the American Rescue Plan Act (Long Beach Housing Authority). PHAs were first told it was funded through 2030 (Home Forward).
2. **March 2025.** HUD said EHV money was being spent faster than planned and would run out in 2026 (Terner Center, SHRA).
3. **FY 2026 budget.** NLIHC reports the FY 2026 spending bill "included more than $600 million for TPVs", with a provision for PHAs that would otherwise end EHVs.
4. **2026-09-29.** HUD issued Notice PIH 2026-25: TPVs for all EHV families under lease on 2026-09-30 (SHRA).
5. **End of November 2026.** The Legal Aid Society of San Diego tells tenants to call their PHA if they have no word by then.
6. **2026-12-31.** The EHV program ends. Eligible families are on TPVs, "a type of Housing Choice Voucher" (Home Forward, Legal Aid Society of San Diego).

## Which EHV tenants keep their help?

A tenant keeps rental help only if the family was under lease with an EHV on 2026-09-30. The Legal Aid Society of San Diego (updated 2026-10-16) says "families must be under lease in the EHV program to qualify for conversion". Home Forward says EHV households not leased up by that date lost eligibility for ongoing EHV help.

Some PHAs ended EHV before that date. Their former EHV tenants may not be on the list. This table shows what each PHA told owners, by date.

| PHA | What it told owners | Date of the notice |
|---|---|---|
| Georgia Department of Community Affairs (DCA) | EHV ends 2026-06-30. HAP stops and the HAP contract ends 2026-07-01. You need a direct lease from 2026-07-01. No rent increases until the end. | Landlord FAQ, after HUD's 2025-03-06 notice |
| Housing Authority of DeKalb County (HADC), Georgia | Same as DCA: HAP through 2026-06-30, HAP contract ends 2026-07-01, no rent increases | Landlord FAQ, February 2026 |
| Housing Authority of the City of Santa Barbara (HACSB) | Funds expected to run out about 2026-09-30. A 60-day HAP termination notice would follow at the end of July if no new money came. No new EHV HAP contracts, moves or ports. | Landlord letter, May 2026 |
| Housing Authority of the City of Long Beach (HACLB) | Funds expected to run out around October 2026. "The lease between tenant and landlord remains valid even if the subsidy ends." | Program page, as of March 2026 |
| San Francisco Housing Authority (SFHA) | Funds "projected to be exhausted by December 31, 2026". SFHA and the city plan a transition so households do not lose help. | FAQ, 2026-06-17 |
| Sacramento (SHRA) | Families under lease on 2026-09-30 move to a TPV. SHRA expects to finish by 2026-12-31. | Program page, after 2026-09-30 |

Watch for: the Georgia, Santa Barbara and Long Beach notices came before PIH 2026-25. Ask your PHA, in writing, which rule now applies to your tenant.

## What changes for the owner when EHV becomes a TPV?

The TPV is a regular Housing Choice Voucher, so the PHA's normal HCV rules apply. SHRA says families "may stay in their current home" and do not need a new eligibility screening. HUD treats them as "continuously assisted" (SHRA).

3 changes matter to you:

- **Payment standard.** NAHRO says the PHA's HCV payment standard rules apply to EHV-TPV families. EHV payment standards could be up to 120% of fair market rent (Terner Center). The Legal Aid Society of San Diego warns the tenant's share "could change". A lower payment standard can mean a bigger tenant share or a smaller HAP.
- **No reissue at turnover.** NAHRO says "EHV-TPVs may not be reissued". When your tenant moves out, the voucher ends with them. Your next tenant comes from the regular waiting list or the open market.
- **Funding after 2026.** NAHRO says EHV-TPVs move into the HAP renewals account, which depends on the yearly appropriations process. That is the same risk as any HCV.

The sources we read do not say if the PHA signs a new HAP contract with you at conversion. Ask your PHA. For the 2027 voucher budget and shortfall rules, read [Will Section 8 keep paying landlords in 2027?](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/section-8-funding-2027).

What to do if you have an EHV tenant:

- [ ] Ask your PHA, in writing, if your tenant was under lease on 2026-09-30 and gets a TPV.
- [ ] Ask the conversion date and if the HAP contract changes or a new one is needed.
- [ ] Ask the new payment standard and the tenant's new share of rent.
- [ ] Check each HAP deposit from October 2026 to January 2027 against the expected amount.
- [ ] Keep the lease in force. A funding change is not the tenant's lease breach.
- [ ] If the PHA says help will end, ask for the date and the notice it sent the family. Then follow your lease and state law.

## How do I find these programs locally?

Start with your PHA's "special programs" or landlord page. Look for these names: Emergency Housing Voucher, Mainstream, Foster Youth to Independence (FYI), Family Unification Program (FUP) and VASH (for veterans). Sonoma County, for example, lists Mainstream, FUP and FYI under "Special Programs".

Then contact the referral partners. For EHV and Mainstream, that is often the local CoC or Coordinated Entry. For FYI, it is the child welfare agency. Tell them you have a unit that accepts vouchers, its size and its rent. Check the rent against local limits first in our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits), and estimate the tenant and HAP split with the [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator).

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

A property manager with 150 doors can spend whole days on data entry, calendar juggling and "did you get my request" calls. None of that needs a license. Much of it does not need to happen in your time zone. That is the case for a virtual assistant (VA): a remote worker who does admin tasks for a monthly or hourly fee.

The case against is also real. Some tasks need a license. Some tasks need a person on site. And every VA is one more login to owner bank details and tenant files.

## Which property management tasks fit a VA?

Back-office tasks with clear steps fit a VA best. Buildium's guide to property management VAs lists scheduling, marketing materials, research on clients and vendors, tenant request intake, and data entry into property management software.

Here is a task split we use to plan VA work. The license column uses Texas as the example, from TREC's 2024 article on unlicensed assistants. Your state may draw the line in a different place.

| Task | Fit for a VA? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Data entry in your property management software | Yes | TREC allows it when a license holder directs it |
| Work order intake and vendor scheduling | Yes | Admin work, no license activity |
| Listing upkeep (photos, text, syndication) | Yes, with review | Admin work; the broker stays responsible for the ad |
| Setting showing appointments | Yes | TREC allows it if a license holder does the showing |
| Answering ad questions (beds, baths, rent) | Only facts already in the ad | TREC Rule 535.5(f) |
| Bookkeeping entries and owner statement prep | Yes, with review | TREC allows bookkeeping; only a license holder may withdraw or move trust money (Rule 535.146(c)(7)) |
| Calls to find owners who want to rent out a home | No | TREC Rule 535.4(f): only license holders may solicit |
| Showing a unit or unlocking a door for a prospect | No | TREC Rule 535.4(c) |
| Reviewing or negotiating a lease | No | TREC: an unlicensed assistant cannot review a contract or facilitate a transaction |
| Inspections, key handoffs, site visits | No | Needs a person on site |

Buildium makes the same point about on-site work. A VA cannot do showings or inspections. Buildium also suggests that tasks with legal or financial weight may fit in-house staff better.

## What does an offshore VA cost?

A full-time offshore VA costs about $1,000 to $2,000 a month at the vendors we checked. We read two vendor pricing pages on 2026-10-19:

| Vendor and plan | Monthly price | Hours | Effective hourly rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wing, general VA part-time | $699 | 80 | $8.74 |
| Wing, general VA full-time | $999 | 160 | $6.24 |
| MyOutDesk, Tier 1 managed outsourcing | from $1,988 | Full-time (hours not on the page) | Not shown |
| MyOutDesk, Tier 2 specialized outsourcing | from $2,500 | Full-time (hours not on the page) | Not shown |

We worked out the hourly rates from Wing's price and hours. Neither vendor is a recommendation. Ask each for a written quote for your task list.

Compare that with an in-office hire. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics gives a median wage of $48,310 a year, or $23.23 an hour, for secretaries and administrative assistants (May 2025). That figure is before payroll taxes, benefits, desk space and equipment.

A quick check for 200 doors:

| Option | Monthly cost | Per door per month |
|---|---|---|
| Wing full-time VA | $999 | $5.00 |
| MyOutDesk Tier 1 | $1,988 | $9.94 |
| US admin at BLS median ($48,310 ÷ 12), wage only | $4,026 | $20.13 |

The VA is cheaper on paper. Your real cost adds your own time to train, check and fix work. Plan for weeks of training, written steps for each task, and a daily review at the start.

## Which tasks need a licensed person?

Tasks that count as brokerage under your state's license law need a licensed person. TREC's article lists the line for Texas:

- **Not allowed for unlicensed assistants:** calls to find out if a person wants to buy, sell or lease property; showing a property, which includes unlocking or giving access to a prospect; hosting an open house.
- **Allowed:** setting a showing appointment for a license holder; confirming facts already advertised; data entry and typing contracts as a license holder directs; ordering supplies and scheduling office maintenance; bookkeeping.
- **Trust money:** TREC Rule 535.146(c)(7) lets only a license holder withdraw or transfer money from a trust account.

TREC also says unlicensed brokerage activity is a Class A misdemeanor in Texas. A broker or sales agent who employs the unlicensed person commits it too.

Money handling has its own rules. In California, the DRE trust fund guide (RE 13) says an unlicensed employee may sign on a trust account only with the broker's written approval and a fidelity bond. The bond must be at least equal to the most trust money that employee can reach. The broker stays liable either way.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I protect owner and tenant data?

Treat a VA like any staff member with access to personal data. The FTC's guide "Protecting Personal Information" gives five principles:

1. **Take stock.** Know what personal data you hold and where, including on remote staff computers.
2. **Scale down.** Keep only the data you need.
3. **Lock it.** Use physical, electronic, employee and contractor safeguards.
4. **Pitch it.** Destroy data you no longer need.
5. **Plan ahead.** Have a plan for a security incident.

The same FTC guide says to follow the "principle of least privilege", to encrypt remote access, and to consider multi-factor authentication. It also says to cut off access when a person leaves.

A VA access checklist:

- [ ] One named login per VA. No shared passwords.
- [ ] Multi-factor sign-in on email and the property management software.
- [ ] A user role with no bank, payment approval or owner bank-detail access.
- [ ] No tenant Social Security numbers or full screening reports unless the task needs them.
- [ ] Work only in company systems, not personal email or personal cloud drives.
- [ ] A written confidentiality agreement.
- [ ] Access removed the same day the VA leaves.

## How do I start with a VA?

Start with one VA and one task family, for example maintenance intake. Write the steps first. Measure the hours the task took your staff before the VA. Then compare after 60 days. If the numbers do not improve, stop or change the task.

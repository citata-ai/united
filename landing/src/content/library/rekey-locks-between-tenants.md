## Should you rekey locks after every tenant?

Yes, you should rekey locks after every tenant moves out. You never know how many copies of a key exist. A former tenant, a roommate, a dog walker or an ex-partner may still hold one. If one of them enters after the next tenant moves in, the risk lands on you.

Some states turn this habit into law. Texas Property Code 92.156(a) says a lock "operated by a key, card, or combination shall be rekeyed by the landlord at the landlord's expense not later than the seventh day after each tenant turnover date." The rule does not apply to closets or other interior doors (92.156(d)).

Texas also gives the tenant strong remedies when the landlord skips this step. Under 92.164, the tenant can rekey and deduct the cost from rent. After a written request and a missed deadline, the tenant can end the lease or sue. A court can then award actual damages and a civil penalty of 1 month's rent plus $500.

Rules differ by state. Here are 3 examples.

| State | Rekey between tenants | Who pays | Tenant lock change | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Texas | Required within 7 days after turnover | Landlord | Tenant can ask for more rekeys at tenant's cost | Property Code 92.156 |
| California | No turnover rule in the sections cited here | Landlord, for abuse-related changes | Victim of abuse may change locks if landlord misses 24 hours | Civil Code 1941.5, 1941.6 |
| Massachusetts | No turnover rule in the section cited here | Landlord may charge new tenant the lock and key cost at move-in | Not covered in 15B | G.L. c. 186, 15B(1)(b)(iv) |

Massachusetts is a useful contrast. Chapter 186, section 15B limits what a landlord may collect at the start of a tenancy to 4 items. One of them is "the purchase and installation cost for a key and lock."

## Rekey vs replace: which costs less?

Rekeying usually costs less than replacing the lock. A locksmith opens the cylinder, swaps the pins and cuts new keys. The lock body, the handle and the deadbolt stay. Replacing means you buy new hardware and pay for the install.

Prices vary a lot by city and by the lock brand, and we found no national survey we trust. So get 2 local quotes and run the numbers on your own unit.

Use this worked comparison. Fill in your own quotes.

1. **Count the cylinders.** Front door knob, front deadbolt, back door knob, back deadbolt, garage side door: 5 cylinders.
2. **Ask for a per-cylinder rekey price and a trip fee.** Rekey total = trip fee + (5 × per-cylinder price).
3. **Ask for a replace price.** Replace total = trip fee + 5 × (lock price + install labor).
4. **Ask to key all cylinders alike.** One key for all doors means fewer keys to track.
5. **Compare.** Replace only the locks that stick, look worn, or fail your insurance or local code.

A rekeyable lock system cuts the cost further. Some lock brands let you rekey the cylinder yourself with a tool and a new key. That can turn a locksmith visit into a short task you do at each turnover.

When to replace instead of rekey:

- The lock is loose, sticks or does not latch.
- The deadbolt does not reach full throw into the strike plate.
- The lock is old and a locksmith cannot buy pins for it.
- You move to a smart lock or a master key system.

## Are smart locks worth it in rentals?

Smart locks are worth it when you turn units often or show them without a person on site. A keypad lock lets you delete the old code and add a new one in minutes. No locksmith visit, no key handover.

Smart locks still need a turnover routine. The Texas rule covers locks "operated by a key, card, or combination." So a code is a key in legal terms there. Treat it the same way.

Smart lock turnover checklist:

- [ ] Delete every tenant code and every guest code.
- [ ] Remove the old tenant from the lock app or the property software.
- [ ] Remove vendor codes that you do not need.
- [ ] Rekey the backup key cylinder, if the lock has one.
- [ ] Change the batteries and test the lock 3 times.
- [ ] Set a new code for the next tenant on move-in day, not before.
- [ ] Log the date, the time and who made the change.

Watch for one risk: a self-showing lockbox or code that you share with prospects. The FTC warns that scammers copy real rental listings and post them with their own contact details. Give each showing a one-time code and expire it after the visit.

## Who pays for lost keys?

The answer depends on the state and the lease. In most cases, the lease decides who pays when a tenant loses a key. State law can set limits on what you charge.

Texas is a good example of the limits:

- **Normal wear.** The landlord may not charge the tenant for a lock repair due to normal wear and tear (92.162(a)).
- **Misuse or damage.** The landlord may charge for misuse or damage by the tenant, a family member, an occupant or a guest. An underlined clause in a written lease must allow it (92.162(b)).
- **Price cap.** The charge may not exceed what a third-party contractor would charge for material, labor, taxes and extra keys (92.162(d)).
- **Extra rekeys.** A tenant can ask for more rekeys at any time, at the tenant's cost (92.156(b)).
- **Early move-out.** If a tenant breaks a written lease and leaves, the landlord may deduct the rekey cost from the deposit only if the lease has an underlined or bold clause for it (92.156(e)).

Put your lost-key and lockout fees in the lease. State the amount or the method, such as "locksmith invoice plus extra keys."

## Can a tenant change the locks?

A tenant usually needs the landlord's consent to change the locks. Most leases say so. But some states give tenants a right to change locks in set cases.

California is the clearest example. Civil Code 1941.5 and 1941.6 cover tenants who are victims of abuse or who hold a protective order. The landlord must change the locks within 24 hours after the tenant gives the required papers, at the landlord's expense. If the landlord does not act in time, the tenant may change the locks without permission, even if the lease says no.

The tenant then has duties too. The new lock must be of similar or better quality. The tenant must tell the landlord within 24 hours and give a key. The landlord must pay the tenant back within 21 days.

Texas also lets a tenant rekey and deduct the cost from rent when the landlord misses the turnover deadline (92.164(a)(1)).

If a tenant changes locks without a legal right, do not force entry. Ask for a key in writing, cite the lease clause, and keep a record.

## South Carolina Security Deposit Rules at a Glance

South Carolina security deposit law is short. It lives in one section, S.C. Code 27-40-410, plus a few lines in other sections of the Residential Landlord and Tenant Act. Here is each rule with its statute. I read the full text on the South Carolina Legislature site on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | No cap in the statute | S.C. Code 27-40-410 |
| Return deadline | 30 days after the latest of: tenancy ends, possession returned, tenant demand | 27-40-410(a) |
| Itemized list | Required in writing for any deduction | 27-40-410(a) |
| Allowed deductions | Accrued rent and damage from breach of tenant duties | 27-40-410(a), 27-40-510 |
| Forwarding address | Tenant must give it in writing | 27-40-410(a) |
| Penalty | 3 times the amount wrongly kept, plus attorney's fees | 27-40-410(b) |
| Posted standards | More than 4 adjoining units with different deposit rules | 27-40-410(c) |
| Deposit status | Held in trust by the landlord | 27-40-210(18) |
| Sale of the building | Seller stays liable unless deposit moves to buyer with written notice | 27-40-450(a) |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What Is the Maximum Security Deposit in South Carolina?

South Carolina has no maximum security deposit. S.C. Code 27-40-410 tells the landlord how to return a deposit, but it does not limit the amount. The market and your lease set the number.

One rule limits how you set it. Subsection (c) applies if you rent more than 4 adjoining dwelling units on the premises. If you also use different standards to set deposits for different tenants, you must show those standards before the lease is signed. You can post them where rent is paid, post them in a clear place on the premises, or give each applicant a written copy.

Watch for: if you skip that step, the gap between a tenant's deposit and the lowest deposit for a comparable unit cannot pay for damage. You lose that part of your cushion.

What to do: write your deposit standards down, for example "1 month's rent, or 1.5 months with a cosigner". Apply them to every applicant the same way. That also helps you meet Fair Housing rules.

## How Many Days Does a South Carolina Landlord Have to Return a Deposit?

A South Carolina landlord has 30 days. The clock starts at the latest of 3 events, per S.C. Code 27-40-410(a):

1. **The tenancy ends.** The lease term runs out or a notice ends it.
2. **The tenant delivers possession.** Keys back, unit empty.
3. **The tenant demands the deposit.** Treat a written request as the safe proof.

The tenant must also give you a forwarding address in writing. If the tenant does not, you have a defense. The tenant cannot claim damages under subsection (a) if 2 facts are true. You had no notice of where the tenant went. You mailed the notice and any balance to the last known address.

Worked example: the lease ends on June 30. The tenant returns keys on July 2 and emails a demand with a new address on July 5. The latest event is July 5, so your deadline is August 4. Send the notice and the check on or before that date, by a method you can prove.

## What Can a South Carolina Landlord Deduct From a Deposit?

A South Carolina landlord may deduct 2 things: accrued rent, and damage the landlord suffers because the tenant broke a duty in S.C. Code 27-40-510. The statute names no other deduction.

Section 27-40-510 lists the tenant duties. The tenant must keep the unit reasonably safe and clean and dispose of waste properly. The tenant must keep plumbing fixtures reasonably clean and use appliances and systems in a reasonable manner. The tenant must not deliberately or negligently damage, deface or remove any part of the premises, or let a guest do so.

The statute does not use the phrase "normal wear and tear". But the deduction must link to a breach of these duties. Paint that fades with time is not a breach. A hole in a wall is.

Fire is a special case. Under S.C. Code 27-40-650(b), if a fire was not the tenant's fault and the lease ends, you return the deposit and all prepaid rent. If the tenant caused the fire, you may withhold, but you must still send the 27-40-410(a) notice.

| Charge | Deductible? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Unpaid rent through move-out | Yes | Accrued rent, 27-40-410(a) |
| Broken window from a tenant's guest | Yes | 27-40-510(6) |
| Trash left in the unit | Usually | 27-40-510(3) waste duty |
| Carpet worn from normal walking | No clear basis | No breach of a 27-40-510 duty |
| Repaint after 5 years of normal use | No clear basis | No breach of a 27-40-510 duty |

## Must a South Carolina Landlord Itemize Deductions?

Yes. S.C. Code 27-40-410(a) says any deduction must be itemized in a written notice to the tenant. The notice goes out with the amount due, if any, within the 30 days.

Use this checklist for each move-out:

- [ ] Date-stamped photos at move-in and move-out
- [ ] Each deduction on its own line, with the cost
- [ ] An invoice or receipt for each repair
- [ ] The balance due, with a check or transfer in the same envelope
- [ ] Proof of mailing to the forwarding address
- [ ] A copy in the tenant file

## Must You Pay Interest or Use a Separate Account in South Carolina?

S.C. Code 27-40-410 has no interest rule and no separate account rule. The text does not require either one.

The definition in S.C. Code 27-40-210(18) still matters. It calls the deposit money "held in trust by the landlord". A separate account is not required, but it makes the money easy to trace.

If you sell the building, S.C. Code 27-40-450(a) applies. The seller stays liable for the deposit, unless the deposit moves to the buyer and the tenant gets written notice within a reasonable time. Then the buyer is liable.

## What Is the Penalty for Keeping a Deposit Wrongly in South Carolina?

The penalty is 3 times the amount wrongly withheld, plus reasonable attorney's fees, under S.C. Code 27-40-410(b). It applies when the landlord does not return the deposit or prepaid rent with the required notice.

Worked calculation:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Deposit held | $1,400 |
| Deductions you can prove | $350 |
| Deductions you cannot prove | $250 |
| Refund you sent | $800 |
| Amount wrongly withheld | $250 |
| Tenant claim at 3 times | $750, plus attorney's fees |

Subsection (d) also keeps other claims open for both sides. A lease that breaks other rules can cost more. S.C. Code 27-40-330(b) covers a landlord who deliberately uses a banned lease clause and tries to enforce it. That landlord can owe up to the deposit amount on top of actual damages.

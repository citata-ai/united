## What is a tenant ledger?

A tenant ledger is the running record of every charge, payment and credit on one tenant's lease. Each line has a date, a description, an amount and the balance after that line. On any date, the ledger answers one question: what does this tenant owe?

Here is a sample tenant ledger for 3 months:

| Date | Description | Charge | Payment | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-07-01 | Rent, July | $1,500 | | $1,500 |
| 2026-07-03 | Payment, portal ACH | | $1,500 | $0 |
| 2026-08-01 | Rent, August | $1,500 | | $1,500 |
| 2026-08-06 | Late fee per lease section 4 | $75 | | $1,575 |
| 2026-08-09 | Payment, portal ACH | | $1,500 | $75 |
| 2026-08-20 | Tenant damage: broken blind, WO #2231 | $45 | | $120 |
| 2026-09-01 | Rent, September | $1,500 | | $1,620 |
| 2026-09-02 | Payment, portal ACH | | $1,620 | $0 |

The math on 2026-08-20: $75 + $45 = $120. The ledger always shows the running balance, so nobody has to add it up by hand.

## What entries go on a tenant ledger?

A tenant ledger holds 7 kinds of entries. Each one needs a source document, so you can prove it later.

| Entry type | Example | Source document |
|---|---|---|
| Recurring charge | Monthly rent, pet rent, parking | Lease and addenda |
| One-time charge | Late fee, NSF fee, lock change | Lease clause and notice |
| Tenant damage charge | Broken window | Work order, photos, invoice |
| Payment | ACH, check, money order, cash | Bank record or receipt |
| Credit | Rent concession, utility credit | Signed agreement or approval |
| Deposit | Security deposit, pet deposit | Lease and receipt |
| Reversal | Returned payment, voided charge | Bank notice or correction note |

Use this checklist for a clean ledger:

- [ ] Every charge cites the lease section or work order behind it
- [ ] Every payment shows the method and a reference number
- [ ] Late fees match the lease amount and the grace period
- [ ] Corrections use a new reversing line, never a deleted line
- [ ] Deposits sit in their own section or account, apart from rent
- [ ] Credits and concessions have a written approval

The rule on corrections matters most. If you delete a wrong line, the ledger loses its history. A reversing line keeps the record honest and shows what changed.

## Why does a tenant ledger matter in a rent dispute?

A tenant ledger matters in a rent dispute because it is the main proof of what the tenant owes. In a dispute or an eviction case, you may need to show how each dollar of the balance came about. A ledger with clear lines and source documents makes that simple.

Watch for these 4 ledger problems. Each one weakens your case:

1. **Unexplained charges.** A line that says "misc" with no lease clause or work order.
2. **Fee stacking.** A late fee charged on a past late fee, if the lease does not allow it.
3. **Wrong payment order.** Payments applied to fees first when the lease or local law says rent comes first.
4. **Missing payments.** A payment the tenant can prove but the ledger does not show.

A worked example. A tenant says they owe $0. The ledger shows $120. You print the ledger and point to 2 lines. One is a $75 late fee (lease section 4, paid 5 days late). The other is a $45 blind charge (work order #2231, with photos). The tenant can now see the source of each dollar, and the talk moves from "who is right" to "is this charge fair".

Some states set rules for receipts, which feed the ledger. Minnesota statute 504B.118, as shown on the Revisor's site, says a landlord who receives rent in cash "must provide a written receipt". The receipt is due at once for an in-person payment, or within 3 business days otherwise. The Revisor page notes a 2026 amendment, so check the current text.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Tenant ledger vs rent ledger: what is the difference?

A tenant ledger tracks one tenant or lease across every type of charge. A rent ledger, as many managers use the term, tracks rent across units for a period. The words overlap, and software vendors use them in slightly different ways.

| Feature | Tenant ledger | Rent ledger or rent roll |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | One tenant or one lease | Many units |
| Time | Whole lease, from move-in | Usually one month or period |
| Entries | Rent, fees, damage, credits, deposits | Rent due, rent paid, balance per unit |
| Main use | Disputes, move-out, collections | Owner reports, occupancy, cash flow |
| Who sees it | Tenant, manager, court | Owner, manager, lender |

Both come from the same transactions. If your software posts a payment to the tenant ledger, the rent roll updates too. If the 2 reports disagree, a posting error exists. Find it before you send the owner statement.

## How long should you keep tenant ledgers?

Keep tenant ledgers at least as long as the IRS and your state require, and longer if a dispute is open. The IRS page "How long should I keep records?" gives these periods for records that support a tax return:

| Situation | Keep records for |
|---|---|
| General rule | 3 years from filing |
| Unreported income over 25% of gross income | 6 years |
| Claim for a bad debt deduction | 7 years |
| No return filed | Indefinitely |

Rent from tenants is income on the owner's tax return. The ledger supports that income. So the owner's tax record clock applies to it. Your state's real estate license rules may also set a period for a manager's trust records. Use the longest period that applies.

Store ledgers in your software and export a copy at move-out. If you change software, export every ledger first.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## Can a tenant ask for a copy of the ledger?

A tenant can ask for a copy of the ledger, and you should give one. A copy shows good faith and often ends a dispute fast. Many tenant portals show the ledger all the time.

Some states require receipts on request. Nevada statute NRS 118A.250 covers receipts. The landlord "shall deliver to the tenant upon the tenant's request a signed written receipt" for deposits, fees and rent paid. The same section says "the tenant may refuse to make rent payments until the landlord tenders the requested receipt." That is a strong reason to answer requests fast.

Before you send the copy, check 3 things:

1. **Dates.** The ledger covers the whole period the tenant asked about.
2. **Notes.** Internal notes do not show on the tenant copy.
3. **Balance.** The balance matches what the portal shows.

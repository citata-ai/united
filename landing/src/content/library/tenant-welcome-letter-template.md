## What is a tenant welcome letter for?

A tenant welcome letter is for the first week of a tenancy. It answers the questions a new tenant asks in their first 7 days, in one place, before they need to call you. A clear letter can cut phone calls in the first month and sets a calm, clear tone for the tenancy.

The letter is not the lease. It points to the lease and says that the lease controls if the two differ.

## Which contacts and rent payment steps go in the letter?

The contacts and rent steps go at the top, because tenants look for them first. Put them in a short table.

| Item | What to write |
|---|---|
| Office | Name, phone, email, hours |
| Emergency line | 24/7 phone number and what counts as an emergency |
| Repair requests | Portal link or email, and the response times you promise |
| Rent amount | The monthly amount, and any prorated first-month amount |
| Due date | The day rent is due and the last day before a late fee, as the lease states |
| How to pay | Portal, ACH or other methods you accept; any fees for card payments |
| First payment | What is due before move-in, and what is due on the next due date |

Watch for: say only what the lease says about late fees. A welcome letter that gives a different grace period than the lease starts a dispute.

## How do you explain move-in rules?

You explain move-in rules with a short list of the steps for move-in day and the 4 or 5 lease rules that cause the most questions. Long lists do not get read.

Move-in day:

1. **Key pickup:** where, when and what ID to bring.
2. **Elevator or parking for the truck:** how to book it, if needed.
3. **Move-in condition report:** fill in, add photos, and return within [X] days.
4. **Smoke alarms:** test each one on day one. The U.S. Fire Administration says to test smoke alarms monthly.
5. **Find the shut-offs:** main water valve, electrical panel, gas shut-off.

Rules that cause the most questions:

- **Parking:** assigned spots, guest parking, towing.
- **Trash and recycling:** pickup days and where bins go.
- **Pets:** what the pet addendum allows.
- **Guests:** how long a guest may stay under the lease.
- **Changes to the unit:** painting, mounting a TV, changing locks.

## What utility setup info should you add?

Add a table of each utility, who pays it, and the provider to contact. The tenant should start service in their name on the move-in date, so there is no gap.

| Utility | Who pays | Provider and contact | Start-of-service note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electricity | [Tenant / owner] | [Name, phone, website] | Set start date to [move-in date] |
| Gas | [Tenant / owner] | [ ] | [ ] |
| Water and sewer | [Tenant / owner] | [ ] | [ ] |
| Trash | [Tenant / owner] | [ ] | Pickup day: [ ] |
| Internet | Tenant | [Providers that serve the address] | Book early |

Add 2 short home tips that save tenants money and you service calls. The U.S. Department of Energy's Energy Saver Guide suggests a water heater setting of 120°F and cleaning or replacing HVAC filters once a month or as recommended. If the tenant changes filters, say so and give the filter size.

Also remind the tenant to contact 811 before any digging, even to plant a shrub. The 811 Before You Dig site says the service is free and that damage to buried utilities costs the U.S. about $30 billion a year.

## Which legal notices can go with the letter?

Legal notices that the lease does not already hold can go with the letter. The letter is a good carrier, but check that each notice meets its own deadline.

- **Florida:** a landlord with 5 or more units must give written notice within 30 days of receiving a deposit or advance rent. It names where the money is held and whether interest is paid (Fla. Stat. 83.49(2)).
- **New York:** a landlord who deposits a security deposit in a bank must tell the tenant in writing the bank's name and address and the amount (Gen. Oblig. Law 7-103).
- **Pre-1978 housing:** the EPA lead disclosure and pamphlet must be given before the lease is signed. The welcome letter is too late for that, so confirm it is in the lease file.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Tenant welcome letter template

Copy this text, fill in the brackets, and change it to fit your lease.

> **Welcome to [address]**
>
> Dear [tenant names],
>
> Welcome to your new home. We are glad you are here. This letter has what you need for your first weeks. Your lease is the full agreement; if this letter and the lease differ, the lease controls.
>
> **Your contacts**
> Office: [phone], [email], [hours].
> Emergency line, 24/7: [phone]. Use it for fire, gas smell (leave and call the gas company first), flooding, no heat in cold weather, sewage backup, or a door that will not lock.
> Repair requests: [portal link]. We respond to urgent requests within [X] hours and routine requests within [X] business days.
>
> **Rent**
> Rent is $[amount], due on the [day] of each month. Pay through [method]. Your first payment of $[amount] is due [date]. See section [X] of your lease for late fees.
>
> **Move-in day: [date]**
> Pick up keys at [place] at [time]. Bring photo ID. [Book the elevator / loading zone at (contact).]
> Please complete the move-in condition report, with photos, and return it by [date]. It protects your deposit.
> Please test each smoke alarm on day one, and then once a month.
> Your main water shut-off is [location]. The electrical panel is [location].
>
> **Utilities**
> Please start these in your name from [date]: [list with providers]. We pay [list].
> [If tenant changes filters:] HVAC filter size: [size]. Please change it every [X] months.
> Before you dig anywhere on the property, contact 811 a few business days ahead.
>
> **House rules to know**
> Parking: [rule]. Trash: [day and place]. Pets: [rule]. Guests: [rule]. Changes to the unit: ask us first in writing.
>
> **Your deposit**
> We hold your deposit of $[amount] at [bank name and address, if required]. [Add any state-required deposit notice.]
>
> If you have any question, call or write to [name] at [phone] or [email]. Welcome home.
>
> [Name], [title], [company]

## How do you make the letter work?

You make the letter work by sending it early, in the format the tenant reads, and by checking it against the lease each time. Send it by email and post a printed copy in the unit on move-in day.

Worked example: say a manager with 120 doors places 4 new tenants a month. With one template, each letter takes about 10 minutes to fill in: about 40 minutes a month. If each letter prevents just 2 "how do I pay?" or "who do I call?" calls, it saves 8 calls a month.

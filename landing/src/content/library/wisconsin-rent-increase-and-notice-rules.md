## Wisconsin rent and notice rules at a glance

Wisconsin has few rent rules, and one strong ban. Wis. Stat. 66.1015 stops local rent control. Chapter 704 sets the notices that end a tenancy. We read both from the Wisconsin Legislature's official files on 2026-10-19. The live site did not load from our network, so we read the Internet Archive copies.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Statewide rent cap | None | Ch. 704, ch. 66 |
| Local rent control | Banned for cities, villages, towns and counties | Wis. Stat. 66.1015(1) |
| Rent increase notice | No separate notice in the statutes | Ch. 704 |
| End month-to-month | At least 28 days, written, at the end of a rental period | Wis. Stat. 704.19(2)(b), (3) |
| Rent paid more often than monthly | Notice equal to the rent period | Wis. Stat. 704.19(3) |
| Fixed-term lease | Rent set by the lease | The lease |
| Automatic renewal clause | Enforceable only with a separate reminder notice | Wis. Stat. 704.15 |
| Retaliatory increase | Banned | Wis. Stat. 704.45 |
| Lease lets you raise rent for calling police or health services | Lease void | Wis. Stat. 704.44(1m) |

## Does Wisconsin have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

No. Wisconsin has no statewide rent cap and bans local rent control. Wis. Stat. 66.1015(1) says: "No city, village, town or county may regulate the amount of rent or fees charged for the use of a residential rental dwelling unit."

The ban has two narrow exceptions in 66.1015(2). A local government or housing authority may set rent in a unit it owns or operates. It may also make an agreement with a private person who chooses to regulate rent. Wisconsin courts read the second exception narrowly. The annotation cites Apartment Ass'n of South Central Wisconsin v. City of Madison (2006).

Wis. Stat. 66.1015(3) also bars local inclusionary zoning. A local government may not require a set share of units to be rented below market to lower-income households.

So the size of a Wisconsin rent increase depends on the lease and the market. Two state limits still apply:

- **No retaliation.** Wis. Stat. 704.45(1) bars a rent increase that would not occur but for a tenant's protected act.
- **No penalty for calling for help.** A lease is void if it lets you raise rent because a tenant contacted police, health or safety services (Wis. Stat. 704.44(1m)).

## How many days notice does a Wisconsin landlord need to raise the rent?

The Wisconsin statutes set no separate notice for a rent increase. For a periodic tenancy, the timing comes from the termination rule in Wis. Stat. 704.19.

A month-to-month tenancy ends only with at least 28 days' written notice, at the end of a rental period (704.19(2)(b)1. and (3)). A rent change is a change of terms. So the safe path is to give at least that much written notice before the new rent starts.

**Worked example.** Rent is $1,250, due on the first. You want $1,325 from July 1. June 30 is the end of a rental period. Count back at least 28 days: give written notice on or before June 3. If you mail the notice, add time. Wis. Stat. 704.19(7)(c) says a notice mailed within the state counts as given on the 2nd day after mailing.

| Notice method | When notice counts as given | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Hand delivery to the tenant | The day you give it | 704.19(7)(a) |
| Mail within Wisconsin | 2nd day after mailing | 704.19(7)(c) |
| Actual receipt | The day the tenant receives it | 704.19(7)(e) |

Each rule counts that day as the first day of the notice period (704.19(7)). Use one of the methods in Wis. Stat. 704.21 and keep proof.

If the notice gives too few days, it is not void. Under 704.19(5), it takes effect on the first date that could have been properly set.

## Can a Wisconsin landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

Only if the lease allows it. A fixed-term lease sets the rent for the term. We found no statute that lets a Wisconsin landlord change the rent mid-term without the tenant's agreement.

To build in an increase, put it in the lease:

1. **Escalation clause.** State the date and the new amount or percent.
2. **Renewal offer.** State the new rent for the next term.

Watch automatic renewal clauses. Under Wis. Stat. 704.15, a residential clause that renews the lease unless the tenant gives notice is not enforceable unless you send a reminder. Send it in writing 15 to 30 days before the tenant's deadline to give notice, and point the tenant to the clause.

ATCP 134.09(3) repeats this rule. A landlord may not enforce an automatic renewal without that separate written notice.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Wisconsin?

A Wisconsin month-to-month tenancy ends with at least 28 days' written notice, at the end of a rental period. Wis. Stat. 704.19 applies to every periodic tenancy and tenancy at will, however created.

| Tenancy | Notice to end | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Month-to-month | At least 28 days, ending at the end of a month of the tenancy | 704.19(3) |
| Rent paid weekly | At least one rent period | 704.19(3) |
| Year-to-year, residential | At least 28 days, ending at the end of the rental year | 704.19(2)(b)1., (3) |
| Year-to-year, agricultural | At least 90 days | 704.19(3) |

The notice must be in writing. It must inform the other party of the intent to end the tenancy and the end date (704.19(4)). Small errors that do not mislead do not void it.

Either side may agree to another way to end the tenancy, but it must be proved by clear and convincing proof (704.19(2)(a)1.). A shorter notice is allowed for nonpayment, a breach or certain crimes under 704.17 (704.19(2)(b)2.).

## Do cities in Wisconsin add their own rent or notice rules?

Wisconsin cities may not set rent rules. Wis. Stat. 66.1015(1) bars any city, village, town or county from regulating the amount of rent or fees for a residential unit. That covers Milwaukee, Madison and every other municipality.

Cities may still run other rental programs, such as inspections or registration. Those do not set rent. Check the local code for them.

The state consumer rule, ATCP 134, also applies everywhere. ATCP 134.10 covers how the chapter interacts with local ordinances. Before you sign a lease, ATCP 134.04 requires you to disclose certain facts. One example: whether the rent includes water, heat or electricity.

## Wisconsin rent increase checklist

- [ ] Lease type confirmed: fixed term or periodic.
- [ ] Fixed term: increase only by a written escalation clause or at renewal.
- [ ] Month-to-month: written notice gives at least 28 days and ends at the end of a rental period.
- [ ] Mail time added: in-state mail counts on the 2nd day after mailing.
- [ ] No link between the increase and a tenant's complaint or call for help.
- [ ] Automatic renewal reminder sent 15 to 30 days before the tenant's notice deadline.

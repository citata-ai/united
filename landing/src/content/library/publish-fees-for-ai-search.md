## What does AI say when your fees are hidden?

When your fees are hidden, AI quotes an average from other pages or names a company that shows its fees. An AI answer can only repeat facts it finds. If your fee page says "competitive pricing, call us", there is no number to repeat.

ClearLead Digital, an agency that sells marketing to property managers, says AI tools tell owners the "average management fee" is 8–12%. ClearLead says that range comes from pages like its own fee guide. It also calls fee publishing "the highest-leverage change" a manager can make. That is an agency's view, not a study. But the logic is simple: an engine cannot cite a number that is not on the page.

Our own data shows where engines look. In our 50-city test on 2026-10-19, Gemini linked a property manager's own site in 48 of 50 answers, and Claude in 43 of 50 (our data, [AI Property Manager Index 2026](/research/ai-property-manager-index-2026)). When an engine opens your site, the fee page is your chance to give it a number.

Google says the same thing in general terms. Its guide on generative AI search says content that is "unique, compelling, and useful" is the factor most likely to affect presence over time. A real fee table is unique. A "call for pricing" line is not.

## Which fees should you show?

Show every fee an owner can pay, and name the fees you do not charge. Owners compare on the full cost, not on the monthly percent alone. AI answers to "property management fees in <city>" often list several fee types, so a full table gives the engine more to quote.

Use this checklist:

- [ ] Monthly management fee (percent of collected rent, or flat amount)
- [ ] Minimum monthly fee, if any
- [ ] Leasing or tenant placement fee
- [ ] Lease renewal fee
- [ ] Setup or onboarding fee
- [ ] Maintenance markup or coordination fee
- [ ] Vacancy fee (do you charge when the unit is empty?)
- [ ] Inspection fees (move-in, move-out, periodic)
- [ ] Eviction coordination fee
- [ ] Early cancellation fee and notice period
- [ ] Late fee split (who keeps tenant late fees?)
- [ ] Fees you do not charge, stated in words: "No setup fee."

The last line matters. Owners search for "property manager no leasing fee". A plain "We do not charge a leasing fee" sentence answers that search directly.

## How do you show ranges?

Show a range only with the condition that moves the price. "7–10%" alone leaves the engine to guess. "8% for 1 to 4 units, 7% for 5 or more" gives it a fact it can repeat.

Here is a format that works. The numbers below are an example layout only, not market rates.

| Fee | Price | When it applies |
|---|---|---|
| Management | 8% of collected rent | 1–4 units |
| Management | 7% of collected rent | 5+ units |
| Leasing | 50% of first month's rent | New tenant placed |
| Lease renewal | $150 | Tenant renews |
| Setup | $0 | We do not charge one |
| Maintenance markup | 0% | We pass vendor bills at cost |
| Cancellation | $0 after 60 days' notice | Any time after month 12 |

Then add one worked example in plain words. Example: a home rents for $2,000 and the tenant pays every month. At 8%, the monthly fee is $160 and the yearly fee is $1,920. A new lease adds $1,000 once (50% of $2,000). Total for year 1: $2,920.

A worked example helps 2 readers. The owner sees the real cost. The engine sees a full sentence it can quote. Our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) runs the same math for any rent and fee mix.

Watch for: fees that do not match across pages. If your fee page says 8% and your city page says 7%, an engine may quote either one. Keep one fee table and link to it.

## Where should the fee table live?

Put the fee table on one page on your site, then copy the same numbers to your listings. Each copy must match.

1. **Your site.** One fee page, in an HTML table, not an image or a PDF. Add the date you last changed it.
2. **Each city page.** A short fee line and a link to the full table.
3. **Google Business Profile.** Google's help page says you "select Edit services", pick the service, and "add info such as price and description". Google says to avoid prices in custom service names, and it rejects custom services that break its policy.
4. **Directories.** Many list sites have a pricing field. Use the same numbers.

OpenAI warns that search results "can be incomplete, outdated, or incorrect". An old fee on a forgotten directory page is one way that happens. Fix the old copy, or remove it.

## Does a fee table cost you leads?

We do not know of a study that measures this for property managers. Some owners fear that a price scares leads away or helps competitors undercut them. ClearLead's answer to the second fear: "Your competitors already know your pricing. The AI answering an owner's question does not."

So test it on your own numbers. Here is a 90-day plan:

1. **Log a baseline.** Count owner form leads per month for the last 3 months.
2. **Add a source question.** Ask each lead: "How did you find us?" Include "ChatGPT or another AI" as an answer.
3. **Publish the table.** Same day on the site and on your Google Business Profile.
4. **Run 10 fee prompts in AI.** "Property management fees <city>", "<your company> fees". Log what each engine says.
5. **Compare after 90 days.** Leads, lead quality, and whether AI now quotes your real numbers.

Our guide on [how to track AI traffic and AI mentions](/guides/track-ai-traffic-property-management) shows how to set up step 2.

A public fee table also screens leads. An owner who reads your fees before the call already accepts your price range. That is a hypothesis, not a measured result. Your 90-day log will tell you if it holds for your company.

## How do I get a Philadelphia rental license?

You get a Philadelphia Rental License from the Department of Licenses and Inspections (L&I), online in eCLIPSE or at the Permit and License Center. The license was once called a Housing Inspection License. L&I reviews an application within 5 business days (City of Philadelphia).

Here is what the city asks for:

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Tax account | Be current on all city taxes and fines. A tax clearance helps. |
| Business license | Commercial Activity License, or an Activity License Number if you live in the property and rent 3 or fewer units |
| Ownership proof | Recorded deed, signed settlement sheet, or OPA record after 6 months |
| Legal occupancy | Certificate of Occupancy, a prior rental license from the last 3 years, or a pre-2000 zoning permit with an affidavit |
| Lead | Lead-safe or lead-free certification for a building from before March 1978 |
| Violations | No open L&I violations |
| Managing agent | Required if you live outside Philadelphia: a person with a Philadelphia mailing address |
| Supplemental form | Rental License Supplemental Information form, for a new application |

One license covers all units in one building. A property with 2 buildings needs 2 licenses. The license is not transferable: a new owner closes the old license and applies again.

## How much does the rental license cost?

The Philadelphia Rental License costs $69 per unit per year. The same rate applies to a renewal. The maximum fee is $27,830, and owner-occupied units pay nothing (City of Philadelphia, read 2026-10-19).

A worked example for a 3-unit building where you live in 1 unit:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Rented units | 2 |
| Fee per unit | $69 |
| Owner-occupied unit | $0 |
| Yearly license fee | 2 × $69 = $138 |
| Late renewal, 4 months past expiry | $138 × 1.5% × 4 = $8.28 extra |

The late fee applies only when you renew more than 60 days after the due date. L&I emails a reminder 45 days before expiry and mails one about 30 days before. A credit card payment adds a 2.10% surcharge.

## What is the Certificate of Rental Suitability?

The Certificate of Rental Suitability is a free L&I document that you give the tenant before move-in. It must be issued no more than 60 days before the lease starts (City of Philadelphia). L&I says you need a new one each time you rent to a new tenant or renew a lease. The Fair Housing Commission page says the move-in documents do not apply to renewals. The two pages differ, so the safe choice is a new certificate at each renewal too.

To get it in eCLIPSE, you enter the rental license number and certify that the fire alarm and smoke detectors work. You cannot get it with open violations, except ones under appeal.

The Fair Housing Commission says the certificate confirms that:

- **Licenses:** you hold the needed licenses, including the Rental License.
- **Repairs:** the property has no open maintenance issues or fire code violations.
- **Safety gear:** the unit has working fire extinguishers and smoke detectors.
- **Systems:** the operating systems have no health and safety defects.
- **Upkeep:** you will maintain the property through the tenancy.

With the certificate, you also give the tenant the "Partners in Good Housing" handbook. In buildings with 3 or more units, you give the building smoking policy too (City of Philadelphia).

## What does the Philadelphia lead certification law require?

Philadelphia landlords must test and certify a rental as lead-safe or lead-free before a new or renewed lease. The same certificate is needed to get or renew a rental license. This applies to any property built before March 1978 (City of Philadelphia). For a later building, you file a Property Owner Statement of Exemption.

The steps:

1. **Check the build year.** Use the deed or OPA record. Before March 1978 means you test.
2. **Hire a certified pro.** Use an EPA-approved lead dust wipe sampling technician or a PA Department of Health certified lead abatement firm.
3. **Upload the result.** Submit it to the city's Lead Certification Submission System.
4. **Give the tenant a copy.** Keep your own copy with the lease.
5. **Repeat on schedule.** Test again before the next lease or license renewal if your certificate does not cover it.

The original 2011 law covered pre-1978 rentals with children age 6 or younger (City of Philadelphia lead resources). The city's license pages now tie lead certification to every pre-March-1978 rental license.

## Can a Philadelphia landlord collect rent without a license?

No. Philadelphia Legal Assistance says it is illegal for a landlord without a rental license to collect rent. A 2026 Philadelphia court opinion, as summarized by the law firm Nochumson P.C., quotes the code:

- **Section 9-3202:** "no person shall collect rent" on a property that needs a license "unless a valid rental license has been issued."
- **Section 9-3903:** at the start of each tenancy, you give the tenant the certificate, your attestation and the handbook.
- **Section 9-3901(4)(e):** an owner who does not comply "shall be denied the right to recover possession of the premises or collect rent during or for the period of noncompliance."

In that case, the judge refused the rent claim but still gave the landlord possession. Do not plan on that result. The safe path is simple: hold a valid license and a fresh certificate before each lease starts. Tenants can check your license on the city's Atlas map.

## What does Philadelphia's Good Cause rule cover?

Philadelphia Code 9-804 requires good cause to end or not renew a lease shorter than 1 year, including month-to-month (Fair Housing Commission). Good cause reasons include:

- Habitual nonpayment or late payment of rent
- Breach of a material lease term
- Nuisance, or substantial damage to the property
- Refusal of access after written notice
- Refusal to sign an extension on generally the same terms
- Owner or immediate family member moves in
- Refusal of a reasonable rent increase or lease change, after notice
- Renovation that needs an empty unit, with notice and options

You must give at least 30 days' written notice with the reason, by hand or first-class mail with proof of mailing. Without that notice, the lease renews month to month. The tenant has 15 business days to challenge it at the Fair Housing Commission or in court.

The same section bans a rent rise or a notice to vacate in response to a code citation. Self-help eviction, such as a lockout or a utility shutoff, is illegal under Chapter 9-1600.

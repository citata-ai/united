## How many states ban source-of-income discrimination?

Nineteen states plus the District of Columbia and Guam ban it, per the Poverty & Race Research Action Council (PRRAC). PRRAC's Appendix B, updated March 2026, is the most complete public list we found. It has tracked these laws since 2009.

PRRAC estimated in 2022 that over 57% of voucher holders were covered. With Michigan's law in 2025, it now says "it is likely that over 60% of voucher holders are now protected."

PRRAC groups the states in three lists:

| Group | States |
|---|---|
| Laws that protect voucher holders | 19 states, plus DC and Guam |
| Laws weakened or narrow | Maine and Minnesota (court rulings), Texas (homeowners association rules only), Wisconsin (does not cover vouchers) |
| Laws that block local protections | Florida, Idaho, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Missouri, Texas |

Note: the HUD Inspector General also counted these laws. Its site blocked our reader, so we do not use its numbers here.

The scope differs a lot. Colorado and Michigan exempt small landlords. For New York, PRRAC notes a recent court ruling against the law. The law still stands while the case is on appeal. Read the full text for your state before you rely on this table.

## Which cities and counties have local laws?

Many cities and counties have their own laws, and some apply in states without one. PRRAC's appendix lists local laws in 26 states. Examples:

| State | Local laws PRRAC lists (examples) |
|---|---|
| Arizona | Phoenix, Tempe, Tucson |
| California | Los Angeles, San Diego, San Francisco, San Jose, Santa Monica |
| Colorado | Boulder, Denver |
| Florida | Miami-Dade County, Broward County, Tampa (partial state preemption) |
| Georgia | Atlanta (city-supported properties only) |
| Illinois | Chicago, Cook County, Naperville, Oak Park |
| Kansas | Lawrence |
| Maryland | Baltimore City, Montgomery County, Prince George's County |
| Wisconsin | Dane County, Madison, Milwaukee County |

Some listed laws no longer work. PRRAC marks Boise and three Iowa cities as preempted by state law in 2021, and Louisville and Lexington in 2024. Some local laws, such as Wheeling, Illinois, do not cover vouchers.

## Is there a federal rule?

There is no general federal ban. The Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. 3604, does not list source of income among its protected classes.

Federal rules do apply to some funded properties. PRRAC lists these:

- **Low-Income Housing Tax Credit.** 26 U.S.C. 42(h)(6)(B)(iv) bars refusing a voucher holder.
- **HOME program.** 42 U.S.C. 12745 and 24 CFR 92.252(d) bar the same.
- **Others.** The Capital Magnet Fund, Mark-to-Market, the National Housing Trust Fund and some properties bought from HUD.

## What are the penalties?

Penalties vary by state and city. PRRAC gives two ends of the range:

- **Hawai'i** limits fines to $2,500 and bars attorney's fees for winning claimants. PRRAC calls it "an extraordinarily weak state law."
- **Michigan** allows injunctive relief, damages and attorney's fees.

Several laws also cover more than a refusal. Washington's RCW 59.18.255 bars ads that show a source-of-income preference. It also bars telling someone a unit is not available when it is. If you use an income test, you must subtract the voucher from the rent before you apply it.

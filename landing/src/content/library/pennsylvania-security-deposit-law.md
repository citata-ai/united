A tenant pays $1,500 a month and moves out on June 30. The landlord has until July 30 to send a written damage list and the refund. Miss that date and the landlord loses the right to keep any of the deposit. That rule sits in Section 512 of the Pennsylvania Landlord and Tenant Act of 1951. This page puts the deposit sections of that Act in one place. I read the official text from the Pennsylvania General Assembly on 2026-10-19.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Summary Table: Pennsylvania Deposit Rules

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit, first lease year | 2 months' rent | Section 511.1(a) |
| Maximum deposit, second year and renewals | 1 month's rent | Section 511.1(b) |
| Tenant in place 5 years or more | Rent increase needs no deposit increase | Section 511.1(d) |
| Escrow account | Deposits over $100, at a regulated bank | Section 511.2(a) |
| Written notice of the bank | Bank name, address and deposit amount | Section 511.2(a) |
| Interest to tenant | After the 2nd anniversary; landlord keeps 1% a year | Section 511.2(b), (c) |
| Bond instead of escrow | Allowed, from a bonding company licensed in Pennsylvania | Section 511.3 |
| Damage list and refund | 30 days after lease end or surrender | Section 512(a) |
| No list in 30 days | Landlord forfeits right to withhold or sue for damage | Section 512(b) |
| Late or no refund | Double the amount wrongly kept | Section 512(c) |
| Waiver by tenant | Void | Sections 511.1(f), 512(d) |

The rules apply to residential leases only (Sections 511.1(e) and 512(f)).

## What Is the Maximum Security Deposit a Landlord Can Charge in Pennsylvania?

The Pennsylvania deposit cap depends on the lease year. Section 511.1(a) says no landlord may require more than 2 months' rent in escrow during the first year of any lease. Section 511.1(b) drops the cap to 1 month's rent in the second and later years, and during any renewal.

A third rule protects long-term tenants. After a tenant has lived in the unit 5 years, a rent increase does not require a higher deposit (Section 511.1(d)).

Here is how the cap works for a unit at $1,500 a month:

| Lease year | Cap | Maximum deposit |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | 2 months' rent | $3,000 |
| Year 2 or renewal | 1 month's rent | $1,500 |
| Year 6, rent raised to $1,700 | No forced increase | Deposit can stay at $1,500 |

## How Many Days Does a Pennsylvania Landlord Have to Return a Security Deposit?

A Pennsylvania landlord has 30 days. Section 512(a) starts the clock at the end of the lease or at the landlord's acceptance of surrender, whichever comes first. Within that time, the landlord gives the tenant a written list of damages. The payment of the balance goes with the list.

The tenant has one duty too. Section 512(e) says the landlord has no liability under Section 512 if the tenant does not give a new address in writing at move-out. Ask for the forwarding address on your move-out form.

## What Can a Landlord Deduct From a Security Deposit in Pennsylvania?

A Pennsylvania landlord may deduct three kinds of cost under Section 512(a):

1. **Damage the tenant caused.** The landlord keeps the actual amount of damage to the unit caused by the tenant.
2. **Unpaid rent.** The Act lets the landlord refuse to return the deposit for nonpayment of rent.
3. **Other lease breaches.** The landlord may also keep funds for the breach of any other lease condition.

Section 512(c) puts the burden of proof for damage on the landlord. Keep move-in and move-out photos, invoices and dated notes. The Act does not list normal wear and tear by name. Deduct only for damage you can show the tenant caused.

## Does a Pennsylvania Landlord Have to Give an Itemized List of Deductions?

Yes. The Pennsylvania landlord must send a written list of any damages within 30 days (Section 512(a)). The list is not a formality. Under Section 512(b), a landlord who does not send it in time forfeits all rights to withhold any part of the deposit. That landlord also loses the right to sue the tenant for damage to the unit.

Use this checklist at move-out:

- [ ] Note the lease end date or surrender date. Add 30 days.
- [ ] Get the tenant's new address in writing.
- [ ] Do a walk-through with photos.
- [ ] List each damage item with its cost.
- [ ] Add unpaid rent, if any, as a separate line.
- [ ] Add the escrow interest that the tenant has not yet received.
- [ ] Send the list and the balance together. Keep proof of mailing.

## Must a Pennsylvania Landlord Pay Interest or Keep the Deposit in a Separate Account?

Yes, for deposits over $100. Section 511.2(a) says these funds go into an escrow account at a regulated bank. The listed regulators include the Federal Reserve Board and the Pennsylvania Department of Banking. The landlord must tell each tenant in writing the bank's name and address and the amount on deposit.

Interest starts after the second anniversary of the deposit (Section 511.2(c)). In an interest-bearing account, the landlord may keep 1% a year as a fee for costs. The rest of the interest goes to the tenant each year on the lease anniversary (Section 511.2(b)).

A worked example for a $2,000 deposit in an account that pays 3% a year, in year 3:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Interest for the year (3% of $2,000) | $60 |
| Landlord's fee (1% of $2,000) | $20 |
| Paid to the tenant on the anniversary | $40 |

Section 511.3 gives one other path. Instead of escrow, the landlord may guarantee the return of the deposit with a bond from a bonding company licensed in Pennsylvania.

## What Is the Penalty for a Pennsylvania Landlord Who Keeps a Deposit Wrongly?

The penalty is double the amount wrongly kept. Section 512(c) covers a landlord who does not pay back the difference within 30 days. That landlord owes double the amount by which the deposit and interest exceed the actual damage.

A worked example:

1. **Deposit and interest held.** $2,000.
2. **Actual damage the landlord can prove.** $500.
3. **Amount owed to the tenant.** $1,500.
4. **Landlord returns nothing.** The tenant can claim double: 2 × $1,500 = $3,000.

A lease clause cannot change this. Section 512(d) makes any tenant waiver of Section 512 void.

## Do Pennsylvania Cities Add Their Own Deposit Rules?

The Act sets the statewide floor. Some cities add rental license rules or tenant protections on top of it. Philadelphia's Fair Housing Commission enforces local tenant rules under Philadelphia Code Chapter 9-804. Check your city's code before you write a lease.

## Does the PHA cover damages in a Section 8 unit?

The PHA does not cover tenant damage under the federal voucher rules. 24 CFR 982.307(a)(1) says the PHA "has no liability or responsibility to the owner or other persons for the family's behavior or suitability for tenancy." The owner screens the tenant and carries the risk, as with any lease.

The federal rules make the owner the collector. 24 CFR 982.452(b)(5) lists the owner's duty to collect from the family "any charges for unit damage by the family."

Who pays, in order:

| Step | Who pays | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Security deposit | 24 CFR 982.313(c) |
| 2 | The tenant, for the balance | 24 CFR 982.313(e) |
| 3 | A local PHA damage fund, if one exists | Local PHA policy |
| 4 | Your own insurance or reserves | Your policy |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Can I use the security deposit for Section 8 tenant damage?

You can use the security deposit for damage, the same as with any tenant. 24 CFR 982.313(c) says the owner, subject to state or local law, may use the deposit and any interest for unpaid tenant rent, unit damage, or other amounts owed under the lease.

Two duties come with it:

1. **Itemize.** Give the tenant a written list of each charge and its amount (982.313(d)).
2. **Refund the rest.** Return the unused balance promptly. Your state sets the exact deadline.

A worked example (made-up numbers):

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Security deposit held | $1,200 |
| Damage: broken door, 2 damaged walls | $1,850 |
| Unpaid tenant share, last month | $300 |
| Total owed | $2,150 |
| Deposit applied | −$1,200 |
| Balance to claim from the tenant | $950 |

You may not charge the deposit for normal wear and tear. You also may not charge the tenant for a HAP the PHA did not pay: the family does not owe that part of the rent (24 CFR 982.451(b)(4)(iii)).

## What is a PHA damage mitigation fund?

A damage mitigation fund is a local PHA program that pays an owner part of the cost of tenant damage. It is not a federal right. Each PHA sets its own limits and rules, and many PHAs have no fund at all.

One example: in a post dated March 16, 2022, the Philadelphia Housing Authority described an Owner Assurance Fund. It reimbursed up to $2,500 for damage beyond normal wear and tear, after the security deposit held for damage. The post does not say if the fund is still open.

Questions to ask your PHA:

- [ ] Do you have a damage fund or owner assurance fund?
- [ ] What is the maximum payment per unit?
- [ ] Do you subtract the security deposit first?
- [ ] What proof do you need: photos, invoices, move-in checklist?
- [ ] What is the deadline to file a claim after move-out?
- [ ] Must I re-rent to a voucher holder to qualify?

## Can I take the Section 8 tenant to small claims court?

You can take a voucher tenant to small claims court for damage the deposit does not cover. 24 CFR 982.313(e) says that if the deposit is not enough, "the owner may seek to collect the balance from the tenant." Your state sets the court's dollar limit and filing steps.

Your case is stronger with:

1. **A signed move-in checklist** with dated photos.
2. **The PHA inspection report** from move-in, which shows the unit passed.
3. **Move-out photos** of each damaged item.
4. **Invoices or quotes** for each repair.
5. **The itemized deposit letter** you sent the tenant.

## What if tenant damage causes a failed inspection?

When the family causes a failed item, the PHA may move the duty to fix it to the family. Under 24 CFR 982.404(a)(4), for damage beyond ordinary use caused by a household member or guest, the PHA may waive the owner's duty to fix it. If the PHA waives it, the PHA may not withhold or abate your HAP for that item.

The family then has the same clocks as an owner (982.404(b)(2)):

- **Life-threatening item:** the family must act to fix it within 24 hours.
- **Other items:** within 30 calendar days, or a PHA-approved extension.

Tell the PHA in writing when you find tenant damage at an inspection. Send photos. Ask the PHA to record the item as family-caused.

## Does unpaid damage affect the tenant's voucher?

Serious damage can cost the family its voucher. Under 24 CFR 982.404(a)(4) and (b)(3), the PHA may end assistance for a family-caused breach of housing standards beyond ordinary use. 24 CFR 982.552(c)(1)(i) also lets the PHA end assistance when a family breaks its program obligations.

Eviction adds more weight. 24 CFR 982.310(d)(1)(ii) lists "destruction of property" and habits that damage the unit as examples of good cause to end the tenancy. If you evict the family for a serious lease violation, 24 CFR 982.552(b)(2) says the PHA must end its assistance.

Unpaid money matters too. Under 982.552(c)(1)(vi), the PHA may deny or end assistance if the family has not paid back a PHA for amounts the PHA paid an owner for damage. So a PHA that pays your damage claim can then collect from the family.

## How to lower your damage risk

- **Screen every applicant the same way.** The owner screens, not the PHA (24 CFR 982.307(a)(2)).
- **Document move-in.** A signed checklist and dated photos of every room.
- **Inspect on a schedule.** Follow state notice rules before you enter.
- **Price the deposit right.** Charge the same deposit you charge all tenants.

## When should I collect move-in funds?

Collect the security deposit and first month's rent after both sides sign the lease and before move-in day. Do not hand over keys until the money is in your account and cleared. Keys first, money later, is how most move-in losses start.

Use this order:

1. **Approve the applicant** in writing.
2. **Sign the lease.** It states the rent, the deposit, the due date and the move-in date.
3. **Send a move-in invoice.** List each charge and the date it is due.
4. **Collect the funds** by a traceable method.
5. **Confirm the funds cleared.**
6. **Do the move-in inspection** with the tenant, with dated photos.
7. **Hand over keys.**

Set the due date 3 to 5 business days before move-in. That gives a check time to clear and gives you time to react if it does not.

## Should I accept a personal check for move-in funds?

You can accept a personal check, but treat it as unpaid until it clears. The CFPB explains that a bank must make some deposits available fast, but that is not the same as a good check. The FTC says, "Even if you see the funds in your account, that doesn't mean it's a good check." The FTC also says fake checks "can take weeks to be discovered."

The CFPB lists when check funds must be available:

| Deposit | When funds must be available |
|---|---|
| Check of $225 or less, deposited in person | Next business day |
| Cashier's, certified or government check | Next business day |
| Local check over $225, up to $5,525 | First $225 next business day; rest within 2 business days |
| Amount over $5,525 | Generally within 7 business days |
| Account open less than 30 days, or fraud suspected | The bank may hold the funds longer |

Watch for an overpayment. A renter sends a check for more than the move-in total and asks you to wire back the extra. The FTC calls this a scam pattern. Do not accept a check for more than the amount due.

Some states limit what you can demand. California Civil Code 1947.3 says a landlord must allow at least one form of payment for rent and deposit that is neither cash nor electronic funds transfer. A landlord may demand cash only for up to 3 months after a check bounces or a tenant stops payment. So in California, you cannot require online payment alone. Check your state's rule.

## Which payment methods work best?

Online payments and certified funds are the safest choices, because each one leaves a record. Compare the options:

| Method | Traceable | Clears fast | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Online rent payment (ACH) | Yes | Ask your processor | Wait for the processor to show the payment as settled |
| Cashier's or certified check | Yes | Next business day | Fake cashier's checks exist; call the issuing bank |
| Money order | Yes | Ask your bank | Can be faked; verify with the issuer |
| Personal check | Yes | See the CFPB table above | Bounced or fake check |
| Cash | Only with a receipt | Now | No proof without a signed receipt |

If you take cash, give a signed, dated receipt. Note the amount, the unit, and what the money is for.

## How do I prorate the first month?

Prorate the first month when the tenant moves in on a day other than the 1st. Charge only for the days the tenant has the unit. Most landlords use the daily rate for that month.

Worked example:

- Monthly rent: $1,800.
- Move-in date: June 20. June has 30 days.
- Days the tenant has the unit in June: June 20 to June 30, so 11 days.
- Daily rent: $1,800 ÷ 30 = $60.
- Prorated rent for June: $60 × 11 = $660.

Some landlords then collect July's full $1,800 at move-in too, so the first payment covers more than 2 weeks. Here the move-in total would be $660 + $1,800 + the deposit. Put the method in the lease, so the tenant sees the math before signing.

Some landlords use a 365-day year instead: $1,800 × 12 ÷ 365 = $59.18 a day. Pick one method and use it for every tenant.

## Where do I hold the deposit?

Hold the deposit the way your state law says. Some states only cap the amount. Others also say where the money goes and what you must tell the tenant.

| State | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Florida | Hold the deposit in a separate Florida account, with or without interest, or post a surety bond. Do not mix it with other funds. Tell the tenant in writing within 30 days where it is held. | Statute 83.49(1), (2) |
| California | The deposit may not exceed 1 month's rent, in addition to the first month's rent, with some exceptions. Take photos of the unit at the start of tenancies that begin on or after July 1, 2025. | Civil Code 1950.5(c), (g) |

Other states set interest, account and receipt rules of their own. Read your state's deposit law before your first move-in.

Even where no account rule exists, keep the deposit in a separate account. A separate account makes the move-out math simple. It also shows a judge that you did not spend the tenant's money.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What paperwork should I give the tenant?

Give the tenant a receipt and a copy of the move-in record the day you collect. Keep a copy for yourself.

- A receipt for each payment: amount, date, method, and what it covers.
- The deposit amount and where you hold it, if your state requires that notice.
- The signed lease with all addenda.
- The move-in inspection form with photos.
- How to pay rent from month 2 on.

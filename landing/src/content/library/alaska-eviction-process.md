Alaska eviction law sits in three places. The Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (AS 34.03) sets the reasons and the notice periods. The forcible entry and detainer (FED) statutes (AS 09.45.060 to 09.45.160) set what a notice to quit must say. Alaska Civil Rule 85 sets the court timing. We read all three on 2026-10-20.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice for unpaid rent | 7 days to pay in full | AS 34.03.220(b) |
| Notice for most lease breaches | At least 10 days, with a right to fix | AS 34.03.220(a)(2) |
| Same breach again within 6 months | At least 5 days | AS 34.03.220(a)(2) |
| Deliberate damage over $400, or illegal activity | 24 hours to 5 days | AS 34.03.220(a)(1) |
| Utility cut off for the tenant's nonpayment | 5 days | AS 34.03.220(e) |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | 30 days before the rental due date | AS 34.03.290(b) |
| End a week-to-week tenancy | 14 days | AS 34.03.290(a) |
| Notice served by mail | Add 3 days | AS 09.45.090(c) |
| Eviction hearing | No more than 15 days after filing | Civil Rule 85(a)(2) |
| Summons | Served at least 2 days before the hearing | AS 09.45.120 |
| Lockout or utility shutoff | Not allowed | AS 34.03.280 |

## What notice must an Alaska landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

An Alaska landlord must give a written notice of nonpayment. AS 34.03.220(b) says the notice must state that the rent is unpaid and that the landlord will end the rental agreement if the tenant does not pay in full within 7 days.

If the tenant does not pay in full in 7 days, the tenancy ends. Under AS 09.45.100(b), this notice also counts as the notice to quit, so you do not need a second one.

Rules to know:

- Only one written notice is needed for any one default.
- You may accept a partial payment and extend the eviction date to match (AS 34.03.220(b)).
- Serve the notice in person, leave it at the unit if the tenant is away, or send it by registered or certified mail (AS 09.45.100(c)).
- If you mail it, add 3 days to the 7 (AS 09.45.090(c)).

Be careful with rent you accept later. AS 34.03.240 says rent accepted with knowledge of a default waives the right to end the lease for that breach, unless you both agree otherwise after the breach.

## Can an Alaska landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes. The notice period depends on the reason.

| Reason | Notice | Can the tenant fix it? | Statute |
|---|---|---|---|
| Material breach of the lease or tenant duties | At least 10 days | Yes | AS 34.03.220(a)(2) |
| Same breach again within 6 months | At least 5 days | No cure right stated | AS 34.03.220(a)(2) |
| Deliberate damage over $400 | 24 hours to 5 days | No | AS 34.03.220(a)(1) |
| Prostitution or other illegal activity | 24 hours to 5 days | No | AS 34.03.220(a)(1) |
| Utility cut off for tenant's nonpayment | 5 days | Yes, within 3 days | AS 34.03.220(e) |
| End of a month-to-month tenancy | 30 days before rental due date | Not a breach | AS 34.03.290(b) |

Every notice to quit must meet AS 09.45.105. It must state the breach or reason, the fix and the deadline if the breach can be fixed, and the date and time the tenancy ends. It must also tell the tenant to leave by that time and that the landlord may sue to recover possession.

No-cause ending works for periodic tenancies. Either side may end a month-to-month tenancy with written notice at least 30 days before the rental due date in the notice (AS 34.03.290(b)). A fixed-term lease ends on its date.

Watch for retaliation. AS 34.03.310 bars an eviction because a tenant complained about repairs, used rights under the act or joined a tenant union. Some reasons are exempt, such as unpaid rent.

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Alaska, and what does it cost?

An Alaska landlord files a forcible entry and detainer (FED) complaint in the Alaska trial courts. Civil Rule 85(b) says a case filed in superior court may be referred to the district court for trial when the amount is within district court limits.

Civil Rule 85(a)(1) says the complaint must describe the premises clearly. It must also carry the court's notice on when the clerk may dismiss the case. The Alaska Court System prints that notice on its FED complaint forms.

AS 09.45.110 lets you file on or after the date the tenant unlawfully holds possession. In a rent case, that is after the 7 days run out.

The filing fee is set by the court, not by these statutes. Ask the clerk of your local court for the current FED fee before you file.

## How long does an eviction take in Alaska?

An Alaska eviction can move fast because Civil Rule 85 sets tight court times. Here is our calculation for a rent case with a notice served by hand.

| Step | Minimum time | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Notice of nonpayment | 7 days | AS 34.03.220(b) |
| File the FED complaint | Day 8 or later | AS 09.45.110 |
| Serve the summons | At least 2 days before the hearing | AS 09.45.120, Civil Rule 85(a)(2) |
| Eviction hearing | No more than 15 days after filing | Civil Rule 85(a)(2) |
| Continuance | No more than 2 days, unless the tenant posts security for rent | AS 09.45.120 |

So if you file on day 8, the hearing should fall by about day 23 after the notice, unless the court sets a later date. Add 3 days if you mailed the notice. Removal after the hearing takes more time. Our total is a calculation from the rules, not an official estimate.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Alaska?

A peace officer, or a person the state appoints, serves the court's papers. Alaska Civil Rule 4(c)(1) says service of all process is made by a peace officer, by a person the Commissioner of Public Safety appoints, or by mail where a rule allows. Rule 4(c)(3) says an appointed person must get a peace officer's help if they meet physical resistance.

The landlord does not remove the tenant. If the tenant leaves property behind, AS 34.03.260 applies. You must give at least 15 days' notice before you sell or dispose of it, and you must store it with reasonable care.

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Alaska?

No. AS 34.03.280 says a landlord may not take possession by action or otherwise except as the act allows. The ban names the wilful cutoff of electricity, gas, water, sanitary or other essential services. The only exceptions are abandonment, surrender and energy conditions beyond the landlord's control.

The tenant has a strong remedy. Under AS 34.03.210, a tenant who is locked out or cut off may recover possession or end the lease. In either case the tenant may recover up to 1.5 times actual damages. AS 34.03.350 adds attorney fees for the prevailing party.

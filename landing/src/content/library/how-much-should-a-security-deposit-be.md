## Is one month's rent the norm?

One month's rent is a common deposit, and in some states it is also the legal maximum. Start with your state's law, then set an amount that covers your real risk.

The table compares four large states. Each row is from the statute or the state's official guide.

| State | Deposit cap | Other key rule | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | 1 month's rent, plus first month's rent (since July 1, 2024) | Small landlords may charge up to 2 months, but not to a service member | Civil Code 1950.5(c) |
| New York | 1 month's rent | Deposits are trust funds; return within 14 days for non-regulated units | NY Attorney General guide |
| Texas | No dollar cap in the refund sections we read | Refund within 30 days after surrender | Property Code 92.103 |
| Florida | No dollar cap in 83.49 | Hold in a separate account or post a surety bond | Statutes 83.49(1) |

Other states set their own caps and rules. Check yours before you set a number.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What does the California cap allow?

California Civil Code 1950.5(c)(1) says a landlord may not take security over "one month's rent, in addition to any rent for the first month." The cap applies to security collected on or after July 1, 2024.

There is one exception for small landlords. Under 1950.5(c)(5), the cap is two months if both of these are true:

1. **The landlord is a natural person** or an LLC whose members are all natural persons.
2. **The landlord owns no more than 2 residential rental properties** with no more than 4 units in total.

The exception does not apply if the applicant is a service member. You also may not refuse to rent to a service member because of the lower cap.

Worked example, California, rent $1,600:

| Landlord | Maximum deposit | Due at move-in, with first month |
|---|---|---|
| Company with 30 units | $1,600 | $3,200 |
| Individual with 1 duplex | $3,200 | $4,800 |
| Individual with 1 duplex, service member tenant | $1,600 | $3,200 |

## Can I charge more for higher risk?

You can charge more for higher risk only if your state cap leaves room, and only with a written, equal rule. Write the rule into your screening criteria. For example: "Applicants with a ResidentScore from 524 to 537 pay a deposit of 1.5 months' rent."

Two rules apply when you raise the deposit:

- **FCRA notice.** The FTC lists "requiring a larger deposit than might be required for another applicant" as an adverse action. If a screening report led to it, send an adverse action notice.
- **Service member rule in California.** Since April 1, 2025, Civil Code 1950.5(c)(4) covers a higher deposit for a service member based on credit or housing history. You must give a written statement of the amount and the reason. You must return the extra amount after no more than 6 months if the tenant is not behind on rent.

## Do pet deposits count toward the cap?

In capped states, pet deposits usually count toward the cap. California's law, 1950.5(b), defines "security" as "any payment, fee, deposit, or charge" taken at the start of the tenancy for damages and other listed uses. A pet deposit fits that definition.

So in California, a $1,600 deposit plus a $500 pet deposit on a $1,600 rent goes over the cap for most landlords. Combine them into one deposit of $1,600 or less.

An assistance animal is not a pet under fair housing law. HUD's rule, 24 CFR 100.204, gives the example of a blind applicant with a seeing eye dog in a no-pets building. Refusing that applicant is a violation. Ask a local attorney before you apply any pet charge to an assistance animal.

## Can I take last month's rent as well?

It depends on the state. In capped states, last month's rent often counts toward the cap.

- **New York:** The Attorney General's guide says the one-month limit "means that a landlord cannot ask for last month's rent and a security deposit."
- **California:** 1950.5(b) counts any advance payment of rent as security, except the first month. One exception in 1950.5(c)(2) allows advance payment of at least 6 months' rent on a lease of 6 months or longer.

There is also a tax point. IRS Publication 527 says that if an amount called a security deposit is to be used as final rent, it is advance rent. You include it in income in the year you receive it. A true deposit that you plan to return is not income when you receive it.

This is general tax information. Ask a tax professional about your case.

## What is a deposit alternative?

A deposit alternative replaces some or all of the cash deposit with another product. Common types are a surety bond or deposit insurance that the tenant buys, and a monthly fee in place of a deposit. The tenant usually pays a smaller, non-refundable amount. The landlord claims against the product for damage.

Before you offer one, check three points:

- [ ] Does your state allow it, and does the fee count toward the deposit cap?
- [ ] Can the tenant still choose a normal cash deposit?
- [ ] Who pays if a claim is denied, and can the provider collect from the tenant later?

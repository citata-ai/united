## Does autopay raise on-time payment rates?

Autopay removes the most common cause of late rent: a tenant who forgets. When the payment runs by itself, the tenant does not need to remember a date or log in.

Autopay does not fix a tenant who has no money in the account. A debit on an empty account fails, and you still have late rent. So treat autopay as a tool for timing, not a guarantee.

Measure it in your own portfolio. Compare the share of on-time payments for autopay tenants and for manual payers over 6 months. Your own numbers tell you more than any vendor claim.

## Can I require autopay?

Whether you can require autopay depends on your state. Some states limit how much a landlord can force electronic payment. Check before you write "autopay required" in a lease.

California is a clear example. Civil Code 1947.3 says a landlord must allow a tenant to pay rent and deposit by at least one form of payment that is neither cash nor electronic funds transfer. The statute's definition of electronic funds transfer includes ACH transfers and transfers authorized in advance to recur. So in California, autopay can be offered but not be the only choice.

California also says:

- A landlord may not charge a fee for paying by check.
- A tenant and landlord may agree to cash or electronic payment, as long as another form is also allowed.
- A waiver of these rules in a lease is void.

Other states may have no such rule, or different ones. Read your state's landlord-tenant code and any city rules.

Federal rules also apply. Under Regulation E (12 CFR 1005.10(b)), a preauthorized transfer from a consumer's account needs a written or similarly authenticated authorization signed by the consumer. The person who gets the authorization must give the consumer a copy.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I get more tenants to use autopay?

Get more tenants on autopay by making it the easy default, not a demand. Follow these steps:

1. **Ask at lease signing.** Set it up while the tenant is with you. The first month is the best time.
2. **Show the steps.** A one-page guide with screenshots of your rent portal cuts setup time.
3. **Explain control.** Tell the tenant how to change the date or stop the payment. People join more readily when they know they can leave.
4. **Match the pay cycle.** Ask when the tenant gets paid. Pick a debit date after payday.
5. **Remove fees where you can.** If your platform charges tenants for card payments, steer autopay to free bank transfers.
6. **Remind once.** Send a short note to manual payers at renewal. Do not nag.

Keep a non-electronic option where your state requires it, such as a check by mail.

## When should autopay run?

Run autopay so the money settles by the due date. Most landlords run it 1 to 2 business days before the due date.

Nacha, the body that governs the ACH network, says ACH debits settle either the same day or the next banking day. It estimates that 80% of all ACH payments settle in one banking day or less.

| Due date | Debit runs | Settles | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st (a Monday) | Friday before | Friday or Monday | Weekend gap; run on Thursday for margin |
| 1st (a Wednesday) | Monday or Tuesday | By Wednesday | Normal week |
| 5th (after a payday on the 3rd) | 3rd or 4th | By the 5th | Fits a tenant paid early in the month |

Your rent platform may add its own processing days. Read its timing page and test it with your own account first.

## What if the autopay fails?

If autopay fails, the bank returns the debit and the rent counts as unpaid. Act fast and follow the same steps every time.

1. **Tell the tenant the same day.** Say the payment was returned and give the amount.
2. **Ask for payment** by a method that settles fast.
3. **Apply your lease terms** for late fees and returned payment fees, within state limits.
4. **Note the event** in the tenant file.
5. **After a repeat failure,** review whether to require a different method, where your state allows it.

California Civil Code 1947.3 lets a landlord demand cash for up to 3 months after a tenant pays with a check on insufficient funds or stops payment on a check. The landlord must give written notice with a copy of the dishonored instrument. That rule names checks. Ask a local attorney how it applies to a returned electronic payment in your state.

**Stop payments:** under Regulation E (12 CFR 1005.10(c)), a tenant can stop a preauthorized transfer by telling their bank at least 3 business days before the scheduled date. If a tenant stops autopay, the rent is still due. Ask for another payment method.

## What should the autopay authorization say?

The autopay authorization should be clear enough that the tenant knows what will leave their account and when. Most rent platforms create the form for you. Check that it covers:

- [ ] The amount, or how the amount is set if it can vary.
- [ ] The debit date each month.
- [ ] The bank account to debit.
- [ ] How to change or cancel the authorization.
- [ ] The tenant's signature or electronic consent.

Regulation E (12 CFR 1005.10(d)) also covers debits that vary in amount. The payee or bank must send written notice of the amount and date at least 10 days before the transfer. The consumer may instead choose notice only when a debit falls outside an agreed range. If rent plus fees can change month to month, ask your platform how it handles this notice.

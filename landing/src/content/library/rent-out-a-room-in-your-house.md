## Does Fair Housing apply to a shared home?

Fair Housing coverage needs a review of the actual home and arrangement. Do not assume that living beside the tenant removes every rule. Federal exemptions, advertising rules, and state or local protections can differ.

The federal statute at 42 USC 3603 includes a limited exemption for some owner-occupied buildings with no more than 4 families. The exemption preserves the advertising prohibition in section 3604(c). A shared-room arrangement also needs careful local legal review.

Ask an attorney which rules apply before you advertise or set screening criteria. Describe the space, common areas, occupancy, and any broker involvement. Do not ask for an exemption based only on an informal label such as lodger.

Use neutral property facts in the listing. Describe the room, rent, utilities, shared facilities, access, and agreed household rules. Avoid language that states a preference based on a protected class.

Keep the same written process for inquiries and applications. Record the criteria and the facts used in the decision. If you buy screening reports, have counsel check the related notices and privacy duties. This guide does not supply a legal screening policy for every shared home.

## How is room rent taxed?

Room rent needs its own records even though you also live in the home. IRS Publication 527 explains how to divide costs between rental and personal use. Keep the rental share separate from the household total.

The IRS allows a reasonable allocation method for shared expenses. Give your tax preparer the room measurements, shared-space plan, rental dates, and actual bills. Ask which method fits each type of cost rather than applying one percentage to every payment.

Direct costs for the rental room and shared costs may need different treatment. Save detailed invoices. Flag work that changes the property so your preparer can distinguish a repair from an improvement.

Keep rent receipts, deposits, refunds, and personal reimbursements in separate ledger categories. Explain what each payment covers. Do not call rent a gift because the renter knows you.

Use an assumed allocation example only as a math check. If a supported allocation were 20% and a shared eligible bill were $500, the rental share would be $100. Your actual allocation needs facts and tax review. The example does not establish which costs qualify or how to treat shared spaces.

## What should a room rental agreement say?

A room agreement should define the rented space, shared access, payments, and each person's duties. Have local counsel review it before use. Clear terms help both people understand what daily life will involve.

Use this drafting checklist:

| Topic | Detail to settle |
| --- | --- |
| Space | Private room and permitted common areas |
| Payments | Rent, due date, utilities, and payment route |
| Deposit | Amount, handling, and return process under local law |
| Access | Keys, locks, privacy, and repair arrangements |
| House use | Parking, laundry, storage, and quiet rules |
| Service | Repairs, cleaning, and emergency contact |
| End of agreement | Lawful notice and move-out process |

Avoid broad terms that let either person change the agreement without a clear process. Explain how you will record an agreed change. Keep a signed copy accessible to both parties.

Define household rules as conduct rules. For example, describe how shared storage works rather than guessing which type of person will be tidy. A written agreement should make expectations clear without replacing legal duties or creating a right the law does not allow.

## Does your insurance cover a lodger?

Only your insurer can confirm whether the policy covers your actual room rental use. Tell the insurer before the renter moves in. Describe whether the stay is long term or a series of short bookings.

NAIC warns that home-sharing use can affect coverage under a homeowners policy. Its guidance focuses on short-term rentals, so do not treat it as a ruling for every long-term room arrangement. Ask the insurer to review your exact facts.

Request written answers about property damage, liability, shared areas, and lost rent after a covered event. Ask whether you need an endorsement or a different policy. Keep the final documents with the agreement.

If you require tenant insurance, have counsel review the clause and ask the agent what that insurance can cover. Do not assume the renter's policy replaces your own liability coverage or pays for your building.

Also tell the insurer about material changes, such as added locks, a separate entrance, or more rental rooms. A room rental can evolve beyond the use originally described. Review the policy when the arrangement changes rather than waiting for a claim to reveal the difference.

## How do you decide whether sharing your home fits?

Compare the expected net cash with the loss of privacy and the work you accept. Include utilities, cleaning, extra wear, insurance changes, and any paid help. Keep a reserve for gaps between renters.

Walk through an ordinary week in the shared home. Decide how repairs, guests, packages, food storage, and access will work. Resolve unclear expectations before either person relies on them.

If you want help with leasing or records, ask a local manager whether it serves room rentals. The [management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) can support a cost review. Get a quote for the actual service, since a whole-house management offer may not fit.

Your next step is to check local permission and insurance, then write the agreement and budget. Advertise only after the practical and legal questions have clear answers. Treat the renter as a person with privacy and service needs, even when the room is inside your own home.

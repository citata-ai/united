## How do I calculate prorated rent?

Prorated rent is the daily rate times the number of days the tenant has the unit. You use it when a tenant moves in or out on a day other than the rent due date.

The formula has two steps:

1. **Find the daily rate.** Divide the rent by a number of days. The method decides which number.
2. **Multiply by days of use.** Count from the move-in date to the last day of the period, including both days.

State law can set a default. Florida Statutes 83.46(1) says that "unless otherwise agreed," periodic rent is payable at the start of each period and "rent is uniformly apportionable from day to day." California Civil Code 1947 has a different default: with no contract to the contrary, rent is due at the end of each period. A lease can override these defaults, so follow the lease.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Should the daily rate use days in the month or 365 days?

Use the method your lease names. If the lease is silent, pick one, write it into the next lease, and use it for everyone. The three common methods are:

| Method | Daily rate formula | $1,500 rent, October (31 days) | $1,500 rent, February (28 days) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days in the month | Rent ÷ days in that month | $48.39 | $53.57 |
| 30-day month | Rent ÷ 30 | $50.00 | $50.00 |
| 365-day year | Rent × 12 ÷ 365 | $49.32 | $49.32 |

Each method has a trade-off:

- **Days in the month** is easy to explain. A full month always equals one month's rent. The daily rate changes each month.
- **30-day month** is simple math. It charges more per day in 31-day months than the days-in-month method.
- **365-day year** keeps one daily rate all year. A short month like February costs less in total than one month's rent.

## Which method does the lease use?

Look for a proration clause in the rent section. Good lease language names three things:

- [ ] The method: days in the month, 30 days or 365 days.
- [ ] Whether the move-in and move-out days count.
- [ ] When the prorated amount is due: at signing, at move-in, or with the next full month.

Sample clause (adapt it to your lease and local law):

```text
Proration. If the tenancy begins or ends on a day other than the
first day of the month, rent for that month is the monthly rent
divided by the number of days in that month, times the number of
days the Tenant has possession, counting the first and last days.
Prorated rent for the first month is due at move-in.
```

## Worked example: move-in on October 18

The tenant moves in on October 18. Rent is $1,500 and is due on the 1st. October has 31 days.

Days of use: October 18 through 31, counting both days, is 14 days.

| Method | Daily rate | × 14 days | Prorated rent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days in the month | $1,500 ÷ 31 = $48.3871 | × 14 | $677.42 |
| 30-day month | $1,500 ÷ 30 = $50.00 | × 14 | $700.00 |
| 365-day year | $1,500 × 12 ÷ 365 = $49.3151 | × 14 | $690.41 |

The gap between the highest and lowest result is $22.58. That is small, but it is the kind of number that starts a dispute. Round only at the last step.

A February example shows a bigger gap. Move-in February 18 in a 28-day February gives 11 days. Days in the month: $1,500 ÷ 28 × 11 = $589.29. 365-day year: $49.3151 × 11 = $542.47. The difference is $46.82.

## Do I prorate the first or the second month?

Both ways are common. The choice changes cash at move-in, not the total.

| Option | Move-in on October 25 | Due at move-in | Next payment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prorate the first month | 7 days at $48.3871 | $338.71 | $1,500 on November 1 |
| Prorate plus next full month | 7 days plus November | $1,838.71 | $1,500 on December 1 |

The second option suits a move-in late in the month. It saves you a small payment a week later. Tell the applicant the move-in total in writing before they sign.

The same math works at move-out. If a lease ends on the 10th, rent for that month is 10 days at the daily rate, if the lease allows a mid-month end. Check your state's notice rules for month-to-month tenancies first.

## How does prorated rent affect taxes and deposits?

Prorated rent is still rent. IRS Publication 527 says you include advance rent in income in the year you receive it, whatever period it covers. If you collect December's full rent and January's prorated rent in December, both count for that year.

Keep proration separate from the deposit. Do not net a prorated amount against the security deposit at move-in. Show each on its own line in the move-in receipt.

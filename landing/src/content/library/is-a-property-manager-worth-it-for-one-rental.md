## What is the fee on one house in dollars?

On one house renting at $2,000, a full-service manager costs about $2,250 to $3,600 a year at 2026 average fees. The high end is a year with a new tenant.

The averages come from iPropertyManagement's survey of 80 metros, updated 2026-09-23:

| Fee | 2026 average | On a $2,000 house |
|-----|--------------|-------------------|
| Monthly fee | 8.49% of rent | $170/month, $2,038/year |
| Flat-fee option | $101.04/month | $1,212/year |
| Leasing fee with management | 70.6% of 1 month | $1,412 |
| Setup | $185.24 | $185 once |
| Renewal | $211.92 | $212 |

Two full years for one house:

| Year | What happens | Cost |
|------|--------------|------|
| Year 1 | Setup and new tenant | $3,635 |
| Year 2 | Same tenant renews | $2,250 |
| 2-year total | | $5,885 |
| As a share of 2 years of rent ($48,000) | | 12.3% |

A one-door owner can face a minimum fee. Home365's 2026 guide notes that managers in lower-rent markets often charge a higher percentage or set a monthly minimum. Ask each manager for theirs.

Management fees are a deductible rental expense. IRS Publication 527 lists management fees among the common rental expenses. The after-tax cost is lower than the sticker cost.

## How much time does one rental take?

One rental takes little time in a quiet year and a lot of time in a turnover year. No official survey measures owner hours well, so track your own.

A worksheet you can fill in. The hours are made-up examples for one house.

| Task | Quiet year (hours) | Turnover year (hours) |
|------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| Rent collection and books | 12 | 12 |
| Tenant calls and repairs | 15 | 15 |
| Renewal | 2 | 0 |
| Move-out inspection and deposit | 0 | 5 |
| Make-ready: paint, clean, vendors | 0 | 15 |
| Listing, photos, inquiries | 0 | 8 |
| Showings | 0 | 10 |
| Screening and lease | 0 | 6 |
| Taxes and records | 6 | 6 |
| Total | 35 | 77 |

Break-even hourly rate = management cost / hours you would spend.

- Quiet year: $2,250 / 35 hours = $64 per hour.
- Turnover year: $3,635 / 77 hours = $47 per hour.

If your time is worth more than these numbers, and the manager does the work as well as you, the manager is worth it on time alone.

## Which owners of one rental benefit most?

Owners of one rental benefit most from a manager when distance, time or skill is the limit. Money alone rarely decides it.

| Owner | Manager worth it? | Why |
|-------|-------------------|-----|
| Moved more than 1 hour away | Usually yes | Repairs and showings need someone local |
| Busy job, no weekday time | Often yes | Showings and vendor visits happen on weekdays |
| New to landlord law | Often yes | Deposit, notice and fair housing rules differ by state |
| Lives nearby, handy, has time | Often no | Self-management saves the fee |
| Rents to a long-term, steady tenant | Maybe | Leasing-only or a low flat fee may fit |
| Plans to sell within a year | Maybe | A short agreement with a low exit fee |

The fee also buys things you cannot do in hours. A manager has a vendor list, a screening process and a lease that fits local law. If one bad tenant would wipe out a year of your cash flow, that matters.

## Can a manager pay for itself?

A manager can pay for part of the fee in some cases. Three places to look:

1. **Faster leasing.** At $2,000 rent, each week of vacancy costs about $460. The US rental vacancy rate was 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026 (Census Bureau).
2. **Rent at market.** $50 more per month is $600 a year.
3. **Lower turnover.** A tenant who stays saves a leasing fee, make-ready costs and a vacant month.

Do not count on these. Ask each manager for their average days to lease and renewal rate for homes like yours, in writing.

## Can I hire a manager for leasing only?

Yes. Many managers sell leasing-only service, also called tenant placement. They market the home, screen applicants and sign the lease. Then you manage the tenant.

iPropertyManagement found an average fee of 81.8% of one month's rent for placement-only service, with a range of 8% to 150%. On a $2,000 house, that is about $1,636 per new tenant.

Leasing-only fits an owner who:

- Lives close enough to handle repairs.
- Finds showings and screening the hardest part.
- Wants a lease written for local law.

Ask what the fee includes: photos, showings, screening reports and the lease. Ask if there is a free re-lease if the tenant leaves early.

## A one-night decision test

1. **Price it.** Put your rent in our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).
2. **Count your hours** with the worksheet above.
3. **Divide.** Fee / hours = your break-even rate.
4. **Add the distance test.** More than 1 hour away? Lean toward a manager.
5. **Get 3 quotes.** See [property management](/property-management) for how owners compare managers.

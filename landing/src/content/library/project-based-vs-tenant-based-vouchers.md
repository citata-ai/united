## What is the difference between project-based and tenant-based vouchers?

Tenant-based vouchers follow the family, and project-based vouchers stay with the building. That is the core rule in 24 CFR 982.1(b). With tenant-based help, the family selects the unit. The family may rent anywhere in the area of a housing authority (PHA) that runs a voucher program.

The tenant-based HAP contract (the Housing Assistance Payments contract between you and the PHA) covers one unit and one family. If the family moves out, the contract ends. The family can take the voucher to a new unit.

Project-based vouchers (PBV) work in a different way. 24 CFR 983.5 says the assistance is "attached to the structure". The PHA signs a HAP contract with you for named units. The subsidy stays with those units while the contract runs.

Here are the two side by side, from parts 982 and 983 of the federal rules (eCFR, read 2026-10-19).

| Point | Tenant-based voucher (part 982) | Project-based voucher (part 983) |
|---|---|---|
| Who picks the unit | The family | The PHA selects your project |
| What the HAP contract covers | 1 unit, 1 family | Named contract units |
| Contract term | Same as the lease term | 1 to 20 years per unit, extensions up to 40 years remaining |
| Initial lease | At least 1 year, as a rule | Lease with each family from the PBV waiting list |
| Who finds the tenant | You market the unit | You lease from the PHA's PBV waiting list |
| When the family moves | HAP contract ends | Unit stays under contract, you fill it again |
| Rent cap | Reasonable rent | Lowest of 110% FMR minus utilities, reasonable rent, your ask |
| Vacancy pay | Not in the base rule | Move-out month; up to 2 more months if the PHA allows |

## How does a PBV contract attach to units?

A PBV HAP contract attaches to units because it names them. The PHA and the owner sign it for units in existing housing, or in new or rehabbed housing (24 CFR 983.5). For new construction, the owner and PHA can first sign an Agreement. The PHA then signs the HAP contract when the work is done.

The rule also limits how many units in one project can get PBV. Under 24 CFR 983.54, the cap is the greater of 25 units or 25% of the units in the project. In an area where vouchers are "difficult to use", the cap goes up to the greater of 25 units or 40%. Units for elderly families and some supportive-service units do not count toward the cap.

Each PHA also has an agency cap. 24 CFR 983.6 lets a PHA project-base up to 20% of its authorized vouchers, plus 10% more for some groups such as homeless families and veterans.

## How long is a PBV contract?

A PBV contract lasts 1 to 20 years for each contract unit (24 CFR 983.205). You and the PHA may agree to extend it before it ends. Each extension can be up to 20 years. The total remaining term, with extensions, may never be more than 40 years.

A tenant-based HAP contract is much shorter. 24 CFR 982.309 says it starts on the first day of the lease and ends on the last day. The first lease must be at least 1 year, unless the PHA approves a shorter term. You may not raise the rent during that first term.

Watch for: a long PBV term gives you steady income, but it also ties the units to program rules for years. Read the termination and notice terms in 24 CFR 983.206 before you sign.

## Can a PBV tenant move with a voucher?

Yes, a PBV family can move with a voucher after 1 year of PBV help. Under 24 CFR 983.261, the family may end the lease at any time after 1 year. The family gives you advance written notice, with a copy to the PHA. The PHA must then offer the family continued tenant-based help, such as a regular voucher.

If no voucher is free at once, the PHA puts the family first in line for the next one. If the family ends the lease before 1 year, it gives up that right.

For you, the unit stays under the HAP contract after the family leaves. 24 CFR 983.352 lets you keep the HAP for the move-out month, unless the PHA finds the vacancy is your fault. The PHA may also choose to pay you for up to 2 more vacant months. The payment cannot be more than the rent to owner minus what you got from the tenant, including the deposit.

## How does an owner apply for PBVs?

An owner applies for PBVs when the PHA asks for proposals. You cannot just sign up. Each PHA decides if it will run a PBV program at all (24 CFR 983.5(c)). If it does, its Administrative Plan must say how it selects proposals.

24 CFR 983.51 gives the PHA 2 main ways to select:

1. **Request for proposals (RFP).** The PHA runs a competition. It may not limit the RFP to one site.
2. **Earlier competition.** The PHA may pick a project that won another government housing competition within 3 years. That competition must not have counted on PBV.

The PHA also checks that the units are eligible, that the project is within the unit cap, and that the site meets site rules.

What to do:

1. **Find your PHA's Administrative Plan.** Search for the PBV chapter.
2. **Ask the PHA** if it has an open or planned RFP.
3. **Check the rent cap.** Use the 110% FMR limit and your utility allowance to see if the numbers work. Our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) shows the local fair market rents.
4. **Read 24 CFR part 983** before you write a proposal. Note that HUD published changes at 90 FR 56688 on 2025-12-08. Check what applies on your date.

## Which suits a small owner?

For most small owners, tenant-based vouchers are the easier start. You list the unit, a voucher holder applies, and the PHA approves the tenancy. No competition, no long contract. If the tenant leaves, you are free to rent to anyone.

PBV can suit a small owner in some cases. The rules allow PBV in a single-family building (24 CFR 983.5). One proposal can cover several single-family buildings, if all are the same housing type (24 CFR 983.51). PBV fits best when:

- you build or rehab units and want income certainty for years;
- your PHA runs a PBV program and has an open RFP;
- you are ready to lease only from the PHA's waiting list and keep written tenant selection rules (24 CFR 983.253).

A quick self-check:

- [ ] Do I want a 1 to 20 year commitment for these units?
- [ ] Does my local PHA run PBV and select projects now?
- [ ] Does the rent cap (lowest of 110% FMR minus utilities, reasonable rent, my ask) cover my costs?
- [ ] Can I fill the units from a waiting list I do not control?

If most answers are "no", start with tenant-based vouchers. You can estimate a voucher rent with our [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator).

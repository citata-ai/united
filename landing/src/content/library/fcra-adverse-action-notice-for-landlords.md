## What is adverse action under the FCRA?

Adverse action is any decision that is unfavorable to the applicant or tenant. The FTC's guide for landlords defines it as "any action by a landlord that is unfavorable to the interests of a rental applicant or tenant."

The Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) rule is in 15 U.S.C. 1681m(a). It applies when you take adverse action "based in whole or in part on any information contained in a consumer report". A tenant screening report with credit, eviction or criminal data is a consumer report.

The FTC lists these examples:

| Decision | Adverse action? |
|---|---|
| Deny the application | Yes |
| Require a co-signer on the lease | Yes |
| Require a larger deposit than for another applicant | Yes |
| Charge a higher rent than for another applicant | Yes |
| Approve on the same terms as everyone else | No |
| Deny only because the applicant's income, from a pay stub, is below your rule | Not under 1681m(a), if no consumer report played a part |

The words "in part" matter. If the report was one of three reasons, you still need the notice.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What must the adverse action notice include?

The notice must include four items under 15 U.S.C. 1681m(a). Use this checklist for each letter:

- [ ] **Notice of the action.** Say what you decided: denied, co-signer required, deposit raised.
- [ ] **Credit score, if used.** Give the numerical score and the key factors that the law lists by cross-reference to 1681g(f). This part must be written or electronic.
- [ ] **The agency's details.** The name, address and phone number of the consumer reporting agency. Include its toll-free number if it is a nationwide agency.
- [ ] **The "not our decision" statement.** The agency did not make the decision and cannot give the specific reasons.
- [ ] **The applicant's rights.** The right to a free copy of the report from that agency within 60 days, and the right to dispute the accuracy or completeness of the report with the agency.

Some screening products generate the letter for you. Check that it lists the right agency. A report can include data from more than one company.

## What does a sample notice look like?

A short notice covers every required item. Adapt this text to your case and check state law.

```text
[Date]

Dear [Applicant name],

Thank you for applying for [address].
We have [denied your application / approved your application on the
condition that you provide a co-signer / approved your application with
a security deposit of $____].

This decision was based in whole or in part on information in a
consumer report from:
  [Agency name]
  [Agency address]
  [Agency phone, and toll-free number if a nationwide agency]

[If a credit score was used:] Your credit score: ___ (range ___ to ___).
Key factors that affected the score: ___. Date of score: ___.

[Agency name] did not make this decision and cannot tell you the
specific reasons for it.

You have the right to get a free copy of your report from [Agency name]
if you ask within 60 days of this notice. You also have the right to
dispute with [Agency name] the accuracy or completeness of any
information in the report.

[Your name, company, phone]
```

## Does a conditional approval need a notice?

Yes, a conditional approval needs a notice when the condition came from a consumer report. The FTC names "requiring a co-signer on the lease" and "requiring a larger deposit than might be required for another applicant" as adverse actions.

So the common "approve with conditions" path is not a way around the rule. Write the condition into the same letter: "We approved your application with a deposit of $2,000 instead of $1,500."

Also check state limits on the condition itself. A higher deposit must still fit your state's deposit cap.

## What are the risks if I skip the notice?

The risks are lawsuits and damages. 15 U.S.C. 1681n covers willful failures. It allows the consumer's actual damages or statutory damages of "not less than $100 and not more than $1,000". A court can add punitive damages, costs and reasonable attorney's fees. Negligent failures can bring actual damages and fees under 1681o.

The FTC enforces the FCRA, according to its landlord guide. State law can add rules on screening reports:

| State | Extra rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| California | If the applicant paid a screening fee, give a copy of the credit report within 7 days of receiving it | Civil Code 1950.6(f) |
| New York | Give the applicant a copy of the credit or background check and the invoice, or you may not charge for it | NY Attorney General guide |

## How do I keep records of adverse action notices?

Send the notice in writing or by email, and keep a copy in the applicant file. The FTC says written notices "allow you to provide proof of FCRA compliance."

Good practice for each file:

1. **Save the report** and the date you pulled it.
2. **Save the notice** with the date and method you sent it.
3. **Write the reason** in one line, for your own file.
4. **Dispose of reports safely** when you no longer need them. The FTC says you "must securely dispose of the report" so it "can't be read or reconstructed."

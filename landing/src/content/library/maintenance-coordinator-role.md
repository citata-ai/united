## Which Tasks Does the Maintenance Coordinator Own?

The maintenance coordinator should own the movement of a repair request through the approved process. Technical diagnosis, legal decisions and spending authority need clear limits.

Property Meld's coordinator metrics guide connects work-order volume, communication, scheduling and capacity. It notes that demand differs across locations and property classes. That supports a workload review rather than a single staffing ratio.

Write the role around handoffs. The coordinator gathers the report, checks urgency through an approved process and routes the request. They confirm authority, assign an approved vendor and keep the resident informed. They collect completion evidence and identify remaining work.

Name decisions the coordinator cannot make alone. These can include spending outside authority, legal notices and technical work beyond their training. Give them an escalation route and a backup person. Responsibility without authority or support creates a stalled request.

Keep after-hours work explicit. State who receives calls, who approves urgent dispatch and how the daytime team receives the record. Do not assume a salaried role means unlimited coverage. Review schedules and applicable employment duties with qualified advisers.

## How Many Doors Can a Coordinator Handle?

Estimate capacity from arriving work and the time required to handle it. Door count gives context, but it does not measure the actual coordination load.

Measure the tasks that take time. Include intake, vendor follow-up, owner approval, access changes, invoice questions and reopened jobs. Separate handling time from waiting for a part or approval.

Use a planning formula: expected request volume multiplied by handling time, plus fixed duties and backup needs. All figures below are invented assumptions. They demonstrate arithmetic, not a staffing benchmark.

| Example input | Assumption |
|---|---:|
| Requests during the planning week | 60 |
| Average handling time per request | 20 minutes |
| Direct handling time | 1,200 minutes, or 20 hours |
| Other duties and planned support | 10 hours |
| Total planned work | 30 hours |

The example leaves out unexpected events and absence. Add room for those before deciding more work fits. If handling time rises, the same door count can overload the role.

Use actual records to replace the assumptions. Review a range of normal and busy periods. Do not staff the role from the quietest week or the performance of someone who works through breaks.

## Which KPIs Should Measure the Role?

Use performance measures that show flow and quality together. A fast closure count can hide unresolved repairs.

A KPI is a key performance indicator. Define each measure before using it in a review. Record source fields, timing and exclusions so staff can reproduce the result.

Suggested measures include acknowledgment time, time to approved dispatch, open-request age and repeat visits for the same unresolved fault. Add resident feedback and completion evidence quality. These are proposed checks, not universal targets.

Separate waiting reasons. A request delayed by an owner decision needs a different response from a missed vendor follow-up. Keep the total resident wait visible, but do not attribute every hour to the coordinator's effort.

Property Meld describes coordinator-level work orders, chats, scheduling status and cycle times in its reporting. Check your own system before promising those reports. A field in one product may need manual records in another.

Review examples behind the chart. Read a delayed request, a reopened request and a well-handled complex job. This stops an average from hiding work that needs attention or deserves recognition.

## When Should You Add Another Coordinator?

Add capacity when verified demand exceeds a reasonable work plan after fixing clear process gaps. A growing backlog is a signal to investigate, not the only decision rule.

Look for sustained missed updates, overtime, unresolved scheduling and poor backup coverage. Ask which tasks create repeated work. A broken approval route or weak vendor list can make another person busy without fixing the cause.

Try a clear process change and measure its effect. For example, improve intake fields or give owners a consistent approval route. Do not change the measure at the same time and then claim the process worked.

Price the added role using actual employment and system costs. Compare that cost with service duties and expected demand. Avoid counting on a new hire to operate at full capacity before training.

Plan the division of work. Split by geography, trade, owner group or process step only when handoffs remain clear. Every request still needs a named lead. Shared queues can become unowned work when everyone assumes someone else replied.

## What Should the Coordinator's Operating Checklist Include?

Give the coordinator a practical work list and an escalation guide for busy days. Review the list with the person who covers their absence.

- Check urgent requests and unresolved risks first.
- Confirm each request has a lead and next action.
- Review owner approvals and vendor responses awaiting follow-up.
- Verify access plans before scheduled visits.
- Collect completion notes before closing work.
- Route invoice differences and repeat faults for review.
- Hand over unresolved urgent work before ending coverage.

Keep phone and portal requests in the same record. A conversation in a personal inbox can leave the backup person unaware. Make updates brief, factual and clear about the next step.

Review the checklist with vendors too. Ask what information is often missing at dispatch. Better intake can reduce repeat calls without requiring staff to rush the resident or skip important details.

For service-cost discussions, use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) with your own staffing model. Before adding doors, review whether the role can cover a busy week without missed work or unreasonable hours.

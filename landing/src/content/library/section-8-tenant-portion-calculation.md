## What is Total Tenant Payment (TTP)?

Total Tenant Payment (TTP) is the minimum amount a voucher family puts toward rent and utilities each month. 24 CFR 5.628 defines it as the highest of these amounts, rounded to the nearest dollar:

1. **30% of monthly adjusted income.** This is the usual winner.
2. **10% of monthly income.** Gross income, before deductions.
3. **A welfare rent.** Only where a public agency marks part of a welfare payment for housing costs.
4. **The minimum rent.** Set by the PHA, up to $50 for vouchers (24 CFR 5.630).

TTP is not always what the family pays you. The family's real share also depends on your rent and on who pays utilities.

## Why is the family share about 30% of income?

The family share is about 30% of income because 30% of monthly adjusted income is the first test in 24 CFR 5.628. For most families it is the highest of the four amounts. "Adjusted" income means annual income after the deductions in 24 CFR 5.611, so 30% of it is a bit less than 30% of gross pay.

The mandatory deductions in 24 CFR 5.611(a):

| Deduction | Base amount in the rule |
|---|---|
| Each dependent | $480 a year, adjusted by HUD each year for inflation |
| Elderly or disabled family | $525 a year, adjusted by HUD each year for inflation |
| Medical and disability care costs (elderly or disabled family) | The part above 10% of annual income |
| Child care needed to work or study | Reasonable cost |

The PHA verifies income, not you. You only see the result on the PHA's notice: the HAP amount and the tenant rent.

## How does the PHA calculate its payment?

The PHA pays the lower of two numbers, under 24 CFR 982.505(b):

- the payment standard minus the TTP, or
- the gross rent minus the TTP.

Gross rent is the rent to owner plus the utility allowance (24 CFR 982.4). The utility allowance is the PHA's estimate of the utilities the tenant pays. The payment standard is the PHA's cap, usually 90% to 110% of HUD's Fair Market Rent (24 CFR 982.503(c)).

Then the split is simple (24 CFR 982.515):

- **Family share** = gross rent − HAP.
- **Family rent to owner** = rent to owner − HAP.

The PHA may not pay any part of the family share (982.515(c)). You collect the family's part from the family.

## What happens when rent is above the payment standard?

When the gross rent is above the payment standard, the family pays the whole difference. The HAP stops at the payment standard minus TTP, so each extra dollar of rent is a dollar more for the family.

A limit protects new tenancies. 24 CFR 982.508 says that at first occupancy, when gross rent exceeds the payment standard, the family share may not exceed 40% of adjusted monthly income. If your rent would push the family past 40%, the PHA cannot approve the lease at that rent.

## What is a minimum rent?

A minimum rent is the floor on TTP. For the voucher program, 24 CFR 5.630(a)(2) lets the PHA set a minimum rent of up to $50 a month. A family with zero or very low income still pays at least that amount, unless it gets a hardship exemption.

The PHA must grant the exemption when a family cannot pay because of a financial hardship (5.630(b)). The rule lists examples: loss of a benefit, a threat of eviction for not paying, a drop in income from a job loss, or a death in the family.

## Worked example of the HAP split

Here is one tenancy, step by step. The family and rent are made-up numbers. The formulas are the federal ones.

**Inputs**

| Input | Amount |
|---|---|
| Annual income (gross) | $24,000 |
| Dependents | 1 child |
| Payment standard, 2 bedrooms | $1,600 |
| Rent to owner | $1,500 |
| Utility allowance (tenant pays electric and gas) | $150 |

**Step 1: TTP**

| Test | Math | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Adjusted income | $24,000 − $480 = $23,520 a year | $1,960 a month |
| 30% of adjusted monthly income | 0.30 × $1,960 | $588 |
| 10% of monthly income | 0.10 × $2,000 | $200 |
| Minimum rent | PHA sets, up to $50 | $50 |
| TTP (highest) | | $588 |

We used the $480 base deduction from the rule. HUD adjusts it each year, so the PHA's real number may be a little higher.

**Step 2: HAP and shares**

| Line | Math | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Gross rent | $1,500 + $150 | $1,650 |
| Payment standard − TTP | $1,600 − $588 | $1,012 |
| Gross rent − TTP | $1,650 − $588 | $1,062 |
| HAP (lower of the two) | | $1,012 |
| Family share | $1,650 − $1,012 | $638 |
| Family rent to owner | $1,500 − $1,012 | $488 |
| 40% cap check | $638 ÷ $1,960 | 32.6%, under 40% |

So each month the PHA sends you $1,012. The family sends you $488 and pays its own utility bills. The family share is $50 above its TTP, because the gross rent is $50 above the payment standard.

## What the tenant share means for you as the landlord

- **Collect two payments.** The HAP from the PHA and the tenant rent from the family.
- **Expect changes.** The PHA recalculates when income changes, so the split can move during the lease.
- **Price inside the cap.** A rent far above the payment standard can fail the 40% test at move-in.
- **Do not take extra.** The tenant may not pay more than rent to owner minus HAP (24 CFR 982.451(b)(4)).

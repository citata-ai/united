## Which insurance policies does a property management company need?

A property management company needs cover for 4 risks: injury and damage to others, mistakes in your professional work, theft and fraud, and data loss. Payroll and vehicles add 2 more. The table shows each policy, what it pays for, and Insureon's median monthly price for property managers.

| Policy | Pays for | Median per month (Insureon) | Who needs it |
|---|---|---|---|
| General liability | Third-party injury and property damage | $44 | Every company |
| Errors and omissions (E&O) | Claims that your work was negligent, late or not done | $83 | Every company; required for licensees in some states |
| Business owner's policy (BOP) | General liability plus commercial property in one policy | $212 | Companies with an office and equipment |
| Workers' compensation | Employee injury: medical bills and lost wages | $73 | Companies with employees, as state law requires |
| Commercial auto | Accidents in company vehicles | $167 | Companies that own vehicles |
| Cyber | Data breach and cyber attack costs | $58 | Companies that store tenant and owner data |
| Surety bond | Pays a client if you do not meet a contract or bond term | $21 | When a state, a client or a contract asks for it |
| Crime / fidelity | Theft and fraud by employees or outsiders | Not given by Insureon | Companies that hold owner and tenant money |

Insureon takes these numbers from the median cost of policies for property managers who apply for quotes through Insureon. Your price changes with limits, deductible, revenue, staff count, claims history and state.

## What does general liability cover?

General liability covers claims from third parties for bodily injury and property damage. Insureon gives two property manager examples: a client slips on a wet floor at an open house, or you drop and break a tenant's phone while the tenant shows you a problem. General liability also covers claims of advertising injury, such as defamation or copyright infringement.

Insureon's typical general liability policy for property managers costs $44 a month, or $528 a year. Its limits are $1 million per occurrence and $2 million aggregate. Insureon says 90% of its customers choose those limits.

The per-occurrence limit is the most the insurer pays for one claim. The aggregate limit is the most it pays for all claims in the policy period, usually one year.

## What does E&O cover?

Errors and omissions (E&O) insurance covers client claims that your professional work was not good enough, negligent, late or never done. Insureon gives two property manager examples: a tenant discrimination claim and a wrongful eviction claim. E&O pays for legal defense, settlements and other financial losses.

Insureon's typical E&O policy for property managers costs $83 a month, or $996 a year. It has $1 million per occurrence, $1 million aggregate and a $1,000 deductible.

E&O is where most property management claims go: a missed lease clause, a late deposit return, a repair that should have been done. General liability does not cover these. For a deeper look at E&O limits and prices, use your broker's quote, not an average.

## Do I need crime, fidelity or cyber cover?

You need crime or fidelity cover if you hold owner rents and tenant deposits, and cyber cover if you store tenant and owner data. Most property managers do both.

Insureon splits crime cover into two types:

- **First-party crime coverage** protects your own business from employee dishonesty, theft, fraud, forgery, and some crimes by outsiders, such as social engineering scams and funds transfer fraud.
- **Third-party crime coverage**, often called a fidelity bond, protects your clients from losses caused by theft or other dishonest acts by your employees while they work for you.

Insureon says the two are usually bought separately. First-party employee dishonesty cover can sometimes be added to a BOP as an endorsement. Insureon also notes that crime and cyber policies often cover different losses. A fake wire request can fall under crime cover, while a data breach falls under cyber cover.

Cyber insurance costs real estate businesses a median of $58 a month, or $692 a year, per Insureon. It can pay for breach investigation, cyber extortion, and the cost to follow your state's data breach laws. Insureon calls it most important for companies that store credit card numbers, Social Security numbers and email addresses. A tenant screening file has most of those.

## What do states require?

State rules differ. Two types of rules come up most: E&O for real estate licensees, and workers' comp for employers.

E&O, one state example: Colorado statute 12-10-204 says every licensee under that part, except an inactive broker and some attorney licensees, must keep errors and omissions insurance. The state Division of Real Estate offers a group policy, and a licensee may buy an independent policy that meets the division's minimum terms. Licensees file a certificate of coverage each year.

Workers' comp: Insureon says real estate businesses in California, Pennsylvania and New York must carry workers' comp with 1 or more employees. In Florida, the line is 4 or more employees. In Texas, coverage is optional.

Bonds: some states and some contracts ask for a surety bond. Ask your state real estate commission what it needs for a property management license.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What do owners ask me to carry?

Owners and HOAs often ask for proof of coverage before they sign. Large owners may ask you to list them as an additional insured on your general liability policy. Insureon notes that additional insured endorsements change your premium.

Checklist for an owner or HOA request:

- [ ] Which policies: general liability, E&O, crime or fidelity, auto
- [ ] Minimum limits per occurrence and aggregate
- [ ] Additional insured: who, and on which policy
- [ ] Certificate of insurance: who gets it, and how often
- [ ] Notice of cancellation: how many days

Keep a one-page insurance summary in your owner welcome packet. It answers the question before the owner asks.

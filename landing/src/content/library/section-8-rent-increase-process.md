## When in the lease term can rent go up?

Rent can go up only after the initial lease term ends. 24 CFR 982.309(a)(3) says: "During the initial term of the lease, the owner may not raise the rent to owner." The initial term must be at least one year, unless the PHA approves a shorter one. The HUD tenancy addendum repeats the rule in section 4(b).

After the first term, the lease decides how and when rent can change. The Santa Cruz County housing authority says a rent increase "can be requested at any time after the initial term of the lease." Tacoma Housing Authority says that after the initial term "you may change the rent and the terms of the lease."

Some changes need a new HAP contract, not just a rent notice. Under 24 CFR 982.308(g)(2), these include a change in who pays utilities or provides appliances, and a change to the lease term.

## How much notice must I give the tenant and the PHA?

You must give the PHA at least 60 days' notice. 24 CFR 982.308(g)(4) says the owner "must notify the PHA of any changes in the amount of the rent to owner at least sixty days before any such changes go into effect."

Notice to the tenant follows the lease and state law. PHAs often ask for both notices together.

| Housing authority | What it asks for | Timing rule |
|---|---|---|
| Federal rule (all PHAs) | Notice to the PHA | At least 60 days before the change |
| Tacoma Housing Authority (WA) | Written notice on its Landlord Rent Change Request form | Received at least 60 days before; January 1 increase needs notice by November 1 |
| Santa Cruz County (CA) | A 60-day notice to the tenant, with a copy to the PHA | Effective on the first of the month at least 60 days after notice; you may pick a date up to 90 days out |

State rent caps also apply. 24 CFR 982.509 says rent to owner "may be subject to rent control limits under State or local law." The Santa Cruz County housing authority says California's Tenant Protection Act (AB 1482) applies to most voucher tenancies. That law limits a 12-month increase to 5% plus the cost-of-living change, or 10%, whichever is lower. Santa Cruz will not approve a request that appears to break that cap.

## Does the PHA check rent reasonableness again?

Yes, every time. 24 CFR 982.507(a)(2) says the PHA "must redetermine the reasonable rent" before any increase in the rent to owner. The new rent may not be more than the rent for comparable unassisted units. The PHA looks at location, quality, size, unit type, age, amenities, services and the utilities you pay.

Two more limits apply:

- **Your own units.** The rent may not be more than you charge for comparable unassisted units in the same premises (tenancy addendum, section 4(c)).
- **Your own comps.** The Santa Cruz County housing authority lets owners appeal a rent decision by sending its comparable rents form.

Help the PHA say yes. Send 3 to 5 comparable unassisted rentals with your request: address, bedrooms, size, rent and what the rent includes.

## What if the new rent is over the payment standard?

The PHA can approve a rent above the payment standard, if the rent is reasonable. The family then pays more. The HAP is capped by the payment standard minus the family's total tenant payment (24 CFR 982.505).

The 40% cap does not stop a later increase. It applies only "at the time the PHA approves a tenancy for initial occupancy" (24 CFR 982.508). The Santa Cruz County housing authority says: "After the initial lease term, the tenant may pay more than 40% of income in rent."

Here is a worked example. The numbers are for illustration only.

| Line | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Rent to owner | $1,700 | $1,800 |
| Utility allowance | $150 | $150 |
| Gross rent | $1,850 | $1,950 |
| Payment standard | $1,900 | $1,900 |
| Total tenant payment | $550 | $550 |
| HAP: lower of (payment standard or gross rent) − TTP | $1,300 | $1,350 |
| Tenant pays owner | $400 | $450 |

The $100 increase costs the PHA $50 and the tenant $50, because the gross rent passed the payment standard. Our [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator) runs this split for your numbers.

If the PHA denies the increase, Tacoma Housing Authority describes three outcomes. You accept the lower amount it approves. Or you end the tenancy at the end of the term, under state and local law. Or the tenant leaves the program and pays 100% of the rent.

## Rent increase request template

Use your PHA's form if it has one. Tacoma and Santa Cruz both do. If not, send a letter with these items:

- [ ] Date of the letter.
- [ ] Owner name, phone and email, and the management company if any.
- [ ] Tenant name and unit address.
- [ ] Current rent to owner.
- [ ] Proposed new rent to owner.
- [ ] Effective date, at least 60 days after the PHA receives the notice.
- [ ] Statement that utility and appliance duties do not change.
- [ ] 3 to 5 unassisted comparable rents, with addresses and details.
- [ ] Copy of the notice you gave the tenant.
- [ ] Signature.

Sample wording: "I request an increase in the rent to owner for [address] from [current amount] to [new amount], effective [date]. The lease term and the utility and appliance responsibilities do not change. Comparable unassisted rents are attached. A copy of the tenant notice is attached."

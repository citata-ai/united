## How do Stessa, Baselane, REI Hub and Landlord Studio compare?

The tools differ most in how they organize the work around your rental books. Stessa lists bank feeds, rental reports and rent collection. Baselane combines bookkeeping with banking features. REI Hub lists rental categories, loan tracking and fixed assets. Landlord Studio combines accounting with tenant and property tasks.

Build your shortlist around your hardest recurring job. If you lose receipts, test receipt capture and attachment exports. If loans cause errors, test principal and interest treatment with your accountant. If you manage several entities, test the reports for each entity.

Avoid judging a tool from its dashboard alone. Open the transaction record behind the chart. Check whether a correction changes the right property without changing an unrelated report.

These are fit recommendations, not a claim that we tested every product. Use your own sample records before choosing a paid plan.

## Which make Schedule E reports?

All the reviewed vendors describe Schedule E reporting, but access depends on the plan. Stessa lists the report in Manage. Landlord Studio lists tax-ready reports in Pro. Baselane describes an accountant tax package, and REI Hub lists Schedule E reports.

A report label does not prove that every transaction is correct. Ask your accountant to review categories, property splits and transfers. A bank transfer should not become rent income simply because money arrived.

Test a refund, an owner contribution and a loan payment. Check whether the report handles each as intended. Keep supporting invoices attached or stored in a clear archive.

Ask how the software treats a property sold during the year. Do not assume a general rental report resolves every filing question. This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## Which connect to your bank?

Bank connections appear on the reviewed vendor pages, but coverage and tier differ. Stessa lists automatic bank feeds in Essentials. Baselane describes syncing external accounts. REI Hub places automatic imports among Professional features. Landlord Studio lists bank feeds on paid plans.

Test the actual bank and account type you use. Do not accept a bank logo as proof that your specific account connects. Ask how far back the first import reaches and how staff fill gaps.

Review an imported statement against the source statement. Look for duplicate entries after a reconnect. Check whether a pending card transaction appears again when it settles.

Decide who reviews imported categories. Automation can suggest a category, but your business still needs a consistent rule. Keep a short list of uncertain transactions for accountant review instead of guessing.

## Which are free?

Free subscriptions exist, but free use does not mean every service has no charge. Stessa Essentials and Baselane Core publish free base plans. REI Hub shows free bookkeeping with limits on automation. Landlord Studio Go limits units and features.

Start with the feature you cannot do without. A free plan may be enough for manual tracking, yet require an upgrade for the export your accountant needs. Check reports, document storage and access for another user.

Payment fees deserve a separate review. A tool can charge no subscription while renters still pay transaction charges. Keep the business bill and the renter experience visible in your comparison.

Do not move all records just to claim a free account. Import a small scrubbed sample, produce a report and export it again. The exit test matters even when the subscription costs nothing.

## When do you need a CPA tool instead?

You need stronger accountant involvement when your books become hard to review or your structure exceeds the tool's working model. This is a decision about the records, not a fixed unit threshold.

Ask your accountant whether the tool supports the required entity reports, assets, loans and corrections. If the accountant rebuilds the books elsewhere each period, examine why. Missing source records may be the cause, rather than the software.

For a third-party manager, client funds add needs beyond tracking an owner's income and expenses. Do not assume software for self-managing landlords fits a professional trust-accounting process.

Keep a division of work in writing. State who categorizes transactions, reviews exceptions and approves the period close. A capable tool still needs a clear bookkeeping process and an accountable reviewer.

## How should you test the shortlist?

Test a full path from source record to accountant export. Use the same sample in every product so the comparison stays fair.

- **Import:** connect a test account or import a scrubbed statement.
- **Tag:** assign each entry to the correct property and category.
- **Attach:** add a receipt and find it from the transaction.
- **Correct:** fix a duplicate while keeping a clear record.
- **Report:** produce property and portfolio views.
- **Share:** give the accountant only the access they need.
- **Export:** download usable records and attachments.

Ask the accountant to review the export before you subscribe. Confirm how records stay available after a downgrade or closure. Record any manual task that remains outside the tool.

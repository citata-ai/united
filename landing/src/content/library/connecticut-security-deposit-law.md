Connecticut puts almost all of its deposit rules in one section: Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-21. I read the current text on the General Assembly site and the 2026 supplement on 2026-10-19. The table below holds the rules you will use most.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit, tenant under 62 | 2 months' rent | § 47a-21(b)(1) |
| Maximum deposit, tenant 62 or older | 1 month's rent | § 47a-21(b)(2) |
| Return deadline | 21 days after the tenancy ends, or 15 days after a written forwarding address, whichever is later | § 47a-21(d)(2) |
| Itemized list of damages | Required with any deduction, same deadline | § 47a-21(d)(2) |
| Escrow account | Deposit goes in at once, at a financial institution in Connecticut | § 47a-21(h)(1) |
| Notice of where the deposit is held | Within 30 days after you receive it | § 47a-21(h)(4) |
| Interest rate | At least the deposit index: 0.49% for 2026 | § 47a-21(i); Dept. of Banking |
| Penalty for a late or wrong return | Twice the deposit | § 47a-21(d)(2) |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Connecticut?

The maximum security deposit in Connecticut is 2 months' rent for a tenant under 62 years of age (§ 47a-21(b)(1)). For a tenant aged 62 or older, the cap is 1 month's rent (§ 47a-21(b)(2)).

The age rule also works after move-in. A tenant who turns 62 after paying a larger deposit can ask for the part above 1 month's rent. You must return that part on request.

The statute defines a "security deposit" as any advance rental payment, except an advance payment for the first month's rent or a deposit for a key or special equipment (§ 47a-21(a)(11)). So first month's rent paid up front does not count toward the cap.

Worked example for a $1,800 unit:

| Tenant | Cap | Maximum deposit |
|---|---|---|
| Age 40 | 2 × $1,800 | $3,600 |
| Age 65 | 1 × $1,800 | $1,800 |
| Turns 62 during the lease, paid $3,600 | Return the excess on request | $1,800 refund |

## How many days does a Connecticut landlord have to return a security deposit?

A Connecticut landlord has 21 days after the tenancy ends, or 15 days after receiving the tenant's forwarding address in writing, whichever is later (§ 47a-21(d)(2)). You deliver the money to the forwarding address.

Recent change: Public Act 23-207 cut the return time from 30 days to 21 days. It also cut the time to return accrued interest from 30 days to 21. The history note under § 47a-21 on the General Assembly site records both changes.

Two dates decide the deadline. Write both in your move-out file:

1. **Record the end date.** The tenancy ends. Day 21 is your first limit.
2. **Record the address date.** The tenant gives a forwarding address in writing. Day 15 from that date is your second limit.
3. **Use the later date.** If the address comes 10 days after move-out, day 25 after move-out is the deadline.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Connecticut?

A Connecticut landlord can deduct the value of damages caused by the tenant's failure to meet the "tenant's obligations" (§ 47a-21(d)(1)). Section 47a-21(a)(14) lists those obligations:

- **Unpaid rent or utilities.** Any rental or utility payment the tenant owes the landlord.
- **Duties under § 47a-11.** The tenant duties listed in that section of the landlord-tenant chapter.
- **Lock change cost.** The actual reasonable cost to change the locks under § 47a-7b, if the tenant has not paid it.

The statute speaks of "damages". Keep move-in and move-out photos and invoices. They show the value of each deduction.

## Does a Connecticut landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. If you keep any part of the deposit, you must send the balance with "a written statement itemizing the nature and amount of such damages" (§ 47a-21(d)(2)). The list goes out by the same 21-day or 15-day deadline.

A good list has one line per item: what the damage was, where in the unit, and the dollar amount. Attach invoices or quotes where you have them.

## Must a Connecticut landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

Yes to both. Section 47a-21 requires an escrow account and yearly interest.

Escrow rules in § 47a-21(h):

- Put the full deposit "immediately" into one or more escrow accounts at a financial institution in Connecticut.
- Act as escrow agent. Withdraw only for the reasons the statute lists, such as a refund, interest, or damages after the tenancy ends.
- Within 30 days, tell the tenant in writing the amount held and the name and address of the bank.
- If the Banking Commissioner asks for account details, answer within 7 days.

Interest rules in § 47a-21(i):

- The rate for each calendar year is at least the deposit index under § 36a-26. The Department of Banking set it at 0.49% for 2026 (0.52% for 2025).
- Pay interest on each anniversary of the tenancy, or credit it to the next rent payment.
- If the tenancy ends before an anniversary, pay accrued interest within 21 days.
- No interest is due for a month in which rent was more than 10 days late, unless you charged a late fee for that month.
- You may not raise rent because you must pay interest.

Worked example: a $3,600 deposit held for all of 2026 earns at least $3,600 × 0.49% = $17.64. The Department of Banking posts an interest calculation table for years with different rates.

## What is the penalty for a Connecticut landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

A landlord who breaks the return rules in § 47a-21(d) is liable for twice the amount of the deposit. If the only failure is the interest, the landlord owes $10 or twice the interest, whichever is greater.

Section 47a-21(k) adds fines:

| Violation | Maximum fine | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Knowingly and wilfully fail to return a deposit when due | $250 per offense | § 47a-21(k)(1) |
| Knowingly and wilfully break the escrow rules | $500, or 30 days in jail, or both | § 47a-21(k)(2) |
| Knowingly and wilfully fail to pay interest | $100 per offense | § 47a-21(k)(3) |

A good-faith belief that you could deduct damages is a defense to the $250 fine. A landlord with fewer than 4 tenants who paid a deposit has a defense to the escrow fine. The tenant can also sue to get the deposit back (§ 47a-21(g)), and the Banking Commissioner can investigate complaints (§ 47a-21(j)).

## A move-out checklist for Connecticut

- Check the tenant's age at move-in and the deposit cap.
- Deposit the money in escrow the same day. Send the 30-day bank notice.
- Pay or credit interest on each anniversary.
- At move-out, record the end date and ask for a forwarding address in writing.
- Inspect, photograph and price each item of damage.
- Send the balance, interest and the itemized list by the later of day 21 or day 15 after the address.

## South Carolina Rent and Notice Rules at a Glance

South Carolina gives landlords wide room on rent. The state sets no cap and bars local rent control. The key notice rule is the one that ends a periodic tenancy. I read each section below on the South Carolina Legislature site on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Statewide rent cap | None | No statute sets one |
| Local rent control | Banned for private rentals | S.C. Code 27-39-60 |
| Rent increase notice | No set period in the Act | Chapter 27-40 is silent |
| End week-to-week tenancy | 7 days written notice | 27-40-770(a) |
| End month-to-month tenancy | 30 days written notice | 27-40-770(b) |
| Default tenancy with no fixed term | Month-to-month (week-to-week for a weekly roomer) | 27-40-310(d) |
| Rent during a fixed term | As the lease sets | 27-40-310(a) |
| Retaliatory increase | Banned above fair-market value | 27-40-910(a) |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Does South Carolina Have a Rent Cap or Rent Control?

South Carolina has no rent cap and no rent control. No state statute limits how much a landlord may raise rent on a private unit.

S.C. Code 27-39-60 also blocks local rent control. It says no county or municipal corporation may "enact, maintain, or enforce" a rent ordinance. The ban covers privately owned residential and commercial rental property. The law dates from 1985 Act No. 184.

The same section leaves 2 doors open for local governments:

- **Public property.** A county or city may regulate rent on property it owns.
- **Agreements.** A county or city may sign agreements with private owners that limit rent. Affordable housing deals often work this way.

Watch for: if your unit has a public subsidy, a tax credit or a city agreement, that contract may limit rent. The ban on local rent control does not cancel a deal you signed.

## How Much Notice Does a South Carolina Landlord Need to Raise Rent?

No South Carolina statute sets a notice period for a rent increase. Chapter 40 of Title 27 has no section on rent increase notice.

The rent is a lease term. S.C. Code 27-40-310(a) lets the landlord and tenant set the rent, the term and other terms in the rental agreement. So the lease decides how and when rent can change.

For a month-to-month tenant, a safe floor is 30 days. S.C. Code 27-40-770(b) requires 30 days written notice to end a month-to-month tenancy. If the tenant refuses the new rent, that is the notice you need to end the tenancy. So give at least 30 days notice of a rent change, in writing.

What to do:

1. **Read the lease first.** It may set a notice period longer than 30 days.
2. **Give written notice.** Name the new rent and the date it starts.
3. **Count from the end of a rental period.** Make the new rent start on a rent due date.
4. **Keep proof of delivery.** A dated copy and a mailing receipt are enough.

## Can a South Carolina Landlord Raise Rent During a Fixed-Term Lease?

A South Carolina landlord can raise rent during a fixed term only if the lease allows it. The lease sets the rent under S.C. Code 27-40-310(a). A one-year lease at $1,500 a month stays at $1,500 until the term ends, unless a clause says otherwise.

Some leases include a step-up clause, for example "rent rises 3% on month 7". That clause binds both sides because both signed it. Without such a clause, a mid-lease increase needs the tenant's written consent.

At the end of the term, you can offer a renewal at a new rent. If the tenant stays after the term with your consent, S.C. Code 27-40-770(c) says 27-40-310(d) applies. That makes the tenancy month-to-month, unless a weekly roomer rule fits.

## How Much Notice Ends a Month-to-Month Tenancy in South Carolina?

Either side must give at least 30 days written notice to end a month-to-month tenancy in South Carolina. S.C. Code 27-40-770(b) counts the 30 days before the termination date named in the notice. A week-to-week tenancy needs 7 days written notice under 27-40-770(a).

Worked calendar example for a month-to-month lease:

| Step | Date |
|---|---|
| Notice delivered to tenant | September 1 |
| Earliest end date (30 days later) | October 1 |
| Safer end date (end of next full rental period) | October 31 |

The statute counts from the date in the notice. Many managers still pick the last day of a rental period. That avoids a fight over partial-month rent.

If the tenant stays past the end date without your consent, S.C. Code 27-40-770(c) lets you bring an action for possession. If the holdover is a willful breach, you may also recover more. The cap is 3 months' periodic rent or twice actual damages, whichever is greater, plus attorney's fees.

## Do South Carolina Cities Add Their Own Rent or Notice Rules?

South Carolina cities cannot regulate private rents. S.C. Code 27-39-60 blocks any county or city ordinance on the amount of rent for private rental property. Charleston, Columbia and Greenville follow the same state rule on rent.

The Residential Landlord and Tenant Act applies to every dwelling unit in the state, under S.C. Code 27-40-110. So the 7-day and 30-day notice rules are the same everywhere. Check local codes for other topics, such as rental registration or short-term rental permits. Those rules do not set rent.

## What Rent Increase Is Illegal in South Carolina?

A retaliatory rent increase above fair-market value is illegal in South Carolina. S.C. Code 27-40-910(a) bars it after a protected complaint. That means a complaint to a housing code agency about a health or safety violation. It also means a complaint to the landlord about a violation of the Act.

If a landlord retaliates, the tenant can use the remedies in 27-40-660 as a defense to an action for possession. If the tenant raises the defense in bad faith, the landlord may recover up to 3 months' rent or treble damages.

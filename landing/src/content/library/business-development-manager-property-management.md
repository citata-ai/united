## What does a property management BDM do all day?

A property management BDM finds owners, follows up with them and signs management agreements. DoorGrow founder Jason Hull, in episode 145 of the DoorGrow Show, calls a BDM "basically a salesperson in a property management business."

A typical week splits into four kinds of work:

| Work | Examples | Share of a typical week (our estimate) |
|---|---|---|
| Lead follow-up | Call web leads within minutes, book calls, send fee schedules | 30–40% |
| Prospecting | Call past owners, investors, real estate agents, local lenders | 20–30% |
| Sales meetings | Owner calls, property walk-throughs, proposals | 20–25% |
| Handover and tracking | Pass signed owners to onboarding, update the CRM, report numbers | 10–15% |

The shares are our planning estimate, not survey data. Set your own after a month of time logs.

A BDM does not fix a weak offer. If owners leave after 6 months because service is poor, a BDM only fills a leaking bucket faster.

## Am I ready to hire a BDM?

You are ready when the sales work is clear enough to teach. Jason Hull's view in DGS 145 is blunt: most owners who hire a BDM see the hire fail. He says the common reason is that the owner does not like sales, has not learned to do it, and so cannot train, script or judge the new hire.

His advice comes down to two paths:

1. **Learn to sell first.** Do the calls, write the scripts and learn the owner objections. Then hire and train a BDM to your standard.
2. **Bring in a proven BDM as a partner.** A BDM who has added hundreds of doors elsewhere will not come cheap. Hull says such a person may want a share of revenue or of the business.

Use this readiness checklist before you post the job:

- [ ] You have closed at least a few owners yourself this year
- [ ] You have written call scripts and answers to the top 10 owner objections
- [ ] You have a written fee schedule and management agreement
- [ ] Leads come in each month that nobody follows up within one day
- [ ] Onboarding can take on more owners without service falling
- [ ] You track leads, calls, proposals and signed doors in a CRM
- [ ] You know your gross margin per door

If you check fewer than five, fix the gaps first.

## At what door count does a BDM pay off?

A BDM pays off when the gross margin from the new doors covers the BDM's full cost, usually within 12 to 18 months. Door count alone does not decide it. Revenue per door and lead flow decide it.

Start with the value of one door. All Property Management gives a baseline fee of 8–12% of monthly rent for a typical residential firm. Here is a worked example with a $1,800 rent and made-up cost numbers:

| Line | Input | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly fee per door | 9% × $1,800 | $162 |
| Yearly fee revenue per door | $162 × 12 | $1,944 |
| Other revenue per door per year (leasing, renewals) | Estimate | $400 |
| Total revenue per door per year | | $2,344 |
| Gross margin after direct service cost | 40% (your number) | $938 |

Now the BDM cost. Say a base of $55,000, plus taxes and tools of $10,000, plus a bonus of $250 per door. These are example numbers, not market pay data.

| BDM adds per year | Bonus | Full cost | Gross margin from new doors | Year-1 result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 doors | $12,500 | $77,500 | $46,900 | −$30,600 |
| 100 doors | $25,000 | $90,000 | $93,800 | +$3,800 |
| 150 doors | $37,500 | $102,500 | $140,700 | +$38,200 |

Doors signed during the year earn for only part of the year, so real year-1 results are worse. But doors that stay keep earning in year 2 with no new bonus. In this example, a BDM needs about 100 doors a year to break even in year 1. Run your own inputs. ## Should I pay base plus commission per door?

Yes, base plus a per-door bonus is the most common shape, and it works best when the bonus follows doors that stay. A pure commission plan draws people who chase any door, including bad fits. A pure salary gives no push to close.

A plan that rewards the right doors:

1. **Base salary.** Enough to cover the slow months while the pipeline builds.
2. **Bonus per door, in two parts.** For example, half at the first rent collected and half after 6 or 12 months.
3. **Quality rules.** No bonus for doors below your minimum rent or outside your service area.
4. **Large-account rule.** A cap or a review for any single owner above a set door count.

The last rule matters. Hull describes a client whose BDM added about 300 doors in three or four months, largely through one owner. He calls it "a dangerous situation normally," because one owner then holds too much power over the business. That client later had to set boundaries and be ready to walk away.

## How do I measure a BDM?

Measure outcomes first and activity second. Track these each month:

| Metric | What it tells you | Target to set |
|---|---|---|
| Doors added | Raw output | From your break-even math |
| Doors still managed after 12 months | Fit and quality | Your current retention or better |
| Revenue per new door | Did they sign the right properties? | At or above your average |
| Lead response time | Speed on inbound leads | Same business day |
| Lead-to-signed rate | Sales skill | Track a 3-month baseline first |
| Cost per door added | Efficiency | Below one year of gross margin per door |

Give a new BDM 90 days to build a pipeline before you judge doors added. Judge activity in those first 90 days instead.

## What changes the BDM math today?

Lead flow changes the BDM math most. A BDM with no leads spends the day cold calling. Many managers expect to grow: in AppFolio's 2025 benchmark survey, as the National Apartment Association reports, 65% of respondents expected their units under management to go up.

Where owner leads come from is also moving. More owners now ask AI tools to suggest a manager. Read [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping) before you plan BDM targets on last year's lead volume.

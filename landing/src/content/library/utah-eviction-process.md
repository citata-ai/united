## Utah eviction rules at a glance

Utah eviction law lives in Utah Code Title 78B, Chapter 6, Part 8, "Forcible Entry and Detainer". The section numbers in this part did not change in 2024 to 2026. But the 2026 General Session amended several of them. We read each section on le.utah.gov on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Unpaid rent: pay or quit notice | 3 business days after service | 78B-6-802(1)(c) |
| Lease breach the tenant can fix: comply or quit | 3 calendar days after service | 78B-6-802(1)(h) |
| Waste, unlawful business, nuisance, criminal act: quit | 3 calendar days after service | 78B-6-802(1)(d)–(g) |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | Serve 15 calendar days or more before the period ends | 78B-6-802(1)(b)(i) |
| End a tenancy at will | Not less than 5 calendar days | 78B-6-802(1)(b)(ii) |
| Fixed term ends | No notice needed | 78B-6-802(1)(a) |
| Tenant answers the summons | 3 business days after service | 78B-6-807(3)(a) |
| Occupancy hearing, on request | Within 10 business days after the answer | 78B-6-810(2)(a) |
| Trial starts | Within 60 days after the complaint is served | 78B-6-810(1)(b) |
| Tenant leaves after order of restitution | 3 calendar days after service | 78B-6-812(2)(b) |
| Lockout without a court order | Unlawful | 78B-6-814 |

## What notice must a Utah landlord give for unpaid rent?

A Utah landlord must serve a written notice that gives the tenant 3 business days to pay the rent and other amounts due, or leave. Utah Code 78B-6-802(1)(c) lets you serve this notice any time after the rent is due. If the tenant pays in full inside the window, the lease goes on (78B-6-802(2)).

Business days skip weekends and court holidays. The Utah State Courts give this count: a notice served on a Friday runs Monday (day 1), Tuesday (day 2) and Wednesday (day 3). The tenant is in unlawful detainer after the end of Wednesday.

Serve the notice in one of the ways in Utah Code 78B-6-805:

1. **Hand it to the tenant.** This is the clearest proof.
2. **Send it by registered or certified mail.** Address it to the tenant at the residence or the leased property.
3. **Leave it with an adult at the unit.** The person must be of "suitable age and discretion".
4. **Post it on the unit.** Use this only when no such person is there. Put it in a clear, visible place.

The Utah State Courts publish a form for each notice. Form 1001EV is the 3 day notice to pay or vacate.

## Can a Utah landlord evict for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes, a Utah landlord can evict for a lease violation, and can end a month-to-month tenancy without a stated cause. The notice depends on the reason.

- **A breach the tenant can fix:** a 3 calendar day notice to comply or quit (78B-6-802(1)(h)).
- **Subletting against the lease, or waste:** a 3 calendar day notice to quit (78B-6-802(1)(d)).
- **An unlawful business, a nuisance or a criminal act on the premises:** a 3 calendar day notice to quit (78B-6-802(1)(e)–(g)).
- **No cause, month-to-month:** serve notice 15 calendar days or more before the end of the month or period (78B-6-802(1)(b)(i)).
- **No cause, fixed term:** the tenancy ends at the end of the term with no notice (78B-6-802(1)(a)).

Watch for: HB 591 "Nuisance Amendments" took effect May 6, 2026. Nuisance abatement by eviction now points to Utah Code 78B-6a-303. The courts' eviction page says its forms are not yet updated for that bill. For a nuisance case, talk to an attorney first.

## Where does a landlord file a Utah eviction, and what does it cost?

A Utah landlord files the eviction in district court. The Utah State Courts list four papers. They are a civil cover sheet (1044XX), a summons (1105EV), a complaint for unlawful detainer (1100EV) and a disclosure notice (1150EV). Attach the lease as exhibit 1 and the notice as exhibit 2.

The filing fee depends on the amount you claim. Here are the civil complaint fees on the Utah Courts fee page, effective May 6, 2026 (Utah Code 78A-2-301):

| Amount you claim | Filing fee |
|---|---|
| $2,000 or less | $105 |
| More than $2,000 and less than $10,000 | $215 |
| $10,000 or more | $375 |

You cannot serve the summons yourself. Any adult who is not part of the case can serve it for free. A sheriff, constable or process server charges a fee. You must serve the papers within 120 days of filing (Utah State Courts).

## How long does an eviction take in Utah?

A Utah eviction with no answer can end about 2 to 3 weeks after the notice. Add the time the court takes to sign. A contested case takes longer. Here is a worked timeline for an unpaid rent case. It is a sample, not a promise.

| Step | Example day | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Serve the 3 day pay or quit notice | Friday, week 1 | 78B-6-802(1)(c) |
| Notice runs out | End of Wednesday, week 2 | 3 business days |
| File the complaint | Thursday, week 2 | Utah State Courts |
| Serve the summons | Friday, week 2 | 78B-6-807 |
| Answer deadline | End of Wednesday, week 3 | 3 business days |
| File for default and order of restitution | Thursday, week 3 | Utah State Courts |
| Judge signs, order is served | Depends on the court | 78B-6-812 |
| Tenant must leave | 3 calendar days after service | 78B-6-812(2)(b) |

If the tenant files an answer, even late, you cannot get a default (Utah State Courts). Either side may then ask for an evidentiary hearing. The court must hold it within 10 business days after the answer (78B-6-810(2)(a)). At that hearing the judge decides who keeps the unit during the case, and may decide the whole case. Otherwise the trial must start within 60 days after the complaint is served, unless the parties agree to a different date (78B-6-810(1)(b)).

Before a default, check if the tenant is in the military. The courts' page says the court might delay the eviction for up to 90 days for a servicemember.

The judgment covers the rent and 3 times the damages the court assesses (78B-6-811(3)). The court must award costs and reasonable attorney fees to the prevailing party (78B-6-811(5)(a)).

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Utah?

Only a sheriff or constable removes a Utah tenant. The order of restitution tells the tenant to leave within 3 calendar days after service (78B-6-812(2)(b)). If the tenant stays, a sheriff or constable may enter by force "using the least destructive means possible" (78B-6-812(4)(a)).

After the removal, Utah Code 78B-6-812 (effective September 1, 2026) sets these duties:

- [ ] Give the tenant reasonable access within 5 business days to collect clothing, ID, financial and immigration papers, public benefit papers, and medical items and medicine.
- [ ] Store other property in a suitable place if the sheriff or constable gives you that task.
- [ ] If a pet is there and the tenant is not, notify local animal control.
- [ ] Give animal control the tenant's name and last known contact details.
- [ ] Treat stored property as abandoned under 78B-6-816: the tenant has 15 calendar days after your notice to claim it.

## Is a self-help eviction legal in Utah?

No. Utah Code 78B-6-814 says it is unlawful for an owner to willfully exclude a tenant "in any manner except by judicial process". Utah Code 78B-6-801 defines willful exclusion as keeping the tenant out with intent to deny entry. So a lockout or a lock change without a court order breaks the law.

The one exception is abandonment. If the tenant has abandoned the unit, you may remove the contents under 78B-6-816 and rent the unit again.

Utility shutoffs: Utah Code 57-22-4 requires an owner to maintain electrical systems, plumbing, heating, and hot and cold water. Do not cut a service to force a tenant out. Ask an attorney before you touch any utility in a dispute.

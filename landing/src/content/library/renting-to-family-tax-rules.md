## What is a fair rental price for a family rental?

IRS Publication 527 generally defines fair rental price as the rent an unrelated person would be willing to pay.

The publication says substantially below-comparable rent is not a fair rental price. It asks owners to compare purpose, size, condition, furnishings, and location. A family agreement does not establish market rent just because everyone signs it.

Gather current local comparisons that match the property. Record the source, date, included utilities, furniture, parking, and lease term. Ask a local manager for a written opinion with supporting examples. Keep the opinion and evidence with the lease.

Do not use the mortgage payment as the rent standard. Your loan balance reflects your financing, not what an unrelated tenant would pay. A rent that covers debt can still be below market. A debt-free house can still have a fair rental price.

| Record | What it supports |
|---|---|
| Comparable homes | Market price for similar use and condition |
| Written lease | Agreed rent, duties, and occupancy |
| Payment history | What the relative actually paid |
| Use calendar | Who occupied the home and when |
| Expense file | Costs and personal-use allocation review |

Review concessions as part of the arrangement. Free months, owner-paid bills, or side promises can affect the economics. Give the full facts to your preparer rather than showing only the stated monthly rent.

## When does a family rental count as personal use?

Publication 527 treats certain family occupancy as personal use unless its stated main-home and fair-rent exception applies.

The publication identifies family as spouses, siblings, half siblings, ancestors, and lineal descendants for this rule. It also says below-fair-rent use by anyone can count as personal use. The rule therefore needs more than a simple family-or-stranger check.

For a family member, the exception requires use as the main home and payment of fair rental price. Paying fair rent for a holiday stay does not necessarily meet that main-home condition. Ask your preparer to evaluate the actual occupancy pattern.

Publication 527 says a dwelling is used as a home when personal use exceeds the greater of 14 days or 10% of fair-rent rental days. Those thresholds have a specific role in the dwelling-use rules. Do not apply them without counting the right categories of days.

Keep owner stays, family stays, outside rentals, and genuine repair days in the calendar. The publication has special rules for days spent substantially on repairs and maintenance. A trip that includes a little work does not automatically become a nonpersonal trip.

Tell your preparer about any shared ownership or exchanged use of another property. Publication 527 includes additional personal-use rules for those arrangements. A simple bank ledger cannot reveal who actually used the home.

## Which rental deductions can I lose or limit?

Personal use can require expense allocation and can limit deductions, depending on how the dwelling was used.

Publication 527 explains separate treatment for a home with rental and personal use. If the property is treated as a home, rental deductions can face income-based limits under those rules. A fully personal or not-for-profit arrangement needs its own analysis. Do not assume normal Schedule E treatment applies to every discounted family lease.

Keep shared expenses separate from direct rental costs. Ask the preparer how to allocate insurance, interest, taxes, utilities, repairs, and depreciation. The correct treatment depends on the expense and use. Avoid applying a single guessed percentage to everything.

Do not claim a deduction for rent you chose not to charge. Publication 527 explains that a cash-method taxpayer generally cannot deduct uncollected rent never included in income. A family discount is not an extra rental expense merely because you could have charged more.

Loss rules add another review. Publication 925 addresses passive activity and at-risk limits where applicable. A fair-rent rental can still have a deduction limited by those rules. Solving the personal-use question does not settle every loss issue.

If your goal is family support, state that goal openly to your adviser. You can then compare support choices without forcing the arrangement into an unsuitable rental classification. Keep personal goals and claimed business treatment consistent.

## How do I document a fair-rent family arrangement?

Use the same clear lease, payment records, condition evidence, and expense discipline you would need with an unrelated tenant.

Start with a dated rent file and an explanation of the chosen amount. Update the evidence when the property or arrangement changes. A comparison from an unrelated location or a different type of home offers weak support. Ask a local professional about uncertain differences.

Collect rent through a traceable method and preserve payment records. Document changes, concessions, and unpaid amounts. Do not create backdated receipts to make a family arrangement appear different from what occurred.

Apply the written maintenance and reporting process consistently. Keep vendor invoices and notes about work performed. If you hire a manager, compare the cost using the [management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator). The manager should receive the actual family arrangement and lease terms.

Give the preparer the complete file before filing season. Include personal-use dates, rent evidence, payments, shared bills, and the prior depreciation schedule. Ask for a written explanation of the reporting position and any limited deductions.

Before signing a new family lease, review both the tax plan and local tenant rules. Tax treatment does not replace landlord duties. Use a local attorney for lease questions and a tax professional for the federal use and deduction rules.

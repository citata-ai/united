## How do you calculate NOI on a rental?

You calculate NOI by subtracting operating expenses from the income the property actually collects. Northspyre gives the formula in two forms:

- NOI = gross operating income − operating expenses
- NOI = (gross operating income + other income) − operating expenses

Gross operating income is the rent you collect, not the rent you hope for. So start with full rent for 12 months, then take out vacancy and unpaid rent. Other income covers fees such as late fees, pet rent and parking.

Here is a worked example for one single-family rental for one year. The numbers are an example, not market data.

| Line | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduled rent | $2,000 × 12 months | $24,000 |
| Minus vacancy | 1 month empty | − $2,000 |
| Other income | Late fees and pet rent | + $300 |
| **Gross operating income** | $24,000 − $2,000 + $300 | **$22,300** |
| Property tax | County bill | $3,200 |
| Insurance | Landlord policy | $1,400 |
| Repairs and maintenance | Routine work orders | $1,500 |
| Management fee | 8% × $22,000 rent collected | $1,760 |
| Leasing fee | 1 new tenant, 70% of $2,000 | $1,400 |
| HOA dues | $50 × 12 | $600 |
| **Operating expenses** | Sum of 6 lines | **$9,860** |
| **NOI** | $22,300 − $9,860 | **$12,440** |

The 8% management fee and 70% leasing fee are close to the averages in iPropertyManagement's 722-branch sample (8.49% and 70.6%, updated 2026-09-23). Use your own agreement's numbers.

## What counts as an operating expense for NOI?

An operating expense is a cost you pay to run and keep the property in its current state. Northspyre lists utilities, insurance, property taxes, repairs and property management fees.

| Counts in NOI (operating expense) | Does not count in NOI |
|---|---|
| Property tax | Mortgage principal and interest |
| Landlord insurance | Capital improvements (new roof, HVAC, appliances) |
| Routine repairs and maintenance | Depreciation and amortization |
| Utilities the owner pays | Income tax |
| Management, leasing and renewal fees | Owner's personal travel to the property |
| HOA dues | Loan fees and points |
| Landscaping, snow removal, pest control | Security deposits held for tenants |

Northspyre says NOI excludes loan payments, capital expenditures, depreciation and amortization. It calls NOI a pre-tax figure. Here "tax" means income tax: property tax is an operating expense.

Security deposits are not income while you hold them to return. The IRS says a deposit you plan to return is not income when you receive it. A deposit used as the last month's rent is advance rent.

## Does NOI include the mortgage payment?

No. NOI does not include the mortgage payment. Buildium's cap rate entry notes that NOI excludes mortgage payments and financing costs.

This is on purpose. Two buyers can finance the same house in very different ways. One pays cash. One borrows 80%. The property's NOI is the same for both. Only their cash flow differs. That is why lenders and buyers use NOI to compare properties, and why cap rate is built on NOI.

Your tax return is a different view. The IRS lets you deduct mortgage interest, property tax, operating costs, depreciation and repairs on Schedule E. So your Schedule E profit is not your NOI. This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current rules or ask a tax professional.

## What is a good NOI for a single-family rental?

A good NOI is one that covers the mortgage and capital costs with room to spare, and grows each year. No single dollar figure is "good", because NOI depends on rent, taxes and price. Use ratios to judge it:

1. **NOI margin.** NOI ÷ gross operating income. In the example: $12,440 ÷ $22,300 = 55.8%. Track it each year.
2. **Debt coverage.** NOI ÷ yearly mortgage payments. Above 1.0 means NOI covers the loan. Lenders look at this ratio, Northspyre notes.
3. **Cap rate.** NOI ÷ property value. In the example, at a $250,000 value: $12,440 ÷ $250,000 = 4.98%.

Compare these ratios with similar homes in the same market, not with a national average.

## How does a property manager raise NOI?

A property manager raises NOI in 2 ways: collect more income, or spend less to run the property. Each dollar of either adds one dollar to NOI.

- **Cut vacancy.** In the example, one month empty costs $2,000. Two weeks less vacancy adds about $1,000 to NOI.
- **Keep good tenants.** Each renewal avoids a leasing fee. In the example, that saves $1,400.
- **Collect on time.** Rent that is never collected never reaches NOI.
- **Price rent to the market.** Check rent at each renewal.
- **Bid out large repairs.** Get 2 or 3 quotes above a set amount.
- **Do preventive work.** A yearly HVAC service can prevent a larger emergency call.
- **Check fixed costs once a year.** Shop insurance and appeal the tax value if it is too high.

## What is the difference between NOI and cash flow?

NOI measures how the property performs. Cash flow measures what reaches your pocket after the loan and capital costs. Continue the example:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| NOI | $12,440 |
| Minus mortgage payments ($850 × 12) | − $10,200 |
| Minus capital reserve (roof and HVAC fund) | − $1,200 |
| **Cash flow before income tax** | **$1,040** |

The same house shows a healthy NOI of $12,440 and a thin cash flow of $1,040. A higher interest rate could turn the cash flow negative while NOI stays the same.

## What does a per-lease commission look like?

A per-lease commission pays for a clearly defined leasing result under a written plan. The plan must say when the employee earns the amount and how the company records it.

Do not start by copying a competitor's amount. List the work the agent performs and the result you want to reward. A role that only shows homes differs from a role that handles the full application and signing process.

| Pay model | What it can support | Main design question |
|---|---|---|
| Salary or hourly base | Availability, service and administrative work | What duties and schedule does the base cover? |
| Per-lease amount | A defined completed leasing event | When is the amount earned? |
| Percentage commission | Pay linked to a stated fee or amount | What is the exact calculation base? |
| Base plus commission | Ongoing work plus a leasing incentive | How do the parts work together? |

For an invented example, a plan pays $100 for each qualifying completed lease. If 6 leases qualify, the calculation is $600. Those are sample inputs, not a market pay rate or approved legal plan.

Specify the treatment of shared work, withdrawn applications and delayed move-ins. Have counsel check when compensation becomes earned and whether any adjustment is allowed. A clear accounting rule still needs to comply with local employment law.

## How does the pay model affect agent behavior?

A pay plan directs attention toward the work it rewards. Test for unwanted incentives before you launch it rather than assuming a commission always improves service.

A plan based only on signatures may encourage staff to focus on easy leases. A plan based only on volume can overlook record quality and unresolved resident questions. These are design risks to review, not measured outcomes claimed by this guide.

Include quality checks that staff can understand and influence. Confirm that listings are accurate, applications follow the reviewed process and lease files are complete. Do not create a vague quality exception that lets a manager change pay after the work.

Explain how leads and properties are assigned. Agents need to know how shared tours and transferred cases affect credit. Keep assignments visible so the pay system does not depend on private negotiation.

Check whether the agent has authority over the result being rewarded. An owner may control price approval while another team handles screening. Pay should not hold one person responsible for an outcome that only others can change.

Review results with the team after launch. Look at complaints, incomplete files and old listings as well as signed leases. A plan needs revision if staff reach the paid target while essential work remains undone.

## Are there license and wage rules for commission?

License and wage rules depend on the duties and jurisdiction. Commission pay does not by itself remove employee protections or create permission to perform licensed work.

TREC says that paid showing or leasing for a property owner requires a license in Texas. It also discusses licensing for control over rent acceptance or deposits for single-family residential property. Check your own state's rules before assigning duties or paying for them.

Federal outside sales rules also require a duties review. Under 29 CFR section 541.500, the exemption requires qualifying primary sales duties and customary, regular work away from the employer's place of business. Do not decide exemption from the title leasing agent alone.

Ask an employment adviser to review the actual job, schedule and pay terms. Include office work, remote contact and field duties. Check applicable state and local wage rules as well as federal rules.

Put the approved plan in writing before the measurement period. Explain the calculation, earning event, pay timing and process for correcting an error. Ask counsel how the plan should handle a person leaving the company.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How should I track leasing commissions?

Track commissions from the lease record through approval and payroll. Each line should explain the calculation without requiring staff to search old messages.

Use the property identifier, agent, lease identifier, qualifying event date and pay-plan version. Record the calculation base and approved amount. Mark pending items separately from amounts already paid.

Keep a review step for shared credit and exceptions. The reviewer should check the source record rather than only the total. Do not let the same lease appear twice under different property labels.

For a percentage plan, define whether the base uses billed fees or collected fees. Include any allowed exclusions in the written plan. Keep refunds and corrections traceable to the original entry.

Give agents a clear statement they can check. Allow them to raise a missing lease or wrong assignment through a named contact. Resolve questions with a recorded correction rather than changing the ledger silently.

Reconcile the approved commission list with payroll after each pay run. Ask your payroll adviser how the payments affect any required wage calculations. Software can store the amounts, but someone still owns the review.

## How can I choose an affordable plan?

Choose an affordable plan by testing the full employment cost against your leasing revenue and workload. Include training, supervision, travel and coverage during slow periods.

Use your own completed lease volume instead of assuming the busiest month continues all year. Test a quiet season and a delayed owner approval period. State each assumption in the planning sheet.

The [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) can help inspect the recurring fee side of your business. Keep leasing income and the costs of leasing work visible as separate budget lines.

Discuss the draft plan with the people who will administer it. Ask payroll to calculate a sample period and ask an agent to explain the earning rule. Revise any term they interpret differently.

Take your current job duties to a local employment and licensing adviser. Confirm the legal structure before choosing the final salary, hourly rate or commission amount.

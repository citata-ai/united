## What is a 1031 exchange?

A 1031 exchange is a trade of one investment or business real property for another like-kind real property, with the tax on the gain put off. The name comes from Internal Revenue Code section 1031. The IRS says that in a qualifying exchange "you are not required to recognize a gain or loss".

The tax is deferred, not erased. The gain from the old property carries into the new one. If the owner later sells without another exchange, the tax comes due then.

Since the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, the IRS says section 1031 "now applies only to exchanges of real property". Trades of equipment, vehicles or art no longer qualify after 2017. Real property held mainly for sale, such as a flip, does not qualify either.

The owner reports the exchange on IRS Form 8824.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## What are the 45-day and 180-day rules?

The 45-day and 180-day rules set the deadlines in a deferred exchange. A deferred exchange is one where the owner sells first and buys the replacement later. The IRS Form 8824 instructions (2025) give both deadlines:

1. **45 days to identify.** Identify the replacement property in writing "no later than 45 days after the date you transferred the property you gave up".
2. **180 days to close.** Receive the replacement property by the earlier of the 180th day after the transfer or the due date of your tax return, with extensions.

Both clocks start on the same day: the day the old property transfers. The 45 days sit inside the 180 days, not before them.

### Worked timeline

An owner closes the sale of a rental on 2026-03-02.

| Event | Date | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Sale of old rental closes | 2026-03-02 | Day 0 |
| Last day to identify replacement | 2026-04-16 | Day 45 |
| Last day to close on replacement | 2026-08-29 | Day 180 |
| Tax return due date (with extension) | Later than day 180 | So day 180 is the limit |

Now a different case. The sale closes on 2026-12-15. Day 180 falls in June 2027. The owner's 2026 return is due in April 2027. If the owner does not file an extension, the return due date comes first and becomes the deadline. A tax professional can confirm the dates for your case.

Treasury regulation 1.1031(k)-1(c)(4) also limits how many properties you may identify. You may name up to 3 properties of any value. Or you may name any number, as long as their total fair market value stays within 200% of the value of the property you sold.

## What is a like-kind property?

A like-kind property is real property of the same nature or character as the one you give up. The IRS says real properties "are generally like-kind" whether improved or unimproved. So a single-family rental can be exchanged for a small apartment building, a strip of land held for investment, or a commercial building.

Here is what qualifies and what does not, from the IRS pages:

| Property | Qualifies? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rental house for apartment building | Yes, generally | Both are real property held for investment |
| Rental condo for vacant land held for investment | Yes, generally | Improved and unimproved real property are like-kind |
| U.S. rental for a rental in another country | No | U.S. and foreign real property are not like-kind |
| Your own home for a rental | No | Property used solely as a personal residence does not qualify |
| A house you bought to flip | No | Property held mainly for sale does not qualify |
| Rental for equipment or a vehicle | No | Only real property qualifies after 2017 |

If the owner gets cash or other non-like-kind property in the trade, that part is taxable. The IRS says you "must recognize a gain to the extent of the other property and money received". You cannot claim a loss in a 1031 exchange.

The Form 8824 instructions also have a related-party rule. If you exchange with a related party and either side sells the property within 2 years, the deferred gain generally becomes taxable in that year.

## Can the property manager be the qualified intermediary?

No. A property manager who is the owner's agent cannot be the qualified intermediary. A qualified intermediary (QI) holds the sale money between the sale and the purchase, so the owner never touches it.

Treasury regulation 1.1031(k)-1(k) says the QI must not be a "disqualified person". A disqualified person includes anyone who "is the agent of the taxpayer at the time of the transaction". It also includes a person who acted as the owner's employee, attorney, accountant, broker or real estate agent within the 2 years before the transfer. A property manager acts as the owner's agent under the management agreement. So the manager is out.

The IRS Form 8824 instructions add a warning: if the QI's own failure causes a missed deadline, the transaction may not qualify. Pick a QI with care. That choice belongs to the owner and their tax adviser.

## What records does a property manager give for a 1031 exchange?

A property manager gives the owner the records that show how the property was held and what it earned. The owner's tax professional and QI use these to prove the property was held for investment and to value the deal.

Use this checklist when an owner tells you about a planned exchange:

- [ ] Current rent roll with lease start and end dates
- [ ] Copies of all current leases and addenda
- [ ] Security deposit list by tenant, for transfer at closing
- [ ] Income and expense statements for the last 2 to 3 years
- [ ] Year-to-date owner statement
- [ ] List of capital improvements with dates and invoices
- [ ] Open work orders and known repair items
- [ ] Service contracts that may transfer (pest, landscaping)
- [ ] Copies of Form 1099-MISC you issued to the owner

Then plan the handover. If the sale ends your management of that property, follow your offboarding steps: deposits, keys, tenant notices and a final statement.

## Can you 1031 into a property under management?

Yes. An owner can exchange into a replacement property and hire a property manager to run it. The replacement must be held for business or investment use, and a rental under management fits that use.

This is the best part of a 1031 exchange for a manager. An owner who sells one rental may buy 2 or 3 more with the same equity. Ask your owners about their plans each year. If an owner is near the 45-day deadline, a fast rent estimate and a management proposal can help their choice.

Three practical points for managers:

1. **Do not give tax advice.** Send the owner to a tax professional for exchange rules.
2. **Be fast with data.** Rent comps and expense history help the owner pick in 45 days.
3. **Watch the related-party rule.** If the owner wants to buy from a relative or a company they control, flag the 2-year rule and send them to their adviser.

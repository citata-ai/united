Nevada sets its deposit rules in one section: NRS 118A.242. The Nevada Legislature last amended it in 2021, per the history note in the current code (Rev. 4/15/2026). Here is the short version.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | 3 months' periodic rent, last month's rent included | NRS 118A.242(1) |
| Surety bond instead of cash | Allowed only if the landlord agrees | NRS 118A.242(2)–(3) |
| Return of deposit | Within 30 days after the tenancy ends | NRS 118A.242(4) |
| Itemized list | Written accounting, due with the refund | NRS 118A.242(4) |
| Allowed deductions | Unpaid rent, damage beyond normal wear, reasonable cleaning | NRS 118A.242(4) |
| Penalty for late or no refund | Entire deposit, plus up to 1 more deposit | NRS 118A.242(6) |
| Nonrefundable deposit | Void, except a reasonable cleaning charge | NRS 118A.242(8) |
| Receipt | Signed receipt when the tenant asks | NRS 118A.250 |
| Sale of the property | Transfer the deposit or return it | NRS 118A.244 |

## What is the maximum security deposit in Nevada?

The maximum security deposit in Nevada is 3 months' periodic rent. NRS 118A.242(1) counts the deposit, any surety bond and the last month's rent together. The total may not exceed 3 months of rent.

NRS 118A.240 defines a "security deposit" by its use, not its name. Any payment, fee or charge for unpaid rent, damage or cleaning counts. So a "pet fee" or "move-in fee" used for damage is part of the deposit and part of the cap.

A worked example for a $1,800 unit:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Monthly rent | $1,800 |
| Cap (3 × $1,800) | $5,400 |
| Last month's rent paid up front | $1,800 |
| Pet fee used for damage | $400 |
| Largest cash deposit left under the cap | $3,200 |

Nevada also lets a tenant buy a surety bond in place of all or part of the deposit. You do not have to accept a bond. You also may not force a tenant to buy one (NRS 118A.242(3)).

## How many days does a Nevada landlord have to return a security deposit?

A Nevada landlord has 30 days to return a security deposit. The clock starts when the tenancy ends, for any reason, by either party (NRS 118A.242(4)).

The statute names two ways to pay the refund:

1. **Hand it over** to the tenant at the place where the tenant paid rent.
2. **Mail it** to the tenant's present address, or to the last known address if you do not know the present one.

Ask each tenant for a forwarding address at move-out. A refund mailed to the unit the tenant just left may not reach the tenant.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Nevada?

A Nevada landlord may deduct only 3 kinds of cost. NRS 118A.242(4) allows amounts "reasonably necessary" to:

- **Cover unpaid rent.** Rent the tenant owes under the lease.
- **Repair damage.** Damage the tenant caused, other than normal wear.
- **Clean the unit.** The reasonable cost of cleaning the premises.

NRS 118A.110 defines "normal wear" as deterioration without negligence, carelessness or abuse. That covers the tenant, the household and guests. Faded paint after 3 years is normal wear. A hole in a door is not.

Good records make each deduction easy to defend. NRS 118A.200(3)(k) says a written lease must include a signed record of the inventory and condition of the premises. Use that record at move-in and repeat it at move-out, with dated photos.

## Does a Nevada landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. A Nevada landlord must give the tenant an itemized, written accounting of the deposit. The list is due with the refund, within the same 30 days (NRS 118A.242(4)).

A clear accounting shows:

- the deposit amount you received;
- each deduction, with the reason and the cost;
- copies of repair or cleaning invoices, where you have them;
- the balance you return.

If a surety bond covered part of the deposit, NRS 118A.242(5) gives the tenant a right to dispute items with the surety. If the tenant sends a written dispute within 30 days, the surety may not report the claim to a credit agency without a judgment.

## Must a Nevada landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

Chapter 118A of NRS has no section that requires interest on deposits. It also has no section that requires a separate or trust account for them. We read the full chapter on the Nevada Legislature site (Rev. 4/15/2026).

The chapter does protect the deposit in two other ways:

- **Priority over creditors.** The tenant's claim to the deposit comes before the claim of any creditor of the landlord (NRS 118A.242(9)).
- **Sale of the property.** The landlord must transfer the deposit to the new owner and notify the tenant in writing, or return it (NRS 118A.244). The new owner must accept the deposit and may not demand a new one during the lease.

A licensed property manager may have separate trust account duties under other laws. Ask your broker or attorney.

## What is the penalty for a Nevada landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

A Nevada landlord who does not return the deposit within 30 days owes 2 amounts. NRS 118A.242(6) sets them:

1. **The entire deposit.** Not only the part the landlord kept.
2. **A further sum.** The court sets it, up to the amount of the entire deposit.

So a landlord who holds a $2,000 deposit can owe up to $4,000. The court decides the second sum. Under NRS 118A.242(7), the court looks at 3 points: good faith, the course of conduct between the parties, and the harm to the tenant.

A lease cannot waive these rights. NRS 118A.242(8) voids any clause that calls the deposit nonrefundable or that cuts the tenant's rights. The one exception is a reasonable nonrefundable cleaning charge in the lease.

## Move-out checklist for Nevada landlords

- [ ] Note the date the tenancy ended. Add 30 days to get your deadline.
- [ ] Do the move-out walk-through against the signed move-in record.
- [ ] Keep each deduction to rent, damage beyond normal wear or cleaning.
- [ ] Write the itemized accounting and attach invoices.
- [ ] Hand or mail the refund and the list before day 30.
- [ ] Keep proof of mailing.

## Does the HAP contract transfer to the buyer?

The HAP contract can transfer to the buyer, but only through the housing authority. The HAP contract (Housing Assistance Payments contract) is between the owner and the PHA. A deed does not move it. The PHA must accept the new owner and assign the contract.

NYC's Department of Housing Preservation and Development (HPD) runs a voucher program. Its change-of-ownership packet shows the steps in plain terms:

- The seller must notify HPD "at least 30 days in advance of the anticipated closing date".
- HPD "will only release payments to the purchaser once property ownership documentation has been supplied and the HAP contract assigned".
- The purchaser "must be eligible for participation" and meet the federal owner rules in 24 CFR 982.306.
- The purchaser should send the documents "within 30 days of the date of sale".

Norfolk Redevelopment and Housing Authority states the same rule: the owner "must receive written consent from NRHA prior to assigning a HAP contract to a new owner", and the new owner "must agree to be bound by and comply with the HAP contract".

On the NYC assignment form, the buyer agrees to accept "all of the conditions, obligations and benefits" of the HAP contract. So the buyer takes on the contract as written, including any open repair items.

## What forms does the PHA need?

The PHA needs proof of the sale, proof the buyer can be paid, and the buyer's agreement to the contract. NYC HPD's list is a good model. Your PHA's list may differ, so ask for its packet.

| Document | Who sends it | Why the PHA needs it |
|---|---|---|
| Notice of intent to sell, with closing date | Seller | Lets the PHA hold payments from the sale date |
| Copy of the deed (or co-op/condo papers) | Buyer | Proves the new owner |
| Attorney's closing letter, if the deed is not yet recorded | Buyer | Proves the sale before recording |
| Notarized letter naming the HAP payee | All owners on the deed | Only one party may receive HAP |
| W-9 (substitute W-9 in NYC) with SSN or EIN | Buyer or payee | Tax reporting; avoids backup withholding |
| IRS EIN letter or Social Security card | Buyer or payee | Verifies the tax ID |
| Management agreement, if any | Buyer | Shows who acts for the owner |
| Assignment of HAP contract request form | Buyer | The buyer accepts the HAP contract |
| Owner certifications | Buyer | No close-relative conflict, not debarred or suspended |

The certifications match federal rules. Under 24 CFR 982.306, a PHA must not approve an owner who is debarred, suspended or under a limited denial of participation. It may refuse an owner with past HAP contract breaches, fraud, unpaid local taxes or a record of code violations.

## Who gets the HAP for the sale month?

The HAP for the sale month goes by the PHA's rules and the closing date. Federal rules do not set a split, so plan it in the purchase contract.

The 2 PHAs we read handle it this way:

- **Tacoma Housing Authority:** if you get HAP after the sale, "you will be responsible for returning the HAP payment" to the housing authority.
- **NYC HPD:** HPD holds payments from the reported closing date. If it paid the seller after the sale date, "it will be the purchaser's responsibility to contact and obtain these funds from the seller". HPD may pay the buyer up to 60 days of back payments, if the seller gave notice and the buyer sent documents within 30 days.

Worked example: HAP is $1,300 per month. Closing is on the 10th of a 30-day month. The PHA paid the full month to the seller on the 1st. If you agree to prorate by days owned, the seller keeps 9 days and the buyer gets 21 days.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Seller's share: $1,300 × 9 ÷ 30 | $390 |
| Buyer's share: $1,300 × 21 ÷ 30 | $910 |
| Seller credits buyer at closing | $910 |

Write this into the purchase contract. Also say who returns any HAP the PHA asks back.

## Can the buyer end the tenancy?

The buyer can end the tenancy only on the grounds in the lease and 24 CFR 982.310. Tacoma Housing Authority says "the new owner must honor the current lease".

The rule allows 3 grounds: serious or repeated lease violations, some law violations, and "other good cause". Other good cause includes "a business or economic reason", and the rule names "sale of the property" as an example. But during the initial lease term, an owner may not use that reason. Also, an owner may not end a tenancy because the PHA did not pay the HAP (982.310(b)).

To end a tenancy, the owner must give the tenant a written notice of the grounds, give the PHA a copy of any eviction notice, and go to court. State and local law can add more steps or limits.

## Do source-of-income laws bind the buyer?

Yes, where those laws exist, they bind the buyer like any landlord. A source-of-income law bars a refusal to rent because a tenant pays with a voucher. The Poverty & Race Research Action Council (PRRAC) keeps a list of these laws. Its March 2026 update estimates they now cover "over 57% of voucher holders".

So a buyer who plans to "leave the program" may not be free to do so. In a covered city, refusing to renew because of the voucher can be a violation. Check the state and local law before you price the deal on market rents.

## Step-by-step for a seller

1. **Tell the tenant and the buyer.** Disclose the voucher tenancy before the sale.
2. **Ask the PHA for its change-of-ownership packet.** Do this when you sign the purchase contract.
3. **Send the notice of intent to sell** at least 30 days before closing, or by your PHA's deadline.
4. **Write the HAP proration** into the purchase contract.
5. **Transfer the security deposit** and the lease file to the buyer.
6. **Report any change in the closing date** to the PHA at once.
7. **Return any HAP** paid after closing, as the PHA tells you.

## Step-by-step for a buyer

1. **Check you qualify** under 24 CFR 982.306.
2. **Get the lease, HAP contract and last inspection report** from the seller.
3. **Send the PHA your documents** within 30 days of closing, or by your PHA's deadline.
4. **Set up direct deposit** in your name.
5. **Plan for a gap** in HAP until the assignment is done.

## What are the pros and cons at a glance?

The voucher program trades some control for a public payer. Here is the balance, with the rule or source behind each line.

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| The PHA pays its share directly, usually by direct deposit (Tacoma, Maricopa) | First payment can take up to 60 days (Tacoma, Maricopa) |
| The tenant cannot be evicted for the PHA's late payment, but the PHA must pay promptly (982.451) | Payments stop if repairs miss the deadline (982.404) |
| The PHA re-checks family income at least once a year (982.516) | Rent is capped at the reasonable rent (982.507) |
| Some PHAs pay bonuses, vacancy pay or damage cover (DSHA, Santa Cruz) | Inspections at least every two years, with 24-hour and 30-day fixes (982.405) |
| Incentives can offset setup costs (OIG, via NLIHC) | No rent increase in the first lease term (982.309) |
| | Eviction only for good cause during the lease (982.310) |
| | PHA can end the contract if funding runs short (982.454) |

## Is part of the rent guaranteed?

No. The PHA's share is reliable, but it is not guaranteed. Three rules show the limits:

1. **Repairs.** If you miss the 24-hour or 30-day repair deadline, the PHA must abate the HAP. It does not pay for the abated period (24 CFR 982.404(d)).
2. **Funding.** The PHA "may terminate the HAP contract" if funding "is insufficient to support continued assistance" (24 CFR 982.454).
3. **Changes.** The HAP amount "is subject to change during the HAP contract term" (24 CFR 982.451(b)(1)).

What you do get: the PHA "must pay the housing assistance payment promptly when due" (982.451(b)(5)). The tenant's share is the only part you collect from the tenant.

## How much time do paperwork and inspections take?

The setup takes weeks, and inspections recur. A real timeline from published PHA rules:

| Task | Time | Source |
|---|---|---|
| RFTA review | Within 14 days of a complete packet | Maricopa County |
| Inspection scheduling | Up to 24 days from RFTA receipt | Maricopa County |
| First payment | Up to 60 days | Tacoma, Maricopa |
| Periodic inspection | At least every 2 years (3 for small rural PHAs) | 24 CFR 982.405(b) |
| Repair after an inspection | 24 hours or 30 days | 24 CFR 982.404 |
| Rent increase notice to the PHA | At least 60 days before | 24 CFR 982.308(g)(4) |

Inspection rules are also in transition. PHAs must move from HQS to NSPIRE by February 1, 2027. HUD's 2024 notice also says PHAs "are struggling to recruit and retain private landlords."

## How do rent limits affect income?

Rent limits cap the top of your rent, not the bottom. Two caps apply:

- **Rent reasonableness.** The rent "may not exceed the reasonable rent" for comparable unassisted units (24 CFR 982.507(a)(4)). It also may not exceed what you charge unassisted tenants for comparable units in the premises.
- **The 40% rule at move-in.** If the gross rent is above the payment standard, the family share cannot exceed 40% of adjusted income (982.508). This limits rents for lower-income families in the first lease.

The payment standard itself is not a hard rent cap. You can charge above it, if the rent is reasonable. The family pays the difference. Here is a quick illustration with made-up numbers:

| Line | Unit A | Unit B |
|---|---|---|
| Rent to owner | $1,600 | $1,800 |
| Payment standard (owner pays all utilities) | $1,700 | $1,700 |
| Family TTP | $500 | $500 |
| HAP | $1,100 | $1,200 |
| Tenant pays you | $500 | $600 |

In Unit B, the family pays $100 above its TTP. At move-in, that only works if $600 is no more than 40% of its monthly adjusted income. ## What risks do landlords report?

The best public signal is what PHAs say about landlords. HUD's Inspector General (Memorandum 2021-LA-0803, summarized by NLIHC) found that the incentives PHAs rated most effective met common landlord concerns. Examples were streamlining the inspection process and a single point of contact. Direct deposit and landlord portals rated high among nonmonetary incentives.

That suggests the main pain points: slow inspections, slow payments and hard-to-reach staff. Ask about each before you sign:

- [ ] How long does your PHA take to inspect after an RFTA?
- [ ] How long until the first payment, and on what dates are payments made?
- [ ] Does it accept photos as proof of repairs?
- [ ] Is there a landlord liaison or portal?
- [ ] Does it offer incentives, such as Delaware's $1,000 signing bonus?
- [ ] Is your area covered by a source-of-income law?

## Who is the program a good fit for?

The program fits owners who already keep units in good repair and run on written processes. For them, inspections are a check, not a project. It fits less well if you need cash from the first month, or if you plan to raise rents or sell within the first lease term. Read the HAP contract and your PHA's landlord page before you decide.

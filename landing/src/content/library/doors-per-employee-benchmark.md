## What do NARPM and NAR data say about doors per employee?

The main benchmarks put a residential property management company at 40 to 59 doors per person. They do not agree exactly, because each one counts a different group of people.

| Benchmark | Figure | What it counts | Reported by |
|---|---|---|---|
| NARPM Financial Performance Guide 2022, average | 49.29 units per direct team member | Staff with 50%+ of time on owner or tenant work | LetHub |
| Same guide, top 25% of firms | 58.55 | Same | LetHub |
| Same guide, bottom 25% of firms | 41.27 | Same | LetHub |
| AppFolio Q3 2025 market update, US | 54 rental units per employee | All property management staff | LetHub |
| AppFolio, Salt Lake City / New Orleans | 35 / 77 | All staff, by city | LetHub |
| NAR 2023 Member Profile | median 40 properties per manager (45 for residential specialists) | Each manager's own portfolio | LetHub |

A note on sources: I could not open the NARPM guide or the AppFolio market update myself. Both are behind a member or report wall. All figures in the table come from LetHub's 2026-08-09 summary, which names each source. LetHub sells leasing software, so read its conclusions with that in mind. The numbers above are its citations, not its opinions.

## How do I calculate my own doors per employee?

Doors per employee is doors under management divided by full-time-equivalent (FTE) staff. Count part-time roles by hours: a 20-hour role is 0.5 FTE.

Calculate two ratios, so you can compare to both kinds of benchmark:

1. **Direct ratio:** count only people who spend most of their time on owners, tenants, leasing or maintenance. Compare to the NARPM 49.29 figure.
2. **All-staff ratio:** count everyone, back office included. Compare to the AppFolio 54 figure.

Worked example for a 300-door company:

| Role | FTE | Direct? |
|---|---|---|
| Property managers | 3 | Yes |
| Leasing agent | 1 | Yes |
| Maintenance coordinator | 1 | Yes |
| Bookkeeper | 1 | No |
| Part-time admin, 20 hours | 0.5 | No |

- Direct ratio: 300 ÷ 5 = 60 doors per direct team member. That is just above the 58.55 top-quarter figure.
- All-staff ratio: 300 ÷ 6.5 = 46 doors per employee. That is below the 54 US figure.

The same company looks strong on one benchmark and weak on the other. That is why you must match the count before you judge.

Three more things change your ratio:

- **Door density.** Doors far apart need more drive time per inspection and showing.
- **Property type.** 100 units in one building need fewer trips than 100 scattered houses.
- **Service level.** A company that does monthly inspections needs more people per door than one that inspects once a year.

Note these next to your ratio, so a later change in mix does not look like a change in staff output.

## How many doors per leasing agent?

No benchmark we could verify gives a separate leasing agent figure. The NAR and NARPM numbers mix leasing with other work.

A leasing agent's load depends on vacancies, not total doors. A 300-door company with 30% annual turnover has about 90 leases a year to fill. That is a rough 7 to 8 a month. Plan leasing staff from your own move-out count:

`Leases per month = doors × annual turnover rate ÷ 12`

Then track how many leases one agent closes in a month. LetHub's point holds here: the limit is hours spent on inquiry replies and showings. Self-guided tours and fast automated replies take those hours off the agent.

## How many doors per maintenance coordinator?

We found no public, verified benchmark for maintenance coordinators either. Plan from work orders, not from doors.

1. Count work orders for the last 12 months and divide by 12.
2. Track how many work orders one coordinator closes in a week, from intake to owner invoice.
3. Divide monthly work orders by one person's monthly capacity.

Example: 300 doors produce 150 work orders a month (example figure). If one coordinator closes 120 a month, you need 1.25 coordinators. Older buildings and slow vendors push the number up.

## How does software change the ratio?

Software raises doors per employee when it removes repeat tasks from people. It does not help when it adds a second place to type the same data.

Buildium's 2026 State of the Property Management Industry Report, summarized on Buildium's blog, found that the share of property management companies that use AI rose from 20% to 58% in one year. LetHub links the spread between the bottom quarter (41.27) and the top quarter (58.55) to two things: fast replies to inquiries and self-guided showings.

To test a tool, measure before and after:

- [ ] Record your direct ratio and all-staff ratio this month.
- [ ] Log hours per week on inquiry replies, showings and rent follow-up.
- [ ] Add the tool. Wait 90 days.
- [ ] Measure the same hours and ratios again.

What a better ratio is worth: one direct team member costs real money. The BLS median wage for administrative assistants alone was $48,310 in May 2025. If a tool lets 5 people handle the work of 6, the saving is at least one salary, minus the tool cost.

## What to do with your ratio

Below 40 doors per direct team member, look at process before you hire. Above 60, check service quality: slow replies and missed inspections are the first signs of a team at its limit.

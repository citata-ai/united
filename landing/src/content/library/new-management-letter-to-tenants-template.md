## What must the letter say about rent payment?

The letter must tell tenants exactly where to pay, how, and from which date. Rent sent to the wrong place is the most common problem in a handover. A clear payment section prevents most of it.

California shows what the law can require. Civil Code 1962 says the owner must disclose the name, phone number and address of the person who receives rent. If tenants can pay in person, the notice must give the usual days and hours. The owner may instead list a bank account within 5 miles of the property, or the details for electronic transfer. The notice must also state the forms of payment accepted.

The payment section of your letter needs:

- [ ] **The switch date.** "Pay your [month] rent and all later rent to us."
- [ ] **What to do before the switch date.** Keep paying the old manager as usual.
- [ ] **The new methods.** Portal link, ACH, check or money order, and the payee name.
- [ ] **Office hours** if tenants can pay in person.
- [ ] **Autopay.** Tell tenants to cancel autopay to the old manager and set it up again.
- [ ] **Late fees.** Say whether you will waive a late fee in the first month if a payment goes to the old manager by mistake.

## How do you handle the security deposit transfer?

Tell each tenant who holds the deposit now and how much it is. Tenants worry most about the deposit when management changes. A clear line in the letter stops that worry.

Steps for the new manager:

1. **Get the deposit list** from the old manager: tenant, unit, amount, date paid.
2. **Get the funds**, moved into your trust account.
3. **Match each deposit** with the lease and the tenant ledger.
4. **Tell each tenant** the amount you hold in the letter.
5. **Ask tenants to reply** within a set time if the amount is wrong.

California Civil Code 1950.5(i) covers the transfer. When the landlord's interest ends, the landlord may transfer the remaining deposit, after lawful deductions, to the successor. The landlord may instead return it to the tenant with an accounting.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do tenants submit maintenance requests now?

Give tenants one main way to submit requests and one number for emergencies. Then say what counts as an emergency, so the after-hours line is used for the right calls.

Include in the letter:

- **Portal or app link** for routine requests, with photos.
- **Emergency phone number**, 24 hours.
- **What is an emergency:** no heat in cold weather, active water leak, no water, gas smell, sewer backup, a door that will not lock.
- **Open requests:** "If you sent a request to the old manager that is not fixed, please send it to us again."
- **Entry notice:** how you will give notice before you enter.

## When should the letter go out?

Send the letter as soon as you have the signed agreement and the tenant list. Aim for at least 10 to 15 days before the next rent due date. Tenants need time to change autopay.

California sets a legal clock. Civil Code 1962(c) says a successor owner or manager must comply with the disclosure rules within 15 days. Until the successor complies, it may not serve a 3-day notice or evict for rent that accrued in that time. The tenant still owes the rent.

A practical handover timeline:

| Day | Step |
|---|---|
| 0 | Agreement signed. Ask the old manager for tenant list, ledgers and deposits. |
| 1–5 | Set up tenants in your software and portal. |
| 5–7 | Send the new management letter by mail and email. |
| 10 | Call or text tenants who have not opened the portal. |
| 15 | Deadline in California to meet Civil Code 1962. |
| First rent day | Check every unit: paid to you, paid to old manager, or unpaid. |

## Copy-ready new management letter to tenants

Copy the letter and fill in each bracket. Delete lines that do not apply.

> [Date]
>
> [Tenant full name(s)]
> [Property address, unit]
>
> **Your building has new management**
>
> Dear [Tenant name],
>
> From [start date], [Company name] manages [property address] for the owner, [owner name or entity]. Your lease does not change. All its terms stay the same.
>
> **Rent**
>
> - Pay your [month] rent and all later rent to [Company name].
> - Pay online at [portal link], or by [check / money order] made out to [payee name], mailed to [address].
> - Office hours for in-person payment: [days and hours].
> - If you use autopay with [old manager], please cancel it and set it up in our portal.
>
> **Security deposit**
>
> We hold your security deposit of $[amount]. If this amount is not correct, please tell us by [date].
>
> **Repairs**
>
> - Routine requests: [portal link] or [email].
> - Emergencies, 24 hours: [phone].
> - If you have an open request with [old manager], please send it to us again.
>
> **Contacts**
>
> - Manager: [name], [phone], [email], [office address]
> - Person who accepts legal notices for the owner: [name], [address]
>
> We look forward to working with you.
>
> Sincerely,
> [Name, title]
> [Company name, license number if required]

This is a generic template, not a state legal form. Check your lease and local law before you use it.

## How do you check that the letter worked?

Check 3 numbers after the first rent day: units paid to you, units paid to the old manager, and units unpaid. Follow up on the last two the same week. Credit any rent the old manager forwards on the date the old manager received it.

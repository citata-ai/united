## The 6 steps at a glance

Choosing a property manager takes 6 steps and about 2 weeks. The goal is a short list of 3, compared on paper, not on charm.

| Step | Do this | Time |
|------|---------|------|
| 1 | Build a list of 5 to 8 managers | 1 hour |
| 2 | Check license and reviews; cut to 3 | 2 hours |
| 3 | Ask each for the agreement and fee schedule | 1 email each |
| 4 | Interview each for 30 to 45 minutes | 3 calls |
| 5 | Score them side by side | 1 hour |
| 6 | Read the agreement line by line, then sign | 1 hour |

## Where do I find property managers to compare?

You find property managers in 5 places. Use at least 3, because each source shows a different slice of the market.

1. **Google search and Maps.** Search "property management [your city]" and "single family property management [your city]".
2. **AI answers.** Ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Google AI Mode the same question. Write down the names and the sources they link.
3. **Review sites and directories.** Yelp, Google reviews and directories such as Expertise.com.
4. **NARPM.** The National Association of Residential Property Managers has a find-a-manager search on its site and offers designations such as RMP, MPM and CRMC.
5. **Referrals.** Ask other owners, a local agent or your HOA.

Our own test shows why one source is not enough. We asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude for the best property management company in the 50 largest US cities (150 answers, 2026-10-19). The engines named 8.8 companies per answer, and 76% of the names were local firms. But they took most names from a few lists: Expertise.com was a source in 93 of 150 answers, Yelp in 50 and iPropertyManagement.com in 50. A good local manager that is not on those lists may never show up.

AI answers also change. We asked the same 150 questions again on 2026-11-02. Only 58% of the names came back, and 3 of 150 answers gave the same list. So ask 2 engines, ask twice, and add every name to your list. See the full data in our [AI Property Manager Index](/research/ai-property-manager-index-2026).

## How do I check license and reviews?

Check the license first, on your state's official lookup. Then read reviews with a filter: owner reviews count more than tenant reviews for this decision.

License check:

- **Find the rule.** Many states treat renting property for others as real estate brokerage. Florida's statute 475.01 defines a broker to include a person who, for pay, rents or offers to rent real property of others. Other states have their own rules and exceptions.
- **Look up the company and the person in charge.** Use the state real estate commission's license search.
- **Check status and discipline.** Active, expired, or with a complaint record.

Review check:

| Read for | Good sign | Bad sign |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| Owner reviews | Specific: "statements on time, fixed a leak in 1 day" | Late payouts, surprise fees |
| Tenant reviews | Calm replies from the manager | Many unanswered repair complaints |
| Replies | The company answers bad reviews with facts | Angry or no replies |
| Dates | Recent reviews in the last 12 months | All reviews are years old |

Tenant complaints matter to you too. A tenant who cannot get a repair done often leaves. That costs you a vacancy and a leasing fee.

## What should I compare across three managers?

Compare cost, process and terms, using the same rent for all three. A scorecard makes the gaps clear.

Worked scorecard for a $2,000 house. The managers and their terms are made-up examples.

| Item | Manager A | Manager B | Manager C |
|------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Monthly fee | 8% of collected rent | $120 flat | 10% of collected rent |
| Leasing fee | 75% of 1 month | $1,000 | 50% of 1 month |
| Renewal fee | $200 | $0 | $250 |
| Setup fee | $0 | $250 | $150 |
| Year 1 cost, new tenant | $3,420 | $2,690 | $3,550 |
| Repair approval limit | $300 | $500 | $250 |
| Average days to lease (their number) | 21 | 30 | 18 |
| Written screening criteria | Yes | No | Yes |
| Trust account for deposits | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Exit notice and fee | 30 days, $0 | 60 days, $500 | 30 days, $0 |

How to read it:

- **Cost.** B is cheapest on paper. But B has no written screening criteria and the slowest leasing.
- **Speed.** Each extra week of vacancy costs about $460 at $2,000 rent.
- **Exit.** B's $500 exit fee is a cost you may pay later.

## What fees are normal in 2026?

Compare each quote with a market average before you score it. iPropertyManagement surveyed advertised fees in 80 metro areas (updated 2026-09-23):

| Fee | Average | Range found |
|-----|---------|-------------|
| Monthly fee, % of rent | 8.49% | 3.75% to 14.0% |
| Monthly fee, flat | $101.04 per unit | $49 to $250 |
| Tenant placement, with management | 70.6% of 1 month's rent | 6% to 125% |
| Setup fee | $185.24 | — |
| Lease renewal fee | $211.92 | — |
| Inspection | $106.72 | — |

A quote far below the average often moves the cost into other fees. A quote far above it must buy something you can see, such as faster leasing or a lower repair markup. Ask the manager to explain each gap in writing.

## Which red flags should stop me?

Some red flags should end the talk at once. Others are worth a direct question.

Stop at once:

- **No license** where your state requires one.
- **No written fee schedule**, or fees that change between calls.
- **No separate trust account** for rent and deposits.
- **Pressure to sign today.**
- **A guarantee of rent or zero vacancy** without a clear contract term.

Ask and judge the answer:

- **A long agreement term** with a large early-exit fee.
- **Maintenance markups** with no cap.
- **Owner reviews about late payouts.**
- **Vague answers** on screening: "we just know a good tenant."

Screening must follow fair housing law. Ask to see the written criteria. The same rules should apply to every applicant.

## How do I read the management agreement before I sign?

Read the management agreement for 6 items. Mark each one before you sign.

1. **Term and renewal.** How long, and does it renew by itself?
2. **Exit.** Notice period, fee, and who keeps the tenant if you leave.
3. **Fees.** Every fee from the fee schedule, in the contract.
4. **Repair limit.** The dollar amount above which they must ask you.
5. **Money.** When you get paid each month, and where deposits sit.
6. **Reports.** What you get each month, and access to an owner portal.

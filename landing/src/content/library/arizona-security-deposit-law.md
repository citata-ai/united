Arizona puts most of its deposit rules in one short statute: A.R.S. 33-1321. We read the current text on the Arizona Legislature site on 2026-10-19. This table holds each rule, the limit and the subsection.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | 1.5 months' rent, prepaid rent included | A.R.S. 33-1321(A) |
| Nonrefundable fees | Purpose must be in writing, or the fee is refundable | A.R.S. 33-1321(B) |
| Move-in paperwork | Signed lease, move-in damage form, notice of right to attend move-out inspection | A.R.S. 33-1321(C) |
| Return deadline | 14 days, excluding weekends and legal holidays | A.R.S. 33-1321(D) |
| Itemized list | Required, mailed first class with any refund | A.R.S. 33-1321(D) |
| Tenant dispute window | 60 days after the list is mailed | A.R.S. 33-1321(D) |
| Penalty | Money due plus twice the amount wrongly withheld | A.R.S. 33-1321(E) |
| Interest or separate account | No rule in the statute | A.R.S. 33-1321 |

## What is the maximum security deposit in Arizona?

The maximum security deposit in Arizona is 1.5 months' rent. A.R.S. 33-1321(A) says a landlord may not demand or receive security "however denominated" above that amount. Prepaid rent counts toward the cap.

The name of the charge does not matter. A "move-in deposit", a "pet deposit" and last month's rent all count as security if they are refundable. One exception: a tenant may choose to pay more than 1.5 months' rent in advance.

Worked example for a $1,800 unit:

1. **Find the cap.** $1,800 × 1.5 = $2,700.
2. **Add up all security.** A $1,800 deposit plus a $500 refundable pet deposit is $2,300.
3. **Check the room left.** $2,700 − $2,300 = $400. A prepaid last month's rent of $1,800 would put you over the cap, unless the tenant offers it.

Nonrefundable fees follow a separate rule. A.R.S. 33-1321(B) says the landlord must state the purpose of each nonrefundable fee or deposit in writing. If the lease does not mark a fee as nonrefundable, the fee is refundable.

## How many days does an Arizona landlord have to return a security deposit?

An Arizona landlord has 14 days to return a security deposit, not counting Saturdays, Sundays or other legal holidays. In practice, that is about 3 calendar weeks. A.R.S. 33-1321(D) starts the clock only when 3 things are true:

- The tenancy has ended.
- The tenant has given back possession.
- The tenant has asked for the deposit.

The landlord must mail the itemized list and any refund by first-class mail to the tenant's last known address. The tenant and landlord can agree in writing on another method.

Abandonment is different. Under A.R.S. 33-1370(B), when a landlord retakes an abandoned unit after the notice steps in that section, the deposit is forfeited and goes to accrued rent and other reasonable costs from the abandonment.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Arizona?

An Arizona landlord can deduct 3 kinds of costs, per A.R.S. 33-1321(D):

1. **Unpaid rent.** All rent due.
2. **Lease charges.** Charges named in the signed lease, or allowed by the landlord-tenant act, subject to the landlord's duty to mitigate.
3. **Damage.** The landlord's damages from the tenant's failure to meet A.R.S. 33-1341.

A.R.S. 33-1341 lists the tenant's duties. The tenant must keep the unit clean and safe, use appliances and systems in a reasonable manner, and not destroy, deface or damage the premises. The tenant must also tell the landlord in writing about repairs that the landlord needs to make.

Arizona's deposit statute does not use the phrase "normal wear and tear". The test in the text is a breach of A.R.S. 33-1341. A move-in form helps you show which damage was new. A.R.S. 33-1321(C) makes the form a duty: give it at move-in, with a signed copy of the lease. Also give written notice that the tenant may attend the move-out inspection.

## Does an Arizona landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. An Arizona landlord must give an itemized list of all deductions, with the amount due to the tenant, if any. A.R.S. 33-1321(D) sets the same 14-day deadline for the list and the refund.

Use this checklist for each move-out:

- [ ] Note the date the tenant gave back the keys and the date of the deposit request.
- [ ] Count 14 days, skipping Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays.
- [ ] List each deduction with a dollar amount and a short reason.
- [ ] Attach photos and invoices to your file. The statute does not require them, but they help in a dispute.
- [ ] Mail the list and the check by first-class mail, unless the tenant agreed in writing to another method.
- [ ] Keep proof of the mailing date. The tenant's 60-day dispute window starts on that date.

The 60-day window protects you. Under A.R.S. 33-1321(D), if the tenant does not dispute the list within 60 days after you mail it, the list becomes final. Further claims by the tenant are waived.

## Must an Arizona landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

No. A.R.S. 33-1321 has no rule that requires interest on a deposit or a separate bank account. Subsection G says the landlord may use refundable deposits during the tenancy as the property management agreement allows. At the end of the tenancy, the landlord must refund them under the same section.

Subsection H adds a rule for a sale. The owner of the landlord's interest at the end of the tenancy is bound by the deposit rules. If you buy a rental with tenants in place, get the deposit records at closing.

## What is the penalty for an Arizona landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

The penalty is the money due plus damages equal to twice the amount wrongly withheld. A.R.S. 33-1321(E) gives the tenant this remedy when the landlord does not follow subsection D.

Worked example: a landlord keeps $1,000 with no valid itemized list. The tenant can recover the $1,000 plus $2,000 in damages, for $3,000 in total. Subsection F adds that other damages under the act are still possible.

A second rule applies after a lockout. A.R.S. 33-1367 says if the tenant ends the lease after an unlawful ouster or utility shutoff, the landlord must return all security recoverable under 33-1321.

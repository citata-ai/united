## Why does SEO still matter for owner leads?

SEO still matters because Google sends the largest share of owner leads. Goodjuju tracked 35 property management companies from July 8 to October 5, 2026. Google Search and the Map Pack brought 50.6% of their owner leads: 639 of 1,262. Google delivered about 15 times more owner leads than all AI tools together.

| Lead source (fall 2026) | Share of traffic | Share of owner leads | Conversion rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search & Map Pack | 37.9% | 50.6% | 1.2% |
| Direct / Maps | 45.0% | 35.6% | 0.7% |
| Other search | 3.2% | 4.3% | 1.2% |
| Referral | 3.0% | 3.7% | 1.1% |
| AI tools | 0.8% | 3.4% | 3.9% |
| Paid search | 5.3% | 1.2% | 0.2% |
| Social media | 4.3% | 0.8% | 0.2% |

Source: Goodjuju Property Management Lead Source Report 2026, fall update. Only 6 of the 35 companies ran paid search in that window, so the paid row is not a benchmark.

Search demand is not stable, though. For our client Fridman Properties in Chicago, Keyword Planner showed a 46% yearly drop in searches for 7 generic property management terms in the Chicago area, and 43% in the US (our data). The cause is a hypothesis. Fridman's owner is CEO of MyRemotely, our partner, so this is a related party. Read more in [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping).

## Which pages rank for owner searches?

The pages that rank for owner searches are pages built for one owner question each. An owner types "property management Oak Park", "property management fees Chicago" or "how much rent can I get for my house". Each search needs a page that answers it.

| Page | Owner search it targets | Must hold |
|---|---|---|
| Home page | "[company name]", "property management [main city]" | What you manage, where, fee range, a form |
| Owner services | "full service property management [city]" | Each service, what is included, what costs extra |
| Fees | "property management fees [city]" | A full fee table in numbers |
| City pages | "property management [suburb]" | Local facts, doors you manage there, local rules |
| Rental analysis | "how much rent can I get" | A form and a sample report |
| Reviews | "[company name] reviews" | Owner and tenant reviews, with replies |
| Niche pages | "Section 8 property management [city]" | Only if you do this work every week |

Google's SEO Starter Guide says a good title is "unique to the page, clear and concise". Give each page its own title with the city and the service.

Do not build one page per suburb with only the name swapped. Google's spam policies say "doorway abuse is when sites or pages are created to rank for specific, similar search queries." Build a city page only where you can say something true and local.

## How do local SEO and organic SEO differ?

Local SEO ranks your Google Business Profile in the map pack. Organic SEO ranks your website pages in the normal results. Owners see both on one results page, and the two lean on each other.

| | Local SEO | Organic SEO |
|---|---|---|
| What ranks | Google Business Profile | Website pages |
| Where it shows | Map pack, Google Maps | Blue links below the map |
| Main factors (Google) | Relevance, distance, prominence | Relevance and quality of the page |
| Main work | Category, services, reviews, photos, matching name and phone | Owner pages, titles, content, links, speed |
| Limit | Distance: weak far from your office | Can rank in any city you write a strong page for |

Google's local ranking help page says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity". Prominence uses "how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have." Google also says "there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking."

So reviews and links help both sides. Distance is the one factor you cannot change without a real office.

## How much content does a property management site need?

A property management site needs fewer pages than most agencies sell, but each page must be strong. Start with the 6 core pages in the table above, plus one page per city you really serve. A 300-door manager in 4 suburbs needs about 10 owner pages before any blog post.

Then add articles in this order:

1. **Local owner questions.** Rental license rules, security deposit law in your state, typical rents in your city.
2. **Fee and cost questions.** "Is a 10% fee normal in [city]", "what does a lease-up fee cover".
3. **Decision questions.** "Rent or sell my house in [city]", "self-manage or hire a manager".
4. **Tenant pages.** Only where they cut calls (how to pay rent, how to report a repair). They seldom bring owner leads.

One checked, local article a month beats four generic ones. Google's spam policies list "scaled content abuse": many pages made "for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings", including AI pages "without adding value for users".

## How do I measure SEO in signed doors?

Measure SEO in signed doors: owner leads from Google, then the share that signed. Traffic and rankings are steps on the way, not the result.

Here is a worked example. Goodjuju's fall 2026 benchmark puts the middle company at 928 visitors and 7 owner leads a month, or 8.9 leads per 1,000 visitors.

| Step | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly visitors | 928 | Goodjuju benchmark, median |
| Owner leads per month | 7 | Goodjuju benchmark, median |
| Share of leads from Google Search & Map Pack | 50.6% | Goodjuju lead source report, fall |
| Google owner leads per month | about 3.5 | 7 x 50.6% |
| Close rate | your number | your CRM |
| Signed doors from SEO per month | 3.5 x your close rate x doors per owner | your CRM |

To get the numbers:

1. **Mark key events.** In Google Analytics 4, mark the owner form submit and the call click as key events. Google calls a key event "an action that's particularly important to the success of your business."
2. **Tag the source.** Store the first source of each lead in your CRM: Google organic, Map Pack, direct, referral, AI.
3. **Count signed doors.** Each month, count the doors that signed from each source.
4. **Compare with cost.** Divide your SEO spend by signed doors. Put your fee into our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to see what one door is worth.

If a report from your agency shows traffic but no leads and no signed doors, ask for the missing rows.

Maine's eviction law is in Title 14, chapter 709, "Entry and Detainer." I read the sections on the Maine Legislature site on 2026-10-19. The page footer said the data was extracted on 10/05/2026. The core of the process is a 7-day or 30-day notice, then a forcible entry and detainer (FED) case.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| No-cause end of tenancy at will | At least 30 days' written notice | § 6002 |
| Rent 7+ days in arrears | 7 days' written notice | § 6002(1)(C) |
| Other 7-day causes | Substantial damage, nuisance, violence, unauthorized occupant | § 6002(1) |
| Pay before 7 days expire | Notice void | § 6002(1) |
| Pay arrears + fees before writ | Tenancy at will reinstated | § 6002(1) |
| Service after 3 good-faith tries | Mail and leave at the home | § 6002, § 6004 |
| Court | District Court | § 6003 |
| Hearing | No later than 10 days after the return day | § 6003 |
| Writ of possession | Issued 7 calendar days after judgment | § 6005 |
| Tenant leaves | Within 48 hours after service of the writ | § 6005 |
| Illegal eviction | Actual damages or $250, plus fees | § 6014 |

## What notice must a Maine landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

A Maine landlord gives 7 days' written notice once the tenant "is 7 days or more in arrears in the payment of rent" (14 M.R.S. § 6002(1)(C)).

The notice must:

- **State the ground.** Name the specific cause (§ 6002(2)).
- **State the amount.** Give the rent that is 7 or more days in arrears as of the notice date (§ 6002(2)(A)(1)).
- **Quote the payment notice.** Include the statutory text that tells the tenant that paying before the notice expires voids it, and how reinstatement works later (§ 6002(2)(A)(2)).
- **Tell of the right to contest.** Every termination notice must say the tenant may contest it in court (§ 6002).

You may combine a 30-day notice and a 7-day notice in one notice (§ 6002). Payment or written assurance of payment through general assistance counts the same as cash.

Since PL 2023, c. 594, the chapter defines rent as money paid on a recurring basis to meet the lease. It excludes fees and penalties (§ 6000(1-B)). So count only rent in the arrears amount.

## How long does an eviction take in Maine?

A Maine eviction for unpaid rent often runs several weeks. The statute clocks below are a worked example, not a promise:

| Step | Days | Running total |
|---|---|---|
| 7-day notice | 7 | 7 |
| File and serve summons and complaint | Varies | 7+ |
| Hearing no later than 10 days after return day | up to 10 after return day | 3 to 4 weeks |
| Writ issued 7 calendar days after judgment | 7 | 4 to 5 weeks |
| Tenant leaves within 48 hours of service | 2 | 4 to 6 weeks |

The return day comes from the summons and court rules, not chapter 709. Mediation can add time (§ 6004-A). A tenant can also appeal (§ 6008).

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Maine, and what does it cost?

A Maine landlord files in the District Court, which "shall have jurisdiction of cases of forcible entry and detainer" (§ 6003).

The landlord starts the case and serves it like other civil actions (§ 6004(1)). If 3 good-faith tries on 3 different days fail, the landlord may mail the papers first class and leave them at the tenant's home, then file an affidavit.

For a residential tenant, attach the judicial branch's 1 to 2 page form notice to the summons and complaint (§ 6004(2)). It explains the process, rental help, legal aid and mediation.

The filing fee is set by the court, not in chapter 709. Check the Maine Judicial Branch fee schedule before you file.

## Can a Maine landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes, by tenancy type.

**Tenancy at will, no cause.** Give at least 30 days' written notice (§ 6002). If the tenant paid rent through a later date, the notice must expire on or after that date.

**Tenancy at will, cause.** Section 6002(1) allows 7 days' notice if the landlord can show:

- substantial damage the tenant has not repaired,
- a nuisance or a violation of law about the tenancy,
- rent 7 or more days in arrears,
- domestic violence, sexual assault or stalking against another tenant,
- violence or threats against another tenant, a guest or the landlord, or
- an occupant who is not authorized.

**Written lease.** FED may be brought at the end or forfeiture of the lease term (§ 6001(1)). If the lease has no termination or notice language for a breach, use the 7-day rules of § 6002 (§ 6001(1-B)).

**Retaliation.** A case is presumed retaliatory if, within 6 months before filing, the tenant used rights under listed sections or made a good-faith code or repair complaint (§ 6001(3)).

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Maine?

A sheriff or constable serves the writ of possession. "Seven calendar days after the judgment is entered, the court shall issue the writ of possession" (§ 6005).

If the tenant does not leave within 48 hours of service, the tenant "is deemed a trespasser," and goods left behind are treated as abandoned under § 6013. In a rent case, no writ may issue if the tenant paid the amount needed to reinstate the tenancy under § 6002.

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Maine?

No. Section 6014(1) says evictions done "without resort to the provisions of this chapter are illegal and against public policy." Illegal evictions include:

- cutting any utility, such as water, heat, electricity or gas, except for repairs or emergencies;
- denying the tenant access to the unit, other than by court process;
- holding the tenant's property, other than by court process.

The court awards the tenant actual damages or $250, whichever is greater, plus costs and reasonable attorney fees (§ 6014(2)).

## Maine eviction checklist

- Pick the right notice: 30 days, or 7 days for a listed cause.
- In a rent notice, state the arrears and quote the statutory payment text.
- Tell the tenant of the right to contest in court.
- Make 3 good-faith service tries before you mail and leave the notice.
- File in District Court and attach the court's form notice.
- Wait 7 calendar days after judgment for the writ.
- Let a sheriff or constable serve it.

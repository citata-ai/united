## What does one door cost me to manage each month?

Your cost per door is your total monthly business cost divided by your door count. It is the floor for your fee. A fee below it loses money on every door, no matter how many doors you add.

Use 12 months of real costs. One month hides seasonal work, like spring turnovers and year-end 1099s. Count every cost the business pays:

- **Payroll.** Wages, payroll taxes, benefits and your own salary.
- **Software.** Property management software, screening, e-signature and payment fees you do not pass on.
- **Insurance.** General liability, errors and omissions, and auto.
- **Office and vehicles.** Rent, phones, fuel and mileage.
- **Marketing.** Website, ads, listing fees and directory profiles.
- **Professional fees.** Accounting and legal.

Two public numbers help you check your estimate. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics gives a median annual pay of $69,990 for property, real estate and community association managers (May 2025). AppFolio Core starts at $1.49 per unit per month, with a 50-unit minimum and a minimum spend. Buildium Growth starts at $192 per month (both pricing pages, read 2026-10-19).

## A worked example: 150 doors

This example uses one real wage figure and example numbers for the rest. Replace each line with your own costs.

| Cost line | Monthly cost | How we got it |
|---|---|---|
| 2 staff at the BLS median | $11,665 | 2 × $69,990 / 12 |
| Payroll taxes and benefits | Your number | Not in the example |
| Software | $192 | Buildium Growth starting price |
| Insurance, office, marketing, other | $3,000 | Example only |
| **Total** | **$14,857** | Sum of known lines |
| **Cost per door (150 doors)** | **$99.05** | $14,857 / 150 |

Now add a margin. The formula is fee = cost per door / (1 - margin).

- At a 10% margin: $99.05 / 0.90 = $110.06 per door.
- At a 20% margin: $99.05 / 0.80 = $123.81 per door.
- At a 30% margin: $99.05 / 0.70 = $141.50 per door.

To turn the dollar fee into a percent, divide by rent. At $1,500 rent, $123.81 is 8.25%. At $1,000 rent, it is 12.4%. So the same cost per door needs a higher percent on cheaper doors.

## How do I find the local market fee range?

You find the local fee range by reading local managers' published fee pages. State averages help as a check, but owners compare you with the 3 to 5 managers they find in search and AI answers.

Steps:

1. **List 10 or more local managers.** Use Google Maps, Yelp, Expertise.com and ChatGPT for "property management [city]".
2. **Open each fee page.** Write down the monthly rate, its base (collected rent or rent due) and each add-on fee.
3. **Mark the ones with no public fees.** Note them as "quote only".
4. **Find the median rate** and the range.
5. **Compute an effective rate.** Add leasing, renewal and other fees over a typical year, then divide by yearly rent.

State samples from iPropertyManagement (updated 2026-09-23) show how much markets differ:

| State sample | Average percent fee | Average flat fee |
|---|---|---|
| California (143 locations) | 7.44% | $111.61 |
| Arizona (47 locations) | 8.92% | $85.42 |
| Georgia (27 locations) | 8.03% | $101.14 |
| Florida (67 locations) | 9.04% | $107.53 |
| National (722 branches) | 8.49% | $101.04 |

The California sample shows a low percent but a high flat fee. High rents explain part of that: a low percent of a high rent is still a large dollar amount.

## What margin should my fee include?

We found no reliable public benchmark for property management profit margins, so we do not give one. Set a target that pays for growth, bad months and your own time. Then test it against the local range.

Three checks help:

- **Your fee is below the local median and your margin is thin.** You are likely under-priced. Raise the fee or cut cost per door.
- **Your fee is above the local median.** You need a clear reason owners can see: faster leasing, a guarantee, better reports or a narrow niche.
- **Your margin depends on add-on fees.** Owners notice. Read the effective-rate step above and show the total on your site.

## How do I price a low-rent door?

Price a low-rent door with a minimum monthly fee. A common form is "the greater of X% of collected rent or $Y per month". The work on a $900 door is close to the work on a $1,800 door: the same calls, inspections and statements.

Worked example with the 150-door cost above. Cost per door is $99.05. At 8% of a $900 rent, the fee is $72. That door loses $27.05 a month, or $324.60 a year. A $110 minimum fee covers its cost at a 10% margin.

Low-rent doors can also bring more turnover and more repair calls. Track hours per door for 3 months before you set the minimum. Then price from data, not from habit.

## When should I walk away from a door?

Walk away from a door when your minimum fee cannot cover its cost, or when the owner blocks the work the law needs. A losing door also takes staff time from the doors that pay.

Checklist before you sign:

- [ ] The fee covers cost per door at your target margin.
- [ ] The owner agrees to fund the repair reserve.
- [ ] The owner accepts habitability repairs and safety work.
- [ ] The owner accepts your screening rules, applied the same way to every applicant under fair housing law.
- [ ] The property is inside your service area, so drive time does not eat the fee.
- [ ] The owner accepts your agreement terms without side deals.

If 2 or more boxes stay empty, decline in a kind, clear message. A short "we are not the right fit" keeps your reputation and your margin. Fewer owner leads make each yes more costly; read [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping).

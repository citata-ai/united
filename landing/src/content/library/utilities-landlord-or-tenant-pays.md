## Which utilities are usually in the tenant's name?

Utilities with a meter for each unit usually go in the tenant's name. That means electric and gas in most single-family homes and in many apartments with separate meters. Internet, cable and streaming are almost always the tenant's choice and the tenant's bill.

The split below is a common starting point. Your city, your meters and your market change it.

| Utility | Single-family, own meters | Small multifamily, separate meters | Building with one shared meter |
|---------|---------------------------|------------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Electric | Tenant | Tenant | Owner (common areas always owner) |
| Gas / heat | Tenant | Tenant | Owner |
| Water and sewer | Owner or tenant, by city rules | Owner, often billed back | Owner |
| Trash | Varies by city | Owner | Owner |
| Internet / cable | Tenant | Tenant | Tenant |
| Lawn and snow | Lease decides | Owner | Owner |

Rule of thumb: if you cannot meter it per unit, the owner pays and prices it into rent. If you can meter it, the tenant usually pays.

## Why does water often stay with the owner?

Water often stays with the owner because the water bill can follow the property, not the person. A city water department sells a service to an address. If a tenant leaves without paying, many cities can collect from the owner. They use a lien (a legal claim against the property) or the property tax bill.

The rules differ a lot by state. Three examples:

| Place | Rule | Source |
|-------|------|--------|
| Iowa | Unpaid city water, sewer and trash charges are a lien on the property once certified to the county treasurer. A rental where the tenant has a separate water meter and pays the city directly is exempt, if the landlord gives the utility written notice. The utility may require a deposit of up to 90 days of usual water cost. | Iowa Code 384.84(4)(a) and (4)(d) |
| Ohio | A city may certify unpaid water charges to the county auditor, and the amount becomes a lien on the property. The tax-list route applies when the service contract was made directly with an owner who lives at the property. The city may also sue an owner, tenant or other person who owes the charges. | Ohio Rev. Code 743.04(A)(1) |
| La Crosse, Wisconsin | The city says putting the bill in the tenant's name "does not remove your responsibility." On November 15, unpaid balances go on the property tax roll and become the landlord's responsibility. | City of La Crosse Utilities |

So in La Crosse, a tenant-named water account still leaves the owner at risk. In Iowa, a landlord who files the right notice can avoid the lien for water usage. Ohio's tax-list route turns on whether an owner-occupant signed for service. Many owners keep water in their own name for this reason: they see the bill each month instead of finding out at tax time.

## Can unpaid tenant water become a lien?

Unpaid tenant water can become a lien in many cities, and the details decide your risk. Read your own city's utility rules before you sign the next lease.

Iowa Code 384.84 shows what a careful process looks like:

1. **Notice before the lien.** The utility must mail written notice of intent to certify a lien at least 30 days before certification. If the account is in a tenant's name and the landlord asked in writing, the landlord also gets the notice.
2. **The rental exemption.** For a separately metered unit where the tenant pays the city, the landlord files a written notice. It names the tenant, the address and the date occupancy begins.
3. **Tenant changes.** For a residential rental, a new notice is due within 30 business days of each tenant change.
4. **Repairs are different.** The exemption does not cover delinquent charges for repairs to the water service.

La Crosse gives landlords a shorter list. Request delinquency notices and duplicate bills in writing. Give the utility a forwarding address within 21 days after a tenant moves out.

Watch for: a tenant who falls behind on water often falls behind on rent too. Delinquency notices give you an early signal.

## How do I write utilities into the lease?

Write utilities into the lease as a list, not a sentence. Each utility needs 4 facts: who pays, whose name is on the account, when the transfer happens, and what happens on a lapse.

Sample clause to adapt:

> Utilities. Tenant shall place the following in Tenant's name, effective on the lease start date, and pay them in full when due: electric, gas, internet. Landlord shall pay: water, sewer, trash. Tenant shall reimburse Landlord for water and sewer at the amount billed by the city, due with the next rent payment. Tenant shall not let any utility in Tenant's name be shut off. Tenant shall give Landlord proof of final payment at move-out.

Then add 3 habits:

- **Confirm the transfer.** On move-in day, call each utility or check the portal to confirm the tenant's account starts.
- **Read the meters.** Photograph each meter with the date at move-in and move-out.
- **Never use a shutoff as pressure.** Cutting a utility to force a tenant out is a self-help eviction. In California, Civil Code 789.3 makes the landlord liable for actual damages plus up to $100 per day, with a $250 minimum.

Before you bill tenants for a shared meter, for example with RUBS (splitting a shared bill by a formula), check your state and city rules.

## What does each choice cost the owner?

Keeping a utility in your name costs you money and time. It also gives you control. Moving it to the tenant saves admin but adds the risks above.

| Choice | Owner's upside | Owner's risk |
|--------|----------------|--------------|
| Utility in tenant's name | No monthly bill, no bill-back work | Lien risk for water in some cities, frozen pipes if heat is cut |
| Utility in owner's name, included in rent | Simple for the tenant, owner sees usage | Tenant has no reason to save water or power |
| Utility in owner's name, billed back | Owner controls the account, tenant pays for use | Admin time, state rules on bill-backs |

Worked example: a duplex has one water meter and a $140 monthly water and sewer bill. Billed back 50/50, each unit pays $70. Included in rent instead, you would add about $70 per unit to the rent to cover it. Over 12 months that is $1,680 for the building that you either collect or absorb.

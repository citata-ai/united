## How do Rent Manager and AppFolio price their plans?

Rent Manager requires a quote, while AppFolio publishes starting per-unit rates. Those rates do not give your final monthly bill. AppFolio states that minimum spend applies. Rent Manager describes Basic, Plus and Premium bundles, but shows no dollar amount on the page reviewed.

Give both sales teams the same portfolio file. Include active units, property types, staff roles and bank accounts. Add your expected rent payments, signed documents and service calls. Ask each team to price the same work.

Separate the base plan from payment fees and optional services. Ask which parts need separate contracts. Record whether vacant units remain billable. Also ask how the bill falls when an owner leaves.

Keep the quote beside the order form. A sales email can explain a feature, but signed terms should state the price. For another plan comparison, see our [AppFolio and Buildium guide](/guides/appfolio-vs-buildium).

## Which has more accounting depth?

Accounting depth depends on the records your team must keep and correct. Rent Manager lists a complete accounting system, custom reports and custom workflows in Basic. AppFolio lists property accounting in Core and advanced budgeting and accounting in Plus. These are vendor claims, not proof that either fits your books.

Use a closed accounting period for the demo. Remove names and bank details first. Ask the presenter to post rent, allocate an invoice and make an owner distribution. Then ask them to reverse a returned payment.

Watch the correction path. Can staff see who changed a record? Can the accountant trace the owner statement back to the source entry? Ask for exported reports, not screenshots alone.

A tool passes when your accountant can explain the balances without a separate repair spreadsheet. Give the accountant a chance to operate the system. A polished sales demonstration can hide a hard daily process.

## Which has a better mobile app?

Mobile fit depends on your field tasks, device and connection. Rent Manager's mobile page describes rmAppSuite Pro for inspections, service issues and communication. Its pricing page places the app in Plus. AppFolio lists mobility in Core, but that label does not prove desktop feature parity.

Ask a field manager to test the same vacant property. Find the unit, add a photo and record an issue. Attach the issue to the right property and send a vendor message. Then check the office record.

Try poor reception before you approve the tool. Ask what happens to photos or notes when a connection drops. Check whether the app clearly marks an upload that failed.

Use actual staff phones where possible. A task that works on a large demo screen may feel slow at a doorway. Choose based on completed work and clear records, rather than the appearance of the app.

## Which suits a portfolio above a hundred units?

Either can suit a growing portfolio if the workflows and full costs fit. A larger unit count does not choose the software for you. Scattered rental homes create different field work from a single building. Mixed properties can also need different lease and report fields.

Assign responsibility before you buy. Decide who owns accounting setup, training and report design. Custom workflows need an internal owner who keeps them current. Built-in workflows still need staff who understand exceptions.

Build a short acceptance sheet:

- **Accounting:** reconcile a closed period and explain the owner balances.
- **Leasing:** move a prospect through the steps your team uses.
- **Maintenance:** route a request and close it with an invoice.
- **Reporting:** export the records your owners and accountant need.
- **Access:** restrict each staff role to the work it performs.

Ask for every failed item to be resolved before launch. A feature promise is not the same as a working configuration. Budget staff training alongside the subscription.

## What do Reddit users say about each?

Reddit comments can suggest useful questions, but they do not establish a winner. We have not verified a representative sample of Reddit users for this comparison. We therefore make no claim about broad user sentiment or review scores.

When you read a thread, record the writer's role and software tier. Ask whether the complaint concerns current software or an old version. A bookkeeper and a leasing agent may judge the same product differently.

Turn each concern into a demo task. A comment about poor reports becomes a request for an owner packet. A comment about support becomes a question about escalation terms. Do not turn an isolated complaint into a product fact.

Ask each vendor for a reference with a similar property mix. Speak with the staff who close the books, not only the buyer. Ask what still requires manual work after setup.

## What should you test before signing?

Choose the product that passes your essential workflows at a price you can support. Write down which existing tools the new software replaces. Cancel those subscriptions only after the replacement works.

Keep access to old records until exports and opening balances pass review. Ask the implementation team to show the import plan. Record which documents, histories and payment details can move. Treat anything outside that list as a separate task with an assigned owner.

## What does a turnover cost?

A turnover costs lost rent plus the work to get the unit ready and leased again. Most owners count only the rent and underestimate the rest. Do the math for your own unit before you decide on a renewal offer.

**Worked example** (example numbers; use your own):

| Cost item | Example |
|---|---|
| Rent | $1,800/month, $59/day |
| Vacant days (turnover plus leasing) | 30 days = $1,770 |
| Cleaning | $350 |
| Paint touch-up | $600 |
| Small repairs | $400 |
| Listing and screening time | $200 |
| **Total turnover cost** | **$3,320** |

That is almost 2 months of rent. Compare it with what keeping the tenant would cost. A $50/month smaller rent increase costs $600 a year. A carpet clean costs a few hundred. Both cost less than the turnover.

The vacancy part depends on your market. The U.S. Census Bureau reported a national rental vacancy rate of 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026. Local rates differ a lot. Check your own days on market.

## Does fast maintenance raise renewals?

Fast maintenance is one of the simplest ways to keep tenants. A tenant who waits 3 weeks for a heater fix starts to look for a new home.

Some states set repair deadlines. Texas Property Code 92.056 creates a rebuttable presumption that 7 days is a reasonable time to repair a condition after notice. The time can change with the severity of the problem and the availability of parts and labor. Texas 92.0563 lists tenant remedies, including a civil penalty of one month's rent plus $500.

Set your own targets and track them:

| Request type | Target to respond | Target to fix |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency (no heat in winter, active leak, no water) | Same day | 24 hours |
| Urgent (appliance down, one of two toilets not working) | 1 business day | 3 business days |
| Routine (drip, loose handle) | 2 business days | 7 days |

Tell tenants the targets. Then meet them. If a part is delayed, say so the same day.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Do renewal incentives work?

Renewal incentives work when they cost less than a turnover and the tenant values them. A gift card the tenant does not need does little. A fix the tenant asked for does a lot.

Common incentives:

- **Smaller rent increase** than the market rate.
- **Professional carpet clean** or deep clean.
- **A small upgrade** the tenant asked for, such as a new faucet or ceiling fan.
- **Longer lease with a fixed rent,** for tenants who value stability.

Test one incentive at a time. Track how many tenants renew with it and without it. After 10 renewals, you will know if it helps.

Offer the same incentive menu to every tenant in similar units. A menu you apply fairly avoids Fair Housing risk.

## Should I upgrade the unit at renewal?

Upgrade at renewal when the upgrade also raises the unit's long-term value. A good upgrade helps you keep this tenant and rent faster to the next one.

| Upgrade | Helps renewal | Helps next leasing | Typical choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fresh paint in one room | Yes | Yes | Good |
| New LED light fixtures | Some | Yes | Good |
| New appliance at end of life | Yes | Yes | Good |
| Smart lock | Some | Yes | Fair |
| Custom design the tenant picks | Yes | Maybe not | Weak |

Plan upgrades in occupied units with the tenant. Agree on the date in writing and give the notice your state requires for entry.

## How do I ask tenants what they want?

Ask with a short survey 90 to 120 days before the lease ends. Short surveys get answers. Long ones do not.

Use 3 questions:

1. **What works well** about living here?
2. **What does not work** or needs a fix?
3. **What one thing** would make you more likely to renew?

Read every answer. Reply within a week, even if the answer is "we cannot do that, but here is what we can do." A reply shows the tenant you listened.

## What is the renewal checklist?

Use this checklist for every lease that ends in the next 120 days.

- [ ] **120 days out:** send the 3-question survey.
- [ ] **100 days out:** review repair history. Close any open requests.
- [ ] **90 days out:** check market rent. Compute your turnover cost.
- [ ] **90 days out:** decide the offer: rent, term, any incentive.
- [ ] **75 days out:** send the renewal offer in writing, with a response date.
- [ ] **60 days out:** follow up by phone or message if no answer.
- [ ] **Deadline in your state:** send any required notice of rent change or non-renewal.
- [ ] **After renewal:** schedule any promised upgrade.
- [ ] **If the tenant leaves:** start marketing, and give the notice your state requires for showings.

Track two numbers each year: your renewal rate and your average days to fix a routine request. If repair time drops and renewals rise, your process works.

## Who owns the website and domain?

Your company must own the domain, the website files and every marketing account, and the agency gets access, not ownership. This is the first question because it decides how hard it is to leave. If an agency holds your domain, it holds your phone calls, your email and years of search history.

Ask these questions:

1. **"Who is the registrant of my domain?"** The answer must be your company, with an email address you control.
2. **"Can I log in to the registrar myself?"** You want your own login, not a shared one.
3. **"Who owns the website files and content if I leave?"** You want a written yes: you keep them.
4. **"Is the site on your own platform?"** Some agencies host on a closed system. Ask how you would export the pages.
5. **"Who is the owner of my Google Business Profile, Google Ads and GA4 property?"** You want your company as owner in each one.

ICANN backs your right to move. Its registrant FAQ says, "it is your right to transfer your domain name registrations between registrars." The registrar must give you the transfer code, called the Auth-Code, "within five (5) calendar days of your request" (ICANN). Watch for one trap: ICANN also says you cannot transfer a domain to another registrar within 60 days after you change the registrant details.

Account roles matter too. The right role gives the agency what it needs and keeps control with you.

| Account | Role you keep | Role the agency gets | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Domain registrar | Account owner, registrant | Delegate access or none | Only the registrant can move the domain |
| GA4 property | Administrator | Editor or Marketer | In GA4, only an Administrator can add or delete users (Google Analytics Help) |
| Google Business Profile | Primary owner | Manager | Managers cannot add or remove users or delete the profile (Google Business Profile Help) |
| Google Ads | Admin on your own account | Standard access | You keep billing and history |
| Website host or CMS | Owner login | Editor or admin user | You can remove the agency in one step |

Google also says a Business Profile has only one primary owner, and you need at least one other owner or manager to transfer that role. Set this up on day one, not on the day you leave.

## How does the agency report leads and signed doors?

A good agency reports leads by source each month and helps you count signed doors by source. Traffic, rankings and "impressions" are steps on the way. They do not tell you if the money worked.

Ask:

6. **"What is in the monthly report?"** Ask for a sample report from a real client, with the name hidden.
7. **"How do you count a lead?"** A form fill, a phone call of a set length, a chat? Get the rule in writing.
8. **"How do you track the source?"** Ask about GA4 setup, call tracking and the CRM field for lead source.
9. **"Do you report signed doors?"** The agency cannot see your contracts, so ask how you will match leads to signed owners together.
10. **"Do you report AI search?"** Ask if they track visits from ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity, and if they check what AI answers say about you.

Know the base rates before you judge a report. In the Goodjuju fall 2026 study of 35 property management companies, the average site converted 0.9% of visitors into leads. Google Search and Map Pack brought 50.6% of leads, and AI tools brought 3.4% (Goodjuju, 2026). If an agency reports a rate far from these, ask how it counts.

## What are the contract and cancel terms?

The contract must state the term length, the notice period, any cancel fee, and what you keep when you leave. Many disputes start because these were only in a sales call.

Ask:

11. **"How long is the term?"** Month to month, 6 months, 12 months?
12. **"How much notice do I give to cancel?"** Get the number of days in writing.
13. **"Is there a cancel fee or a setup fee I lose?"**
14. **"Does the price go up after the first term?"**
15. **"What do you hand over on the last day?"** Site files, content, logins, ad history, call tracking numbers.
16. **"Do the call tracking numbers stay with me?"** If the agency owns them, your old ads and listings point to a number you lose.

| Term | Answer you want | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Term length | Month to month, or a short first term | 24 months with auto-renew |
| Notice | A short, fixed notice period in writing | No cancel clause |
| Exit | All files and accounts handed over | "The site is licensed, not sold" |
| Price | Fixed for the term, increase rule in writing | "Rates may change" |
| Guarantee | Clear terms you can check | "Page 1 guaranteed" |

Be careful with ranking promises. Google decides rankings, and AI answers change from day to day. An agency can promise work and reports. It cannot honestly promise a spot. ## Which client results can it show?

An agency should show 2 or 3 named property management clients with dates, numbers and permission to call. Logos alone prove little.

Ask:

17. **"Which of your clients are property managers like me, in door count and market?"**
18. **"Can I call 2 of them?"**
19. **"What were the leads per month before and after, and over what dates?"**
20. **"What did not work for a client, and what did you change?"**

Then check the claims on your own:

- [ ] Search each named client on Google. Do they show in the map pack for their city?
- [ ] Open each client site. Does it have owner pages, a fee table and a rental analysis form?
- [ ] Ask ChatGPT or Gemini for property managers in that client's city. Does the client show up?
- [ ] Ask the client on the phone if the case study numbers are right.
- [ ] Ask if the agency works with a direct competitor in your city.

The last question matters for local work. Two property managers in one city compete for the same map pack. Ask how the agency handles that.

## How do I score the answers?

Score each answer as yes, partial or no, and treat any "no" on ownership as a stop. Ownership comes first because it decides your cost to leave. Reports come second because they decide whether you can judge the work. Price comes last.

Keep this list in the same document as the proposal. Ask the agency to answer in writing, then attach the answers to the contract.

## What are the deposit deadlines and penalties?

Deposit deadlines and penalties are set by state law. A landlord who misses the deadline can lose the right to keep any of the deposit, even for real damage. Start with your state's rule.

| State | Deadline | Burden of proof | Bad-faith penalty | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| California | 21 days after the tenant vacates | Landlord | Statutory damages up to 2x the deposit, plus actual damages | Civil Code 1950.5 |
| Texas | 30 days after the tenant surrenders | Landlord | $100 + 3x the amount wrongly withheld + attorney's fees | Prop. Code 92.103, 92.109 |
| New York | 14 days after the tenant vacates | Landlord | Punitive damages up to 2x the deposit for willful violation | GOL 7-108 |

Other details matter too:

- **Texas** presumes bad faith if you do not return the deposit or send an itemized list within 30 days (Prop. Code 92.109(d)).
- **New York** says a landlord who misses the 14-day statement forfeits any right to keep part of the deposit (GOL 7-108(1-a)(e)).
- **California** requires photos at move-out before repairs, and again after repairs, since April 1, 2025 (Civil Code 1950.5(g)).

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What evidence wins a deposit dispute?

Evidence that wins shows the start condition, the end condition, and the cost to fix the gap. A judge wants to see all three for each deduction.

| Evidence | What it proves | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Move-in photos and signed condition form | Start condition | Date-stamped, every room, every wall |
| Move-out photos | End condition | Same angles as move-in |
| Itemized statement | What you kept and why | Sent before the deadline; one line per item |
| Receipts, invoices or quotes | Cost | Match each line of the statement |
| Lease | The rules the tenant agreed to | Cleaning, pets, smoking clauses |
| Messages and notices | What the tenant knew | Repair requests, move-out letter |

**Watch for:** normal wear and tear. In most states you cannot charge for it. Faded paint after 3 years and light carpet wear are normal wear. A hole in a door or a pet stain is damage. If a deduction looks like wear, drop it.

## How do I respond when a tenant disputes deductions?

Respond in writing within a few days, and keep the tone calm. A fast, clear reply ends many disputes before they grow.

1. **Read the tenant's message** and list each deduction they dispute.
2. **Pull your file:** photos, statement, receipts, lease.
3. **Score each deduction.** Strong means photos show damage and a receipt shows the cost. Weak means one of those is missing.
4. **Drop or reduce weak deductions.** Say so in your reply.
5. **Explain strong deductions** with the photo and the receipt attached.
6. **Offer a date** by which you will pay any refund.
7. **Keep a copy** of every message.

Do not argue by phone. Write it down so both of you have the same record.

## Should I offer a partial refund?

Offer a partial refund when a deduction is weak, when you missed a step, or when the amount in dispute is small. A refund often costs less than a day in court.

Run the numbers before you decide. Here is an example in Texas, with a $1,500 deposit and $600 kept:

| Outcome | Your cost |
|---|---|
| You refund $300 now to settle | $300 |
| Court finds $300 was kept in bad faith | $300 + $100 + 3 × $300 = $1,300, plus the tenant's attorney's fees |
| Court finds all $600 was fair | $0, plus your time |

The bad-faith formula comes from Texas Property Code 92.109(a). When your evidence is strong, you can stand firm. When it is not, the downside is much larger than the refund.

Put any settlement in writing. Say that the payment settles all claims about the deposit.

## What if the tenant sues in small claims court?

If the tenant sues in small claims court, prepare the same file you would show the tenant, and bring 3 copies. Small claims courts are fast and simple. In California, the self-help site says you can get advice from a lawyer before court, but you cannot have one with you in court. It lists a filing fee of $30 to $100 and a trial in about 1 to 2 months. Other states have their own rules.

California Civil Code 1950.5 allows deposit cases in small claims court within the court's dollar limit. The California Courts self-help site says the limit is generally $12,500 for an individual and $6,250 for a business.

At the hearing:

- **Go deduction by deduction.** Show the move-in photo, the move-out photo and the receipt.
- **Be brief.** Two or three sentences per item.
- **Admit a weak item.** Judges trust a landlord who concedes a small point.
- **Bring the lease** with the relevant clauses marked.

Remember the burden of proof. In California, Texas and New York, you must show your deductions were reasonable. The tenant does not have to prove they were not.

## How do I prevent the next dispute?

Prevent disputes with a system that runs the same way for every tenant.

- [ ] Signed move-in condition form with dated photos.
- [ ] Written move-out cleaning standard given at notice.
- [ ] Pre-move-out inspection offer where the law requires it. California Civil Code 1950.5(f) requires you to offer one.
- [ ] Move-out photos before any repair.
- [ ] Itemized statement with receipts sent well before the deadline.
- [ ] Deposit refund by a method the tenant can trace.

A system also protects you from Fair Housing claims. When every tenant gets the same process, no tenant can say you treated them differently.

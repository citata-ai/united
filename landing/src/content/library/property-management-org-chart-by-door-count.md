## How many staff does each door count need?

Each door count needs about 1 owner- or tenant-facing person for every 49 to 59 doors. Those are the 2021 average and top-25% ratios in the 2022 NARPM Financial Performance Guide.

The study calls these people "direct labor": team members who spend 50% or more of their time on owner or tenant work. Managers and sales staff are "management labor" and sit on top.

| Doors | Direct staff at 49 per person | Direct staff at 59 per person | Yearly revenue at $222 a door per month |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 2.0 | 1.7 | $266,400 |
| 300 | 6.1 | 5.1 | $799,200 |
| 500 | 10.2 | 8.5 | $1,332,000 |

We did the math from the NARPM ratios and the 2021 average revenue per door of $222 a month. The study built these ratios from single-family-focused companies. Apartment portfolios need fewer people per door.

## What payroll can each size afford?

Each size can afford direct payroll of about 29% to 38% of revenue. In the NARPM study, direct labor was 38.4% of revenue on average and 29.4% for the most profitable 25%.

| Doors | Direct payroll at 38.4% | Direct payroll at 29.4% |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | $102,298 | $78,322 |
| 300 | $306,893 | $234,965 |
| 500 | $511,488 | $391,608 |

Management labor added 17.2% of revenue on average and 13.7% for the top group. Payroll taxes and benefits added about 5%.

What do people cost? O*NET shows these US median salaries from Bureau of Labor Statistics 2025 data:

| Role (BLS occupation) | US median salary |
|---|---|
| Property manager (property, real estate and community association managers) | $69,990 |
| Leasing agent (real estate sales agents) | $52,830 |
| Bookkeeper (bookkeeping, accounting and auditing clerks) | $50,670 |
| Front desk or admin (receptionists and information clerks) | $38,010 |

Local pay differs a lot. Check the state wages on O*NET for your area.

## Which roles exist at 100 doors?

At 100 doors, the owner does most of the work, with 1 or 2 helpers. The NARPM study found that companies with 0 to 100 units averaged -1% profit in 2021, after a market salary for the owner. At this size, the company mostly pays the owner a wage.

A common 100-door chart:

- **Owner/broker:** sales, owner relations, leases, money decisions, licensed tasks.
- **Admin or leasing assistant:** showings, applications, tenant calls, work order intake.
- **Outside bookkeeper or software service:** trust account reconciliation and owner statements, part-time.
- **Vendors:** maintenance by outside contractors.

Watch for: at 100 doors, payroll room at the average ratio is about $102,000. One full-time US property manager at the median salary takes 68% of it.

## What changes at 300 doors?

At 300 doors, the owner stops doing daily work and starts to manage people. You need 5 to 6 direct staff. This is also the size where you choose a team model.

A 300-door chart, one way to build it:

- **Owner/broker:** sales, key owners, finance, hiring.
- **2 property managers:** owners and residents, renewals, notices.
- **1 leasing agent:** listings, showings, applications, move-ins.
- **1 maintenance coordinator:** work orders, vendors, inspections.
- **1 bookkeeper:** trust accounting, payables, owner statements.

A check with the BLS medians: 2 managers at $69,990, 1 leasing agent at $52,830 and 1 bookkeeper at $50,670 come to $243,480. Add a coordinator at the receptionist median of $38,010, and the total is $281,490. That fits inside the $234,965 to $306,893 range above, before taxes and benefits.

The NARPM study also tracked team models. The table shows DLER, which is revenue for each $1 of direct labor. Higher is better.

| Labor model | Average DLER, 2019–2021 | Companies (average) |
|---|---|---|
| Departmental + pod hybrid | 2.78 | 8 |
| Departmental | 2.77 | 23 |
| Departmental + portfolio hybrid | 2.69 | 17 |
| Pod | 2.37 | 2 |
| Portfolio | 2.25 | 11 |

Source: 2022 NARPM Financial Performance Guide, "DLER by Labor Model". The benchmark DLER was 3.96. The study says execution matters more than the model. The pod sample is very small.

Blanket describes the 3 models. Departmental teams split by function. A portfolio manager does everything for a set of owners. A pod is usually 3 people, sometimes 4: one for owners, one for residents, one for maintenance. Rentvine sees more companies move to pods.

## What changes at 500 doors?

At 500 doors, you add a layer of leaders between you and the team. You need 8 to 10 direct staff. Nobody can manage that many people well alone.

A 500-door chart:

- **Owner/CEO:** strategy, finance, key hires, large owner accounts.
- **Business development:** new owner leads and sales. In the NARPM study, sales and marketing labor was 7.5% of revenue on average.
- **Operations manager or director:** runs the property management team.
- **2 pods or departments:** 4 to 5 people each for owners, residents, leasing and maintenance.
- **Accounting lead plus 1 clerk:** trust accounting, payables, statements.

Many companies at this size add offshore staff. In the NARPM data, companies with 0 non-US team members averaged 9% profit in 2021. Companies with 6 or more averaged 16%. Keep licensed tasks with licensed people in your state.

## Which role does the owner keep?

The owner keeps 3 roles at every size: sales, finance and the final word on owner relations. The tasks under them change.

| Doors | Owner keeps | Owner hands off |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | Sales, finance, owners, leases | Admin, showings, part of bookkeeping |
| 300 | Sales, finance, key owners, hiring | Daily owner and tenant work, leasing, maintenance |
| 500 | Strategy, finance, top accounts, leaders | Sales pipeline, daily operations, team management |

Margin also rises with size. The NARPM study found 4% profit at 101 to 250 units and 26% at 801 to 1,000 units in 2021. A clear chart is one reason why.

## What is the average monthly management fee in Orlando?

The usual monthly management fee in Orlando is 8% of rent. On 2026-10-19 we read the published prices of 10 managers in the Orlando metro, which includes Orange, Osceola, Seminole and Lake counties. 7 of them charge a percent of rent, and all 7 start at 8%. We did not use old survey averages. Each number below is on the manager's own page.

What the table shows:

- **Percent fees.** The floor is 8% at all 7 percent-based managers. Higher plans cost 9% (24PropertyManagement), 10% (The Listing) or 12% (JAG, Orlando Realty).
- **Higher plan, lower leasing fee.** At 24PropertyManagement and JAG, the higher monthly rate comes with a 50% leasing fee, not 75%.
- **Flat fees.** Renters Warehouse ($119), Ziprent ($150) and Specialized PM ($125 to $225) charge a fixed amount per home.

Watch for: McCormack's 8% Package charges $100 a month when the home is vacant. Its other plan charges no fee when vacant. 24PropertyManagement says it charges only on rent it collects. Ask each manager what you pay in a vacant month.

## What leasing and renewal fees do Orlando managers charge?

Orlando leasing fees run from 50% to 100% of one month's rent. The 75% rate is more common in Orlando than in many metros: 24PropertyManagement, JAG, McCormack and The Listing all use it for at least one plan. Ziprent charges a flat $1,500 instead.

Renewal fees on the 10 pages:

Other fees on the pages: HomeRiver lists $89 for each optional inspection and $10 a month for eviction protection. JAG adds 10% to maintenance bills. Evernest lists a $149 yearly property condition report. Renters Warehouse lists a $350 eviction admin fee.

An Orlando manager costs about $2,900 to $3,350 in year 1 on a typical 2-bedroom, by our math. ## Which local rules add cost for Orlando owners?

Vacation rental rules add the most cost for Orlando-area owners. Florida Statutes 509.242 classes a vacation rental as a condo unit, or a house with 1 to 4 units, that is also a transient public lodging establishment. Under Florida Statutes 509.241, each public lodging establishment must get a license from the Division of Hotels and Restaurants. The license renews each year. Running one without a license is a second-degree misdemeanor.

So a long-term lease and a vacation rental are two different jobs in the Orlando area. Vacation rental managers handle guest turnover, cleaning and lodging taxes, and they price their work differently. Ask for a separate quote if you want both. Check county and city rules and your condo or HOA rules before you list a short stay.

## Must an Orlando property manager hold a Florida license?

An Orlando property manager who works for pay must hold a Florida real estate license in most cases. Florida Statutes 475.01(1)(a) defines a broker as a person who, "for another, and for a compensation," rents or leases real property. The definition also covers "all persons who advertise rental property information or lists." The Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) licenses brokers and sales associates.

Florida Statutes 475.011 lists exemptions. An owner who leases its own property is exempt. The exemption ends if the owner pays an agent on a per-deal basis to lease to the public. Salaried leasing staff in an apartment community's onsite office are also exempt.

Checklist before you sign:

- [ ] Ask for the broker's name and Florida license number.
- [ ] Look up the license on the DBPR license search.
- [ ] Ask where the manager holds rent and security deposits.
- [ ] Get every fee in the management agreement, including vacancy and maintenance fees.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Flat fee or percent: which model is common in Orlando?

The percent model is more common in Orlando. 7 of the 10 managers we checked charge a percent of rent. 3 sell a flat monthly fee.

Use rent to choose. At 8%, the fee equals $150 when rent is $1,875 a month. Below that rent, 8% costs less than Ziprent's $150 plan. Above it, the flat plan costs less. Renters Warehouse's $119 equals 8% of $1,487.50. Compare the full year, with the leasing fee, before you choose.

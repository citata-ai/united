## What counts in revenue per door?

Revenue per door counts every dollar of property management revenue you keep, divided by the doors you manage. It includes the base management fee and all other fees. It leaves out rent you pass to owners and revenue from separate maintenance or brokerage divisions.

The NARPM Accounting Standards call this metric RPU (revenue per unit). The 2022 NARPM Financial Performance Guide measures it per month.

The formula:

> Revenue per door per month = property management revenue for the month ÷ doors

Count these:

- Management fees
- Leasing (tenant placement) fees
- Lease renewal fees
- Application fees you keep
- Other owner-paid fees: admin fees, inspection fees, owner protection programs
- Other tenant-paid fees you keep: pet rent, late fees, resident benefit packages, lease initiation fees
- Maintenance coordination fees and vendor referral income

Do not count these:

- Rent and deposits held in trust
- Repair invoices you pass through at cost
- Revenue from an in-house maintenance or sales division

Pick one door count and keep it. The study shows both "overall" and "occupied" numbers. For its middle bracket in 2021, that was $178 overall and $197 occupied.

## What is a good annual revenue per door?

A good annual revenue per door is $3,000 or more. The 2021 average was about $2,665 a year. The top 25% reached about $3,803.

| Measure (2021) | Average | Top 25% |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue per occupied door, monthly | $222 | $317 |
| Revenue per occupied door, yearly | about $2,665 | about $3,803 |
| Revenue per door, 2017 (monthly) | $171 | $238 |

Source: 2022 NARPM Financial Performance Guide, "Game of Pricing". The study left out companies with 20% or more multifamily units. So the numbers fit single-family managers best.

Revenue per door also falls a little as companies grow. Companies with 22 to 158 units averaged $237 per occupied door. Companies with 476 to 1,329 units averaged $211.

The study says a 10% rise in revenue per door "can easily lead to a 100% increase in profitability". The math works because new revenue on existing doors needs almost no new labor.

## Which fees move revenue per door the most?

Fees beyond the base management fee move revenue per door the most. The top group got 45% of its revenue from those fees. The bottom group got 32%.

| Fee per occupied door per month (2021) | Bottom 25% | Average | Top 25% |
|---|---|---|---|
| Management fees | $96.45 | $134.06 | $174.62 |
| Leasing fees | $10.52 | $30.18 | $45.28 |
| Renewal fees | $2.38 | $8.33 | $16.07 |
| Application fees | $6.00 | $7.51 | $9.91 |
| Other tenant-paid fees | $16.57 | $25.35 | $37.14 |
| Other owner-paid fees | $2.91 | $6.27 | $10.25 |
| Maintenance coordination | $3.39 | $4.46 | $8.25 |
| Vendor referral and other | $3.23 | $5.78 | $15.39 |
| **Total** | **$142.16** | **$222.11** | **$316.91** |

Source: 2022 NARPM Financial Performance Guide, "Revenue Per Unit Detail". We added vendor referral and other fees into one row. Rows may not add up to the total because of rounding in the source.

The gap between bottom and top is $174.75 a door. Management fees explain $78 of it. The other $97 comes from event fees and small recurring fees.

Fee adoption also differs. In the top group, 57% used a tiered pricing structure and 81% charged owners a renewal fee. In the bottom group, 30% used tiers and 60% charged a renewal fee.

Watch for: each fee must be in your management agreement and legal in your state. Some states and cities cap tenant fees. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How does rent level change revenue per door?

Higher rent raises revenue per door in dollars, but lowers it as a share of rent. A 10% fee on $2,500 rent earns more than a 10% fee on $1,000 rent. But the top companies in low-rent areas charge a larger share.

| Average rent | Typical company (50th bracket) | Most aggressive 25% |
|---|---|---|
| $0–$1,000 | 16.3% of rent | 24.0% of rent |
| $1,001–$1,500 | 13.7% | 18.4% |
| $1,501–$2,000 | 12.7% | 18.9% |
| $2,001–$2,500 | 10.5% | 13.7% |
| $2,501+ | 8.9% | 11.4% |

Source: 2022 NARPM Financial Performance Guide, "RPU Aggressiveness Brackets". Each number is total revenue per door as a percent of average rent.

What this means for you: if your rents are under $2,000, total revenue near 15% to 24% of rent is within the range the top companies reach. If rents are over $2,000, the top range is 10% to 13%.

A worked example. Your average rent is $1,400. You earn $165 a door each month. That is 11.8% of rent, the bottom bracket for that rent band. Move to 15.4%, the 75th bracket. That is $216 a door. On 200 doors, the gap is $51 × 200 × 12 = $122,400 a year.

Will owners leave? The study found no meaningful link between pricing level and churn. Churn sat between 18.29% and 20.91% in every pricing bracket.

## How does revenue per door connect to the value of a door?

Revenue per door times how long an owner stays gives the lifetime value of a door. Rentvine shows the formula with an example: $2,200 a year × 4 years × 1.2 units per owner = $10,560.

The NARPM study measures the same idea per unit. In 2021, average unit lifetime revenue was $16,128. The top 25% reached $30,190. Lower churn and higher revenue per door both raised it.

Use the number to set a limit on what you spend to win a door. The average all-in cost to add one unit was $1,666 in 2021.

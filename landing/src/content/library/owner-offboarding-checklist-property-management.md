## What must a property manager hand over when an owner ends management?

A property manager hands over money, records, access and information when an owner ends management. A clean handover protects the tenants, the owner and the manager. A bad handover leaves a tenant who does not know where to pay rent, and a deposit that nobody can find.

Start with the management agreement. Read 3 clauses before you do anything else:

1. **Termination notice.** How many days of notice each side must give.
2. **Termination fees.** Any fee due on early exit, and any lease-up fee the manager keeps.
3. **Final accounting.** How many days the manager has to send the last statement and funds.

Then work through the checklist below. Each box is one task with one owner of the task.

### Full owner offboarding checklist

**Part 1: Money**

- [ ] List every tenant's security deposit, pet deposit and prepaid rent
- [ ] Add any earned interest your state requires
- [ ] Transfer deposits to the owner or the next manager, with a per-tenant accounting
- [ ] Get a written receipt for the deposits
- [ ] Pay outstanding vendor invoices or list them for the owner
- [ ] Release the owner's maintenance reserve
- [ ] Stop automatic rent payments into your trust account

**Part 2: Records**

- [ ] Current leases, renewals and addenda for each unit
- [ ] Tenant ledgers from lease start
- [ ] Rental applications kept for each current tenant, if you may share them under your state's privacy rules
- [ ] Move-in and move-out inspection reports with photos
- [ ] Open and closed work orders for the last 12 months or more
- [ ] Warranties, appliance manuals and serial numbers
- [ ] Vendor list with contacts
- [ ] Notices served (late notices, lease violations) and any open eviction file
- [ ] Insurance policy details and any open claims
- [ ] Permits, licenses and rental registration numbers

**Part 3: Keys and access**

- [ ] All physical keys, labeled by unit and door
- [ ] Mailbox, garage and amenity keys or fobs
- [ ] Lockbox and smart lock codes, then change the codes
- [ ] Utility account logins the owner pays for
- [ ] Listing accounts and photos the owner paid for

**Part 4: Tenants**

- [ ] Letter to each tenant: last day of your management, new payment address, new repair contact
- [ ] Statement of who now holds the deposit
- [ ] Turn off the tenant portal on the right date
- [ ] Forward any rent that arrives after the handover

**Part 5: Final statement and tax**

- [ ] Final owner statement that ends at zero for this owner
- [ ] Year-to-date income and expense report
- [ ] Note who will issue the owner's 1099-MISC for the year
- [ ] Signed handover receipt from the owner or the next manager

This is a generic checklist, not a state legal form. Check your state's laws and your management agreement.

## How do you transfer security deposits and trust funds?

Transfer security deposits and prepaid rent to the owner or the next manager, with a separate line for each tenant. The tenant's deposit belongs to the tenant until the lease ends, so the money must stay traceable.

Florida gives a clear example. Florida statute 83.49(7) covers a sale or "a change in the designated rental agent". In both cases, deposits and advance rents go to the new owner or agent. The transfer includes any earned interest and "an accurate accounting showing the amounts to be credited to each tenant account". The statute also releases the old party once it hands over the funds and records and gets a written receipt.

Other states have their own rules. Some say the old landlord stays liable until the tenant gets notice. Find your state's deposit statute before you move any money.

Use a transfer table like this one:

| Unit | Tenant | Security deposit | Pet deposit | Prepaid rent | Interest | Total transferred |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1A | Tenant A | $1,500 | $300 | $0 | $0 | $1,800 |
| 1B | Tenant B | $1,400 | $0 | $1,400 | $0 | $2,800 |
| 2A | Tenant C | $1,600 | $0 | $0 | $0 | $1,600 |
| Total | | $4,500 | $300 | $1,400 | $0 | $6,200 |

The amounts are an example. The total must match the deposit liability in your trust account for this owner.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Which records and keys go to the owner or next manager?

Records and keys go to the person who will run the property next: the owner, or the new manager the owner names in writing. Do not send records to a new manager until the owner tells you to in writing.

Sort the records by unit. A new manager needs the lease, the ledger and the move-in report for each unit on day one. Without the move-in report, the next manager cannot charge a fair deposit deduction when that tenant leaves.

Keep a copy of your own records. You may need them for a later dispute, an audit of your trust account or a tax question. Your state's real estate license rules may set how long you keep them.

For keys, list every key and code on one sheet. Have the receiver sign it. Then change smart lock and lockbox codes after the handover, so old staff and vendors lose access.

## How do you tell tenants about the change?

Tell each tenant in writing before the handover date. Tenants need 4 facts:

1. **The last day** you manage the property.
2. **Where to pay rent** from the next due date.
3. **Who to call** for repairs and emergencies.
4. **Who holds the deposit** now.

Some states set a deadline for this notice. Florida statute 83.49(2), for example, says the landlord must tell the tenant within 30 days when the way the deposit is held changes. That subsection does not apply to landlords with fewer than 5 units.

Send the letter by the methods your lease allows for notices. Keep a copy with the date sent. If a tenant pays you after the handover, forward the payment and tell the tenant again.

## What final statement does the owner get?

The owner gets a final owner statement that ends at zero in your trust account for that owner. The statement shows each dollar that came in and went out from the last regular statement to the handover date.

Here is a worked example for a 3-unit building:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Opening balance (last statement) | $2,100 |
| Rent collected this period | + $4,500 |
| Management fee (8% of rent) | − $360 |
| Repairs paid | − $640 |
| Maintenance reserve released | + $500 |
| Net paid to owner | $6,100 |
| Closing balance | $0 |

The math: $2,100 + $4,500 − $360 − $640 + $500 = $6,100 owed to the owner. The $6,200 of deposits and prepaid rent is tenant money, not owner income. Show it on its own transfer page, not inside the net payout.

Then send the year-to-date income and expense report. The owner needs it for tax filing. The IRS instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC cover this. A manager who collects rent "must use Form 1099-MISC to report the rent paid over to the property owner". For tax year 2026, the rent threshold is $2,000. So tell the owner that you will still send the 1099-MISC for the months you managed.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## How do you make offboarding faster next time?

Make offboarding faster by keeping each owner's file ready for handover all year. Clean ledgers, signed inspection reports and labeled keys turn a 2-week job into 2 days.

## Which core tool covers the most tasks?

A solo manager needs a core system that handles the full path from rent to owner report. Choose through tasks, not a count of features.

Your test should include a lease, rent charge, payment, repair invoice and statement. Ask the vendor to show the links between them. Then make a correction and inspect the result.

| Need | Core function to test | Add another tool only when |
|---|---|---|
| Money records | Ledger and reconciliation | Required reports remain missing |
| Rent collection | Charges, payments and returns | A needed method is absent |
| Owner service | Statements and documents | The owner workflow fails |
| Maintenance | Requests, vendors and updates | Dispatch or backup is missing |
| Follow-up | Tasks and reminders | Sales or operations need more scope |

These are our buying tests, not a claim that every plan includes them. Rent Manager's bundle page lists accounting, portals and workflows. Yardi Breeze's residential page lists operations and reporting. Put the proposed plan through your actual work.

## What can you automate alone?

Automate repeat work after you understand the exception path. Start with reminders, recurring charges and routing that can be checked from the record.

Write the trigger, action and stop condition for each rule. A reminder should stop when the required payment or task is complete. Test what happens when data arrives late or is corrected.

Keep approval for decisions that need judgment. An owner expense exception, unclear lease term or disputed charge deserves a visible review route. Do not assume a fluent AI response resolves the issue.

Use sample records before enabling live messages. Confirm the recipient, amount, date and source. Record where failures appear and who sees them. As a solo operator, you need a backup for that review when absent. Automation reduces repeat steps only when you can trust its inputs and inspect its outputs.

## How do you handle after-hours maintenance alone?

Use a named backup and a documented escalation route. A request form does not guarantee a person will answer or a vendor will attend.

Propertyware lists a maintenance contact center on its pricing page. Rent Manager lists a call-center add-on. Those are service options to investigate, not proof that either meets your emergency needs.

Ask who answers, how they identify urgent issues and when they call you. Confirm vendor coverage, spending authority and the record left in your system. Ask what happens if the first vendor declines.

Rehearse a sample request. Follow the call, work order, resident update and invoice. Ensure someone can reach the home through the approved access process. Keep emergency instructions clear for residents. Do not let an automated response imply help is on the way until dispatch has actually been confirmed.

Budget backup separately. If you buy maintenance coverage, ask about scope and usage charges. Count your own accounting review and resident support time. A base subscription does not represent the cost of running the company alone.

## Which tools can you skip?

Skip a tool when a tested core feature already completes the required work. Avoid adding a second customer database or expense log without a clear purpose.

A separate CRM may help owner sales, but first test your existing follow-up process. A reporting app may help a missing analysis, but first inspect available exports. Keep each purchase tied to an unresolved task.

List every place where the same fact gets entered. Decide which system owns the final record. Extra connections can create work when updates fail or duplicates appear.

Keep a short tool register with the purpose, cost, owner and exit route. As the sole operator, you still need to document those details for a backup. Cancel a duplicate only after verifying exports and the remaining workflow. Avoid losing history merely to reduce the number of subscriptions.

## How do you keep the stack manageable?

Use a repeatable work list and a trained backup. Software should make the next task clear without relying on your memory.

Practise the accounting close and emergency handoff before you grow. Buildium's onboarding page describes tailored training and data import support. Ask any vendor to connect training to the tasks you must perform alone.

Review unresolved tasks, failed automation and unclear balances before adding features. Keep source documents linked to entries. Give the backup only the access needed and test it.

Write down the backup process before taking on another property. Test that the backup can find approved records without using your personal login.

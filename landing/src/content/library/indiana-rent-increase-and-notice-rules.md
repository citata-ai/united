## Does Indiana have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

No. Indiana has no state cap on rent, and it bars local rent control.

IC 32-31-1-20(b) says a unit of local government "may not regulate rental rates for privately owned real property, through a zoning ordinance or otherwise, unless the regulation is authorized by an act of the general assembly."

The section does not apply to private property that gets government funds or benefits "for the express purpose of providing reduced rents to low or moderate income tenants" (IC 32-31-1-20(a)). Those units follow their program rules.

## How many days notice does an Indiana landlord need to raise the rent?

We found no Indiana statute that sets a specific notice period for a rent increase.

For a month-to-month tenant, a new rent takes effect at a new period. The safe path is to give the notice that ends the tenancy: "one (1) month notice in writing, delivered to the tenant" (IC 32-31-1-1(a)). Name the new rent and the date it starts.

Worked example. Rent is due on the 1st.

| Notice delivered | 1 month later | New rent starts |
|---|---|---|
| March 1 | April 1 | April 1 |
| March 10 | April 10 | May 1 |
| March 31 | April 30 | May 1 |

We start the new rent on the next rent day after the month ends. That keeps the change in line with the rental period.

## Can an Indiana landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

Not unless the lease allows it. A fixed-term lease sets the rent for the term. If you want the right to raise rent mid-term, write a clause that gives the amount or formula and the notice.

At the end of the term, offer a renewal at the new rent. If the tenant stays on with your consent and no new lease, IC 32-31-1-2 treats a general tenancy "occupied by the express or constructive consent of the landlord" as month to month.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Indiana?

Indiana uses 2 rules:

- **Tenancy at will:** "one (1) month notice in writing, delivered to the tenant" (IC 32-31-1-1(a)). A tenancy at will can arise only from an express contract (IC 32-31-1-1(b)).
- **Period tenancy of 3 months or less:** for a tenancy that runs from one period to the next, "notice to the tenant equal to the interval between the periods is sufficient" (IC 32-31-1-4).

For a month-to-month tenancy, both rules point to about 1 month. For week to week, IC 32-31-1-4 points to 1 week.

| Tenancy | Notice | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| At will | 1 month, in writing | IC 32-31-1-1 |
| Month to month | 1 month (the interval) | IC 32-31-1-2, -4 |
| Week to week | 1 week (the interval) | IC 32-31-1-4 |

Deliver the notice by a method you can prove, and keep a copy.

## Do cities in Indiana add their own rent or notice rules?

No, with few exceptions. IC 32-31-1-20(c) bars a unit of local government from regulating these parts of a landlord-tenant relationship, unless the General Assembly authorizes it:

1. the screening process for tenants,
2. security deposits,
3. lease applications,
4. leasing terms and conditions,
5. disclosures about the property, the lease or the parties' rights,
6. the rights of the parties to a lease, and
7. any fees charged by a landlord.

"Any ordinance or regulation that violates this subsection is void and unenforceable." The section history lists amendments in 2017, 2020 and 2021 (P.L. 266-2017, P.L. 168-2020, P.L. 215-2021).

The section starts with "Subject to IC 36-1-3-8.5," which we did not read. Ask an Indiana attorney about local registration or inspection rules where your unit is.

## Indiana rent increase checklist

- Read the lease. No mid-term increase unless it allows one.
- For month-to-month, give 1 month's written notice.
- Start the new rent on a rent day after the notice runs.
- Keep a copy and proof of delivery.
- Check whether the unit gets rent subsidy funds; program rules may apply.

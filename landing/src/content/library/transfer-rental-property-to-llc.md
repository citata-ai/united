## What should happen before you sign a deed?

Review the property, loan, entity, and coverage before preparing the transfer document. A deed changes title. It does not by itself complete the rest of the rental's legal and operating changes.

Start with the current deed and loan documents. Identify every owner, borrower, lien, and title issue. Then confirm the LLC's exact legal name, ownership, and authority to accept the property.

The SBA explains that LLCs provide a separate business structure with limited liability protection. The protection has limits. Ask a local attorney whether the proposed entity suits the property and owners before paying to transfer title.

Use a written review sheet so the process does not stop at entity formation. Keep each adviser responsible for a clear question.

| Review | Who should confirm the result |
|---|---|
| Entity and ownership plan | Local attorney |
| Existing loan transfer terms | Servicer and attorney |
| Deed and recording | Attorney or title professional |
| Transfer and ongoing taxes | Tax professional and local tax office |
| Named insured and rental use | Insurance adviser |
| Lease, deposits, and licenses | Attorney and property manager |

Do not transfer title while a key answer is still uncertain. Ask advisers to resolve conflicting instructions together. A cheap deed service cannot evaluate a loan or policy it has not reviewed.

## What deed should you use?

The correct deed depends on the location, title, and intended transaction. Have a qualified local professional prepare it with the legal description and required execution details. Do not choose a form only because another owner used it.

Ask about title warranties, existing title insurance, ownership shares, recording requirements, and any required transfer return. The professional should explain which interests move and which liabilities stay with you.

Confirm the LLC exists and can receive title before signing. Use its legal name consistently across the deed and other records. A misspelled or incomplete entity name can create extra work later.

After recording, obtain the recorded document and verify the public record. Update the mailing address for tax notices. Keep the old deed and transfer advice as part of the property's ownership history.

Recording is a milestone, not the finish. The insurer, tenants, bank, local offices, and manager may still need properly reviewed updates. Plan those steps before the deed is submitted.

## Will the due-on-sale clause apply?

An LLC transfer needs a loan-specific review. Federal law lists protected transfers, but a general LLC exemption does not appear among the listed categories in 12 USC Section 1701j-3(d). Do not substitute the trust exception for an LLC transfer.

Fannie Mae's servicing guide separately permits certain transfers to an LLC controlled by the original borrower or majority-owned by that borrower. It applies to loans purchased or securitized on or after June 1, 2016. The guide also requires compliance with the security instrument and permitted occupancy conditions.

Ask your servicer whether those rules apply to your loan and ownership plan. Provide the proposed entity ownership and deed details. Get the answer in writing before recording.

Fannie Mae also says the property must return to a natural person to qualify for a refinance under its selling requirements. Include that possible future step in the decision. A permitted transfer can still add work later.

A deed does not automatically remove the original borrower's debt obligation. Ask whether the lender requires an assumption or any other separate process. Do not describe the loan as an LLC debt unless the documents support that statement.

## Does insurance need to change?

Tell the insurer about the proposed new legal owner before transfer. Ask how the LLC, individual owner, manager, and any lender should appear in the policy. Request a written confirmation of effective dates.

Travelers describes landlord coverage as protection for rental property and certain liability risks, subject to terms. That product description does not confirm coverage for your entity. The actual named insured, property use, limits, and exclusions control.

Review liability coverage alongside property coverage. Ask how a claim naming both you and the LLC would work. Confirm whether an umbrella policy also needs an update.

Avoid a gap between deed recording and the coverage change. Coordinate timing with the title professional and insurer. Keep the final policy documents with the recorded deed.

## Will the transfer trigger taxes or fees?

Tax and recording results depend on the actual transfer and jurisdiction. Ask for written estimates before signing. Include transfer tax, recording charges, reassessment questions, and any effect on current benefits.

For federal income tax, the IRS generally treats a single-member LLC as disregarded unless it elects corporate treatment. That status does not answer every transfer question. Multiple owners, debt, and tax elections need separate review.

Ask the tax professional to preserve basis and depreciation records correctly. A deed to your LLC is not a reason to restart depreciation from a new market value without advice. Keep the full asset history.

## Which operating records must you update?

Update the records that tell tenants and service providers who owns and runs the property. Use counsel-reviewed notices and documents where needed. Do not assume the recorded deed automatically reaches everyone.

- **Review leases** for assignment, owner identity, and notice needs.
- **Reconcile deposits** and confirm lawful custody after the change.
- **Update banking** for the entity and authorized users.
- **Check local filings** for licenses and tax mailing details.
- **Update management records** and contractor billing instructions.
- **Save the complete file** with consents, deed, policies, and tax advice.

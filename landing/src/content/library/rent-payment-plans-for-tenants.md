## When is a payment plan better than eviction?

A payment plan is better than eviction when the tenant has a temporary problem, a clear way to catch up, and a record of keeping promises. In that case, a plan keeps rent coming in. It also avoids a vacant unit, turnover work and court time.

Eviction may be the better path when the tenant has no income, has broken an earlier plan, or has caused damage. Use this table to decide.

| Sign | Points to a plan | Points to notice and eviction |
|---|---|---|
| Cause of the shortfall | Job gap, medical bill, late paycheck | No clear cause |
| Path to catch up | New job, benefit start date, tax refund | None |
| Past record | Paid on time before | Many late months |
| Communication | Tenant called you first | Tenant stopped answering |
| Amount owed | 1–2 months | Large and growing |
| Earlier plans | None, or kept | Broken before |

**Worked example (hypothesis, not market data).** Rent is $1,500. The tenant owes $1,500 from one missed month. A plan of $375 extra a month over 4 months recovers the full $1,500. Now compare an eviction. Assume 2 months of court and turnover with no rent, plus $1,000 in legal and cleaning costs. That path loses $3,000 in rent plus $1,000 in costs, and the old $1,500 may never come back. Your own numbers will differ, but run them before you decide.

## What terms go in a written payment plan?

A written payment plan lists the debt, the schedule and the rules. Never agree to a plan by phone only. Use this checklist.

- [ ] **Names and address:** landlord, every tenant on the lease, and the unit.
- [ ] **Amount owed:** the exact past-due total and the months it covers.
- [ ] **Schedule:** each payment date and amount, in a table.
- [ ] **Current rent:** a line that says regular rent stays due on the normal date.
- [ ] **Payment method:** how and where the tenant pays.
- [ ] **Late fees:** whether new late fees pause during the plan.
- [ ] **Missed payment:** what happens if a plan payment is late or short.
- [ ] **Notice rights:** a line that the landlord keeps the right to serve state notices.
- [ ] **No waiver:** a line that the plan does not waive other lease terms.
- [ ] **Signatures and date:** every adult tenant and the landlord.

A sample schedule for the example above:

| Date | Regular rent | Plan payment | Total due |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nov 1 | $1,500 | $375 | $1,875 |
| Dec 1 | $1,500 | $375 | $1,875 |
| Jan 1 | $1,500 | $375 | $1,875 |
| Feb 1 | $1,500 | $375 | $1,875 |

Give a receipt for every payment. Note the date, the amount and the balance left.

## How does partial rent affect an eviction notice?

Partial rent can waive or reset an eviction notice in some states. Check your state's rule before you take any money after a notice. Three examples show how the rules differ.

| State | Notice for unpaid rent | Partial rent rule | Statute |
|---|---|---|---|
| Illinois | Not less than 5 days | A partial payment does not void the notice only if the notice says, prominently: "Only FULL PAYMENT of the rent demanded in this notice will waive the landlord's right to terminate the lease under this notice, unless the landlord agrees in writing to continue the lease in exchange for receiving partial payment." | 735 ILCS 5/9-209 |
| Florida | 3 days, not counting Saturday, Sunday and legal holidays | Partial rent does not waive the right to terminate. But after posting the notice, the landlord must give a receipt with the new due date and balance, put the money in the court registry, or post a new 3-day notice | Florida Statutes 83.56(3), 83.56(5)(a) |
| Texas | At least 3 days' written notice to vacate, unless a written lease sets a shorter or longer period | Late fees must be in a written lease, be reasonable, and start only after rent is unpaid 2 full days | Texas Property Code 24.005(a), 92.019 |

The Illinois law also says a landlord may agree in writing to continue the lease in exchange for partial payment. A payment plan is that kind of written agreement. Write it with care.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What happens if the tenant misses a plan payment?

The plan itself should answer this. A common term is that the full past-due balance becomes due at once. The landlord may then serve the notice that state law requires.

The plan cannot replace your state's notice steps. You still must serve a proper notice and wait the full period before you file. In Florida, Florida Statutes 83.56(4) says these notice rules cannot be waived in the lease.

Before you act on a missed payment:

1. **Call the tenant.** Ask what happened. A one-day delay is not the same as a stop.
2. **Check the plan's terms.** Follow what you both signed.
3. **Send a written notice of default.** Name the missed date and amount.
4. **Serve the state notice if needed.** Use your state's form and timing.
5. **Keep every record.** The plan, receipts, messages and notices.

## Should late fees stop during the plan?

Many landlords pause new late fees while the tenant keeps the plan. Stacking fees on a tenant who is catching up can make the plan fail. Whatever you choose, write it in the plan.

Your state may limit late fees. Texas Property Code 92.019 is one example. It allows a late fee only if notice of the fee is in a written lease, the fee is reasonable, and rent stays unpaid for 2 full days after the due date. The statute treats a fee as reasonable if it is not more than 12% of the monthly rent in a building with 4 or fewer units, or 10% in a building with more than 4 units.

**Worked example under Texas Property Code 92.019.** Rent is $1,500 in a duplex. The safe-harbor cap is 12% of $1,500, or $180 for that rental period. A landlord who breaks the rule owes the tenant $100, three times the improper fee, and attorney's fees.

Other states set different caps or none. Check yours before you set a fee in the plan.

## How do I make a payment plan work?

Make the plan short, clear and easy to pay. Plans fail when the payments are too large or the terms are vague.

- **Keep it short.** 2 to 6 months is easier to keep than a year.
- **Match payday.** Set plan dates a day or two after the tenant's paycheck.
- **Use online payments.** Automatic records cut disputes.
- **Check in.** A short message before each due date helps.
- **Offer the same terms to similar cases.** Treat tenants in the same situation the same way, so fair housing questions do not come up.

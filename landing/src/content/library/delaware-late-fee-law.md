Delaware sets its late fee rules in one sentence group: 25 Del. C. § 5501(d). I read it on the Delaware Code Online site on 2026-10-19. It gives you a cap, a grace period, and a rule about where tenants can pay.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum late fee | 5% of the monthly rent | § 5501(d) |
| Grace period | No late fee within 5 days of the agreed due date | § 5501(d) |
| Lease term | The rental agreement must provide for the late charge | § 5501(d) |
| Counting the 5 days | Skip weekends and legal holidays (period under 7 days) | § 5112 |
| No local payment office | Due date moves 3 days later | § 5501(d) |
| Late fee status | Counts as additional rent under the Code | § 5501(d) |
| Cash receipt | Give a receipt within 15 days; keep records 3 years | § 5501(e) |
| Demand for past-due rent | Only after the late-fee date; at least 7 days to pay | § 5502(a) |

## What is the maximum late fee a landlord can charge in Delaware?

The maximum late fee in Delaware is 5% of the monthly rent. Section 5501(d) says the late charge "shall not exceed 5 percent of the monthly rent."

The cap ties to the monthly rent, not to the amount that is late. If a tenant pays $1,200 of a $1,500 rent on time and $300 late, the cap is still 5% of $1,500.

| Monthly rent | Maximum late fee (5%) |
|---|---|
| $900 | $45 |
| $1,200 | $60 |
| $1,500 | $75 |
| $2,000 | $100 |
| $2,800 | $140 |

## Does Delaware law require a grace period before a late fee?

Yes. "The late charge shall not be imposed within 5 days of the agreed time for payment of rent" (§ 5501(d)).

Section 5501(d) adds a second rule. You must keep an office or other permanent place in the county where tenants can pay rent. If you do not, the agreed due date moves 3 days later. The 5-day grace period then starts from that later date.

Worked timeline for rent due on the 1st:

| Your setup | Rent due date | Earliest day a late fee can apply |
|---|---|---|
| You keep a payment office in the county | 1st | After the 5-day window from the 1st |
| You keep no payment office in the county | 4th (1st + 3 days) | After the 5-day window from the 4th |

Count the 5 days with § 5112, the Code's time rule. The day rent is due does not count. For a period under 7 days, Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays in the middle do not count. If the last day falls on one of them, the period runs to the next business day. So a 5-day window often takes a full week.

## Must the late fee be written in the lease in Delaware?

Yes, in practice. Section 5501(d) applies "where the rental agreement provides for a late charge." Write these items in the lease:

- **Amount.** A flat dollar figure or a percentage, at or below 5% of monthly rent.
- **Due date.** The day rent is due and the place or method of payment.
- **Trigger.** The day the late charge applies, after the 5-day window.
- **Payment place.** The address of your office in the county, if you have one.

## Can a Delaware landlord charge a daily late fee?

Delaware law does not ban a daily format. It does cap "such late charge" at 5% of the monthly rent. So the sum of all daily amounts in a month must stay at or below 5% of the monthly rent.

Worked example for $1,600 rent:

| Fee design | Total after 20 late days | Legal under the 5% cap? |
|---|---|---|
| Flat $80 once | $80 | Yes, $80 is 5% |
| $10 per day | $200 | No, above $80 |
| $10 per day, capped at $80 | $80 | Yes |

The simplest safe choice is one flat fee at or below 5%.

## Can a Delaware landlord evict a tenant for unpaid late fees?

Delaware treats a late charge as rent for Code purposes. Section 5501(d) says "A late charge is considered as additional rent for the purposes of this Code." Section 5502(b) lets an action for rent include late charges that have accrued as additional rent.

Non-payment of agreed rent is a ground for summary possession (§ 5702(2)). Before you file, § 5502(a) requires a written demand. You may send it only after the late-fee date. It must give the tenant at least 7 days to pay. The Delaware Courts Right of Redemption page says SS 1 to SB 116 raised this from 5 days to 7.

Three more points from the same sections:

- **Full payment stops the case.** If the tenant pays all rent due before you file, you may not file for possession on that non-payment (§ 5502(c)).
- **Right of redemption.** In a non-payment case, the tenant can pay all past-due amounts plus court costs and fees at any time before the eviction is carried out (§ 5716A). Most tenants with 2 non-payment judgments in 18 months do not get this right.
- **Deposit use.** Late charges are a lawful use of the security deposit at the end of the lease (§ 5514(c)(2)).

A case built only on late fees is a weak case to bring. Ask a Delaware attorney before you file one.

## A late fee checklist for Delaware

- Write the late fee in the lease, at or below 5% of monthly rent.
- Keep a payment office in the county, or move your due date 3 days later.
- Do not charge within 5 days of the due date.
- Cap any daily fee at 5% of monthly rent in total.
- Give a receipt for cash within 15 days. Keep cash records for 3 years.
- Send any demand for past-due rent after the late-fee date, with at least 7 days to pay.

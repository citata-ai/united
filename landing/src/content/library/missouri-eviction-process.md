Missouri has 2 main eviction tracks. Unpaid rent goes through "rent and possession" in RSMo chapter 535. A tenant who stays after the lease ends goes through "unlawful detainer" in RSMo chapter 534. We read each section from the Missouri Revisor of Statutes on 2026-10-19. The revisor site did not answer our requests, so we read its pages through Internet Archive copies.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Before filing for unpaid rent | Demand the rent; no notice period required | RSMo 535.020 |
| Where to file | Associate circuit judge in the county of the property | RSMo 535.020 |
| Court date | No more than 21 business days after the summons issues | RSMo 535.030.1 |
| Personal service | At least 4 days before the court date | RSMo 535.030.1 |
| Posting and mailing service | At least 10 days before the court date | RSMo 535.030.2 |
| Tenant motion or trial de novo | 10 days after judgment | RSMo 535.030.4 |
| Stay on appeal | Only with a bond, posted within 10 days | RSMo 535.110 |
| Officer delivers possession | Within 5 days of receiving the execution | RSMo 535.040.1 |
| Landlord retakes with police | If officer fails within 7 days; within 60 days of judgment | RSMo 441.060.5 |
| Lease violation (drugs, waste, unauthorized assignment, written lease breach) | 10 days' notice to vacate | RSMo 441.040 |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | One month's written notice, ending on a rent date | RSMo 441.060.4 |
| Lockout or utility shutoff | Illegal without a court order | RSMo 441.233 |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What notice must a Missouri landlord give before filing for unpaid rent?

A Missouri landlord must demand the rent, but no fixed notice period applies. RSMo 535.020 lets the landlord file once rent "has become due and payable", payment "has been demanded", and the tenant has not paid. The same section says the one-month notice in RSMo 441.060 "is not required" before a rent and possession case.

Keep proof of the demand. The landlord files a statement, verified by affidavit, that states the rental terms, the rent due and that the rent was demanded and not paid. The statement also describes the property.

You may join other unpaid sums from the lease, such as fees, in the same case. RSMo 535.020 says those sums are not rent. A judgment for them alone does not give you possession. Property damage cannot be joined.

## How long does an eviction take in Missouri?

A Missouri rent and possession case can reach possession in about 6 weeks. That figure is a hypothesis: we added the statute's maximums, and real court calendars vary. Here is the sequence from RSMo 535.030 and 535.040:

| Step | Statute limit | Running total |
|---|---|---|
| Rent demanded, not paid; file the statement | No waiting period | Day 0 |
| Clerk issues the summons | "Immediately" | Day 0–1 |
| Court date | Up to 21 business days (about 4 calendar weeks) | About week 4 |
| Judgment becomes final | 10 days after judgment | About week 5–6 |
| Officer delivers possession | Within 5 days of receiving the execution | About week 6 |

Things that add time:

- **The tenant pays.** If the tenant tenders all rent and costs on the judgment date, the case stops (RSMo 535.160).
- **Trial de novo or appeal.** The tenant has 10 days to apply. Execution is stayed only if the tenant posts a bond for rent, damages and costs within 10 days (RSMo 535.110).
- **Service problems.** If the officer cannot find the tenant, you can serve by posting and mailing. But then no money judgment is entered if the tenant defaults (RSMo 535.030.2).
- **You consent to a later date.** The 21-business-day limit applies unless you agree in writing to a later court date.

## Where does a landlord file, and what does it cost?

A Missouri landlord files with an associate circuit judge in the county where the property is. RSMo 535.020 sets this for rent and possession, and RSMo 534.060 does the same for unlawful detainer. Local court rules may send these cases to a central docket.

Fees differ by county. One example is the 16th Judicial Circuit (Jackson County, Kansas City). Its fee sheet dated 2026-09-01 lists these deposits:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Rent and possession filing deposit | $62.50 |
| Service fee per defendant, Jackson County deputy | $46.00 |
| Example: 2 adult defendants, deputy service | $62.50 + 2 × $46.00 = $154.50 |

Ask your county's circuit clerk for its current list before you file.

## Can a Missouri landlord evict for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes, a Missouri landlord can evict for some lease violations, with 10 days' notice. RSMo 441.040 gives the landlord the right to reenter after "ten days' notice to vacate" when the tenant violates RSMo 441.020 or 441.030. Those sections cover:

- assigning the lease without the landlord's written consent;
- breaking a condition of a written lease;
- waste (serious damage) to the premises;
- gambling, prostitution, or illegal drug possession, sale or distribution on the premises.

For drug-related crime or an emergency that threatens injury or major damage, RSMo 441.710 to 441.880 add an expedited eviction. A landlord or the local prosecutor may bring it (RSMo 441.710).

No-cause endings work through notice. A month-to-month tenancy ends with one month's written notice that ends on a rent-paying date (RSMo 441.060.4). An oral lease counts as month to month (RSMo 441.060.3). If the tenant holds over after the notice or after a fixed lease ends, that is "unlawful detainer" under RSMo 534.030.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Missouri?

The service officer removes the tenant, not the landlord. After judgment, the judge issues an execution, and RSMo 535.040.1 says the officer "shall deliver possession" to the landlord within 5 days of receiving it. In unlawful detainer cases, the execution for possession issues no sooner than 10 days after judgment (RSMo 534.350).

If the officer does not deliver possession within 7 days, RSMo 441.060.5 gives you a backup. Within 60 days of the judgment, you may change the locks and remove property, in the presence of a local law enforcement officer. Show the officer a true copy of the judgment and execution. The officer acknowledges this in writing, and you file that acknowledgment in court within 5 days.

Under RSMo 535.040.2, you are not liable for property left behind after a court-ordered execution, except for willful, wanton or malicious acts. Items labeled as a third party's property need a certified-mail notice and 5 business days to collect.

## Is a self-help eviction legal in Missouri?

No. RSMo 441.233 covers a landlord who removes a tenant or the tenant's property without "judicial process and court order". That landlord is guilty of forcible entry and detainer. Removing doors or locks counts too. So does a willful cutoff of essential services, such as electric, gas, water or sewer, unless done for health or safety reasons.

The only exception is abandonment under RSMo 441.065. It needs 30 days of unpaid rent, a posted and mailed notice, and 10 days with no reply from the tenant.

## What must you export before you cancel?

Export the records needed to explain money, contracts and open work before access ends. A list of contacts is not a complete archive.

Buildium's [onboarding page](https://www.buildium.com/features/onboarding/) names property, unit, lease, owner, resident and vendor data as import inputs. That describes a destination process. It does not prove your current system exports every field.

Make an export register before the new vendor starts:

| Record group | Check before cancellation |
|---|---|
| Properties and people | Stable IDs, addresses, ownership and contact details |
| Leases | Terms, dates, charges and signed documents |
| Money | Ledgers, deposits, reserves, unpaid bills and open items |
| Documents | File names, links, attachments and readable copies |
| Work | Open repairs, vendor assignments and scheduled tasks |
| History | Statements, messages and prior financial reports |

Open exported files outside the old product. Confirm that a document link still works without an active login. Ask how deleted or inactive records appear.

Keep a copy in storage your business controls. Restrict access to private records. Record who made the export and which period it covers.

Do not cancel on the strength of a salesperson's general assurance. Ask the person who runs the conversion to approve the register.

## How long does a migration take?

A migration needs a schedule based on your records and acceptance checks. There is no safe universal duration for every portfolio.

Rentvine's [pricing page](https://www.rentvine.com/pricing), read 2026-10-19, advertises a 45-day path to full onboarding. Treat that as the vendor's stated offer, not your guaranteed deadline. Ask what must be ready before the clock starts.

Buildium describes data import and a tailored training plan. Its page does not set a universal completion date. Your plan should name the source files, responsible staff and checkpoints.

Use stages rather than an unsupported date estimate:

- **Prepare.** Clean duplicate people, incomplete addresses and unresolved balances.
- **Map.** Agree which source field goes into which destination field.
- **Test.** Import a sample and record gaps.
- **Correct.** Repair the source or mapping, then repeat the check.
- **Approve.** Sign off on records, balances and live-payment setup.
- **Cut over.** Switch the agreed work and support users.

Ask which delay would move the launch date. A missing bank approval can matter as much as an import error.

## Should you run both systems in parallel?

Keep both systems available for checking, but define a single live record for each task. Parallel access does not mean duplicate payments or untracked double entry.

Use the old system for historical review while testing the new system. Compare the same tenant, owner and property across both. Record differences in a shared issue list.

At cutover, write down which system owns rent charges, payment collection, bills and work orders. Assign each switch to a named person. Confirm that old automatic jobs stop.

If a late payment arrives in the old system, use a documented exception process. Record where cash settled and how the new ledger reflects it. Do not post a second receipt without checking the bank.

Keep old portal notices clear. A tenant should not have to guess which payment screen is valid. Give staff a short script for answering questions.

Read-only access may have a cost or end date. Include that condition in the exit plan before cancellation.

## How do you reconcile balances after the move?

Reconcile the bank and detailed records at the same agreed cutoff. Approve the result before live owner payments leave the new system.

Save the final source reports. Compare tenant balances, deposits, owner funds, reserves, unpaid vendor bills and uncleared payments. Ask the accountant to explain each difference.

Use this acceptance record:

| Check | Evidence to retain |
|---|---|
| Bank cash | Statement and outstanding-item list |
| Owner balances | Source and destination detail |
| Tenant amounts | Charges, credits and unpaid rent |
| Deposit records | Liability detail linked to leases |
| Bills | Open invoices and payment status |
| Fees | Earned fees and unpaid amounts |

Do not fix a mismatch with an unexplained balancing entry. Find whether the cause is a missing transaction, mapping error or timing difference.

Test a returned payment after import. Confirm that the reversal reaches the correct tenant and owner. Test an imported bill that later clears.

Have the person responsible for the books sign the acceptance record. A vendor's import-success message does not replace financial approval.

## How do you tell owners and tenants?

Tell owners and tenants what changes, when it changes and how to verify instructions. Keep the notice practical and brief.

For owners, explain portal access, statement timing and the contact for questions. State whether payment timing changes. Avoid promising a service improvement you have not tested.

For tenants, explain the new payment portal and start date. Tell them whether they must set up automatic payments again. Confirm the actual process with the payment provider before sending instructions.

Use a notice checklist:

- **Name the sender.** Use an address people already know.
- **Give the date.** State the last old-system action and first new-system action.
- **Explain access.** Tell users where invitations arrive.
- **Explain payment setup.** Include verified steps and any changed fees.
- **Provide help.** Give a known phone number or contact.
- **Confirm receipt.** Track unresolved invitations and questions.

Do not ask users to send bank details through ordinary email. Direct them to the verified setup process.

## When is the best month to switch?

Choose a month with a clean close and enough staff time for support. There is no month that removes the need for testing.

Avoid a launch while key accounting staff are away. Consider renewals, inspection work and seasonal demands. If another major project shares the same staff, separate the launches where possible.

Set the cutover only after the test results pass. Keep a written fallback for failed payments, missing records or users who cannot log in.

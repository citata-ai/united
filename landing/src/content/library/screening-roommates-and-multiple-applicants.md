## Does each adult roommate apply separately?

Each proposed lease signer should have an identifiable application record under your reviewed screening process. Do not confuse every adult occupant with a person who must become a co-tenant.

New York Real Property Law section 235-f limits certain lease restrictions on occupants. That rule shows why a blanket demand that every occupant sign can need local review. [Read New York's occupancy statute](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/235-F).

Washington RCW 59.18.257 requires screening disclosures before obtaining information about a prospective tenant. Set that disclosure step for each person you actually screen. [Read Washington's screening rule](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=59.18.257).

Use a household cover record that links the applications without merging private reports. Record each person's proposed role: tenant, guarantor or permitted occupant. Ask counsel how local law affects those roles.

Avoid collecting unrelated family information to decide whether the group feels like a fit. Federal Fair Housing law restricts covered discrimination. Focus the process on lawful criteria and the actual housing arrangement.

## Do I combine incomes for the rent rule?

Use the household-income method you publish in your lawful criteria. Do not decide between combined income and individual income after reviewing the people.

Define what you mean by income and how you verify it. State whether the household meets the affordability standard together. Explain any lawful guarantor path before collecting fees.

Check for double counting. Roommates can share a business, bank account or support payment. The same money should not appear as separate income merely because it appears in several documents.

Ask for clarification when the source is unclear. Keep the explanation in the application file. A missing detail should lead to a specific request rather than a broad character judgment.

Use the same evidence approach for comparable income sources. Wage records, self-employment records and lawful assistance payments can require different documents. Different documents do not require different respect or a hidden standard.

Do not publish an income multiple as a legal requirement. Your affordability policy needs review for local law, housing program rules and protected income sources.

## What is joint and several liability?

Joint and several liability can make each liable party responsible for the whole obligation. Cornell's Legal Information Institute explains the general concept. Its operation in a rental depends on the lease and applicable law. [Read Cornell's definition](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/joint_and_several_liability).

Explain the actual lease in plain words before signing. A roommate's private agreement to pay a share may not limit the obligation to the landlord. Ask counsel to approve the clause and explanation.

Keep the account structure aligned with the contract. A shared lease and separate room leases are different arrangements. Do not create separate contractual balances simply because your portal accepts split payments.

| Issue | Shared lease question | Record to keep |
|---|---|---|
| Rent | What obligation does each signer accept? | Reviewed lease terms |
| Payment split | How will partial payments post? | Account policy |
| Deposit | Who holds the return claim? | Deposit terms and receipts |
| Departure | What creates a release? | Signed amendment |
| Replacement | What approval is required? | Application and consent |
| Dispute | Who receives account information? | Access and communication policy |

The table prompts contract review. It does not establish liability by itself.

## What happens when a roommate leaves?

A departure needs a documented lease review, even if the remaining roommates agree privately. Record the proposed date and whether the group wants a replacement, release or full move-out.

Do not promise release in a casual message. Check who must consent and whether a guarantor's approval matters. Have counsel review the proposed amendment before removing a signer from the system.

Screen a proposed replacement through the lawful process that applies to that person. Check whether occupancy rights limit the approval requirement. A replacement applicant should receive the relevant disclosures and criteria.

Review deposit treatment separately. Do not refund part of the deposit from the held funds merely because the roommates calculate a private share. Ask how the governing law and lease handle a continuing tenancy.

Update names, contact details and access permissions only after the decision is complete. Preserve the old lease and amendment. A future account reviewer needs to know who held duties during each period.

## How do I document the household decision?

Keep a clear decision record for the group and separate support for each screened person. The record should show the criteria, evidence and outcome without sharing private reports among roommates.

The FTC explains that adverse action based partly on a consumer report requires notice. Review who receives the notice when a person's report affects the household outcome. [Read the FTC landlord guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/using-consumer-reports-what-landlords-need-know).

Use a recommended closeout checklist:

- **Confirm roles.** Identify the proposed tenants, occupants and guarantors.
- **Verify disclosures.** Save the criteria and notices given before screening.
- **Review income.** Apply the published method without duplicate receipts.
- **Explain obligations.** Make sure the shared rent and deposit terms are clear.
- **Record the decision.** Keep the basis and any required notices.
- **Set change rules.** Define how later departures and replacements receive review.

Give staff a script for account questions that protects each person's sensitive data. A shared lease does not mean every roommate should receive another person's full credit report.

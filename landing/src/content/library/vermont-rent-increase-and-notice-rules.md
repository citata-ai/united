A Vermont landlord mails a rent increase on October 20. The new rent cannot start in November or even December. Under 9 V.S.A. 4455(b), the first possible date is January 1. This page shows why, with the statute text as read on legislature.vermont.gov on 2026-10-19. The site says its text includes the actions of the 2026 session.

## Does Vermont Have a Cap on Rent Increases or Rent Control?

Vermont's landlord-tenant law sets no cap on how much a landlord may raise the rent. 9 V.S.A. chapter 137, "Residential Rental Agreements", controls the timing of an increase in section 4455. We found no section in the chapter that limits the amount.

Two limits still apply:

- **Retaliation.** 9 V.S.A. 4465(a) bars a landlord from "establishing or changing terms of a rental agreement" to punish a tenant who complained to a housing agency, complained to the landlord under chapter 137, or joined a tenant union. A rent increase is a change of terms. The tenant can recover damages and attorney's fees (4465(b)).
- **Subsidy contracts.** Subsidized housing can have its own rent limits from a state or federal agency. Section 4468a(c) mentions housing "monitored by a State or federal agency for rent limitations." Check your program rules.

## How Many Days' Notice Does a Vermont Landlord Need to Raise the Rent?

A Vermont landlord needs at least 60 days' actual notice to raise the rent. The text of 9 V.S.A. 4455(b): "An increase in rent shall take effect on the first day of the rental period following no less than 60 days' actual notice to the tenant."

So two tests apply. First, 60 full days must pass after the tenant gets the notice. Second, the new rent starts only at the start of the next rental period after that.

"Actual notice" is defined in 9 V.S.A. 4451(1). It means written notice, hand-delivered or mailed to the last known address. If you send it by first-class or certified mail, the law presumes the tenant got it 3 days after mailing. A text message or a phone call is not written notice under that definition.

## Worked Example: When Does the New Rent Start?

Here is the date math for a monthly tenancy with rent due on the 1st. The 3-day mail rule comes from 4451(1).

| Step | Date |
|---|---|
| You mail the notice by first-class mail | October 20, 2026 |
| Presumed receipt (mail date + 3 days) | October 23, 2026 |
| 60 days after receipt | December 22, 2026 |
| First day of the next rental period | January 1, 2027 |
| New rent starts | January 1, 2027 |

Now hand-deliver the same notice on October 20. The 60 days end on December 19. The next rental period still starts on January 1. To start the new rent on December 1, the tenant must get the notice by October 1, so that 60 full days end on November 30.

Rule of thumb: get the notice to the tenant at least 2 full rental periods plus a few days before the start date you want. Keep a copy and your proof of mailing.

## Can a Vermont Landlord Raise the Rent During a Fixed-Term Lease?

A fixed-term lease sets the rent for the term. 9 V.S.A. 4451(7) and (8) define rent and the rental agreement as the agreed terms between the parties. Section 4455(b) sets the notice for an increase, but it does not give a landlord a right to change the agreed rent mid-term.

In practice:

1. **Read the lease.** If the lease does not allow a mid-term increase, plan the increase for the renewal.
2. **Give 60 days' actual notice anyway.** The notice rule in 4455(b) does not have an exception for renewals.
3. **Pair it with the renewal offer.** Send the new rent and the new term together, well before the end date.

Ask a Vermont attorney before you rely on a lease clause that allows a mid-term change.

## How Much Notice Ends a Month-to-Month Tenancy in Vermont?

A Vermont landlord needs at least 60 days' notice to end a month-to-month tenancy for no cause when there is no written lease. The notice rises to 90 days if the tenant has lived there continuously for more than 2 years (9 V.S.A. 4467(c)(1)).

| Situation | Minimum notice | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly rent, no written lease, 2 years or less | 60 days | 4467(c)(1)(A) |
| Monthly rent, no written lease, more than 2 years | 90 days | 4467(c)(1)(B) |
| Weekly rent, no written lease | 21 days | 4467(c)(2) |
| Written lease, tenancy of 2 years or less | 30 days before end of term | 4467(e) |
| Written lease, tenancy of more than 2 years | 60 days before end of term | 4467(e) |
| Written week-to-week lease | 7 days | 4467(e) |
| Building under contract for sale, no written lease | 30 days | 4467(d) |
| Room in the owner's home with shared space, monthly rent | 15 days | 4467(h) |

Every notice must state the exact termination date (4467(f)). If the tenant does not leave, you must start the court case no later than 60 days after that date (4467(k)).

## Do Cities in Vermont Add Their Own Rent or Notice Rules?

Some Vermont housing has extra state rules, and towns can add some local rules. Two examples are in the statute:

- **Age-restricted housing.** An owner of private age-restricted housing that is not a licensed care facility must give written notice of any rent increase to all affected residents and to the Department of Housing and Community Development. The notice is due no later than 60 days before the increase, on the department's form. It must show the amount, the date, the percent increase and a copy of the residents' rights. Without it, the increase is "ineffective and unenforceable" (9 V.S.A. 4468a). The section took effect on June 17, 2024.
- **Security deposit ordinances.** 9 V.S.A. 4461(g) lets a town adopt a deposit ordinance that adds to state law.

We did not verify any city rent ordinance for this page. Before you raise rent in Burlington or another city, check the city code and any housing board rules.

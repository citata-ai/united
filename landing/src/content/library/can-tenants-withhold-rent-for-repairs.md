## Can tenants withhold rent for repairs?

Tenants can withhold rent or pay less rent only where state law gives them that right, and only after they follow its steps. No federal rule covers this. Each state sets its own remedies, and some cities add more.

Most state remedies share 3 conditions:

1. **A serious defect.** The problem makes the home unfit, or affects health or safety. A slow drain or a dent in a door usually does not count.
2. **Notice.** The tenant told the landlord about the problem. Many states want written notice.
3. **Time to fix.** The landlord had a reasonable time to repair and did not.

If a tenant skips these steps, the tenant usually owes the rent. But a landlord who ignores a real defect can lose an eviction case. So treat each repair request as a legal event, not only a chore.

## What is repair and deduct?

Repair and deduct lets a tenant pay for a repair the landlord did not make, then take the cost out of the next rent. States that allow it set a dollar cap and a notice rule. Compare 4 states:

| State | Remedy | Cap and limits | Notice and time | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| California | Repair and deduct, or move out | Up to 1 month's rent; no more than twice in any 12 months | Written or oral notice; after 30 days the tenant is presumed to have waited a reasonable time | Civil Code 1942 |
| Texas | Repair and deduct for listed conditions | 1 month's rent or $500, whichever is greater, per month | Notice under 92.056, and one notice must say the tenant plans to repair | Property Code 92.0561 |
| Florida | Rent defense in court | The court decides how much to reduce the rent | 7 days after written notice that names the problem and says the tenant will withhold | Statute 83.60(1)(b) |
| New York | Warranty of habitability | The court decides any rent reduction | The tenant must not have caused the condition | Real Property Law 235-b |

Texas limits repair and deduct to a short list of conditions in Property Code 92.0561(d)(3). The list includes raw sewage backup inside the home, flooding from broken pipes, total loss of water the landlord agreed to supply, and heating or cooling that the landlord agreed to supply but that does not work well. In California, Civil Code 1942(c) removes the remedy when the tenant caused the damage.

A worked example in Texas: rent is $1,200. The deduction cap is the greater of $1,200 or $500, so $1,200 a month. In California, the same tenant could repair up to $1,200, but only twice in 12 months.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What notice must a tenant give?

The tenant must tell the landlord about the defect before using any remedy. The form and the wait time depend on the state.

- **California.** Civil Code 1942 accepts written or oral notice to the landlord or agent. If the tenant repairs after the 30th day, the law presumes the wait was reasonable. A shorter wait can also be valid if the facts require it.
- **Texas.** Property Code 92.0561(d)(2) requires the notice that 92.056 describes. At least one notice must say the tenant plans to repair, with a reasonable description of the repair.
- **Florida.** Statute 83.60(1)(b) needs written notice that names the problem and says the tenant will not pay rent because of it. The defense is open after 7 days.

Florida adds a rule that protects landlords too. Under Statute 83.60(2), a tenant who raises any defense other than payment must pay the alleged rent into the court registry. If the tenant does not pay it, or move the court to set the amount, within 5 business days after service, the tenant waives those defenses.

## What is the warranty of habitability?

The warranty of habitability is the landlord's legal promise that the rental is fit to live in. New York Real Property Law 235-b puts this promise into every written or oral residential lease. It covers the unit and the shared areas. Under 235-b, tenants must not face conditions that threaten their life, health or safety. The landlord does not breach the warranty when the tenant, or people under the tenant's control, caused the condition.

Many other states have a similar rule, in a statute or in court cases. The warranty is the reason a tenant can raise repairs as a defense when you sue for rent. Some states let a written lease shift some repair duties; Texas Property Code 92.0561(d)(1) refers to waivers under Section 92.006. Check your state before you rely on an "as is" clause.

## How do I avoid a rent withholding dispute?

Answer repair requests fast and keep proof of each step. Most withholding fights start with a request that sat for weeks. Use this checklist:

1. **One intake channel.** Give tenants one portal, email or phone line for repairs. Log every request with a date and time.
2. **Same-day reply.** Confirm the request the day it arrives. Give a repair window in writing.
3. **Triage.** Sort each request: emergency (no heat, no water, sewage, gas, flooding), urgent (in days), or routine.
4. **Entry notice.** Give the notice to enter that your state requires. Note the date and time you sent it.
5. **Proof.** Keep the invoice and before and after photos for each repair.
6. **Close the loop.** Ask the tenant to confirm the fix in writing.
7. **No retaliation.** Do not raise rent, cut services or file an eviction because a tenant complained.

Retaliation rules can turn a rent case against you. In California, Civil Code 1942.5(a) bars a landlord from evicting, raising rent or cutting services within 180 days after a tenant's good-faith repair notice or complaint, if the tenant is not behind on rent. Other states have their own time windows.

If a tenant already withholds rent, ask for the complaint in writing. Inspect the unit, and fix real defects at once. Then talk about the rent. A short agreement that credits a fair amount for the days without service often costs less than a court case.

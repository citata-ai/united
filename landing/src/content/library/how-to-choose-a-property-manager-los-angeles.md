## Which license must a Los Angeles property manager hold, and how do I look it up?

A Los Angeles property manager who rents your units or collects your rent for a fee must hold a California Department of Real Estate (DRE) license. Business and Professions Code 10131(b) defines a real estate broker as a person who, for compensation, "leases or rents or offers to lease or rent," solicits tenants, or "collects rents from real property" for others.

How to check a manager in 4 steps:

1. **Ask for numbers.** Get the license number of the person and of the broker firm.
2. **Search the DRE.** Use the Public License Lookup at dre.ca.gov.
3. **Match the names.** The firm on your contract should match the broker on the DRE record.
4. **Read the record.** Check that the license is active and see if any discipline is listed. Ask about anything you find.

A manager with no license, or a firm whose name does not match the record, is a reason to stop.

## Which local rules must a Los Angeles manager know?

A good Los Angeles manager can tell you which city program covers each of your units, and what it costs you. Use these as test questions.

| Rule | What it says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| RSO coverage | Generally, rentals first built on or before October 1, 1978: apartments, duplexes, 2+ houses on one parcel, ADUs, JADUs | LAHD RSO overview |
| RSO yearly increase | 3% for July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027 | California Attorney General chart |
| RSO utilities add-on | Since February 2, 2026, no extra percentage for utilities | LAHD RSO overview |
| JCO coverage | Most units not under the RSO, including newer buildings and some single-family homes, once the tenant has lived there 6 months or the first lease has expired | LAHD JCO page |
| Relocation | Required for no-fault evictions under the RSO and JCO, such as owner move-in or demolition | LAHD |
| Rent Registry | Pay the yearly fee and report each unit's rent by the last day of February | LAHD Rent Registry |
| Right to Counsel notice | Since August 20, 2025, post it in a common area and give it at move-in and with any eviction notice | LAHD JCO page |
| Deposit cap | One month's rent; up to 2 months for small landlords (2 properties, 4 units max), not for service members | Civil Code 1950.5(c) |

A wrong answer on any line can cost you money or a case in court.

Worked example: an RSO two-bedroom at $2,964 a month, HUD's FY2027 Fair Market Rent for the LA metro. The most you can add from July 2026 to June 2027 is 3%, or $88.92 a month. A manager who suggests a statewide AB 1482 increase of 8.7% instead, $257.87, has the wrong rule for that unit. See the [Los Angeles Fair Market Rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/los-angeles-fair-market-rent-2027) for other sizes.

## What should a Los Angeles management agreement say?

A Los Angeles management agreement should name each duty and each fee in plain words. Check for these items:

- **Fees.** The monthly fee, leasing fee, renewal fee, and any markup on vendor bills.
- **Rent rules.** Who checks the RSO or JCO status of each unit and sets increases within the cap.
- **City filings.** Who pays the RSO, JCO and SCEP bill and files the Rent Registry each February.
- **Notices.** Who posts the Registration Certificate and the Right to Counsel notice.
- **Evictions.** Who decides, who pays relocation, and which lawyer handles cases.
- **Deposits.** Where they are held and how the manager returns them under Civil Code 1950.5.
- **Repair limit.** The dollar amount the manager may spend without your approval.
- **Exit.** The notice period, any cancellation fee, and how files and deposits come back to you.
- **License.** The broker's name and DRE number in the contract.

## Which questions should I ask a Los Angeles manager before I sign?

Ask questions that need a document or a number, not a promise. Twelve to start with:

1. What are your DRE license number and your broker's number?
2. How many doors do you manage in the City of Los Angeles, and how many under the RSO?
3. How do you check if a unit is under the RSO or the JCO?
4. What is this year's RSO allowable increase, and when does it change?
5. Who files my Rent Registry, and can I see last year's confirmation?
6. How do you handle relocation payments for a no-fault eviction?
7. Show me a sample monthly owner statement.
8. What is your repair approval limit, and do you mark up vendor bills?
9. How do you screen applicants with one written standard for everyone?
10. How do you return deposits, and what was your last deposit dispute?
11. What happens to my deposits and files if I end the contract?
12. Can I call 2 owners who have used you for more than 2 years?

The screening question matters for Fair Housing. One written standard, applied to all, protects you and the applicant.

## Text, email, portal or phone?

Each channel fits one kind of message. Mixing them is where requests get lost. Pick a channel of record, which is the one place every request and notice must land, and tell tenants about it at move-in.

| Channel | Best for | Weak spot | Record kept? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tenant portal | Repair requests, rent, documents | Some tenants do not log in | Yes, with time stamps |
| Email | Notices where the law allows, long answers, photos | Spam filters, slow replies | Yes |
| Text (SMS) | Visit windows, reminders, "vendor on the way" | Easy to miss, hard to file | Only if you save it |
| Phone | Emergencies: water, gas, fire, no heat | No record unless you write a note | No |
| Paper letter | Legal notices that a statute requires | Slow | Yes, with proof of mailing |

A simple rule for a small portfolio:

1. **Portal or email** is the channel of record for requests.
2. **Text** confirms times and sends short updates.
3. **Phone** is for emergencies only, with a 24-hour line.
4. **Paper** is for notices that the statute says must be delivered a set way.

If a tenant reports a repair by text or phone, copy it into the portal or log yourself. Then the record is complete.

## What should always be in writing?

Anything with a legal effect or a deadline goes in writing. Many state statutes say so directly.

- **Entry notices.** California Civil Code 1954(d) requires reasonable written notice before entry, with the date, approximate time and purpose. It presumes 24 hours is reasonable.
- **Tenant's notice before withholding rent.** In Florida, a tenant can raise the landlord's repair failure as a defense. The tenant must first give written notice and wait 7 days (Fla. Stat. 83.60).
- **Repair requests, when the lease says so.** Texas Property Code 92.052(d) requires written notice only if the lease is written and requires it.
- **Notices by email.** Florida Statutes 83.505 lets either side deliver notices by e-mail only after both sign an addendum. The addendum must say the choice is voluntary and can be revoked.

Also put these in writing, even where no statute requires it:

- [ ] Rent changes and renewal offers.
- [ ] Lease violations and the date to cure them.
- [ ] Move-out instructions and the inspection date.
- [ ] Any agreement to change the lease, such as a pet or a new occupant.
- [ ] A summary after a phone call about a dispute.
- [ ] Office hours and the emergency number.

A written summary after a call takes 2 minutes. Send it the same day: "Per our call today, the plumber will come on Thursday between 10:00 a.m. and 12:00 p.m."

## How fast should I reply?

Reply to emergencies at once, to urgent repairs the same day, and to routine messages within 1 business day. State law sets the outer limit for repairs, and courts look at what you did after notice.

Two examples of how states set the clock:

- **Texas.** Property Code 92.056 sets 7 days as a presumed reasonable time to repair. It applies to a condition that materially affects health or safety. The severity of the condition and the supply of labor and materials can move that number.
- **California.** Civil Code 1942 lets a tenant repair and deduct if the landlord neglects a repair after notice. A tenant who acts after the 30th day is presumed to have waited a reasonable time.

A response-time standard you can publish:

| Request type | Examples | First reply | Fix target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emergency | Water leak, gas smell, no heat in winter, fire damage | Within 1 hour | Make safe the same day |
| Urgent | No hot water, broken lock, fridge out | Same day | 1–3 days |
| Routine | Dripping faucet, loose cabinet door | 1 business day | Within 7 days |
| Questions | Lease, rent ledger, parking | 1 business day | Not applicable |

These targets are our suggestion, not a legal rule. Set them so you can meet them every time. A promise you break hurts trust more than a slower promise you keep.

## How do I set office hours?

Office hours tell tenants when to expect a reply. A separate emergency line covers the rest of the day.

Steps to set them up:

1. **Pick the hours.** For example, Monday to Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.
2. **Define an emergency.** List 5–8 examples: active water leak, gas smell, fire, no heat, sewage backup, a door that will not lock.
3. **Set the emergency line.** Use a business number that forwards to the person on call.
4. **Add an auto-reply.** After hours, email and portal messages get a reply with the emergency number.
5. **Put it in the lease packet.** Add the hours, the channel of record and the emergency list.
6. **Repeat it twice a year.** A short message in January and July helps.

## How do I keep communication fair to every tenant?

Use the same channels, templates and reply times for every tenant. Consistent treatment is the base of Fair Housing compliance.

Some tenants need a different format. The Fair Housing Act regulation at 24 CFR 100.204 covers this. It makes it unlawful to refuse reasonable accommodations in "rules, policies, practices, or services" that a person with a disability needs. A tenant who cannot use a portal may need phone or paper instead. Treat that request as a reasonable accommodation request and answer it in writing.

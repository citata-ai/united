## Is LIHTC a subsidy to the owner or the tenant?

LIHTC is a subsidy to the owner, and Section 8 is a subsidy to the tenant. The Low-Income Housing Tax Credit, in 26 U.S.C. 42, gives the owner federal tax credits for a 10-year credit period (42(f)(1)). The owner pays for it with capped rents and income limits on a share of units, for at least 30 years.

A Housing Choice Voucher works the other way. The PHA pays a housing assistance payment (HAP) to the owner each month on behalf of one household (24 CFR 982.505). The voucher follows the family when it moves. The owner can be in the program for one lease, then leave.

## Table: side-by-side terms

| Term | LIHTC | Section 8 voucher (HCV) |
|---|---|---|
| Who gets the subsidy | Owner, as tax credits | Tenant, paid to the owner as HAP |
| Federal agency | IRS (Treasury), via state credit agencies | HUD, via local PHAs |
| Main rule | 26 U.S.C. 42; 26 CFR 1.42-5 | 24 CFR part 982 |
| Tied to | The building, for 30+ years | The household, for each lease |
| Income limit | 50% or 60% of AMI, or an average of 60% (42(g)(1)) | HUD income limits, checked by the PHA at admission |
| Rent cap | Gross rent ≤ 30% of the imputed income limit (42(g)(2)) | Reasonable rent; HAP capped by the payment standard |
| Tenant pays | Up to the LIHTC rent, whatever their income | About 30% of adjusted income (24 CFR 5.628) |
| Length | 15-year compliance period plus 15+ years extended use | Initial lease of at least 1 year (24 CFR 982.309) |
| Inspections | Agency inspects at least every 3 years | PHA inspects before move-in and at least every 2 years |
| Who audits | State or local housing credit agency, reports to IRS | PHA, overseen by HUD |
| Must accept the other? | Must not refuse voucher holders (42(h)(6)(B)(iv)) | No federal rule; check local law |

## Can a voucher holder rent a LIHTC unit?

A voucher holder can rent a LIHTC unit, and a LIHTC owner may not refuse one for holding a voucher. 26 U.S.C. 42(h)(6)(B)(iv) requires the extended use commitment to prohibit "the refusal to lease to a holder of a voucher or certificate of eligibility under section 8." The commitment binds later owners and is recorded as a restrictive covenant.

Two income tests apply to that household. It must qualify for the voucher, and it must be under the LIHTC income limit for the unit. The voucher payment does not count as LIHTC rent: 42(g)(2)(B)(i) says LIHTC gross rent "does not include any payment under section 8."

## Can a LIHTC rent be above the LIHTC limit with a voucher?

The total rent to owner can be above the LIHTC limit with a voucher, within HUD limits. 24 CFR 982.507(c) sets three cases:

1. **Rent at or under the LIHTC rent for non-voucher units.** No market comparison is needed. The PHA can approve it as reasonable (982.507(c)(1)).
2. **Rent above the LIHTC rent.** The PHA must run a normal reasonable rent test against similar unassisted units (982.507(c)(2)).
3. **The cap.** The rent may not exceed the lower of the reasonable rent and the PHA's payment standard for the unit size (982.507(c)(2)).

A worked example, with made-up numbers:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| LIHTC maximum gross rent, 2 bedrooms (60% AMI, example) | $1,500 |
| LIHTC rent, tenant pays utilities ($150 allowance) | $1,350 |
| PHA payment standard, 2 bedrooms (example) | $1,800 |
| Reasonable rent from market comparables (example) | $1,700 |
| Maximum rent to owner with a voucher | $1,700 (lower of $1,700 and $1,800) |
| Family Total Tenant Payment | $450 |
| Gross rent with the voucher ($1,700 + $150) | $1,850 |
| HAP (payment standard − TTP, since gross rent is higher) | $1,350 |
| Family rent to owner | $350 |

In this example the gross rent of $1,850 is above the payment standard, so the family pays $50 more than its TTP. The PHA must also check the 40% cap at move-in. An owner who wants the family to pay only its TTP can set the rent so that gross rent equals the payment standard: here, $1,650.

## Which has stricter inspections?

For one unit, Section 8 inspects more often. 24 CFR 982.405 requires the PHA to inspect each unit before the lease and at least every 2 years after. Under 26 CFR 1.42-5(c)(2), the LIHTC agency must inspect all buildings and review a sample of low-income unit files by the end of the second year after the last building is placed in service, then at least once every 3 years.

The standards overlap. The LIHTC agency may use local codes or HUD's physical condition standards in 24 CFR 5.703 (26 CFR 1.42-5(d)(2)). PHAs move to the same 5.703 standards for vouchers by February 1, 2027. So a LIHTC unit with a voucher tenant can face two inspectors using similar rules on different schedules.

| Inspection point | LIHTC | Section 8 |
|---|---|---|
| Before move-in | No per-unit rule in 26 CFR 1.42-5 | Yes, every unit |
| Routine frequency | At least every 3 years, sample of units | At least every 2 years, every assisted unit |
| Penalty for failure | Noncompliance report to IRS; credit risk | HAP abatement, then contract ends |

## What this means for an owner

- **Owning a LIHTC property:** expect voucher applicants and treat them like everyone else. Keep both income tests in your file.
- **Owning a market-rate property:** you may choose voucher tenants where local law allows; no LIHTC rules apply.
- **Pricing a LIHTC unit for a voucher tenant:** check the payment standard and a rent reasonableness estimate first.

## What are the more-than-750-hours and more-than-half tests?

IRS Publication 925 requires both annual work tests, not a choice between them.

You must perform more than 750 hours in real property trades or businesses in which you materially participate. More than half your personal services in all trades or businesses must also fall within those qualifying activities. Meeting only the hours test is not enough.

Publication 925 identifies real property activities such as development, construction, rental, operation, management, and brokerage. The actual work and participation matter. Owning a rental or holding a real estate license does not by itself establish the status.

Consider an illustrative case. Assume you perform 800 qualifying real property hours and 1,600 hours in unrelated employment. The real property hours exceed 750 but do not exceed half the combined work. Those assumed figures show why a separate job can affect the result.

| Test | What to document |
|---|---|
| Qualifying activity | Nature of the real property work |
| Annual qualifying hours | Services and supporting records |
| More-than-half test | Work across all trades and businesses |
| Material participation | Applicable test and actual activity facts |
| Loss limits | Basis, amount at risk, and other restrictions |

Publication 925 includes special rules for employee services. Discuss them with your preparer rather than counting all real estate employment automatically. Review the tests every year because your work pattern can change.

## What records support real estate professional hours?

Records should identify the work, date, property or activity, time spent, and evidence supporting the entry.

Publication 925 allows any reasonable method of proving participation. It says contemporaneous daily logs are not always required when other evidence establishes the work. It names calendars, appointment books, and narrative summaries as possible support. That flexibility does not make an unsupported guess reliable.

A current task log is a practical way to avoid later reconstruction. Link entries to emails, invoices, site visits, vendor appointments, and work orders. Describe the service rather than writing a vague property management label. Keep unrelated employment records for the more-than-half comparison.

Separate active work from investor-type review and personal use. Publication 925 discusses participation as an investor and other exclusions. Ask your preparer which tasks count under the test you intend to use. Do not count every minute spent thinking about a future investment.

Spouse rules also need care. Publication 925 says you do not combine spouse services to meet the professional-status requirements on a joint return. Spouse participation can matter for material participation in an activity. These are different tests with different uses of the records.

Ask your preparer to review the method before the year ends. An early review can identify missing evidence. It cannot create hours you did not work or turn an ineligible task into qualifying service.

## How does material participation differ from professional status?

Professional status addresses the rental classification exception, while material participation evaluates your work in the relevant activity.

Publication 925 explains several material participation tests. One test uses more than 500 hours of participation. Other tests use different facts and conditions. Do not assume that professional status automatically meets a separate activity test.

Ask how each rental is treated for participation purposes. Publication 527 discusses the choice to treat rental real estate interests as one activity. A grouping choice needs tax review because it can affect current treatment and later dispositions. Do not group properties in a spreadsheet and assume that creates a valid tax election.

A property manager's work matters to the factual analysis. For example, a participation test can depend on how your work compares with other people's work. Obtain service records where relevant. Do not assume a manager's time counts as your personal work.

Keep the investment decision separate. Hiring a manager can improve service and reduce owner workload, even if it affects a hoped-for tax result. Use the [management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to compare operating cost. Ask your tax adviser to analyze participation under the resulting service model.

Review other restrictions as well. Publication 925 discusses at-risk and passive activity limits, and other tax limits can apply. A nonpassive label does not establish unlimited deductions against all income.

## Why does this status need careful audit support?

The status depends on detailed facts, so your records must support both the annual tests and the claimed losses.

This guide does not claim that a specific share of real estate professional returns receives an audit. No verified audit-rate figure supports that claim here. The practical concern is whether you can explain each condition and provide the evidence behind it.

Reconcile the claimed hours with the rest of your calendar. Check overlapping appointments, employment schedules, and personal trips. Resolve duplicate entries and vague descriptions before filing. Do not increase a log merely to cross a threshold.

Keep the loss calculation separate from the time file. Preserve depreciation schedules, invoices, lease records, ownership documents, and prior suspended loss records. A strong time log cannot fix an unsupported expense. A valid expense can still face a deduction limit.

Ask your preparer for a plain explanation of the filing position. It should identify the tests, any grouping choice, and the records used. Review uncertain employee services, travel, and investor tasks before signing the return.

If the facts do not support status, plan using the rules that do apply. A rental should still have sound cash flow and reserves without a hoped-for wage offset. Base the purchase and management plan on that operating budget.

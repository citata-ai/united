## Which clauses must every residential lease have?

Every residential lease needs clauses that answer 4 questions: who, where, how long and how much. The rest of the lease sets the rules for money, repairs, access and the end of the tenancy. Use this checklist when you write or review a lease.

| # | Clause | What it says | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Parties | Every adult tenant and the landlord or agent | Each adult is bound by the lease |
| 2 | Premises | Address, unit number, parking, storage | No dispute about what is rented |
| 3 | Term | Start date, end date, or month-to-month | Sets the notice to end the tenancy |
| 4 | Rent | Amount, due date, where and how to pay | Arizona's default is rent due at the start of each month (A.R.S. 33-1314(C)) |
| 5 | Late fee | Amount, grace period | Must match state limits |
| 6 | Deposit | Amount, what it covers, how it is returned | Must match state caps and deadlines |
| 7 | Nonrefundable fees | Name and purpose of each fee | Arizona treats a fee not marked nonrefundable as refundable |
| 8 | Utilities | Who pays which service | Avoids shut-off disputes |
| 9 | Repairs | How to report, who fixes what | Habitability duties cannot be waived |
| 10 | Entry | Notice and hours for entry | Must match state entry law |
| 11 | Occupants and guests | Who may live there | Sets the basis for a lease breach |
| 12 | Pets | Allowed pets, fees, rules | Assistance animals are not pets |
| 13 | Notices | Address and method for notices | A bad notice can sink an eviction |
| 14 | Lead paint | Federal warning for pre-1978 homes | Federal law |
| 15 | Attorney fees | Prevailing party only, where allowed | Some states limit fee clauses |
| 16 | Severability | Void terms do not void the lease | Protects the rest of the lease |

## Which clauses cover rent, fees and late payment?

The rent, late fee, deposit and fee clauses cover money. State law limits each one, so check the cap before you fill in a number.

- **Late fee.** New York Real Property Law 238-A allows a late fee only after rent is 5 days late. The fee "shall not exceed fifty dollars or five percent of the monthly rent, whichever is less." Arizona refers to "a reasonable late fee set forth in a written rental agreement" (A.R.S. 33-1368(B)). Put the exact amount in the lease.
- **Deposit cap.** Arizona caps security, "however denominated," at 1.5 months' rent (A.R.S. 33-1321(A)). New York General Obligations Law 7-108 caps a deposit or advance at one month's rent for most units.
- **Other fees.** New York RPL 238-A bars most fees before or at the start of a tenancy. The exception is a background and credit check fee of the actual cost or $20, whichever is less. Arizona requires the purpose of every nonrefundable fee in writing (A.R.S. 33-1321(B)).

Worked check for New York: on $2,400 rent, 5% is $120. The cap is the lesser of $50 and $120, so the most you can charge is $50.

## What maintenance clauses do I need?

A maintenance clause tells the tenant how to report a repair and lists what each side must do. It cannot shift the landlord's legal duties to the tenant.

Arizona lists the landlord's duties in A.R.S. 33-1324. They include keeping the home "in a fit and habitable condition" and keeping plumbing, heating and air-conditioning in good working order. New York RPL 235-B makes the landlord warrant the home is "fit for human habitation". Any tenant waiver of that warranty "shall be void".

Write these into the maintenance clause:

- How to report a repair: portal, email or phone, and an emergency number.
- What the tenant handles: light bulbs, batteries, damage they cause.
- That a repair request gives permission to enter for that repair. Arizona law already says this in A.R.S. 33-1343(B).
- Who pays for damage the tenant, a guest or a pet causes.

## Which clauses cover entry and access?

An entry clause states when and how you may enter the unit. Match it to state law, because the notice periods differ.

| State | Notice to enter | Hours | Law |
|---|---|---|---|
| Florida | At least 24 hours for repairs | 7:30 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. | Fla. Stat. 83.53(2) |
| Arizona | At least 2 days, except emergencies | "Reasonable times" | A.R.S. 33-1343(D) |

Both states let the landlord enter without notice in an emergency. Both also bar a landlord from using entry to harass the tenant.

## What federal clause does a pre-1978 home need?

A home built before 1978 needs the federal lead paint disclosure. The EPA says the landlord must, before the lease is signed:

1. **Give the pamphlet** "Protect Your Family From Lead In Your Home".
2. **Disclose known lead paint** and lead hazards, and give any reports.
3. **Include a Lead Warning Statement** in the lease or attached to it, in the same language as the lease.
4. **Keep the signed copy** for 3 years after the lease starts.

The EPA lists exemptions. They include leases of 100 days or less, zero-bedroom units, and housing tested lead-free by a certified inspector.

## Which clauses are often unenforceable?

Many states void lease clauses that take away a tenant's rights under state law. A void clause does not help you in court, and it can cost you money.

- **Florida:** Fla. Stat. 83.47 voids any provision that waives the tenant's rights under the landlord-tenant law or limits either side's liability under law. The other side can recover actual damages.
- **Arizona:** A.R.S. 33-1315 voids clauses that waive tenant rights, make the tenant pay the landlord's attorney fees in all cases, or limit the landlord's liability. It also voids clauses that penalize a tenant for calling police or emergency help. A landlord who knowingly uses such a clause can owe up to 2 months' rent.
- **New York:** a waiver of the warranty of habitability is void (RPL 235-B).

Common risky clauses: "tenant waives all repairs," "landlord not liable for any injury," "tenant pays all attorney fees," and "deposit is nonrefundable" without a stated purpose.

## Do I need a severability clause?

Yes, a severability clause is cheap protection. It says that if a court strikes one term, the rest of the lease stays in force. State laws void some common terms, as shown above, so one bad clause should not take down the whole lease.

A sample line: "If any part of this lease is found invalid, the other parts remain in effect."

Review your lease each year, and after each change in state or local law. If you manage many doors, a [property management company](/property-management) usually keeps a state-specific lease form. Read more owner guides in [the guides library](/guides).

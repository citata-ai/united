You live 800 miles from your rental. At 9 p.m. the tenant texts a photo of water on the kitchen floor. Your next 30 minutes decide whether this is a $200 fix or a $6,000 claim. A long-distance landlord wins or loses in those 30 minutes.

This guide shows how to set up the team, the inspections and the online tools before that text comes.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Who Do I Need on the Ground?

You need people who can reach the home in hours, not days. Build the team before you need it.

| Role | What they do | Have it before move-in |
|---|---|---|
| Handyman | Small repairs, lockouts, smoke alarm checks | ☐ |
| Licensed plumber | Leaks, water heaters, drains | ☐ |
| HVAC company | No-heat and no-cooling calls, yearly service | ☐ |
| Electrician | Outages, panel issues | ☐ |
| Cleaner | Turnovers between tenants | ☐ |
| Key holder | Opens the door for vendors and inspections | ☐ |
| Insurance agent | Claims help after a loss | ☐ |
| Local attorney | Lease review and evictions | ☐ |

Watch the license line. A friend can help you for free. But if you pay someone to lease the home or collect rent, many states treat that as broker work. Florida Statutes 475.01 defines a broker to include a person who rents property for another for pay. Texas Occupations Code 1101.002 lists controlling the acceptance or deposit of rent from a single-family tenant as a broker act.

Tell the tenant who to contact. In Florida, Statutes 83.50 says the landlord must disclose in writing the name and address of the landlord or the person authorized to receive notices.

## How Do I Do Inspections From Far Away?

Use a mix of local visits and scheduled video walks. Give notice under the law of the state where the rental is.

| State | Entry notice rule | Law |
|---|---|---|
| Arizona | At least 2 days' notice, entry at reasonable times, except emergency or where impractical | ARS 33-1343(D) |
| Washington | At least 2 days' written notice with the date and time window, and a phone number for objections | RCW 59.18.150(6) |
| Florida | At least 24 hours' notice for repairs, between 7:30 a.m. and 8:00 p.m. | Statutes 83.53(2) |

Here is a remote inspection checklist:

- **Every visit:** water heater and under-sink leaks, smoke and CO alarms, HVAC filter, signs of pests.
- **Twice a year:** roof and gutters (from the ground), exterior paint, caulk at tubs and windows.
- **Each turnover:** full photo and video set, appliance check, meter readings.
- **After storms:** a quick outside check by your key holder.

For video walks, send the tenant a short list of what to show. Ask for the same camera path each time so you can compare.

Vacancy needs care too. The Texas Department of Insurance says most companies stop coverage when a home is vacant for 60 days or more. If the home will sit empty, call your insurer.

## Which Tasks Can I Run Online?

Most office work can run from anywhere. Put these online before the first tenant:

| Task | Online tool or method |
|---|---|
| Rent collection | Bank transfer or a rent payment app, with automatic reminders |
| Repair requests | A form or app with photos, and a 24-hour response rule |
| Listing and showings | Rental sites plus a local agent or self-guided tours |
| Screening | A tenant screening service with written criteria |
| Lease signing | E-signature, if your state allows |
| Bookkeeping | A rental ledger tied to a separate bank account |
| Documents | One shared folder: lease, photos, invoices, warranties |

Screening has federal rules. The FTC says that if you deny an applicant based partly on a consumer report, you must give an adverse action notice. Use the same criteria for every applicant, under fair housing law.

Trips can be a tax item. IRS Publication 527 says you can deduct ordinary and necessary travel when the main purpose is to collect rent or manage, conserve or maintain the rental. Trips to improve the property are not deductible as travel; that cost is recovered through depreciation. Keep records of each trip.

## When Do I Need a Local Property Manager?

You need a local manager when the work needs a person on site more often than your team can cover. Use this test:

- **Emergencies.** Can someone you trust reach the home within 2 hours, at night?
- **Showings.** Will you have frequent turnovers or many showings?
- **Rent problems.** Could a late payer lead to court in a city you can't easily reach?
- **Rules.** Does the city have rental registration, inspections or special notices you do not know well?
- **Time.** Do you spend more than a few hours a month on calls and scheduling?

If you answer yes to two or more, price a manager. Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to compare the cost with your own hours. Our guide on [how owners choose a property manager with AI](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai) lists what to ask.

## What Is the 30-Day Setup Plan?

1. **Week 1:** Hire the handyman, plumber and HVAC company. Save their numbers in one list.
2. **Week 2:** Set up rent collection, repair requests and a separate bank account.
3. **Week 3:** Do a full photo and video record of the home. Name a key holder.
4. **Week 4:** Send the tenant a welcome letter with contacts, the emergency number and the repair process.

Then test it. Ask your key holder to visit once, on short notice, and report back. A team that works once when nothing is wrong will work again when water is on the floor.

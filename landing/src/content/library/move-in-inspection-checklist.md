## What should a move-in inspection checklist cover?

A move-in inspection checklist covers every room, every fixture and every system the tenant will use. Rate each item with one word and add a short note for any flaw. "Good" with no note means no visible damage.

Use one rating scale for the whole report:

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| New | Installed or replaced in the last 30 days |
| Good | Works, no visible damage |
| Fair | Works, with wear you describe in the note |
| Poor | Damaged or does not work; repair or note it |

## How do you document condition with photos?

Take photos in a fixed order, with dates, before the tenant moves in any belongings. A fixed order makes it simple to match each photo with the same view at move-out.

Photo routine:

1. **Start at the front door** and move clockwise through each room.
2. **Take one wide shot per wall**, then close-ups of every flaw you note.
3. **Put a scale in close-ups.** A coin or a tape measure shows the size of a scratch.
4. **Open appliances** and photograph the inside of the fridge, oven and dishwasher.
5. **Run water** and photograph under every sink.
6. **Name files by room and number**, for example "kitchen-03", and write that number on the checklist line.
7. **Store the set with the lease** and send the tenant a link.

California makes move-in photos a legal duty. Civil Code 1950.5(g) covers tenancies that begin on or after July 1, 2025. For those, the landlord must photograph the unit immediately before, or at the start of, the tenancy.

## Should the tenant sign the move-in checklist?

Yes. Ask the tenant to sign, and give them a copy the same day. A signature shows the tenant saw the report and had a chance to add notes.

Some states set a default if the tenant stays silent. Virginia's Code 55.1-1214 says the landlord must give the tenant a written report of existing damage within 5 days after occupancy. The report is deemed correct unless the tenant objects in writing within 5 days of receipt. Virginia also lets a landlord adopt a written policy under which the tenant writes the report. Then the landlord has 5 days to object.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How long does a tenant have to return it?

Set a clear return window in the lease and on the checklist itself, and follow your state's rule if it has one. Virginia's window is 5 days after the tenant receives the report. Where the law is silent, choose a short window, write it in the lease, and use it for every tenant.

Write the deadline in the first line of the form: "Please return this report with your notes by [date]." Then log the date you got it back.

## How does the move-in checklist protect the deposit?

The move-in checklist is the baseline for every deposit decision. At move-out, you compare each line. Damage that was on the move-in report is not the tenant's to pay for. New damage beyond normal wear may be.

California Civil Code 1950.5 shows why the baseline matters. It says a landlord may use the deposit to repair damage caused by the tenant, but not for ordinary wear and tear. It also requires move-out photos for deductions, starting April 1, 2025. Without move-in photos, you cannot prove the damage is new.

Virginia's Code 55.1-1226 adds a move-out step. If the tenant asks in writing, the tenant may attend the move-out inspection, which must happen within 72 hours of delivery of possession.

Store the signed move-in report with the lease until the deposit is settled. For other owner and manager guides, see [/guides](/guides).

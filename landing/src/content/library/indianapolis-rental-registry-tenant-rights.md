## Who must register in the Indianapolis rental registry?

All owners or landlords of residential rental units in the City of Indianapolis must register with the Department of Business and Neighborhood Services (BNS). The program started on 2015-01-01. BNS runs it under Chapter 851 of the revised code of the Consolidated City of Indianapolis-Marion County.

Rentals in 4 excluded cities are exempt: Beech Grove, Lawrence, Southport and Speedway. So first check where your parcel is.

Who files:

- **The owner,** or
- **A property management company** for the owner. A manager that represents several owners must file a separate form for each owner.

What you need to register:

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Owner contact | Name, address, phone, email |
| Landlord contact | The property manager's contact data. This person must live in Indiana. |
| Parcel number | The 7-digit parcel number for each property. The portal fills in the address. |
| Unit count | Number of rental units on each parcel |
| Fee | $5, by eCheck or card |

You file online in the BNS Citizen Access Portal, after you create a user account. The public can see the registration list in the same portal.

## How much does the Indianapolis registry cost and how do you renew?

The Indianapolis registry costs $5. The BNS fact sheet says an owner with several rental properties can register all of them together and pay one $5 fee. Registration renews each year, and BNS says the renewal needs "minimal action" from registered landlords.

Worked example:

| Portfolio | Registrations | Yearly cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1 house, owner files | 1 | $5 |
| 12 houses, 1 owner | 1 | $5 |
| 3 owners, 1 manager files for all | 3 | $15 |

The registry is small next to the rent. HUD's FY2027 fair market rent for a 2-bedroom in the Indianapolis-Carmel metro is $1,536 a month, up 4.3% from $1,473. See all sizes on the [Indianapolis fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/indianapolis-fair-market-rent-2027).

## How big is the Indianapolis registry, and is it enforced?

The Indianapolis registry lists about 17,500 parcels. Chapter 851 (Sec. 851-108) makes BNS report performance measures each year. Here is the BNS annual report.

| Measure | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 (to January) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Parcels listed | 13,148 | 13,427 | 17,519 | 17,587 |
| Registration cases | 4,087 | 3,648 | 4,387 | 4,426 |
| Complaints received | 30 | 18 | 15 | 0 |
| Notices of violation | 30 | 18 | 15 | 0 |
| Citations issued | 4 | 4 | 1 | 0 |
| Citation fines collected | $0 | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Landlord Registration Fund | $195,638.16 | $204,367.16 | $219,956.16 | $219,956.16 |

The numbers show light enforcement. BNS issued 1 citation in 2025 and collected $0 in fines. But the city counted 622 local code violations against registered properties in 2025. Register anyway: the list is public, and a missing registration is an easy citation.

## What fines apply in Indianapolis?

Indianapolis registry fines are small in practice, and the city pages we read do not list the dollar amounts. The BNS report counts citation fines: $0 collected in each year from 2023 to early 2026. The $500 fine for a missing rights notice belonged to the 2020 Tenants Bill of Rights, which state law later targeted. Ask BNS at licensing@indy.gov or (317) 327-4316 for the current fine schedule before you assume a cost.

## What are the Indianapolis short-term rental rules?

Indianapolis short-term rentals need a separate annual permit. City-County Council passed the Short-Term Rental Permit Program in August 2024, and it took effect on 2025-01-01. The rules are in Chapter 852 of the municipal code.

- **Permit per unit.** Apply each year for each short-term rental unit.
- **Fee.** A one-time $150 fee with the first permit. Renewals have no fee.
- **Building code.** The unit must be in a legally built dwelling that meets all building code rules.

## What happened to the Indianapolis tenant rights ordinance?

The Indianapolis tenant rights ordinance was passed in February 2020, not 2022. City-County Council approved a Tenants Bill of Rights that required landlords to notify tenants of their rights, connected tenants to legal aid, barred retaliation, and barred discrimination based on expunged or sealed convictions (WISH-TV, 2021-02-08). A second ordinance set aside $250,000 for tenant legal aid. A landlord who did not give the rights notice could face a $500 fine (WRTV, 2020-03-11).

The state moved against it:

1. **March 2020.** The legislature passed Senate Bill 148, which bars local rules on tenant screening, security deposits, lease applications, lease terms, required disclosures and landlord fees. The Senate vote was 29-19 and the House vote 64-32.
2. **2020-03-25.** Governor Eric Holcomb vetoed it. He called it "overly broad."
3. **February 2021.** The Senate and then the House overrode the veto, and SEA 148 became law.

Local officials said the law voids city rules on landlord-tenant relations. Reports did not say exactly which provisions of the 2020 ordinance it voids. The registry itself still runs and reported data into 2026.

What this means for you:

- [ ] Follow Indiana state landlord-tenant law for deposits, leases and screening. The city cannot add its own terms there.
- [ ] Keep your registry entry current.
- [ ] Ask an attorney before you act against a tenant who just reported a code problem.
- [ ] Use the same written screening criteria for every applicant, under the federal Fair Housing Act.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

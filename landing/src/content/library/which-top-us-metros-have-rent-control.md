## Which states ban local rent control?

Of the 50 largest HUD metro areas, 21 sit in states where we read a statute that bans or blocks local rent control. We took the metro list from HUD's FY2027 fair market rent file, ranked by 2024 population. Then we read each state's statute at its legislature's site, or at a public.law copy where the official site blocked us. These are the bans we verified:

| State | Statute | What it says, in short | Metros in our top 50 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Texas | Local Gov't Code 214.902 | Only in a disaster, with the governor's approval | Houston, Dallas, Fort Worth, San Antonio, Austin |
| Arizona | ARS 33-1329 | Rent control is preempted by the state | Phoenix |
| Florida | Fla. Stat. 125.0103(2) | No local measure that controls rents | Tampa, Orlando, Miami, Fort Lauderdale, Jacksonville, West Palm Beach |
| Colorado | C.R.S. 38-12-301 | No county or city may control rent | Denver |
| Illinois | 50 ILCS 825/10 | Home rule units may not control rent | Chicago |
| Michigan | MCL 123.411 | No local ordinance that controls rent | Detroit |
| North Carolina | G.S. 42-14.1 | No county or city may regulate rent | Charlotte, Raleigh |
| Missouri | RSMo 441.043 | No county or city may regulate rent | St. Louis, Kansas City |
| Kansas | K.S.A. 12-16,120 | No political subdivision may control rent | Kansas City (Kansas side) |
| Wisconsin | Wis. Stat. 66.1015 | No city, village, town or county may regulate rent | Milwaukee |
| Massachusetts | M.G.L. ch. 40P, s. 4 | No rent control, except a voluntary-only form | Boston |

Most bans keep a narrow door open. They let a city limit rent on housing it owns, or through a voluntary deal with a developer (Colorado, Wisconsin, Michigan, Kansas). Arizona exempts housing a state or city agency owns, finances, insures or subsidizes.

## Which states have a statewide cap?

California, Oregon and Washington cap yearly rent increases for most housing across the state. 10 of our 50 metros are in these states.

| State | Statute | Yearly cap | Main exemption | Metros |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| California | Civil Code 1947.12 | 5% plus the change in cost of living, or 10%, whichever is lower | Housing with a certificate of occupancy in the last 15 years; some single-family homes | Los Angeles, Riverside-San Bernardino, San Diego, Orange County, Oakland, Sacramento, San Jose, San Francisco |
| Oregon | ORS 90.323, 90.324 | 7% plus CPI, or 10%, whichever is lower | First certificate of occupancy less than 15 years ago | Portland |
| Washington | RCW 59.18.700 | 7% plus CPI, or 10%, whichever is less | Listed in RCW 59.18.710 | Seattle (and the Washington side of Portland) |

Three rules in these laws matter most for an owner:

1. **One increase in 12 months.** California counts from the lowest rent in the prior 12 months. Oregon allows one increase per 12 months, with none in the first year.
2. **Long notice in Oregon.** ORS 90.323 requires 90 days' written notice. An increase above the cap costs 3 months' rent plus damages.
3. **No increase in year one in Washington.** RCW 59.18.700 bars an increase in the first 12 months of any tenancy. The Department of Commerce publishes the cap each year from June 1, 2025, using Seattle-area CPI.

Worked example on a $2,000 rent, with a CPI of 3% (an example value, not the official figure):

| State | Formula | Cap | Most you can add |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | 5% + 3% = 8%, under 10% | 8% | $160 |
| Oregon | 7% + 3% = 10%, at the 10% limit | 10% | $200 |
| Washington | 7% + 3% = 10%, at the 10% limit | 10% | $200 |

Each state publishes the real figure. Use that number, not this example.

## Which metros have city rent control today?

City or district rent regulation is in force in at least 6 of our 50 metros, based on the official pages we read:

- **New York City.** Rent stabilization generally covers buildings built after 1947 and before 1974, plus some units with J-51, 421-a or 421-g tax benefits (NYS HCR, Fact Sheet 1).
- **Nassau-Suffolk.** Outside New York City, the ETPA covers buildings of 6 or more units built before 1974 in places that declare an emergency, such as Nassau County (NYS HCR, Fact Sheet 8).
- **Washington, DC.** DC's rent control caps the 2026 increase at 4.1%, or 2.1% for a registered elderly tenant or tenant with a disability (DC Office of the Tenant Advocate).
- **Los Angeles.** The Rent Stabilization Ordinance covers most units built on or before October 1, 1978 (LAHD).
- **San Francisco.** Many units built on or before June 13, 1979 have rent control and eviction protection (SF.gov).
- **Oakland.** The city runs a Rent Adjustment Program with rent registration and allowable increases (City of Oakland).

In the Minneapolis-St. Paul metro, Minnesota Statutes 471.9996 bans local rent control unless voters approve it in a general election. Saint Paul's city site lists a Rent Stabilization program. We did not read its terms, so we do not describe them.

## What does a rent cap mean for a single-family rental owner?

A rent cap often does not reach a single-family home, but you must check each rule. Here is how the 3 state caps treat a house:

- **California:** Civil Code 1947.12(d)(5) exempts a home that can be sold apart from any other unit. The owner must not be a REIT, a corporation, or an LLC with a corporate member. And the tenant must get the exact exemption notice that the law spells out. Without that notice, the cap applies.
- **Oregon:** ORS 90.323 has no general single-family exemption. The cap applies unless the home got its first certificate of occupancy less than 15 years before the notice, or it is regulated affordable housing.
- **Washington:** RCW 59.18.700 points to the exemptions in RCW 59.18.710. We did not read that section, so check it before you rely on an exemption.

City rules can reach further than state rules. In Los Angeles, the Just Cause Ordinance can apply to a property with one single-family home, even though it does not cap rent (LAHD).

Checklist for a single-family owner in a capped state:

- [ ] Find the year of the first certificate of occupancy.
- [ ] Check who holds title: a person, a trust, an LLC or a corporation.
- [ ] In California, put the exact 1947.12(d)(5) notice in the lease.
- [ ] Calendar the notice period: 90 days in Oregon for most increases.
- [ ] Check your city's own ordinance.

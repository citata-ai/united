## What is a pre-move-out inspection?

A pre-move-out inspection is a walk-through of the unit before the tenant leaves. You and the tenant look at each room. You list what you would charge to the deposit today. The tenant then has time to clean or fix those items.

The pre-move-out inspection is different from the final move-out inspection:

| | Pre-move-out inspection | Final move-out inspection |
|---|---|---|
| When | 1 to 2 weeks before the tenant leaves | After the tenant returns possession |
| Unit | Often still furnished | Empty |
| Goal | Give the tenant a fix-it list | Record the final condition |
| Result | Itemized list of likely deductions | Deposit refund or itemized deductions |
| Tenant can still act | Yes | No |

California writes the pre-move-out inspection into law. Civil Code 1950.5(f) calls it an "initial inspection." The landlord must offer it, and the tenant may say yes or no.

## Is a pre-move-out inspection required?

A pre-move-out inspection is required in some states and optional in others. Here is how 4 states treat it:

| State | Pre-move-out inspection | Related rule | Statute |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | Landlord must offer one after notice to end the tenancy | 48 hours' written notice; itemized list; tenant may fix items | Civil Code 1950.5(f) |
| Arizona | Not described in 33-1321 | Tenant may be present at the move-out inspection, and must be told so at move-in | A.R.S. 33-1321 |
| Florida | Not required by 83.49 | Landlord must send notice of a claim within 30 days, or lose the claim | Fla. Stat. 83.49(3) |
| Texas | Not required by chapter 92 | Refund due by the 30th day after surrender; written forwarding address needed | Property Code 92.103, 92.107 |

Where the law is silent, you can still offer one. It costs you 30 minutes and can save a dispute.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## When should the pre-move-out inspection happen?

The pre-move-out inspection should happen about 1 to 2 weeks before move-out. That gives the tenant enough time to act, and the unit is close to its final state.

California sets the window. Under Civil Code 1950.5(f), the inspection can occur "no earlier than two weeks before the termination or the end of lease date." The landlord must give at least 48 hours' written notice of the date and time. Both sides can waive the 48 hours in a signed writing.

A sample timeline for a lease that ends on November 30:

1. **October 31.** Tenant gives 30 days' notice. You send move-out instructions and offer the pre-inspection.
2. **November 14.** Earliest date for the pre-inspection under the California rule.
3. **November 15.** You give 48 hours' written notice for November 17.
4. **November 17.** You walk the unit and hand over the itemized list.
5. **November 17 to 30.** Tenant cleans and fixes listed items.
6. **November 30.** Tenant returns keys. You do the final inspection.
7. **December 21.** California deadline: 21 calendar days after the tenant vacates.

Shift the dates to match your lease and your state.

## What do I tell the tenant to fix?

Tell the tenant to fix what you would otherwise deduct: damage beyond normal wear and the cleaning needed to reach the move-in level. California limits deposit cleaning charges to what returns the unit to "the same level of cleanliness it was in at the inception of the tenancy." Bring the move-in form and photos so you compare against day 1, not against a new unit.

Use this checklist during the walk-through:

- [ ] **Walls.** Holes from nails or anchors, marks, unapproved paint colors.
- [ ] **Floors.** Stains, burns, pet damage, deep scratches.
- [ ] **Kitchen.** Oven and stovetop grease, fridge condition, broken shelves.
- [ ] **Bathrooms.** Soap scum, mold on caulk, slow drains, cracked fixtures.
- [ ] **Doors and windows.** Broken blinds, torn screens, damaged locks.
- [ ] **Fixtures.** Missing bulbs, missing smoke alarm batteries, broken outlet covers.
- [ ] **Outside.** Yard waste, oil stains, trash, items in storage.
- [ ] **Keys.** Confirm the count against the move-in list.

Write each item as a task, not a judgment. "Patch 4 nail holes in the living room wall" is clear. "Walls in poor shape" starts an argument.

In California, the itemized statement must include the text of Civil Code 1950.5(b)(1) to (b)(4). Those paragraphs list what a deposit may cover.

## Does a pre-move-out inspection reduce deposit disputes?

A pre-move-out inspection reduces deposit disputes because it removes the surprise. Most disputes start when a tenant first learns about a charge after the money is gone. A pre-inspection moves that moment forward, while the tenant can still fix the item.

The inspection does not lock you in. California Civil Code 1950.5(f) still lets the landlord deduct for damage the tenant's things hid at the pre-inspection, or damage that happened after it.

To make the result hold up, keep a clean record:

1. **Photos.** Take dated photos at the pre-inspection and again at the final inspection. California now requires photos after move-out and after repairs, from April 1, 2025.
2. **The signed list.** Give the tenant a copy and keep one.
3. **Receipts.** California requires copies of bills, invoices or receipts with the itemized statement.
4. **Proof of mailing.** Send the result to the forwarding address and keep proof.

## Washington rent increase rules at a glance

Washington changed its rent rules in 2025. House Bill 1217 (2025 c 209) added a statewide cap in RCW 59.18.700 to 59.18.720 and amended the notice rule in RCW 59.18.140. We read each section on the Washington State Legislature site, and the cap figures on the Department of Commerce site, on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute or source |
|---|---|---|
| Rent increase notice | At least 90 days, written | RCW 59.18.140(3)(a) |
| Subsidized, income-based rent | At least 30 days | RCW 59.18.140(3)(b) |
| Increase during a fixed term | Not before the term ends | RCW 59.18.140(3)(a) |
| First 12 months of tenancy | No increase | RCW 59.18.700(1)(a)(i) |
| Annual cap | 7% plus CPI or 10%, whichever is less | RCW 59.18.700(1)(a)(ii) |
| 2026 cap | 9.683% | Department of Commerce |
| 2027 cap | 10% | Department of Commerce |
| Notice form | Set text in the statute | RCW 59.18.720 |
| Rent gap between lease types | No more than 5% | RCW 59.18.700(4)(b) |
| Landlord ends month-to-month | Only for a listed cause | RCW 59.18.650 |
| Tenant ends month-to-month | 20 days before the period ends | RCW 59.18.200(1)(a) |
| Seattle notice | 180 days | City of Seattle |

## Does Washington have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

Yes. Since 2025, Washington has a statewide cap on rent increases. RCW 59.18.700 says a landlord may not increase rent in any 12-month period by more than 7% plus the consumer price index, or 10%, whichever is less. The cap applies to month-to-month tenancies and to fixed terms of any length.

The Department of Commerce publishes the number each July. It uses the June 12-month change in the CPI for all urban consumers in the Seattle area. Commerce's Landlord Resource Center shows the 2026 math:

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| June 2024 Seattle CPI-U | 354.824 |
| June 2025 Seattle CPI-U | 364.344 |
| CPI change | 2.683% |
| Plus 7% | 9.683% |
| Cap for 1/1/2026 to 12/31/2026 | 9.683% (below the 10% ceiling) |

For 1/1/2027 to 12/31/2027, Commerce lists the cap as 10%.

**Worked example.** Rent is $1,800. A 2026 increase may add at most $174.29 a month (9.683% of $1,800), for a new rent of $1,974.29.

The cap has exemptions under RCW 59.18.710. They include units whose first certificate of occupancy came 12 or fewer years before the notice. Public housing authority, nonprofit and tax-credit units are also exempt. So are some owner-occupied homes and 2-to-4 unit buildings, unless the owner is a REIT or a corporation. If you claim an exemption, the notice must state the facts.

The cap law expires on July 1, 2040 (RCW 59.18.700(8)).

## How many days notice does a Washington landlord need to raise the rent?

A Washington landlord needs at least 90 days' prior written notice (RCW 59.18.140(3)(a)). Give it to each affected tenant. For a subsidized tenancy where rent depends on the tenant's income, the minimum is 30 days (RCW 59.18.140(3)(b)).

The notice must follow the form in RCW 59.18.720. The form states:

- The new total of rent and recurring charges, in percent and dollars.
- Whether the increase is lower than the cap, at the cap, or exempt.
- The exemption claimed, with supporting facts, if any.

Serve the notice as RCW 59.12.040 requires. The form covers rent and all recurring or periodic charges in the lease, not only base rent. Voucher tenancies use the form too (RCW 59.18.720(2)).

## Can a Washington landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

No. A Washington rent increase may not take effect before the lease term ends (RCW 59.18.140(3)(a)). The one exception is a subsidized, income-based tenancy, which may change sooner by mutual consent (RCW 59.18.140(3)(b)).

Plan the timeline backward from the lease end:

1. **Day minus 120.** Check the cap for the year the increase starts.
2. **Day minus 100.** Fill in the RCW 59.18.720 form.
3. **Day minus 90 at the latest.** Serve the notice.
4. **Lease end.** The new rent starts.

A tenant who gets an increase above the cap, without a valid exemption, may send a written demand to fix it. The tenant may also end the lease with 20 days' notice before the increase starts (RCW 59.18.700(3)). The court may award the excess rent, up to 3 months of unlawful rent, and attorneys' fees. The Attorney General may seek up to $7,500 per violation (RCW 59.18.700(5)).

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Washington?

A Washington landlord cannot end a month-to-month tenancy with a simple notice. RCW 59.18.650(1)(d) says a landlord may end a periodic tenancy only for a cause in subsection (2). Examples: unpaid rent, a lease breach not fixed, or an owner move-in with 90 days' notice.

The tenant has a simpler rule. Under RCW 59.18.200(1)(a), a tenant ends a month-to-month tenancy with written notice of 20 days or more before the end of a rental period. A tenant in the armed forces with change of station or deployment orders may give less.

Some landlord notices are longer. A change to a policy of excluding children needs 90 days. A condominium conversion needs 120 days. Demolition, substantial rehabilitation or a change of use needs 120 days (RCW 59.18.200(2)).

## Do cities in Washington add their own rent or notice rules?

Yes. Seattle adds stricter rules. The City of Seattle says all rental housing cost increases in Seattle require 180 days' advance written notice. That is twice the state's 90 days.

Seattle also runs the Economic Displacement Relocation Assistance program (EDRA). It applies when increases total 10% or more within 12 months. You must attach the city's EDRA notice to the increase. Eligible tenant households at or below 80% of area median income who move out can get 3 times the monthly housing cost from the city. The city then bills the landlord for that amount.

**Worked example.** A Seattle tenant pays $2,000. You raise rent 9.683%, the 2026 state cap. That is below Seattle's 10% EDRA trigger, but you still need 180 days' notice.

Other cities may have their own rules. Check the city code before you send any notice.

## Washington rent increase checklist

- [ ] Tenant has lived in the unit for more than 12 months.
- [ ] Increase is within the cap for the start year, or an exemption applies.
- [ ] Notice uses the RCW 59.18.720 form, with exemption facts if claimed.
- [ ] Served at least 90 days before the effective date (180 in Seattle).
- [ ] Effective date is on or after the lease end.
- [ ] Seattle increases of 10% or more include the EDRA notice.

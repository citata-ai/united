## Which metros had the biggest FMR rise for FY2027?

Kansas City, Chicago and Columbus, Ohio had the biggest FY2027 fair market rent rises among the 50 largest HUD metro areas. We ranked the areas by 2024 population in HUD's county-level FMR file. Then we compared the FY2027 2-bedroom FMR with the revised FY2026 one. The 2-bedroom unit is HUD's base unit: HUD sets it first and derives the other sizes from it (Federal Register, 91 FR 56156).

The FMR (fair market rent) is HUD's estimate of the 40th percentile gross rent paid by recent movers. Gross rent means shelter rent plus utilities. Housing authorities use it to set voucher payment standards (24 CFR 982.503).

The 10 biggest rises, 2-bedroom:

| Rank | Metro area | FY2026 | FY2027 | Change | % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kansas City | $1,358 | $1,546 | +$188 | +13.8% |
| 2 | Chicago | $1,781 | $2,011 | +$230 | +12.9% |
| 3 | Columbus, OH | $1,430 | $1,602 | +$172 | +12.0% |
| 4 | St. Louis | $1,218 | $1,349 | +$131 | +10.8% |
| 5 | Washington, DC | $2,246 | $2,438 | +$192 | +8.5% |
| 6 | Baltimore | $1,857 | $2,006 | +$149 | +8.0% |
| 7 | Pittsburgh | $1,299 | $1,389 | +$90 | +6.9% |
| 8 | Cincinnati | $1,353 | $1,442 | +$89 | +6.6% |
| 9 | San Juan, PR | $663 | $706 | +$43 | +6.5% |
| 10 | Charlotte | $1,686 | $1,785 | +$99 | +5.9% |

Source: HUD FY2027 and revised FY2026 FMR files, our ranking (2026-10-19).

The pattern is clear: 5 of the top 8 are in the Midwest. Chicago gained the most dollars, $230 a month for a 2-bedroom. The median change across all 50 areas was +1.4%.

## Which metros had FMR drops?

22 of the 50 largest metro areas had a lower FY2027 2-bedroom FMR. Most of them are in the Sun Belt and in California. Here are the 10 biggest drops:

| Rank | Metro area | FY2026 | FY2027 | Change | % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dallas | $1,931 | $1,807 | -$124 | -6.4% |
| 2 | San Jose | $3,483 | $3,272 | -$211 | -6.1% |
| 3 | San Diego | $3,001 | $2,823 | -$178 | -5.9% |
| 4 | Phoenix | $1,839 | $1,734 | -$105 | -5.7% |
| 5 | Milwaukee | $1,338 | $1,263 | -$75 | -5.6% |
| 6 | Orange County, CA | $3,236 | $3,057 | -$179 | -5.5% |
| 7 | Houston | $1,573 | $1,497 | -$76 | -4.8% |
| 8 | Riverside-San Bernardino | $2,201 | $2,103 | -$98 | -4.5% |
| 9 | Oakland | $2,912 | $2,805 | -$107 | -3.7% |
| 10 | Raleigh | $1,750 | $1,689 | -$61 | -3.5% |

Source: HUD FY2027 and revised FY2026 FMR files, our ranking (2026-10-19).

San Jose lost the most dollars: $211 a month. Milwaukee is the one Midwest metro in the bottom 10. Other drops include Atlanta (-3.2%), Tampa (-3.0%), Las Vegas (-2.8%), Portland, Oregon (-2.3%) and Austin (-1.9%).

None of these hit HUD's floor. Under 24 CFR 888.113, an FMR cannot fall below 90% of the prior year's FMR. The biggest drop here, Dallas at -6.4%, is well above that -10% limit.

## Why did FMR fall in Dallas, Phoenix and San Jose?

The FY2027 notice does not give a reason for each metro. It does describe the method, so you can see where a drop can come from. HUD builds each FMR in 4 steps (Federal Register, 91 FR 56156):

1. **Base rent.** HUD starts with 5-year American Community Survey (ACS) data for 2020 to 2024.
2. **Recent-mover factor.** HUD compares that base with the 1-year 2024 ACS rent of recent movers. This factor can raise the base, but HUD does not let it lower the base.
3. **Inflation to 2025.** HUD applies a gross rent inflation factor. About 55% of it comes from private rent data (Apartment List, CoStar, Cotality, Moody's, RealPage and Zillow). About 45% comes from the CPI rent index. A utility factor is added.
4. **Trend to FY2027.** HUD applies a forecast based on CPI gross rent data.

So a metro can fall when its recent-mover rents and its private rent data show weaker growth than last year's inputs. That is what an owner sees on the ground in a market where many new apartments compete for tenants. But that is our reading of the method, not a HUD finding for Dallas, Phoenix or San Jose.

One method change also matters for FY2027. HUD now builds the utility part of the inflation factor from Energy Information Administration price data. HUD did this because the Bureau of Labor Statistics stopped publishing the local fuels and utilities CPI. The effect of this change on each metro is not in the notice.

## What does a falling FMR mean for a Section 8 owner?

A falling FMR means the payment standard in your area may go down, but not at once for a family already in your unit. The payment standard is the most a housing authority (PHA) uses to figure the voucher subsidy.

Here is the basic range for the 2-bedroom payment standard, 90% to 110% of the FMR (24 CFR 982.503):

| Metro | FY2026 basic range | FY2027 basic range |
|---|---|---|
| Dallas | $1,738 – $2,124 | $1,626 – $1,988 |
| Phoenix | $1,655 – $2,023 | $1,561 – $1,907 |
| San Jose | $3,135 – $3,831 | $2,945 – $3,599 |
| Chicago | $1,603 – $1,959 | $1,810 – $2,212 |
| Kansas City | $1,222 – $1,494 | $1,391 – $1,701 |

Our math from HUD's FMRs, rounded to whole dollars.

What the rules say for a current tenant (24 CFR 982.505(c)(3)):

- **The PHA may hold the old standard.** It can keep the higher payment standard for as long as the family stays in your unit.
- **A cut waits at least 2 years.** If the PHA reduces it, the first reduction cannot apply earlier than 2 years after the decrease takes effect.
- **The family gets 12 months' notice.** The PHA must send written notice at least 12 months ahead.

What to do as an owner:

- [ ] Read your PHA's new payment standard schedule. Each PHA sets its own within the range.
- [ ] Check your PHA's Administrative Plan for its policy on decreases.
- [ ] For a new lease in a falling market, price to the new standard and to rent reasonableness, not to last year's number.
- [ ] In a rising market, ask for a rent increase at renewal. The PHA still checks that the rent is reasonable.

Note that some large metros must use Small Area FMRs (rents by ZIP code) instead of one metro FMR. HUD added 41 metro areas to that list in 2023 (88 FR 73352). In those areas, your ZIP code's number matters more than the metro number above.

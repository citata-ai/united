## Ohio security deposit rules at a glance

Ohio puts almost all of its deposit rules in one short statute: Ohio Rev. Code 5321.16. The law has not changed since it took effect on November 4, 1974. Here is the full rule set in one table.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | No cap | 5321.16 (none set) |
| Return deadline | 30 days after the lease ends and the tenant gives back possession | 5321.16(B) |
| Itemized list | Required for every deduction, in writing, with the amount due | 5321.16(B) |
| Allowed deductions | Past-due rent, and damages from the tenant's breach of 5321.05 or the lease | 5321.16(B) |
| Interest | 5% a year on the part above $50 or 1 month's rent (the greater), if the tenant stays 6+ months | 5321.16(A) |
| Interest payment | Computed and paid each year | 5321.16(A) |
| Forwarding address | Tenant must give it in writing | 5321.16(B) |
| Penalty | Money due, plus damages equal to the amount wrongfully withheld, plus reasonable attorney fees | 5321.16(C) |
| Waiver in the lease | Not allowed | 5321.13(A) |

Ohio Rev. Code 5321.01(E) defines a security deposit as "any deposit of money or property to secure performance by the tenant." So a deposit of property, not only cash, falls under the same rules.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What is the maximum security deposit in Ohio?

Ohio sets no maximum security deposit. Section 5321.16 controls deposits, and it names no cap. You can ask for any amount the market and your lease allow.

The amount still matters, because the interest rule starts above a set line. A deposit of more than one month's rent, or more than $50 when rent is very low, can earn interest for the tenant. Many Ohio managers keep the deposit at one month's rent or less for this reason. That is a business choice, not a legal limit.

Other laws can still apply. Fair housing rules apply to how you set deposits. Set one deposit policy and apply it the same way to each applicant.

## How many days does an Ohio landlord have to return a security deposit?

An Ohio landlord has 30 days to return a security deposit. The clock starts when two things are both true: the rental agreement has ended, and the tenant has delivered possession (Ohio Rev. Code 5321.16(B)).

Delivery of possession usually means the tenant has moved out and returned the keys. If the lease ends on June 30 but the tenant leaves on July 5, the 30 days start July 5.

The tenant has a duty too. The tenant must give you a forwarding or new address in writing. If the tenant does not, the tenant cannot collect the extra damages or attorney fees in 5321.16(C). You still owe the deposit itself.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Ohio?

An Ohio landlord can deduct two kinds of costs from a security deposit (Ohio Rev. Code 5321.16(B)):

1. **Past-due rent.** Rent the tenant owes and did not pay.
2. **Damages from the tenant's breach.** Costs the landlord suffered because the tenant did not follow Ohio Rev. Code 5321.05 or the rental agreement.

Section 5321.05 lists the tenant's duties. They include keeping the unit safe and sanitary, and not damaging the premises on purpose or by neglect. So a deduction needs a link to a broken duty or a lease term. Keep move-in and move-out photos, dated, to show that link.

## Does an Ohio landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. An Ohio landlord must itemize and identify every deduction in a written notice (Ohio Rev. Code 5321.16(B)). The landlord delivers that notice to the tenant together with the amount due, within the same 30 days.

Use this checklist for each move-out:

- [ ] Note the date the lease ended and the date the tenant gave back possession.
- [ ] Count 30 days from the later of the two dates.
- [ ] Confirm the tenant's forwarding address in writing.
- [ ] List each deduction with the item, the cost, and the reason.
- [ ] Attach receipts or quotes where you have them.
- [ ] Add any interest owed under 5321.16(A).
- [ ] Send the list and the balance together, before day 30.
- [ ] Keep proof of the date you sent it.

## Must an Ohio landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

An Ohio landlord must pay interest on part of some deposits. Ohio Rev. Code 5321.16(A) has three conditions:

- The deposit is more than $50 or one month's periodic rent, whichever is greater.
- The tenant stays in the unit 6 months or more.
- Interest runs only on the excess, at 5% a year, and the landlord computes and pays it each year.

Here is a worked calculation for one unit.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Monthly rent | $1,200 |
| Deposit held | $2,000 |
| Line for interest: greater of $50 or 1 month's rent | $1,200 |
| Excess above the line | $800 |
| Interest at 5% a year | $40 per year |

If the deposit was $1,200 or less on that unit, no interest would be due. If the tenant left after 5 months, no interest would be due either.

Section 5321.16 does not require a separate bank account or a named bank. It also does not require a receipt at move-in. A lease or a local rule may add these, so check both.

## What is the penalty for an Ohio landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

The penalty has three parts under Ohio Rev. Code 5321.16(C). If the landlord does not follow division (B), the tenant may recover:

1. The property and money due to the tenant.
2. Damages equal to the amount wrongfully withheld.
3. Reasonable attorney fees.

Here is an example. A landlord holds $1,500 and keeps $600 with no itemized list. A court finds the $600 was wrongly withheld. The tenant can recover the $600, plus $600 in damages, plus reasonable attorney fees. The landlord's cost is at least $1,200, before fees.

The forwarding address rule limits this. A tenant who gave no written address cannot get the damages or the fees.

A lease cannot change any of this. Ohio Rev. Code 5321.13(A) bars any oral or written agreement that waives a provision of Chapter 5321. A clause that extends the 30 days or removes interest has no effect.

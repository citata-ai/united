## What can a franchisee change on a shared site?

A franchisee can change only what the franchise agreement and platform access allow. Search advice does not override those terms. Start with a written list of local fields, publishing rights, and approval steps.

Ask for control over facts that change in your office. These may include staff, local contact details, service scope, approved fees, and contact forms. Ask who fixes broken links and who can see lead reports. A local office needs a path to correct errors promptly.

Do not start by requesting a separate site. First inspect the branch page an owner sees. Can the owner tell where your team works, which properties you manage, and how to contact you? Can the form reach the local team without delay?

Use an access table to turn a broad marketing request into specific work:

| Asset | Local task | Agreement to record |
|---|---|---|
| Branch page | Check services and contact route | Who edits and approves |
| Contact form | Test owner lead delivery | Who receives and tracks leads |
| Business profile | Maintain accurate local facts | Who owns and manages access |
| Local article | Add checked local guidance | Who reviews and publishes |
| Search reports | Review queries and page results | What local staff can see |
| Photos and reviews | Supply approved local proof | Consent and brand review process |

This is a suggested responsibility map. Your contract and platform may differ. Give each request a clear owner and an acceptance test. For a form fix, the test is an actual submission received by the intended team.

## How do franchise offices share Business Profile rules?

Franchise offices must present their real local business accurately. Google's business guidelines call for consistent chain names and categories, subject to real-world exceptions. The guidelines also set conditions for location eligibility.

A franchise territory is not, by itself, evidence of an eligible physical office. Do not create profiles at mailbox addresses to cover a wider map. Ask the franchisor to review eligibility before adding a location.

Keep the office page, public phone, profile, and signage aligned. When the brand allows local name variations, keep written proof of the approved real-world form. Avoid a different keyword phrase on each channel.

Agree who keeps primary ownership and recovery access. Use an account process that survives staff changes. Record the profile URL and responsible contact in the office handoff file. Do not leave a former vendor as the only person who can recover the account.

Review the local facts after an office move, staff change, or phone change. Test the website link from the public profile. It should bring an owner to the correct office information and service contact route.

## Can a franchisee run a local blog?

A franchisee can run a local blog when the brand permits it and the team can maintain it. The useful choice is not simply shared blog versus separate blog. The useful choice is which arrangement gives owners current, reviewed local answers.

Ask whether local articles can appear under the office section of the brand site. Request an author name, local reviewer, clear update process, and links from the branch page. Record who handles corrections and removes old guidance.

Choose topics from real questions. Explain your local repair process, an owner report, a service transfer, or how you inspect a property. Use checked sources for legal rules. Avoid publishing legal summaries from memory.

Do not copy a national article and replace the place name. Add actual service details that your team can confirm. Keep private owner and tenant information out of examples. Label sample reports and invented scenarios so readers do not mistake them for client results.

If a separate domain is allowed, decide who owns it, who maintains it, and what happens when the office changes hands. Include analytics access and content rights. A site that no one can update becomes a service problem as well as a marketing problem.

## How should franchise sites handle territory pages?

Territory pages should describe actual service differences and help owners choose the right office. A boundary on an internal franchise map is not enough content for an owner page. Explain who handles the work and what service is available.

Google's spam policies describe doorway abuse, including similar pages made to rank for specific queries and funnel visitors onward. Use that warning when planning location pages. Do not produce a stack of town names with the same text.

A useful territory page can explain the property types served, contact team, service limits, and local process. Include verified local resources where they answer an owner question. Avoid vague claims that the team knows every street.

Make office boundaries clear without competing claims. If neighboring offices overlap, agree a lead routing rule. Test forms near the boundary with sample property locations. Record what the visitor sees when the property falls outside your service area.

Use a branch directory that helps owners reach the right team. Make the local page easy to find from national navigation. Add links between relevant service and office pages when they help the visitor finish a task.

## How should a franchise office measure local progress?

Measure local progress through qualified owner contacts and clear page evidence. Separate brand-wide traffic from the page and market your office serves. Ask for reports that show local landing pages, queries, forms, and sales outcomes.

Review lead routing alongside search reports. A local page can receive visits while sending inquiries to the wrong team. Compare submissions with the actual sales record before concluding that search demand is weak.

Track changes with dates and a short reason. Keep records of approved page edits, profile updates, and contact tests. Change a clear problem before adding more content. A useful local page deserves maintenance before an unused blog needs expansion.

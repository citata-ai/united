Louisiana uses its Civil Code, not a landlord-tenant act, for lease notice rules. I read the articles on the Louisiana State Legislature site on 2026-10-19. The month-to-month notice is short: 10 calendar days before the end of the month.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| State rent cap | None found | (no statute) |
| Rent increase notice | No separate statute | (none found) |
| Period longer than a month | 30 calendar days before the period ends | CC art. 2728(1) |
| Month-to-month | 10 calendar days before the month ends | CC art. 2728(2) |
| Week or more, under a month | 5 calendar days before the period ends | CC art. 2728(3) |
| Under a week | Any time before the period ends | CC art. 2728(4) |
| Form for a residence | In writing | CC art. 2729 |
| Lessor rights | Changed only by state law | R.S. 9:3258 |
| Notice to vacate | At least 5 days, after the lease ends | CCP art. 4701 |

## Does Louisiana have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

No. We found no Louisiana statute that caps rent or sets rent control.

La. R.S. 9:3258 protects lessor rights. It says every lessor has the right to "ownership, control, use, enjoyment, protection and right to dispose of" private property, including by lease. It adds that those rights "shall not be altered, abridged or diminished except by state law," subject to "the reasonable exercise of the police power."

So the rent amount is a term the parties set in the lease.

## How many days notice does a Louisiana landlord need to raise the rent?

Louisiana statutes set no specific notice period for a rent increase. We found no such article in the Civil Code lease title or in R.S. 9:3251 and after.

For a month-to-month lease, a new rent takes effect at a new month. The safe path is to give the notice that ends the lease under Civil Code art. 2728, at least 10 calendar days before month-end. Offer the new rent for the next month in the same letter.

Worked example. The month ends on the 30th or 31st.

| Month | Last day to give notice | New rent starts |
|---|---|---|
| April (30 days) | April 20 | May 1 |
| May (31 days) | May 21 | June 1 |
| June (30 days) | June 20 | July 1 |

You may give more notice than 10 days. A longer lead time gives the tenant time to plan.

## Can a Louisiana landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

Not unless the lease allows it. A fixed-term lease sets the rent for the term. To raise rent mid-term, the lease needs a clause that says how and when.

Civil Code art. 2726 says an amendment "made without an intent to effect a novation does not create a new lease." So a signed rent change can amend the current lease without starting a new one.

At the end of a fixed term, offer a new lease or a renewal at the new rent. If the tenant stays with your consent and no new lease, the lease can become one with an indeterminate term (art. 2727 refers to a "reconducted lease"). Then art. 2728 sets the notice.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Louisiana?

A month-to-month lease in Louisiana ends with notice given "ten calendar days before the end of that month" (Civil Code art. 2728(2)). The rule binds both landlord and tenant.

Article 2728 sets the other periods:

1. **Longer than a month,** such as year to year: 30 calendar days before the end of that period.
2. **Month-to-month:** 10 calendar days before the end of the month.
3. **A week or more but under a month:** 5 calendar days before the end of the period.
4. **Under a week:** any time before the period ends.

The notice "terminates the lease at the end of the period specified in the notice, and, if none is specified, at the end of the first period for which the notice is timely."

For a residence, the notice must be in writing (art. 2729). The tenant can also give notice by surrendering possession at the right time.

After the lease ends, a tenant who stays gets a notice to vacate. For a lease with no definite term, the termination notice "shall be considered as a notice to vacate" (Code Civ. Proc. art. 4701). So one written notice can do both jobs.

## Do cities in Louisiana add their own rent or notice rules?

R.S. 9:3258 says lessor rights under the Civil Code lease title may not be changed "except by state law." That limits local rent rules.

The same statute keeps "the reasonable exercise of the police power." So check the parish or city where your unit is for local code and safety rules.

## Louisiana rent increase checklist

- Read the lease. No mid-term increase unless the lease allows it.
- For month-to-month, give written notice at least 10 calendar days before month-end.
- For year-to-year, give 30 calendar days before the period ends.
- Name the date the new rent starts.
- Keep a copy and proof of delivery.
- If the tenant will not accept, the same notice can end the lease.

## What is LIHTC, in one paragraph?

The Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) is a federal tax credit for owners of rental housing that stays affordable. It is in section 42 of the Internal Revenue Code, 26 U.S.C. 42. The owner, or investors in the project, claim the credit each year for a 10-year credit period (42(f)(1)). In return, the project must keep a share of its units rent-restricted and rented to income-qualified households for 30 years or more.

LIHTC is a subsidy to the owner, through taxes. It is not rent help paid to the tenant. That is the main difference from a Section 8 voucher.

## What are the 20/50 and 40/60 tests?

The 20/50 and 40/60 tests are the minimum set-asides a project must meet. 26 U.S.C. 42(g)(1) lets the owner elect one of three tests. The election cannot be changed later.

| Test | Minimum share of units | Income cap for those units |
|---|---|---|
| 20/50 | 20% or more | 50% or less of area median gross income |
| 40/60 | 40% or more | 60% or less of area median gross income |
| Average income | 40% or more | Each unit has a designated limit of 20% to 80% of AMI, in 10-point steps; the average may not exceed 60% |

Units in the set-aside must be both rent-restricted and occupied by households under the income cap.

## How are LIHTC rent limits set?

LIHTC rent limits come from income limits, not from the market. 26 U.S.C. 42(g)(2)(A) says a unit is rent-restricted if its gross rent "does not exceed 30 percent of the imputed income limitation applicable to such unit."

Three rules shape the number:

1. **Imputed household size.** The limit assumes 1 person for a unit with no bedroom, and 1.5 people per bedroom (42(g)(2)(C)). A 2-bedroom unit uses a 3-person income limit.
2. **Gross rent includes utilities.** Gross rent includes a utility allowance for tenant-paid utilities (42(g)(2)(B)(ii)).
3. **Section 8 does not count.** Gross rent "does not include any payment under section 8" (42(g)(2)(B)(i)). So a voucher can pay rent on top of what the tenant pays.

A worked example, with a made-up income limit:

| Step | Amount |
|---|---|
| Unit | 2 bedrooms, so 3 people imputed |
| 60% AMI income limit for 3 people (example) | $60,000 a year |
| 30% of that limit | $18,000 a year |
| Maximum gross rent | $1,500 a month |
| Utility allowance (tenant pays) | −$150 |
| Maximum rent the tenant pays you | $1,350 a month |

Your state housing credit agency publishes the real income limits and rent limits for your area. Use those, not this example.

## What is the compliance period?

The compliance period is 15 taxable years, starting with the first year of the credit period (26 U.S.C. 42(i)(1)). After it comes the extended use period. Under 42(h)(6)(D), that period ends on the later of the date in the owner's agreement with the agency, or 15 years after the compliance period ends.

| Period | Length | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Credit period | 10 taxable years | 42(f)(1) |
| Compliance period | 15 taxable years | 42(i)(1) |
| Extended use period | At least 15 more years after the compliance period | 42(h)(6)(D) |
| Total affordability | At least 30 years | |

The extended use period can end early in limited cases, such as acquisition by foreclosure (42(h)(6)(E)). If qualified basis drops during the compliance period, part of the credit can be recaptured (42(j)).

## Must LIHTC owners accept vouchers?

LIHTC owners must not refuse a tenant because the tenant holds a Section 8 voucher. 26 U.S.C. 42(h)(6)(B)(iv) says the extended use commitment must prohibit "the refusal to lease to a holder of a voucher or certificate of eligibility under section 8 ... because of the status of the prospective tenant as such a holder." The commitment is recorded as a restrictive covenant and binds future owners (42(h)(6)(B)(v)-(vi)).

The voucher holder still has to meet your other screening criteria and the LIHTC income limit. On rent, the PHA uses a special rule: if your LIHTC rent for a voucher unit is no higher than your rent for non-voucher LIHTC units in the project, no market comparison is needed (24 CFR 982.507(c)).

LIHTC is also a "covered housing program" (34 U.S.C. 12491(a)), so the CARES Act 30-day notice to vacate rule in 15 U.S.C. 9058(c) reaches LIHTC properties too.

## Who audits LIHTC compliance?

The state or local housing credit agency audits LIHTC compliance, and reports problems to the IRS. The rules are in 26 CFR 1.42-5:

- **Annual owner certification.** The owner certifies at least each year that the project met its set-aside test and other rules (1.42-5(c)(1)).
- **On-site inspections and file reviews.** The agency inspects all buildings and reviews tenant income certifications by the end of the second calendar year after the last building is placed in service, then at least once every 3 years (1.42-5(c)(2)).
- **Inspection standard.** Local health, safety and building codes, or HUD's physical condition standards in 24 CFR 5.703 (1.42-5(d)(2)).
- **Report to the IRS.** The agency tells the owner first, then reports noncompliance to the IRS on Form 8823 (1.42-5(e)).

Checklist for a LIHTC owner:

- [ ] Know which set-aside test you elected.
- [ ] Keep tenant income certifications and proof for every low-income unit.
- [ ] Use the agency's current rent and income limits each year.
- [ ] Track the utility allowance used in gross rent.
- [ ] Never refuse a tenant for holding a voucher.
- [ ] Keep code violation notices and file them with your annual certification.

If you are weighing LIHTC against renting to voucher holders in a market-rate unit, read [Section 8 rent limits by metro](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) for the voucher side.

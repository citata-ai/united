## When does a guest legally become a tenant?

A guest becomes a tenant when the person gets a right to occupy the unit under a rental agreement. The number of nights alone does not decide it. No federal law sets a night count. Each state defines "tenant" in its own landlord-tenant law.

Oregon gives a clear example. ORS 90.100 defines a tenant as a person "entitled under a rental agreement to occupy a dwelling unit to the exclusion of others". The same section says a tenant "does not mean a guest or temporary occupant".

What usually turns a guest into a tenant:

- **Rent.** You or the tenant take money from the guest for the stay.
- **Your consent.** You agree, in writing or by your acts, that the person lives there.
- **Mail and records.** The guest gets mail, a driver's license or utility bills at the address.
- **Time.** The guest stays past the lease limit and you do nothing about it.

The safest rule for you: decide the status in writing before the stay gets long. A clear lease clause and a written agreement remove the doubt.

## What is a guest clause?

A guest clause is the part of the lease that limits how long a guest can stay. It also says what happens when a guest stays longer. The clause protects you from an unscreened adult who lives in the unit with no lease.

A good guest clause has 5 parts:

1. **The limit.** A number of nights in a set period.
2. **The trigger.** What happens when a guest passes the limit.
3. **The process.** The guest applies, or leaves.
4. **The tenant's duty.** The tenant answers for the guest's conduct.
5. **The fair rule.** The clause applies to every guest the same way.

The Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. 3604(b), bans discrimination in the "terms, conditions, or privileges" of a rental. The protected classes in that section are race, color, religion, sex, familial status and national origin. So a guest clause must not treat a visiting grandchild differently from a visiting friend.

## How many nights is common in a guest clause?

No statute we read sets a night count for a guest clause. So the limit is your choice. An example is 14 nights in any 6-month period. Pick a limit you can track, write it in the lease, and apply it to every unit.

Here is how state law changes the picture in 4 states.

| State | Rule | What it means for a guest clause |
|---|---|---|
| Oregon | ORS 90.275: landlord, tenant and guest can sign a temporary occupancy agreement | You can let a guest stay longer without making the guest a tenant |
| New York | RPL 235-f: a single tenant may share with immediate family, 1 more occupant and that occupant's children | A clause cannot limit occupancy to the tenant and immediate family |
| Florida | Fla. Stat. 82.036: sheriff can remove unauthorized occupants, but not current or former tenants | Status matters: a former guest the tenant let in may not fit this process |
| California | Civil Code 1946.5: rules for 1 lodger in an owner-occupied home | Different rules apply when you live in the home and rent 1 room |

New York's rule is the one that surprises most owners. RPL 235-f says a lease clause that limits occupancy to the tenant and immediate family is "unenforceable as against public policy". The tenant must tell you the occupant's name within 30 days after the occupant moves in, or within 30 days after you ask. Under a lease with 2 or more tenants, the total of tenants and occupants (not counting the occupants' dependent children) cannot pass the number of tenants on the lease.

## Can I make a long-term guest apply?

Yes, in most cases you can ask a long-term guest to apply, if the lease gives you that right. Use the same written criteria you use for every applicant. Do not add steps for one guest that you skip for another.

Oregon has a special tool for this. Under ORS 90.275, the landlord, the tenant and the guest sign a temporary occupancy agreement. The rules:

- The landlord may screen the guest for conduct or criminal record.
- The landlord "may not screen the proposed temporary occupant for credit history or income level".
- The temporary occupant is not a tenant and has no tenant rights.
- The tenant can end the agreement at any time without cause.
- The landlord can end it only for a material violation of the agreement.
- The agreement may have an end date, and the parties can extend it.

Outside Oregon, use these steps when a guest passes the limit:

1. **Send a written note** to the tenant. Quote the guest clause and the dates.
2. **Offer 2 options:** the guest leaves by a set date, or the guest applies.
3. **Screen the guest** with your standard criteria if the guest applies.
4. **Add the guest** to the lease with a signed addendum if approved.
5. **Keep records** of each notice and reply in the tenant file.

If you add the guest to the lease, the guest becomes a tenant. The guest then has the full rights and duties of a tenant under your state law.

## What if the guest refuses to leave?

If a guest refuses to leave, do not change the locks or remove the person's things. Use the legal process your state gives you. A self-help removal can be an illegal eviction.

The right process depends on the person's status:

- **Guest of a current tenant.** The tenant controls the guest. Send the tenant a lease violation notice. If the tenant does not fix it, the remedy is against the tenant, often by the eviction process.
- **Person who may be a tenant.** If the person paid rent or you accepted the person as an occupant, use a court eviction.
- **Unauthorized occupant with no tenant rights.** Some states have a faster process. Florida's Fla. Stat. 82.036 lets an owner ask the sheriff to remove unauthorized occupants. All the conditions must be met. One condition is that the occupants are not current or former tenants under a rental agreement the owner authorized, written or oral.

Oregon also covers this case. Under ORS 90.275, a temporary occupant who stays after the tenancy ends, or after the tenant revokes permission, is treated as a squatter. ORS 90.100 defines a squatter as a person who is not entitled to occupy under a rental agreement and is not authorized by the tenant.

California has a separate rule for 1 lodger in a home the owner lives in. Under Civil Code 1946.5, either side can end the stay with written notice. When the notice period ends, the lodger's right to stay ends by operation of law.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Guest clause checklist

Use this list before you sign the next lease:

- [ ] The lease sets a guest limit in nights per period.
- [ ] The limit applies to every guest in the same way.
- [ ] The lease says what happens when a guest passes the limit.
- [ ] The lease lets you ask a long-term guest to apply.
- [ ] Your screening criteria are written and the same for all.
- [ ] You checked your state's occupancy rules, such as New York RPL 235-f.
- [ ] You have a written addendum ready to add an approved occupant.
- [ ] You know your state's legal process to remove a person who stays.

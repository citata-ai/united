## What are rent comps and where do I find them?

Rent comps are homes like yours, near yours, that are for rent now or rented recently. Their rents tell you what renters in your area pay for a home of that kind.

A good comp matches on most of these points:

- **Distance.** Within about 1 mile, or in the same school area or neighborhood.
- **Type.** House to house, townhome to townhome, condo to condo.
- **Beds and baths.** Same bed count. Bath count within half a bath.
- **Size.** Within about 20% of your square feet.
- **Date.** Rented in the last 60 to 90 days, or listed now.

Where to look:

1. **Large rental listing sites.** Filter by your ZIP, bed count and home type. Save active listings and, where the site shows them, recently rented ones.
2. **Local property manager sites.** Many post their vacant homes with the rent.
3. **A local agent or manager.** Many can pull rented prices from the MLS (the local listing database).

Watch for: active listings are asking prices. A home that sat for 6 weeks at $2,300 did not prove the market pays $2,300. Give more weight to homes that rented fast.

## How do I turn comps into one rent?

You adjust each comp to match your house, then take the middle. A simple grid works.

Worked example for a 3-bed, 2-bath house, 1,500 square feet, with a garage. The rents and adjustments are made-up numbers. Use your local market's.

| Comp | Rent | Differences from your house | Adjustment | Adjusted rent |
|------|------|-----------------------------|------------|---------------|
| A | $2,150 | Same beds, 1 bath only | +$75 | $2,225 |
| B | $2,300 | Newer kitchen, no garage | −$50 | $2,250 |
| C | $2,050 | No garage, older carpet | +$125 | $2,175 |
| D | $2,400 | 4 beds | −$150 | $2,250 |
| E | $2,200 | Same in most ways | $0 | $2,200 |

Sorted, the adjusted rents are $2,175, $2,200, $2,225, $2,250 and $2,250. The middle is $2,225. That is your starting asking rent.

Steps in short:

1. Pick 3 to 5 comps.
2. Write each difference from your house.
3. Put a dollar value on each difference.
4. Take the middle of the adjusted rents.
5. List there, and track interest for 2 weeks.

## How do HUD Fair Market Rents compare?

HUD Fair Market Rents (FMRs) are a public check on your number, not your asking rent. Under 24 CFR 888.113, FMRs are estimates of rent plus the cost of utilities, except telephone. HUD sets them at the 40th percentile rent of standard-quality units rented by recent movers.

The 40th percentile means 40% of those units rent for less and 60% rent for more. So a typical market rent is often at or above the FMR. Also, FMR includes utilities. If your tenant pays utilities, compare your rent plus an estimate of the utilities.

FY2027 FMRs, effective 2026-10-01, for 2 and 3 bedrooms (HUD FY2027 file):

| Metro FMR area | 2-bed FMR | 3-bed FMR |
|----------------|-----------|-----------|
| Chicago-Joliet-Naperville, IL | $2,011 | $2,586 |
| Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell, GA | $1,762 | $2,099 |
| Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler, AZ | $1,734 | $2,287 |
| Houston-The Woodlands-Sugar Land, TX | $1,497 | $2,017 |
| Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia, NC-SC | $1,785 | $2,179 |

## Which features raise rent the most?

Features that save the renter money, time or a move tend to raise rent. Your comps show the local value. Compare homes with and without each feature.

Features to check in your comp grid:

- **Bedroom count.** Usually the largest single step.
- **Bathroom count.** A second full bath is often a clear step up.
- **Parking.** A garage or off-street spot, most of all in dense areas.
- **In-unit laundry.** Washer and dryer in the home.
- **Central air.** In hot climates.
- **Updated kitchen and floors.** Condition shows in photos and in days on market.
- **Pet policy.** A pet-friendly home reaches more applicants.
- **Yard and storage.**

Do not price a feature you cannot see in comps. If no comp shows the value of a fence, do not add $100 for one.

## How does the price-to-rent ratio help?

The price-to-rent ratio helps you judge if keeping the house as a rental makes sense. It does not set your rent.

Price-to-rent ratio = home value / (monthly rent x 12)

Worked example:

| Line | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Home value | $360,000 |
| Monthly rent from comps | $2,225 |
| Yearly rent | $26,700 |
| Price-to-rent ratio | 13.5 |

A lower ratio means the rent is high next to the price. That is better for an owner who rents. A high ratio means the rent is low next to the price. In that case, selling may make more sense than renting. Compare the ratio with other homes in your area, not with a national rule.

## How to test your rent after you list

Your listing is the final test. The market answers in about 2 weeks.

- **Many inquiries in 3 days.** You may be below market. Keep the price but screen with care.
- **Steady inquiries, showings, an application in 2 weeks.** You are close to market.
- **Views but few showings.** Price or photos are off. Check both.
- **No interest in 2 weeks.** Lower the rent one step.

Each empty month costs a full month of rent. The US rental vacancy rate was 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026 (Census Bureau). A rent $50 lower that fills 2 weeks sooner often wins over the year.

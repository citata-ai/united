## What does a leasing-only service include?

A leasing-only service finds and places a tenant, then hands the home back to you. The service ends at move-in, or soon after.

Typical leasing-only tasks:

| Task | Leasing-only | Full service |
|------|--------------|--------------|
| Rent price advice | Yes | Yes |
| Photos and listing on rental sites | Yes | Yes |
| Showings | Yes | Yes |
| Application and screening | Yes | Yes |
| Lease drafting and signing | Yes | Yes |
| Collect first month and deposit | Usually | Yes |
| Move-in inspection with photos | Often | Yes |
| Monthly rent collection | No | Yes |
| Repairs and vendor calls | No | Yes |
| Late notices and evictions | No | Yes |
| Periodic inspections | No | Yes |
| Renewal and rent increase | No | Yes |
| Move-out inspection and deposit return | No | Yes |
| Owner statements and 1099 | No | Yes |

Each company draws the line in a different place. Ask for the task list in writing.

A license note. In some states, leasing work for others needs a real estate license, even without ongoing management. Texas Occupations Code 1101.002 counts a person who procures a prospect to effect the lease of real estate, for pay, as a broker. Check that a leasing-only provider holds the license your state requires.

## What does it cost compared with full service?

Leasing-only costs one fee per new lease. Full service costs a monthly fee plus fees per event. iPropertyManagement's 2026 survey (last updated 2026-09-23) gives these averages:

| Fee | Leasing-only | Full service |
|-----|--------------|--------------|
| Placement or leasing fee | 81.8% of one month's rent (range 8% to 150%) | 70.6% of one month's rent (range 6% to 125%) |
| Flat placement fee, where offered | $670.23 average (range $99 to $2,995) | Varies |
| Monthly management fee | None | 8.49% of rent (range 3.75% to 14.0%) |
| Flat monthly fee, where offered | None | $101.04 per unit |
| Setup fee | Ask | $185.24 average |
| Lease renewal, flat | None; you renew | $231.77 average among flat-fee managers |
| Inspection | Usually not included | $106.72 average |

Home365's 2026 fee guide reports similar ranges: tenant placement fees of 50% to 100% of the first month's rent, and repair markups of 10% to 25% at some managers.

Worked 2-year comparison, $2,000 rent, one tenant who renews once (example):

| Cost | Leasing-only | Full service |
|------|--------------|--------------|
| Placement fee | $1,636 (81.8%) | $1,412 (70.6%) |
| Setup fee | $0 | $185 |
| Management, 24 months at 8.49% | $0 | $4,075 |
| Renewal fee | $0 (you do it) | $232 |
| Total fees, 2 years | $1,636 | $5,904 |
| Difference | | $4,268 more for full service |

The difference is the price of your time and risk for 24 months. Divide $4,268 by the hours you expect to spend. That is your hourly "wage" for self-managing. Try your own numbers with our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## Who does the work after move-in?

With leasing-only, you do all the work after move-in. With full service, the manager does it, and you approve large costs.

Your monthly and yearly tasks under leasing-only:

1. **Collect rent** and follow up on late payments.
2. **Answer repair requests,** including nights and weekends.
3. **Hire and pay vendors,** and keep the invoices.
4. **Send legal notices** with the right wording and number of days for your state.
5. **Hold the security deposit** where your state requires. Florida Statutes 83.49, for example, requires the landlord or agent to keep deposit money in a separate Florida bank account and not commingle it.
6. **Inspect the home** at intervals the lease allows.
7. **Handle the renewal:** new rent, new term, signatures.
8. **Do the move-out:** inspection, deposit accounting, turnover.
9. **Keep books** for Schedule E.

Watch for: the hardest month is not move-in. It is the month the water heater fails on a holiday, or rent is 10 days late. Ask yourself if you want to handle that month.

## Which owners fit each option?

Leasing-only fits owners with time, nearby homes and some landlord experience. Full service fits owners who lack one of those.

| Owner situation | Better fit | Why |
|-----------------|------------|-----|
| Lives within 30 minutes, has free evenings | Leasing-only | You can handle repairs and visits |
| Lives in another state | Full service | Someone must be on site |
| First rental, new to local law | Full service, or leasing-only plus an attorney for notices | Notice and deposit errors are costly |
| Long-term tenant, few repair calls | Leasing-only | Low ongoing work |
| Older home with frequent repairs | Full service | Vendor network and quick response |
| Several rentals | Full service | Time adds up fast |
| Tight cash flow | Leasing-only | Saves the monthly fee |

Vacancy matters for both. The Census Bureau reported a national rental vacancy rate of 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026. A good placement that keeps a tenant for 3 years saves 2 placement fees and the empty weeks between leases.

## Questions to ask before you choose

- [ ] What exact tasks are in the leasing-only package?
- [ ] Is the placement fee a percent or flat, and is there a guarantee if the tenant leaves early?
- [ ] Who holds the deposit after move-in?
- [ ] What screening criteria do you use? Are they written and applied the same way to every applicant?
- [ ] Can I upgrade to full service later, and is any fee credited?
- [ ] What is the full-service fee schedule, including renewals, inspections and repair markups?
- [ ] Does your company hold the license my state requires?

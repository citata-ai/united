Nebraska evictions run on a short, fixed clock. The table below gives each step, its limit and the statute. We read each section on the Nebraska Legislature site (through an Internet Archive copy, because the site did not load from our location) on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice for unpaid rent | 7 calendar days to pay | Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1431(2) |
| Notice for a lease breach | Ends in 30+ days; 14 days to fix | 76-1431(1) |
| Same breach again within 6 months | 14 days' written notice, no cure | 76-1431(1) |
| Violent crime, drug sale, threat to safety | 5 days' written notice, no cure | 76-1431(4) |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | 30 days before a rental date | 76-1437(2) |
| End a week-to-week tenancy | 7 days before the end date | 76-1437(1) |
| Serve the summons | Within 3 days of issue (judicial days) | 76-1442 |
| Trial | 10 to 14 days after summons issues | 76-1446 |
| Writ of restitution | Removal within 10 days of the writ | 76-1446 |
| Lockout or utility shutoff | Tenant gets 3 months' rent + fees | 76-1430 |

## What notice must a Nebraska landlord give for unpaid rent?

A Nebraska landlord gives a written 7-day notice for unpaid rent. Under Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1431(2), the notice states that rent is unpaid. It also states that you will end the rental agreement if the tenant does not pay within that period. If the tenant does not pay in 7 calendar days, you may end the agreement.

Watch for: Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1433 says that if you accept rent and you know of the default, you waive your right to end the agreement for that breach. The exception is a new agreement made after the breach. So decide before you take a partial payment.

What to put in a 7-day rent notice:

- [ ] Tenant name and unit address
- [ ] Amount of rent that is unpaid and the due date
- [ ] A clear statement that the agreement ends if rent is not paid within 7 calendar days
- [ ] Date and method of delivery, with a copy for your file

## Can a Nebraska landlord evict for a lease violation or with no cause?

A Nebraska landlord can evict for a lease violation after a 30-day notice with a 14-day chance to fix it. Under 76-1431(1), the notice names the breach. It says the agreement ends on a date at least 30 days after the tenant receives it, unless the tenant fixes the breach in 14 days. If the same breach happens again within 6 months, you may end the agreement on 14 days' written notice.

A faster path applies to danger on the premises. Under 76-1431(4), you may give 5 days' written notice with no right to cure. This covers violent criminal activity, illegal sale of a controlled substance, and other acts that threaten the health or safety of others. Subsection (5) limits this rule. If a person who is not the tenant or a household member did the act, and the tenant sought a protective order or reported the act to police, you may not use this path against the tenant.

There is no "no cause" rule in the lease-breach section. But at the end of a periodic tenancy, either side may end it with notice under 76-1437:

- **Month-to-month:** written notice at least 30 days before the periodic rental date in the notice.
- **Week-to-week:** written notice at least 7 days before the end date.

If the tenant stays after a valid end date, you may sue for possession. If the holdover is willful and not in good faith, 76-1437(3) lets you recover up to 3 months' periodic rent or 3 times actual damages, whichever is greater, plus attorney's fees.

Retaliation is a limit on every path. Under 76-1439, you may not bring or threaten an action for possession because a tenant complained to a housing code agency or joined a tenants' union. The section lists exceptions, for example when the tenant is in default in rent.

## Where does a Nebraska landlord file, and what does it cost?

A Nebraska landlord files a complaint for restitution with the clerk of the county or district court. Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1441 says the complaint must state:

1. **The statute** under which you seek possession.
2. **The facts**, with particularity.
3. **A description** of the premises.
4. **Proof of notice** under the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act.

Note: the old forcible entry and detainer sections (25-21,219 to 25-21,235) do not apply to premises under the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act. Section 25-21,219 says so in its last sentence. Most residential evictions use 76-1440 to 76-1447.

Court costs come from the Nebraska Judicial Branch fee schedule, effective 2026-07-01:

| County court item | Fee |
|---|---|
| Civil filing, $1,500 or less in dispute | $62 total |
| Civil filing, $1,500.01 to $7,500 | $72 total |
| Civil filing, $7,500.01 or more | $82 total |
| Writ of restitution | $5 |

The schedule shows a $62 to $82 range, set by a state docket fee of $0, $10 or $20. Service fees and attorney fees are extra.

Watch for: the Judicial Branch landlord page says a corporation or LLC landlord must have an attorney in court, except in small claims. The page also says no court forms exist for this case type now.

## How long does an eviction take in Nebraska?

A Nebraska eviction for unpaid rent takes about 4 to 5 weeks when nothing delays it. The clock comes from 76-1442 and 76-1446. Here is a worked timeline with a notice delivered on November 2, 2026:

| Step | Rule | Example date |
|---|---|---|
| 7-day notice delivered | 76-1431(2) | Mon, Nov 2 |
| Rent still unpaid; file complaint | 76-1441 | Tue, Nov 10 |
| Summons issued; served within 3 judicial days | 76-1442 | From Nov 10 |
| Trial, 10 to 14 days after issue | 76-1446 | Nov 20 to Nov 24 |
| Writ issued after judgment | 76-1446 | Nov 24 |
| Sheriff restores possession, within 10 days | 76-1446 | By Dec 4 |

That is about 32 days from notice to removal. Delays add time:

- **Continuance.** Under 76-1443, the court may grant one for good cause. A later continuance needs agreement or extraordinary cause. If it moves trial into the next rental period, the court may order the tenant to deposit rent with the clerk.
- **Appeal.** Under 76-1447, a tenant's appeal stops the writ if the tenant deposits the judgment and costs, or gives a bond, and pays monthly rent into court.
- **Posted service.** If normal service fails, 76-1442.01 allows posting on the front door plus first-class mail. In that case, 76-1446 says the court will not grant you a money judgment.

The trial is held by the judge, without a jury (76-1446). If the tenant does not appear after proper service, the court tries the case as though the tenant were present (76-1444).

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Nebraska?

The sheriff or a constable removes the tenant, not the landlord. After judgment, 76-1446 says the court issues a writ of restitution at your request. The writ directs the constable or sheriff to restore possession on a set date no more than 10 days after the writ issues.

For items the tenant leaves behind, 76-1446 tells you to follow the Disposition of Personal Property Landlord and Tenant Act and 76-1414(5). Read that Act before you move or sell anything.

## Is a self-help eviction legal in Nebraska?

A self-help eviction is not a safe option in Nebraska. Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1440 says an action for possession of premises under the Act must start in the way that 76-1440 to 76-1447 describe. And 76-1430 sets the cost of a lockout or utility shutoff.

If a landlord unlawfully removes or excludes a tenant, or willfully cuts electric, gas, water or another essential service, the tenant may:

- recover possession or end the rental agreement;
- recover 3 months' periodic rent as liquidated damages;
- recover a reasonable attorney's fee.

Abandonment is different. Under 76-1432(3), absence without notice for one full rental period or 30 days, whichever is less, counts as abandonment. Even then, document the facts with care.

## Why does the move-in inspection matter?

The move-in inspection is your proof of the unit's condition on day 1. At move-out, state laws such as Virginia's let you deduct for damage, less reasonable wear and tear. Without a dated record of the start condition, you cannot show what changed.

State deposit laws make the stakes clear:

| State | Move-in rule | Move-out deadline for itemized deductions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Virginia | Written move-in report within 5 days after occupancy; tenant has 5 days to object | 45 days | Va. Code 55.1-1214, 55.1-1226 |
| Arizona | Landlord must give a move-in form for existing damage, and notice that the tenant may attend the move-out inspection | 14 days, excluding weekends and holidays | ARS 33-1321(C), (D) |
| Florida | No move-in form in this section | 30 days to send notice of a claim | Fla. Stat. 83.49(3) |

Arizona also lets a tenant recover twice the amount wrongfully withheld if the landlord does not follow the deposit rules. A clear move-in record helps you avoid that dispute.

## Should the tenant attend the inspection?

Yes, the tenant should attend if you can arrange it. A report that you both walk, note and sign is hard to dispute later. Virginia's 55.1-1214 lets the landlord adopt a written policy for a joint move-in report. Once both sign and get a copy, the statute says the report "shall be deemed correct."

If the tenant cannot attend:

1. **Do the inspection yourself** before the tenant gets the keys.
2. **Send the report and photos** to the tenant the same day.
3. **Ask for written notes** within your state's window, or the one in your lease.

## How many photos do I need?

Take enough photos to show every surface, fixture and appliance, plus a close-up of each defect. No statute sets a number. More is fine; gaps are what hurt you.

A simple photo plan for each room:

- **1 wide shot per wall**, from the opposite corner.
- **Floor and ceiling**, one shot each.
- **Windows, doors, locks and screens.**
- **Close-ups of every defect**, with a coin or ruler for scale.
- **Appliances**, inside and out: oven, fridge, dishwasher, washer, dryer.
- **Meters and smoke alarms**, with readings visible.

Turn on the date and time stamp, or keep the original files, which carry the date in their data. Name the folder with the address and date.

## Should I use video?

Video is a strong addition to photos and the written report. A slow, steady walk-through shows how rooms connect and makes it hard to claim a photo was staged.

Video tips:

1. **Say the address, date and time** at the start.
2. **Walk slowly** and open every cabinet, closet and appliance.
3. **Turn on taps and lights** to show they work.
4. **Keep the file whole.** Do not edit it.

## Room-by-room move-in checklist

Use one row per item and one of 3 ratings: Good, Fair (note the detail), Damaged (note and photograph).

| Area | Check |
|---|---|
| Entry | Door, locks, deadbolt, peephole, doorbell, floor |
| Living room | Walls, paint, ceiling, floor or carpet, windows, blinds, outlets, lights |
| Kitchen | Counters, cabinets, sink, faucet, disposal, stove, oven, hood, fridge, dishwasher, floor |
| Bathrooms | Toilet, sink, faucet, tub or shower, caulk, tile, mirror, fan, towel bars |
| Bedrooms | Walls, closet doors, floor or carpet, windows, screens, outlets |
| Systems | Smoke and CO alarms, thermostat, water heater, HVAC filter |
| Outside | Yard, fence, patio, garage door, exterior lights |
| Keys | Count of keys, fobs, remotes handed over |

For homes built before 1978, also give the EPA lead disclosure before the lease is signed. EPA says landlords must give the "Protect Your Family From Lead in Your Home" pamphlet, disclose known lead paint and hazards, give available reports, and include a Lead Warning Statement. Keep the signed disclosure for 3 years after the lease begins.

## How long does the tenant have to add items?

The window depends on the state. Virginia's 55.1-1214 gives a clear model. The landlord must give a written report within 5 days after occupancy. The report "shall be deemed correct unless the tenant objects to it in writing within five days after receipt."

Where your state is silent, set a window in the lease, such as 5 to 7 days. Ask for additions in writing, with photos. Then:

1. **Check each item** the tenant adds.
2. **Fix what you can**, or note it in the report.
3. **Sign and date the final version.** Give the tenant a copy.

## How do I store the report?

Store the signed report and every photo and video file together, in one folder per tenancy. Keep it until the deposit is settled and any dispute period has passed.

Storage checklist:

- **Two copies:** cloud storage plus a local drive.
- **Original files**, not compressed copies from a messaging app.
- **The signed PDF** of the report and any tenant additions.
- **Repair invoices** during the tenancy, so you can show what was new.
- **The move-out report** in the same folder, with matching photo angles.

When the tenant moves out, repeat the same checklist and the same photo angles. A side-by-side comparison is the clearest evidence you can bring. To see how managers handle this work at scale, read about [property management](/property-management).

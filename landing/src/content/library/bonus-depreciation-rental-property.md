## Which rental assets can qualify for bonus depreciation?

Eligible shorter-life assets can qualify when they meet the rules in IRS Publication 946. The asset's tax classification matters more than the label rental property. Identify each asset and its supported cost before calculating a deduction.

Publication 946 generally includes qualifying tangible property depreciated under MACRS with a recovery period of 20 years or less. Other eligible categories and exclusions exist. Used property also has specific conditions.

IRS Publication 527 lists rental appliances, carpets, and furniture with shorter recovery periods than a building. That table helps identify assets for review. It does not prove every purchase qualifies for immediate deduction.

| Asset category | IRS treatment to review | Bonus question |
|---|---|---|
| Residential building and structural parts | Generally 27.5-year GDS property under Publication 527 | Building itself generally does not qualify |
| Land | Not depreciable under Publication 527 | No bonus deduction for land |
| Appliances and rental furniture | Publication 527 lists shorter-life categories | Check Publication 946 eligibility and dates |
| Certain site improvements | Classification depends on the actual work | Review asset class and qualifying basis |
| A major building improvement | May follow building recovery treatment | Do not assume a contractor invoice qualifies |

Keep the purchase invoice, installation date, use, and classification support. A single total for a renovation hides the details needed for review. Ask your tax professional to separate the assets correctly.

## Does the rental building itself qualify?

The residential rental building generally does not qualify for bonus depreciation. Publication 527 gives residential buildings and structural components a 27.5-year GDS recovery period. That differs from the shorter-life category generally eligible under Publication 946.

Land is not depreciable. Allocate purchase cost between land and depreciable property using a supported method. A purchase price alone is not the building's depreciation basis.

Do not treat a new roof as furniture because it was installed after purchase. Publication 527 lists structural components and improvements separately from shorter-life personal property. Classification follows the asset and rules, not your preferred deduction.

A cost allocation review can identify assets that need different treatment. Any study must support the actual components and costs. It cannot turn the entire purchase price into qualifying equipment by choosing a percentage without evidence.

If you convert a former home, ask about conversion basis and prior personal use. The depreciation calculation can differ from simply using the original purchase price. Preserve valuation and improvement records from before the rental began.

## What changed in recent tax law?

IRS Publication 946 describes restored 100% special depreciation for certain qualifying property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025. IRS Notice 2026-11 provides related guidance. Both dates and eligibility conditions matter.

Do not repeat an old phase-down percentage without checking which acquisition rules apply. Publication 946 also describes treatment for earlier acquisitions and available elections. A service date alone may not settle the rate.

Ask your preparer how contracts, acquisition dates, installation, and availability affect the asset. Preserve the signed order and invoice as well as proof of service. A bank payment date does not always explain every relevant fact.

The IRS guidance also discusses elections to use different treatment in specified situations. Ask whether an election fits your whole return and future plans. The largest immediate deduction is not automatically the most useful choice.

State income tax treatment may differ from federal treatment. Have your preparer review conformity and adjustments for the property's jurisdiction. Keep the state result separate in any savings estimate.

## How is bonus depreciation different from Section 179?

Bonus depreciation and Section 179 are separate deduction rules. IRS Publication 946 explains each, including qualifying property and limits. Do not assume that equipment eligible for one always qualifies for the other.

Section 179 has a business income limit and a dollar limit. Publication 946 also describes restrictions for certain property leased to others by noncorporate lessors. That rental restriction deserves specific review before an owner chooses Section 179.

Bonus depreciation follows its own eligibility and election rules. It can affect the timing of deductions without matching Section 179's income limit. Other limits can still restrict the loss you use on the return.

Ask the tax professional to compare treatments for the actual asset list. Include expected future income, a possible sale, and any state adjustment. A quick comparison should show both the deduction and when it can be used.

## Does a bonus deduction create immediate tax savings?

A deduction only creates current savings to the extent the tax rules allow you to use it. IRS Publication 925 describes passive activity and at-risk limits. A rental loss may remain suspended rather than reduce wage income now.

Here is a hypothetical calculation, not tax advice or a forecast. Assume $10,000 of eligible basis qualifies for 100% bonus treatment. The initial depreciation amount would be $10,000 before considering the owner's other limitations.

That does not mean a $10,000 refund. Tax rates, allowed loss use, other deductions, elections, and state treatment affect the result. Ask for an adviser-reviewed tax projection before spending cash to obtain a deduction.

A later sale can also change the tax cost. Keep basis and depreciation schedules so the preparer can calculate gain and any recapture. Faster deductions change timing; they do not erase the need for sale reporting.

## What records should you give your preparer?

Provide an asset file that supports cost, classification, dates, and use. Keep the underlying records even if a study or software produces the final schedule.

- **List each asset** with a clear description and location.
- **Save cost evidence** including invoices and installation charges.
- **Record acquisition terms** with orders and signed contracts.
- **Confirm service dates** and rental availability.
- **Preserve use records** for any personal or mixed use.
- **Review loss limits** alongside the depreciation schedule.

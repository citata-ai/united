## Which duties belong in the description?

List the work the person will actually perform, then separate work owned by other roles. A long list of every possible property task can hide the job's real priorities.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics lists common duties such as arranging repairs, maintaining rental records and preparing financial reports. Use those duty areas to check coverage. The BLS describes a broad occupation, so it does not define your company's exact role.

Use this generic template as a starting point. Replace each bracket with an approved fact. Check local law before publishing the finished description.

```text
Role: Residential Property Manager
Company and work location: [Name, location, remote or field arrangement]
Reports to: [Role]
Portfolio scope: [Property types, service area and assigned workload]

Purpose:
Manage the assigned homes with clear records, timely follow-up
and service within approved authority.

Core duties:
- Record and respond to owner and resident requests.
- Coordinate repair work and confirm follow-up.
- Keep lease, inspection and account records current.
- Review property reports and explain exceptions.
- Coordinate leasing with the responsible licensed staff.
- Escalate safety concerns, disputes and decisions outside authority.

Authority:
May approve [work or decisions] within [written limits].
Must refer [decisions] to [responsible role].

Schedule and travel:
[Hours, field visits, travel area and after-hours duties]

Required skills and credentials:
[Job-related skills and locally required licenses]
Training provided: [Processes and tools]

Pay and benefits:
[Approved range, pay basis, incentive terms and benefits]

Early success:
[Training, supervised work, record checks and review milestones]

Application:
[How to apply, contact and accommodation request route]
```

Do not leave the bracketed limits out of the final version. Staff need to know which decisions they own and when they must ask for approval.

## Which skills and licenses should I request?

Request skills that match the duties and credentials that local rules require. Separate required qualifications from preferences you can teach after hiring.

Clear writing, careful records and dependable follow-up matter for many management roles. Test these skills with a job-related sample rather than assuming a software name on a resume proves competence.

Ask the responsible broker to review the role before you advertise it. TREC says paid showing or leasing for an owner requires a license in Texas. Its guidance also covers control of rent acceptance or deposits for single-family residential property. Other states need their own check.

Avoid a national license statement based on that example. A person's duties, property type and exemptions can matter. State the exact credential your reviewed role needs.

Describe field work accurately. Explain travel, property visits and the equipment or access the job uses. Ask advisers how to write essential duties and handle accommodation requests.

The EEOC says employers can ask applicants to demonstrate specific tasks, but limits pre-offer questions about disability. Apply that guidance to the application and interview as well as the written description.

Do not add a degree or long experience requirement without a reason tied to the work. An unnecessary filter can exclude someone who can perform the role with training.

## How should I write the pay range?

Use an approved pay range that reflects the role, location, schedule and compensation plan. Check applicable pay disclosure rules before publishing. This guide does not provide a universal legal disclosure requirement.

Show whether the base uses salary or hourly pay. If incentives apply, describe the structure clearly enough that applicants can distinguish base pay from possible extra pay. Do not present an uncertain bonus as guaranteed earnings.

Review the full cost with your payroll adviser. Include employer costs, travel arrangements, training and after-hours coverage. A role with regular field work and urgent calls needs an honest description of those expectations.

Use BLS occupation data as background only if you fetch the relevant current location and measure. A broad national occupation figure does not set a leasing agent's local wage. This template therefore leaves the actual range blank.

Make sure the advertised terms match the offer and payroll setup. If you change the role during hiring, update the written description before asking applicants to accept it.

Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to check service economics when planning the role. It helps inspect fee revenue; it does not determine a lawful or competitive wage.

## How should I describe success in the first 90 days?

Use the first 90 days as a suggested review period, not a claim about how quickly every hire should perform independently. Match the goals to experience and training support.

Start with process knowledge and safe escalation. Ask the hire to explain the service scope, authority limits and record locations. A person should know how to get help before taking full account responsibility.

Next, use supervised cases. Review a repair file, an owner update and a reporting exception. Give feedback against the same written standards you used in hiring.

Then increase responsibility when the work supports it. Check whether the hire closes follow-up tasks, records decisions and flags missing facts. Do not assign a larger portfolio merely because the calendar reaches a planned date.

Write goals that describe observable work. For example, require complete case notes and a clear next action in the reviewed sample. Avoid a vague instruction to take ownership without explaining the evidence.

Keep goals under the hire's control. Rent levels, owner approvals and property condition may affect results. Review those factors before judging the person on final outcomes alone.

## What should I check before posting the role?

Check the description against the actual organization and current agreements. The role should not promise authority that the company cannot grant.

Have the supervisor confirm duties and training. Have payroll confirm compensation terms and have the broker confirm licensed activities. Ask counsel to review local employment requirements and the final application process.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Give the draft to someone who does similar work. Ask what remains unclear about a normal day, a difficult call and the escalation route. Resolve those gaps before you publish the opening.

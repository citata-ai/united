## Who must register a rental in Cleveland?

Every owner of a non-owner-occupied residential unit in Cleveland must register it each year. The Department of Building and Housing says this covers all units the owner does not live in. It can include units where family or friends live for free.

Codified Ordinances 365.02 sets two steps:

1. **Register the unit.** You get a Certificate of Non-Owner-Occupied Residential Unit Registration. The certificate itself says it does not prove the unit is lead safe or meets code.
2. **Get a Certificate Approving Rental Occupancy.** You need it before you rent. It shows you met the code, lead and tax rules.

A vacant unit uses a separate Vacant Property Registration. A government owner is exempt until it plans to rent.

The timeline, per Building and Housing: renewals open January 1, and all fees must be paid by March 31. The ordinance text says apply on or before March 1. A new rental must register before the tenant moves in.

## What does Cleveland rental registration cost?

Cleveland rental registration costs $70 per unit per year, under Codified Ordinances 365.03.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Registration fee | $70 per non-owner-occupied unit, each year |
| Yearly cap per owner | $30,000 for all units in the city |
| Late renewal | $70 fee plus a $100 late fee |
| Lead Safe Certificate application | No fee (Building and Housing) |
| Lead clearance or risk assessment | Set by the private contractor |

Worked example: you own 4 doubles (8 units) and one 6-unit building. That is 14 units × $70 = $980 a year. The $30,000 cap only matters above 428 units. If you renew late, add $100 per expired certificate.

## What documents does Cleveland need for a Certificate Approving Rental Occupancy?

Cleveland needs up to 6 documents, depending on the property. The Building and Housing page lists them:

| Document | Who needs it |
|---|---|
| Property tax statement | All applications |
| Lead Safe Certificate from the City of Cleveland | Rental units built before 1978 |
| Ohio Secretary of State Certificate of Good Standing | Owners that are LLCs |
| Local Agent in Charge affidavit, notarized | Owners outside Cuyahoga, Summit, Lake, Portage, Medina, Lorain and Geauga counties |
| HVAC certificate from a licensed HVAC contractor | Buildings with 4 or more units |
| Utility bills | Owners who pay the tenants' utilities |

For buildings with a separate HVAC system in each unit, the city asks for 10% of the systems, with a minimum of 5 units. You also need no open code violations, no unpaid city fees or fines, and current property taxes or a county payment plan.

You can apply before you have every document. The city gives you a receipt as proof, and sends the certificate when the file is complete.

A Local Agent in Charge can be held liable for code problems if the owner cannot be found. Pick that person with care.

## What is the Cleveland Lead Safe Certificate?

The Cleveland Lead Safe Certificate is the city's proof that a pre-1978 rental unit had no lead hazards at the last check. Cleveland City Council passed the law in 2019 (Ordinance 747-2019). Codified Ordinances 365.04 presumes that every rental built before January 1, 1978 has lead-based paint.

How to get it, per 365.04 and the Building and Housing page:

1. **Hire an independent professional.** A clearance technician, lead inspector or lead risk assessor.
2. **Get a clearance exam or lead risk assessment.** It must show no lead hazards in the unit.
3. **Submit the report within 90 days** of the inspection, in the Citizen Access Portal.
4. **Upload the certificate** to your rental registration once the city issues it.
5. **Renew every 2 years.** Apply no earlier than 30 days before it expires.

For a pre-1978 building with 5 or more units, the report can cover a sample of units, based on the HUD Guidelines (2012 edition).

Independence is strict. Under 365.04(f), the inspector may not be you, your spouse or dependent child, your employee, your property manager, or anyone with a stake in the lab result.

## Which rentals must have the certificate, and when?

All Cleveland rental units built before January 1, 1978 must have lead-safe certification. The ordinance phased it in from March 1, 2021 and set a final deadline of March 1, 2023. So in 2026, every pre-1978 rental should already hold a current certificate.

Three cases differ:

- **20-year exemption.** Submit a comprehensive lead risk assessment and paint inspection showing the unit was abated under 40 CFR 745.227. The report must be less than 20 years old. The Residents First page calls this the 20-year path.
- **Empty units.** A unit that is unoccupied and earns no rent needs no certificate. You file a yearly affidavit instead.
- **Built in 1978 or later.** No Lead Safe Certificate is needed.

Money help exists. The Lead Safe Cleveland Coalition says rental owners can get $1,000 per 20-year lead-free exemption or $750 per Lead Safe Certification from the Lead Safe Home Fund, within set limits. Grants for repair work are also open to rental owners. Ask your neighborhood fund administrator for the current terms.

## What fines apply for missing registration in Cleveland?

Missing registration or a missing Lead Safe Certificate is a first-degree misdemeanor in Cleveland. Codified Ordinances 367.99(d) names Sections 365.02 and 365.04 directly. Each day of a continuing violation is a separate offense.

Building and Housing adds that renting without a Certificate Approving Rental Occupancy may lead to civil or criminal action against the owner or the Local Agent in Charge. A court can also order you to pay inspection fees for violations you did not fix.

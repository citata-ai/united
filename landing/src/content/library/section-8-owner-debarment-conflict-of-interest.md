## Which owners can never rent to a voucher holder?

Three kinds of owner face a hard bar under the voucher rules. The PHA has no choice in these cases, except where the rule names one.

| Hard bar | Rule | Exception |
|---|---|---|
| Owner is debarred, suspended or under a limited denial of participation | 24 CFR 982.306(a) | None in the rule |
| Owner is a close relative of a family member | 24 CFR 982.306(d) | PHA approves it as a disability accommodation |
| PHA official or program decision-maker has an interest | 24 CFR 982.161 | HUD field office waiver for good cause |

A fourth bar applies when HUD directs it. The PHA must refuse an owner when the federal government has a pending Fair Housing Act action against the owner, or a court or agency found a violation (982.306(b)).

"Owner" here includes "a principal or other interested party" (982.306(f)). So an LLC member, a partner or a co-owner can trigger a bar.

## Can I rent to a relative with a voucher?

Not if you are a close relative, unless the PHA approves it as an accommodation. 24 CFR 982.306(d) says the PHA "must not approve a unit if the owner is the parent, child, grandparent, grandchild, sister, or brother of any member of the family".

The rule has 2 limits:

1. **Disability exception.** The PHA may approve the unit if it "would provide reasonable accommodation for a family member who is a person with disabilities".
2. **Timing.** The bar applies "at the time a family initially receives tenant-based assistance for occupancy of a particular unit". It does not apply to a new tenancy with continued assistance in the same unit.

Relatives outside that list, such as cousins, aunts, uncles, nieces and nephews, are not named in the rule. Your PHA's Administrative Plan may still ask about them. Project-based vouchers have the same close-relative rule in 24 CFR 983.251(a)(4).

## What is a conflict of interest with PHA staff?

A conflict of interest exists when a person tied to the PHA has an interest in a voucher contract. 24 CFR 982.161 bars the PHA and its contractors from any HCV contract in which these people have "any interest, direct or indirect", during their tenure and for 1 year after:

- present or former PHA members or officers, except a participant commissioner;
- PHA employees, contractors or agents who make policy or influence program decisions;
- public officials, governing body members and state or local legislators with duties tied to the program;
- members of Congress.

Each of these people must disclose the interest to the PHA and HUD. The HUD field office may waive the bar for good cause.

Watch for: an indirect interest counts. A PHA inspector who co-owns an LLC that owns your building is a problem, even if you sign every form.

## What does debarment mean?

Debarment means a federal agency has excluded a person from federal transactions. 2 CFR 180.925 defines it as an action "to exclude a person from participating in covered transactions". HUD's own rules are in 2 CFR part 2424. HUD can also issue a limited denial of participation, which excludes a person from a specific program in a HUD field office's area for a set time (2 CFR 2424.1100).

Causes for debarment in 2 CFR 180.800 include:

- conviction or civil judgment for fraud or a crime tied to a public or private agreement;
- embezzlement, theft, forgery, bribery, false statements or false claims;
- serious failure to perform a public agreement, or a history of poor performance;
- knowingly doing business with an excluded person;
- an unpaid, uncontested federal debt.

2 CFR 180.865 says debarment "generally should not exceed three years". For covered transactions, 2 CFR 180.300 names SAM.gov Exclusions as one way to check that a person is not excluded. You can search your own name there too.

## Can a PHA refuse an owner with code violations?

Yes, at its discretion. 24 CFR 982.306(c) lets a PHA deny approval for 7 reasons:

1. The owner violated a Section 8 HAP contract.
2. The owner committed fraud, bribery or a corrupt or criminal act in a federal housing program.
3. The owner engaged in drug-related or violent criminal activity.
4. The owner has a history or practice of HQS failures, or of failing other federal housing standards.
5. The owner has a history of not ending tenancies for criminal activity that threatens others.
6. The owner has a history or practice of renting units that fail state or local housing codes.
7. The owner has not paid state or local real estate taxes, fines or assessments.

The rule also says nothing in it gives an owner "any right to participate in the program" (982.306(e)). So a PHA refusal is hard to challenge.

The same acts can end an existing contract. Under 24 CFR 982.453, a breach of any Section 8 HAP contract, fraud, bribery, drug-related crime or violent crime by the owner breaches the HAP contract.

## What disclosures does the PHA ask for?

The PHA forms we read ask owners to prove ownership and certify that no bar applies. The exact forms vary. NYC HPD's HAP assignment form shows typical certifications. The owner certifies:

- they are "not the parent, child, grandparent, grandchild, sister or brother" of any family member, or that a reasonable accommodation was requested;
- they "have not been debarred, suspended, or subject to a limited denial of participation";
- the federal government has not started legal action against them for Fair Housing violations.

Owner checklist before you submit an RFTA (Request for Tenancy Approval):

- [ ] Deed or proof of ownership, in the same name as the W-9.
- [ ] List of all owners, LLC members and partners.
- [ ] Check each one in SAM.gov Exclusions.
- [ ] Confirm no owner is a close relative of any family member.
- [ ] Confirm no owner is a PHA official, staff member who shapes decisions, or local official with program duties, now or in the past year.
- [ ] Pay any open real estate taxes, city fines and assessments.
- [ ] Clear open code violations on your other rentals.
- [ ] Sign the PHA's owner certifications.

If one item fails, ask the PHA before you sign a lease. A tenancy the PHA cannot approve leaves the family and you without a HAP. Once you are cleared, estimate the HAP for the unit with our [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator).

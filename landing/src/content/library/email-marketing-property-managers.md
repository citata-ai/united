An owner fills in your rental analysis form in March. They are not ready. Their tenant moves out in August, and they call the first manager they think of. Email decides if that manager is you.

Most owners do not sign on the first contact. A monthly email keeps your name in front of them for the months between the first form and the real need. The same email shows current owners that you watch their market.

## Which email lists should a PM build?

A property manager should build three marketing lists and keep tenants apart. Each list gets different content.

| List | Who | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| Current owners | Every owner you manage for | Monthly owner newsletter, law changes, year-end tax reminders |
| Owner leads | Owners who asked for a quote or analysis but did not sign | Follow-up series, then the monthly newsletter |
| Partners | Real estate agents, lenders, attorneys, past clients | Quarterly note, referral terms, market numbers |
| Tenants (service only) | Current residents | Maintenance, rent and lease notices, not marketing |

Keep tenants off the marketing lists. Their emails are service messages, and mixing them in makes your open rates and lead counts wrong.

Grow the owner lead list from four places:

1. Your owner contact and rental analysis forms.
2. A downloadable owner guide, such as a fee sheet or a local law checklist.
3. Business cards and sign-in sheets at landlord meetups.
4. Owners who call. Ask if they want the monthly market email, and log the yes.

## What content do owners open?

Owners open email that helps them make or protect money on their rental. Local numbers do best, because owners cannot get them from a national newsletter.

Content that fits each list:

- **Local rent and vacancy numbers.** Median rent for 2- and 3-bedroom homes, days on market, from your own leases.
- **Law and rule changes.** A new deposit rule, notice period or rental license fee in your city, with the source.
- **Cost answers.** What a turn cost this quarter, what a roof or HVAC job costs now.
- **One case.** A short story of one property: the problem, what you did, the result in numbers.
- **One call to action.** "Reply for a free rental analysis" or "Send this to a friend who owns a rental."

Write the subject line as a fact or a question. "[City] rents rose 3% this quarter" works better than "October newsletter". Only use a number you can show a source for.

## How often should I send?

Send the owner newsletter once a month, and send new leads a short follow-up series. One a month is often enough to stay known and rare enough to avoid spam complaints.

A sample follow-up series for a new owner lead:

| Day | Email | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Thank you, plus the rental analysis or fee sheet | Deliver what they asked for |
| 3 | Short answer to the top owner question in your city | Show local knowledge |
| 10 | One case study or review | Proof |
| 30 | "Is now a good time to talk?" | Invite a call |
| 60+ | Monthly newsletter | Stay known |

Stop the series when the owner books a call or replies. A person should answer every reply within one business day.

## Which rules apply to owner emails?

The federal CAN-SPAM Act applies to commercial email, including email to past clients. The FTC lists these duties (FTC, CAN-SPAM guide):

- Accurate "From", "To" and routing details.
- A subject line that does not mislead.
- A clear note that the message is an ad, where it is one.
- Your valid physical postal address.
- A clear way to opt out, which works for at least 30 days after you send.
- Opt-outs honored within 10 business days.

Each separate email that breaks the law can bring a penalty of up to $53,088 (FTC). This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Gmail adds technical rules. Since February 1, 2024, all senders to Gmail accounts must set up SPF or DKIM and keep the spam rate in Postmaster Tools below 0.3%. Senders of more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail must also pass DMARC and support one-click unsubscribe (Google Workspace Admin Help). SPF, DKIM and DMARC are DNS records that prove the email is really from your domain. Ask your website or email vendor to set them up once.

## Which tools fit a small PM company?

Any email tool with list segments, simple automation and unsubscribe handling fits a small property manager. Check these features before you pick one:

- Separate lists or tags for owners, leads and partners.
- An automated series for new leads.
- A form you can put on your website.
- Clear reports: opens, clicks, unsubscribes, spam complaints.
- SPF, DKIM and DMARC setup help.

Cost depends on contact count. Mailchimp, for example, offers a basic Free plan for businesses with fewer than 500 contacts (Mailchimp, read 2026-10-19). Many property management software tools also send owner email. Check if yours can segment owners from tenants before you buy a second tool.

## How do I know email is working?

Email works if it brings owner calls, signed doors and fewer cancellations. Opens alone do not pay fees. Track three numbers each month:

1. Owner leads that came back after an email, by the "How did you first hear about us?" answer and the email link tags.
2. Signed doors from the owner lead list.
3. Owners who left, compared with last year.

## What day of the month should I pay owner distributions?

Pay owners on one fixed day, after the rent grace period ends and rent has cleared. Write that day in the management agreement. A fixed day stops "where is my money" calls, because the owner knows the date.

We found no public survey that shows which payout day most companies use. So pick the day from your own cash flow, not from habit. The table compares common choices for a lease with rent due on the 1st.

| Payout schedule | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| 1 run, early (for example the 5th) | Owners get money fast, which suits owners with a mortgage due early | Late rent misses the run; ACH returns can arrive after payout |
| 1 run, mid-month (for example the 10th to 15th) | Most rent has cleared; fewer returns after payout | Owners wait longer |
| 2 runs (early and mid-month) | Fast for on-time rent, catches late rent | Twice the review work and payout fees |
| End of month | One clean close with all bills paid | Owners wait the longest |

Buildium's guide on owner distributions says payouts follow the schedule in the agreement: monthly, quarterly or custom. It also says to reconcile the trust account and close the books before you calculate each owner's net cash. That order matters more than the day.

## Why does ACH clearing time change the payout date?

ACH clearing time changes the date because a rent payment can bounce after it posts. If you pay the owner first, a return leaves a hole in your trust account.

Plaid's guide to ACH return codes gives the windows. A return for insufficient funds (code R01) must come back within 2 banking days of the settlement date. Consumer entries have an extended return window of up to 60 calendar days for unauthorized debits. So a payout right after rent posts has the most risk.

Some states put this in a rule. Oregon rule OAR 863-025-0027(7) says a property manager may not disburse funds from a trust account based on a wire or electronic deposit until the manager verifies the deposit. The same rule, at (3), bars any disbursement unless the owner's ledger has sufficient funds.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I handle late rent before the owner payout?

Pay the owner only what has cleared on the payout date. Late rent goes into the next run. Do not front the money, and never cover one owner's gap with another owner's funds.

Oregon's rule is strict on this point. OAR 863-025-0027(4) lets a manager move funds between different owners' ledgers only with a separate signed agreement from each owner. Texas rule 22 TAC 535.146(b) forbids commingling trust money with the broker's own money. So you cannot pay a late owner from your operating account and refill it later through the trust account.

Use this 6-step run each month:

1. Post all rent and charges received.
2. Wait until payments have cleared for the window you chose.
3. Reconcile the trust bank account to your ledger.
4. Post bills, your management fee and other fees.
5. Hold back the reserve set in each agreement.
6. Review unusual payouts by hand, then send the batch.

Buildium's guide suggests bulk approval for normal payouts and a manual check of odd ones, such as a negative balance or a large amount. It also suggests a minimum payout, such as $50 or $100, so small balances roll to next month.

## ACH, check or another method for owner payouts?

ACH is the default for owner payouts. It costs less than checks and arrives faster. Keep checks and wires for special cases.

| Method | Time to arrive (Buildium) | Cost | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACH | 1 to 2 business days | Low | Regular monthly payouts |
| Check | 5 to 7 business days | Higher: print, postage | Owners without ACH |
| Wire | Same or next day | Highest | Urgent or international payouts |

Get the owner's bank details on a signed form. Confirm any change of bank details by a phone call to the number you already have on file. This step stops a fake "new bank account" email from moving a payout.

## How do reserves change the owner payout?

A reserve lowers the payout because you keep a set amount in the trust account for repairs. The agreement sets the amount. Without a reserve, a $400 repair in week 2 leaves the owner's ledger negative.

Worked example for one property:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Rent cleared | $1,800 |
| Management fee, 9% of $1,800 (example rate) | −$162 |
| Repair paid this month | −$120 |
| Owner balance before reserve | $1,518 |
| Reserve target in agreement | $300 |
| Reserve already held | $150 |
| Top-up to reserve | −$150 |
| Owner payout | $1,368 |

Buildium's guide lists 3 reserve types: maintenance, vacancy and capital. It names about 1 month's rent as a common vacancy reserve. It names a property cushion of about 1 month of typical operating expenses. Treat these as starting points, not rules.

Reserve money in the trust account is still owner money. Show it on every owner statement.

## What do state trust rules say about owner payouts?

State trust rules decide what you may pay and when, and they differ by state. Here are three examples.

| State | Rule | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Oregon | No disbursement without sufficient funds in the owner's ledger. No disbursement on an electronic deposit until verified. Earned management fees leave the trust account at least monthly, unless the agreement sets another schedule. | OAR 863-025-0027(3), (6), (7) |
| Texas | Trust money must be in a trust account by the close of the second working day after receipt. Broker gives each beneficiary an accounting at least monthly when the account had activity. Disburse only as the agreement says. | 22 TAC 535.146(b)(3), (c)(6), (d)(1) |
| Florida | Broker funds stay in escrow until disbursement is properly authorized. A broker may keep up to $5,000 of personal or brokerage funds in the property management escrow account. | Fla. Stat. 475.25(1)(k) |

Your state may set other limits. Read your real estate commission's trust account rules before you set the payout day.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What should the management agreement say about distributions?

The management agreement should name the payout day, the method, the reserve amount and the minimum payout. Each one closes a gap that otherwise becomes a phone call.

Add these 5 lines to your agreement template:

- **Payout day.** "On or about the 12th of each month."
- **Method.** "By ACH to the account on the owner's signed form."
- **Reserve.** "Manager keeps $300 per unit in the trust account."
- **Minimum.** "Balances under $50 roll to the next month."
- **Late rent.** "Rent that clears after the payout day is paid in the next cycle."

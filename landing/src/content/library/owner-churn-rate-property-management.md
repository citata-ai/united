## How do I calculate door churn per year?

Door churn per year is the number of doors that left in 12 months, divided by the doors you managed at the start. The formula fits on one line:

**Annual churn = doors lost in 12 months ÷ doors at the start of the 12 months**

Fourandhalf uses a simple example: you sign 100 owners, and 80 stay at the end of the year. That is 20% churn (Fourandhalf, 2022).

A single yearly division has one weak point. If you grow fast during the year, the start count is too small, and the rate looks too high. Peter Lohmann, who runs RL Property Management in central Ohio, uses a monthly method instead. He divides each month's losses by the count at the start of that month, then adds the 12 monthly rates (Lohmann, issue #74, 2023).

### Worked example

You start January with 400 doors. You lose 5 doors a month and add 9 doors a month.

| Method | Math | Annual churn |
|---|---|---|
| Yearly | 60 lost ÷ 400 at start | 15.0% |
| Monthly sum | 5 ÷ 400, 5 ÷ 404, 5 ÷ 408 ... 5 ÷ 444, added up | about 14.2% |

The monthly sum is lower because the base grows each month. The gap gets bigger when you grow faster or buy a book of doors mid-year.

Churn also gives you average tenure. Lohmann divides 1 by the annual churn rate. At 15% churn, the average door stays about 6.7 years (1 ÷ 0.15). His own 2023 client churn of 15.1% gave him a tenure of about 6.6 years (issue #74).

## What is the NARPM benchmark?

The NARPM benchmark for churn is not one clear number in public sources. The study behind it is the NARPM and ProfitCoach financial performance benchmarking study. We did not find the study text online, so every number below is what someone else reported.

| Number | What it is | Who reported it |
|---|---|---|
| 25% | Annual average churn in the first benchmarking study | Daniel Craig, ProfitCoach, on The Property Management Show (Fourandhalf, 2022) |
| 19.5% | Industry-average unit churn for 2021, NARPM / ProfitCoach | Peter Lohmann, issue #74 (2023) |
| 9.6% | Called "the benchmark", study not named | Peter Lohmann, issue #108 (2024) |
| 17.3% | Average attrition in Australia, 2020 Real Estate Dynamics Rent Roll Growth Report | Daniel Craig (Fourandhalf, 2022) |

What to do: do not quote one of these as "the" industry rate. If you are a NARPM member, ask NARPM for the current study. Compare your number against your own past years first. That trend tells you more than a national average.

Watch for: vendor blogs often round these into a range like "8% to 15%" with no source. We left those out.

## Which churn is not my fault, such as an owner who sells?

Some churn comes from events you cannot control. An owner sells the home. An owner moves back in. An owner dies and the heirs sell. These doors still leave your revenue, so they stay in total churn.

Tag each lost door with one reason when it leaves. Then split churn into two numbers:

- **Uncontrollable churn.** Sale, owner moves in, estate. You can still win some of these back with a referral to a sales agent or a new owner.
- **Controllable churn.** The owner switched managers or started to self-manage. This number shows how well you serve owners.

Property Meld sees a link between the two. Its blog says owners who see weak returns on their year-end reports may sell (Property Meld, "3 main reasons property owners sell"). So a sale is not always a neutral event. High maintenance costs or high tenant turnover can push an owner to sell.

Ray Hespen of Property Meld gave a number for this in 2022. Owners whose maintenance costs went over 12% of collected rent were far more likely to leave their property manager (Fourandhalf, 2022).

## Should I track churn by owner or by unit?

Track both, because they answer different questions. Lohmann put it this way in January 2026. Client churn is the best metric for service quality. Unit churn is best for growth forecasts (issue #173).

| Metric | Formula | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Unit (door) churn | Doors lost ÷ doors at start | Revenue forecast, growth ceiling, valuation |
| Client (owner) churn | Owners lost ÷ owners at start | Service quality, team targets |
| Controllable churn | Doors lost to a switch or self-management ÷ doors at start | Retention work |

Daniel Craig recommends unit churn as the main number. His reason: one client with 200 properties is not one revenue stream, it is 200 (Fourandhalf, 2022). Lohmann prefers client churn for his own company, because he manages many multifamily buildings (issue #74).

## What churn rate is a strong result?

A strong churn rate is under 15% a year, by the scale Daniel Craig gave in 2022:

| Annual churn | Daniel Craig's rating |
|---|---|
| Under 10% | Some companies reach this |
| Under 15% | Great |
| 15% to 20% | Okay |
| Over 20% | Not good |

Real companies move around this scale. Lohmann published his client churn for four years: 18.4% in 2020, 17.9% in 2021, 25.0% in 2022 and 15.1% in 2023 (issue #74). In 2024 he reported 22% annual unit churn and called it better than in years (issue #108). One company can swing 10 points from year to year.

Churn matters more as you grow. Craig gives the example of 1,000 doors at 25% churn: you lose 250 doors a year, about 20 a month (Fourandhalf, 2022). Many companies add 10 to 30 doors a month, he says, so churn can eat most of the new business.

## Checklist: start to measure churn this month

1. **Export** every door that left in the last 12 months from your software, with the date it left.
2. **Tag** each one with a reason: sold, owner moved in, switched manager, self-manage, you ended the agreement.
3. **Calculate** unit churn and client churn with the monthly method.
4. **Split** controllable from uncontrollable churn.
5. **Repeat** each month and plot the 12-month rolling number.
6. **Ask** each leaving owner one short question about why. Fourandhalf notes that owners do not always give the real reason, so ask a follow-up.

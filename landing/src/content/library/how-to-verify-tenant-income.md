## Which proof of income is best: pay stubs, bank statements, offer letter?

The best proof is a document plus a check with the source. A document alone can be edited. A call or a direct data check confirms it.

| Proof | Best for | What it proves | Weak point |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 recent pay stubs | Employees | Pay rate, hours, year-to-date pay | Easy to fake without a check |
| Offer letter | New job | Start date and salary | No pay history yet; confirm with HR |
| W-2 or tax return | Employees and self-employed | Last year's total income | Can be a year old |
| 3–12 months of bank statements | Self-employed, gig, mixed income | Real deposits over time | Deposits are not all income |
| Benefit letter | Social Security, pension, disability | Monthly amount | Confirm the date of the letter |
| Voucher paperwork | Housing Choice Voucher holders | The housing authority's share of rent | Check the tenant share |

Ask every applicant for the same set of documents. List them in your written screening criteria. In Texas, Property Code 92.3515 names "current income" as one ground for denial that your printed criteria may list.

## How do I calculate whether income meets my rule?

Convert all income to a gross monthly amount, then compare it with your ratio. The ratio is your choice. Write it down and use it for everyone.

Worked example, rent $1,500 per month, rule of 3 times rent:

| Step | Math | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Required gross monthly income | $1,500 × 3 | $4,500 |
| Applicant paid every 2 weeks, $2,100 gross per check | $2,100 × 26 ÷ 12 | $4,550 |
| Second adult, $1,200 per month in benefits | Add | $5,750 household |
| Meets rule? | $5,750 vs $4,500 | Yes |

Use these pay-period factors:

- **Weekly:** pay × 52 ÷ 12.
- **Every 2 weeks:** pay × 26 ÷ 12.
- **Twice a month:** pay × 2.
- **Self-employed:** net income on the tax return ÷ 12.

## How do voucher and benefit income change the math?

Voucher and benefit income changes the math in many states. Use the tenant's share of the rent, not the full rent, where the law says so.

California Government Code 12955(o) covers renters with a government rent subsidy. You may not use an income standard "that is not based on the portion of the rent to be paid by the tenant." Example: rent is $1,500 and the voucher pays $1,100. The tenant share is $400. With a 3 times rule, the applicant needs $1,200 per month, not $4,500.

The same section limits credit checks for a subsidized applicant. You must let them offer other proof of ability to pay, such as "government benefit payments, pay records, and bank statements."

New York bans discrimination based on lawful source of income. The Attorney General's guide says this includes Social Security, public assistance and Section 8 vouchers (Executive Law § 296(5)).

HUD's November 4, 2024 guidance on source of income restrictions was withdrawn, according to a Federal Register notice of 2026-07-17. State and local laws like those above still apply.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I verify income for self-employed applicants?

Ask for two years of tax returns and recent bank statements, then compare them. The tax return shows the official net income. The bank statements show whether money still comes in.

Steps:

1. **Get the returns.** The last two years, with the business schedule.
2. **Find net income.** Use net profit, not gross receipts. Divide by 12.
3. **Get bank statements.** 3 to 12 months, all pages.
4. **Average the deposits.** Remove transfers between the applicant's own accounts.
5. **Compare.** If deposits and the return differ a lot, ask why.
6. **Consider a 1099 or client letter** for new contracts.

A falling trend matters as much as the total. A business that earned $60,000 two years ago and $35,000 last year needs a closer look.

## How do I verify employment by phone?

Call the employer at a number you find yourself. Do not use the phone number on the application or the pay stub without a check.

1. **Find the number.** Use the company's website or a public listing.
2. **Ask for HR or payroll.** Many large employers use a third-party verification service instead.
3. **Ask yes/no questions.** "Does [name] work there? Since when? Full-time?"
4. **Ask about pay only if they allow it.** Some employers confirm only dates.
5. **Write it down.** Name, title, date, time and answers.

Signs of a fake: the "employer" answers a mobile phone with no company name. The pay stub has round numbers and no taxes. Or the year-to-date total does not match the pay rate.

## What is automated income verification?

Automated income verification reads payroll or bank data directly, with the applicant's consent. The service returns a summary of income and deposits. It is faster than calls and harder to fake.

Some tenant screening products include it. RentRedi's help page says its TransUnion partnership covers "tenant income and asset verification" along with credit, criminal and eviction checks.

If a screening company gives you the income data and you deny because of it, treat it like any screening report. The FTC says adverse action includes a denial or a co-signer requirement, and you must send the notice.

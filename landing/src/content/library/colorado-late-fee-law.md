## Colorado late fee rules at a glance

Colorado sets late fee rules for every residential landlord in C.R.S. 38-12-105. The same section covers mobile home owners in a park. We read the statute text, taken from the official 2024 Colorado Revised Statutes, on 2026-10-19. We found no 2025 or 2026 act that changed it.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Grace period | Rent must be at least 7 calendar days late | C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(a) |
| Maximum fee | Greater of $50 or 5% of the past-due rent | C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(b) |
| Lease disclosure | Fee must be in the rental agreement | C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(c) |
| Eviction for late fees | Not allowed | C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(d), (e) |
| Subsidy portion | No fee on the part a rent subsidy provider pays | C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(f) |
| More than 1 fee | Allowed only if the total stays under the cap | C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(g) |
| Interest on a late fee | Not allowed | C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(h) |
| Take fee from rent payment | Not allowed | C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(i) |
| Written notice of the fee | Within 180 days after the rent was due | C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(j) |
| Penalty | $50 per violation; $150 to $1,000 if not cured in 7 days | C.R.S. 38-12-105(3) to (5) |

## What is the maximum late fee a landlord can charge in Colorado?

The maximum Colorado late fee is the greater of $50 or 5% of the amount of the past-due rent payment (C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(b)). The cap is a total for each late payment, not a cap per day.

The break-even point is $1,000 of past-due rent. At that amount, 5% equals $50.

| Past-due rent | 5% of past-due rent | Legal maximum fee |
|---|---|---|
| $800 | $40 | $50 |
| $1,000 | $50 | $50 |
| $1,600 | $80 | $80 |
| $2,400 | $120 | $120 |
| $3,500 | $175 | $175 |

Note the base: 5% of the past-due payment, not 5% of the monthly rent. If a tenant pays $1,800 of a $2,400 rent and owes $600, the past-due amount is $600. Then 5% is $30, so the $50 floor applies.

## Does Colorado law require a grace period before a late fee?

Yes. A Colorado landlord may not charge a late fee unless the rent payment is late by at least 7 calendar days (C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(a)). Calendar days include weekends and holidays.

Worked example: rent is due on the 1st. On the 2nd through the 7th, the payment is late by 1 to 6 days. The payment is late by 7 calendar days on the 8th. So the earliest fee date is the 8th.

The landlord must also give the tenant written notice of the late fee within 180 days after the date the rent was due (C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(j)). A fee you forget to bill for 6 months is lost.

## Must the late fee be written in the lease in Colorado?

Yes. A Colorado landlord may not require a tenant to pay a late fee unless the rental agreement discloses the fee (C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(c)). A lease term that breaks any rule in subsection (1) is void and cannot be enforced (C.R.S. 38-12-105(2)).

Lease clause checklist:

- [ ] The fee starts no earlier than 7 calendar days after the due date.
- [ ] The fee amount is the greater of $50 or 5% of the past-due rent, or less.
- [ ] Any repeat or daily fee stops at that same total.
- [ ] No interest on late fees.
- [ ] No fee on the part of rent a subsidy provider pays.
- [ ] Rent payments go to rent first, not to late fees.
- [ ] The lease does not call a late fee "rent".

## Can a Colorado landlord charge a daily late fee?

A Colorado landlord may charge more than 1 late fee for the same late payment only if the total stays at or below the cap (C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(g)). So a daily fee is legal only until it reaches the greater of $50 or 5% of the past-due rent. After that, it must stop.

Worked example: rent is $2,000 and the tenant pays nothing. The cap is 5% × $2,000 = $100. A $10 daily fee from day 8 reaches $100 after 10 days. Every day after that adds $0.

Three related bans also apply:

1. **No interest on a late fee.** The landlord may not require any interest on a late fee (C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(h)).
2. **No recoup from rent.** The landlord may not take any part of a late fee out of a rent payment (C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(i)). A $2,000 payment counts as $2,000 of rent.
3. **No fee on subsidy money.** The landlord may not charge a late fee for the part of the rent that a rent subsidy provider, not the tenant, must pay (C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(f)).

## Can a Colorado landlord evict a tenant for unpaid late fees?

No. A Colorado landlord may not remove or exclude a tenant, or start a court case to remove the tenant, because the tenant did not pay late fees (C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(d)). The landlord also may not end a tenancy at will or a mobile home park lease for that reason (C.R.S. 38-12-105(1)(e)).

A late fee is distinct from rent. A rental agreement may not call a late fee rent for the 10-day nonpayment demand under C.R.S. 13-40-104(1)(d) (C.R.S. 38-12-105(7)). So your 10-day demand for unpaid rent must leave late fees out.

Repeated late rent can still matter at renewal. Under HB24-1098, where the for-cause rules apply (most tenants of 12 months or more), a landlord may start a no-fault eviction at the end of the lease term if the tenant paid rent late more than 2 times during the lease. A payment counts as late only if it came more than 10 calendar days after the due date and the landlord served a proper 10-day notice. It does not count if the tenant paid within the cure period. The landlord must give at least 90 days' written notice (C.R.S. 38-12-1303(3)(f)).

## What are the penalties for an illegal late fee in Colorado?

A Colorado landlord who breaks C.R.S. 38-12-105(1) owes the tenant $50 for each violation (C.R.S. 38-12-105(3)). The landlord then has 7 days to cure, from the day the landlord gets written or electronic notice of the violation (C.R.S. 38-12-105(4)).

If the landlord does not cure in time, the tenant may sue for (C.R.S. 38-12-105(5)):

- **Compensatory damages** for injury or loss.
- **A penalty** of at least $150 and up to $1,000 for each violation.
- **Costs,** including reasonable attorney fees to the prevailing party.
- **Other equitable relief** the court finds fit.

The tenant may also raise a late fee violation as an affirmative defense in an eviction case (C.R.S. 38-12-105(6)).

A property management company sits in the middle of the money. Rent comes in from tenants, goes out to owners, and goes out again to vendors. The IRS sees you as the payer for much of that flow. So each January, you file 1099 forms for people who are not your employees.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current IRS instructions or ask a tax professional.

## Which 1099 form does a property manager file for owners and vendors?

A property manager files Form 1099-MISC for owners and Form 1099-NEC for most service vendors. The IRS instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC (revision 12/2026) set the rules.

| Who you paid | What you paid | Form and box | Threshold (2026 payments) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Property owner (individual, partnership, LLC not taxed as a corporation) | Rent you collected and paid over | 1099-MISC, box 1 (Rents) | $2,000 |
| Plumber, electrician, cleaner, handyman (not a corporation) | Labor and services | 1099-NEC, box 1a | $2,000 |
| Attorney or law firm (even a corporation) | Legal fees | 1099-NEC, box 1a | $2,000 |
| Attorney | Gross proceeds, for example a settlement paid through the attorney | 1099-MISC, box 10 | $600 |
| Corporation (C or S, or LLC taxed as one) | Services | Generally none | Not required |
| Supply store, utility, phone company | Merchandise, utilities, telephone | None | Not required |

The owner line is the one that surprises new managers. A tenant does not have to send a 1099 for rent paid to a property manager. Instead, the IRS says the property manager "must use Form 1099-MISC to report the rent paid over to the property owner". The instructions point to Regulations section 1.6041-3(d) and 1.6041-1(e)(5), Example 5.

## Which amount goes on the owner's 1099-MISC?

The owner's 1099-MISC reports the rent you paid over for that owner in the calendar year. Software can show this as gross rent collected or as the net draw after your fee and repairs. Ask your tax professional which figure fits your setup.

Check how your software defines the box 1 figure before you file. Then tell owners in your January letter which number they will see. A clear note stops a lot of "my 1099 is wrong" emails.

## What changed for 2026: the $2,000 threshold

The IRS threshold for rents and for nonemployee compensation went up to $2,000. The instructions say the change applies "for tax years beginning after 2025". The IRS also says the amount may be adjusted for inflation from calendar year 2027.

Buildium's 1099 guide puts the change plainly: the old $600 minimum rises to $2,000 for the 2026 tax year. So:

- **Forms you file in early 2026** cover 2025 payments and use the old $600 rule.
- **Forms you file in early 2027** cover 2026 payments and use $2,000.

A vendor you paid $1,500 in 2026 for a few repairs no longer needs a 1099-NEC from you. A vendor you paid $1,500 in 2025 still did.

Two numbers did not move. Attorney gross proceeds in 1099-MISC box 10 stay at $600. Royalties stay at $10.

## What is the 1099 deadline?

The 1099-NEC deadline is January 31, and it applies to both the IRS copy and the vendor copy. The IRS instructions cite section 6071(c) for this date. There is no later date for e-filing.

| Form | Copy to recipient | File with IRS on paper | File with IRS electronically |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1099-NEC | January 31 | January 31 | January 31 |
| 1099-MISC | January 31 | February 28 | March 31 |

If a date falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, the next business day counts.

You must e-file if you file 10 or more information returns in total. The IRS counts all types together: 1099-MISC, 1099-NEC, W-2 and others. This rule applies to returns due on or after January 1, 2024. Most firms with 10 or more owners pass that line.

## Which W-9 forms do I need first?

You need a Form W-9 from every owner and every vendor before you pay them. The IRS says people use Form W-9 to give their correct taxpayer identification number (TIN) to the person who must file an information return.

The W-9 tells you three things:

1. **The legal name** that must match the TIN.
2. **The TIN**: a Social Security number or an EIN.
3. **The tax class**: individual, partnership, C corporation, S corporation, or LLC and how it is taxed. This decides if a 1099 is needed at all.

Buildium's guide gives the same advice: ask for a W-9 each time you bring on a new owner or law firm.

Make the W-9 part of onboarding, not a January chase.

- **Owners:** ask for the W-9 with the management agreement. No W-9, no first draw.
- **Vendors:** ask for the W-9 and the insurance certificate in the same email. No W-9, no first work order.
- **Attorneys:** ask even if the firm is a corporation. Legal fees are reportable to corporations too.

If an attorney does not give a TIN, the IRS instructions say you must backup withhold on the reportable payments. Ask your tax professional how backup withholding applies to other payees.

## How does property management software file 1099s?

Property management software files 1099s from the payments you already recorded. The steps are about the same in each system.

1. **Mark each payee as 1099-eligible** and pick the form: MISC for owners, NEC for vendors.
2. **Enter the TIN and legal name** from the W-9.
3. **Run the 1099 preview report** in early January.
4. **Check each total** against owner statements and vendor bills.
5. **Fix names and TINs** that do not match.
6. **E-file the batch** and send recipient copies, by mail or the owner portal.
7. **Keep copies.**

## A January 1099 checklist

Use this list each year in the first week of January.

- [ ] Every owner and vendor paid last year has a W-9 on file.
- [ ] Each vendor's tax class is set: corporation (no 1099) or not.
- [ ] Attorneys are flagged as 1099-NEC payees even if they are corporations.
- [ ] Owner totals in the 1099 report match the year-end owner statements.
- [ ] Owners who sold or left during the year still get a 1099 for their months with you.
- [ ] You use the right threshold for the tax year ($600 for 2025 payments, $2,000 for 2026 payments).
- [ ] 1099-NEC batch filed and sent by January 31.
- [ ] 1099-MISC copies sent to owners by January 31.
- [ ] 1099-MISC filed with the IRS by March 31 (e-file).

## How do I explain the 1099 to owners?

Send each owner one short note with the 1099. Say which number is in box 1, what it includes, and where to find your fees and repairs. Point them to the year-end owner statement. Ask them to send it to their tax preparer.

A manager with 120 doors has $2,000 a month for marketing. One agency pitches SEO. Another pitches Google Ads. Both show a chart that goes up. The real question is simple: which one gives a signed management agreement for less money over 2 years?

This page compares the two on speed, cost and fit, with public benchmarks and one worked calculation you can redo with your numbers.

## Which brings leads faster, SEO or Google Ads?

Google Ads brings leads faster. An ad can show as soon as the campaign runs and Google approves it. You pay only when someone clicks, as Google's Ads help page explains.

SEO (search engine optimization: work that helps your pages rank in the free results) is slower. Fourandhalf's guide on SEO and SEM says SEO takes longer to establish but its results are cumulative. New city pages, reviews and links take months to move rankings.

| Factor | SEO | Google Ads |
|---|---|---|
| Time to first lead | Months | Days |
| You pay for | Content, fixes, links, reviews (fixed monthly) | Each click (variable) |
| When you stop paying | Rankings fade slowly | Leads stop the same day |
| Position on the page | Below ads, in organic and Map Pack | Top or bottom, with an "Ad" label |
| Main risk | Slow start, no guarantee | Cost per click rises with competition |
| Best for | A base source over 2+ years | Fast tests, new markets, slow months |

## What do the benchmarks say about cost and conversion?

The public benchmarks favor organic search for property management sites, but the paid sample is small.

WordStream's Google Ads Benchmarks 2026 used 13,474 US search campaigns from April 1, 2025, to March 31, 2026. For the real estate category, it reports these medians:

| Metric (real estate, WordStream 2026) | Value |
|---|---|
| Click-through rate | 7.61% |
| Cost per click | $3.22 |
| Conversion rate | 3.70% |
| Cost per lead | $102.51 |

WordStream also found real estate had the biggest cost-per-click rise of any industry, up 27.27% year over year. "Real estate" mixes home sales, rentals and agents, so owner-lead keywords for property management may differ.

The property management data comes from Goodjuju's lead source report, fall 2026 update. It tracked 35 property management companies and 2,525 leads with GA4.

| Channel (Goodjuju, fall 2026) | Share of traffic | Share of leads | Conversion rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search and Map Pack | 37.9% | 50.6% | 1.2% |
| Paid search | 5.3% | 1.2% | 0.2% |

Read this with care. Only 6 of the 35 companies ran paid search in the fall window. Goodjuju counted only form leads on each main site, and says its paid figures are not a benchmark. Ads that send people to a call or a landing page may look better in other data.

## Which costs less per signed door over 2 years?

The cheaper channel over 2 years depends on two numbers: your ads cost per lead and your close rate. SEO cost stays flat while leads grow. Ads cost the same per lead in month 24 as in month 1, or more.

Here is a worked example. The SEO price is ClearLead's published Foundation plan. The ads cost per lead is the WordStream real estate median. Lead counts and the close rate are made up for the example. Replace them with your data.

**Assumptions:** close rate 20% of leads to signed agreements, 1.5 doors per new owner.

| | SEO | Google Ads |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly spend | $1,199 (ClearLead Foundation) | $1,199 (ad spend, no management fee) |
| Leads per month, months 1–6 | 1 (example) | $1,199 / $102.51 = about 11.7 |
| Leads per month, months 7–24 | 6 (example) | 11.7 |
| Total leads in 24 months | 6 + 108 = 114 | about 281 |
| Total cost in 24 months | $28,776 | $28,776 |
| Signed owners (20%) | about 23 | about 56 |
| Signed doors (x 1.5) | about 34 | about 84 |
| Cost per signed door | about $841 | about $343 |

At the WordStream median, ads win in this example. Now change one input. If your real owner-lead cost per lead is $300, not $102.51, ads give about 96 leads, 19 owners, about 29 doors, and about $1,000 per door. SEO then wins.

So the answer depends on your cost per lead and your close rate for each channel. Measure both before you decide. Our [fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) helps you put a value on each new door.

## Do paid ads help organic rankings?

No. Google's Ads help page says: "your payment will have no impact on the organic search results that person's search produces." Ads also "will never appear within the organic search results themselves."

Ads can still help SEO in indirect ways:

- **Keyword data.** The search terms report shows which owner phrases bring calls. Write pages for those phrases.
- **Page tests.** You can test two headlines on a landing page in 2 weeks, not 6 months.
- **Brand searches.** People who see an ad may search your name later. That is a hypothesis, and hard to measure.

## How do I split budget between them?

Fund the base first, then test ads with a fixed budget and a clear stop rule. Use this order:

1. **Google Business Profile and reviews.** Goodjuju's data shows Google Search and Map Pack brought about half of all leads.
2. **Core owner pages.** A fee page, one page per real service city, and a rental analysis form.
3. **A 90-day ads test.** Fix the monthly ad spend. Bid on owner terms in your top city only.
4. **Track to the signed door.** Tag every lead with its source in your CRM. Count agreements, not form fills.
5. **Decide on the number.** After 90 days, compare cost per signed door for each channel. Move money to the lower one.

| Situation | Suggested lean |
|---|---|
| New company, no rankings, needs owners now | More ads, start SEO in parallel |
| Ranks in the Map Pack for your main city | More SEO, small ads test |
| Entering a new city | Ads first in that city, then a city page |
| Ad cost per lead above your door value | Pause ads, fund SEO |

Organic search also feeds AI answers, which take a share of owner research. See [why landlord leads from Google are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping).

## What does Google say about AI content?

Google says it rewards original, helpful content however it is made, and it treats mass pages without value as spam. Its February 8, 2023 post states: "Appropriate use of AI or automation is not against our guidelines." The same post says the target is content made "primarily to manipulate search rankings."

Google's current help page on generative AI content adds three points (Google Search Central):

| Google point | What it says | What it means for a PM blog |
|---|---|---|
| AI for research and structure | AI "can be particularly useful when researching a topic, and to add structure to original content" | Use AI for the outline and first draft |
| Scaled content abuse | Making "many pages without adding value for users" may break the spam policy | Do not mass-produce city pages or thin posts |
| Accuracy | It is "critical to manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content" | A person checks every fee, law and number |
| Disclosure | "Consider adding information on how your content was created" | A short note or a real author byline |

Google's spam policy defines scaled content abuse as many pages made to manipulate rankings and not to help users, "no matter how it's created." So the risk is not the tool. The risk is volume without value.

## Which parts of a post should a person write?

A person should write every part an owner could act on: fees, law, local facts, examples and advice. AI can draft the parts that are the same in every city.

| Part of the post | Who writes it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Outline and H2 questions | AI drafts, person edits | AI is fast at structure |
| Plain definitions (what a rent roll is) | AI drafts, person checks | General knowledge, low risk |
| Your fees and contract terms | Person | Only you know them; a wrong fee is a false claim |
| State or city law, deadlines | Person, from the statute | Models predict words and can invent sections |
| Local rents, vacancy, market data | Person, from a named source | AI does not know this month's numbers |
| Your examples and stories | Person | This is the experience Google's E-E-A-T asks for |
| Opinion and advice | Person | Owners hire you for your judgment |
| FAQ | AI drafts, person rewrites answers | Answers must match the body facts |
| Title and meta description | AI drafts, person checks | Google's review rule covers metadata too |

E-E-A-T stands for experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trustworthiness. Google's 2023 post says its ranking systems aim to reward content that shows these qualities.

## How do I add local facts AI does not know?

Give the AI your facts before it writes, then check that it used them. A chat model does not know your county's eviction backlog, your average days to lease, or this year's Fair Market Rent.

Collect these facts in one note before you prompt:

- **Your numbers.** Average days to lease, renewal rate, doors managed, years in business. Use your own software reports.
- **Local rents.** A named source, such as HUD Fair Market Rents. Our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) lists FY2027 numbers by metro.
- **Local law.** The statute or city ordinance text, with the section number.
- **Local places.** Neighborhoods, ZIP codes and building types you manage.
- **One real story.** A turn, a repair, an owner question from last month (no tenant names).

Then use a prompt like this: "Write a draft for owners in [city]. Use only the facts in the note below. If a fact is missing, write [FACT NEEDED]. Do not add numbers that are not in the note."

The [FACT NEEDED] tag matters. It turns a silent guess into a visible gap that a person must fill.

## How do I check AI drafts for errors?

Check each draft against a fixed list, and check facts by opening the source. Google notes that generative AI output "may contain inaccuracies (also known as hallucinations)."

We learned this on our own name. In our October 2026 test of 20 prompts across ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude, the engines confused citata.ai with Citate.ai and other firms (our data, 2026-10-18). If AI mixes up a company name, it can mix up a deposit deadline.

Use this 12-point check on every draft:

1. Every number has a source a person opened today.
2. Every law has a statute section, checked in the official text.
3. Fees match your current fee schedule.
4. No promise of rent, vacancy time or results.
5. No words that steer renters by a protected class.
6. City and state names are right in every paragraph.
7. No made-up client, review or quote.
8. No [FACT NEEDED] tags left.
9. Title and meta description match the body.
10. One clear next step for the owner.
11. A real author name and role on the post.
12. The post says something a generic page does not: a number, a local rule or a story.

## How much AI content is safe each month?

There is no safe number of posts. Google's rule is about value, not count. One checked post a week that answers a real owner question beats 30 unchecked posts.

Here is a worked time budget for one 1,200-word post:

| Step | Who | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Collect facts note | Person | 30 min |
| AI outline and draft | AI + person | 15 min |
| Write fees, law, story | Person | 30 min |
| 12-point check | Person | 45 min |
| Total | | 2 hours |

The times are our estimate, not a study. The point holds: AI saves drafting time. It does not remove the check. If you cannot spend about 2 hours a post, publish fewer posts.

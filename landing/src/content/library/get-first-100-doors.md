## Where do the first 100 doors come from?

The first 100 doors of a property management company mostly come from people who already know you. Strangers find you later, once you have reviews and a website that answers their questions.

Common early sources, in the order most new companies can use them:

| Source | First step this week | What makes it work |
|---|---|---|
| Your own rentals and family | Put them under a signed agreement | Real case history to show owners |
| Friends and past clients who own rentals | Send a short personal email | A clear offer and fee |
| Realtors | Meet 5 local agents | Send their rental clients back to them for sales |
| Real estate investor meetups | Attend 2 a month | Show up often, teach, do not pitch |
| Contractors and vendors | Ask them who owns many rentals | Pay them fast, so they want to help |
| Your website and Google Business Profile | Publish services, area and fees | Reviews and clear answers |
| Lead services | Test one with a small budget | Reply within 15 minutes |

Buildium's first-100-doors guide covers the same ground: realtor, contractor and client referrals, a website with testimonials, local ads and local events. It adds one rule worth a poster on your wall: aim to follow up with a new lead within 15 minutes.

More owners now ask AI tools to suggest a manager. Our [AI search checklist for property management websites](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website) shows what to put on your site so those tools can read it.

## Should I buy a small book of business?

Buying a small book of business can add dozens of doors in one step. It needs cash, and it needs time to onboard owners who did not choose you.

Small books are for sale often. In one January 2026 issue, Peter Lohmann's newsletter listed a 36-door property management company in the Sacramento and Bay Area with an asking price of $225,000. An asking price is not a sale price, but it shows the scale of cost.

Before you buy, check at least:

- [ ] The trust account balances match tenant deposits and owner reserves.
- [ ] Each owner agreement allows transfer to a new manager.
- [ ] How many owners left in the past 2 years, and why.
- [ ] The seller holds the license your state requires.
- [ ] The seller stays on for a handover period and helps you meet each owner.

If 20 of 36 doors stay after a year, you paid for 20 doors, not 36. Plan the price and the payment terms around the doors that stay.

## Which owners should I take or decline?

Take owners who fit your service area, property type and standards. Decline the rest, even when you need doors. An early bad fit uses the hours you need to win good owners.

Buildium's growth guide quotes Realty Solutions on this point: the company prioritized "the right clients" over new doors and shed clients who were not a match. Its advice is to vet each owner before you sign and review your client list once a quarter.

Owner fit checklist:

- [ ] The property is inside your service area.
- [ ] You manage this property type (single-family, small multifamily, condo).
- [ ] The owner agrees to fix safety and habitability issues.
- [ ] The owner accepts your fee without a special deal.
- [ ] The owner accepts your screening criteria and Fair Housing rules.
- [ ] The rent is high enough for your fee to cover the work.

Score each yes as 1. Take owners at 6 of 6. Think hard at 5. Decline at 4 or less.

The rent test matters most for profit. A percent fee on a low rent can pay less than the work costs. Check with the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) before you sign.

## How long does 100 doors take?

The time to 100 doors is 100 divided by your net new doors per month. Net means doors won minus doors lost. No public study we could verify gives a typical time, so use your own pace.

| Net new doors per month | Months to 100 doors | Years |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 50 | about 4.2 |
| 4 | 25 | about 2.1 |
| 6 | 17 | about 1.4 |
| 8 | 13 | about 1.1 |

Worked example: you win 5 doors a month and lose 1. Net growth is 4 doors a month. You reach 100 doors in about 25 months. If you buy a 30-door book in month 6 and keep 24 of them, you reach 100 doors in about 19 months.

The math for the second case: 6 months × 4 = 24 doors, plus 24 bought = 48. The other 52 doors take 13 more months at 4 a month. That gives 19 months in total.

## How should I set up the company to reach 100 doors?

Set up the company so that door 100 runs the same way as door 10. Buildium's guide lists the roles a company needs on the way to 100 doors: property manager, leasing agent, maintenance coordinator, bookkeeper and business development. At first you do most of these yourself.

Steps:

1. **Define your offer.** Write your service area, property types and fee in one paragraph.
2. **Write your processes.** Owner onboarding, maintenance, screening and owner reports, each as a checklist.
3. **Track sources.** Record where every new owner came from.
4. **Ask for reviews.** After each good result, ask the owner for a Google review.
5. **Track churn.** Record the reason each time an owner leaves.

The checklists matter when you hire. The person who takes your admin work at door 60 learns from them.

## What to do this week

Email 10 people you know who own rentals. Tell them what you manage, where, and for what fee. Then time your replies to every new lead for one month, and get the average under 15 minutes.

## What is the MTW demonstration?

The Moving to Work (MTW) demonstration is a HUD program that lets selected housing authorities (PHAs) run public housing and vouchers with fewer federal rules. Congress created it in 1996. The 2016 expansion law "authorized HUD to expand the MTW demonstration program from 39 PHAs by an additional 100 PHAs over a period of seven years" (Federal Register, 2026-09-01).

The program has 3 goals set by statute, per the same notice:

1. **Cost.** Reduce cost and get more value from federal spending.
2. **Work.** Give incentives to families where the head of household works, seeks work or trains for work.
3. **Choice.** Increase housing choices for low-income families.

To meet those goals, MTW agencies "are given exemptions from some existing public housing and HCV rules". An MTW program covers the agency's tenant-based vouchers, project-based vouchers and public housing, unless the MTW Operations Notice says otherwise.

What this means for you: 2 PHAs in the same metro can follow different voucher rules. If one is an MTW agency, do not assume the standard 24 CFR part 982 rules apply in full.

## Which rules can MTW agencies change?

MTW agencies can change many rules that touch landlords. HUD's MTW Operations Notice has an Appendix I of standard waivers. An agency may use any of them "without further HUD approval" if it lists the activity in its plan and stays within the safe harbors (limits) HUD sets.

Here are the waivers from the 2026 list that matter most to owners:

| Waiver group | Waivers that touch landlords | What can change for you |
|---|---|---|
| Payment standards and rent reasonableness | Payment standards based on Small Area FMRs or FMRs; rent reasonableness process; third-party requirement | The subsidy ceiling and how the PHA tests your rent |
| Reexaminations | Alternative reexamination schedule (HCV) | How often the tenant's share and your HAP change |
| Landlord leasing incentives | Vacancy loss; damage claims; other landlord incentives | Extra payments to you |
| Housing quality standards | Pre-qualifying unit inspections; reasonable penalty payments for landlords; third-party requirement; alternative inspection schedules | When and how the PHA inspects and enforces |
| Rent and income | Tenant payment as a modified percentage of income; fixed subsidy; alternative utility allowance; initial rent burden | The tenant's share and the split |
| Term limits | Short-term and term-limited assistance (HCV) | How long the tenant keeps help |
| Project-based vouchers | Higher PBV program and project caps; longer HAP contract; higher rent to owner | PBV deal terms |
| Moving On | Waive initial HQS inspection requirement (HCV) | Lease-up for families moving from supportive housing |

Source: Appendix I table of contents, Federal Register notice of 2026-09-01. The detailed safe harbors for each waiver are in the notice's appendix.

## Do MTW agencies pay incentives?

Many MTW agencies pay incentives, and the waiver list makes it easy. "Vacancy Loss", "Damage Claims" and "Other Landlord Incentives" for tenant-based vouchers are all standard MTW waivers.

The best data on use comes from a 2021 HUD Office of Inspector General (OIG) review of MTW agencies (Memorandum 2021-LA-0803), as summarized by NLIHC:

| Finding | Number |
|---|---|
| MTW agencies that answered the survey | 34 of 39 |
| Used some landlord incentive, 2016–2019 | 28 of 34 (82%) |
| Of those, used non-cash incentives | 27 (96%) |
| Of those, used money incentives | 20 (71%) |
| Said incentives helped keep landlords in the program | 82% |
| Said incentives were less effective at getting landlords to add units | 61% |

The OIG found the most effective non-cash incentives were direct deposit and landlord portals. The most effective money incentives were new landlord bonuses, damage reimbursements and vacancy loss or re-rent bonuses.

## Can inspection schedules differ?

Yes. "Alternative Inspection Schedules (HCV)" is a standard MTW waiver. Under the regular rule, 24 CFR 982.405(b), a PHA must inspect each voucher unit "at least biennially", or every 3 years at a small rural PHA. An MTW agency may adopt a different schedule within HUD's safe harbors.

The same waiver group lists 3 more inspection waivers: "Pre-Qualifying Unit Inspections", "Reasonable Penalty Payments for Landlords" and "Third-Party Requirement". The detailed terms sit in the notice's appendix and in each agency's plan. Ask your PHA how it uses each one, for example whether it will inspect a vacant unit before a tenant applies.

Watch for: an alternative schedule can mean fewer visits, but repair duties stay. Keep your own yearly walk-through, even if the PHA inspects less often.

## How do I read my PHA's MTW plan?

Read your PHA's MTW plan for the activities it adopted, then match each to the landlord topics above. Expansion agencies list their activities in an "MTW Plan-Expansion". The 2026 notice changed that name from the "MTW Supplement". The original 39 agencies file an Annual MTW Plan and Annual MTW Report.

Steps:

1. **Find the plan.** Search the PHA site for "Moving to Work", "MTW Plan" or "Agency Plan". Use the latest fiscal year.
2. **Search the plan** for these words: "landlord", "vacancy", "damage", "inspection", "payment standard", "rent reasonableness", "reexamination".
3. **List each activity** with its start date and status: planned, in use, on hold or closed.
4. **Note the limits.** Incentive amounts, how many days of vacancy loss, inspection cycle years.
5. **Check the Administrative Plan too.** It holds the day-to-day procedures and forms.
6. **Ask the landlord liaison** to confirm any activity before you rely on it.

A short notes table helps:

| Activity in plan | Status | What it means for my units |
|---|---|---|
| Vacancy loss | In use | Up to one month; claim form X |
| Triennial inspections | In use | Next inspection 3 years from last pass |
| Payment standard at 120% FMR | Planned | Possible higher rent cap next year |

These rows are an example, not a real PHA's plan. To see how a payment standard change would move the HAP and tenant split, use our [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator), and compare local numbers in our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits).

## What leasing and renewal fees do Atlanta managers charge?

Atlanta leasing fees run from half a month's rent to a full month. The median in our 10 pages is about 72.5%, or $1,277.45 at $1,762.

- **50% of one month:** Evernest ($500 minimum) and Mynd.
- **60% to 75%:** Specialized PM Atlanta (60%), Excalibur and PMI Beltline (70%, PMI with a $1,200 minimum), Keyrenter Atlanta and Northpoint (75%).
- **One full month:** Vineyard and Bay Property Management Group Atlanta.
- **Flat:** Ziprent at $1,500.

Renewal fees are higher in Atlanta than in our Dallas count. The median is $360. Percent renewals at 25% come to $440.50 at a $1,762 rent. PMI Beltline's 15% comes to $264.30.

Watch for the extras. Bay PMG Atlanta lists a $99 start-up fee. Vineyard charges $125 per optional inspection and $500 for eviction filing. Northpoint adds 10% to maintenance invoices, while PMI Beltline and Excalibur say they charge no maintenance markup.

## Which local rules add cost for Atlanta owners?

Georgia rules shape Atlanta costs more than city rules. Three to know:

1. **Licensed manager.** O.C.G.A. 43-40-1 says a broker includes a person who "collects rents" or "performs property management services" for a fee. Hiring a licensed firm is a legal need, not an extra.
2. **Trust accounts.** The Georgia Real Estate Commission FAQ says a broker's trust account for security deposits must be designated as a trust account and registered with the Commission (License Law 43-40-20, Rule 520-1-.08).
3. **Deposit deadline.** O.C.G.A. 44-7-34 requires the landlord to return the deposit within 30 days after getting possession back, with a written statement of reasons for any amount kept.

We did not find an official City of Atlanta rental registration fee to add. If your property sits in another metro city, ask the manager about local permits.

## Must an Atlanta property manager hold a GA license?

Yes. O.C.G.A. 43-40-1 lists 10 broker acts. Two of them describe what a property manager does: "collects rents, assessments, or other trust funds" and "performs property management services or community association management services." A person who does these for another, for a fee, is a broker under Georgia law.

How to check:

- **Search the license** on the Georgia Real Estate Commission's "Verify a License" tool.
- **Check the firm and its qualifying broker**, not only the agent you meet.
- **Ask about the trust account** for rents and deposits, and confirm it is registered with the Commission.
- **Look at disciplinary actions**, which the Commission publishes on its site.

## Flat fee or percent: which model is common in Atlanta?

Percent pricing is more common in Atlanta. Of our 10 priced managers, 7 charge a percent of rent and 3 charge a flat monthly fee (Specialized PM Atlanta, Ziprent and Mynd).

| Rent | 8% fee | 10% fee | $125 flat | $150 flat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1,400 | $112 | $140 | $125 | $150 |
| $1,762 | $140.96 | $176.20 | $125 | $150 |
| $2,500 | $200 | $250 | $125 | $150 |
| $3,000 | $240 | $300 | $125 | $150 |

At 8%, a $150 flat fee is cheaper once rent passes $1,875. At 10%, it is cheaper above $1,500. Some percent firms add floors or tiers for low rents: Keyrenter Atlanta charges 10% under $2,000, and Vineyard charges a flat $125 under $1,400.

Compare the whole year. A flat-fee firm with a $1,500 placement fee can cost more in year one than a 9% firm with a 50% leasing fee.

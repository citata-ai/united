Idaho keeps its deposit rules short. The core is one section, Idaho Code § 6-321, in the unlawful detainer chapter. I read it and the sections it connects to on the Idaho Legislature site on 2026-10-20. The rule that trips most managers is the 21-day default: 30 days applies only when the lease sets it.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | No cap in the statute | § 6-321 |
| Local deposit rules | Barred since 2025 | § 55-306 |
| Deduction for normal wear and tear | Not allowed | § 6-321(1) |
| Refund, no time in the lease | Within 21 days after surrender | § 6-321(2) |
| Refund, time set in the lease | As agreed, but within 30 days at most | § 6-321(2) |
| Partial refund | Signed, itemized statement with a list of expenditures | § 6-321(2) |
| Separate account | Required only for third-party managers | § 6-321(4) |
| Sale of the property | New owner is liable for the refund | § 6-321(3) |
| Tenant's demand before suing | 3 days' written notice | § 6-320(d) |
| Damages in court | Up to 3 times actual damages | § 6-317 |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Idaho?

Idaho sets no maximum security deposit. Section 6-321 defines a deposit as any amount the tenant pays "for any purpose other than the payment of rent". It says nothing about the amount.

Local governments cannot add a cap. Idaho Code § 55-306, added in 2025, says a local government unit may not enact or enforce a rule that would regulate the "rent, fees, or deposits charged for leasing private residential property".

One related rule applies to fees, not deposits. Under Idaho Code § 55-305, fees charged to a residential tenant must be reasonable and must appear in the rental agreement. That section applies to agreements made or renewed on or after July 1, 2023.

## How many days does an Idaho landlord have to return a security deposit?

An Idaho landlord has 21 days to return a security deposit if the lease sets no time. If the lease sets a time, the landlord follows the lease, but the refund is due within 30 days after surrender in every case (§ 6-321(2)).

The clock starts at "surrender of the premises by the tenant", not at the lease end date. Here is how the 2 cases compare for a tenant who hands back the keys on June 10:

| Lease says | Refund due by | Days |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing about refund time | July 1 | 21 |
| "Within 30 days" | July 10 | 30 |
| "Within 45 days" | July 10 (the 30-day limit wins) | 30 |

Write the refund period into your lease if you want the full 30 days. Without it, you have 21.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Idaho?

An Idaho landlord can deduct the amounts needed to cover "the contingencies specified in the deposit arrangement" (§ 6-321(1)). So the lease or deposit agreement decides what the deposit covers. Common items are unpaid rent, cleaning and damage, if the agreement lists them.

The landlord may not keep any part of the deposit for normal wear and tear. The statute defines it as deterioration from the intended use of the unit, without negligence, carelessness, accident, misuse or abuse by the tenant, household members, invitees or guests.

Practical rule: list the deposit contingencies in the lease in plain words. A deduction for an item the agreement does not name is hard to defend.

## Does an Idaho landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

An Idaho landlord must give an itemized statement with any refund that is less than the full deposit. Section 6-321(2) requires a signed statement that shows 3 things:

1. **The amounts kept.** Each amount the landlord lawfully retained.
2. **The purpose.** Why the landlord kept each amount.
3. **The expenditures.** A detailed list of what the landlord spent from the deposit.

Example statement for a $1,500 deposit:

| Item | Purpose | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Carpet stain repair, bedroom 2 | Damage beyond normal wear | $220 |
| Unpaid utility bill (lease section 9) | Lease contingency | $85 |
| Total kept | | $305 |
| Refund to tenant | | $1,195 |

Keep the invoices with the file. The detailed list of expenditures is part of the statute, not an extra.

## Must an Idaho landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

Idaho law does not require interest on security deposits. Section 6-321 has no interest rule.

A separate account is required in one case. Under § 6-321(4), when a third-party manager manages the rental for a landlord, the deposit must sit in a separate account at a federally insured financial institution. That account must stay separate from the manager's operating account.

The account rule does not apply to:

- A property owner who manages the property.
- Managers who share members or principals with the owner entity.
- A real estate licensee.
- A nonprofit organization under chapter 30, title 30, Idaho Code.

If the property changes owners during a tenancy, the new owner is liable for the refund (§ 6-321(3)). Get the deposit ledger at closing.

## What is the penalty for an Idaho landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

An Idaho landlord who does not return a deposit "as and when required by law" can be sued under Idaho Code § 6-320(a)(4). The tenant must first give the landlord 3 days' written notice that lists the failure and demands a cure (§ 6-320(d)).

If the tenant wins, 2 sections raise the cost:

- **Treble damages.** Idaho Code § 6-317 says that for an action under § 6-320, judgment may be entered for 3 times the actual damages. The word is "may", so the court decides.
- **Attorney fees.** Idaho Code § 6-324 gives the prevailing party attorney fees in any action under the chapter, except where treble damages are awarded.

Worked example: a landlord keeps $600 without a valid itemized statement. If a court finds $600 in actual damages and trebles them, the judgment is $1,800, plus costs.

## Idaho deposit checklist

- Put the refund period and the deposit contingencies in the lease.
- Note the surrender date, not only the lease end date.
- Diary day 21, or the lease date, never past day 30.
- Sign the itemized statement and attach the list of expenditures.
- Third-party managers: hold deposits in a separate insured account.
- Answer any 3-day demand from a tenant at once.

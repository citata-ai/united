## When Should You Ask for an Exit Interview?

Offer the interview after acknowledging notice, when the owner can choose a convenient time. Do not make the interview a condition of a clean transfer.

An early conversation can reveal an unresolved issue before handoff. A later conversation may produce a calmer answer after final records arrive. Offer a written reply if the owner does not want a call.

Four and Half's discussion with Property Meld's Ray Hespen examines owner churn and the value of keeping clients. It provides context for asking why owners leave. It does not prove an interview itself reduces cancellations.

Choose an interviewer who can listen without defending every decision. If the complaint concerns the account manager, use another qualified person where practical. Explain that the aim is to learn and complete the transfer correctly.

Keep service recovery separate from the invitation. If the owner raises an active repair or funds issue, route that work immediately. Do not wait until the interview to act. Ask permission before recording a call and follow applicable rules.

## Which Questions Give Useful Exit Answers?

Ask neutral questions that connect the decision with specific events and unmet needs. Use this full template as a draft, then adapt it to your service and local rules.

The template contains 8 proposed questions. That is a design choice, not a proven response-rate formula.

```text
Owner exit interview
Owner or account:
Property or portfolio:
Notice received:
Interview date and format:
Interviewer:
Owner's stated reason, in their words:

1. What changed that led you to end management now?
2. Which part of our service helped you most?
3. Which part did not meet your expectations?
4. Can you describe a specific event behind that concern?
5. When did you first raise the concern, and what happened next?
6. What could we reasonably have done differently?
7. What are you choosing next, and what need does it meet?
8. Which records, funds or open tasks still need attention?

Owner's words and examples:
Evidence to review:
Primary reason code:
Contributing reason codes:
Handoff task and receiving person:
Service fix, responsible person and review date:
Owner permission for further contact:
```

Avoid a question that assumes the owner is wrong or a discount would solve the problem. Ask for an example when an answer is broad. Poor communication becomes useful feedback when tied to a missed repair update.

Do not press for personal details the owner does not want to share. Record enough to understand the service issue. Keep sensitive information out of a general staff report.

## How Do You Sort Owner Exit Reasons?

Store the owner's stated reason and your reviewed reason codes separately. This preserves the original feedback while allowing consistent analysis.

Use practical categories such as sale, self-management, service scope, billing clarity, repair handling and communication. These are suggested internal categories. Add one only when it helps identify a different decision or fix.

Allow contributing reasons. An owner may sell after a long service dispute. Calling the exit a sale alone can hide evidence. Another may leave after excellent service because their plans changed. Do not force either case into a convenient story.

Check records before assigning a final service finding. Review the agreement, messages, statements and work orders tied to the example. Ask the responsible team member for context. The interview may not contain the whole timeline.

Mark uncertain findings as uncertain. A declined interview does not prove satisfaction or dissatisfaction. Use unknown as a valid result rather than inventing a reason. Keep the coding guide clear enough for different reviewers to apply consistently.

## How Do You Turn Exit Data Into Fixes?

Turn a repeated reason into a specific change with a named lead and a later check. Counting reasons without changing the process adds little value.

Start with a failure you can observe. If owners cite surprise charges, compare proposals with actual statements. If they cite repair silence, inspect updates. Choose a fix that addresses the observed gap.

Give the fix a completion test. A revised fee table should let staff explain each charge from the same source. A repair-update rule should produce a next action in open cases. Avoid vague tasks such as improve customer service.

Share a brief report with people who can act. Include the reason, examples, change and responsible person. Remove unnecessary owner or resident details. Give staff context without turning feedback into blame.

Review later exits and active-account complaints for the same pattern. A fix can be complete on paper while the problem remains. Check actual cases before declaring success. Keep the original measure stable so the comparison remains useful.

## How Do You Keep the Handoff Separate From Feedback?

Use a transfer checklist with its own lead, deadlines and records. Willingness to give feedback should not decide whether the transfer proceeds correctly.

- Confirm the end date and receiving contact.
- List open repairs and promised follow-up.
- Prepare records and access items for transfer.
- Review funds, unsettled payments and disputed charges.
- Tell residents the future contact route.
- Record receipt of transferred items.

Have local counsel review notice, record and funds duties. The template is generic and does not change the agreement or applicable law. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

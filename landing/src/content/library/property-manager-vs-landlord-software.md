## Which jobs can landlord software support?

Landlord software supports repeatable record and payment tasks. A manager can also provide people and local action, depending on the contract. Compare the work each option includes before comparing bills.

| Task | Software role | Human work still needed |
|---|---|---|
| Applications | Collect information and reports | Apply lawful criteria and make decisions |
| Rent | Track charges and payments | Resolve disputes and failed payments |
| Repairs | Receive a request and store records | Diagnose, arrange access, and check work |
| Leasing | Store forms and signatures | Confirm terms and local compliance |
| Books | Export transactions and reports | Reconcile records and classify costs |
| Price | Use a current itemized vendor quote | Use a full manager proposal for the property |

Zillow Rental Manager's screening page describes application, credit, and background report tools. Its rent collection page describes online payments. These are useful functions, but they do not establish that every owner task is covered.

Check features in the actual plan you intend to buy. Ask about exports, permissions, support, failed payment handling, and add-on charges. A headline feature list can hide limits that matter to your process.

## Which jobs still need a person?

Repairs, judgment, and physical access still need people. A portal can record a water leak. Someone must decide whether it needs immediate action, contact the right contractor, and check the result.

A tenant also needs a reliable contact during an urgent problem. That contact must have authority and a way to pay for work. A phone number without access or funds is incomplete cover.

Screening also involves judgment and legal duties. A report is evidence, not a ready-made decision. Ask a local adviser to review your criteria and notices before relying on an automated rejection setting.

Showings, inspections, keys, and move-out records need a local plan. You can hire people for separate tasks, but you must coordinate them. Confirm who handles access when a contractor cancels or the tenant cannot attend.

A property manager may accept many of these tasks. Read the exclusions, spending limits, and after-hours process. A management contract is a work agreement, not a promise that the owner will never receive a call.

## What does each option cost per year?

Use current quotes for both options and calculate a full-year cost. There is no universal software price or manager fee. The plan, payment methods, property, and service scope change the result.

List software subscription charges, payment fees, screening charges, signatures, inspections, and record exports. Identify charges paid by the owner and those paid by an applicant or tenant. Check local rules before passing costs through.

For a manager, list the base fee, leasing, renewal, inspections, project oversight, cancellation, and vacant-property charges. Ask whether vendor markups or related-party services can apply. Request the terms in writing.

Here is a hypothetical comparison, using invented inputs. Assume software and outside task help cost $1,200 per year. Assume a management proposal totals $3,000 for the same year and stated scope.

The visible difference is $1,800. If self-management requires an assumed 60 extra owner hours, the difference equals $30 per extra hour. This calculation is a decision aid, not a wage estimate or industry benchmark.

## When does an owner outgrow software alone?

An owner outgrows software alone when they cannot provide timely, reliable work around it. Unit count matters less than the actual task load. Distance, repair needs, and available backup can change the decision quickly.

Watch for unfinished requests, missing receipts, repeated late notices, and delayed showings. These are process gaps. Buying another tool may help only if someone has time and authority to use it.

A change in your normal job can also reduce available time. Plan coverage before a long trip, relocation, or busy season. A difficult tenant issue rarely waits for a convenient week.

You can use a mixed model. Keep the books yourself while hiring local repair help. Hire a leasing agent for turnover. Or use a full manager while reviewing the reports and budgets yourself.

Define ownership for each task. Shared responsibility without a named lead can leave work undone. Record who receives the request, who approves it, and who confirms completion.

## What should you check before changing systems?

Check records, contracts, and payment continuity before switching. Export data while you still have access. Keep invoices and lease documents outside a single vendor account.

IRS Publication 527 explains that rental income and expense treatment depends on the transaction. A manager statement or software category does not determine tax treatment. Preserve enough detail for your tax professional to review repairs, improvements, and deposits.

- **Test an export.** Confirm the file includes dates, amounts, properties, and transaction details.
- **Map open work.** Assign every unfinished repair and tenant question.
- **Confirm payment routing.** Tell the tenant clearly where future rent goes.
- **Check authority.** Remove old access only after the replacement process works.
- **Reconcile balances.** Match owner cash and tenant deposits to the records.
- **Review the agreement.** Confirm end dates, record access, and any exit charges.

Choose the arrangement that keeps the home cared for and the records clear. The [guide library](/guides) can help you compare related owner tasks before you sign.

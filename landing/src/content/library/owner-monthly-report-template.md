## Which numbers belong on an owner statement?

An owner statement holds 6 numbers: beginning balance, income collected, expenses paid, management fee, reserve change and the owner draw. Every other line explains one of these 6. If the 6 numbers add up, the owner can check the statement in 2 minutes.

Some states set rules for the report. Arizona's ARS 32-2173, for example, says a property management agreement must state the type and frequency of status reports to the owner. It must also state the amount and purpose of any operating reserve. So read your own agreement first: the report you send must match what the agreement promises.

Count income only when cash reaches the trust account. Buildium's guide to owner distributions says to start with money actually deposited, and to leave out unpaid rent and pending fees. Unpaid rent goes in the notes, not in income.

## How do you show income, expenses and the owner draw?

Show the draw as the result of a simple subtraction, in one block, with no hidden steps. Owners lose trust when the draw does not match the rent they expect. A clear calculation stops most of those calls.

Here is a worked example for one single-family rental. The numbers are an example, not market data.

| Line | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Rent collected | Tenant paid in full | $1,850.00 |
| Late fee collected | One late payment | $50.00 |
| **Total income** | $1,850 + $50 | **$1,900.00** |
| Management fee | 8% × $1,850 rent collected | $148.00 |
| Repair: kitchen faucet | Work order #1042 | $235.00 |
| **Total expenses** | $148 + $235 | **$383.00** |
| Net for the month | $1,900 − $383 | $1,517.00 |
| Reserve top-up | Reserve already at $250 target | $0.00 |
| **Owner draw** | $1,517 − $0 | **$1,517.00** |

The owner sees why the draw is $333 less than the rent: a $148 fee and a $235 repair, offset by a $50 late fee. The example takes the fee on rent only. Your agreement may say otherwise, so follow it.

Software shows the same logic. TenantCloud's help page gives the distribution formula as owner balance minus property reserve, times the ownership share. It also leaves security deposits out of the calculation. Buildium's guide lists the order: income, expenses, management fee, then reserves and minimum balances.

## Should the report include photos and notes?

Yes. Photos and 2–4 sentences of notes answer the questions numbers cannot. "Why did I pay $235?" becomes a before and after photo of the faucet. "Is the unit OK?" becomes a short note from the last drive-by.

Use these rules to keep notes short and useful:

1. **Lead with the decision.** If the owner must approve a job or a renewal rent, put it first, with a date.
2. **Name the unit.** "Unit 2B is 6 days late. We sent a notice on day 3." Not "some rent is late."
3. **Give the next step.** Every problem in the notes has an owner, an action and a date.
4. **Attach photos to the job.** Link each photo to its work order number, so the owner can match it to the expense line.
5. **Say when nothing happened.** "No repairs, no late rent, no complaints" is a useful note too.

Buildium's guide also suggests a note on any unusual item, such as an emergency repair. Send the statement on the same day each month. A fixed day matters more than a fast one.

## What extra items go in a year-end owner report?

The year-end report adds a 12-month view and the tax papers. Owners and their tax preparers use it to file Schedule E. The IRS says rental income and expenses go on Schedule E (Form 1040), Part I.

Year-end checklist:

- [ ] **12-month income summary.** Rent, late fees and other income by month and by category.
- [ ] **12-month expense summary.** Totals by category: repairs, utilities, insurance, tax, HOA, fees.
- [ ] **Form 1099-MISC.** The IRS says a property manager must use Form 1099-MISC to report the rent paid over to the owner. For payments made in 2026, the box 1 rent threshold is $2,000.
- [ ] **1099 dates.** The IRS sets January 31 for the recipient copy of Form 1099-MISC. File with the IRS by February 28 on paper or March 31 if you file electronically.
- [ ] **Capital items list.** New roof, HVAC, appliances. The IRS says improvements are recovered through depreciation, not deducted at once.
- [ ] **Security deposits held.** Each tenant, amount and lease end date.
- [ ] **Large invoices.** Copies of invoices above a set amount. The IRS asks owners to keep receipts, canceled checks or bills to support expenses.
- [ ] **Next-year notes.** Leases that end, planned repairs and the reserve target.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current rules or ask a tax professional.

## How do you start using the template?

Start with one owner and one month. Build the template in your software, send it, and ask the owner one question: "What did you look for first?" Change the order to match the answer.

## How do I open a sales call with a rental owner?

Open by confirming time and purpose, then hand the owner the floor. The first minute sets the tone. An owner who feels rushed or pitched will hold back the facts you need.

Prepare before you dial. Read the lead form and look up the property on a map. Check rent for similar homes nearby. Note how the owner first heard of you.

### Opening script

```text
Hi [first name], this is [your name] from [company]. Thanks for
reaching out about [street name]. Is this still a good time?
Do you have about 20 minutes?

Here's what I'd like to do: learn about the property and what you
want from it, answer your questions, and if it makes sense, talk
about next steps. If it's not a fit, I'll tell you. Anything you
want to make sure we cover?
```

The line "If it's not a fit, I'll tell you" matters. It lowers the owner's guard and keeps your pipeline clean.

## Which discovery questions matter most on an owner call?

The questions that matter most reveal the owner's goal, their fears and their timeline. Ask them in order, and write down the owner's own words. You will use those words when you present fees.

| # | Question | What you learn |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Tell me about the property. How long have you owned it?" | Type, age, history |
| 2 | "Is it rented now, or empty?" | Urgency and the current tenant situation |
| 3 | "What's your goal for it over the next 3 to 5 years?" | Hold, sell, add doors |
| 4 | "Who manages it today? What works, and what doesn't?" | Pain points |
| 5 | "What happened with past tenants?" | Fears to address |
| 6 | "How involved do you want to be?" | Reports and approvals they expect |
| 7 | "What repair amount should we handle without calling you?" | Approval limit |
| 8 | "What rent are you hoping for? Where did that number come from?" | Expectations to correct early |
| 9 | "Do you own other rentals, or plan to?" | Lifetime value |
| 10 | "When do you need to decide, and who else is part of it?" | Timeline and decision makers |

Listen more than you talk. If the owner says "My last manager never called me back", that is the most important sentence of the call.

### Fair housing on the call

Owners sometimes ask you to screen out kinds of tenants. Do not agree. The Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. § 3604, bars refusing to rent because of race, color, religion, sex, familial status or national origin, and bars discrimination because of a handicap. State and local laws may add more protected classes. Explain that you apply the same written screening criteria to every applicant. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I present property management fees on a call?

Present fees after discovery, as a full yearly total, and link each fee to what the owner told you. Owners compare quotes by the monthly percent. Your job is to show the full cost and what it buys.

### Fee script

```text
Based on what you shared, rent around [$X], and you want [their
words: e.g. "fewer calls at night" and "a tenant who stays"], here's
what we'd charge and what each part covers:

- Monthly management, [x]%: rent collection, owner statements by
  the [day] of each month, repair calls 24/7, and repairs under
  [$limit] without bothering you.
- Leasing, [amount]: marketing, showings, screening with the same
  written criteria for every applicant, and the lease.
- Renewal, [amount]: [what it covers].

For your home, that's about [$total] a year in total. Want me to
email this as a one-page table so you can compare it with other
quotes, line by line?
```

Send the table the same day. An owner who can compare total cost line by line trusts the number more. The [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) can help you build it.

If the owner pushes back on price, ask what they compare with. Do not cut your fee on the call. Say you will look at it and reply in writing.

## How do I close the call for a next step?

Close by asking for one specific next step with a date. "Let me know" is not a next step. A good close gives the owner an easy yes.

### Next steps by owner stage

| Owner stage | Next step to ask for |
|---|---|
| Ready to sign | "I'll send the agreement today. Can we review it together Thursday at 10?" |
| Wants proof | "Can I connect you with a current owner who had a similar home?" |
| Unsure about rent | "Let me run a rental analysis and walk you through it Monday." |
| Comparing managers | "I'll send the fee table today. Can I call you Friday after you compare?" |
| Not ready (6+ months) | "Can I check back in [month]? I'll send you a short note before then." |

### Closing script

```text
Thanks for walking me through all of this. From what you said, the
biggest things are [1] and [2]. I think we can help with both.
The next step would be [specific step]. Does [day and time] work?
```

After the call, log the outcome, the owner's goal in their words, and the next step date in your CRM. Results vary a lot by market and lead source. Goodjuju Marketing's Traverse case study reports one client's closing ratio of 38%; that is one company's claim, not a benchmark. Track your own rate by lead source.

Most owners research you before the call. Goodjuju's 2026 study found Google Search and the Map Pack brought 50.6% of owner leads across 35 property managers in its fall window. ## What should I do right after the owner call?

Send a follow-up email within 2 hours. It should repeat the owner's goal in their words, list what you agreed, attach the fee table and confirm the next step date. That email often decides the deal more than the call itself.

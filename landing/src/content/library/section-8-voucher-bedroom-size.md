## Which payment standard applies when unit and voucher size differ?

The lower payment standard applies when the unit size and the voucher size differ. 24 CFR 982.505(c)(1) says the payment standard for the family is the lower of:

- the payment standard amount for the family unit size (the size on the voucher); or
- the payment standard amount for the size of the unit the family rents.

The payment standard is the maximum monthly subsidy the PHA uses in the HAP formula. So the voucher size works as a ceiling, and a smaller unit lowers it further.

| Voucher size | Unit size | Payment standard used | Utility allowance used |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 bedrooms | 2 bedrooms | 2-bedroom | 2-bedroom |
| 2 bedrooms | 3 bedrooms | 2-bedroom | 2-bedroom (unless a reasonable accommodation) |
| 2 bedrooms | 1 bedroom | 1-bedroom | 1-bedroom |
| 3 bedrooms | 2 bedrooms | 2-bedroom | 2-bedroom |

Source: 24 CFR 982.402(c), 982.505(c) and 982.517(d). The utility allowance rule says the PHA uses the allowance for the smaller of the unit size or the family unit size. When the bigger unit is a reasonable accommodation, the PHA uses the allowance for the unit actually leased.

## Can a family rent a bigger unit?

Yes, a family can rent a bigger unit. 24 CFR 982.402(d)(2) lets a family lease "an otherwise acceptable dwelling unit with more bedrooms than the family unit size". But the subsidy does not grow. The payment standard stays at the voucher size, and so does the utility allowance in most cases.

For you as the owner, this means a bigger unit costs the family more. The family pays the gap between the gross rent and the HAP. That gap can break the 40% rule at move-in.

## Can a family rent a smaller unit?

Yes, a family can rent a smaller unit, if the unit meets the space standards. 24 CFR 982.402(d)(1) allows a unit "with fewer bedrooms than the family unit size", but it "must meet the applicable HQS space requirements". HQS means the housing quality standards that the PHA inspects against.

The subsidy then drops to the smaller unit's payment standard. A smaller unit often has a lower rent too, so the family share may stay close to the same.

Watch for: if the family later grows and the unit fails the space standards, the PHA issues a new voucher. When an acceptable unit is available, the PHA ends the HAP contract (24 CFR 982.403).

## How does the PHA set voucher size?

The PHA sets voucher size with its own written subsidy standards. 24 CFR 982.402(b) sets the rules those standards must follow:

1. **Smallest size without overcrowding.** The standards give the fewest bedrooms needed.
2. **Consistent with HQS space rules.**
3. **Same for all like families.** Families of like size and composition get the same size.
4. **Foster children count.** A child away in foster care is still a family member for sizing.
5. **Pregnancy counts.** A pregnant woman with no other persons is a 2-person family.
6. **Live-in aides count.** An approved live-in aide counts in the size.
7. **Single persons.** Without a live-in aide, a single person gets a zero- or one-bedroom size.
8. **Exceptions.** The PHA may grant an exception for age, sex, health, disability, relationship or other personal reasons.

The size goes on the voucher (982.402(a)(3)). If you want to know which payment standard a family has, ask to see the voucher. Do not ask about the family's members or composition to guess. Size decisions belong to the PHA.

## Example with numbers

Here is one family and 3 possible units. The payment standards are an example, not a real PHA's schedule. Look up your area's numbers in our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits).

Example payment standards: 1-bedroom $1,300, 2-bedroom $1,600, 3-bedroom $1,950.

Family: 2-bedroom voucher, adjusted monthly income $2,500. Total tenant payment (TTP) is 30% of adjusted income, $750 (24 CFR 5.628, simplified).

The HAP is the lower of (payment standard − TTP) or (gross rent − TTP) (24 CFR 982.505(b)).

| Line | 1-bedroom unit | 2-bedroom unit | 3-bedroom unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross rent (rent + utility allowance) | $1,250 | $1,650 | $1,900 |
| Payment standard used | $1,300 (1-BR) | $1,600 (2-BR) | $1,600 (2-BR) |
| HAP | $500 | $850 | $850 |
| Family share (gross rent − HAP) | $750 | $800 | $1,050 |
| Family share as % of adjusted income | 30% | 32% | 42% |
| Passes the 40% rule at move-in? | Yes | Yes | No |

The 3-bedroom fails. Under 24 CFR 982.508, when the gross rent is above the payment standard at move-in, the family share may not exceed 40% of adjusted monthly income. Here 40% is $1,000, and the family share is $1,050.

How to find the highest gross rent the PHA can approve for this family in the 3-bedroom: payment standard + (40% of adjusted income − TTP) = $1,600 + ($1,000 − $750) = $1,850. If the gross rent is $1,850 or less, and the PHA finds the rent reasonable, the tenancy can work.

## What should an owner do?

1. **List the real bedroom count.** Do not list a unit as "for 2-bedroom vouchers only". Let each family check its voucher.
2. **Know the local payment standards** for each bedroom size near your rent.
3. **For a bigger unit, price near the voucher-size limit.** A rent far above the smaller payment standard often fails the 40% test.
4. **For a smaller unit, check space rules** before the inspection.
5. **Ask the PHA early.** Ask what rent it can approve for this family and unit before you sign a lease.

Arizona gives landlords wide room on rent. The state bars rent control, and the landlord-tenant act sets no cap on an increase. The rules that do apply are about notice, the lease term and retaliation. We read each section on the Arizona Legislature site on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Rent control by cities or towns | Not allowed on private housing | A.R.S. 33-1329(A) |
| Cap on the size of an increase | None in the act | A.R.S. Title 33, Ch. 10 |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | Written notice 30 days before the rental date | A.R.S. 33-1375(B) |
| End a week-to-week tenancy | Written notice 10 days before the end date | A.R.S. 33-1375(A) |
| No lease term stated | Month-to-month (week-to-week for a weekly roomer) | A.R.S. 33-1314(D) |
| Start submetering or ratio billing for current tenants | 90 days' notice | A.R.S. 33-1314.01(G) |
| Retaliatory rent increase | Not allowed; 6-month presumption | A.R.S. 33-1381 |
| City rental sales tax | Banned from and after 2024-12-31 | A.R.S. 42-6004(H) |

## Does Arizona have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

No. Arizona has no state cap on rent increases and no local rent control. A.R.S. 33-1329(A) says rent control on private residential housing is "of statewide concern." The power to control rents is "preempted by the state." Cities, charter cities and towns "shall not have the power to control rents."

The ban has one limit. A.R.S. 33-1329(B) says it does not apply to housing that a state agency or a city or town owns, finances, insures or subsidizes. Those units can carry rent rules from the program that funds them.

## How many days notice does an Arizona landlord need to raise the rent?

The Arizona landlord-tenant act names no separate notice period for a rent increase. The rule that controls in practice is the notice to end a periodic tenancy. A.R.S. 33-1375(B) says either side may end a month-to-month tenancy with written notice at least 30 days before the periodic rental date in the notice.

Because the landlord can end the tenancy on 30 days' notice, many landlords give the same 30 days, in writing, before a new rent starts. That is a common practice, not a separate statute. If your lease sets a longer notice for changes, follow the lease.

Worked example for a month-to-month tenant whose rent is due on the 1st:

1. **Pick the start date.** The new rent starts on December 1.
2. **Count back 30 days.** December 1 minus 30 days is November 1. Deliver the written notice by November 1 at the latest. We suggest a few days earlier.
3. **Add mail time.** A.R.S. 33-1313(B) says a notice sent by registered or certified mail counts as received on delivery, or 5 days after mailing, whichever comes first. Mail it by October 27.
4. **Keep proof.** Keep a copy of the notice and the mail receipt.

One notice rule is fixed in statute. To start submetering or ratio utility billing for an existing tenant, A.R.S. 33-1314.01(G) requires at least 90 days' notice. The lease must also describe the billing method.

## Can an Arizona landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

A fixed-term lease sets the rent for its term. A.R.S. 33-1314(A) lets the landlord and tenant put rent, the term and other conditions in the rental agreement. The act has no rule that lets a landlord change the rent on its own in the middle of a term.

So a mid-lease increase needs one of two things:

- **A lease clause** that allows the increase and says how and when.
- **The tenant's agreement** in writing, such as a signed lease amendment.

Without either, raise the rent at renewal. If the lease does not fix a term, A.R.S. 33-1314(D) treats it as month-to-month, so the 30-day rule above applies.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Arizona?

Written notice at least 30 days before the periodic rental date in the notice. A.R.S. 33-1375(B) sets this for both landlord and tenant. For a week-to-week tenancy, A.R.S. 33-1375(A) sets 10 days.

If the tenant stays after the end date without consent, the landlord may sue for possession. If the holdover is willful and not in good faith, A.R.S. 33-1375(C) lets the landlord recover up to 2 months' rent or twice actual damages, whichever is greater.

Retaliation limits both increases and non-renewals. A.R.S. 33-1381(A) bars a rent increase, a cut in services or a possession case after a tenant:

- Complains to a code agency about a health and safety violation.
- Complains to the landlord about a repair duty under A.R.S. 33-1324.
- Organizes or joins a tenant union.

A complaint within 6 months before the landlord's act creates a presumption of retaliation (A.R.S. 33-1381(B)).

## Do cities in Arizona add their own rent or notice rules?

Cities in Arizona cannot add rent control, because A.R.S. 33-1329 preempts it. Other city rules on rental housing can exist. Check the city code for the property's location before you send a notice.

One recent state change affects rent bills in many cities. From and after 2024-12-31, A.R.S. 42-6004(H) bars a city, town or other taxing jurisdiction from a transaction privilege, sales or similar tax on renting real property for residential purposes. The ban applies whether or not the city adopted the model city tax code. It does not apply to hotels, motels, other transient lodging, or health care and long-term care facilities. If your lease or ledger still passes a city rental tax to tenants, review it.

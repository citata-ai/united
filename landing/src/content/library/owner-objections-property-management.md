## How do I answer "your fee is too high"?

Answer the fee objection with the full cost, not a defense of the percent. Most owners compare one number, the monthly fee, across managers. Two managers with the same monthly fee can cost very different amounts once leasing, renewal, markup and setup fees are added.

Start with a question:

> "Fair question. What are you comparing it with? If you share the other quote, I'll put both side by side, fee by fee."

Then show a table like this one. Use your real fees and the owner's real rent.

| Cost per year on $1,800 rent | Manager A (you) | Manager B (low monthly fee) |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly fee | 9% = $1,944 | 7% = $1,512 |
| Leasing fee (1 lease every 2 years) | 50% of 1 month ÷ 2 = $450 | 100% of 1 month ÷ 2 = $900 |
| Renewal fee | $0 | $250 |
| Maintenance markup on $2,000 repairs | 0% = $0 | 10% = $200 |
| Setup fee (spread over 3 years) | $0 | $300 ÷ 3 = $100 |
| Total yearly cost | $2,394 | $2,962 |

These are example numbers. The point is the method: total cost per year, line by line. If your total is higher, say so, and explain what the extra buys.

Do not run down the other manager. Owners remember how you talk about competitors.

### Proof that helps

- Your full fee table, on your website, with nothing hidden.
- A sample monthly owner statement.
- Your vacancy and days-to-lease numbers, from your own records, dated.

Owners can test the math on the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## How do I answer "I can do it myself"?

Agree first. Many owners can self-manage one or two homes well. Arguing makes you look like you need the deal. Your job is to help the owner see the full job, then let them choose.

> "You're right, a lot of owners do it well. Can I share what the job looks like in a normal year, so you can decide with all the facts?"

Then walk through a year:

| Task | Times per year (typical home) | Who does it now |
|---|---|---|
| Collect rent and chase late payments | 12 | Owner |
| Answer repair calls, including nights | Varies | Owner |
| Hire and pay vendors | Varies | Owner |
| Inspect the home | 1 to 2 | Owner |
| Renew or re-lease, screen applicants | 0.5 to 1 | Owner |
| Follow notice, deposit and fair housing rules | Every step | Owner |
| Year-end statements for taxes | 1 | Owner |

Ask how many hours they want to give it, and how they will handle a vacancy or an eviction. Notice and deposit rules differ by state, and fair housing rules apply to every rental. A manager handles those every week; an owner may face them once in 5 years.

Some owners will still choose to self-manage. Thank them and offer a smaller service, such as tenant placement only, if you have one.

## How do I answer "I had a bad manager before"?

Ask what went wrong, then listen to the whole story. This objection is not about you; it is about trust. The owner wants proof that the same thing will not happen again.

> "I'm sorry that happened. What went wrong? I want to understand it, so I can tell you honestly whether we'd do it differently."

Common stories and what to show:

| What went wrong before | What to show |
|---|---|
| "I never heard from them" | Your owner update schedule and a sample report |
| "Repairs cost too much" | Your approval limit and markup policy, in writing |
| "They placed a bad tenant" | Your written screening criteria (applied the same to every applicant) |
| "I couldn't get out of the contract" | Your cancel terms: notice period, any fee |
| "Money was late" | Your owner payout date and a statement sample |

Offer a short, clear exit. An owner who knows they can leave in 30 days fears signing less.

## Which proof helps each answer?

Proof beats promises in every objection. Owners trust documents, numbers and other owners more than your words.

1. **Full fee table** on your website and in your first email.
2. **Sample owner statement** with the owner's name removed.
3. **Written cancel policy** in plain words.
4. **Reviews from owners.** Google says reviews must reflect a genuine experience, and incentives for reviews are prohibited. The FTC's 2024 final rule also bans rewards conditioned on a review with a set sentiment. Ask every owner the same way.
5. **A reference call** with a current owner who agrees to take it.
6. **Your own numbers**, dated: average days to lease, renewal rate, owner retention.

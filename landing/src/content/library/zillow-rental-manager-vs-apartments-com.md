## What does each charge to list?

Both vendors describe a free route for basic rental marketing or management. Paid promotion is a separate buying decision. Confirm the offer shown for your property before payment.

Zillow's Premium help page lists a one-time $39.99 upgrade for benefits lasting up to 90 days. The page says it is not a subscription. These terms were read on 2026-10-19.

Apartments.com's Rental Manager page describes free tools but does not give a universal price for every advertising package. Treat paid advertising as quote only unless your checkout gives a clear offer.

Separate listing cost from screening and payment fees. A free property listing does not make every renter action free. Review the renter-facing screen before sending applicants there.

Do not buy promotion to fix missing photos, wrong rent, or a broken inquiry link. Correct the listing first. Then test whether the paid offer improves the outcome you track.

## Which sites does each syndicate to?

Zillow's direct listing page names Zillow, Trulia, and HotPads. Apartments.com's Rental Manager page describes its network and highlights Homes.com among partner destinations.

A direct listing and a third-party software feed can have different distribution rules. Ask the source platform which account controls the listing. Keep that control clear if an agent or previous manager posted it.

Inspect the destinations after publication. Compare the address, unit, rent, availability, fee details, and application link. A network promise does not prove every field arrived correctly.

Also test an update and removal. Leased units should leave the active feed promptly under the supported process. Record a contact route for duplicate or stale listings.

Do not add together network audience claims to estimate unique renters. People can use more than one site. Your own lead records are a better basis for a property-level decision.

## Which has better screening and leases?

Both offer screening and lease-related tools, but the exact report and state coverage need checking. Compare the workflow you can use for your property.

Zillow's listing page describes applications, document upload, and a lease builder in selected states. Apartments.com lists tenant screening and online lease creation. Neither short product description establishes local document suitability.

Ask each vendor for a sample report and an approved demo of the lease process. Check signer roles, attachments, correction steps, and export. Confirm which report charges the applicant sees.

If you list on both sites, select a clear application route. Avoid making applicants buy duplicate reports because staff cannot see which one they already submitted.

Have local counsel review the lease you will use. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Which brings more leads?

Neither vendor's product page establishes which will bring more useful leads for your rental. Local demand, rent, listing quality, and response speed affect the result.

Track the path from inquiry to showing, completed application, and signed lease. A large inquiry total may include duplicates or questions from people who never apply.

Use the same property facts and comparable response standards on both sites. Record major changes to rent or availability. Otherwise, a lead comparison may reflect the change rather than the channel.

Ask applicants how they first found the listing, while preserving the source tag when available. Do not require extra personal information just to improve attribution.

Choose your next advertising spend after this review. A channel that produces fewer inquiries may still produce useful applications with less staff work. Keep that distinction visible.

## Can you use both?

You can operate listings on both platforms if you keep source control and application instructions clear. Check feed restrictions before publishing the same unit through several routes.

Choose a staff member to own listing accuracy. Keep a master record of rent, photos, availability, contact route, and current listing links.

When a property changes status, update every supported source. Confirm the final result on the destination sites. Do not assume removing a listing in one account removes a duplicate created elsewhere.

Keep tenant service separate from advertising where useful. A rental can be marketed on several sites while rent and repair records stay in your selected management system.

Before adding paid promotion, compare the results from the free routes. Document what worked, what failed, and what you will change for the next vacancy.

## How should you test the choice before you pay?

Test the full workflow with sample records before you move live data. Use the same case in each product. Ask the person who will do the daily work to run the test. A sales demo alone does not show where your team will need help.

Keep a decision sheet with the task, result, missing step, and person who checked it. Mark each result as shown, promised, or still open. Ask for written terms when a feature needs an upgrade or another service. Include staff setup time in your cost review.

Check the exit path while you still control the choice. Export the sample records and open them outside the platform. Confirm that notes, files, dates, and links remain useful. Ask how you will get records after you close the account.

Choose a tool when the test proves a clear gain for your workflow. Keep your current process until the new process passes. Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to see how software costs affect your service margin.

## How to use this list

Use this list in a 30 to 45 minute call with each of 3 managers. Ask the same questions in the same order. Write the answers in one table so you can compare.

Ground rules:

- **Ask for documents before the call:** the management agreement, the fee schedule and a sample owner statement.
- **Listen for specifics.** "We list in 48 hours with pro photos" beats "we move fast."
- **Get money and terms in writing.** A spoken promise is not in the contract.
- **Start with license.** In many states, managing rentals for others needs a real estate license. Florida's statute 475.01, for example, defines a broker to include a person who rents real property of others for pay. Ask for the license number and check it on your state's lookup.

## What questions show how they screen tenants?

Screening questions show whether the manager picks tenants with a fair, written process. A bad tenant costs more than any fee.

| # | Question | Good answer |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Can I see your written screening criteria? | Yes, in writing, the same for every applicant |
| 2 | What income do you require? | A clear rule, such as a multiple of rent, applied to all |
| 3 | Which reports do you pull? | Credit, rental history and background checks, where state law allows |
| 4 | Do you verify income and past landlords? | Yes, with documents and calls |
| 5 | How do you handle housing vouchers? | They follow state and local law on source of income |
| 6 | What notice do you send when you deny an applicant? | An adverse action notice when a report was used |
| 7 | How many applications do you get per vacancy here? | A number, not a guess |

Why question 6 matters: the FTC says a landlord that takes adverse action based on a consumer report, such as a denial, must give the applicant notice. A manager who does not know this is a risk to you.

Fair housing: screening must not steer or reject applicants by a protected class. Written criteria, applied the same way to all, is the standard to ask for.

## What questions show how they handle repairs?

Repair questions show how the manager spends your money and treats your tenant. Both affect how long tenants stay.

| # | Question | Good answer |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 8 | Above what dollar amount must you ask me first? | A clear limit in the agreement |
| 9 | What counts as an emergency? | No heat, active leak, no water, safety risk |
| 10 | Who answers repair calls at night and on weekends? | A person or a staffed line, not voicemail only |
| 11 | Do you use your own staff or outside vendors? | Either, with licensed and insured vendors |
| 12 | Do you add a markup to vendor bills? | A stated percentage or none, in the fee schedule |
| 13 | Do you get more than one bid on large jobs? | Yes, above a set amount |
| 14 | Do I get photos and invoices? | Yes, in the owner portal |

## What questions reveal hidden fees?

Fee questions reveal the full yearly cost. The monthly percentage is only one line.

| # | Question | What to compare |
|---|----------|-----------------|
| 15 | What is the monthly fee, and is it on collected or scheduled rent? | Collected rent means no fee in an unpaid month |
| 16 | What is the leasing fee? | iPropertyManagement's 2026 average is 70.6% of one month's rent with management |
| 17 | Is there a setup fee? | 2026 average $185.24 (iPropertyManagement) |
| 18 | Is there a renewal fee? | 2026 average $211.92 (iPropertyManagement) |
| 19 | Are there inspection, eviction or court fees? | Each in writing |
| 20 | Who keeps the late fees? | Owner, manager or a split |
| 21 | Is there a fee to end the agreement? | Amount and conditions |

Then do the math for one full year with your rent. Our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) does it for you.

## What questions check how fast they fill vacancies?

Vacancy questions check how fast the manager turns an empty home into rent. Each empty month costs a full month of rent.

| # | Question | Good answer |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 22 | What were your average days to lease for homes like mine last year? | A number from their records |
| 23 | What is your renewal rate? | A number, and how they track it |
| 24 | Where do you list my home? | Major rental sites, their own site, signs |
| 25 | How do you set the rent? | Local comps, with a written rent analysis |
| 26 | Who does the showings, and when? | Agents or self-showing tech, evenings and weekends included |

For context, the US rental vacancy rate was 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026 (Census Bureau). Ask how the manager's homes compare.

## How do I end the contract if I am not happy?

You end the contract under its termination section. Read that section before anything else.

| # | Question | What to look for |
|---|----------|------------------|
| 27 | How long is the term, and does it renew by itself? | A short first term, or a clear renewal date |
| 28 | How much notice do I give, and is there a fee? | A notice period in days and a fixed fee or none |
| 29 | Can I end it for cause at once? | Yes, for a clear breach, such as missing funds |
| 30 | What do I get when I leave? | Lease, deposit records, keys, tenant ledger and files, by a set date |

Watch for: a clause that lets the manager keep the leasing fee or charge you if you keep the tenant it placed. It may be fair, but you should know the amount.

## After the interviews: a 5-minute scorecard

1. **Put the 3 fee schedules side by side** and compute one year.
2. **Mark any question each one dodged.**
3. **Call 2 owner references** for each finalist.
4. **Read the termination section** one more time.
5. **Pick the one you trust with a bad month**, not only a good one.

## What is the initial franchise fee?

The initial franchise fee is the one-time price to use the brand and system. For Keyrenter, the franchisor's own site shows $50,000 on 2026-10-19. Keyrenter also lists a $5,000 training fee and a $10,000 initial marketing and start-up package fee.

Keyrenter shows three discounts on its economics page:

- **Conversion:** existing managers with 20 or more residential properties pay a $30,000 franchise fee.
- **Veterans:** 20% off the franchise fee for the first location.
- **Real estate license:** $5,000 off the franchise fee.

For Real Property Management (RPM) and Property Management Inc. (PMI), we did not find the fee on a franchisor page. Third-party franchise sites give different 2026 numbers for both brands. We do not print a number we could not check at the source. Ask each brand for its current FDD and read Item 5.

## What royalty and brand fund do they charge?

A royalty is a share of your revenue paid to the franchisor each month for the full term. A brand fund (also called a marketing fund) is a second share that pays for national advertising. These two fees cost far more than the franchise fee over a 10-year term.

Keyrenter's site does not show its royalty rate. It says the full fee schedule is in its 2026 FDD, which you get in its discovery process. The same is true for most brands: Item 6 of the FDD ("Other Fees") is the only reliable place for the royalty.

Here is how a royalty adds up. The rates below are example inputs, not any brand's terms.

| Example input | Value |
|---|---|
| Doors under management | 300 |
| Management revenue per door per month | $150 |
| Annual gross revenue | $540,000 |
| Royalty, 7% of gross | $37,800 a year |
| Brand fund, 2% of gross | $10,800 a year |
| Monthly tech fee, $150 | $1,800 a year |
| Total ongoing fees | $50,400 a year |
| Over a 10-year term, at flat revenue | $504,000 |

At those inputs, ongoing fees are 9.3% of revenue. Compare that to your margin. If your net margin is 15%, the franchise takes more than half of it. The brand must bring you enough new doors or higher fees to pay for that.

Check three details in Item 6. First, what counts as "gross revenue": some brands charge royalty on maintenance and leasing fees too. Second, is there a minimum monthly royalty? Third, do fees rise over the term?

## What is the total startup investment?

The total startup investment is the franchise fee plus everything you spend to open: training travel, office, software, insurance, marketing and working capital. Item 7 of the FDD shows it as a table with low and high estimates.

| Brand | Total initial investment | Other requirements | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keyrenter | $118,750 to $244,400 | Fee $50,000 ($30,000 conversion) | Keyrenter franchise site, 2026 FDD |
| Real Property Management | $91,718 to $234,150 | $250,000 net worth, $250,000 liquid capital | FranNet broker listing |

FranNet is a franchise broker, not the franchisor. Its range may come from an older FDD. Check it against Item 7 of the FDD you receive.

The high end of each range matters more than the low end. Plan to fund the high end plus 6 months of your own living costs. A new office often runs at a loss while it builds doors.

## What do the Item 19 numbers say?

Item 19 ("Financial Performance Representations") is where a franchisor may show what its franchisees earn. It is optional. Keyrenter shows these 2025 numbers from its 2026 FDD on its own site:

- **Average gross revenue, franchisees open 3+ years:** $780,609.
- **Average gross revenue, all franchisees:** $650,633.
- **Average net owner benefit, 3+ years:** $209,803.

Read these with care. They are averages, so a few large offices can pull them up. Ask for the median and the number of offices in each group. Then call franchisees from Item 20, which lists current and former owners with contact data. Former owners often tell you the most.

## Where do I find the FDD numbers?

You find the FDD numbers in the document itself. The FTC Franchise Rule (16 CFR Part 436) requires the franchisor to give you the FDD at least 14 calendar days before you sign a binding agreement or pay any money (16 CFR 436.2(a)).

The rule fixes the order of 23 items (16 CFR 436.5). These are the ones that hold the cost:

| FDD item | Title in the rule | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Item 5 | Initial Fees | Franchise fee, discounts, refund terms |
| Item 6 | Other Fees | Royalty, brand fund, tech fee, minimums, transfer fee |
| Item 7 | Estimated Initial Investment | Total cost range, working capital |
| Item 12 | Territory | Is your area protected? |
| Item 17 | Renewal, Termination, Transfer, and Dispute Resolution | Exit terms, non-compete |
| Item 19 | Financial Performance Representations | Revenue and profit of franchisees |
| Item 20 | Outlets and Franchisee Information | Openings, closings, owner contacts |
| Item 21 | Financial Statements | The franchisor's own health |

Some states register franchises and publish filings. California's Department of Financial Protection and Innovation runs a self-service portal that lists filings. A filing appears only if the brand registered in that state.

## Franchise or independent?

A franchise makes sense when the brand fills a gap you cannot fill alone: a proven system, training, software deals or a name owners trust. It costs you a share of revenue for the whole term.

An independent company keeps that share. The trade-off is that you build your systems and your reputation yourself. Owners now often ask AI tools to compare local managers. A brand name helps less there than reviews, clear fee pages and third-party listings. See [how owners choose a property manager with AI](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai).

Before you decide, run your own fee and margin numbers with the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney, and have a franchise attorney read the FDD and agreement before you sign.

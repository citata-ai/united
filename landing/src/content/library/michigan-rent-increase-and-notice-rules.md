## Michigan rent and notice rules at a glance

Michigan leaves rent amounts to the lease, with few limits. Here are the rules we found in the 2025 Michigan Compiled Laws (read on Justia, 2026-10-19).

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| State cap on rent increases | None in the statutes we read | n/a |
| Local rent control | Banned for counties, cities, villages, townships | 123.411(2) |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | Notice equal to 1 rent period (1 month for monthly rent) | 554.134(1) |
| End a year-to-year tenancy | Ends 1 year after the notice | 554.134(3) |
| Notice to quit for unpaid rent | 7 days, in writing | 554.134(2) |
| Separate rent increase notice | None in the statutes we read | n/a |
| Rent increase as retaliation | A defense to eviction | 600.5720(1)(e) |
| Different price or terms by source of income | Banned for landlords with 5+ units (from 2025-04-02) | 554.601c |
| Damages for a source-of-income violation | Actual damages or up to 3 times monthly rent, whichever is less, plus fees | 554.601d(1) |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Does Michigan have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

Michigan has no rent control and no state cap in the statutes we read. MCL 123.411(2) says a local governmental unit "shall not enact, maintain, or enforce" an ordinance that would control the rent charged for private residential property. The term covers counties, cities, villages and townships (MCL 123.411(1)).

The ban has 2 limits:

- **Public property.** A local government may still manage and control housing in which it has a property interest (MCL 123.411(3)).
- **Voluntary incentives.** A local government may adopt a plan of voluntary incentives and agreements to add moderate- or low-cost rental housing (MCL 123.411(4)).

The last amendment to MCL 123.411 in the history note is 2018 Public Act 585, in effect 2019-03-28. We found no 2024 to 2026 change to the ban.

## How many days notice does a Michigan landlord need to raise the rent?

The Michigan statutes we read set no separate notice period for a rent increase. In a month-to-month tenancy, the timing comes from the notice to end the tenancy. MCL 554.134(1) lets either party end a tenancy at will with 1 month's notice. If rent is due at intervals under 3 months, notice equal to the interval between payments is enough.

So a practical approach for monthly rent is to give the new rent in writing at least 1 full rent period ahead. The tenant can then accept the new rent or leave.

Here is a worked example for a monthly tenancy with rent due on the 1st:

| Step | Date | Why |
|---|---|---|
| You deliver the written notice | 2026-11-30 | Notice equal to 1 rent period, MCL 554.134(1) |
| 1 rent period (1 month) ends | 2026-12-31 | The notice ends the tenancy at the end of a period equal to the rent interval |
| New rent, or tenant leaves | 2027-01-01 | Tenant accepts the new terms or moves |

MCL 554.134(1) also says a notice "is not void" because its end date does not match the start or end of a rent period. The notice still ends the tenancy at the end of a period equal to the rent interval. Many managers still pick the 1st of a month to keep the books clean.

Watch for: your lease may require more notice than the statute. Read the renewal and notice clauses before you send anything.

## Can a Michigan landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

A Michigan landlord can raise the rent during a fixed-term lease only if the lease allows it. A lease is a contract that sets the rent for its term. The statutes we read give no right to change the rent mid-term. The safe time to raise the rent is at renewal.

MCL 600.5714(1)(c)(ii) lets you recover possession when a tenant holds over after the term ends. So at the end of the term, you can offer a renewal at a new rent. If the tenant does not accept and stays, you may file a summary proceeding.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Michigan?

Notice equal to 1 rent period ends a month-to-month tenancy in Michigan. For monthly rent, that is 1 month (MCL 554.134(1)). Either party can give it. No reason is needed, with 2 exceptions:

1. **Local government housing.** The tenant is not holding over unless the tenancy ended for just cause (MCL 600.5714(2)).
2. **Mobile home parks.** The tenant is not holding over unless the lease ended for just cause under chapter 57a (MCL 600.5714(3)).

Other notice periods in MCL 554.134:

- **Unpaid rent:** a written 7-day notice to quit (554.134(2)).
- **Year-to-year tenancy:** the notice ends the lease 1 year after service (554.134(3)).
- **Drug clause with a police report:** a written 24-hour notice to quit (554.134(4)).

## When is a rent increase illegal in Michigan?

A rent increase is illegal in Michigan when it is retaliation or source-of-income bias. Both limits apply even without a rent cap.

Retaliation: MCL 600.5720(1)(e) blocks a judgment for possession in one case. You raised the tenant's obligations as a penalty for a lawful act. And the tenant's failure to pay the increase is the main reason for the eviction. Lawful acts include a code complaint to a government agency and membership in a tenant organization (MCL 600.5720(1)(b), (c)). If the tenant took official action within 90 days before you filed, the law presumes retaliation (MCL 600.5720(2)).

Source of income: MCL 554.601c was added by 2024 Public Act 178, in effect 2025-04-02. A landlord may not make any distinction in "price, terms, conditions, fees, or privileges" based on a tenant's source of income. Source of income includes housing choice vouchers, Social Security, veterans' benefits and public assistance (MCL 554.601(f)). The rule does not apply to a landlord, with related entities, of fewer than 5 rental units in Michigan (MCL 554.601c(2)).

A person can sue for a violation. Damages are actual damages or up to 3 times the monthly rent, whichever is less. Court costs and reasonable attorney fees come on top (MCL 554.601d, added by 2024 Public Act 199).

## Do cities in Michigan add their own rent or notice rules?

Michigan cities cannot control rent amounts, because MCL 123.411 bans it. Cities can still adopt voluntary incentive plans for moderate- or low-cost housing (MCL 123.411(4)). We did not review each city's code for other landlord rules, such as registration or inspection. Check the city where the unit is before you send a notice.

A short checklist before a Michigan rent increase:

- [ ] Is the lease fixed-term? If yes, wait for renewal unless the lease allows a change.
- [ ] Is the notice at least 1 full rent period ahead?
- [ ] Is the increase the same for tenants with vouchers or benefits as for others?
- [ ] Did the tenant file a code complaint in the last 90 days? If yes, document a lawful reason.

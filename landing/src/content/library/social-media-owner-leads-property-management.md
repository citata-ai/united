## What share of owner leads comes from social media?

Social media brings under 1% of owner leads to most property management companies in the best data we found. Goodjuju Marketing, a property management SEO agency, studied the GA4 data of 35 client companies. The fall 2026 window ran from July 8 to October 5, 2026.

Here are the channel numbers from that study, fall window:

| Channel | Share of traffic | Share of owner leads | Conversion rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search and Map Pack | 37.9% | 50.6% | 1.2% |
| Direct / Maps | 45.0% | 35.6% | 0.7% |
| Other search | 3.2% | 4.3% | 1.2% |
| Referral | 3.0% | 3.7% | 1.1% |
| AI tools | 0.8% | 3.4% | 3.9% |
| Paid search | 5.3% | 1.2% | 0.2% |
| Social media | 4.3% | 0.8% | 0.2% |

Social media sent more traffic than referral sites. It sent less than a quarter as many leads. In the spring window, social had 4.1% of traffic and 1.6% of leads. So the share of leads fell by half from spring to fall.

Read the data with 3 limits in mind:

- **Goodjuju sells SEO.** The agency says so on the page. It has a reason to favor Google.
- **Clients only.** The 35 companies are Goodjuju clients. They likely rank better in Google than the average manager.
- **Form leads only.** GA4 counts web form leads. An owner who saw your post, then called you, does not show up as a social lead.

Even with these limits, the gap is large. Social traffic converts at 0.2%. Google Search traffic converts at 1.2%, which is 6 times higher.

## Why does social traffic convert so badly?

Social traffic converts badly because the visitor is not looking for a manager at that moment. An owner who searches "property management near me" has a need now. An owner who scrolls past your post may own a rental but has no plan to hire.

Goodjuju's own words: social media "just isn't where owners go to choose a property manager." The data fits that view. People find managers by search, by map and by asking someone they trust.

There is a second reason. Much of a property manager's social audience is not owners. It is tenants, applicants, vendors and job seekers. That mix is useful, but it does not fill an owner pipeline.

## Which platforms do owners use?

Owners use the same platforms as most US adults, and YouTube and Facebook lead by far. We found no study of platform use by rental owners in particular. So we use the general US numbers from Pew Research Center.

Share of US adults who say they use each platform (Pew, survey ending 2026-06-18):

| Platform | US adults who use it |
|---|---|
| YouTube | 84% |
| Facebook | 70% |
| Instagram | 51% |
| TikTok | 37% |
| WhatsApp | 33% |
| LinkedIn | 31% |
| Snapchat | 26% |
| Reddit | 25% |
| Nextdoor | 18% |
| X (formerly Twitter) | 18% |

Reach is not the same as leads. Facebook reaches 70% of adults, but the Goodjuju data says social as a whole brings 0.8% of owner leads. Pick 1 or 2 platforms where your past clients already follow you. Do not try to be on all 10.

## What is social media good for if not leads?

Social media is good for proof, hiring and client retention. These are real jobs. They just do not show up in a lead report.

Use social for these 5 jobs:

1. **Proof for owners who already found you.** An owner who finds you on Google often checks your Facebook or LinkedIn page. Recent posts show that the company is active.
2. **Hiring.** Job posts and team photos help you hire leasing agents and maintenance staff.
3. **Current owners.** Market updates and rent trend posts remind owners why they pay you. A client who stays is cheaper than a new lead.
4. **Vacancy marketing.** Listing posts can reach renters. Follow Fair Housing rules in every ad and post.
5. **Referral partners.** Agents, lenders and contractors who see your work are more likely to refer owners.

Goodjuju says the same: social "still earns its keep for brand, recruiting and staying in front of existing clients."

## How much time should I spend on social media?

Spend time on social in proportion to what it returns. Here is a simple way to set the budget.

Worked example (hypothetical company, your numbers will differ):

| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Owner leads per month, all sources | 40 |
| Leads from social, at the study's 0.8% share | 0.32 per month |
| Marketing hours per week | 10 |
| Social share of hours, if matched to lead share | under 0.1 hours |

Matched to leads, social would get almost no time. That is too low, because social also does the jobs listed above. A fair middle is a fixed time box. For example, 1 hour per week for 2 posts and replies. Put the rest of the hours into the channels that send leads: your Google Business Profile, your website and referral partners.

Then check your own data. Use a "How did you first hear about us?" field on every owner form. Goodjuju recommends this step, and it catches leads that GA4 misses. After 3 months, compare social's share of leads to its share of your hours. Move hours if the 2 numbers are far apart.

## What should I do first if I want more owner leads?

Start with the channels that bring most owner leads. In the Goodjuju fall 2026 data, Google Search and the Map Pack brought 50.6% of owner leads. Direct and Maps brought another 35.6%.

A short order of work:

- **Google Business Profile.** Check categories, hours, photos and reviews.
- **Website.** Make one clear page per service area, with your fee table.
- **Referrals.** Ask agents, attorneys and vendors to send owners your way.
- **AI answers.** AI tools brought 3.4% of owner leads in the same study and converted best. Our guide on [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping) covers this.
- **Social.** Keep it small and steady.

Social media is a trust channel for property managers. Treat it that way, and measure it before you give it more time.

## Homeowners vs landlord insurance at a glance

Homeowners insurance protects a home you live in, your things and your family's liability. Landlord insurance protects a home you rent to others and the rent it earns.

| Item | Homeowners policy | Landlord (dwelling) policy |
|------|-------------------|----------------------------|
| Who lives there | You | Your tenant |
| Building | Covered | Covered (DP-1, DP-2 or DP-3) |
| Your belongings | Covered | Only items you keep there for the rental, in some forms |
| Tenant's belongings | Not covered | Not covered; tenant needs renters insurance |
| Liability | Usually included | Often an add-on (NC Department of Insurance) |
| Living costs if the home is unfit | Loss of use for you | Not needed; you do not live there |
| Lost rent after a covered loss | Not the purpose | Fair rental value or loss of rents coverage |

The rule of thumb: the policy follows who lives in the home.

## When does a homeowners policy stop covering?

A homeowners policy stops fitting the day a tenant moves in and you move out. It is written for an owner who lives in the home.

The North Carolina Department of Insurance says a dwelling fire policy is typically used for someone who does not make the property a primary residence. It lists rental properties, vacant homes and second homes as uses.

What to do:

1. **Call your insurer before the lease starts.** Tell it the date the tenant moves in.
2. **Ask what policy it requires** for a rented home.
3. **Get the change in writing.**
4. **Send the new declarations page to your lender.** Your mortgage likely requires it.

Why the lender cares: section 5 of the standard Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac mortgage (Illinois Form 3014) requires the borrower to keep the property insured in the types and amounts the lender requires. If the lender has reason to believe you failed to keep that coverage, it may buy coverage at your expense.

Watch for: a vacant gap. The NCDOI page notes a DP-2 condition: some water damage is covered only if the building was not vacant for 60 days before the loss. If the house sits empty between your move-out and the tenant's move-in, ask how your policy treats vacancy.

## What does a DP-3 policy mean?

DP-3 is the most complete of the three dwelling policy forms. The NC Department of Insurance calls it the special form.

The three forms, from the NCDOI page:

| Form | Name | Building covered for | Claim payment |
|------|------|----------------------|---------------|
| DP-1 | Basic form | Named perils: fire, lightning, internal explosion; more if extended coverage is added | Usually actual cash value |
| DP-2 | Broad form | More named perils, such as vandalism, windstorm, hail, falling objects and freezing | Typically replacement cost |
| DP-3 | Special form | All types of damage except listed exclusions ("open perils") | Check the policy; ask for replacement cost |

Under DP-3, NCDOI says, personal property is still covered on a named-perils basis. Common DP-3 exclusions include neglect, intentional loss, mold, rust, rot, constant leaks, earth movement and wear and tear.

Actual cash value (ACV) pays the cost minus wear. Replacement cost pays what it costs to fix or replace it new. On a 15-year-old roof, the gap can be large.

## What coverages should a landlord policy have?

A landlord policy should cover the building, your liability and the rent. The Idaho Department of Insurance lists the typical coverages:

- **Dwelling.** The structure, against losses such as fire, smoke, wind and hail.
- **Other structures.** Detached sheds, fencing and similar.
- **Fair rental value.** Pays if a covered loss forces tenants out and you cannot collect rent during repairs. Idaho notes it may not be standard, and you may pay extra.
- **Liability.** Protects you when you unintentionally injure a tenant or damage their property on your premises.
- **Personal property.** Some policies cover appliances and furniture you keep in the rental.

State Farm's rental dwelling policy lists similar parts: dwelling, personal property used to maintain the property, loss of rents and liability.

Add-ons to ask about:

- **Umbrella policy.** Extra liability above the landlord policy limit.
- **Water backup.** Often excluded unless you add it.
- **Flood and earthquake.** Usually separate policies.

## How much more does landlord insurance cost?

Landlord insurance often costs more than homeowners insurance for the same house, but the gap depends on your home and state. State Farm says actual premiums vary by the coverages you select, the amounts, deductibles and other factors.

How to compare quotes fairly:

| Check | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Same dwelling limit | Rebuild cost, not market value |
| DP-3, not DP-1 | DP-1 often pays only actual cash value |
| Liability limit | Match it across quotes |
| Fair rental value months | 6 vs 12 months changes the price |
| Deductible | A higher deductible lowers the premium |

The premium is a rental expense. IRS Publication 527 lists insurance among the common deductible rental expenses. If you pay for more than 1 year in advance, you deduct each year's part in that year.

## When must I switch?

Switch before the first day of the lease. Earlier, if the home will sit empty while you prepare it.

A switch checklist:

1. **Pick a date:** your move-out or the lease start, whichever comes first.
2. **Get 2 to 3 dwelling policy quotes** with the same limits.
3. **Add liability** or an umbrella.
4. **Ask about vacancy rules** between tenants.
5. **Cancel the homeowners policy** only when the new one starts, so you have no gap.
6. **Send proof to your lender.**
7. **Require renters insurance** in your lease, where your state allows it.

If you hire a manager, ask to be named on its vendors' insurance and ask the manager for its own coverage. Our [property management](/property-management) page explains how owners compare managers.

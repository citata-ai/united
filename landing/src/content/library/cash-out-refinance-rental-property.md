## How much equity can you take out?

Usable refinance cash equals the new loan minus the old payoff and transaction costs. Equity on a property website is not a lender commitment. Ask for a written loan amount based on the appraised value and your full file.

Fannie Mae's Occupancy Types guide applies separate loan pricing adjustments to investment properties. Your lender must also check the product rules. Do not use a primary-home offer as proof of the terms for a rental.

Ask the lender to list every condition before you pay for reports. Include title, loan history, ownership, credit, rent records, and reserve funds. Ask which costs you pay even if the loan does not close.

Here is an assumed example, not a loan offer:

| Cash calculation | Assumed amount |
| --- | ---: |
| New loan | $240,000 |
| Old payoff | $180,000 |
| Closing costs paid from proceeds | $8,000 |
| Usable cash | $52,000 |

The example leaves $52,000, not $60,000. Ask whether the lender also needs cash reserves outside closing. Fannie Mae's reserve guide does not count subject-property cash-out proceeds as an acceptable reserve source.

## How does a new rate change cash flow?

A refinance works only if the rental can carry the new total payment. Compare principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and required association charges. Also allow cash for vacancy, repairs, and future replacements.

Use the same rent and operating budget for the old and new loan. Otherwise a better rent forecast can hide the effect of more debt. Keep a separate column for the planned use of the cash.

In an assumed example, monthly cash after operating costs and debt falls from $700 to $350. That cuts annual cash available by $4,200. The new project must justify that cost, the closing fees, and its own risk.

Do not treat the cash received at closing as recurring income. The lender expects repayment under the new note. A longer term can lower a payment while extending the time you remain in debt.

The CFPB recommends comparing loan offers on a consistent basis. For a business-purpose rental loan, ask which disclosures apply and request an equivalent written cost breakdown. Check the rate lock, points, fees, term, and any prepayment charge. Compare offers from the same date when possible.

## Is refinance interest deductible?

The use of refinance proceeds matters for the rental interest deduction. IRS Publication 527 says interest tied to proceeds outside rental use generally is not a rental expense. The house securing the loan does not settle that question.

Keep the closing statement and a clear trail from the loan deposit to each payment. Separate money for the rental from money for personal spending. Ask your tax professional how to allocate interest before you move funds between accounts.

For a repair project, save the scope, invoices, and proof of payment. For another investment, save the transfer records and purchase documents. Do not classify every dollar as a rental expense because the loan sits on a rental house.

Loan costs need their own review. The cash budget shows what leaves your account. The tax return may treat the same payment differently. Ask for both explanations before you sign.

A deduction also does not make interest free. Compare after-tax costs only after your preparer confirms the treatment. Keep a conservative case that still works if the expected tax benefit is smaller or delayed.

## How does a rental HELOC compare?

A rental HELOC can preserve an existing first mortgage, but its terms can differ sharply from a refinance. A HELOC is a home equity line of credit. Ask each lender for the actual draw and repayment terms.

Compare whether the rate can change, when principal payments start, and whether the line can close or shrink. Ask about annual fees and minimum draws. Do not compare only the payment during the opening draw period.

A cash-out refinance replaces the existing mortgage. A HELOC usually adds another payment beside it. Put both debts into the same property budget. Include the later payment period when you stress-test the line.

Match the loan to the use. A project with an uncertain finish date needs cash flexibility. A project with uncertain returns needs a strict limit on debt. More available credit does not improve the project itself.

Use the [management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to include paid management in both cases. That expense matters if the new project leaves you less time to manage the existing rental.

## What should you decide before you apply?

Write a short use-of-funds plan with a maximum cost and a fallback. State what you will buy or repair, when it should finish, and how you will repay the debt.

Then test lower rent, a repair bill, and a delay in the new project. Check whether you could carry both the property and your own living costs. Keep required cash separate from the money you intend to invest.

Ask the lender for a final written breakdown before closing. Compare it with the first quote and resolve changes. Make sure you understand who receives each payment from the closing proceeds.

Proceed only when the project and the rental both work under the loan terms you can actually obtain. If the plan depends on another refinance later, include a case where that refinance never happens.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

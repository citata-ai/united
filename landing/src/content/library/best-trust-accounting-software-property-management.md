## What is trust accounting in property management?

Trust accounting tracks money held for owners or residents and identifies whose money it is. Your accounting design must make that ownership clear.

Rentvine's [trust accounting page](https://www.rentvine.com/features/trust-accounting) describes property and portfolio accounting, client-fund records and reconciliation. Use that description as a starting point for your accountant's requirements.

A bank balance alone cannot explain every client's position. Ask the product to show the detail beneath the total. Then trace a payment from tenant receipt through fees, bills and owner distribution.

Review reserves, deposits and unpaid amounts separately. Do not assume available bank cash equals distributable owner cash.

Your local rules and agreements determine the required account setup and treatment. This guide does not state a universal state-law requirement.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Have your accountant approve the design before you rely on automated distributions.

## Which tools do three-way reconciliation natively?

Rentvine explicitly describes native three-way reconciliation. The other pages reviewed here do not establish an equivalent native workflow merely by listing bank reconciliation.

Rentvine describes matching the bank statement, trust ledger and property ledgers. It also describes diagnostics for negative balances and reconciliation gaps. Treat those as vendor claims to test.

Ask every candidate to complete the same reconciliation. Include an outstanding check, deposit in transit and a correction. Ask where each item appears and how the system reaches its adjusted balance.

A bank-feed match is not the whole test. Matching a transaction can still leave a client allocation wrong. Ask the accountant to inspect both the bank result and client detail.

Use this acceptance checklist:

- **Bank:** statement balance and outstanding items are clear.
- **Ledger:** transaction totals trace to the books.
- **Clients:** detailed balances explain the client-fund total.
- **Exceptions:** differences appear rather than disappear.
- **Approval:** a responsible person reviews and records the close.

Keep the reports and explanation from the demo. Do not accept an unexplained adjustment just because the totals then match.

## Which tools pass a state audit?

No software choice alone establishes that your firm will pass an audit. Evaluate the product against the actual rules and records your reviewer needs.

A vendor may describe its software as audit-ready. That is not evidence that your staff have configured accounts, retained records or used approvals correctly.

Ask a qualified local adviser for a current requirement list. Give that list to each vendor. Request a demonstration and sample report for each item.

Then test the operating process. Who reviews exceptions? Who can change an owner's bank details? Who approves a transfer? Who checks the reviewer completed the work?

Keep product evidence separate from legal advice. A vendor's description of another state's practice does not define your obligations.

If the vendor promises a certification or external assurance, ask for the exact document and scope. Do not infer a state approval from a general marketing statement.

Choose only after the accountant can explain the setup and remaining manual controls.

## Can QuickBooks handle trust accounting?

Intuit documents a way to record property management transactions in QuickBooks Desktop. That is evidence of a workflow, not proof of every trust-control requirement.

Intuit's guide separates rental-property transactions from the management company's own transactions using separate company files. It describes tenant, property, owner and account setup, including deposit liabilities.

Do not apply the Desktop instructions to QuickBooks Online without checking the relevant product guidance. They are different products.

Ask your accountant to demonstrate the full reporting and reconciliation process. Identify any outside sheet, manual allocation or review step. Assign responsibility for those steps.

Compare that complete design with a specialist product. Include staff time, correction risk and report preparation. A familiar accounting interface can still require more process work.

Do not claim that QuickBooks cannot record client-related transactions. Also do not assume a general accounting guide guarantees your local trust-accounting fit.

Document the control design before choosing either approach.

## What reports should you prepare for a reviewer?

Prepare a traceable report package based on the reviewer's current request. The list below is a planning checklist, not a universal statutory list.

| Record | Purpose of the check |

Ask the vendor to export that package. Open the files outside the product. Check that report periods and property identifiers remain clear.

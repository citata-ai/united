## Which supports association accounting?

Both vendors advertise association accounting, so compare the reports and control steps your bookkeeper needs. A feature label alone cannot decide the better fit.

| Platform | Vendor-listed association scope | Price evidence | Demo focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buildium | Association accounting, violations, committee workflows | Association page starts at $62 monthly; confirm quote | Board payables and report access |
| AppFolio | Dues, homeowner access, violations, board approvals | HOA page: quote only; minimums apply | Approval trail and field work |
| Vantaca | Association-focused platform | Quote only on page reviewed | Match your association workflow |
| CINC Systems | Financial and community management tools | Quote only on page reviewed | Bank work and homeowner access |

Sources: the vendors' association pages, read on 2026-10-19. These are vendor-described capabilities, not results from a hands-on trial.

Give each vendor the same accounting sample. Ask for assessment charges, a payment, an unpaid balance, a vendor bill, and a bank reconciliation. Have your bookkeeper inspect the output.

Check the association boundary. Records and access should remain clear when a company manages separate communities. A combined dashboard should not hide which association owns a balance.

## How do they handle violations and architectural requests?

Both Buildium and AppFolio describe violation and architectural workflows. Choose by how the tool records evidence, routes decisions, and informs the homeowner.

Buildium lists committee voting for architectural requests. AppFolio lists architectural reviews with in-app messaging and mobile violation tracking. Those labels justify a demo, not a claim that their workflows are identical.

Use a sample request with an attachment and a missing detail. Ask the vendor to show the request, follow-up, committee review, decision, and final owner message.

Check who can change the decision and whether the history remains visible. A board member should not need to reconstruct a decision from a private inbox.

For violations, test a correction and a disputed record. Keep the evidence, status, and approved response together. Ask how the platform distinguishes an observed issue from an approved enforcement step.

Do not use automation to bypass your association's review process. Have the board's adviser check the process before live enforcement. This guide does not claim that any platform makes a notice legally valid.

## What does each cost for associations?

Get written association quotes from both vendors and compare the same scope. Do not apply a residential per-unit rate to an HOA without confirmation.

Buildium's association page shows starting packages. Its general pricing page directs association buyers to call. AppFolio's HOA page offers a quote and notes a 50-unit minimum plus a minimum spend.

Keep the date and package name beside each quote. The pages reviewed on 2026-10-19 do not establish the full cost of your particular board workflow.

Ask for subscription, setup, data migration, training, payment processing, printed mail, and partner costs. Include optional services that your process needs rather than only the base package.

Ask which terms change when communities enter or leave the portfolio. Confirm how the bill counts units and which minimums remain in force.

Compare a normal operating period and a busy assessment period. Use your own expected transaction volumes. Label any cost calculation as a planning estimate.

Do not assume that a free trial includes every quoted module. Test the package you intend to buy and keep the accepted scope with the price.

## How do they compare with Vantaca and CINC?

Add Vantaca and CINC when association work is your main operating need. Both market association-focused systems, but a category label does not prove a better fit.

CINC's solutions page describes financial management, homeowner portals, community tools, and voting. Vantaca presents a community association platform with implementation and accounting services in its product offering.

Ask the same people to review every demo. Your accounting lead, community manager, and board contact will notice different gaps. Give each reviewer a clear task rather than asking for a general impression.

Check the handoff between field work and board approval. Then check how the decision appears in reports and homeowner records. A fast field app can still leave a weak office process.

Ask who supports the bank connection and who resolves a failed payment import. Require a clear support route for issues involving another vendor.

Keep a pass list and an unresolved list. A platform should not win because its sales presentation covered more features than the competing demo.

## Can you mix rentals and HOAs in one account?

A mixed manager should verify both property types in the proposed account structure before signing. Shared access is useful only if financial and operating boundaries remain clear.

Do not turn every association into a rental building with renamed fields. Test a rental owner statement beside an association board report. Confirm that users see only their assigned properties and communities.

Check how the company adds a new portfolio, grants board access, and removes a former client. Ask whether archived records remain exportable after service ends.

Use this final buying sequence:

- **Define scope:** List rental and association workflows separately.
- **Test accounting:** Have your bookkeeper approve sample reports and reconciliation steps.
- **Test governance:** Complete a request, approval, and homeowner update.
- **Test access:** Restrict a board user to the correct community.
- **Price the work:** Compare quotes with the same transaction and service assumptions.
- **Plan the handoff:** Name data owners, training roles, and unresolved tasks.

Choose the platform that passes the required tasks with a clear total cost. Keep unsupported work visible before the contract starts.

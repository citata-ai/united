Ahrefs checked about 14 billion pages. It found that 96.55% of them get no traffic from Google at all (Ahrefs, 2023). So the honest answer to "does blogging help?" is: most blogging does not. A small set of posts does, and they share one trait. Each one answers a question that a buyer asks before a purchase.

For a property management company, the buyer is a rental owner. The question is about money, law or risk. That kind of post can still bring owner leads.

## What results can a PM blog bring?

A property management blog can bring owner leads from search, citations in AI answers, and trust on the sales call. The leads are the smallest part in volume and the biggest part in value.

Data from Goodjuju's fall 2026 study of 35 property management companies (Goodjuju, 2026):

| Channel | Share of owner leads | Conversion rate |
|---|---|---|
| Google Search and Map Pack | 50.6% | 1.2% |
| Direct / Maps | 35.6% | 0.7% |
| Other search | 4.3% | 1.2% |
| Referral | 3.7% | 1.1% |
| AI tools (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and others) | 3.4% | 3.9% |
| Social media | 0.8% | 0.2% |

Two lines matter for a blog. Google Search brings half of all owner leads, and a good post can rank for owner questions. AI tools bring a small share, but those visitors converted at 3.9%, the highest rate in the study. Goodjuju says AI tools reward the same work as Google: a strong site, reviews, accurate listings and useful content.

Goodjuju also warns that its numbers come from its own clients, who likely have better SEO than average. Read the table as a sample, not as the market.

## How many posts are needed before results?

No study gives a fixed number of posts for property managers. Plan 2 to 4 owner answer pages per month for 6 months. That gives you 12 to 24 pages grouped into 2 or 3 topics.

Google says the length of a page alone does not matter for ranking, and there is no magic word count (Google Search Central). The same logic applies to post count. Ten sharp answers beat 50 thin posts.

Google also says some changes show in hours and others take several months. Plan a 6-month test:

| Month | Pages written | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | 4–8 | Pages indexed in Search Console |
| 3–4 | 4–8 | First owner queries in Search Console; AI test prompts |
| 5–6 | 4–8 | Owner leads that started on a post; AI citations |
| 6 | Review | Keep, fix or stop |

## Which posts get cited by AI tools?

AI tools cite posts that give a direct answer in the first lines, with numbers and a named source. They cut pages into chunks and quote the clearest chunk.

Our own test shows this pattern. On 2026-10-18 we ran 20 prompts about property management marketing in ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude. The most cited sources were agency blogs and guides: clearleaddigital.com in 22 answers, firstpagesage.com in 15, gogoodjuju.com in 13 and propertymanagerwebsites.com in 11 (our data, 2026-10). These sites publish many answer-style pages.

For owner questions in one city, the cited sources differ. In our baseline for a Chicago manager, AI answers cited manager websites, Yelp (35 answers), Expertise.com (22) and Thumbtack (17). The manager is Fridman Properties, whose owner is CEO of MyRemotely, our partner. See the [Fridman Properties case study](/case-studies/fridman-properties).

Post types that tend to get cited:

- **Cost answers.** "What does property management cost in [city]?" with a fee table.
- **Local law answers.** Deposit limits and notice periods, with the statute named.
- **Calculations.** Vacancy cost, rent-vs-sell, return on a rental, with the math shown.
- **Comparisons.** Flat fee vs percent fee, self-manage vs hire, with a table.

Google's people-first guidance asks if the content shows first-hand expertise (Google Search Central). A local manager has it. Put your own days-on-market, turn costs and court timelines on the page.

## When is a blog a waste of money?

A blog is a waste of money when it publishes for volume, not for owners. Watch for these signs:

1. **Tenant or homeowner topics.** "10 spring cleaning tips" brings visits that never become owner leads.
2. **No local facts.** A post that could run on any city's site gives AI tools no reason to pick yours.
3. **Copied or spun text.** Google calls copying others' content "a different story" from harmless duplicate URLs.
4. **No tracking.** If nobody checks owner leads per post, you cannot tell good posts from bad.
5. **No updates.** A fee or law post from 2023 with old numbers can hurt trust.

## How do I check if my blog works?

Check the blog by owner leads and AI citations, every month. Three steps:

1. **Owner leads per post.** In GA4, look at the landing page for each owner form lead.
2. **First-heard answer.** Ask every new owner how they first heard about you. Goodjuju found GA4 missed about 4 in 10 AI-referred leads it could trace (Goodjuju, 2026).
3. **AI test prompts.** Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode 10 owner questions each month. Write down if the answer names or links your site.

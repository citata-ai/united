Google Search and the Map Pack gave 50.6% of owner leads in the Goodjuju Marketing fall 2026 study of 35 property management companies. Add "Direct / Maps", which includes clicks on the website button of a Google Business Profile, and Google's local results gave 86.2%. So the profile matters. The question is which parts of it matter, and posts and photos get more credit than the evidence gives them.

## Do Google Business Profile posts and photos help a property manager rank?

Posts and photos help a property manager win the click more than they help it rank. Google's help page "Tips to improve your local ranking on Google" says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and prominence."

| Factor | What Google says | Where posts and photos fit |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | How well the profile matches the search; give complete, detailed info | Category, services and description matter more |
| Distance | How far the business is from the searcher | You cannot change this with content |
| Prominence | How well known the business is; links and review count | Reviews and links matter more |
| Photos | Listed under "Add photos & videos" on the same page | A named way to improve the profile |
| Posts | Not named on that page | Trust and conversion, not a named ranking factor |

Google also says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. So treat posts as a sales tool. An owner who compares 3 managers in the Map Pack reads your latest post, sees your team, and decides who to call.

## What should a property manager post on its profile?

A property manager should post facts an owner can use. Google's posts help page lists three post types: updates, offers and events. Each can carry a photo, a video and a button with a link.

Good post ideas for an owner audience:

1. **Monthly rent snapshot.** "Median 3-bed rent in our area this month, from our own listings." Use your real data only.
2. **New law reminder.** A short note on a state or city rule change, with a link to the source.
3. **Team spotlight.** One person, their role, a photo.
4. **Owner FAQ.** "What does a leasing fee cover?" Answer in 3 lines, link to your fee page.
5. **Seasonal checklist.** Winter pipe checks, smoke alarm tests.
6. **Event.** A free owner workshop, with start and end times.

Avoid posts that rank applicants or describe the "ideal tenant" by family status, age or any protected class. Fair Housing rules apply to posts too.

Google's content tips for posts: keep the text professional, skip auto-generated filler, and link only to trusted sites. A post with a phone number in the text can be rejected if Google cannot connect that number to the business.

## How often should I post on Google Business Profile?

Post at least once a month, and 2–4 times a month if you can keep it up. Google sets no required pace. But its help page says "posts older than 6 months are archived unless a date range is set." A profile whose newest post is a year old tells an owner the company does not watch it.

You do not have to post by hand each week. Google lets you schedule a post and set it to repeat weekly, monthly or on a custom schedule. A manager with several offices can post to several profiles at once.

### A 4-week plan

| Week | Post type | Topic | Button |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Update | Local rent snapshot | Learn more → rental analysis page |
| 2 | Update | Team spotlight with photo | Call |
| 3 | Update | Owner FAQ: your fees | Learn more → fee page |
| 4 | Event or update | Workshop or seasonal checklist | Sign up or learn more |

That is 4 posts and about 1 hour a month for one person.

## Which photos build owner trust?

Real photos of your people and your work build owner trust. Google's business photo guidelines give a minimum for each type. The types that fit a property manager:

- **Team photos:** at least 3. Google says to show yourself and your staff "to highlight your personalities."
- **Exterior photos:** at least 3. Your office and sign, from each direction an owner would arrive.
- **Interior photos:** at least 3, if owners visit your office.
- **Photos at work:** at least 3. An inspection, a maintenance visit, a move-in walk-through.

Technical rules from the same page: JPG or PNG, 10 KB to 5 MB, 720 × 720 px recommended, 250 × 250 px minimum. Google asks for photos that are in focus, well lit and without "excessive use of filters or AI." Photos show only on a verified profile.

Photo checklist:

- [ ] 3+ team photos, with names in the captions where staff agree
- [ ] 3+ office exterior photos, sign visible
- [ ] 3+ photos at work, no tenant faces or unit numbers
- [ ] Owner consent for any photo of a managed home
- [ ] No stock photos and no AI-made images

## Can I see results in Google Business Profile insights?

You can see profile-level results, but not post-level leads. The Performance report, per Google's help page, shows:

- **Views:** unique people who viewed the profile on Search and Maps, counted once a day.
- **Searches:** the search terms that showed your profile, updated at the start of each month.
- **Calls:** clicks on the call button.
- **Website clicks:** clicks on the website link.
- **Directions** and **messages**.

The report includes views and actions from both organic results and Google Ads. Performance data needs a verified profile.

To tie posts to leads, add UTM tags (tracking labels in a link) to each post button. Then Google Analytics shows "gbp-post" as its own source. Goodjuju makes the same point about the main website link: without a tag, Map Pack clicks show up as plain "google / organic". Our [guide to tracking AI and search traffic](/guides/track-ai-traffic-property-management) shows a tagging setup.

### Worked example

Before posts: 1,200 views, 40 website clicks, 18 calls in a month. After 3 months of weekly posts and new photos: 1,250 views, 55 website clicks, 24 calls.

- Website click rate: 40 ÷ 1,200 = 3.3% before; 55 ÷ 1,250 = 4.4% after.
- Calls rose by 6 with almost the same views.

Those are example numbers. If your own numbers move like this, posts and photos help conversion even when rank stays the same. If nothing moves after 3 months, spend that hour on reviews instead. For the bigger picture, read [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping).

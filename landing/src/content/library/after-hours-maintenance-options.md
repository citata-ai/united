## What does an answering service cost?

An answering service needs a scope-based quote; there is no single price for all maintenance coverage. Ask whether the bill measures minutes, calls, units, or a base plan with overages.

Define the task before collecting prices. Taking a message is different from confirming urgency or finding an available plumber. Ask which actions require a separate coordination service.

Use your call log to build a cost request. Include repeat calls, unanswered transfers, language needs, and calls about existing work orders. Do not price only the first contact.

Ask about holidays, overflow, recordings, setup, and extra users. Make the vendor identify what happens when a call lasts longer than expected.

Compare a normal month and a busy weather event using your own assumed volumes. Label those figures as planning assumptions. Avoid treating a low advertised entry price as the cost of full repair coverage.

## What do Latchel and similar services do?

Latchel describes both front-office response and maintenance coordination. Its current page separates Frontline from Autopilot maintenance work and describes a human support layer.

HappyCo's pricing page also describes service choices, including after-hours coverage through Happy Force. Confirm the exact product and included work in the proposal.

Ask each provider to demonstrate the same request. Start with an unclear leak report, add a failed vendor call, and then request an owner update. Inspect the final record in your PMS.

Keep vendor availability separate from answering availability. A service can answer a call without having a qualified contractor nearby. Confirm who maintains the roster, insurance records, and access instructions.

Clarify who approves extra work and who tells the resident when help will arrive. A smooth intake means little if the request stops at an unaccepted assignment.

## Can AI take emergency calls?

AI can collect details and route a call, but emergency handling needs a tested process beyond intake. Do not make a general model the final authority on resident safety.

Latchel describes AI troubleshooting with expert human support. AppFolio describes maintenance intake, prioritization, and dispatch through Maintenance Performer. These are vendor descriptions, not an independent safety test.

Use controlled test calls with ambiguous symptoms, interruptions, background noise, and a request for a person. Check whether the handoff preserves the address, callback number, and issue.

Stop troubleshooting when the issue may involve immediate danger. Follow your approved emergency instructions and local emergency-service guidance. Do not require a resident to complete a long script before reaching help.

Ask for an outage plan. Your tenants need a working contact path if the AI service, phone system, or PMS connection fails.

## What counts as a true emergency?

Your emergency policy should separate immediate danger, urgent property damage, and routine repair. Local duties and property conditions affect the response. A universal online list cannot replace that review.

Use possible fire, suspected gas, uncontrolled water, and unsafe electrical conditions as escalation scenarios in training. These examples do not set legal deadlines or diagnose a specific incident.

Have qualified staff and local counsel approve the policy. Keep the resident instructions short and easy to find. Do not tell residents to attempt hazardous repairs.

For each scenario, name the contact route, person responsible, spending authority, and fallback vendor. Record what staff must do when nobody accepts a dispatch.

Review incidents after closure. Check whether the intake category matched the actual issue, and update the script when needed.

## Which option fits different portfolio sizes?

A portfolio of 50, 150, or 400 doors can need very different coverage. These are planning scenarios, not thresholds backed by a vendor study.

At a smaller portfolio, named staff coverage may work if someone is reliably available. Test illness, leave, and simultaneous calls before relying on a single person.

As calls grow, an answering service may protect staff time while your team keeps dispatch control. Review whether the handoff causes repeat calls or lost work.

A larger or scattered portfolio may benefit from managed coordination when the vendor roster and PMS connection work. Door count alone does not prove the service is worth its cost.

Choose with your actual night-call log. Count staff time, failed transfers, repeat contacts, and unresolved requests. Require a morning report that clearly shows what still needs action.

## How should you test the choice before you pay?

Test the full workflow with sample records before you move live data. Use the same case in each product. Ask the person who will do the daily work to run the test. A sales demo alone does not show where your team will need help.

Keep a decision sheet with the task, result, missing step, and person who checked it. Mark each result as shown, promised, or still open. Ask for written terms when a feature needs an upgrade or another service. Include staff setup time in your cost review.

Check the exit path while you still control the choice. Export the sample records and open them outside the platform. Confirm that notes, files, dates, and links remain useful. Ask how you will get records after you close the account.

Choose a tool when the test proves a clear gain for your workflow. Keep your current process until the new process passes. Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to see how software costs affect your service margin.

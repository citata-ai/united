## How do you calculate cap rate from NOI and price?

You calculate cap rate by dividing a property's yearly net operating income by its price or current value. Buildium's dictionary gives the formula:

- Cap rate = NOI ÷ current market value (or purchase price)

Buildium's example: $40,000 of yearly NOI on a property worth $500,000 is an 8% cap rate. The property returns 8% of its value each year from operations.

NOI is income from the property minus operating expenses. Northspyre says NOI leaves out loan payments, capital spending, depreciation and income tax. So cap rate is a return before financing.

Worked example for a single-family rental. The numbers are an example.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Rent collected for the year | $2,000 × 11 months (1 month vacant) | $22,000 |
| Other income | Late fees, pet rent | $300 |
| Operating expenses | Tax, insurance, repairs, management, HOA | $9,860 |
| **NOI** | $22,300 − $9,860 | **$12,440** |
| Purchase price | Offer price | $250,000 |
| **Cap rate** | $12,440 ÷ $250,000 | **4.98%** |

You can also run the formula backward to test a price:

- Value = NOI ÷ cap rate
- At a 6% cap rate: $12,440 ÷ 0.06 = $207,333
- At a 5% cap rate: $12,440 ÷ 0.05 = $248,800

A one-point change in the cap rate moves the value by about $41,500 here. That is why small cap rate changes matter so much.

## What is a good cap rate for rentals?

A good cap rate is one that pays you enough for the risk of that property in that market. There is no single good number. Buildium says a higher cap rate often points to a better return, if location and condition are also good.

Read the cap rate as a trade-off:

| Cap rate | What it often means | Questions to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Higher than similar homes nearby | More income per dollar of price | Why is it cheap? Condition, tenant risk, location, vacancy |
| Same as similar homes | Priced at market | Is the NOI real? Check the rent roll and expenses |
| Lower than similar homes | Paying more for each dollar of income | Is there rent growth or a value-add plan to justify it? |

For context, CRE Daily's report on CBRE's H1 2026 survey shows all-property cap rates near 6.6%. For stabilized multifamily in New York City, cap rates moved from 4.5%–5% in H2 2025 to 5%–5.5% in H1 2026. These are commercial survey numbers. A single-family rental in your town can differ a lot.

Check the NOI before you trust the cap rate. A seller's NOI with no vacancy, no repairs or no management fee will show a high cap rate that is not real.

## Why do cap rates change by market?

Cap rates change by market because investors price risk, growth and interest rates differently in each place and for each kind of property. A market with steady demand and rent growth gets a lower cap rate. A market or building with more risk gets a higher one.

What moves cap rates, with 2026 examples:

- **Interest rates.** When borrowing costs rise, buyers need more income per dollar, so cap rates tend to rise. CRE Daily reports the 10-year Treasury peaked at 4.67% in May 2026.
- **Rent growth outlook.** CBRE's H1 2026 brief says investors were more concerned about rising multifamily cap rates. CBRE ties this to softer rent growth expectations and higher rates.
- **Property class.** CBRE says more investors expect higher cap rates for Class C assets. It points to larger capital needs and more leasing risk.
- **Market mood.** In CBRE's H1 2026 survey, about 63% expected no change in cap rates over 6 months. That is up from about 53% in H2 2025. About 20% expected compression, down from about 42%.

## What is the difference between cap rate and cash-on-cash return?

Cap rate measures the property. Cash-on-cash return measures your money in the deal, after the loan. Use cap rate to compare properties. Use cash-on-cash return to compare your own financing choices.

Corporate Finance Institute defines cash-on-cash return as total cash earned divided by total cash invested. Cash invested is the down payment plus fees, not the borrowed money.

Continue the example. You buy the $250,000 home with a 25% down payment and $7,500 of closing costs. The loan payment is an example.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Cash invested | $62,500 down + $7,500 costs | $70,000 |
| NOI | From above | $12,440 |
| Mortgage payments | $1,000 × 12 (example) | − $12,000 |
| Yearly cash flow | $12,440 − $12,000 | $440 |
| **Cash-on-cash return** | $440 ÷ $70,000 | **0.63%** |
| **Cap rate** | $12,440 ÷ $250,000 | **4.98%** |

The same house has a 4.98% cap rate and a 0.63% cash-on-cash return. The loan payment takes almost all the NOI. With an all-cash purchase, cash-on-cash return equals the cap rate (ignoring closing costs). With a cheaper loan, cash-on-cash return rises.

| | Cap rate | Cash-on-cash return |
|---|---|---|
| Formula | NOI ÷ price | Cash flow after loan ÷ cash invested |
| Includes the mortgage? | No | Yes |
| Best for | Comparing properties | Comparing your deal structure |
| Changes with financing? | No | Yes |

## How can a property manager change the cap rate?

A property manager cannot change the price, but can raise NOI, and a higher NOI raises the cap rate on the price you paid. In the example, $1,000 more NOI a year moves the cap rate from 4.98% to 5.38%. Shorter vacancy, renewals instead of turnovers and bid-out repairs all add to NOI.

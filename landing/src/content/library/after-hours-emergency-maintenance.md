## What counts as a maintenance emergency?

An emergency maintenance plan should put immediate threats to people first. Active damage and loss of essential services also need prompt review. The correct response depends on the facts, weather and local rules.

The EPA advises people who suspect a gas leak or damaged gas pipe to leave the area. It says to call local authorities or 911 from a safe location. Put that safety instruction ahead of your repair intake form.

Use the following table as a starting point for staff training. Have local safety and legal advisers check the final policy.

| Report | First response | Next management step |
|---|---|---|
| Suspected gas leak | Leave and contact emergency services from safety | Record official instructions and notify staff |
| Active water escape | Keep people clear of unsafe areas | Dispatch a qualified vendor |
| Unsafe electrical condition | Keep people away from the hazard | Contact emergency services or qualified help as appropriate |
| Loss of heat or cooling | Assess conditions and reported danger | Apply the local response plan |
| Cosmetic fault | Record the request | Schedule routine review unless facts change |

Do not ask residents to diagnose electrical, gas or structural faults. A call handler needs enough facts to route help safely. Technical repair advice belongs with qualified people.

Write the policy in plain language. Explain how residents can reach a person if the portal fails. Never make an owner approval delay the only path for reporting danger.

## Should I use an in-house team or an answering service?

Choose the coverage model that can receive calls, assess urgency and confirm real help. An answering service may take messages without arranging repairs. A staff rotation may offer judgment but still lack a backup.

Map the full process before you compare offers. Who answers? Who has authority to dispatch? Who follows up when the vendor declines? Who checks that the resident received an update?

Property Meld's maintenance preparation guide recommends planning for common faults and keeping emergency contacts ready. Use that advice to build a current vendor list by trade and service area. A name without confirmed night coverage is not a backup plan.

Ask a service provider to walk through a realistic call. Include an unreachable owner, unavailable vendor and caller who cannot use the portal. Check whether the service handles those conditions or simply sends an email.

For an internal rotation, name the lead and backup in the shared schedule. Give each person approved access to property contacts, spending authority and current work orders. Check labor and pay rules with your adviser before you set the rotation.

Keep a handover note when shifts change. An unresolved leak should not become a new case with no history when another person answers.

## What does after-hours coverage cost?

After-hours coverage cost depends on call handling, staff pay, vendor rates and the work included. Get written quotes for your own service area. There is no verified universal price in this guide.

Build a comparison from the same scope. Include the base charge, usage charges, dispatch charges, staff review time and any extra software. Keep repair invoices separate so you can see coverage cost without hiding the cost of actual work.

For a planning example, assume a service costs $300 a month plus $10 for each handled call. At 20 calls, the example cost is $500. These are invented inputs for calculation, not vendor prices.

That example excludes repairs, staff follow-up and tax. If the provider only records messages, add the time your team spends deciding and dispatching. Compare that total with a staffed plan that covers the same duties.

Review missed calls and repeat contacts as well as price. A cheap call plan that cannot reach a vendor leaves your team with the same unresolved work. Ask each provider what happens during a local storm when several properties call together.

Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to test whether your fee covers the chosen service. Explain any owner charges before they appear on a statement.

## Which vendor rules should cover nights and weekends?

After-hours vendor rules should state the approved work, charge terms, access process and escalation route. Confirm the rules before the first dispatch.

Give each vendor a property contact route and a method for safe access. Share only the data needed for the job. Ask vendors to report arrival, findings, immediate work and any further repair proposal.

Separate making the property safe from full replacement work. Set spending authority in the management agreement and vendor instruction. Have counsel check the local duties and emergency exceptions that may affect that authority.

Ask for the night rate, minimum charge, travel charge and cancellation terms in writing. Do not present an estimated amount as the final invoice. Record approval when the scope changes.

Require evidence that fits the work: notes, photos where appropriate, parts used and an itemized bill. Check whether the resident can confirm that the reported issue has stopped. A vendor's completed status may only mean that the first visit ended.

Keep a backup vendor for each critical trade where possible. Confirm availability rather than assuming the daytime supplier will answer. Record who owns follow-up when parts or further work are needed.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How should I test and review the response plan?

Test the whole response path with a practice call and a sample property. Tell the participating staff that the case is a drill. Do not trigger emergency services for a test.

Check whether the number works, the handler finds the property and the backup accepts responsibility. Verify that the work order preserves the original report and each handoff. Review what happens when internet access fails.

After a real event, separate the safety response from the service review. Ask what caused delay, whether the resident knew the next step and whether the owner received a clear cost explanation. Update the plan when the review finds a gap.

Start with your current after-hours contact list. Confirm every role and phone route before you promise coverage to another owner.

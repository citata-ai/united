## Why would a manager leave AppFolio?

A manager should leave AppFolio when a verified work problem costs more than the move. Possible reasons include a poor fit at your size, a missing connection or difficult reporting. These are buying tests, not claims about how often customers leave.

Write down the exact task that fails. Your accountant may need a report by owner and property. Your leasing team may need to pass showing data into another tool. Ask AppFolio to show the task before you assume a replacement will solve it.

Record the work time and errors involved. Separate a missing feature from a feature your team has not learned. A training fix may cost less than a system change.

Give every replacement the same task and source data. A polished demo with sample records can hide the hard part. Ask the person who performs the work to judge the result. Keep their notes with the buying decision.

## Which alternatives avoid AppFolio's unit minimum?

Yardi Breeze explicitly states no unit minimum, but a monthly charge still applies. AppFolio's Core page states a 50-unit minimum. Its spend minimum is a separate condition.

Buildium's pricing page does not state a minimum unit count. That is not a promise that every buyer qualifies. Ask sales to confirm your portfolio size and business type.

Rentvine's minimum-unit FAQ does not give a number. Treat eligibility and minimum spend as quote only. Do not replace that gap with a figure from an old comparison.

For a small firm, the right test is cash paid before growth arrives. Ask for the bill at your current signed door count. Add a quote for the portfolio you expect to manage. Label that growth as your forecast, not a promised outcome.

A low per-unit rate can still produce a large bill when a minimum applies. Compare the full subscription before you divide by doors.

## Which alternative is closest in features?

The closest alternative reproduces your required work with the fewest manual steps. Buildium, Rentvine, Yardi Breeze and DoorLoop each describe broad property management functions. Their labels do not prove equal depth.

Build a short acceptance checklist:

- **Post rent.** Check the charge, payment, receipt and owner balance.
- **Handle a return.** Reverse the payment and follow the unpaid amount.
- **Pay a vendor.** Check approvals, property coding and the payment record.
- **Report to owners.** Include reserves, fees, bills and distributions.
- **Close the books.** Explain outstanding items and every balance difference.
- **Export records.** Open the files outside the product.

AppFolio's API access differs by plan, according to its pricing page. An API is a software connection. If a connection drives your decision, test the required read or write action. A vendor logo does not confirm the detail of a data transfer.

## What does switching really cost?

Switching costs include the new bill, setup, staff time and overlap with your old system. Ask each vendor to separate these costs. Do not assume the subscription covers a full history import.

Buildium's onboarding page names property, unit, lease, owner, resident and vendor data. It does not promise that every AppFolio attachment or transaction transfers. Ask for the exact scope in your contract.

Use a cost worksheet with these rows:

| Cost | What to request |
|---|---|
| Subscription | Current doors, minimum and billing term |
| Payments | Incoming, outgoing, returns and bank setup |
| Leasing | Screening, signatures and listing tools |
| Move | Import, cleanup, documents and training |
| Exit | Notice period, export and archive access |

Add your own staff estimate. Keep vendor fees separate from your assumptions. A useful quote shows what changes when you add or lose a managed property.

## How hard is it to move data off AppFolio?

The move depends on export scope, data quality and the new vendor's import process. Do not accept a general promise that everything transfers.

Ask for a sample export before you cancel. Inventory leases, attachments, owner records, tenant balances, deposits, unpaid bills and work orders. Confirm how recurring charges and payment instructions will be rebuilt.

Test an owner with several properties and a tenant with an unpaid balance. Compare source records with imported records. Have your accountant approve opening balances.

Keep the old system available for reference under an agreed access plan. Choose a single system for live payments after the cutover. Duplicate payment instructions create confusion.

Send owners and tenants a clear notice with the portal, start date and support contact. Tell them how to verify a message before changing payment details.

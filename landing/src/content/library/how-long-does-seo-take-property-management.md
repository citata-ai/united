## What changes in months 1 to 3 of property management SEO?

Months 1 to 3 change the parts you control: site fixes, new owner pages, a complete Google Business Profile and working lead tracking. Google's SEO Starter Guide says every change takes some time to show on Google's end. Some take effect in a few hours; others could take several months. Google suggests you wait a few weeks before you judge whether a change helped.

So use the first 90 days to finish work you can check, not to wait for rankings.

| Month | Work | How you check it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Search Console and GA4 set up; lead forms tagged; GBP link tagged with UTM codes | Search Console shows data; a test lead shows its source |
| 1 | Fix crawl errors, slow pages, missing titles | Search Console Pages report |
| 2 | Owner service page, fee page, one page per core city | Pages show as indexed in Search Console |
| 2 | Google Business Profile complete: category, service areas, photos, hours | Profile shows all fields |
| 3 | Review requests to current owners; listings fixed | New reviews; one name, address and phone everywhere |
| 3 | First owner questions answered as articles | Impressions start for long-tail owner terms |

Tracking comes first for a reason. Goodjuju Marketing's 2026 lead source report found that Google Business Profile clicks show as plain "google / organic" in GA4 unless you tag the profile link. Without that tag you cannot tell Map Pack leads from website search leads later.

## When do property management rankings usually move?

Rankings for small, specific searches usually move first. Rankings for broad big-city terms move last. Google does not publish a timeline, and Google's page on hiring an SEO tells you to ask any agency what results to expect and in what timeframe. Ask that question, and get the answer in writing.

The order is predictable even when the dates are not:

1. **Your brand name.** Searches for your company name should show your site and profile first within weeks of a fix.
2. **Long-tail owner questions.** "How much does property management cost in [suburb]" has few competitors.
3. **Small cities and suburbs.** "Property management [suburb]" near your office.
4. **Map Pack for your home city.** Driven by your profile, reviews and distance.
5. **"Property management [big city]".** The hardest term, with the most competitors.

Search Console shows this order. Its Performance report gives clicks, impressions, click-through rate and average position. The default view covers the past three months. Watch impressions for owner terms. Impressions usually rise before clicks do.

## What makes SEO slower in big cities?

Big cities are slower because more competitors have more of what Google calls prominence. Google's local ranking help page names three main factors: relevance, distance and prominence. Prominence is how well known a business is, based on facts like links to the business and the number of reviews.

A new company cannot add years of reviews and links in a quarter. In a big city, the top 3 Map Pack results often belong to firms with hundreds of reviews. You can match their relevance in a month. You cannot match their prominence that fast.

Demand also moves under you. In our own data for a Chicago-area property manager (a related party: its owner is CEO of our partner MyRemotely), Google Keyword Planner showed search demand for 7 generic property management terms fell 46% in the Chicago area and 43% across the US year over year. We treat AI search as a possible cause, not a proven one. See the [Fridman Properties case study](/case-studies/fridman-properties). A falling market means fewer searches to win, so the same rank can bring fewer leads.

### Big city or small city: what to expect

| Factor | Small city or suburb | Big city |
|---|---|---|
| Competitors in Map Pack area | Few | Many |
| Reviews needed to compete | Dozens | Often hundreds |
| First long-tail rankings | Sooner | Sooner (long-tail is still open) |
| Broad "property management [city]" term | Reachable | Slow, may never reach top 3 |
| Best first target | Home city term | Suburb pages and owner questions |

## What can speed up property management SEO?

Nothing makes SEO instant, but some work pays off sooner than other work.

- **Fix tracking before content.** You need to see which page brings owners.
- **Complete the Google Business Profile.** Google says complete and accurate profiles are more likely to show in local results.
- **Ask every current owner for a review.** Use the same honest request for all; never pay for reviews.
- **Write for owner questions, not tenant questions.** Owner pages bring owner leads.
- **Target suburbs first.** Win "property management [suburb]" before the big-city term.
- **Fix listings.** Use one name, address and phone on every directory.
- **Avoid agencies that promise a date.** Google's "Do you need an SEO?" page warns that an irresponsible SEO can damage your site.

Do not judge the work by traffic. Goodjuju's fall 2026 data shows why: Direct and Maps traffic was 45.0% of visits but 35.6% of leads, because many visits are tenants and vendors. Count owner leads and signed doors by source. Our guide to [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping) explains what else changes owner demand.

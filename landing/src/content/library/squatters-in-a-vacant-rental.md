## How do you secure a vacant rental?

You secure a vacant rental by making it hard to enter and making it look cared for. Squatters look for units that seem forgotten. A unit with mail piled up, a dead lawn and dark windows sends that signal.

Use this checklist on the day the unit goes vacant:

- [ ] Rekey or recode every exterior lock.
- [ ] Lock every window and sliding door. Add a bar to each slider.
- [ ] Lock the garage, the shed and any crawl space or basement door.
- [ ] Put 2 or more lights on timers, in rooms you can see from the street.
- [ ] Keep the lawn cut and the walk clear.
- [ ] Forward the mail and stop any deliveries.
- [ ] Post a "No trespassing" sign where your state allows it.
- [ ] Install a doorbell camera or a motion light at the main door.
- [ ] Tell 1 or 2 neighbors the unit is empty and give them your number.
- [ ] Take dated photos of every room and every door.

Your lockbox and showing codes are a weak spot. Give each prospect a one-time code. Expire it after the visit. Never leave a key in a lockbox that anyone with an old code can open.

Watch your listing as well. The FTC warns that scammers copy the photos and text of real rental listings and repost them with their own contact. Some fake landlords list homes they do not own. A renter who pays a scammer may then arrive with a forged lease and a key copy. Search your address on the big listing sites each week while the unit is vacant.

## How often should you check a vacant property?

Check a vacant property at least once a week, and after every storm. A weekly visit catches a broken window, a leak or a new occupant before it gets worse. Vary the day and the time so the pattern is hard to read.

Your insurance policy adds a reason. Many property policies limit cover after a long vacancy. The ISO HO 00 03 homeowners form, quoted by PropertyCasualty360, excludes vandalism "if the dwelling has been vacant for more than 60 consecutive days immediately before the loss." Landlord policies use their own forms. Read the vacancy clause in your policy and ask your agent.

A weekly check, step by step:

1. **Drive by first.** Look for new cars, open windows, lights you did not set, or trash.
2. **Walk the outside.** Check doors, windows, the garage and the meter.
3. **Go inside.** Look for bedding, food, new locks, or moved furniture.
4. **Check utilities.** A jump in water or power use can mean someone lives there.
5. **Log it.** Date, time, photos and what you found.
6. **Leave a sign of care.** Pick up flyers, move the timer times, rake the leaves.

If you live far away, pay a local manager or a trusted person to do the visit. A written log matters more than who does it.

## What is the difference between a squatter and a tenant?

A tenant lives in the unit with the owner's permission. A squatter enters and stays without it. The difference decides which legal process you must use, so get it right before you act.

New York says this plainly. RPAPL 711 states: "A tenant shall not include a squatter." It defines a squatter as a person who "enters onto or intrudes upon real property without the permission of the person entitled to possession" and stays without title, right or permission.

The line can blur in 3 cases:

- **A former tenant or guest who stayed.** That person entered with permission. Most states treat this as a tenant or holdover case, not a squatter case.
- **A renter with a fake lease.** The person may truly believe they rent the unit. Police may treat it as a civil matter until the lease is shown to be fake.
- **A person who pays you.** If you take money for rent, you may create a tenancy. Do not take money from an occupant until you get advice.

Here is how 3 states handle unauthorized occupants.

| State | Rule | What the owner does | Key limits | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Florida | Sheriff may remove unauthorized persons without a court case | File a verified complaint with the county sheriff | Occupants are not current or former tenants, not family, and no case is pending | Fla. Stat. 82.036 |
| Georgia | Unlawful squatting is a misdemeanor | Report to police; occupant gets 3 business days to show papers | If papers are shown, a hearing within 7 days | O.C.G.A. 16-7-21.1, added by HB 1017 (2024) |
| New York | A squatter is not a tenant | Use the process for non-tenants with police or the court | Tenant status still applies to lawful occupants | RPAPL 711 |

Florida's statute lists 8 conditions the owner must meet. Among them: the property is a dwelling, the persons entered unlawfully, the owner told them to leave, and they are not current or former tenants. The sheriff must then serve a notice to vacate "without delay" and put the owner in possession. The owner signs under penalty of perjury. A person removed by mistake can sue for damages, including 3 times the fair market rent.

Georgia's HB 1017 gives the cited person 3 business days to show "properly executed documentation," such as a lease or proof of rent payments. If the person cannot, the person is subject to arrest. If the papers fail at a hearing, the court can order removal and add a fine based on fair market rent. This text is from the 2024 act as passed. Check the current Georgia code before you rely on it.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Who do you call first?

Call the police first. Do not call a locksmith, and do not go in alone. A person who lives in your unit without permission may claim a lease, and some will fight.

Steps once you find someone inside:

1. **Leave and stay safe.** Do not argue at the door.
2. **Call the police non-emergency line**, or 911 if you see a break-in or a threat.
3. **Bring proof of ownership.** A deed or a tax bill, plus your ID.
4. **Bring your vacancy log and photos.** They show the unit was empty and when.
5. **Ask for a report number.** You need it for the sheriff, the court and your insurer.
6. **Call your insurer.** Report the entry and any damage.
7. **Call a local attorney.** Ask which route your state allows: police removal, a sheriff complaint, or an eviction or ejectment case.
8. **Do not cut utilities or change locks** while a person is inside, unless the police or a court tells you to.

Act fast. Each day a person stays makes the claim of tenancy look stronger and the damage larger.

Florida evictions run under Part II of Chapter 83, the Florida Residential Landlord and Tenant Act. I read each section below in the 2026 Florida Statutes on the Florida Senate site on 2026-10-19. The process is fast on paper. Most delays come from bad notices, so the notice step gets the most space here.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice for unpaid rent | 3 days, excluding Saturday, Sunday and court holidays | § 83.56(3) |
| Notice for a curable lease breach | 7 days to cure | § 83.56(2)(b) |
| Notice for a non-curable breach | 7 days to vacate | § 83.56(2)(a) |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | At least 30 days before the end of a monthly period | § 83.57(3) |
| Where to file | County court of the county where the unit is | § 83.59(2), § 34.011 |
| Tenant answer | 5 days after service (summary procedure) | § 51.011(1) |
| Rent into the court registry | Within 5 days, excluding weekends and holidays, or default | § 83.60(2) |
| Filing fee, removal of tenant | Up to $180 | § 34.041(1)(a)8 |
| Writ of possession | Sheriff acts after a 24-hour posted notice | § 83.62(1) |
| Penalty for a lockout or utility shutoff | Actual damages or 3 months' rent, whichever is greater | § 83.67(6) |

## What notice must a Florida landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

A Florida landlord must give a written 3-day notice that demands the rent or possession. Section 83.56(3) excludes Saturday, Sunday and legal holidays from the 3 days. Only court-observed holidays count as legal holidays.

The notice must follow the statutory form in substance. It states the amount of rent owed, the address including the county, the deadline date, and the landlord's name, address and phone number.

Delivery rules come from § 83.56(4):

- Mail it, or hand a true copy to the tenant.
- E-mail it, if the tenant agreed under § 83.505.
- If the tenant is absent, leave a copy at the residence.

The lease cannot waive these notice rules.

Watch for: partial rent. If you accept partial rent after you post the notice, § 83.56(5)(a) gives you 3 choices. Give a receipt with the new balance and due date, put the partial rent in the court registry when you file, or post a new 3-day notice.

## How long does an eviction take in Florida?

Florida law does not set a total eviction time. It sets fixed periods for each step. Here is the minimum chain of statutory periods for a rent case:

| Step | Statutory period | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 3-day notice runs | 3 business days | § 83.56(3) |
| File the complaint | Same day the notice expires, at the earliest | § 83.59(2) |
| Service of the summons | Depends on the process server or sheriff | Not set by statute |
| Tenant response and rent deposit | 5 days, excluding weekends and holidays | § 83.60(2) |
| Default judgment if no rent deposit | "Immediate" | § 83.60(2) |
| Writ posted, sheriff executes | 24 hours; weekends do not stay it | § 83.62(1) |

Add those up and the shortest uncontested path is about 2 to 3 weeks on the calendar, plus service time. That is my estimate from the periods above, not a court figure. A tenant who deposits rent and raises a defense gets a hearing, and that adds time. Section 83.59(2) tells the court to advance the case on its calendar.

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Florida, and what does it cost?

A Florida landlord files a complaint for possession in the county court of the county where the unit is (§ 83.59(2)). Under § 34.011(2), the county court has exclusive jurisdiction over the right of possession, unless the money claim is above its limit.

Who may file: the landlord, the landlord's attorney, or the landlord's agent. An agent who is not an attorney may file the initial complaint only. After that, the agent may take no other action (§ 83.59(2)).

Costs:

- **Filing fee.** Fla. Stat. § 34.041(1)(a)8 sets up to $180 for a removal of tenant action, plus a separate $4 fee under § 34.041(1)(b).
- **Rent claim.** A claim for unpaid rent in the same case may carry its own fee by amount.
- **Service and writ.** The sheriff or a process server charges for service. Ask your county sheriff for current fees.

When rent is owed and service was personal, the court also enters a money judgment for the rent (§ 83.625). The prevailing party may get attorney fees and costs.

## Can a Florida landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

A Florida landlord can evict for a lease violation after a 7-day notice. Section 83.56(2) splits violations into 2 types:

| Type | Examples in the statute | Notice |
|---|---|---|
| Curable | Unauthorized pets, guests or vehicles; bad parking; not keeping the unit clean | 7 days to fix it, or the lease ends |
| Non-curable | Intentional damage to property; fraudulent entry; repeated unreasonable disturbance; repeat of a warned breach within 12 months | 7 days to vacate |

The statute gives the wording for each notice.

Florida has no "just cause" rule in Chapter 83. A landlord can end a month-to-month tenancy with written notice at least 30 days before the end of a monthly period (§ 83.57(3)). Week-to-week tenancies need 7 days. A fixed-term lease ends on its end date. A tenant who stays after that without permission owes double rent for the holdover period (§ 83.58).

One limit applies everywhere. Section 83.64 bans retaliatory eviction, for example after a tenant complains to a government agency in good faith.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Florida?

The sheriff removes the tenant in Florida. After judgment for the landlord, the clerk issues a writ of possession. The sheriff posts a 24-hour notice on the premises, then puts the landlord in possession (§ 83.62(1)). Saturdays, Sundays and holidays do not stay the 24 hours.

When the sheriff executes the writ, the landlord may move the tenant's personal property to or near the property line (§ 83.62(2)). The landlord can ask the sheriff to stand by while the locks change. The sheriff may charge an hourly rate for that.

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Florida?

Self-help eviction is illegal in Florida. Fla. Stat. § 83.67 bans these acts:

- Cutting off or interrupting any utility, such as water, heat, power, gas or garbage, even if the landlord does not control it.
- Changing the locks or using a bootlock to keep the tenant out.
- Removing doors, locks, the roof, walls or windows, except for repair.
- Removing the tenant's property, except after surrender, abandonment, or a lawful eviction.

The penalty is the greater of actual and consequential damages or 3 months' rent, plus attorney fees (§ 83.67(6)). Each later violation can bring a separate award.

## Florida eviction checklist

1. **Check the rent ledger** before you serve. The notice states the sum owed "for the rent and use of the premises".
2. **Count the 3 days** without weekends and court holidays.
3. **Keep proof of delivery.**
4. **File in county court** with the notice attached.
5. **Watch the 5-day window** for the rent deposit.
6. **Ask for the writ** after judgment and coordinate with the sheriff.

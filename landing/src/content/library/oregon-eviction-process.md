## Oregon eviction rules at a glance

Oregon splits eviction law between 2 chapters. ORS chapter 90 sets the notices. ORS 105.100 to 105.168 set the court case, called forcible entry and detainer (FED).

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Nonpayment notice, option A | 10 days, given on day 8 or later | ORS 90.394(2)(a) |
| Nonpayment notice, option B | 13 days, given on day 5 or later | ORS 90.394(2)(b) |
| Week-to-week nonpayment notice | 72 hours, given on day 5 or later | ORS 90.394(1) |
| Rental assistance notice | Attach to every nonpayment notice and summons | ORS 90.395(2) |
| Lease violation notice | 30 days, with at least 14 days to cure | ORS 90.392(3), (4) |
| Serious acts (violence, major damage) | 24 hours | ORS 90.396 |
| Extra time if mailed only | Add 3 days | ORS 90.155(2) |
| Filing fee | $88 | ORS 105.130(2) |
| First appearance, nonpayment | 15 days after the judicial day after fee payment | ORS 105.135(2) |
| First appearance, other causes | 7 days after the judicial day after fee payment | ORS 105.135(2) |
| Trial, nonpayment | 15 to 30 days after first appearance | ORS 105.137(6) |
| Notice of restitution | 4 days to move out | ORS 105.151 |
| Writ expiry | 30 days after issue | ORS 105.161(3) |

## What notice must an Oregon landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

An Oregon landlord must give a written nonpayment notice under ORS 90.394. For a monthly tenancy, you pick one of 2 options:

- **10-day notice,** given no sooner than day 8 of the rental period.
- **13-day notice,** given no sooner than day 5 of the rental period.

Day 1 is the day rent is due. ORS 90.394(3) says the notice must state the amount due and the date and time to pay.

ORS 90.395 adds a second paper. You must attach the Oregon Judicial Department's rental assistance notice to every nonpayment termination notice. If you skip it, the court must dismiss the case under ORS 90.395(3).

The court must also dismiss if the tenant tenders rental assistance or other payment for the amount in the notice. That rule also covers a landlord who fails to work with a rental assistance program.

### Worked example: rent due on the 1st

| Option | Earliest notice date | Notice period | Earliest termination |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10-day, personal delivery | Day 8 | 10 days | End of day 18 |
| 13-day, personal delivery | Day 5 | 13 days | End of day 18 |
| 13-day, first class mail only | Day 5 | 13 + 3 days | End of day 21 |

ORS 90.160 counts days from the day after service. A period ends at 11:59 p.m. on its last day.

## How long does an eviction take in Oregon?

A contested Oregon nonpayment case takes about 2 to 3 months by the statutory minimums. An uncontested case can end much faster. Court calendars and sheriff workloads add time.

| Stage | Minimum time | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Nonpayment notice | 10 or 13 days (+3 if mailed only) | ORS 90.394 |
| Filing to first appearance | About 15 days | ORS 105.135(2) |
| First appearance to trial | 15 to 30 days | ORS 105.137(6) |
| Notice of restitution | 4 days | ORS 105.151 |
| Sheriff serves writ | After the 4 days end | ORS 105.159, 105.161 |

If the tenant does not come to the first appearance, the landlord gets a default judgment under ORS 105.137(1). The landlord must still show up and testify the tenant remains in the unit.

For other causes, the first appearance is 7 days out. The trial comes within 15 days of that appearance.

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Oregon, and what does it cost?

An Oregon landlord files an FED case in the county where the unit is. ORS 105.110 allows the case in circuit court or before a justice of the peace in that county.

The filing fee is $88 under ORS 105.130(2). The clerk may also collect any other fee that law or ordinance allows. A tenant who wants a trial pays $88 too, under ORS 105.130(3).

Steps to file:

1. **Prepare the complaint** in the form ORS 105.124 sets.
2. **Pay the fee.** The clerk sets the first appearance and fills in the summons.
3. **Serve the tenant.** By the next judicial day, the clerk mails the papers and a process server serves them at the unit.
4. **Attach the rental assistance notice** to the summons for a nonpayment case.
5. **Come to the first appearance.** If you miss it and the tenant comes, the case is dismissed.

A nonattorney agent or employee may appear for the landlord, under ORS 105.130(4).

## Can an Oregon landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes for a lease violation. No-cause eviction is limited to the first year of occupancy.

For a lease violation, ORS 90.392 requires a 30-day notice. If the tenant can fix the problem, the notice must give at least 14 days to cure. A repeat of the same violation within 6 months allows a 10-day notice with no right to cure.

For serious acts, ORS 90.396 allows a 24-hour notice. Examples are serious threats of injury, injury to a person on the premises, or intentional substantial damage.

For no cause, ORS 90.427 allows a 30-day notice only in the first year of occupancy. After that, the landlord needs a tenant cause or a qualifying landlord reason, such as a sale to a buyer who will live there. A qualifying reason needs 90 days' notice. Most landlords must also pay 1 month's rent, unless they own an interest in 4 or fewer units.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Oregon?

The county sheriff removes the tenant. The landlord cannot do it.

ORS 105.151 sets 2 steps after a judgment for the landlord:

1. **Notice of restitution.** The clerk issues it. The tenant gets 4 days to move out and take all property.
2. **Writ of execution.** After the 4 days, the clerk issues the writ. The sheriff serves it, removes the tenant and returns the unit to the landlord.

The landlord must ask for these papers within 60 days after judgment, under ORS 105.159(3). A writ that the sheriff does not serve within 30 days expires, under ORS 105.161(3). After removal, ORS 105.161 makes the landlord responsible for property left behind.

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Oregon?

No. Self-help eviction is illegal in Oregon. ORS 105.105 says any entry must be peaceable and without force. ORS 90.375 sets the penalty.

If a landlord removes or locks out a tenant, or cuts heat, water, hot water or power, the tenant can:

- Get a court order to return to the unit; or
- End the lease and recover 2 months' rent or twice actual damages, whichever is greater.

The landlord must then return all deposits and prepaid rent.

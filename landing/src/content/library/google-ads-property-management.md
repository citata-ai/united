## Which Google Ads campaign type works best for owner leads?

A Search campaign works best for owner leads, because it shows your ad to a person who types a need. Google describes Search as "text ads on search results" and says the type suits sales, leads and site traffic.

Here is how the main campaign types fit a property management company.

| Campaign type | What Google says it does | Fit for owner leads |
|---|---|---|
| Search | Text ads on search results | Best start. You pick keywords and see each search term. |
| Performance Max | All Google channels from one campaign, with Google AI | Later, after you have clean conversion data. Less control. |
| Display | Visual ads on sites, apps and YouTube | Weak. Reach, not intent. |
| Video | Video ads on YouTube and other sites | Brand only. |

Source: Google Ads Help, "Choose the right campaign type".

Start with one Search campaign and one ad group per service: full management, tenant placement, and your Section 8 or HOA work if you sell it. A small budget spread over five campaigns learns nothing.

## How do I target the right area?

Target your real service cities or a radius around your office, and set the location option to Presence. Google has two options:

- **Presence or interest (the default):** people who are in your area, or who showed interest in it.
- **Presence:** people who are likely to be in, or regularly in, your area.

Google says Presence suits advertisers who want to exclude people who are interested in the offer but are located elsewhere. That fits a local service. One trade-off: an out-of-state owner with a rental in your city may search from far away. If those owners matter to you, test a second campaign with Presence or interest and compare the leads.

Watch for: you cannot use ZIP codes as an audience. Since October 19, 2020, Google bars housing, employment and credit ads in the US and Canada from targeting by "gender, age, parental status, marital status, or ZIP code". Google lists "real estate services" as a housing example. Use cities, counties or a radius instead. This rule also protects you under Fair Housing: you do not pick who sees the ad by who they are.

## Which keywords bring owners, not tenants?

Use keywords that only an owner types, and block the rest. "Property management" alone draws renters, job seekers and software buyers.

Google has three match types. Broad match is the default and reaches the most searches. Phrase match uses quotes. Exact match uses square brackets and gives the most control. Start with phrase and exact match on owner terms.

| Keep (owner intent) | Block (negative keywords) |
|---|---|
| "property management company" + city | apartments, apartment |
| "property manager for my rental" | for rent, rentals, rental near me |
| "rental management services" | jobs, job, salary, career |
| "tenant placement service" | software, app, course, license, exam |
| "how much does a property manager charge" | pay rent, tenant portal, login |
| "manage my rental property" | section 8 apartments, low income housing |

Google says negative keywords "won't match to close variants". So "apartments" does not block "apartment". Add both forms. Check the search terms report each week and add new negatives from it.

The last row matters. A renter who types "section 8 apartments" is not your lead for an owner ad. Block the term; do not write ad text that turns any renter away by who they are.

## Which ad copy attracts owners, not tenants?

Ad copy attracts owners when the first headline names the owner and the job. "Property Management for Rental Owners" filters better than "Best Property Management".

Use this checklist for each ad:

- **Name the reader:** "For rental owners", "For landlords in Austin".
- **Name the fee:** if you publish it, show it. "8% monthly, no fee until rented" turns away price shoppers who would not sign.
- **Name one proof:** doors managed, years in business, or review count, with real numbers.
- **Name one next step:** "Get a free rental analysis" or "See our fee table".
- **Send the click to an owner page:** a city page or a pricing page, not the home page with a tenant login button on top.

Do not promise rent amounts or vacancy times you cannot prove. Ad claims must be true.

## How do I track leads from ads?

Track each owner form and each call as a conversion before you raise the budget. Google lets you measure website actions, calls from ads, calls to the number on your site, and offline sales you upload later.

Set up three conversion actions:

1. **Owner form submit.** Fire it on the thank-you page of the owner form only. Tenant and maintenance forms must not count.
2. **Calls from ads.** Google counts a call as a conversion when it lasts longer than a minimum length that you set. Pick a length that removes wrong numbers and tenant calls.
3. **Signed owner.** Upload the deal as an offline conversion when the owner signs. This tells Google which clicks became doors.

Without step 3, the system learns to buy cheap forms, and many cheap forms come from renters. Our guide on [tracking AI and other lead sources](/guides/track-ai-traffic-property-management) shows how to tag the source of each lead.

## How much should I test with?

Test with a budget that buys enough clicks to judge, then judge on signed doors. Here is a worked example. The numbers are placeholders, not a benchmark. Put in your own.

| Step | Example input | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly test budget | $1,500 | |
| Cost per click (from your account) | $6 | 250 clicks |
| Owner form or call rate | 5% | 12 owner leads |
| Close rate on owner leads | 20% | 2 to 3 new owners |
| Doors per new owner | 2 | 4 to 6 doors |
| Cost per new door | | $250 to $375 |

Then compare the cost per door with what a door earns you in a year. The [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) helps you set that number.

For context, paid search is a small source today. In the Goodjuju fall 2026 study of 35 companies (July 8 to October 5, 2026), paid search brought 1.2% of owner leads. Only 6 of the 35 companies ran it, and the authors say the number "is not a benchmark for paid search". Google Search and Map Pack brought 50.6% of leads in the same window.

## What to do this week

1. **Build one Search campaign** with Presence targeting on your service cities.
2. **Add 30 or more negative keywords** from the table above.
3. **Set up owner-only conversions** for forms and calls.
4. **Send clicks to an owner page** with your fees on it.
5. **Review the search terms report** every Monday for 4 weeks.

Ads stop when you stop paying. Your Google Business Profile and site keep working. Read [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping) before you move budget from one to the other.

## What do you need before the one-hour audit?

You need a browser, a timer and a sheet with 4 tabs: prompts, listings, site, score. Log out of your accounts, or use a private window, so past chats do not shape the answers. Pick one city you serve and one owner type, such as single-family landlords.

This audit is a first look, not a full study. AI answers change between runs. One hour gives you a baseline and 3 fixes. If you want the full technical list, our [AI search checklist for property management websites](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website) covers it.

## Which prompts should you run? (minutes 0–20)

Run 5 owner prompts in 3 engines: ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity. That gives 15 answers. Use the words an owner uses, not the words you use.

| # | Prompt (replace the city) | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the best property management company in [city]? | Short list for the main question |
| 2 | Best property manager in [city] for a single-family rental | Fit for your main owner type |
| 3 | How much do property managers charge in [city]? | Whether your fees get quoted |
| 4 | Is [your company] a good property management company? | What AI says about your brand |
| 5 | [Your company] reviews | Which review sites AI reads for you |

For each answer, write down 3 things:

- **Named:** is your company in the answer? At which position?
- **Cited:** does the answer link your site? Which other sites does it link?
- **Correct:** are your facts right? Check the name, phone, city, fees and door count.

Prompts 4 and 5 are brand prompts. Most firms show up there. Prompts 1 to 3 are the real test, because the owner does not know your name yet.

## Which listings should you check? (minutes 20–35)

Check the 6 sources that AI cites most for this question. In our run of 50 city prompts on ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, Expertise.com was a source in 93 of 150 answers. Yelp and iPropertyManagement were sources in 50 each. Reddit followed with 22, ThreeBestRated with 21 and BBB with 14 (our data, 2026-10).

| Source | AI answers that cited it (of 150) | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Expertise.com | 93 | Are you on the list for your city? |
| Yelp | 50 | Claimed? Category, hours, 5 newest reviews |
| iPropertyManagement | 50 | Listed? Reviews and service area correct? |
| Reddit | 22 | Threads that name you, and what they say |
| ThreeBestRated | 21 | Are you on the list for your city? |
| BBB | 14 | Profile claimed? Complaints answered? |

Also open your Google Business Profile. Check the name, address, phone, category and website link. Our full results by city are in the [AI Property Manager Index 2026](/research/ai-property-manager-index-2026).

Spend 2 minutes per source. Do not fix anything yet. Write down what is wrong.

## Which site checks should you run? (minutes 35–50)

Run 7 checks on your own site. Each one takes about 2 minutes.

1. **Open your robots.txt.** Go to yoursite.com/robots.txt. Look for rules that block OAI-SearchBot. OpenAI says this crawler surfaces sites in ChatGPT search, and sites that block it do not show in ChatGPT search answers (OpenAI).
2. **Search your site in Google.** Type site:yoursite.com. Google says a page must be indexed and eligible for a snippet to show as a link in AI Overviews or AI Mode. Google also says there are no extra requirements (Google Search Central).
3. **Find your fee page.** Can an owner see your management fee, leasing fee and other fees on one page? AI answers can only repeat fees they can read.
4. **Find a page for your main city.** Does one page say which city you serve and which homes you manage?
5. **Compare your contact facts.** Is the name, address and phone on your site the same as on Google and Yelp?
6. **Read your About page.** Does it state your founded year, door count and service area in plain words? AI answers repeat these facts.
7. **Test your owner form.** Does it ask "How did you hear about us?" with an option for AI tools?

## How do you score the AI visibility audit? (minutes 50–60)

Score each part, then add up. The total is out of 100 points.

| Part | How to score | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Named in non-brand answers | 4 points per answer that names you, prompts 1–3, 9 answers | 36 |
| Cited (site linked) | 2 points per answer that links your site, prompts 1–3, 9 answers | 18 |
| Facts correct | 3 points per brand answer with no wrong fact, prompts 4–5, 6 answers | 18 |
| Listings | 2 points per profile in good shape, 6 sources plus Google Business Profile | 14 |
| Site checks | 2 points per check passed, 7 checks | 14 |
| **Total** | | **100** |

The score is your own baseline. It is not an industry benchmark. Its value is the trend: run the same audit next month and compare.

## What should you fix first after the audit?

Fix wrong facts first, then listings, then pages. A wrong phone number in an AI answer costs you calls today. A missing city page costs you less this week.

Pick 3 fixes, one from each group:

- **Facts:** correct one wrong fact at its source, such as a Yelp or Google profile.
- **Listings:** claim or complete the top listing you are missing.
- **Site:** add or update the fee page or the main city page.

Write the date and the 3 fixes in your sheet. In 30 days, run the same 5 prompts and fill a new score. Our guide on [how to track AI traffic](/guides/track-ai-traffic-property-management) shows how to add analytics to this monthly check.

## What does this one-hour audit not cover?

The one-hour audit does not cover every engine, city or owner type. It runs each prompt once, and AI answers change from run to run. It also skips deep technical work: schema, page speed, server logs.

Treat the score as a signal, not proof. If one engine names you and two do not, that is normal. If no engine names you for prompts 1 to 3, start with listings. In our data, AI answers for this question lean on directories and review sites more than on company sites.

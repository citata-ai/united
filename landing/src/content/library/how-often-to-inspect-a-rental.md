## Move-in, routine, move-out: what is the difference?

A rental gets 3 main kinds of inspection: move-in, routine and move-out. Each one has a different job.

| Inspection | When | Purpose | Tenant present? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Move-in | Before or on the first day | Record the starting condition | Yes, best practice and required in some states |
| Routine | While the tenant lives there | Find leaks, damage and safety issues early | Optional, with notice |
| Drive-by | Any time, from outside | Check the roof, yard, windows and parking | No entry, no notice |
| Pre-move-out | 1 to 2 weeks before move-out | Show the tenant what to fix | Yes, on request in some states |
| Move-out | After the tenant leaves | Compare to move-in and set deductions | Often allowed |

Some states write the move-in and move-out steps into law:

- **Arizona:** at move-in, the landlord must give the tenant a move-in form to note existing damage. The landlord must also tell the tenant in writing that they may be present at the move-out inspection (A.R.S. 33-1321(C)).
- **New York:** the landlord must offer an inspection before the tenant moves in. If the tenant asks, both sign a written list of existing defects. After either side gives notice to end the tenancy, the tenant may ask for a pre-move-out inspection. It happens no earlier than 2 weeks and no later than 1 week before the end, with at least 48 hours' written notice (GOL 7-108).

## How often should you inspect an occupied rental?

Inspect an occupied rental at least once a year. Add one at 6 months for a new tenant. No federal law sets a routine schedule for private rentals, so this is our suggested schedule, not a legal rule.

| Unit and tenant | Suggested routine inspection | Why |
|---|---|---|
| New tenant, first year | At 3 to 6 months, then at 12 | Catch habits early, before a renewal decision |
| Long-term tenant, clean record | Once a year | Leaks and wear still happen |
| Pets in the unit | Every 6 months | Floors, doors and yards wear faster |
| Older building or old systems | Every 6 months | Plumbing, roofs and wiring fail with age |
| Section 8 unit | Your own yearly check, plus the PHA inspection | The PHA inspects at least every 2 years |

Pair each visit with a seasonal task. For example, change HVAC filters and test alarms on the same visit.

## How much notice must you give?

You must give the notice your state requires, and you must enter at reasonable times. Notice rules differ by state.

| State | Notice | Hours | Law |
|---|---|---|---|
| Florida | At least 24 hours for repairs | 7:30 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. | Fla. Stat. 83.53(2) |
| Arizona | At least 2 days | "Reasonable times" | A.R.S. 33-1343(D) |

Both states let the landlord enter without notice in an emergency. Both also say the landlord shall not abuse the right of access or use it to harass the tenant. In Arizona, a tenant's repair request counts as permission to enter for that repair (A.R.S. 33-1343(B)).

Too many visits can become harassment. Keep routine visits to a schedule you can explain, and put the schedule in the lease.

## Is once a year enough?

Once a year is enough for many stable tenancies, but not for all. A slow leak under a sink can do damage for months before anyone sees it. A tenant cannot report what they do not see.

Use a 6-month visit when the risk is higher:

- The tenant is new to you.
- The unit has pets.
- The building is old, or the water heater or roof is near the end of its life.
- The last visit found a problem.

Section 8 units follow a federal schedule as well. Under 24 CFR 982.405, the housing authority must inspect before the lease starts and "at least biennially" after that. Small rural housing authorities inspect once every 3 years. If a tenant reports a life-threatening problem, the housing authority inspects within 24 hours, and the owner must repair it within 24 hours. A non-life-threatening problem gets an inspection within 15 days and a 30-day repair window. A yearly owner inspection helps you pass these with no surprises. ## Do inspections help insurance claims?

Inspections help insurance claims because they give you dated proof of the unit's condition before a loss. An insurer may ask when you last saw the unit and whether the damage was sudden or slow. A dated photo set and a written report answer both questions.

What to keep from each inspection:

1. **Dated photos** of each room, under each sink and of the water heater.
2. **A written report** with the date, who attended and what you found.
3. **Repair records** that show you fixed what you found.

Ask your insurer what proof it wants. Policies differ, so we do not claim a specific result.

## What do routine inspections look for?

A routine inspection looks for 4 things: safety, water, damage and lease compliance. Use this checklist on each visit.

- **Smoke alarms:** test each one. The U.S. Fire Administration says to test alarms monthly and replace the whole alarm every 10 years. It also says to put alarms inside and outside each sleeping area and on every floor.
- **Carbon monoxide alarms:** test each one, where installed.
- **Water:** look under sinks, around toilets and tubs, at the water heater and on ceilings.
- **HVAC:** check or change the filter.
- **Pests and mold:** look for droppings, stains and musty smells.
- **Damage:** holes, broken doors, cracked windows, damaged floors.
- **Safety:** railings, stairs, outlets, exterior lights, locks.
- **Lease terms:** unapproved pets or occupants, and smoking where it is not allowed.

Note what you find, send the tenant a short summary, and schedule repairs. If you would rather not run this yourself, a [property management company](/property-management) can do it on a fixed schedule.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Why do agents refer owners to managers?

Agents refer owners to a property manager because it keeps the client in the agent's circle. An agent meets two kinds of owners who need a manager:

- **The owner who cannot sell now.** The price is too low or the owner must move fast, so the owner rents the home out.
- **The investor who buys a rental.** The agent sold the property and the buyer asks, "Who can manage it?"

Fourandhalf, a marketing agency for property managers, puts it plainly: "Realtors care about ongoing business more than they care about a one-time referral fee." The agent's real fear is a manager who later sells the home through a different agent. So your program must answer that fear first. The fee comes second.

## How do I promise to send buyers and sellers back?

Put the promise in a one-page written agreement that the agent can show the owner. Fourandhalf advises clear contract terms to ease the agent's worry about losing the client.

Here is a sample of what the page can say. Adapt it and check it with your broker and lawyer.

> **Our promise to referring agents**
> 1. We manage. We do not list or sell homes for owners you refer.
> 2. When your referred owner wants to sell, we tell the owner to call you first, and we tell you the same day.
> 3. When your referred owner wants to buy another rental, we send the owner to you.
> 4. We send you a short update when the home is rented, and once a year after that.
> 5. If we pay a referral fee, we pay it to your broker, as state law requires.

If your company also has a sales team, say how you keep the two apart. An agent will not refer to a manager who competes for the listing.

## What referral fee is normal?

No public standard exists for a property management referral fee. Fourandhalf mentions "$200 or $300" as an example of a fee that is small next to an agent's sales commission, and advises you to sell the program's value, not the fee.

Use a worked example to set your own number:

| Input | Example value |
|---|---|
| Rent | $2,000 per month |
| Management fee | 8% = $160 per month |
| Leasing fee | 50% of 1 month = $1,000 |
| First-year revenue from the door | $1,000 + (12 x $160) = $2,920 |
| Flat referral fee | $250 |
| Fee as share of first-year revenue | 8.6% |

The numbers are an example, not a market rate. Put in your own fee and rent. The [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) helps you see what a door earns.

## Who can I pay, and how?

You can pay only in the way your state's real estate license law allows. The rules differ by state. Here are three examples from the statute text.

| State | Rule | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Texas | A broker may not pay a person for an act of a broker unless the person is a license holder or a broker licensed in another state. A sales agent may accept pay only from the sponsoring broker. | Texas Occupations Code 1101.651(a) and (b) |
| Florida | A licensee may be disciplined for paying a fee "to a person not properly licensed" for the referral of real estate business, clients or prospects. A sales associate may collect money only in the name of the employer. | Florida Statutes 475.25(1)(h) and 475.42(1)(d) |
| California | A broker may not pay a person for licensed acts unless that person is a licensed broker or a salesperson under that broker. A salesperson may accept pay only from the responsible broker. Licensees may share pay through the responsible broker. | California Business and Professions Code 10137 |

What this means in practice:

- **Pay the broker, not the agent.** Send the fee to the agent's brokerage and let the brokerage pay the agent.
- **Do not pay unlicensed people** for referrals in these states: not a past tenant, not a handyman, not a friend. Florida's rule covers "a fee or other compensation", so a gift card can count too.
- **Check whether you need a license** to receive a fee back when you send a buyer to an agent.

One federal rule also applies when a home sale uses a federally related mortgage. RESPA, at 12 CFR 1024.14(b), bars fees for the referral of settlement service business. It has an exception for "cooperative brokerage and referral arrangements" between real estate agents and brokers, when all parties act in a brokerage role. Ask a lawyer before you take a fee on a sale.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I stay top of mind with agents?

Stay in touch on a fixed schedule, and give agents something useful each time. Fourandhalf names regular calls, a referral web page, an online referral form, and useful news such as legal and eviction updates.

Use this 90-day plan for a new agent:

1. **Week 1: meet.** Coffee or a 15-minute call. Ask what kind of owners the agent sees.
2. **Week 1: send the one-page promise** and a link to your referral form.
3. **Week 2: add the agent to a monthly email** with local rent data and law changes.
4. **Week 4: give one free rental analysis** the agent can hand to a client who cannot sell.
5. **Week 6: call** with one fact the agent can use: days on market for rentals, or a new local rule.
6. **Week 8: invite the agent** to a small lunch with 3 to 5 other agents.
7. **Week 12: review.** Did the agent refer anyone? If yes, thank the agent and send the status. If no, ask why.

Track each referral from the form to the signed door, and update the agent at each step. Fourandhalf advises this step for every referred client.

## Why do most programs fail?

Most programs fail because the manager stops after a few weeks. Fourandhalf says relationships take time and managers give up when results are slow. It also says many successful programs bring about 5 to 7 leads a week, but that figure is an agency claim, not a study.

Set your own target from your data:

| Metric | How to count it |
|---|---|
| Active agents | Agents who referred at least 1 owner in 12 months |
| Referrals per active agent | Referrals / active agents |
| Close rate | Signed owners / referred owners |
| Doors from agents | Doors signed from agent referrals per quarter |
| Sales sent back | Buyers and sellers you sent to agents |

The last row is the one agents care about. Report it to them.

Referrals are one source next to search and AI. In the Goodjuju fall 2026 study of 35 companies, web referral traffic brought 3.7% of owner leads, but that count covers referral websites, not agent phone calls.

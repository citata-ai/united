Many owners face the same choice when they move. Sell the old home, or keep it and rent it out. Keeping it can build wealth. It also means two mortgages, one tenant and a new set of risks.

This guide shows how a lender looks at the old home, what cash you need, and how to test the numbers before you sign.

## Will a Lender Count Future Rent as Income?

A lender may count part of the future rent, but not all of it. The Fannie Mae Selling Guide, section B3-3.8-05, covers a "departing residence": a home you will leave and convert to a rental when you buy a new main home.

Here is how the rule works:

1. **Market rent, not your lease.** Fannie Mae says lease agreements are not permitted for any departing residence. The lender uses a full appraisal with market rents, a Form 1007 rent schedule, or market tools such as Zillow, Redfin or the MLS. With tools, the lender needs at least 3 comparable rentals.
2. **75% of that rent.** The lender multiplies the monthly gross rent by 75%. The 25% cut allows for vacancy and costs.
3. **Minus the full payment.** The lender subtracts the old home's PITIA: principal, interest, taxes, insurance and association dues.
4. **Positive or negative.** If the result is positive, it can offset only the old home's payment. If it is negative, it counts as debt in your debt-to-income ratio.

The lender must also document your current housing payment to use any rental income. Lenders may add their own rules on top of Fannie Mae's. Ask yours early.

## Can You Show the Lender's Math?

Yes. This example uses invented numbers.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Market rent from 3 comps | $2,400 |
| × 75% | $1,800 |
| Old home PITIA | −$1,950 |
| Adjusted monthly net rental income | −$150 |
| Effect on your loan file | $150 counts as monthly debt |

The rent covers most of the old payment, but the lender still adds $150 to your debts. If the market rent were $2,800, the result would be +$150. That positive amount offsets the old payment only. It does not add to your income for the new loan.

## How Much Cash Do I Need for the Second Home?

You need a down payment for the new home and reserves for the old one. The new home is your principal residence, so the down payment can be smaller than for an investment property.

| Requirement | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Down payment, new 1-unit main home | Up to 97% LTV on a fixed-rate loan, with conditions | Fannie Mae Eligibility Matrix, August 5, 2026 |
| Down payment, FHA loan | At least 3.5% of appraised value in cash | 12 U.S.C. 1709(b)(9) |
| Reserves for the old home | 6 months of its PITIA if you have under 12 months of property management experience | Fannie Mae B3-3.8-05 |
| Reserves for other financed homes | 2% of the unpaid balance for 1 to 4 financed properties | Fannie Mae B3-4.1-01 |

Fannie Mae says the 6-month reserve for the vacated home is in addition to any reserves for multiple financed properties. With a $1,950 PITIA, that is $11,700 you must show after closing.

Reserves are liquid assets such as bank balances. They are not spent at closing. They prove you can pay both loans for a while.

## What Are the Risks of Two Mortgages?

The main risk is a month with no rent and a large bill. With one home, a vacancy is a calm month. With two, it can hurt.

Watch for these risks:

- **Vacancy.** Every empty month, you pay both payments from your own cash.
- **Big repairs.** A roof or furnace can cost thousands of dollars in one week.
- **Tenant problems.** Late rent or an eviction can take months, by state law.
- **Rate changes.** An adjustable loan on either home can raise the payment.
- **Tax time.** Rental income goes on Schedule E. Depreciation and expenses change your return.
- **Exit window.** IRS Publication 523 says the home sale exclusion needs ownership and use for at least 24 months in the 5 years before sale. Renting for too long can end that option.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## How Do I Check If the Old Home Will Cash Flow?

Run your own numbers, not the lender's. The lender's 75% test is a loan rule. Your test should use real costs.

This example uses invented numbers for a monthly budget:

| Line | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Market rent | $2,400 |
| Vacancy (1 month a year ÷ 12) | −$200 |
| Mortgage principal and interest | −$1,350 |
| Property tax | −$350 |
| Insurance for a rented home | −$150 |
| Repairs and replacement reserve | −$200 |
| Property management, if you hire it | −$0 to −$240 |
| Net cash flow | +$150 to −$90 |

Small changes flip the result. If you hire a manager, the home may lose money each month. That is not always wrong. Some owners accept a small loss for equity growth. Make the choice with eyes open.

## What Should You Do Next?

Do these steps in order:

1. **Pull 3 rental comps** for the old home.
2. **Ask your lender** how it treats a departing residence and what reserves you need.
3. **Get an insurance quote** for the old home as a rental.
4. **Build the monthly budget** above with your own numbers.
5. **Decide your exit date.** Mark the last day you could sell and still use the home sale exclusion.

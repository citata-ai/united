## Which documents should every vendor give you?

Set a vendor document list by the work, property, contract, and applicable local rules. Do not apply an unsupported universal insurance limit to every trade.

Start with the vendor's legal name, contact details, work scope, insurance information, and required tax records. Ask your insurance adviser and local attorney which coverage and endorsements the work needs.

The IRS says Form W-9 provides a correct taxpayer identification number to a person required to file an information return. It is not an insurance document. Keep its access and review separate from field-work records.

New York DFS guidance says a certificate provides evidence of insurance and should not alter the policy's terms. That distinction matters when staff review a document against your contract requirements.

| Record | Purpose | Review owner |
|---|---|---|
| Vendor identity | Match the contractor to the agreement | Vendor setup lead |
| Work scope | Define approved services and properties | Property manager |
| Insurance certificate | Review evidence of listed coverage | Insurance reviewer |
| Required endorsements | Check the actual policy condition | Insurance adviser |
| Tax information | Support the applicable reporting process | Accounting lead |
| Approval record | Show what work can proceed | Authorized manager |

## Which tools track expiry dates?

Use a register or credentialing system that links documents, expiry dates, requirements, and review status. A calendar reminder alone does not explain whether the vendor can do the assigned work.

RealPage Vendor Credentialing describes expiry notifications, insurance monitoring, document validation, and portals for vendors and agents. Test how those features work against your property requirements.

For a simpler register, define the fields before choosing a tool. Include vendor identity, property scope, work type, document status, expiry, reviewer, review date, and next action.

Keep receipt and approval separate. A file can arrive on time but fail review. Staff should see the unresolved issue rather than a reassuring uploaded label.

Ask how the system updates related properties when a policy changes. A vendor may serve separate owners with different requirements. Do not assume approval for one property applies everywhere.

Test a missing endorsement and an expired policy in a sample account. Confirm that the responsible person receives the alert and that assignment staff see the unresolved status.

Ask the vendor for a quote if pricing is not public. Do not invent a per-vendor cost from another company's comparison page.

## What happens if an uninsured vendor gets hurt?

An injury needs an immediate response and review by the relevant insurer and legal adviser. Do not assume a certificate, contract clause, or software approval settles responsibility.

This guide cannot determine liability from a missing document. The result depends on the facts, policy terms, contracts, and applicable law. Ask a local attorney and insurance adviser to review the actual situation.

Preserve the work order, agreement, approval record, available policy information, and communications. Record what happened without guessing about fault or coverage. Follow your approved incident process.

Do not alter a certificate to make it match a requirement. New York DFS guidance states that a certificate should not expand or change the underlying coverage. Ask the agent or insurer for the correct supporting evidence.

Give field staff a clear route for urgent reports. They should know whom to contact without opening private tax records or trying to interpret insurance terms.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Use the event to review the approval process after the immediate response. Keep the review focused on facts and needed controls.

## How do vendor portals help?

Vendor portals can keep document requests and status updates out of scattered inboxes. They help only when a named reviewer owns the next action.

RealPage describes document upload, issue review, and an insurance-agent portal. The vendor also describes direct agent contact with permission. Confirm the proposed account permissions and support route.

Make each request precise. State which document or correction is missing, where to upload it, and whom to contact. Avoid a generic rejected status without a reason.

Keep tax identifiers and bank details restricted. A technician assigned to field work does not need access to the vendor's full administrative record.

Test a vendor handoff between staff members. A new coordinator should find the current issue, uploaded evidence, and reviewer without asking the vendor to start again.

Ask how the portal records replacements. Keep the old and new document history clear. Staff need to know which evidence supported approval at the time of the assignment.

Do not let portal access itself mean permission to work. The approval status should remain a separate, deliberate result.

## How often should you check COIs?

Check approval status before assigning new work and use expiry alerts to trigger renewal review. Recheck when the work, property requirements, or policy changes.

There is no universal review interval established by the sources used here. Choose a documented schedule with your insurance adviser and assign a person to unresolved alerts.

Use this repeat workflow:

- **Define requirements:** Record the work and property conditions before requesting documents.
- **Collect evidence:** Use an approved upload route and match the vendor identity.
- **Review coverage:** Have the right adviser check required terms and endorsements.
- **Record approval:** Name the reviewer, date, scope, and unresolved conditions.
- **Check assignment:** Confirm current status before issuing work.
- **Track changes:** Request review when scope or policy information changes.
- **Handle expiry:** Keep work authorization aligned with the approved renewal process.
- **Keep history:** Preserve the records needed to explain prior decisions.

Review tax records through the accounting process too. This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

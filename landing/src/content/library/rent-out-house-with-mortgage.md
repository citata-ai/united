## What does the owner-occupancy clause say?

The owner-occupancy clause is the promise to live in the home that you signed at closing. Most conventional loans use the Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac uniform security instrument. Section 6 of that form, titled "Occupancy," sets the rule.

In the Illinois version (Form 3014), the borrower must occupy and use the property as a principal residence within 60 days after signing. The borrower must keep living there for at least 1 year after the move-in date. Two exceptions apply: the lender agrees in writing, or extenuating circumstances beyond the borrower's control exist. The form says the lender will not unreasonably withhold consent.

The rule in one table:

| Term | What the uniform instrument says |
|------|----------------------------------|
| Move-in deadline | Within 60 days after you sign |
| Minimum stay | At least 1 year after you move in |
| Exception 1 | The lender agrees in writing |
| Exception 2 | Extenuating circumstances beyond your control |
| After year 1 | No occupancy duty in this clause |

Each state has its own version of the form, so check the one you signed. Your copy is in your closing package.

## How long must I live in the home first?

You must live in the home for at least 1 year under the standard conventional loan clause. Count from the date you moved in, not the closing date.

A worked timeline:

| Event | Date |
|-------|------|
| Loan closes | 2026-03-02 |
| Latest move-in under the 60-day rule | 2026-05-01 |
| You actually move in | 2026-03-20 |
| 1 year of occupancy ends | 2027-03-20 |
| Earliest tenant move-in without lender consent | 2027-03-20 |

Plan the listing so the lease starts after the end date. You can show the home and screen tenants a few weeks earlier.

## What happens if I rent without telling the lender?

If you met the 1-year occupancy term, renting usually breaks no rule, and you do not need the lender's consent. The risk is for owners who never planned to live there.

Section 8 of the uniform instrument covers the loan application. It says you are in default if you gave materially false information, and it names misrepresenting your occupancy or intended occupancy. A default can let the lender demand the full balance.

Federal law also limits when a lender can call a loan due because of a lease. The rule is 12 U.S.C. 1701j-3(d). It covers homes with fewer than 5 units. On those, a lender may not use its due-on-sale clause because you grant a lease of 3 years or less with no option to buy.

What this means in practice:

- **Lived there 1 year, then moved:** Renting is normal. Keep paying on time.
- **Moved out early for a job, illness or family reason:** Write to the lender and keep the proof. The clause allows for circumstances beyond your control.
- **Never moved in:** This is occupancy fraud risk. Talk to a lawyer before you sign any lease.
- **Lease longer than 3 years or with a purchase option:** The federal protection for leases does not apply.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Does an FHA or VA loan change the rules?

FHA and VA loans are also built for homes you live in, and each has its own occupancy rule.

| Loan | Occupancy rule (source) | Renting later |
|------|-------------------------|---------------|
| Conventional (Fannie/Freddie) | Move in within 60 days, live there 1 year (uniform instrument, section 6) | Allowed after the year |
| FHA | Principal residence: the home where you live and spend most of the calendar year; one principal residence at a time (24 CFR 203.18(f)) | Allowed once you have lived there as your home; check your loan papers |
| VA | You certify you intend to occupy the home, and move in within a reasonable time (38 U.S.C. 3704(c)) | Allowed after you have occupied it as your home |

The VA statute adds a rule for service members. If you are on active duty and cannot occupy the home, a spouse or a dependent child can meet the occupancy rule. Fannie Mae's Selling Guide also treats an active-duty borrower absent because of service as an owner occupant.

Ask your servicer for the exact terms of your FHA or VA loan before you list the home.

## Should I refinance into an investment loan?

You do not have to refinance when you turn your home into a rental. Your owner-occupied loan stays in place, at its rate, after you meet the occupancy term. Most owners keep it.

A refinance into an investment loan costs more. Fannie Mae's loan-level price adjustment (LLPA) matrix, updated 2026-09-30, adds a charge for every investment property loan. The charge rises with the loan-to-value ratio (LTV):

| LTV | Investment property LLPA (Fannie Mae) |
|-----|----------------------------------------|
| 60% or less | 1.125% |
| 70.01–75% | 2.125% |
| 75.01–80% | 3.375% |

On a $300,000 loan at 75.01–80% LTV, a 3.375% LLPA is $10,125 in upfront price, often paid through a higher rate. Fannie Mae's eligibility matrix (2026-08-05) also caps a cash-out refinance on a 1-unit investment home at 75% LTV, compared with 80% for a principal residence.

When a refinance can make sense:

1. **You need cash** for the next home and a cash-out refinance is your only source.
2. **Your current rate is far above the market.** Freddie Mac's survey shows the 30-year fixed rate averaged 7.40% on 2026-10-15. Compare your rate first.
3. **You want the loan in an LLC name.** Conventional owner-occupied loans are made to people, not companies.

Before the tenant moves in, run the numbers with the new costs: a landlord policy, a manager if you will use one, and vacancy. Our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) shows the management cost per year.

## Checklist before you rent a mortgaged home

- [ ] Find section 6 of your security instrument and note your move-in date.
- [ ] Confirm 1 year of occupancy has passed, or get the lender's written consent.
- [ ] Keep the lease at 3 years or less with no purchase option.
- [ ] Switch to a landlord or dwelling fire insurance policy.
- [ ] Ask your servicer to send statements to your new address.
- [ ] Check the city's rental license or registration rules.
- [ ] Set a reserve for repairs and empty months.
- [ ] Ask a tax professional how the rental changes your taxes.

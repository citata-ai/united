## Is there a Section 8 surcharge?

There is no standard Section 8 surcharge that we could find. We read two fee sources on 2026-10-19. iPropertyManagement surveyed 722 branches from 669 companies in 31 states (updated 2026-09-23). All Property Management publishes a fee guide. Neither one mentions Section 8 or voucher units.

So any surcharge is a choice of each manager. Some fold voucher work into the normal fee. Some add a line item. The only way to know is the written fee schedule.

What is the same for every unit:

| Fee | Typical level | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Management, percent of rent | 8% to 12% | All Property Management |
| Management, percent, average | 8.49% (range 3.75% to 14.0%) | iPropertyManagement |
| Management, flat, average | $101.04 per unit a month | iPropertyManagement |
| Leasing fee, with full management | 70.6% of one month's rent, average | iPropertyManagement |
| Leasing fee, typical range | 50% to 100% of one month's rent | All Property Management |
| Lease renewal, flat | $231.77 average | iPropertyManagement |
| Setup | $185.24 average per contract | iPropertyManagement |
| Inspection | $106.72 average each (range $15 to $350) | iPropertyManagement |

## What extra work does a voucher unit add?

A voucher unit adds work with the PHA on top of normal management. That is where a manager's extra cost, and any surcharge, comes from:

1. **Lease-up with the PHA.** The Request for Tenancy Approval, the PHA inspection, the HUD tenancy addendum and the HAP contract (24 CFR 982.305, 982.308).
2. **Periodic inspections.** The PHA inspects at least every 2 years, or every 3 years for a small rural PHA (24 CFR 982.405(b)).
3. **Repair deadlines.** Failed items must be fixed within 24 hours if life-threatening, or 30 days for others (24 CFR 982.404(a)(3)). Missed deadlines lead to HAP abatement.
4. **Rent changes.** The owner must notify the PHA at least 60 days before a rent change (24 CFR 982.308(g)(4)).
5. **Two payers.** The manager collects the tenant share from the family and gets the HAP from the PHA (24 CFR 982.452(b)(5)).

## Who pays reinspection fees?

The owner pays any PHA reinspection fee, and only when an owner-responsible repair was not done. 24 CFR 982.405(h) sets the rule:

- The PHA may not charge the owner for the inspection before the lease, or for the first inspection during the tenancy.
- The PHA "may establish a reasonable fee" for a reinspection that shows an owner-responsible item was not fixed.
- "The owner may not pass this fee along to the family."
- The PHA may not charge the family for any inspection (982.405(g)).

For your contract: ask the manager who pays a reinspection fee when the manager missed the repair. A fair contract puts that cost on the party that missed the deadline.

## How do leasing fees change for a voucher unit?

The leasing fee is usually the same formula, a percent of one month's rent. On a voucher unit, ask what "rent" means: the full contract rent, or only the tenant share. You receive the full contract rent, from two payers. Get the fee basis in writing.

Lease-up also takes longer. The PHA inspection and approval come before the lease term starts (24 CFR 982.305(b)). Ask whether the leasing fee covers the PHA steps, or whether they are billed apart.

## Fee table by service: what to ask a manager

| Service | Ask this | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly management | Percent of rent collected, including HAP? | A higher percent only for voucher units |
| Leasing | Percent of full contract rent? Includes the PHA lease-up steps? | A separate "PHA processing" fee |
| PHA inspections | Fee per inspection? Who attends? | Charging you for the free first inspection visit |
| Reinspection fee | Who pays if the manager missed a repair? | Passing the fee to the tenant (not allowed) |
| Rent increase request | Fee to file the 60-day notice and support rent reasonableness? | No fee, but no comparables either |
| Renewal | Flat fee? | Charging for automatic month-to-month terms |
| Eviction or notices | Fee per notice? Copy sent to the PHA? | Missing the PHA copy (24 CFR 982.310(e)) |

## Worked example for a 3-bedroom

HUD's FY2027 FMR for a 3-bedroom in the Chicago-Joliet-Naperville HUD Metro FMR Area is $2,586 a month (HUD User). We assume a contract rent of $2,400 and the fee levels in the table above. The voucher add-ons are made-up examples to show the math, not survey data.

| Line | Market tenant | Voucher tenant |
|---|---|---|
| Rent collected for 12 months | $28,800 | $28,800 |
| Management at 8.49% | $2,445 | $2,445 |
| Leasing fee, 70.6% of one month (once every 2 years, per year) | $847 | $847 |
| Inspection visits at $106.72 (1 a year, assumed) | $107 | $107 |
| Voucher add-on: PHA lease-up fee, example $150 every 2 years | $0 | $75 |
| Voucher add-on: 1 extra PHA inspection visit every 2 years | $0 | $53 |
| Total fees per year | $3,399 | $3,527 |
| Effective rate on rent collected | 11.8% | 12.2% |

In this example, the voucher add-ons cost about $128 a year, or 0.4 points of rent. A surcharge of 2 extra points on the monthly fee would cost $576 a year. Compare a manager's quote with both numbers.

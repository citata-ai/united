## Which license must an Atlanta property manager hold, and how do I look it up?

An Atlanta property manager who works for other owners for a fee should hold a Georgia real estate license through a licensed brokerage. The Georgia Real Estate Commission (GREC) licenses brokers, associate brokers and salespersons. GREC's rules (Chapter 520-1) list a property management agreement as a type of brokerage engagement, the written contract between a broker and a client.

How to look up a manager, step by step:

1. **Ask for two names:** the agent who will run your home and the brokerage firm.
2. **Open the GREC search** at ata.grec.state.ga.us/Account/Search.
3. **Search the person** under "Find a Real Estate Agent" by last name or license number.
4. **Search the firm** under "Find a Company" by firm name.
5. **Check the status.** An inactive license is a red flag.
6. **Check GREC's disciplinary actions page** for the firm and the agent.

Watch for: an owner who manages their own rentals does not need a license. But a "manager" who is not licensed, works for many owners and collects your rent is a risk. Ask the question in writing.

## Which local rules must an Atlanta manager know?

An Atlanta manager must know Georgia's deposit, notice and eviction rules, because Georgia law sets most landlord-tenant rules. The Georgia Landlord-Tenant Handbook, revised 2024-08-29 and hosted by the Department of Community Affairs (DCA), sums them up.

| Rule | What the Georgia handbook says |
|---|---|
| Deposit limit | A landlord cannot require more than 2 months' rent as a deposit |
| Deposit return | Within 30 days if the tenant gave notice and owes nothing |
| Escrow | Owners of more than 10 units, or owners who use a management agent, must keep deposits in a bank escrow account used only for deposits, or post a bond with the superior court clerk |
| Escrow notice | The tenant gets written notice of where the deposit is held |
| Move-in list | The tenant gets a full list of existing damage before the deposit is accepted, and both sign it |
| Manager contact | Tenants should get the property manager's name and address at lease signing; changes within 30 days |
| Eviction | No self-help eviction. File a dispossessory affidavit in magistrate court in the county of the property |
| Tender defense | For non-payment, the tenant has 7 days after service to pay rent, fees and court costs, once in 12 months |

The escrow row matters most for you. Once you hire a manager, the escrow rule applies to you even with one house. The same handbook adds a warning for these owners. A wrongly withheld deposit can cost three times the amount, plus attorney fees.

GREC adds its own rule for brokers. Its FAQ says a broker who keeps a separate account for rentals must register that trust account with the Commission (License Law 43-40-20 and Rule 520-1-.08).

Metro Atlanta covers many counties, and each county has its own magistrate court. Ask which counties the manager files in most.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What should an Atlanta management agreement say?

An Atlanta management agreement should spell out money, authority and exit terms in writing. Use this checklist when you read a draft.

- [ ] **Fees:** monthly fee, leasing fee, renewal fee, setup fee, and any maintenance markup.
- [ ] **Basis:** percent of rent collected or rent due. "Collected" means no fee on an empty month.
- [ ] **Repair limit:** the dollar amount the manager can spend without your approval.
- [ ] **Trust account:** the bank and account where rent and deposits sit, and that it is registered with GREC.
- [ ] **Escrow notice:** who sends the tenant the deposit location notice.
- [ ] **Move-in and move-out lists:** who makes them, with photos.
- [ ] **Evictions:** who files the dispossessory affidavit, in which court, and the fee.
- [ ] **Term and cancellation:** length, notice to cancel, and any cancellation fee.
- [ ] **Tenant ownership:** whether you owe a fee if you keep the tenant after you cancel.
- [ ] **Reports:** monthly statements and a year-end 1099.

## Which questions should I ask an Atlanta manager before I sign?

Ask questions that test the manager on Georgia rules, not just on price. Good answers are specific.

1. **What is your GREC license number and your firm's?** Look both up during the call.
2. **Where do my tenant's deposits sit?** Expect a named bank and an escrow or trust account used only for deposits.
3. **How fast do you return deposits?** Expect "within 30 days, with an itemized list."
4. **Who makes the move-in damage list?** Expect a signed list with photos.
5. **Walk me through an eviction for non-payment.** Expect: notice, dispossessory affidavit, magistrate court, the 7-day tender window.
6. **Which county courts do you file in?** Expect the county where your home is.
7. **How many doors does each manager handle?** A high number can mean slow replies.
8. **What do you charge on top of the monthly fee?** Get a written fee schedule.
9. **How do you screen tenants?** Expect one written standard for every applicant, in line with Fair Housing rules.
10. **Can I talk to 2 current owners?** A good manager says yes.

## Where do Atlanta owners find manager reviews?

Atlanta owners can check reviews in three kinds of places. Use more than one, because each has gaps.

| Source | What it tells you | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| GREC license search and disciplinary actions | License status and formal discipline | Shows only formal actions |
| Google Business Profile and Yelp | Owner and tenant reviews | Tenant complaints often dominate. Read the manager's replies. |
| Owner references from the manager | Real results from similar homes | The manager picks them. Ask for owners with your home type. |

Read tenant reviews too. A manager that treats tenants badly gets more turnover, and turnover costs you a leasing fee and empty months.

What to do: shortlist 3 managers, run the GREC check on each, ask the 10 questions, and compare the written agreements side by side.

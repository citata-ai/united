## Why does a property management About page matter for AI?

The About page is the one page on your site built to hold company facts. AI engines need those facts to describe you. A landlord asks Gemini, "Who owns Acme Property Management, and how long have they been in business?" The engine looks for one clear source. If your About page tells a story with no facts, the engine looks elsewhere.

We learned this on our own company first. On 2026-10-19 we asked ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini what Citata.ai is (our data, 2026-10). Claude answered about other companies with similar names, Citate.ai or Citant.ai, in 4 of 4 brand answers. Gemini merged us with Citant.ai. ChatGPT was right in 2 of 4 answers.

The fix started on our About page. We added a company facts table and a table of the similar names, with what each company does. You can see it at [our About page](/about). A property manager with a common name has the same risk.

## Which facts belong on a property management About page?

Put every fact that an owner or an AI engine might repeat in one table. A table is easy to scan, and each row is a short passage an engine can quote. Use this list.

| Fact | Example of a good row | Why AI needs it |
|---|---|---|
| Legal name and trade name | "Acme Realty LLC, doing business as Acme Property Management" | Stops mixups with similar names |
| Founding year | "Founded 2009 in [city, state]" | Owners ask how long you have been in business |
| Owner or broker | "Jane Ruiz, owner and broker of record" | Answers "who runs this company" |
| Licenses | License type, number, issuing state, lookup link | Lets anyone check the claim |
| Office address and phone | The same text as your Google Business Profile | Matches your listings |
| Service cities | A list of real cities, each linked to its city page | Answers "do they work in my city" |
| Home types | "Single-family homes, condos, small multifamily" | Answers "do they manage my type" |
| Door count and date | "312 doors under management, October 2026" | Gives scale; the date keeps it honest |
| Services not offered | "No HOA management, no short-term rentals" | Stops wrong recommendations |
| Related companies | Brokerage, maintenance company, owner's other firms | Disclosure that owners and AI can check |

Use your real numbers. A date next to each count shows when you checked it. Update the page when a fact changes, and change your listings the same day.

## Should a property management About page name the owner and team?

Yes. Name the owner and the people who do the work. Google's guide on helpful content asks: "Is it self-evident to your visitors who authored your content?" It also asks if content gives "background about the author or the site that publishes it, such as through links to an author page or a site's About page."

For a property manager, that means:

1. **Name the owner or broker.** Give the full name, role and years in the trade.
2. **Name each team lead.** Property manager, leasing lead, maintenance lead, accounting lead. One line each with the role.
3. **Add a short bio for each.** 2 to 3 sentences: what they do for owners and one real credential.
4. **Use real photos.** Stock photos of people who do not work for you mislead owners.
5. **Link each person.** A LinkedIn profile or a page on your site per person.

Staff change. Give each person a short bio block that you can remove in 5 minutes. Keep the company facts table separate, so a staff change does not break it.

## How do you show licenses on the About page?

Show each license as a fact anyone can check. Write the license type, the number, the state and the holder. Then link to the state agency's public license lookup.

A good license row reads: "[State] real estate broker license [number], held by [name]. Check it on the [state agency] license lookup." Fill in your real state, number, holder and lookup link.

Rules on what a broker must show in ads and on websites differ by state. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Should the About page link to outside profiles?

Yes. Link the profiles that describe your company on other sites. Those links tie your site to the listings AI engines read. We asked 3 AI engines for the best manager in 50 cities on 2026-10-19 (our data, 2026-10). A listing site was a source in 132 of 150 answers. Expertise.com alone was a source in 93.

Link these, if you have them:

- [ ] Google Business Profile
- [ ] BBB profile
- [ ] Yelp page
- [ ] LinkedIn company page
- [ ] NARPM or other trade group member page
- [ ] Directory listings that rank for your city, such as Expertise.com
- [ ] Your state license lookup result

Then check each profile. The name, address, phone and founding year must match the About page exactly. If one listing says "founded 2011" and your page says "2009", the engine has to guess.

## What schema should a property management About page use?

Use Organization markup, or a more exact type. Google's Organization structured data guide says: "We recommend placing this information on your home page, or a single page that describes your organization." The About page is that single page.

Google lists no required properties. It recommends you add as many relevant ones as you can. For a local business, Google suggests you use the most exact LocalBusiness subtype. Schema.org's RealEstateAgent type is a subtype of LocalBusiness and of Organization.

Use these properties to match your facts table:

| Facts table row | Schema property |
|---|---|
| Trade name and legal name | name, legalName, alternateName |
| Founding year | foundingDate |
| Owner | founder |
| Licenses | hasCredential |
| Address and phone | address, telephone |
| Service cities | areaServed |
| Team size | numberOfEmployees |
| Profiles | sameAs |

Google describes sameAs as URLs of pages on other sites that give more information about your organization, such as social profiles or review sites. Put the same URLs there as in your profile links list. The markup must match what the page shows. Markup with facts that are not on the page misleads people and engines.

## A 30-minute About page check

Do this once a quarter:

1. **Ask 3 engines about you.** In ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude, ask "What is [your company] in [city]? Who owns it and when was it founded?"
2. **Write down each wrong fact.** Note the engine and the wrong text.
3. **Find the fact on your About page.** If it is missing, add a row.
4. **Find the fact on your listings.** If a listing is wrong, fix it there.
5. **Ask again in 30 days.** Compare with your notes.

AI engines do not update on a fixed schedule. A fix can take weeks to show. Keep the notes, so you can see the change when it comes.

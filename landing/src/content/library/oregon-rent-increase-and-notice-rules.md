## Oregon rent and notice rules at a glance

Oregon is one of the few states with a statewide rent cap. The table holds the core rules from ORS chapter 90 and the state's published cap.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum rent increase, 2026 | 9.5% | ORS 90.323(2)(d), 90.324; OEA |
| Maximum rent increase, 2027 | 10% | ORS 90.324; OEA |
| Notice to raise rent (not week-to-week) | 90 days, written | ORS 90.323(2)(b) |
| Notice to raise rent (week-to-week) | 7 days, written | ORS 90.323(1) |
| Increases per 12 months | 1 | ORS 90.323(2)(c) |
| Increase in first year of tenancy | Not allowed | ORS 90.323(2)(a) |
| Cap exemption, newer buildings | First certificate of occupancy under 15 years old | ORS 90.323(5)(a) |
| Penalty for an increase over the cap | 3 months' rent plus actual damages | ORS 90.323(6) |
| End month-to-month, first year, no cause | 30 days, written | ORS 90.427(3)(b) |
| End month-to-month after first year | Tenant cause, or a qualifying landlord reason with 90 days | ORS 90.427(3)(c), (5) |
| Extra time if notice goes by mail only | Add 3 days | ORS 90.155(2) |

## Does Oregon have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

Oregon has a statewide cap on rent increases. ORS 90.324 tells the Department of Administrative Services to set the cap by September 30 each year. The cap is the lesser of 10% or 7% plus the September 12-month average change in the West Region CPI.

The Oregon Office of Economic Analysis publishes the result. Its rent stabilization page shows 9.5% for 2026 and 10% for 2027.

The cap has two exemptions in ORS 90.323(5):

- **Newer units.** The first certificate of occupancy was issued less than 15 years before the date of the rent increase notice.
- **Regulated affordable units.** The change does not raise the tenant's share of rent, or a program rule or a change in tenant income requires it.

Manufactured home park spaces follow ORS 90.600, not ORS 90.323. This article covers ordinary dwelling units.

Watch for: the 15-year test runs from the notice date. A building can lose its exemption between two increases.

## How many days notice does an Oregon landlord need to raise the rent?

An Oregon landlord needs at least 90 days' written notice to raise the rent. ORS 90.323(2)(b) sets this for every tenancy except week-to-week. A week-to-week tenancy needs 7 days under ORS 90.323(1).

ORS 90.323(3) says the notice must state:

1. **The amount of the increase.** Give it in dollars.
2. **The new rent.** Give the full monthly amount.
3. **The exemption facts.** Only needed if the increase is above the cap.
4. **The effective date.** Count it under the method in ORS 90.160.

Delivery matters too. If you send the notice by first class mail only, ORS 90.155(2) adds 3 days to the notice period. Personal delivery, or mail plus attachment when the lease allows it, avoids the extra days.

### Worked example: a 2026 increase

Your tenant pays $1,800 a month. The tenancy began more than 1 year ago. The last increase took effect more than 12 months ago.

| Step | Figure |
|---|---|
| 2026 cap | 9.5% |
| Largest legal increase | $1,800 × 9.5% = $171 |
| Largest new rent | $1,971 |
| Target effective date | March 1 |
| Last day to deliver notice in person | 90 days before March 1 |
| Last day if you mail it only | 93 days before March 1 |

If the effective date falls in 2027, the 2027 cap of 10% applies. That gives $180, for a new rent of $1,980.

## Can an Oregon landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

An Oregon landlord can raise rent only as the lease allows and never in the first year. ORS 90.323(2)(a) bars any increase in the first year of a tenancy. After year one, the 90-day notice, the once-a-year limit and the cap all apply.

A fixed-term lease that sets the rent for the term is a contract. Most landlords wait for renewal to change the rent. Read your lease before you send any notice.

Watch for: ORS 90.323(4) blocks a common workaround. If you end a first-year tenancy with a 30-day no-cause notice, you cannot charge the next tenant more than the cap allowed for the old one.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Oregon?

The notice depends on how long the tenant has lived in the unit. ORS 90.427 splits the rules at the end of the first year of occupancy.

| Situation | Landlord notice | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Week-to-week tenancy | 10 days, written | ORS 90.427(2) |
| Month-to-month, first year, no cause | 30 days, written | ORS 90.427(3)(b) |
| Month-to-month, after first year | Tenant cause only, or qualifying reason | ORS 90.427(3)(c) |
| Qualifying landlord reason | 90 days, written, with reason and facts | ORS 90.427(5), (6) |
| Tenant ends month-to-month | 30 days, written | ORS 90.427(3)(a) |

ORS 90.427(5) lists 4 qualifying landlord reasons:

- Demolition, or conversion to non-residential use.
- Repairs or renovations that make the unit unsafe or unfit to live in.
- The landlord or an immediate family member will live in the unit.
- The landlord accepted an offer from a buyer who will live in the unit.

For these reasons, the landlord must also pay 1 month's rent with the notice. ORS 90.427(6)(b) excuses landlords who own an interest in 4 or fewer dwelling units.

A landlord who breaks these rules owes 3 months' rent plus actual damages under ORS 90.427(9). The tenant also gets a defense to eviction.

## Do cities in Oregon add their own rent or notice rules?

Yes. Portland adds its own rules in City Code 30.01.085. The state law allows city rules that give tenants more protection.

The Portland rules, as the city code reads on 2026-10-19:

- **Increase notice.** An increase of 5% or more in a rolling 12 months needs 90 days' written notice, or the lease period if longer.
- **Relocation on a 10% increase.** If a tenant gets notice of an increase of 10% or more, the tenant can ask for relocation assistance within 45 days.
- **Relocation on a no-cause or qualifying-reason end.** The landlord pays relocation assistance at least 45 days before the termination date.
- **Amounts.** $2,900 for a studio or SRO, $3,300 for 1 bedroom, $4,200 for 2 bedrooms, $4,500 for 3 or more.
- **Report.** The landlord tells the Portland Housing Bureau of each payment within 30 days.

## Checklist before you send an Oregon rent increase

- The tenancy is more than 1 year old.
- The last increase took effect more than 12 months ago.
- The increase is at or under the cap for the year it takes effect, unless an exemption applies.
- The notice states the increase, the new rent and the effective date.
- The notice gives at least 90 days, plus 3 if mailed only.
- In Portland, you checked the 5% notice and 10% relocation rules.

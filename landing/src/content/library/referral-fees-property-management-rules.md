## Can You Pay an Unlicensed Person for a Referral?

Do not promise a referral fee until the supervising broker checks the recipient and the local rule. A simple introduction can still raise licensing questions.

Texas provides an example of why the details matter. TREC rule 535.20 treats compensated referrals of prospects for a proposed real estate transaction as licensed activity. Rule 535.147 restricts sharing fees with people who perform licensed acts without an active license. These are Texas rules, not a nationwide test.

Do not assume that changing the payment name solves the issue. A gift, marketing allowance or service credit can still need review. Ask counsel to examine the actual activity and payment trigger. A disclosure alone does not make an otherwise barred payment lawful.

Create a review route before staff advertise rewards. Ask where the property is, who will receive the payment and what that person does. Record whether the contact expects compensation before making the introduction.

If the answer is unclear, pause the payment offer. You can still give the owner useful service information without promising compensation. Keep ordinary advertising purchases separate from rewards for sending a particular prospect.

## How Do Agent-to-Agent Referral Fees Work?

An agent referral program needs broker approval, verified license details and written payment terms. Do not treat a license number alone as full approval.

In Texas, TREC rule 535.148 recognizes cooperative brokerage and referral arrangements between active licensed agents and brokers. That does not remove other applicable duties. The broker should review the transaction, parties and any required consent before payment.

Ask the referring professional for the legal brokerage name and license status. Check the official record rather than a business card. Confirm the correct payee with the supervising broker. Keep the verification with the referral file.

Write the trigger plainly. Does the fee depend on a signed management agreement, collected management revenue or another event? Define what happens if the owner cancels or the lead already exists in your system. Avoid disputes by resolving duplicate claims before the sale.

Use a written approval trail for changes. Sales staff should not promise a larger reward in a private message and leave accounting unaware. If there is a dispute about entitlement, route it to the broker instead of deducting money from owner funds.

## How Do You Set Up a Realtor Referral Program?

Build the program around a narrow service promise and a reviewed payment process. Make it easy for a real estate professional to explain your role accurately.

The following checklist is a suggested operating process. It does not replace state legal review.

| Step | Record to keep | Person to assign |
|---|---|---|
| Review program legality | Approved scope and jurisdictions | Broker and counsel |
| Verify referral partner | License lookup and brokerage details | Program lead |
| Accept introduction | Owner contact consent and source | Sales lead |
| Resolve duplicate leads | Dated intake history | Sales manager |
| Confirm payment trigger | Signed referral terms and evidence | Broker |
| Release approved payment | Payee and accounting record | Accounting |

Explain your service area and accepted property types. Tell partners which facts you need to assess fit. Do not ask them to make promises about returns, legal outcomes or resident selection.

Give the owner a direct choice about speaking with your company. Use a short introduction process rather than passing sensitive records through informal messages. Share only the information needed to start the discussion.

Track program cost against signed, serviceable accounts. A large referral list does not show value if the properties fall outside your scope. Review complaints about partner promises as well as sales results.

## What Must You Disclose to the Owner?

Check compensation disclosures separately from the right to pay or receive a referral fee. The timing and consent rules depend on the jurisdiction and transaction.

For Texas, TREC rule 535.148 addresses compensation from someone other than the client. It requires disclosure to the client and consent before receiving covered compensation. The rule also contains settlement-service restrictions. Do not assume a rental referral policy covers a sale, loan or title referral.

Use a direct description of the relationship. Name who pays, who receives the payment and why. Explain any financial interest in a referred provider. Avoid broad boilerplate that leaves the owner unable to identify the actual arrangement.

Ask counsel which disclosures need a separate consent and when. Keep proof with the client record. If the compensation changes, obtain guidance before relying on an older general consent.

Check the website, sales script and agreement together. They should describe the same program. If the public offer suggests a reward that the written terms exclude, fix the offer before staff make new introductions.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How Do You Keep Referral Payments Under Control?

Use a payment gate that checks authority, evidence and the payee. Do not let a sales target override that check.

Keep referral expenses in your company records with a clear business purpose. Ask your accountant what tax information is needed before paying a partner. Separate company marketing expense from owner money unless the owner has approved a lawful charge.

Review failed cases as carefully as successful ones. A payment to the wrong brokerage, an expired license or a duplicate lead can show a process gap. Correct the process before expanding the program.

Test the partner's introduction message. It should say what your company does without implying that every owner qualifies. Give staff a route to ask the broker about unusual cases. Record decisions so the next staff member does not guess.

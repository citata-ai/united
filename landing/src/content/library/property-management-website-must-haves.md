## Which pages must every property management site have?

Every property management site needs a separate path for owners and for tenants, and the owner path needs about 8 pages. Most sites mix the two. A tenant who wants to pay rent and an owner who wants a price should not land on the same page.

The numbers show why the owner path matters. In the Goodjuju 2026 lead source report, 35 companies got 276,128 visitors and 2,525 leads over two 90-day windows. The fall average was 0.9%: fewer than 1 visitor in 100 became a lead (Goodjuju, 2026). Much of that traffic is tenants. So the few owners who arrive must find the owner pages fast.

| Page | Who it serves | What it must answer |
|---|---|---|
| Home | Both | Who you serve, where, and the one next step for owners |
| Owner services | Owners | What you do each month, from leasing to statements |
| Fees | Owners | Every fee, in one table, with what each fee covers |
| City pages | Owners | Local rent, local rules, local proof, for each real service city |
| Rental analysis | Owners | A free rent estimate with a short form |
| About | Owners | People, license number, year founded, doors managed |
| Reviews | Owners | Recent reviews from owners and tenants, linked to the source |
| Contact | Both | Phone, email, office address, hours |
| Listings | Tenants | Units for rent, fed from your software |
| Tenant portal and maintenance | Tenants | Login, pay rent, submit a work order |

City pages need care. Google's spam policy names "multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as doorway abuse (Google Search Central). A city page must hold facts that are true only for that city.

## What goes above the fold on the home page?

The top of the home page must tell an owner in 5 seconds that you serve their city and what to do next. "Above the fold" means the part a visitor sees before they scroll. On a phone, that is about one screen.

Put these 5 items there:

1. **A plain headline.** "Property management for single-family and small multifamily owners in Tampa." Not "Your property, our passion."
2. **One owner button.** "Get a free rental analysis" or "See our fees." One button, not four.
3. **A phone number you can tap.** Many owners still call. Make the number a link on mobile.
4. **One proof line.** Doors under management, years in business, or review count with the star rating. Use only numbers you can show.
5. **A tenant link in the menu.** "Pay rent" and "Maintenance" go in the top menu, so tenants leave the owner path quickly.

Do not fill the top with a large photo slider. A slider pushes the button down and slows the page.

## Which trust signals do owners look for?

Owners look for proof that you are real, licensed and honest about money. An owner hands you an asset worth hundreds of thousands of dollars. They want to see facts, not adjectives.

Checklist of trust signals:

- [ ] Real estate or property management license number, with the state, on the about page and in the footer
- [ ] Doors or units under management, with the date of the count
- [ ] Year founded and the names and photos of the people an owner will talk to
- [ ] A fee table with no "call for pricing" rows
- [ ] Owner protections in writing: cancel terms, any guarantee, and its exact conditions
- [ ] Reviews with a link to the source (Google, Yelp, BBB), not only pasted quotes
- [ ] A sample owner statement or a screenshot of the owner portal
- [ ] Memberships you hold now, such as NARPM, with a link
- [ ] An office address that matches your Google Business Profile

AI answers read the same signals. In our own research, Expertise.com was a source in 93 of 150 AI answers about property managers (our data, 2026-10). Directories and your own site must agree on the same facts. ## Which forms and calls to action convert?

Short forms with a clear reward convert best for owners. A rental analysis form asks for an address and an email, and it promises something of value: a rent range. A "Contact us" form promises nothing.

| Call to action | What the owner gets | Fields to ask | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free rental analysis | A rent range for their address | Address, name, email, phone (optional) | Send the report within 1 business day |
| See our fees | The fee table | None | Put the form under the table |
| Book a call | A time on the calendar | Name, email, number of doors | Show real open times |
| Rent vs sell tool | A side-by-side number | Address, current loan | Keep the math visible |
| Phone call | A person now | None | Answer, or call back within the hour |

Rules for every form:

- Ask for 4 fields or fewer. Each extra field gives the owner one more reason to stop.
- Put the form or a button on every owner page, not only on the contact page.
- Show what happens next: "We send your rent range by email within 1 business day."
- Send leads to a shared inbox or CRM, and track the source. Our guide on [how to track AI traffic](/guides/track-ai-traffic-property-management) shows a GA4 setup.

Traffic source changes the rate a lot. In the Goodjuju fall 2026 data, Google Search and Map Pack visitors converted at 1.2%, AI tool visitors at 3.9%, and social visitors at 0.2% (Goodjuju, 2026). The same form gets very different results from different visitors. So judge a form change on one source at a time.

## How fast and how mobile must the site be?

The site must pass Google's Core Web Vitals on mobile. Google's web.dev page sets three targets for a "good" page (web.dev):

| Metric | What it measures | Good |
|---|---|---|
| Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) | Time until the main content shows | 2.5 seconds or less |
| Interaction to Next Paint (INP) | Delay after a tap or click | 200 ms or less |
| Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) | How much the page jumps while it loads | 0.1 or less |

Google measures these at the 75th percentile of visits, apart for mobile and desktop. Check your own pages free in PageSpeed Insights. Large hero videos, chat widgets and listing scripts are the usual causes of a slow load.

## The full 25-point checklist

Use this list in one sitting. Mark each line yes or no.

**Pages**
1. [ ] Separate owner and tenant paths in the menu
2. [ ] Owner services page
3. [ ] Fee table page
4. [ ] One page per real service city, with local facts
5. [ ] Rental analysis page
6. [ ] About page with people and license
7. [ ] Reviews page with source links
8. [ ] Contact page with address and hours

**Home page**
9. [ ] Headline names who and where
10. [ ] One owner button above the fold
11. [ ] Tap-to-call phone number
12. [ ] One proof line with a real number

**Trust**
13. [ ] License number and state
14. [ ] Door count with a date
15. [ ] Cancel terms in writing
16. [ ] Sample owner statement or portal view
17. [ ] Same name, address and phone as your Google Business Profile

**Forms**
18. [ ] 4 fields or fewer
19. [ ] Owner button on every owner page
20. [ ] "What happens next" line under each form
21. [ ] Lead source tracked in GA4 or a CRM

**Speed and access**
22. [ ] LCP 2.5 seconds or less on mobile
23. [ ] INP 200 ms or less
24. [ ] CLS 0.1 or less
25. [ ] Text readable without zoom on a phone

If you score under 18, start with the fee table and the rental analysis form. Those two answer the owner's first two questions: "What does it cost?" and "What will my home rent for?"

## Which tools are cheap enough for a small HOA?

Compare association tools by full board cost, not just the monthly subscription. A small board also needs a system volunteers can use after officers change.

|---|---|---|---|
| PayHOA | Dues, requests, documents, voting | $54 monthly for up to 25 units; $49 monthly billed yearly | Payment fees and board handoff |
| Buildium | Accounting, violations, board communications | Association page lists Essential from $62 monthly | Get an association-specific quote |

Sources: PayHOA pricing and Buildium association page, read on 2026-10-19. Buildium's general pricing page asks association buyers to call. Confirm the association package in writing.

Do not assume a low unit count means simple work. A board with shared facilities may need approvals, vendor records, and detailed project history. Another board may mainly need dues and files.

Write a short list of required tasks before the demo. Keep nice-to-have features separate. A cheaper subscription is poor value if the treasurer must maintain another ledger to complete the job.

## Which tools handle dues collection online?

PayHOA and Buildium advertise online association payments, but the board must check fees and the accounting result. A payment screen is only part of dues collection.

PayHOA's pricing page lists incoming ACH payments at $2.45 and card payments at 3.5% plus $0.50. Read on 2026-10-19, these fees belong in the budget beside the subscription.

Ask who pays each charge and how the owner sees it before payment. Confirm how the platform records failed payments, refunds, and payments received outside the portal.

Test a regular assessment, an approved special assessment, and a credit. Check the homeowner ledger and the bank deposit record. Ask how the treasurer finds a payment sent with the wrong reference.

Choose who can change charges and who can approve payments to vendors. Keep access separate where your board's process requires it. A shared board password makes the review trail unclear.

Keep an alternate way to contact owners who need help. Software should make the payment process clearer, not leave the treasurer with unexplained exceptions.

## Which tools keep records and votes?

Choose a system that can store association records and preserve the history behind decisions. PayHOA lists document storage and voting tools. Buildium lists architectural requests with committee voting on its association page.

These features serve different purposes. An architectural committee decision is not automatically an association election. Ask the vendor to show the exact workflow you intend to use.

Keep minutes, approved budgets, contracts, and policies in named folders. Distinguish drafts from adopted records. Assign staff or board roles that can publish files without exposing restricted material.

For a vote, check eligibility, duplicate prevention, record export, and the process for disputed submissions. Ask whether the board can reproduce the record after a member leaves office.

Have a local attorney review the voting method against governing documents and local law. Do not treat the presence of a voting button as proof of legal validity.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Use a sample board handoff as part of the demo. A new secretary should find the last approved minutes without asking the outgoing officer for a private folder.

## When should a board hire a management company?

Consider outside help when the board cannot reliably complete the work, even with clear tasks and useful software. The trigger is workload and control, not a universal association size.

Look at missed follow-ups, late reports, unresolved maintenance, and unclear records. Separate a software gap from a lack of time or skills. Another portal will not resolve every problem.

List the work the board can still own. You might need bookkeeping support, project help, or full management. Ask vendors and service firms to define their scope rather than comparing vague packages.

Include transition work in the decision. A manager needs usable records, account access, contracts, and open-item history. Start cleaning those records before the handoff.

Compare the service offer with a written task list. Ask who handles urgent calls, vendor follow-up, owner questions, and report review. Name the board decisions that remain with officers.

## Can a board use rental software?

A board can use a rental platform only when its association features fit the board's actual records and tasks. Rental and association workflows should not become confusing workarounds.

Dues, owner records, violations, approvals, and common-area work need clear labels. Ask the vendor to show those tasks in an association account, not a rental demo with renamed tenants.

Check how the system separates funds and reports. Have the treasurer or bookkeeper review the account structure. Do not accept a combined balance without knowing which records support it.

Use this board acceptance checklist:

- **Collect dues:** Post charges, receive a payment, and correct a failed payment.
- **Share records:** Publish an approved document and restrict a private file.
- **Handle requests:** Route an owner request to the right board role.
- **Approve work:** Record the decision and link the supporting quote.
- **Review reports:** Match a sample ledger to a bank record.
- **Change officers:** Remove an outgoing user and invite a successor.
- **Leave the platform:** Export documents, owner records, and financial detail.

Ask volunteers to run the test themselves. Note where instructions are missing. Choose the system that keeps the board's required work clear across officer changes.

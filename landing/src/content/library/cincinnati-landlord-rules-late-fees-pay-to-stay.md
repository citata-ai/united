## What late fee cap does Cincinnati set?

Cincinnati caps a late fee at $50 or 5% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater. The City of Cincinnati Tenants' Guide (2025) states the rule and points to Cincinnati Municipal Code Chapter 871. The cap applies to rental housing inside city limits only.

The guide lists 3 more late fee rules. A Cincinnati landlord may not:

- **Charge interest** on a late fee.
- **Charge a late fee more than once** for a single late payment.
- **Charge a late fee on the subsidy share.** If a rent subsidy provider, not the tenant, owes part of the rent, you cannot charge a late fee on that part.

The late fee must also be in the rental agreement. Without a lease clause, you cannot assess one.

The cap switches from $50 to 5% at a rent of $1,000.

| Monthly rent | 5% of rent | Maximum late fee |
|---|---|---|
| $800 | $40.00 | $50.00 |
| $1,000 | $50.00 | $50.00 |
| $1,442 | $72.10 | $72.10 |
| $1,900 | $95.00 | $95.00 |

$1,442 is HUD's FY2027 fair market rent for a 2-bedroom in the Cincinnati metro, up 6.6% from $1,353. See all sizes on the [Cincinnati fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/cincinnati-fair-market-rent-2027).

Watch for: a "daily" late fee breaks the once-per-payment rule. Charge one fee, once, within the cap.

## What is pay-to-stay in Cincinnati?

Pay-to-stay is a Cincinnati rule that lets a tenant stop an eviction for nonpayment by paying in full. City Council passed the permanent version on 2021-11-10 by a unanimous vote, with effect 30 days later (WVXU, 2021-11-10). Under Ohio law alone, a landlord can refuse late rent and file. The Cincinnati ordinance changes that inside the city.

How it works:

1. **The tenant falls behind** and you serve notice or file an eviction.
2. **The tenant offers full payment** of all past-due rent and late fees, or proof that an organization will pay the full amount.
3. **You must accept it** if it comes before the initial eviction hearing.
4. **The eviction stops.**

A temporary version from April 2021 expired after Ohio ended its pandemic state of emergency. The permanent version limits the window to the first hearing.

Enforcement history: Hamilton County magistrates first said they would not apply the ordinance, because they saw a conflict with state law. In July 2022, Municipal Judge Dwane Mallory wrote that it "does not conflict with state laws and is a constitutionally valid exercise of its home-rule authority." He dismissed that case on another ground, and the landlord's lawyer called the comment non-binding. The magistrates said they would apply the law case by case (WVXU, 2022-07-19). We found no later appeal ruling.

What to do: accept a full tender before the first hearing. A refusal can lose the case and waste the filing fee.

## What other Cincinnati rules apply to owners?

Other Cincinnati rules cover deposits, repairs and relocation. The Tenants' Guide lists these:

| Rule | What it requires |
|---|---|
| Deposit alternatives | On request, accept 1 of 3: rental security insurance, a deposit in at least 6 equal monthly parts, or a one-time deposit of no more than 50% of the monthly rent |
| Deposit return | Return the deposit and an itemized list within 30 days after move-out |
| Entry | Give 24 hours' notice, except in an emergency |
| Lead | Disclose a lead service line, and known lead hazards in homes built before 1978 |
| Relocation | Pay relocation under Code Section 871-10 if a city vacate order follows from your neglect or deferred maintenance |
| Repair fees | Do not charge tenants for repairs that are your duty |

Ohio law also limits what cities can do. Ohio Revised Code 5321.19, as amended on 2022-09-23, bars any political subdivision from rules that conflict with the state landlord-tenant chapter, or that impose "rent control or rent stabilization." So Cincinnati cannot cap rent.

## Must Cincinnati owners register with the Hamilton County Auditor?

Yes. Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 requires every owner of residential rental property to file contact details with the auditor of the county where the property is. For Cincinnati, that is the Hamilton County Auditor.

Filing checklist:

- [ ] Owner name, address and phone number.
- [ ] For an LLC, a member, manager or officer. For a corporation, an officer. For a trust, a trustee.
- [ ] The street address and permanent parcel number of each rental.
- [ ] Update within 60 days of any change.
- [ ] Respond within 60 days after an auditor notice.

Your property manager may file for you. Under Ohio Revised Code 5323.99, the auditor may add a special assessment of $50 to $150 to the property for a violation.

## What does the Cincinnati source-of-income rule cover?

We could not verify the current Cincinnati source-of-income rule at a primary source, so we do not state its terms here. The city's own Tenants' Guide (2025) lists these protected classes: race, religion, color, national origin, Appalachian origin, natural hairstyles, sexual orientation and gender identity, disability, familial status, reproductive status, ancestry and military status. Source of income is not on that list.

What to do: screen every applicant with the same written criteria. If you are not sure whether you may decline a voucher holder, ask the City of Cincinnati Law Department or a local attorney before you write a listing.

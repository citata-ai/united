## What does an LLC protect and not protect?

An LLC protects your personal assets from debts and claims of the LLC. The California Franchise Tax Board puts it plainly: an LLC protects its members against personal liabilities.

For a rental owner, that means a claim tied to the rental house can be limited to what the LLC owns. Your home, savings and other rentals sit outside it. That is the theory. In practice, the shield holds only if you treat the LLC as a real, separate business.

| An LLC can help with | An LLC does not help with |
|----------------------|---------------------------|
| A tenant's injury claim against the property owner | Your own negligent act, such as a bad repair you did yourself |
| A contract dispute signed in the LLC's name | Debts you personally guaranteed, such as most mortgages |
| Keeping one rental's claim away from another rental (one LLC each) | Claims larger than the LLC's assets plus insurance, if a court ignores the LLC |
| A clear line between business and personal money | Mixed funds: paying rental bills from your personal card |

The habits that keep the shield in place:

- **A separate bank account** for the LLC. Rent goes in, bills go out.
- **The LLC's name on the lease**, the insurance and vendor bills.
- **No mixing.** Do not pay your groceries from the LLC account.
- **State filings on time.** A lapsed LLC can lose its good standing.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What does an LLC cost per year by state?

An LLC costs a formation fee once, plus a yearly fee or tax in most states. The yearly cost is the one that matters for a single rental.

Three states, from their official sites:

| State | Yearly cost to keep an LLC | Source |
|-------|----------------------------|--------|
| California | $800 annual tax, due even if the LLC does no business, until you cancel it | California Franchise Tax Board |
| Delaware | $400 annual tax, due on or before June 1; no annual report required | Delaware Division of Corporations |
| Texas | Franchise tax report each year; LLCs at or below the no tax due threshold file a Public Information Report or Ownership Report | Texas Comptroller |

Delaware adds a $200 penalty plus 1.5% interest per month if you do not pay the annual tax on time (Delaware Division of Corporations).

Other costs to plan for:

- **Registered agent**, if you do not want your home address on public records.
- **Foreign registration**, if the LLC is formed in one state and owns a rental in another.
- **Bookkeeping**, for the separate account.
- **A higher insurance or loan rate**, in some cases, for property owned by an entity.

Worked example for one rental in California, with made-up rent:

| Line | Per year |
|------|----------|
| Rent ($2,400 x 12) | $28,800 |
| LLC annual tax | $800 |
| LLC cost as a share of rent | 2.8% |

That is close to a third of a typical monthly management fee, every year, for a legal shield only.

## Does an LLC change my taxes?

A single-member LLC does not change your federal income tax by default. The IRS says an LLC with only one member is treated as an entity disregarded as separate from its owner, unless it files Form 8832 to be taxed as a corporation.

What that means in practice:

- **Your rental still goes on your Form 1040.** The IRS lists Schedule E (Supplemental Income and Loss) as one of the schedules a single-member LLC's activities go on.
- **The deductions stay the same.** IRS Publication 527 lists the same rental expenses whether you own the house in your name or in an LLC.
- **Two members is different.** The IRS treats a domestic LLC with at least two members as a partnership, unless it elects otherwise. A partnership files its own return.

State tax can differ. California's $800 tax is a state tax on the LLC itself, on top of your federal return.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## When is insurance enough for one rental?

Insurance is often enough for an owner with one rental and modest other assets. Insurance pays claims. An LLC only limits what a claimant can reach.

The North Carolina Department of Insurance notes that dwelling policies typically do not provide liability coverage the way some homeowners forms do. So the first step is to make sure your rental policy has liability coverage. Ask your agent.

A simple decision table:

| Your situation | A common setup |
|----------------|----------------|
| One rental, small savings, rental owned with a home-style mortgage | Dwelling policy with liability plus an umbrella policy |
| One rental, large savings or a business you want to protect | Ask an attorney about an LLC plus the same insurance |
| Several rentals | Ask an attorney about one LLC per property or a series LLC where allowed |
| Rental in a state with a high LLC tax | Compare the yearly LLC cost with a larger umbrella limit |

Watch for: your mortgage. Section 19 of the standard Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac mortgage (Illinois Form 3014) says the lender may require immediate payment in full if you transfer the property without its prior written consent. Talk to your lender before you deed a mortgaged house into an LLC.

## How to decide in 4 steps

1. **List your assets** outside the rental: savings, home equity, other rentals.
2. **Get quotes** for a dwelling policy with liability and a $1 million umbrella.
3. **Price the LLC** in your state: the yearly fee, a registered agent and bookkeeping.
4. **Ask a local attorney** with your list in hand. One hour of advice costs less than one year of a California LLC.

## Which Google Business Profile fields does AI read?

Google has not published which profile fields AI Overviews, AI Mode or Gemini read. What Google does publish is where profile facts come from and that AI features need a current profile.

Google's help page lists four sources for every Business Profile:

1. Public web content, such as your own website.
2. Licensed data from third parties.
3. Facts and content from users, including reviews, photos and owner edits.
4. Google's own interactions with the business.

Google Search Central lists an up-to-date Business Profile as one of the SEO basics for AI Overviews and AI Mode. Whitespark tested AI Mode and saw answers with "star ratings, review volumes, pricing, hours of operation" and other facts. It says these came from Business Profiles "or other sources".

So treat every field as something an AI answer may quote. Here is the setup table for a property manager.

| Field | What to enter | Google rule to know |
|---|---|---|
| Business name | Your real name, as on your sign and letterhead | No marketing taglines or extra words. A name change may need new verification. |
| Primary category | The one category that best fits your core work | Use as few categories as possible, as specific as possible |
| Service areas | Each city or ZIP you serve | Up to 20. About 2 hours of driving max. No radius. |
| Address | Show it only if owners can visit your office | Remove it if you do not serve customers there |
| Services | Leasing, rent collection, maintenance, inspections, owner reports | Custom service names cannot hold prices or phone numbers |
| Description | What you manage, where, and since when | Max 750 characters. No URLs or HTML. |
| Hours | Office hours and holiday hours | Keep special hours current |
| Website | Your owner page, not only the home page | Keep the link live and on your own domain |
| Photos | Office, team, real managed homes | JPG or PNG, 10 KB to 5 MB, 720 x 720 px recommended |
| Reviews | Replies to every review | Reply only after you verify the profile |

## How do you add services and service areas?

Add services and service areas in the profile editor on Google Search or in the Maps app. Both take a few minutes.

To add service areas:

1. Go to your Business Profile and select Edit profile, then Location.
2. Next to "Service area", select Edit.
3. Enter each city or postal code you serve.
4. Select Save. Google says approved edits may take up to 48 hours to show.

To add services:

1. Go to your Business Profile and select Edit services.
2. Select the suggested services that match your work.
3. Select Add custom service for each service that is not listed, such as "Section 8 lease-up" or "owner monthly statements".
4. Add a short description to each service. Then select Save.

Watch for: Google says a service-area business with no office for visits must remove its address. A hybrid business, with an office that owners can visit, may show both.

Owner searches often name a city. If your profile lists 20 cities and your website has a page for each, the facts agree. That is the point. Our [AI search checklist for property management websites](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website) covers the website side.

## Do posts and photos matter for AI search?

Posts and photos keep the profile current, but Google has not said that AI answers use them. Google's local ranking page does advise owners to add photos and videos. It says these help "tell the story of your business".

Facts from Google's help pages:

- **Posts expire.** Posts older than 6 months are archived, unless you set a date range.
- **Phone numbers can block a post.** A post with a phone number in the text may get rejected if Google cannot connect the number to your business.
- **Photo quality has rules.** Google asks for photos in focus, well lit and with no heavy filters, so the image "should represent reality".
- **Videos are short.** Up to 30 seconds, up to 75 MB, 720p or higher.

A simple rhythm works for most managers: 1 post per month and 2 to 4 new real photos per quarter. That is our suggestion, not a Google rule. The goal is a profile that looks run by real people, with no stale news.

## Does a stale profile hurt?

A stale profile hurts when its facts are wrong. Google's ranking page says businesses "with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results". It adds that a profile with wrong info "might not show up for relevant searches".

Owners also judge freshness by reviews. BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults found that 74% look for reviews written in the last three months. 32% want reviews from the last two weeks.

Google may also change your profile without you. Its edit guide says Google may call or text the verified phone number to confirm details. It shows what it collected under Edit profile, then "Collected info", with the source and the date. Check that screen each month. Delete what is wrong.

## Can you control the AI summary on your profile?

You control only part of it. Google's help page names three kinds of short summaries on Maps.

| Summary type | Who writes it | Can you edit it? |
|---|---|---|
| Business description | You, or Google's AI suggestion that you approve | Yes |
| Editorial summary | Google's writers | No |
| Review snippets and Place Topics | An algorithm, from your reviews | No, but you can flag abusive reviews |

Google says it does not remove summaries for being "unclear or negative". It removes only keywords linked to an unrelated place and editorial summaries that name services you do not offer. So to change the summaries, change what reviews say. Ask more owners and tenants to write about real work.

Google now offers an AI-written description. Google warns that "this AI feature writes creative text" and that you should review it for accuracy. Read every word before you save it. A wrong fee or a wrong city in your own description is a fact that AI engines may repeat.

## A monthly 15-minute check

Use this list on the first Monday of each month:

- [ ] Search your exact business name on Google. Check name, hours, phone, website and category.
- [ ] Open Edit profile, then "Collected info". Delete anything wrong.
- [ ] Check that each service area still matches where you work.
- [ ] Reply to every new review, good and bad.
- [ ] Add 1 post with a date range if it is about an event.
- [ ] Ask one owner question in AI Mode with your city. Write down which managers it lists.

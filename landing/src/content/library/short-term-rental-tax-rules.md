## Why does an average stay of 7 days or less matter?

IRS Publication 925 excludes activities with average customer use of 7 days or less from its rental activity definition.

This is a passive activity rule. It does not make your rental income tax-free. It also does not decide every form that belongs in your return. First classify the activity. Then check your participation and the other limits that apply.

Publication 925 explains the average as total days in rental periods divided by the number of rentals. Use actual completed rental records. Do not substitute a listing's minimum stay or your preferred booking length. Keep the booking dates and the method used to count them.

Consider an illustrative example. Assume completed stays total 60 days across 12 rentals. The average is 5 days. Those figures are assumptions used to show the calculation, not market data. Your own average must come from your own rental records.

| Question | Record to review |
|---|---|
| How long did guests stay? | Completed booking dates |
| Did the owner participate? | Tasks, dates, and supporting records |
| Were guest services substantial? | Service contracts and guest offers |
| Did anyone use the home personally? | Owner and family use calendar |
| Were lodging taxes due? | Rules for the property's exact address |

Keep these questions separate. A short average does not prove material participation. A large expense does not prove an allowed current loss.

## When does a short-term rental go on Schedule C?

IRS Publication 527 links Schedule C reporting to substantial services primarily for the tenant's convenience, rather than stay length alone.

Publication 527 gives regular cleaning, linen changes, and maid service as examples of substantial services. It distinguishes heat, light, common-area cleaning, and trash collection. Those ordinary services do not have the same effect by themselves.

Describe what happens during an occupied stay. Cleaning after departure differs from regular guest service during occupancy. Give your preparer contracts, listing claims, and invoices. Labels such as vacation home or Airbnb business do not replace the actual facts.

Publication 527 also warns that self-employment tax may apply when substantial services exist. Ask your preparer to check that issue separately. A host platform's annual statement does not settle the classification.

Track rental receipts and fees as separate entries. The cash deposited in your bank can differ from gross receipts. Platform charges, refunds, and tax collections can explain the gap. Keep the statement that supports each adjustment.

Do not change forms just to reach a desired deduction. Apply the reporting rule to the service model you actually ran. If you change services during the year, tell your preparer when the change occurred. Keep evidence of the old and new arrangements.

## What are local occupancy taxes?

Occupancy taxes are local or state charges that need a separate address-specific review from federal income tax.

This guide does not state a national lodging tax rate or a universal exemption. A rule tied to stay length can differ between jurisdictions. Check the official revenue office for the property's location. Ask which registration, return, and payment duties remain with the owner.

A platform collection line is useful evidence, but it is not a complete compliance review. Request a record of what the platform collected and where it sent the money. Compare that record with the current local instructions. Ask the revenue office about gaps or unclear treatment.

Maintain a separate lodging tax record in your books. Show the guest charge, collected amount, remittance, and any adjustment. Avoid treating money held for tax payment as cash available for owner spending.

Review future bookings when a rule changes. A reservation date and an occupancy date may raise different questions. Use the authority's published transition guidance rather than a host forum's summary.

For an operational comparison, review the broader [rental owner guides](/guides). Keep local permits, building rules, lender terms, and tax registration in separate parts of your file. Approval in one area does not prove approval in the others.

## Why do people call short-term rentals a tax loophole?

The phrase describes a possible result under several tax rules, but it hides the conditions an owner must meet.

Under Publication 925, a qualifying short-stay activity can fall outside the rental activity definition. The owner still needs to examine material participation. Other limits can also restrict a deduction. A booking business with little owner work does not automatically create a loss usable against wages.

Publication 925 includes several material participation tests. Do not assume that paying a manager establishes your own participation. Record who performed each task and why the task belonged to the activity. Ask your preparer which test fits your facts.

Personal use requires another review under Publication 527. Owner stays and certain family stays can change deductions. Keep a full calendar rather than trying to rebuild it from bank records at filing time. Separate repair trips from holidays and preserve supporting work records.

Depreciation is also separate from cash flow. A deduction can reduce taxable income while the property still needs cash for debt and repairs. A future sale can create tax consequences that offset part of an earlier benefit.

Before buying for tax reasons, ask for a written model that includes current limits and the expected sale. Supply your wages, other business activity, guest services, and personal-use plans. Review the result with a tax professional who has read the facts.

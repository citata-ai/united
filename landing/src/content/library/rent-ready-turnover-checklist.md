## What does rent-ready mean?

Rent-ready means a new tenant could move in today with no work left. The unit is clean, every system and appliance works, the safety devices test, the locks are new, and the unit matches the listing. Make-ready (also "turn") is the work between one tenant's move-out and the next one's move-in.

Each vacant day costs rent. On a $1,800 unit, one day is about $59 of lost rent ($1,800 × 12 ÷ 365). A 14-day turn costs about $828 in rent before any repair bill. So the 2 goals are a safe, clean unit and a short turn.

## What is the order of make-ready tasks?

The order of make-ready tasks runs from inspection to the dirtiest work to the cleanest work. A fixed order stops one trade from undoing another's work, for example a painter dripping on new carpet.

| Step | Task | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Move-out inspection with photos and video | Sets the deposit claims and the scope |
| 2 | Scope and order parts, book vendors | Parts and vendors set the timeline |
| 3 | Trash-out and remove the tenant's items | Clears space for trades |
| 4 | Repairs: plumbing, electrical, doors, drywall | Fix before you cover |
| 5 | Paint or touch-up | After drywall, before floors |
| 6 | Flooring: replace or clean carpet, repair hard floors | After paint |
| 7 | Deep clean | After all trades |
| 8 | Final quality check, alarms, locks | Last, so nothing changes after |
| 9 | Listing photos | Unit at its best |

Watch for: deposit rules need photos at the right time. In California, for tenancies that began on or after July 1, 2025, the landlord must photograph the unit at move-in. Since April 1, 2025, the landlord must also photograph it after the tenant returns possession and before repairs, and again after repairs (Civ. Code 1950.5). Take your step-1 photos before any vendor starts.

## Which items do you replace every turn?

Replace a short list of low-cost items at every turn, even if they look fine. A fresh set costs little and cuts first-month service calls.

- [ ] HVAC filter (the DOE says to clean or replace filters once a month or as recommended)
- [ ] Lock cores or rekey all exterior doors; new keys and new codes
- [ ] Smoke alarm batteries; replace any alarm over 10 years old (USFA)
- [ ] Light bulbs to one color and type
- [ ] Stove drip pans and range hood filter
- [ ] Toilet seats, if worn or stained
- [ ] Broken or bent blinds
- [ ] Outlet and switch covers that are cracked
- [ ] Shower head and aerators, if scaled
- [ ] Caulk at tub, shower and kitchen sink, if it is cracked or dark

Keep these items in stock in a turn kit. Buying in bulk for 20 or 50 doors saves a trip on every turn.

## The full rent-ready checklist

Use this list for the final quality check in step 8. Every line must pass before you list the unit as ready.

**Safety**

- [ ] Each smoke alarm and CO alarm sounds on test
- [ ] All exterior doors lock; deadbolts throw fully
- [ ] Windows open, close and lock; screens in place
- [ ] Stair rails firm
- [ ] GFCI outlets trip and reset in the kitchen, baths and outside
- [ ] Dryer vent clear

**Systems**

- [ ] Heat and cooling run; thermostat works
- [ ] Hot water at every tap within a short time
- [ ] No leaks under sinks, at toilets or at the water heater
- [ ] All drains run clear
- [ ] Every light and fan works

**Appliances**

- [ ] Refrigerator cold, clean, no odor
- [ ] Stove: each burner and the oven heat
- [ ] Dishwasher runs a full cycle with no leak
- [ ] Washer and dryer, if included, run

**Finish**

- [ ] Walls clean, patched and painted; no nail holes
- [ ] Floors clean; carpet free of stains and odor
- [ ] Cabinets and drawers open and close; clean inside
- [ ] Bathrooms clean, mirrors clear
- [ ] Yard, walks and entry clean and tidy

**Paperwork**

- [ ] Move-in condition report ready with dated photos
- [ ] Lead-based paint disclosure and EPA pamphlet ready for pre-1978 units
- [ ] Keys and codes logged

## Which safety and lead rules apply to turnover work?

Lead-paint rules apply to turnover work in housing built before 1978. The EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) rule says anyone paid to disturb painted surfaces in a pre-1978 home must be certified and use lead-safe work practices. The EPA says landlords and property management companies must comply when they disturb paint in a pre-1978 rental.

The EPA disclosure rule also applies before the next lease is signed. You must give the pamphlet "Protect Your Family From Lead In Your Home," disclose known lead-based paint and reports, and include the Lead Warning Statement. Keep a signed copy for 3 years after the lease begins.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do you keep turn time short?

You keep turn time short by planning the turn before the tenant leaves. Most lost days come from waiting: for a vendor, a part or a decision.

1. **Do a pre-move-out visit** 2 to 3 weeks before move-out, where your state allows it. List the likely work.
2. **Order long-lead items early**, such as flooring and appliances.
3. **Book vendors** for set days in the week after move-out.
4. **Set an approval limit** with the owner, so small repairs do not wait for a reply.
5. **Run trades in parallel** where they do not conflict, for example a plumber and an electrician on the same day.
6. **Market the unit early** with good photos from the last turn, if the lease allows showings.
7. **Track turn days** for each unit: move-out date to ready date.

Worked example for a light turn on a 2-bedroom unit:

| Day | Work |
|---|---|
| Day 0 | Move-out, inspection, photos |
| Day 1 | Trash-out, repairs start |
| Day 2 | Repairs done, patch drywall |
| Day 3 | Paint |
| Day 4 | Carpet clean, hard floor touch-up |
| Day 5 | Deep clean |
| Day 6 | Final check, rekey, listing photos |

That is 6 days of work if no part is late. At $59 a day on an $1,800 unit, cutting a turn from 14 days to 6 saves about $473 in rent.

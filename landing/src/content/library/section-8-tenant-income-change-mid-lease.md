## What is an interim reexamination?

An interim reexamination is a check of the family's income between yearly reviews. 24 CFR 982.516 requires the PHA (public housing agency) to review income at least once a year. Between those reviews, a family may ask for an interim review when its income or household changes.

The PHA must act in 2 cases:

1. **The family asks.** The family reports a drop in income or a new household member. The PHA may decline if the drop is less than 10% of annual adjusted income, or a lower PHA threshold (982.516(c)(2)).
2. **The PHA learns of an increase.** The PHA must do an interim review when adjusted income will rise 10% or more (982.516(c)(3)).

There are 2 limits on the second case. The PHA may not count a rise in earned income (wages) unless the family got an interim decrease earlier in the same certification period. And the PHA may skip the interim review in the last 3 months before the yearly review.

What this means for you: a tenant who gets a raise often does not trigger a change until the yearly review. A tenant who loses a job often does.

## Does my total rent change?

No. Your total rent does not change. The rent to owner is fixed by the lease and the HAP contract (the Housing Assistance Payments contract). Only the split between the PHA and the family changes.

24 CFR 982.505 sets the HAP as the lower of:

- the payment standard minus the total tenant payment (TTP); or
- the gross rent (rent to owner plus the utility allowance) minus the TTP.

Under 24 CFR 5.628, the TTP is the highest of 30% of monthly adjusted income, 10% of monthly income, a welfare rent where it applies, or the PHA minimum rent. So when income goes down, TTP goes down and the HAP goes up. The rent you receive in total stays the same.

## Example with numbers

Here is one unit before and after an income change. These numbers are an example, not data from a PHA.

- Rent to owner: $1,400
- Utility allowance (tenant pays electric): $100
- Gross rent: $1,500
- Payment standard: $1,600

| Step | Before | After a job loss | After income goes to $0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly adjusted income | $2,000 | $1,000 | $0 |
| TTP (30% of adjusted income, or minimum rent) | $600 | $300 | $50 (minimum rent) |
| HAP: lower of $1,600 − TTP or $1,500 − TTP | $900 | $1,200 | $1,450 |
| PHA pays owner (lesser of HAP or rent to owner) | $900 | $1,200 | $1,400 |
| Tenant pays owner | $500 | $200 | $0 |
| Utility reimbursement to family | $0 | $0 | $50 |
| Owner total | $1,400 | $1,400 | $1,400 |

In the last column the HAP ($1,450) is more than the rent to owner ($1,400). Under 24 CFR 982.514, the PHA pays you the rent to owner and may pay the $50 balance to the family or the utility company as a utility reimbursement.

Simplified: we use 30% of adjusted income and skip the 10% test, deductions and welfare rent. To check your own unit, use our [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator).

## How fast does the PHA change the HAP?

The PHA must do an interim review "within a reasonable period of time". 24 CFR 982.516(c)(1) says that "generally should not be longer than 30 days" after the change is reported. Then the effective date depends on the direction and on whether the family reported on time.

| Case | When the new family share starts | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Family share goes down, reported on time | First of the month after the date of the reported change | 982.516(c)(4)(i) |
| Family share goes up, reported on time | First of the month after a 30-day notice to the family | 982.516(c)(4)(i) |
| Family share goes up, reported late | Back-dated to the first of the month after the change | 982.516(c)(4)(ii) |
| Family share goes down, reported late | No later than the first rent period after the review; back-dating is at PHA discretion | 982.516(c)(4)(ii) |

Watch for: a back-dated change can make a HAP statement look wrong. A one-time catch-up amount may appear. Match the statement to the effective date in the PHA notice before you call.

## What if the tenant's income goes to zero?

If the tenant's income goes to zero, the TTP falls to the minimum rent. 24 CFR 5.630 lets a voucher PHA set a minimum rent of up to $50. The PHA must grant a hardship exemption in some cases, for example when the family lost eligibility for another assistance program.

For you, a zero-income tenant often means the PHA pays almost all of the rent. The HAP goes up to cover the gap, but never above the rent to owner. Any balance goes as a utility reimbursement.

The opposite case also matters. If the tenant's income rises so far that the TTP covers the full gross rent, the HAP becomes $0. The tenant then pays you the full rent. The HAP contract ends automatically 180 calendar days after the last HAP payment (24 CFR 982.455). If income drops again within those 180 days, the HAP can start again.

## Who tells me about the change?

The family tells the PHA, and the PHA changes the HAP. 24 CFR 982.516(d) makes each PHA adopt a policy on when the family must report changes. The 30-day notice in the rule goes to the family. The rule text we read does not set a separate owner notice.

So set up your own check:

- [ ] Compare each month's HAP deposit with the last month.
- [ ] When the HAP changes, look for a PHA change notice or a portal update.
- [ ] Bill the tenant only the new tenant share from the effective date.
- [ ] If the tenant share falls and the tenant already paid the old amount, credit the difference.
- [ ] Keep each PHA notice in the unit file.
- [ ] If a HAP drops to $0, note the date. Mark day 180.

Do not charge the tenant the HAP part of the rent during a PHA delay. Under 24 CFR 982.451(b)(4), the family is not responsible for the portion covered by the HAP. You may not accept more from the tenant than the rent to owner minus the HAP.

## Why the repair or improvement label matters

The label decides when you get the tax benefit. A $12,000 repair can be deducted this year. A $12,000 improvement to a rental house is often spread over 27.5 years, per IRS Publication 527.

Worked example, with example numbers:

| Same $12,000 bill | Year 1 deduction | Years to recover |
|-------------------|------------------|------------------|
| Treated as a repair | $12,000 | 1 |
| Treated as an improvement, 27.5-year straight line | About $436 (less in the first year under the mid-month rule) | 27.5 |

The label also changes your basis. Improvements raise your basis, which lowers your gain when you sell. Repairs do not. Publication 527 says to separate the costs of repairs and improvements and keep accurate records.

## What is the IRS betterment, restoration and adaptation test?

The betterment, restoration and adaptation test is the IRS rule for spotting an improvement. The tangible property regulations set the rule. A unit of property is improved only if you pay for a betterment, a restoration, or an adaptation to a new or different use.

Step 1 is to pick the unit. For a building, the IRS FAQ says you apply the test to the building structure and to each key building system on its own. The key systems are plumbing, electrical, HVAC, elevators, escalators, fire protection and alarm, gas distribution, and security.

Step 2 is the 3 questions:

| Test | IRS meaning (Tangible Property FAQ) | Rental example |
|------|-------------------------------------|----------------|
| Betterment | Fixes a material defect that existed before you bought it; adds a material addition or capacity; or is expected to materially increase productivity, efficiency, strength or quality | Fixing a foundation crack the seller left; adding a bedroom |
| Restoration | Replaces a major component or a substantial structural part, or rebuilds to like-new condition, among other cases | Replacing the whole roof; replacing the full HVAC system |
| Adaptation | Adapts the property to a use not consistent with your ordinary use when you placed it in service | Turning a rental house into an office |

The FAQ says the word "material" has no fixed percentage. Use common sense and reasonable judgment for your own facts.

## What is the de minimis safe harbor?

The de minimis safe harbor is a yearly election to deduct low-cost items instead of capitalizing them. It is the simplest rule for small owners.

The IRS limits, from the Tangible Property FAQ:

| Owner type | Limit per invoice or per item |
|------------|-------------------------------|
| No applicable financial statement (most individual landlords) | $2,500 |
| With an applicable financial statement (for example, a certified audited statement) | $5,000 |

How it works:

- **You elect it each year** by attaching a statement to your timely filed return. Publication 527 says you then deduct these amounts as rental expenses on line 19 of Schedule E.
- **You must treat the cost the same way in your books.** The safe harbor covers amounts you also deduct in your books and records.
- **It is per item or invoice.** A $1,900 refrigerator qualifies. A $4,000 furnace on one invoice does not.
- **The election is not a change in accounting method.** The FAQ says you do not file Form 3115 to use it.

## What is the routine maintenance safe harbor?

The routine maintenance safe harbor lets you deduct recurring work that keeps the property running, even if it would otherwise count as a restoration. The IRS FAQ lists 4 conditions. The work must be:

1. **Recurring** work you expect to do.
2. **Caused by your use** of the property in the rental business.
3. **Meant to keep the property** in its ordinarily efficient operating condition.
4. **Expected more than once in 10 years** for a building structure or building system, judged when you place it in service.

Limits to know:

- **It does not cover betterments.** Upgrades fail this safe harbor.
- **It can cover some restorations**, including replacing a major component, if the 4 conditions hold.

Example: servicing and replacing worn parts of the HVAC system every 3 years can fit. Replacing the whole system after 20 years does not, because you did not expect it more than once in 10 years.

## What is the safe harbor for small taxpayers?

The safe harbor for small taxpayers lets small owners deduct all work on a modest building in a year. The total must stay under a cap. It is the best fit for one or a few lower-cost rentals.

The IRS FAQ lists 3 requirements:

| Requirement | Limit |
|-------------|-------|
| Average annual gross receipts | $10 million or less |
| Unadjusted basis of the building | $1 million or less |
| Yearly repairs, maintenance and improvements on that building | The lesser of $10,000 or 2% of the building's unadjusted basis |

Worked example: your building's unadjusted basis is $300,000. 2% of that is $6,000, which is less than $10,000. If your total work on the building this year is $5,500, you can elect to deduct all of it. If the total is $6,500, the safe harbor does not apply to any of it for that building that year.

You make the election each year with a statement attached to your return. The FAQ says you do not file Form 3115 for it.

## Can you show examples in a table?

Yes. Here is how common rental jobs usually fall. Facts change the answer, so treat this as a guide, not a ruling.

| Job | Usually | Why |
|-----|---------|-----|
| Patch a roof leak | Repair | Keeps the roof working |
| Replace the whole roof | Improvement | Publication 527 lists a new roof as an improvement |
| Repaint between tenants | Repair | Keeps the home in rentable state |
| Replace a broken faucet | Repair | Small part, same quality |
| Replace all plumbing pipes | Improvement | Restores a key building system |
| Service the furnace | Repair | Routine maintenance |
| Replace the furnace | Improvement | Publication 527 lists a furnace as an improvement |
| New refrigerator, $1,800 on one invoice | Capital item, but may be deducted under de minimis | Under $2,500 per item or invoice |
| Add a deck or a bedroom | Improvement | Betterment: material addition |
| Fix a defect that was there when you bought it | Improvement | Betterment: pre-existing defect |
| Replace a few damaged floor boards | Repair | Small part of the structure |
| Convert a house to an office | Improvement | Adaptation |

What to do at year end:

- [ ] List every job over $500 with date, cost and what was done.
- [ ] Apply the 3-question test to each.
- [ ] Check if the de minimis safe harbor covers items up to $2,500.
- [ ] Check if total work on the building fits the small taxpayer cap.
- [ ] Attach the election statements you choose to your return.
- [ ] Add improvements to your depreciation schedule and basis records.

If a manager handles repairs for you, ask for invoices that name the work and the part, not just "maintenance". It makes the test possible. Compare manager costs with our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## Can an agency guarantee that ChatGPT will recommend you?

No agency can guarantee that ChatGPT, Gemini or Google AI will recommend your property management company. The engines decide the answer each time a person asks. The same question can get a different list an hour later.

Google says it plainly for classic search: "No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google." Its guide on hiring an SEO adds that if someone guarantees first place, you should find someone else. Google also says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

AI answers move even more than classic results. On 2026-10-19 we asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude the same question for the 50 largest US cities: "What is the best property management company in <City, ST>? Give names." (our data, 150 answers). The engines did not agree:

| Engine | Companies named per answer (average) | Answers that linked a manager's own site |
|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT | 5.3 | 15 of 50 |
| Gemini | 5.7 | 48 of 50 |
| Claude | 15.5 | 43 of 50 |

By exact company name, 17 of the 50 cities had no company that all 3 engines named. Spelling differences make that number a bit high, but the point holds. "Recommended by AI" depends on the engine, the day and the words of the prompt.

Disclosure: citata.ai sells GEO work too. We do not guarantee a mention, and in our own baseline citata.ai was named in 0 of 108 non-brand answers (our data, 2026-10-18).

## What can a GEO agency control?

A GEO agency (a firm that works on AI answers) controls its own work, not the engine. The useful question is which inputs the agency can change and which it can only influence.

| The agency controls | The agency can influence | No one controls |
|---|---|---|
| Pages it writes on your site | Your listings on Expertise.com, Yelp, BBB | Which sources an engine picks today |
| Facts, fees and city pages it fixes | How many owners leave reviews | The model version and its training data |
| Schema and crawl access | Local news and directory mentions | Personal history of the person who asks |
| The prompt list and the weekly report | How the engines describe you over months | Paid placement (none exists for these answers) |

The middle column matters most. In our 50-city test, Expertise.com was a source in 93 of 150 answers, and directories or review sites appeared in 132 of 150 (our data). An agency can help you get onto those lists. It cannot make an engine read them on a given day.

## What do guarantee terms say?

Guarantee terms fall into 3 kinds, and only 1 of them moves risk to the agency. Citant.ai, an AI search agency, sorts them this way in its own guide. Citant sells a guaranteed pilot, so read its view as a seller's view.

1. **Effort guarantee.** The agency promises work: 8 pages, 20 listings fixed, a monthly report. You can check it, but it says nothing about results.
2. **Trend guarantee.** The agency promises that a metric will go up, such as "AI visibility score". The agency often picks and measures the metric.
3. **Outcome guarantee.** The agency promises a defined result by a date, with a cash remedy if it misses.

Citant's comparison says 6 of the 10 agencies it reviewed promise no outcome in writing. Its own pilot uses a written target: named on at least 3 of 10 agreed queries, on at least 2 of 6 tracked platforms, within 9 weeks, or a cash refund.

You do not need to buy any one agency's offer. Use the shape: a written prompt list, a target, a date and a remedy.

## Money back vs free month: what is the difference?

A money-back remedy costs the agency money when it misses. A free-month remedy costs the agency only time, and you keep paying in waiting.

| Term | Money back | Free extra month |
|---|---|---|
| What you get on a miss | Cash, to the original payment method | More of the same work |
| Who carries the risk | The agency | You |
| What to check | Full or part refund, how fast, cash or credit | Is there a cap, and what happens after it |
| When it fits | Short pilot with a clear target | Long retainer where the work itself has value |

A free month is not a bad deal by itself. It is a service credit, not a guarantee of a result. Name it that way when you compare offers.

## Which promises are red flags?

Some promises are false for any agency to make, because AI answers vary. Treat these as red flags:

- **"#1 in ChatGPT."** AI answers have no fixed rank. Lists change in length and order.
- **"Every query, every platform."** Our test showed Claude naming about 3 times as many companies as ChatGPT for the same question.
- **"Permanent results."** Answers change when models and sources change.
- **"We have a deal with OpenAI or Google."** Google says there is no way to pay for better local ranking.
- **A score only the agency can measure.** If you cannot rerun the test, you cannot check the claim.
- **No prompt list in writing.** Without the list, the agency can pick easy prompts at the end, such as your brand name.

Google's hiring guide also warns about unsolicited emails from SEO firms and about firms that will not explain their work. Both rules apply to GEO sellers.

## How do you write a fair test into the contract?

A fair test fixes the prompts, the engines and the count before work starts. Here is a simple version you can paste into a proposal review.

1. **Agree on 10 to 20 prompts** in writing. Use the words owners use: "best property manager in Tampa", "who manages single-family rentals in Tampa".
2. **Name the engines.** For example ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode.
3. **Set the runs.** Each prompt, each engine, 2 runs. 20 prompts × 3 engines × 2 runs = 120 answers.
4. **Run a baseline in week 0.** Save every answer, not only a summary.
5. **Set the target and the date.** For example: named in 12 of 120 answers by week 12.
6. **Set the remedy.** Refund, part refund or service credit. Write which one.
7. **Exclude brand prompts.** "Is <your company> good?" tests nothing.

Watch for: a vendor that refuses step 4. If there is no baseline, there is no proof of change.

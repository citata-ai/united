## What is the family share at move-in?

The family share is the part of rent and utilities the family pays. 24 CFR 982.515(a) defines it as the gross rent minus the housing assistance payment (HAP). Gross rent is the rent to owner plus the utility allowance for utilities the tenant pays.

Three terms drive the math:

| Term | What it is | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Total tenant payment (TTP) | The highest of 30% of monthly adjusted income, 10% of monthly income, the welfare rent, or the minimum rent | 24 CFR 5.628 |
| Payment standard | The PHA's subsidy cap for the unit size | 24 CFR 982.505 |
| HAP | The lower of (payment standard − TTP) or (gross rent − TTP) | 24 CFR 982.505(b) |

If the gross rent is at or below the payment standard, the family share equals the TTP. That is usually about 30% of adjusted income, so the 40% limit does not bite. If the gross rent is above the payment standard, the family pays the TTP plus the amount over the standard. That is where the 40% limit applies.

The limit is in the statute, 42 U.S.C. 1437f(o)(3), and in 24 CFR 982.508: "the family share must not exceed 40 percent of the family's adjusted monthly income." The PHA cannot approve the tenancy if the unit fails it (24 CFR 982.305(a)(5)).

## How do I calculate whether my rent passes the 40% limit?

Use one formula. It comes straight from the HAP rule above.

**Max gross rent = payment standard + (0.40 × monthly adjusted income − TTP)**

**Max rent to owner = max gross rent − utility allowance**

Steps:

1. **Get the payment standard.** Use the PHA's standard for the smaller of the voucher size and the unit size.
2. **Get the utility allowance.** The PHA's schedule, for the utilities the tenant will pay.
3. **Get the family's monthly adjusted income and TTP.** The PHA has them. They come from income verified no earlier than 60 days before the voucher was issued (982.508).
4. **Apply the formula.** If your rent is at or below the result, it passes the 40% test.
5. **Check rent reasonableness too.** A rent can pass the 40% test and still fail the comparison with unassisted units.

## Does the utility allowance count?

Yes, the utility allowance counts in full. The family share is based on gross rent, and gross rent includes the allowance. The Maricopa County housing authority puts it plainly in its RFTA steps: the family's portion "must not exceed more than 40% of their Adjusted income (for rent AND utilities)."

So the same rent can pass in a unit where you pay the utilities and fail where the tenant pays them. A higher utility allowance lowers the highest rent you can charge, dollar for dollar.

## Does the 40% limit apply at renewal?

No. The limit applies only "at the time the PHA approves a tenancy for initial occupancy of a dwelling unit" (24 CFR 982.508). The Santa Cruz County housing authority confirms it: "After the initial lease term, the tenant may pay more than 40% of income in rent."

A later rent increase still needs PHA approval and must pass rent reasonableness. But the 40% cap is not part of that test.

The limit does apply again if the family moves to a new unit. Each new tenancy with a voucher is a new initial occupancy for that unit.

## Worked example with a 2-bedroom unit

The numbers are for illustration only. Your PHA's payment standard and utility allowance will differ.

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Family annual adjusted income | $24,000 |
| Monthly adjusted income | $2,000 |
| TTP (30% of adjusted income; assume it is the highest of the 4 tests) | $600 |
| 40% of monthly adjusted income | $800 |
| PHA payment standard, 2 bedrooms | $1,900 |
| Utility allowance (tenant pays power and gas) | $150 |

Now apply the formula:

| Step | Math | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Max gross rent | $1,900 + ($800 − $600) | $2,100 |
| Max rent to owner | $2,100 − $150 | $1,950 |

Check three asking rents:

| Asking rent | Gross rent | HAP | Family share | % of adjusted income | Passes? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1,700 | $1,850 | $1,250 | $600 | 30% | Yes |
| $1,950 | $2,100 | $1,300 | $800 | 40% | Yes, at the limit |
| $2,050 | $2,200 | $1,300 | $900 | 45% | No |

At $2,050 the HAP stays at $1,300, because it cannot go above the payment standard minus the TTP. The extra $100 lands on the family and breaks the 40% limit. To pass, you would lower the rent by $100, to $1,950.

Do not fix it with a side deal. The HAP contract form (HUD-52641) says the owner will not receive any payment for the unit beyond the rent to owner. The tenancy addendum says the owner must return any excess rent to the tenant.

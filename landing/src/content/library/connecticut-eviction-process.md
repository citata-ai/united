## Connecticut eviction rules at a glance

Connecticut calls an eviction "summary process". We read each rule below in the official statutes on the Connecticut General Assembly site, including the 2026 Supplement with the 2025 changes.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Rent grace period | 9 days after due date (4 days for a weekly tenancy) | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-15a(a) |
| Notice to quit | At least 3 days before the quit date | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-23(a) |
| Service of notice to quit | Attested copy served by a proper officer | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-23(c) |
| Right-to-counsel notice | Attach to notice to quit and complaint (covered tenants) | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-75(f) |
| Lease violation notice | At least 15 days; tenant may cure in that time | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-15 |
| Complaint return day | At least 6 days after service | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-23a(a) |
| Tenant appearance | Within 2 days after the return day | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-26 |
| Court entry fee | $175 | Conn. Gen. Stat. 52-259(a) |
| Stay of execution | 5 days after judgment, Sundays and holidays excluded | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-35(a) |
| Longer court stay | Up to 6 months; 3 months for nonpayment | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-37, 47a-39 |
| Removal | State marshal; tenant notice at least 24 hours before | Conn. Gen. Stat. 47a-42 |
| Lockout | Class C misdemeanor; double damages | Conn. Gen. Stat. 53a-214, 47a-46 |

## What notice must a Connecticut landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

A Connecticut landlord must first wait out the rent grace period, then serve a notice to quit. Section 47a-15a(a) gives the tenant 9 days after the due date, or 4 days in a one-week tenancy. Only then is nonpayment a ground for a notice to quit under section 47a-23(a)(1)(D).

The notice to quit has strict rules:

- **Time.** It must give at least 3 days before the date the tenant must leave (47a-23(a)).
- **Form.** It must be in writing, name the address and unit, give the quit date and state the reason in the statute's words (47a-23(b)).
- **Service.** The current text, as amended by Public Act 25-78, says a proper officer serves an attested copy on each lessee or occupant, or leaves it at the residence (47a-23(c)). In practice that means a state marshal.
- **Right to counsel.** Since 2021-10-01, the landlord must attach the Judicial Branch's one-page right-to-counsel notice to a notice to quit and to the summons and complaint for a covered tenant (47a-75(f)(2)). Covered tenants include households at or below 80% of state median income and people on programs such as SNAP, Medicaid or a Housing Choice Voucher (47a-75(a)). You often do not know a tenant's income, so ask your attorney whether to attach it to every notice.

**New in 2026.** From 2026-01-01, the grace period is 5 days longer for any month when the landlord's online payment system prevented payment. A nonpayment notice to quit is not allowed in that case (Nov. Sp. Sess. Public Act 25-1, sections 47a-15a and 47a-23).

## How long does an eviction take in Connecticut?

An uncontested Connecticut nonpayment eviction takes about 4 to 5 weeks by the statute minimums. That is our math from the chapter 832 deadlines. Court calendars and tenant defenses add time.

| Step | Statute minimum | Earliest day (rent due Day 1) |
|---|---|---|
| Grace period ends | 9 days after due date | Day 10 |
| Marshal serves notice to quit | Day after grace period | Day 11 |
| Quit date | At least 3 days after notice | About Day 15 |
| Marshal serves summons and complaint | After the quit date | About Day 16 |
| Return day | At least 6 days after service | About Day 22 |
| Tenant appearance deadline | 2 days after return day | About Day 24 |
| Default judgment if no appearance | First court day after the motion | About Day 25 |
| Stay of execution ends | 5 days, Sundays and holidays excluded | About Day 31 |

If the tenant appears, the case goes to pleadings and trial (47a-26a to 47a-26d). The landlord may ask the court to order use and occupancy payments while the case is open (47a-26b). After judgment, a tenant may apply for a longer stay. The court may grant up to 6 months in total, but only 3 months if the judgment was for nonpayment (47a-39). For a nonpayment judgment, the tenant must first deposit the full arrearage with the clerk within 5 days (47a-37(a)).

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Connecticut, and what does it cost?

A Connecticut landlord files summary process in the Superior Court. The complaint is returnable to the Superior Court (47a-23a(a)). Venue is the geographical area where the tenant lives or where the unit is, at the landlord's choice (47a-23a(b)).

In the judicial districts of Hartford, New Britain, New Haven, Bridgeport, Waterbury and Stamford-Norwalk, the case first goes on the housing docket (47a-70(a)).

The entry fee for "summary process and landlord and tenant actions" is $175 (Conn. Gen. Stat. 52-259(a)). The landlord also pays the state marshal for serving the notice, the complaint and the execution. The plaintiff pays the marshal for removal of the tenant's goods and may recover that cost from the tenant (47a-42(c)).

## Can a Connecticut landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes, a Connecticut landlord can evict for a lease violation, but most violations need a 15-day notice first. Section 47a-15 requires a written notice that names the breach and ends the lease on a date at least 15 days after the tenant receives it. If the tenant can fix the breach by repair or by paying damages, and does so in those 15 days, the lease does not end. If the same breach recurs within 6 months, the landlord may go straight to a notice to quit.

The 15-day notice does not apply to nonpayment of rent or to a "serious nuisance" (47a-15). Serious nuisance includes violence or threats against another tenant or the landlord, and willful destruction of the unit. It also covers conduct that is an immediate and serious danger, and use of the unit for prostitution or illegal drug sales.

No-cause evictions are possible in many cases. Section 47a-23(a)(1)(A) lists "lapse of time" as a ground, which covers a lease that has ended. A big exception applies in buildings or complexes with 5 or more units. There, tenants who are 62 or older, or who have a qualifying physical or mental disability, may be evicted only for the reasons in section 47a-23c(b). These include nonpayment, material noncompliance, refusal of a fair rent increase, removal of the unit from the market, and the landlord's own use. Since 2024-01-01, landlords of those buildings must give tenants a written notice of these rights at each new lease or renewal (47a-23c(e)).

Section 47a-33 also makes retaliation a defense. A tenant can defend a case brought only because the tenant reported a code violation or complained to a fair rent commission.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Connecticut?

A state marshal removes the tenant, not the landlord. Under section 47a-42(a), the tenant must leave once judgment enters and any stay ends. If the tenant stays, the landlord gets a summary process execution, and a state marshal removes the occupants and their goods.

Section 47a-42(b), as amended by Public Act 25-78, sets the order. The marshal notifies the town's chief executive officer first. Then the marshal tries to notify the tenant at least 24 hours before the removal and serves a copy of the execution. The execution warns that a person who refuses to leave when the marshal returns may be arrested for criminal trespass in the first degree.

The marshal takes the goods to the town's storage site. If the tenant does not reclaim them and pay storage within 15 days, the town sells them at public auction (47a-42(c)). The court may not issue an execution more than 6 months after judgment, not counting time under a stay (47a-41a).

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Connecticut?

No. A Connecticut self-help eviction is illegal and can be a crime. Section 53a-214 makes it criminal lockout when a landlord or agent, without a court order, deprives a tenant of access to the unit or possessions. Criminal lockout is a class C misdemeanor.

Civil law adds more risk.

- **Lease clauses fail.** A lease may not let the landlord dispossess the tenant without a court order (47a-4(a)(5)). Such a clause is unenforceable.
- **Entry and detainer.** A tenant put out by force or held out of the unit may sue under section 47a-43. The court sets a hearing within 8 days. The tenant may recover double damages and costs (47a-46).
- **Shutoffs.** If a landlord who must supply heat, water, hot water, electricity or gas fails to do so, the tenant may buy the service and deduct the cost, or move to substitute housing. For a willful failure, the tenant may end the lease and recover the greater of 2 months' rent or double damages (47a-13).

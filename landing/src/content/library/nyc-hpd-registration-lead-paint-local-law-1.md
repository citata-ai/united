## Which NYC buildings must register with HPD each year?

NYC owners must register a residential building with the Department of Housing Preservation and Development (HPD) each year if it falls in one of 2 groups, per HPD:

- **Multiple dwellings:** 3 or more residential units.
- **Private dwellings:** 1-2 residential units where neither the owner nor the owner's immediate family lives.

The deadline is September 1 each year. You must also file again whenever ownership changes or the data changes, for example a new managing agent.

How to register, per HPD:

1. **Fill in the form** in HPD's Property Registration Online System (PROS), or use the form HPD mails you.
2. **Print, sign and mail it.** Both the agent and the owner sign. The address is HPD, Church Street Station, PO Box 3888, New York, NY 10008-3888.
3. **Pay the $13 fee.** The Department of Finance bills it on your property tax Statement of Account, due July 1.
4. **Check for a receipt.** If none arrives in 2 to 4 weeks, check HPDONLINE or PROS.

HPD uses the registration contacts for all official notices and for emergencies. Keep them current.

Watch for: HPD registration is not the same as rent stabilization registration. If the building is rent stabilized, you also register the building and its apartments with New York State Housing and Community Renewal (HCR), as HPD calls it, by July 31. HPD notes a fine of $500 per unregistered unit for each month an HCR registration is late.

## What happens if a NYC owner does not register with HPD?

A NYC owner who does not register with HPD faces civil penalties and loses access to key tools. Local Law 71 of 2023 amended Administrative Code 27-2107, effective December 8, 2023.

| Failure | Penalty (per HPD) |
|---|---|
| No registration, multiple dwelling of 5 units or less | $500 to $1,500 |
| No registration, multiple dwelling over 5 units | $1,000 to $5,000 |
| Willfully false registration | $750 to $5,000, and HPD voids the statement |

Other effects of a missing registration, per HPD:

- **HPD may issue Orders** to register.
- **You cannot certify violations** as corrected.
- **You cannot request a Code Violation Dismissal.**
- **You cannot start a court case** to recover possession for nonpayment of rent.

The last point is the costly one. Worked example: a two-bedroom at $2,971 a month, HUD's FY2027 Fair Market Rent for the New York metro. If a tenant stops paying and your registration lapsed, you must register before you can file. Two months of delay is $5,942 of unpaid rent at that level. That is 457 times the $13 fee. See the [New York Fair Market Rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/new-york-fair-market-rent-2027) for other sizes.

## What does Local Law 1 of 2004 require on lead paint?

Local Law 1 of 2004 requires owners of older buildings to presume paint contains lead and to manage it. HPD says it applies to:

- **Multiple dwellings built before 1960.**
- **Multiple dwellings built from January 1, 1960 to January 1, 1978,** if the owner knows there is lead-based paint.
- **Tenant-occupied units in 1-2 family homes built before 1960.**
- **Co-op and condo units** not occupied by the owner or the owner's family.

The duties, per HPD:

1. **Send the Annual Notice** each January. It asks if a child under 6 lives in the unit or spends 10 or more hours a week there. Tenants return it by February 16.
2. **Inspect at least once a year** units with a child under 6, and the common areas.
3. **Repair peeling paint** in units with a child under 6 and in common areas, using certified firms and safe work practices.
4. **Fix at turnover.** When a unit becomes vacant, or a child under 6 comes to live there, remove lead paint from doors and windows and do the other required work.
5. **Keep records for at least 10 years:** notices, tenant replies, inspections, inspector names, test results and exemptions.

HPD defines a lead-based paint hazard as peeling or disturbed paint in a pre-1960 building, in a unit with a child under 6, unless tests show less than 0.5 mg/cm² of lead. HPD picks buildings for record audits through its Building Lead Index. If you miss a repair deadline, HPD may do the emergency repair and bill it to the property through the Department of Finance.

## What did Local Law 31 add on lead inspections?

Local Law 31 of 2020 added a one-time XRF test of all painted surfaces. XRF (X-ray fluorescence) is a device that reads lead in paint without lab samples. HPD's FAQ sets out the rule:

| Question | HPD answer |
|---|---|
| Which buildings | Pre-1960 buildings with rental units; 1960-1978 buildings if the owner knows of lead paint; rental units in 1-2 family homes |
| What to test | All rental units, including vacant ones, and all common areas |
| Deadline | August 9, 2025, or within 1 year after a child under 6 moves in, or a DOHMH order, whichever is first |
| How often | Once |
| Who tests | An EPA-certified lead inspector or risk assessor, not the owner, the owner's agent or a remediation contractor |
| Action level | 0.5 mg/cm² |
| Penalty for missing it | A Class C immediately hazardous violation, civil penalties of $1,500 per unit or common area |
| Records | Keep for 10 years and give to HPD on request |

If a tenant will not give access, HPD expects proof of effort. Send written notice, often 48 to 72 hours ahead, by certified, registered or first class mail with proof of mailing. Keep an affidavit with the dates of each attempt. HPD publishes a sample "Affidavit of No Access to Perform XRF Testing."

The August 2025 deadline has passed. If your pre-1960 building has untested units, the exposure is $1,500 per unit. For a 12-unit walk-up with 3 untested apartments and 1 hallway, that is 4 × $1,500 = $6,000.

## What should a NYC owner do this year?

A NYC owner can cover HPD and lead duties with one yearly calendar:

- **January:** send the Local Law 1 Annual Notice to every unit.
- **February 16:** follow up on notices not returned.
- **Through the year:** inspect units with a child under 6 and the common areas; fix peeling paint with certified workers.
- **At each turnover:** do the required door and window work before the next tenant.
- **July 1:** pay the $13 registration fee on your DOF statement.
- **July 31:** file HCR registration if any units are rent stabilized.
- **September 1:** file the HPD registration.
- **Any time:** update HPD within the year when the owner or managing agent changes.
- **Once:** confirm the Local Law 31 XRF test is done and the records are filed.

If a manager runs your building, ask to see last year's HPD receipt and the XRF test report. For more on hiring a manager, see our [property management page](/property-management).

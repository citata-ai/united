New Jersey puts the security deposit rules in N.J.S.A. 46:8-19 to 46:8-26. The Department of Community Affairs (DCA) publishes the statute text and calls it the Security Deposit Law. I read the DCA statute text and the DCA Truth in Renting guide on 2026-10-19. Here is the short version.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | 1.5 times one month's rent | N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.2 |
| Extra security each year | Not more than 10% of the current deposit | N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.2 |
| Where the money goes | Interest-bearing account at a New Jersey bank, in trust, not mixed with your money | N.J.S.A. 46:8-19 |
| Notice of bank and account | Within 30 days of receipt, and at each yearly interest payment | N.J.S.A. 46:8-19(c) |
| Interest to tenant | Each year, cash or rent credit | N.J.S.A. 46:8-19(c) |
| Return after the lease ends | Within 30 days, with itemized interest and charges | N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.1 |
| Return after fire, flood, condemnation, evacuation | Within 5 business days | N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.1 |
| Penalty for wrongful withholding | Double the amount, plus costs | N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.1 |
| Small claims limit | $5,000, not counting costs | N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.4 |
| Exemption | Owner-occupied, 2 or fewer rental units, until the tenant gives 30 days' notice | N.J.S.A. 46:8-26 |

The law applies to almost every rental home. A lease clause that waives any part of the law is void (N.J.S.A. 46:8-24).

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in New Jersey?

The maximum security deposit in New Jersey is 1.5 times one month's rent. N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.2 sets that cap for property used as a dwelling. You may collect less, or no deposit at all.

The same section limits later increases. If you collect more security, the extra amount each year may not be more than 10% of the current deposit.

Prepaid rent counts toward the cap. The DCA Truth in Renting guide says any prepaid money held to secure future rent is part of the deposit, including "last month's rent". The guide cites Reilly v. Weiss (2009). So at move-in, you can ask for first month's rent plus 1.5 months of security, and no more.

A worked example:

1. **Rent at move-in:** $1,800 a month. Maximum deposit: $1,800 x 1.5 = $2,700.
2. **Most you collect at signing:** $1,800 rent + $2,700 deposit = $4,500.
3. **Rent rises to $2,000 a year later.** The new cap is $3,000.
4. **Yearly increase limit:** 10% of $2,700 = $270. You may raise the deposit to $2,970 this year, not $3,000.

## How many days does a New Jersey landlord have to return a security deposit?

A New Jersey landlord has 30 days after the lease ends to return the security deposit. N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.1 says you return the deposit plus the tenant's interest, less lawful charges. You must use personal delivery, registered mail or certified mail.

Two shorter deadlines apply:

- **Displacement.** Fire, flood, condemnation or evacuation displaces the tenant, and an official posts the unit or certifies a long displacement. You then have the money ready within 5 business days. You send the tenant written notice within 3 business days, and you hold the money for 30 days.
- **Domestic violence termination.** A victim ends the lease under N.J.S.A. 46:8-9.6. You then have the money ready within 15 business days, on demand.

Watch for: a broken lease. The DCA guide says the lease does not end when the tenant leaves early without legal cause. It ends when you rent the unit again or the lease term runs out, whichever comes first. The 30 days start on that date.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in New Jersey?

A New Jersey landlord can deduct the charges the lease allows. N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.1 permits "charges expended in accordance with the terms of" the lease. The DCA guide lists damage beyond normal wear and tear and other money the tenant owes under the lease, such as unpaid rent.

One rule surprises many owners. You may make no deduction from the deposit while the tenant still lives in the unit (N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.1). Bill the tenant for a repair during the tenancy. Do not take it from the deposit.

If the damage and unpaid rent are more than the deposit, the DCA guide says you may sue for the difference.

## Does a New Jersey landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes, a New Jersey landlord must give an itemized list. N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.1 says the interest and each deduction "shall be itemized". You send the list to the tenant by personal delivery, registered mail or certified mail.

A simple move-out checklist:

- [ ] Date the lease ended, or the date you rented the unit again
- [ ] Deposit amount and the interest earned to date
- [ ] Each charge, with the lease clause and a receipt or estimate
- [ ] Net amount due to the tenant
- [ ] Delivery method and date, within 30 days

## Must a New Jersey landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

Yes. A New Jersey landlord must hold the deposit in trust and in an interest-bearing account. N.J.S.A. 46:8-19 says the money stays the tenant's property. It "shall not be mingled" with your own money.

The account rules depend on size. An owner with 10 or more rental units has 2 choices. One is an insured New Jersey money market fund. The other is a variable-rate account at a New Jersey bank. An owner with fewer than 10 units uses an interest-bearing account at a New Jersey bank.

The notice rules in N.J.S.A. 46:8-19(c):

1. **Tell the tenant in writing within 30 days** of receipt. Give the bank name and address, the account type, the current interest rate and the amount.
2. **Repeat the notice** at each yearly interest payment.
3. **Send a new notice within 30 days** if you move the money or sell the property.

The interest belongs to the tenant. You pay it each year in cash or as a rent credit on the lease anniversary. You may pay on January 31 instead, after written notice to the tenant.

If you skip these steps, the tenant may tell you in writing to apply the deposit, plus 7% interest a year, to rent. After that, you may not ask for a new deposit. For a missed yearly notice or payment, the tenant must first give you 30 days to fix it.

If you sell the building, N.J.S.A. 46:8-20 says you turn the deposit and interest over to the buyer. Do it at closing or within 5 days, and tell the tenant by registered or certified mail. The buyer is responsible for the deposit either way (N.J.S.A. 46:8-21).

## What is the penalty for a New Jersey landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

The penalty is double the amount wrongly kept. N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.1 says a court that finds for the tenant awards double the money due, full court costs and, at its choice, reasonable attorney's fees. If you wrongly keep $1,000, you can owe $2,000 plus costs.

Other penalties in the statute:

- **Deposits paid by a State program.** A landlord who willfully keeps such a deposit owes a civil penalty. It is $500 to $2,000 for each offense (N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.1).
- **Diverting trust money.** A person who knowingly diverts deposit money is a disorderly person. The fine is at least $200, or jail up to 30 days, or both (N.J.S.A. 46:8-25).

The tenant files a claim up to $5,000 in the Small Claims section of the Special Civil Part (N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.4). The DCA guide says no State agency decides deposit disputes. Only a court does.

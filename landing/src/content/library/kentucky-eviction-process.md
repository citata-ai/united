Kentucky has 2 sets of eviction notice rules, and the county or city decides which one you use. I read the KRS chapter 383 sections on the Kentucky General Assembly site on 2026-10-19. The court steps in KRS 383.200 to 383.285 apply everywhere.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Unpaid rent, URLTA area | 7 days to pay after written notice | KRS 383.660(2) |
| Lease breach, URLTA area | Lease ends 14+ days after notice; 15 days to fix | KRS 383.660(1) |
| Same breach within 6 months | 14 days' notice, no cure | KRS 383.660(1) |
| Month-to-month, URLTA area | 30 days before a rent date | KRS 383.695(2) |
| Tenancy at will, non-URLTA area | 1 month's written notice | KRS 383.195 |
| Court | District Court of the county | KRS 383.210 |
| Notice of trial | At least 3 days | KRS 383.210 |
| Appeal window | 7 days after the finding | KRS 383.255 |
| Warrant of restitution | Issues on request if no appeal by day 7 | KRS 383.245 |
| Lockout, URLTA area | Banned; up to 3 months' rent + fees | KRS 383.690, 383.655 |

## Which Kentucky eviction rules apply to your unit?

The Kentucky URLTA rules apply only where a local government adopted them. KRS 383.500 lets cities, counties and urban-county governments enact KRS 383.505 to 383.705 "in their entirety and without amendment."

So check first: has your city or county adopted the URLTA? Ask the county clerk or the local code office. The answer decides your notice periods.

KRS 383.198 adds a 2024 rule. Effective March 6, 2024, no local government may "adopt or enforce any ordinance relating to landlord or tenant laws that is in conflict with any law of this Commonwealth" (2024 Ky. Acts ch. 3).

## What notice must a Kentucky landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

In a URLTA area, a Kentucky landlord gives 7 days' written notice. KRS 383.660(2) says the landlord may terminate "if rent is unpaid when due and the tenant fails to pay rent within seven (7) days after written notice" of nonpayment and of the intent to terminate.

Outside URLTA areas, the state statutes we read set no pay-or-quit period. KRS 383.195 says a tenancy at will or by sufferance may end when the landlord gives "one (1) month's notice, in writing." Check the lease and local practice, and ask a Kentucky attorney about rent cases outside URLTA areas.

## How long does an eviction take in Kentucky?

A Kentucky eviction for unpaid rent in a URLTA area can finish in about 3 to 4 weeks if the tenant does not appeal. The statutes set these clocks:

| Step | Days | Running total |
|---|---|---|
| 7-day pay notice | 7 | 7 |
| File; warrant gives at least 3 days' notice of trial | 3+ | 10+ |
| Appeal window after judgment | 7 | 17+ |
| Warrant of restitution issued and served | Varies by county | 3 to 4 weeks |

This is a worked example from the statutes. Court calendars and sheriff schedules add time.

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Kentucky, and what does it cost?

A landlord files a forcible detainer complaint in the District Court of the county where the property, or "a principal part thereof, lies" (KRS 383.210(1)).

A forcible detainer includes "the refusal of a tenant to give possession to his landlord after the expiration of his term" (KRS 383.200(3)). The warrant must give the tenant "at least three (3) days' notice" of the trial (KRS 383.210(1)). The court tries the case unless a party demands a jury (KRS 383.210(2)).

The filing fee is not in chapter 383. Ask the District Court clerk in your county for the current fee and the sheriff's service fee.

## Can a Kentucky landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes to both, with the right notice.

**Lease violation, URLTA area.** Give written notice that names the breach and ends the lease on a date at least 14 days after receipt (KRS 383.660(1)). If the tenant fixes a fixable breach before that date, the lease goes on. If the same breach recurs within 6 months, 14 days' notice ends the lease with no cure.

**No cause, URLTA area.** End a month-to-month tenancy with written notice at least 30 days before the periodic rental date (KRS 383.695(2)). Week-to-week needs 7 days (KRS 383.695(1)).

**Holdover.** A willful, bad-faith holdover adds damages (KRS 383.695(4)). The landlord may recover up to 3 months' rent or 3 times actual damages, whichever is greater, plus attorney fees.

**Retaliation.** KRS 383.705 bars an eviction after a code complaint, a repair complaint or tenant union activity. A complaint within 1 year creates a presumption of retaliation. The bar does not apply if the tenant is in default in rent.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Kentucky?

The sheriff or a constable removes the tenant under a warrant of restitution. If the tenant does not file an appeal "on or before the seventh day after the finding," the court, on request, issues the warrant (KRS 383.245). The warrant commands the officer to put the landlord in possession, "with the power of the county if necessary."

To appeal, the tenant must file within 7 days (KRS 383.255(1)). The tenant must also deposit with the circuit court clerk the rent owed since the case started, plus each month's rent while the appeal runs.

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Kentucky?

Not in a URLTA area. KRS 383.690 says a landlord "may not recover or take possession of the dwelling unit by action or otherwise," including by cutting heat, electric, water, hot water, gas or other essential service. The exceptions are abandonment, surrender, or what the act allows.

The penalty under KRS 383.655: the tenant may recover possession or end the lease, and recover up to 3 months' periodic rent plus a reasonable attorney fee. On $1,100 rent, that is up to $3,300.

Outside URLTA areas, use the court process too. The forcible entry statute treats a landlord's entry on a tenant at will's possession as a forcible entry (KRS 383.200(2)(b)).

## Kentucky eviction checklist

- Find out if your city or county adopted the URLTA.
- URLTA area: 7-day pay notice or 14-day breach notice.
- Other areas: follow KRS 383.195 and the lease; ask an attorney.
- File in District Court in the county of the unit.
- Wait out the 7-day appeal window, then request the warrant.
- Let the sheriff or constable carry out the warrant.

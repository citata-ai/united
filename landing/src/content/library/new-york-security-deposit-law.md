New York gives a tenant strong deposit rights, and the deadline is short. The main law is General Obligations Law (GOL) 7-108. Here are the rules from the statute text on nysenate.gov, read on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit or advance | 1 month's rent | GOL 7-108(1-a)(a) |
| Maximum deposit, rent-stabilized unit | 1 month's rent | GOL 7-107(2) |
| Offer a move-in inspection | After lease signing, before move-in | GOL 7-108(1-a)(c) |
| Tell the tenant of the move-out inspection right | In writing, a reasonable time after notice to end | GOL 7-108(1-a)(d) |
| Move-out inspection, if the tenant asks | 1 to 2 weeks before the end, with 48 hours' written notice | GOL 7-108(1-a)(d) |
| Return balance and itemized statement | Within 14 days after move-out | GOL 7-108(1-a)(e) |
| Miss the 14 days | Landlord forfeits the right to keep any part | GOL 7-108(1-a)(e) |
| Interest-bearing account | Buildings with 6 or more units | GOL 7-103(2-a) |
| Landlord's fee from interest | 1% a year | GOL 7-103(2-a) |
| Willful violation | Punitive damages up to 2 times the deposit | GOL 7-108(1-a)(g) |
| Buyer who knew of the deposit | Liable for it | GOL 7-108(2) |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in New York?

A New York landlord can charge at most 1 month's rent as a deposit. GOL 7-108(1-a)(a) says: "No deposit or advance shall exceed the amount of one month's rent." The word "advance" matters. Prepaid rent counts toward the same cap.

The cap has two exceptions in GOL 7-108: seasonal use units (subdivisions 4 and 5) and some owner-occupied co-op apartments (subdivision 6). The 7-108 rules also do not cover units under the city rent and rehabilitation law or the emergency housing rent control law. Some senior and care facilities are out too, such as licensed assisted living providers.

Rent-stabilized units have their own section. GOL 7-107 applies to units under the New York City rent stabilization law of 1969 or the Emergency Tenant Protection Act of 1974. It sets the same 1-month cap.

RPL 238-a adds limits on other move-in money. It bars payments at the start of a tenancy unless a statute allows them. It also caps a background and credit check fee at the actual cost or $20, whichever is less.

## How many days does a New York landlord have to return a security deposit?

A New York landlord has 14 days after the tenant vacates. GOL 7-108(1-a)(e) says the landlord must give an itemized statement "indicating the basis for the amount of the deposit retained, if any." The landlord must also return any balance in that time.

The penalty for a late return is large. If you miss the 14 days, you "forfeit any right to retain any portion of the deposit." So even a real damage claim is lost if the statement is late.

Here is a worked example. Rent is $2,400, and the deposit is $2,400. The tenant leaves on March 31. The deadline is April 14. You find $600 of tenant damage. You must send $1,800 and an itemized statement for the $600 by April 14. If you send both on April 20, you lose the right to keep the $600.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in New York?

A New York landlord may deduct only four kinds of cost. GOL 7-108(1-a)(b) lists them:

1. **Unpaid rent.**
2. **Damage by the tenant** beyond normal wear and tear.
3. **Unpaid utility charges** that the tenant owes to the landlord directly.
4. **Moving and storage** of the tenant's belongings.

The statute bars two deductions. The landlord "may not retain any amount of the deposit for costs relating to ordinary wear and tear of occupancy." The landlord also may not deduct for damage that a prior tenant caused.

The inspections help both sides. Before move-in, you must offer the tenant an inspection (GOL 7-108(1-a)(c)). If the tenant asks, you both sign a written note of the unit's condition and any defects. You cannot later deduct for a defect in that note.

## Does a New York landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes, a New York landlord must give an itemized statement twice in the process.

- **Before move-out.** After either side gives notice to end, tell the tenant in writing of the right to an inspection (GOL 7-108(1-a)(d)). If the tenant asks, the inspection takes place no earlier than 2 weeks and no later than 1 week before the end. You give at least 48 hours' written notice of the time. After it, you give an itemized list of the repairs or cleaning you plan to deduct for. The tenant can then fix those items before leaving.
- **After move-out.** Within 14 days, you give the final itemized statement with the balance (GOL 7-108(1-a)(e)).

Use this checklist for each move-out:

- [ ] Send the written notice of the inspection right.
- [ ] Hold the inspection 1 to 2 weeks before the end, if the tenant asks.
- [ ] Give the list of proposed deductions and let the tenant cure.
- [ ] Photograph the unit on the move-out day.
- [ ] Send the statement and balance within 14 days.
- [ ] Keep receipts for each deduction.

## Must a New York landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

A New York landlord must keep every deposit separate. GOL 7-103(1) says the deposit stays the tenant's money, held in trust. It "shall not be mingled with the personal moneys" of the landlord.

For a building with 6 or more family units, GOL 7-103(2-a) requires an interest-bearing account at a bank in New York State. The landlord may keep "a sum equivalent to one per cent per annum" for costs. The rest of the interest goes to the tenant. It is held in trust until the deposit is repaid or applied to rent, or paid each year.

If you put the deposit in a bank, GOL 7-103(2) says you must tell the tenant in writing. The notice names the bank, its address and the amount.

If you sell the building, GOL 7-108(2) can make the buyer liable for a deposit the seller did not transfer. That applies when the buyer had actual knowledge of the deposit, in the ways the statute lists.

## What is the penalty for a New York landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

A New York landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly owes actual damages. GOL 7-108(1-a)(g) adds punitive damages for a willful violation, of "up to twice the amount of the deposit or advance."

In any dispute, the landlord has the burden of proof. GOL 7-108(1-a)(f) says you must show that the amount you kept is reasonable. GOL 7-108(3) also makes any lease clause that waives these rights void.

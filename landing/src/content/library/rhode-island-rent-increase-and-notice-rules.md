## Rhode Island rent and notice rules at a glance

Rhode Island notice rules sit in Chapter 34-18, the Residential Landlord and Tenant Act. We read each section on 2026-10-19. The rilegislature.gov server did not answer that day, so we read Internet Archive copies of the official pages, dated 2025-05 to 2026-04.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Rent increase notice | At least 60 days, in writing | § 34-18-16.1(a) |
| Rent increase, month-to-month tenant over 62 | At least 120 days | § 34-18-16.1(b) |
| Facilities excluded | Independent living, assisted living, congregate care | § 34-18-16.1(a), (b) |
| Cap on increase amount | None in Chapter 34-18 | Chapter 34-18 |
| Fee change, no written lease | 30 days' written notice | § 34-18-15(a)(2) |
| End week-to-week | 10 days' written notice | § 34-18-37(a) |
| End month-to-month | 30 days' written notice | § 34-18-37(b) |
| End year-to-year | 3 months before end of occupation year | § 34-18-37(c) |
| Retaliatory increase | Banned; 6-month presumption | § 34-18-46 |

## Does Rhode Island have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

Rhode Island's landlord-tenant chapter has no cap on the amount of a rent increase. Section 34-18-16.1 controls the notice, not the size. We read the full index of Chapter 34-18 and found no section that limits the amount.

Two rules still limit when and why you raise rent:

- **Retaliation.** Section 34-18-46 bars a rent increase because a tenant complained to a code agency, complained to you about repairs, or joined a tenants' union. A complaint in the 6 months before the increase creates a presumption of retaliation. The presumption does not arise if the tenant complained after notice of the increase.
- **Housing programs.** Section 34-18-16.1(c) says the state notice does not override federal or state program rules. If a voucher or subsidy program sets its own process, follow that program too.

## How many days notice does a Rhode Island landlord need to raise the rent?

A Rhode Island landlord needs at least 60 days' written notice before a rent increase takes effect (§ 34-18-16.1(a)). For a month-to-month tenant over 62, the notice is at least 120 days (§ 34-18-16.1(b)).

The section's history line lists P.L. 2024, ch. 243 and ch. 244, both effective 2024-06-24. So the current periods have applied since that date.

Worked example, increase effective March 1, 2027:

| Tenant | Minimum notice | Send notice by |
|---|---|---|
| Any residential tenant | 60 days | December 31, 2026 |
| Month-to-month tenant over 62 | 120 days | November 1, 2026 |

Tip: send the notice a few days early and keep proof of delivery. Count days with a calendar, not by months.

## Can a Rhode Island landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

Usually not, unless the lease allows it. Section 34-18-15(a) makes rent one of the terms of the rental agreement. A fixed-term lease sets the rent for its term. To change it mid-term, you need a lease clause that allows an increase, or the tenant's agreement.

Even with such a clause, the 60-day written notice in § 34-18-16.1 still applies. Fees beyond rent must appear in the same lease section as the rent (§ 34-18-15(a)(1)). If you fail to disclose a fee, the tenant may recover it (§ 34-18-15(a)(5)).

Rent increase checklist:

- [ ] Check the lease: fixed term or periodic? Any increase clause?
- [ ] Check the tenant's age and tenancy type for the 120-day rule.
- [ ] Check for a code complaint or repair request in the last 6 months.
- [ ] Check any voucher or subsidy program rules.
- [ ] Write the new rent, the old rent and the effective date.
- [ ] Deliver in writing and keep proof.
- [ ] Update the ledger on the effective date, not before.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Rhode Island?

Either side may end a Rhode Island month-to-month tenancy with at least 30 days' written notice before the date in the notice (§ 34-18-37(b)). The notice follows the form in § 34-18-56(c).

The same section covers other periods:

| Tenancy type | Notice | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Week-to-week | At least 10 days | § 34-18-37(a) |
| Month-to-month, or any period over 1 month and under 1 year | At least 30 days | § 34-18-37(b) |
| Year-to-year | At least 3 months before the end of the occupation year | § 34-18-37(c) |

Without a fixed term, a tenancy is month-to-month, or week-to-week for a roomer who pays weekly (§ 34-18-15(d)). If the tenant stays after the notice date, the landlord may file a holdover case the next day (§ 34-18-38(a)).

Two tenant rights can end a lease early. A tenant who is 65 or older may end a lease to move into assisted living, a nursing facility or elderly housing (§ 34-18-15(e)). A servicemember with qualifying orders may also end a lease (§ 34-18-15(f)).

## Do cities in Rhode Island add their own rent or notice rules?

The state chapter names two local items. Section 34-18-57 is the Providence and Warwick Absentee Landlord Enforcement Act. Section 34-18-58 sets a statewide mandatory rental registry. We could not read those two sections in full on 2026-10-19, so we do not state their duties here. Read them, and check your city's code, before you send a notice.

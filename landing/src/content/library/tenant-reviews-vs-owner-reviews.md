## Why do tenants post more reviews?

Tenants post more reviews because there are more of them and they deal with the manager more often. A manager with 200 doors has up to 200 renter households. The same 200 doors may belong to far fewer owners, because many owners hold more than one rental.

Tenants also meet you at the hard moments. A repair that takes a week, a deposit deduction, a late fee or a non-renewal. Those moments make people write. Owners see you through a monthly statement. When the statement is right, they have little reason to post.

| Factor | Tenants | Owners |
|---|---|---|
| Head count per 200 doors | Up to 200 households | Fewer, since many own several doors |
| Contact with the manager | Weekly to monthly | Monthly statement, a few calls a year |
| Strong-emotion moments | Repairs, deposits, fees, move-out | Vacancy, a big repair bill, a lawsuit |
| Where they post | Google, Yelp, apartment sites, BBB | Google, BBB, sometimes Yelp |
| Usual tone | Mixed, often about one event | Often about long-term results |

The head counts are an example, not survey data. Check your own ratio: count your owners and your occupied doors.

## Does AI split tenant and owner views?

No AI engine says it splits tenant reviews from owner reviews. ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude read the review pages that rank for a query, and those pages mix both groups.

Our own data shows which review pages AI reads. We asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude for the best property manager in 50 US cities, 150 answers in total. Yelp was a source in 50 answers. Birdeye review pages were a source in 19, and BBB in 14 (our data, 2026-10-19). Expertise.com, a directory, was a source in 93.

Ratings act as a gate in AI answers. SOCi studied local AI answers for multi-location brands in 2026. It found that businesses ChatGPT recommends average 4.3 stars. Recommended businesses average 4.2 stars on Perplexity and 3.9 on Gemini (SOCi, 2026). SOCi did not study property managers. But the pattern matters for you: if tenant reviews pull your average down, AI may skip you for owner questions.

Owner and renter questions also use different sources. For renter questions about apartments, listing sites got 72% of AI citations (Peek, 2026). For owner questions, directories and the manager's own site matter more. Our guide on [how owners choose a property manager with AI](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai) shows the owner side.

Test it yourself. Ask ChatGPT: "What do landlords say about [your company] in [city]?" Then ask: "What do tenants say about [your company]?" Note which reviews and pages it quotes. Repeat each month.

## How do you get more owner reviews?

Ask every owner at fixed moments, with the same message, and never pay for a review. The rules differ by platform.

| Platform | Can you ask? | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Google | Yes | Do not ask only happy customers. No rewards for reviews (Google policy) |
| Yelp | No | "Don't ask anyone to review your business" (Yelp Support) |
| Your site | Yes | Get written consent to publish the owner's words and name |

The FTC rule of August 2024 bans fake reviews and bans rewards that depend on a positive review. It also bans insider reviews that do not disclose the link to the business, and threats to remove bad reviews (FTC, 2024). So staff, family and friends do not review your company.

Ask at these 4 moments:

1. **After the first lease is signed** on a new owner's property.
2. **After the first full owner statement** with rent paid.
3. **After a hard job went well,** such as a fast turn or a big repair at a fair price.
4. **At the yearly review** or contract renewal.

Use one short message for all owners: "Thank you for trusting us with your property. If you have a minute, a Google review helps other owners decide. Here is the link." Google suggests a review link or QR code (Google Business Profile Help).

Watch for: review gating. A survey that sends only happy owners to Google breaks Google's rule against selectively asking for positive reviews.

## Should you answer tenant reviews for owners?

Yes. Answer every tenant review, and write the reply for the owner who reads it later. An owner who reads a bad tenant review wants to see how you handle problems. A calm, factual reply shows that.

BrightLocal surveyed 1,002 US adults in 2026. It found that 89% expect businesses to respond to reviews. 80% are likely to use a business that responds to all of its reviews (BrightLocal, 2026). Google also advises replying to reviews (Google Business Profile Help).

Reply checklist:

- [ ] Reply within 2 business days.
- [ ] Thank the person, and name the issue in general words.
- [ ] Say what you did or what you will do.
- [ ] Give a contact name and phone to continue offline.
- [ ] Never share the tenant's unit, balance, lease terms or other private details.
- [ ] Never argue or blame the tenant.
- [ ] Use the same respectful tone for every reviewer.

A good reply to a deposit complaint: "Thank you for the feedback. We explain each deposit charge with photos and invoices. Please call Maria at our office so we can go over yours."

## What should you put on your own site?

Put owner stories on your own site, with consent. Your site is the one place where you can show the owner view in full. AI engines read manager sites for owner questions.

Add a page of owner reviews with the owner's first name, city and property type. Add one or two short case studies with real numbers, such as days to lease or rent growth, with the owner's written consent. Link that page from your owner services page and your fee page.

Do not copy Google reviews to your site without permission, and do not edit their words. Keep each fact true and dated.

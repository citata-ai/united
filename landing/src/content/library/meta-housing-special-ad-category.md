On June 21, 2022, the US Justice Department sued Meta. The complaint said Meta's housing ad system showed ads by race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status and national origin, in breach of the Fair Housing Act. The court approved a settlement on June 27, 2022 (US DOJ). Every property manager who runs Facebook or Instagram ads today works inside the rules that came from that case.

## What is the housing Special Ad Category?

The housing Special Ad Category is a label that Meta requires on campaigns for ads about housing opportunities. Meta asks you to pick the category when you create the campaign, in Ads Manager under "Special Ad Categories" (Meta Business Help Center). The category limits who you can target, so the ad reaches a broad audience.

Meta has four such categories: housing; employment; financial products and services; and social issues, elections or politics. Meta replaced the old Credit category with financial products and services. Use of that category became required in the US on January 21, 2025 (Meta Business Help Center).

## Which ads must use the housing category?

Ads that promote a rental or sale opportunity must use the housing category. For a property manager, that clearly covers:

- Ads for a vacant unit or a list of available rentals.
- Ads that link to your rental listings page.
- Ads for a move-in special or a leasing event.

Meta says its list of examples is not complete and is not legal advice (Meta Business Help Center). If an ad could be read as a housing offer, declare the category.

## Which targeting options are removed?

For advertisers based in or reaching the US, Meta removes or limits these options on housing campaigns (Meta Business Help Center):

| Option | Normal campaign | Housing campaign (US) |
|---|---|---|
| Age | Any range | Fixed at 18 to 65+ |
| Gender | Select | All genders, cannot edit |
| ZIP code targeting | Yes | Not available |
| Location exclusion | Yes | Not available |
| City or pin drop | Exact | Includes a 15-mile radius |
| Detailed targeting | Full list | Some demographic, behavior and interest options removed |
| Exclusions of detailed targeting | Yes | Not available |
| Lookalike audiences | Yes | Not available |
| Saved audiences | Yes | Updated to comply or not available |

Meta gives an example of the radius rule. An audience for the city of Seattle also includes people within 15 miles of the city center. Canada has the same 15-mile rule. Many European countries use 10 miles (Meta Business Help Center).

## What did the 2022 settlement change?

The 2022 settlement changed how Meta delivers housing ads, not only how advertisers target them. Under the settlement, Meta (US DOJ):

1. Stopped using its "Special Ad Audience" tool for housing ads.
2. Built the Variance Reduction System (VRS) to reduce race and other gaps in who sees housing ads.
3. Agreed not to offer targeting options that describe or relate to Fair Housing Act protected traits.
4. Paid a civil penalty of $115,054, the maximum under the Act at the time.

An outside reviewer, Guidehouse, checks the VRS. It issued reports from June 2023 to October 2024 (US DOJ).

## Do owner-service ads count as housing ads?

Meta does not publish a clear rule for ads that sell property management services to owners, as far as we could find on 2026-10-19. An ad that says "We manage your rental for 8% a month" does not offer housing to a renter. But it talks about rentals, and the landing page may show listings.

A practical rule set:

| Ad | Category |
|---|---|
| "3-bed home for rent, apply now" | Housing, always |
| "See our available rentals" | Housing, always |
| "Free rental analysis for owners" linking to an owner page with no listings | Gray zone; housing is the safe choice |
| "Hiring a leasing agent" | Employment |
| Brand video with no rental or job offer | Usually none, but check Meta's rules |

Declaring housing costs you targeting. Meta warns that ads may be rejected when the right category is not chosen (Meta Business Help Center). When unsure, declare it.

## How do I still reach owners?

You reach owners on Meta with the offer, the copy and your own data, not with demographics. The housing limits stop you from picking people by age, gender or ZIP code. They do not stop a clear message from filtering the audience itself.

Five tactics that work inside the rules:

1. **Write the ad for owners only.** "Own a rental in [metro]? Get a free rent estimate." Renters skip it.
2. **Lead with a tool.** A rent estimate, a vacancy cost calculator or a fee comparison. Owners click tools.
3. **Retarget your own site visitors.** Meta notes that some audiences, such as custom audiences, may be available only in Ads Manager. Check which ones your campaign allows.
4. **Use your past owner leads.** Upload a list of people who asked for a quote, where Meta allows it.
5. **Send the click to a page for owners.** Do not link an owner ad to your rental listings.

## Fair Housing still applies to your ad text

The Fair Housing Act rules apply to what you write, not only to how Meta targets. HUD's rule at 24 CFR 100.75 makes it unlawful to publish a rental ad that shows a preference or limit based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status or national origin. It also covers the choice of media or locations that deny groups information about housing (eCFR, 24 CFR 100.75).

Check every rental ad for these:

- No words about who "fits" the home: "perfect for young professionals", "no kids".
- Photos that do not suggest one type of renter.
- Describe the property, not the people.
- The Equal Housing Opportunity statement or logo where you use it.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Housing ad checklist

- Housing category declared on every listing and leasing ad.
- Owner ads sent to an owner page, not to listings.
- Copy that describes the property or the service, never the person.
- Location set by city or region, with the 15-mile radius in mind.
- Ad results judged by owner leads, not clicks.

Social ads bring few owner leads for most managers. In Goodjuju's fall 2026 study of 35 companies, social media brought 0.8% of owner form leads (Goodjuju, 2026).

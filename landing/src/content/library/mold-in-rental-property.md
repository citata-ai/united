## What should a landlord do first when a tenant reports mold?

A landlord should answer a mold report the same day and inspect within a short window. Mold grows from water. The EPA says "the key to mold control is moisture control." So the first job is to find the water, not to scrub the wall.

Use this response plan:

1. **Log the report** with the date, time, unit, room and the tenant's photos.
2. **Reply to the tenant** the same day with an inspection time.
3. **Inspect** the spot and the area around it: plumbing, roof, windows, vents, exterior grade.
4. **Find the source**: a leak, condensation, a blocked dryer vent, a bath fan that does not work.
5. **Fix the source** first. A clean wall over a wet cavity grows mold again.
6. **Dry wet materials** within 24-48 hours, as the EPA advises.
7. **Clean or remove** the moldy material, based on size and type.
8. **Reinspect** and photograph the result.
9. **Write it up** and give the tenant a copy.

Do not paint or caulk over mold. The EPA says paint over mold is likely to peel, and the surface must be cleaned and dried first.

## Is mold a habitability issue?

Mold is often a habitability issue, but the rule comes from state and local law. No federal standard sets a safe mold level. The EPA says no standards or threshold limit values for airborne mold have been set, and EPA has no regulations for airborne mold.

State law fills the gap in different ways. The table shows 3 states.

| State | What the law says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Virginia | Landlord must keep the unit free of moisture build-up and mold, remediate visible mold promptly, reinspect, and give the tenant a summary | Va. Code 55.1-1220(A)(5) |
| Virginia | Move-in report must say if there is visible mold; if there is and the tenant stays, remediate within 5 business days | Va. Code 55.1-1215 |
| New York | Every lease warrants the unit is fit to live in, with no conditions dangerous to life, health or safety; tenants cannot waive this | N.Y. Real Prop. Law 235-b |
| Florida | Landlord must comply with building, housing and health codes | F.S. 83.51(1) |

Virginia names mold in the statute. New York and Florida use general habitability and code language. Under that general language, a mold problem can still count if it makes the unit unsafe or breaks a local code. Check your city's housing code too.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Who is at fault: a leak or tenant humidity?

Fault depends on the cause, so find the cause before you send a bill. A roof leak or a pipe leak is a building problem. Steam from long showers with the fan off can be a use problem. Often it is both.

Some states put a duty on the tenant. Virginia Code 55.1-1227(A)(10) says the tenant must use reasonable efforts to prevent moisture and mold. The tenant must also promptly tell the landlord about moisture or visible mold. New York's warranty of habitability does not cover a condition caused by the tenant's own misconduct, per Real Property Law 235-b.

Use these signs to sort the cause:

| Sign | Points to |
|---|---|
| Stain grows after rain, near roof or window | Building leak |
| Wet spot under sink, behind toilet, near water heater | Plumbing leak |
| Mold on ceiling above shower, fan not working | Ventilation defect |
| Mold on ceiling above shower, fan works but is never used | Use issue |
| Condensation on cold exterior walls behind furniture | Humidity plus insulation |
| Musty smell, no visible source | Hidden leak; open and check |

The EPA advises indoor humidity below 60%, ideally 30-50%. A humidity meter turns a debate into a number. Leave one in the unit and log the reading.

Even when the tenant caused the moisture, fix the mold. A tenant fault does not make a moldy unit safe. Bill for the cleanup only if your lease and state law allow it, with photos and invoices.

## When do I need a mold inspection or professional cleanup?

You need a professional when the area is large, the water damage is heavy, or the source is hidden. The EPA says a moldy area of less than about 10 square feet is usually a job you can handle yourself. Larger areas need more help.

The EPA also says testing is usually not needed. "In most cases, if visible mold growth is present, sampling is unnecessary." A lab result does not tell you how to fix the leak. Spend the money on the repair.

A professional makes sense in these cases:

- **Area over about 10 square feet**, per the EPA guide.
- **Sewage or other contaminated water**: the EPA says to call a professional.
- **Mold in the HVAC system**: the EPA says not to run a system you think has mold.
- **Water inside walls or ceilings.**
- **Repeat growth** after cleanup, which points to a hidden source.
- **A tenant with health concerns** who asks for a formal report.
- **A local law** that requires a licensed contractor above a set size.

For small jobs, clean with detergent and water and dry the area fully. The EPA says bleach is not recommended as a routine practice during mold cleanup. The EPA says porous materials, such as ceiling tiles and carpet, may have to be thrown away if they become moldy.

## What goes in a mold addendum?

A mold addendum is a signed lease page that sets out both sides' duties for moisture and mold. It does not remove the landlord's duty under state law. It makes the duties clear and gives you a record.

Include these parts:

- **Move-in condition**: a statement that the unit had no visible mold at move-in, or a list of known spots. Virginia requires this in the move-in report under Code 55.1-1215.
- **Tenant duties**: run bath and kitchen fans, keep the heat on in cold months, wipe condensation, do not block vents.
- **Reporting duty**: report leaks, moisture and visible mold in writing within a set number of days.
- **Landlord duties**: inspect and respond to reports, fix leaks, remediate mold.
- **Access**: the landlord may enter for mold inspection and repair on lawful notice.
- **Cost rules**: who pays for cleanup caused by tenant misuse, within state law.

Keep every mold file: the report, photos, the cause, invoices, and the reinspection. Virginia Code 55.1-1220 requires the landlord to give the tenant a summary of mold remediation during the tenancy.

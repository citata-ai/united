## What are the types of Section 8 inspections?

A Section 8 unit gets 4 main kinds of inspection under 24 CFR 982.405. The PHA (public housing agency) runs all of them. Each one checks the unit against the housing quality standards (HQS).

| Inspection type | When it happens | Rule | Who can trigger it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial | Before the first lease term | 982.405(a) | Your Request for Tenancy Approval |
| Periodic (biennial) | At least every 2 years; every 3 years at a small rural PHA | 982.405(b) | PHA schedule |
| Interim (special, complaint) | After a report of a deficiency | 982.405(d) | The family or a government official |
| Supervisory quality control | A sample of inspections, rechecked | 982.405(c) | PHA supervisors |
| Reinspection | After a failed item | 982.405(h), (i) | Your repair, or the end of the repair period |

A PHA may also use an alternative inspection under 24 CFR 982.406. That is an inspection done for another program, such as HOME or Low-Income Housing Tax Credits. If your building already has a tax credit inspection, ask the PHA if it accepts that result.

## What is the difference between initial, biennial and special inspections?

The initial inspection decides if the unit can enter the program. The PHA must do it before the first lease term starts. Tacoma Housing Authority, for example, says it tries to schedule the initial inspection within 5 business days of your Request for Tenancy Approval.

The biennial inspection checks that the unit still meets HQS during the tenancy. "Biennial" means at least once every 2 years. A small rural PHA inspects once every 3 years instead.

The special inspection (the regulation says "interim") starts with a report. The family or a government official tells the PHA about a possible problem. Then the PHA must act on a clock:

| Reported item | PHA must inspect and notify you within | You must repair within |
|---|---|---|
| Life-threatening | 24 hours | 24 hours of PHA notice |
| Not life-threatening | 15 days | 30 days of PHA notice, or a PHA-approved extension |

Source: 24 CFR 982.405(d). HUD may approve an exception in extraordinary cases, such as a presidentially declared disaster area.

## Can a tenant ask for a special inspection?

Yes, a tenant can ask for a special inspection. Under 24 CFR 982.405(d), the PHA must respond when "a participant family or government official" reports a potential deficiency. The PHA must also consider complaints when it schedules inspections (982.405(e)).

DeKalb Housing Authority says owners can also ask for a special inspection. That helps when you want a record that a repair is done.

Watch for: a complaint inspection is a full clock, not a friendly visit. If the item is life-threatening, you have 24 hours after the PHA tells you. Keep a vendor on call for heat, gas, water and electrical items.

## Does the PHA do quality-control inspections?

Yes, every PHA must do supervisory quality-control inspections (24 CFR 982.405(c)). A supervisor rechecks a sample of units that inspectors already passed or failed. The goal is to test the inspectors, but your unit can still get new fail items.

Treat a quality-control visit like any other inspection. Be ready to give access, and fix any item the PHA confirms.

## Can a PHA inspect every year?

Yes, a PHA can inspect every year. The rule says "at least biennially", so every 2 years is the minimum, not the maximum. Some PHAs inspect some programs yearly. DeKalb Housing Authority, for example, inspects most units every 2 years but VASH units (vouchers for veterans) every year.

Look in your PHA's Administrative Plan for its inspection cycle. Also ask which standard it uses. HUD moved voucher inspections from HQS to NSPIRE (the new national inspection standard). The Federal Register notice of 2025-09-30 extended the voucher compliance date to February 1, 2027. Smoke and carbon monoxide alarm rules and lead paint visual assessment rules did not wait for that date.

## Must I be there for the inspection?

Federal rules do not require the owner to attend. Each PHA sets its own access rule. DeKalb Housing Authority asks for "a responsible adult present, age 18 or over" to let the inspector in. It warns that a missed inspection "may jeopardize your rental assistance", and a "No Entry" result counts as a fail.

So you do not always need to attend, but someone must open the door. For a periodic inspection, that is often the tenant. For a vacant unit at initial inspection, it is you or your agent.

## What does an inspection cost the owner?

An inspection costs the owner nothing at first. 24 CFR 982.405(h) bars a fee for the initial inspection and for the first inspection during the tenancy. A PHA may charge a reasonable fee for a reinspection if a repair you owed was not done. You may not pass that fee to the family.

A PHA may also accept photos or other reliable proof that you fixed an item (982.405(i)). Ask your PHA if it does. A photo can save a trip and a week.

## Checklist for a biennial inspection

Use this list 2 to 3 weeks before the date on the PHA notice.

- [ ] Confirm the date and who will give access. Tell the tenant in writing.
- [ ] Test every smoke alarm and carbon monoxide alarm. Replace weak batteries.
- [ ] Check that every window opens, stays open and locks.
- [ ] Check GFCI outlets near water and look for missing cover plates.
- [ ] Look for peeling paint in a pre-1978 building. Lead rules apply.
- [ ] Run the heat and hot water. Check the water heater relief valve and pipe.
- [ ] Check stair rails and handrails for looseness.
- [ ] Clear blocked exits and egress windows.
- [ ] Book a vendor slot after the date, in case of fail items.
- [ ] Save photos of each fix to send to the PHA.

If you run many voucher units, put each unit's last inspection date in one sheet. Add 24 months, then subtract 60 days for a prep reminder. If the HAP payment stops because of a failed item, your income drops for that unit.

## What does ChatGPT say when an owner asks if a company is legit?

ChatGPT and the other engines answer a trust question with whatever they find under your name. If they find little, they guess, and the guess can be about a different company.

I saw this on our own name first. Our test asks 3 engines, twice each: "Is citata.ai a good GEO agency? Reviews and prices." On 2026-10-18, all 6 answers named other brands with similar names, such as Citant.ai and Citate.ai. On 2026-10-19, 3 of 6 answers put citata.ai first. The other 3 still named Citant.ai or Citate.ai (our data, `brand-reviews` prompt).

A property manager with a common name has the same risk. "Premier Property Management" or "Elite Realty" exists in many cities. An owner in one city can get the reviews of a firm in another.

## Which sources does AI use for trust questions?

AI uses the same public sources for trust that it uses for "who is best". We have not yet run "is [company] legit" prompts for property managers. We did run 150 answers to "What is the best property management company in [city]?" across 50 cities and 3 engines (our data, 2026-10-19). These were the review and listing sources the engines cited most.

| Source | Answers that cited it (of 150) | What you control |
|---|---|---|
| Expertise.com | 93 | Your profile facts; the ranking is theirs |
| Yelp | 50 | Your page, your replies |
| iPropertyManagement | 50 | Your listing facts |
| Reddit | 22 | Nothing, except honest answers as a named person |
| ThreeBestRated | 21 | Your listing facts |
| Birdeye reviews | 19 | Your review requests, if you use Birdeye |
| TrustAnalytica | 17 | Your listing facts |
| BBB | 14 | Your file, your complaint replies |

Two more numbers from the same run. 132 of 150 answers (88%) cited a listing site. 106 of 150 (71%) cited at least one company website. So your own site counts, but the listings carry most of the weight.

## Do complaints and lawsuits show up in AI answers?

Complaints can show up when they sit on a source the engine reads. The BBB is the clearest case. Its letter grade comes from a business's BBB file and 7 elements (BBB). They are complaint history, type of business, time in business and transparent business practices. The other 3 are failure to honor commitments to BBB, licensing information and advertising issues.

The BBB's complaint factors include "Appropriate and timely response to complaints" and "Patterns of unresolved issues". The BBB also says: "Customer reviews are not used in the letter-grade calculation." So a slow reply to one complaint can hurt the grade more than a 3-star review.

Lawsuits are a different matter. We have no test data on whether engines name court cases for property managers. Do not assume they will skip them. A news story or a public court page can reach an answer like any other page.

## How do you answer complaints fairly?

Answer a complaint the same way in every place: short, true and calm. Google says "Helpful and positive replies to reviews can show that you're responsive to your customers." Google also checks replies against its content policies before it shows them.

Follow these steps for each public complaint:

1. **Read the full complaint twice.** Check your records before you write.
2. **Thank the person and name the issue.** One sentence.
3. **State the facts you can share.** No tenant names, no lease details, no money owed.
4. **Say what you changed, if anything.** For example: "We now send deposit statements by email within the legal deadline."
5. **Give a direct contact.** A named person and a phone number.
6. **Do not threaten or argue.** The FTC rule bans review suppression by "groundless legal threats" and intimidation.

Watch for: some complaints come from tenants, not owners. Answer them with the same care. An owner reads the tenant reviews to see how you will treat their tenants.

## What must you never do with reviews?

Never buy, fake or hide reviews. The FTC announced its final rule on fake reviews on August 14, 2024. The rule is 16 CFR part 465 (89 FR 68077, August 22, 2024), and it lets the FTC seek civil penalties from knowing violators (FTC).

| Section | What it bans |
|---|---|
| 465.2 | Fake or false reviews and testimonials, including AI-written ones from people who do not exist |
| 465.4 | Paying for positive or negative reviews |
| 465.5 | Insider reviews that hide the link to the company, for example from a manager |
| 465.6 | Calling a site you control an independent review site |
| 465.7 | Review suppression by threats or false accusations |
| 465.8 | Fake followers or views bought to look popular |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

You may still ask real clients for reviews. Ask every owner at the same point, for example 90 days after onboarding. Do not ask only the happy ones.

## Which pages help AI answer trust questions about you?

Pages that state checkable facts help most. Google's guide to helpful content asks if a page presents information "in a way that makes you want to trust it, such as clear sourcing". It also says that for topics with a large effect on people's money or lives, "trust is most important".

Build or fix these pages:

- **About page.** Founder name, year founded, office address, license number and the states you serve.
- **Name section.** If other firms share your name, list them and say how you differ.
- **Fee page.** Every owner fee in one table, plus the cancel terms.
- **Reviews page.** Link to your Google, Yelp and BBB pages. Do not copy only the 5-star reviews.
- **Owner FAQ.** "How do I cancel?" and "When do I get paid?" with exact answers.
- **Team page.** Real names and roles. Google warns against "Fabricating creator profiles".

Then make the listings match. Use the same name, address and phone on Google, Yelp, BBB, Expertise.com and Bing Places. Our [property management service page](/property-management) shows how we do this work for clients.

## How do you check what AI says about your company?

Ask the trust question yourself each month and write down the answer. Use the same words each time, so you can compare.

| Prompt to test | What to record |
|---|---|
| "Is [company name] in [city] legit?" | Right company? Which sources? |
| "[Company name] reviews" | Star rating quoted? Correct? |
| "[Company name] complaints" | Which complaints, from which site? |
| "Is [company name] a good property manager for owners?" | Owner points or only tenant points? |

Run each prompt in ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Copilot, 2 times each. That is 32 answers a month for 4 prompts. If the engine names the wrong company in any answer, fix the name facts first.

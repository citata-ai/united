## What Does Leasing-Only Service Include?

Leasing-only service should cover a defined placement process and end at a clear handoff event. Full management adds ongoing work after the resident moves in.

Rentec Direct separates leasing from regular monthly management in its fee guide. That is a useful service distinction, not a standard contract scope. Your own agreement must explain the work you accept.

Start with a service comparison. The entries below are suggested scope categories. Replace them with your actual offer before using the table with an owner.

| Work | Leasing-only scope to define | Full-management scope to define |
|---|---|---|
| Listing preparation | Condition review and advertising | Intake plus future vacancy work |
| Showings and applications | Who schedules and reviews | Same work within ongoing service |
| Lease completion | Drafting, approvals and signatures | Lease plus later administration |
| Funds at move-in | Who collects and transfers | Ongoing handling under the agreement |
| Repair requests | Handoff point and contact | Routine and emergency process |
| Owner reports | Placement report | Continuing financial and service reports |

Define responsibility for property readiness before marketing. An owner may expect you to supervise repairs simply because you found the defects. State whether that work is included or separately quoted.

Give residents a clear future contact. The owner and resident should not discover after move-in that each expects your company to answer maintenance calls.

## How Should You Price Leasing-Only Work?

Price placement from the work required and the risk you accept. Do not use a fee percentage without checking whether it funds the service.

List advertising, property visits, showing coordination, application handling, lease work and the final transfer. Include staff review and unsuccessful applications. Some work continues even when the owner declines an applicant or delays a decision.

Decide when the fee is earned and payable. Signing a lease, collecting funds and taking possession are different events. Explain the chosen trigger. State what happens if the owner withdraws the property or a resident does not move in.

Review any replacement promise with counsel and your insurer. Define the conditions and the work included. Avoid implying guaranteed rent or a guaranteed resident outcome. A placement offer cannot remove every risk of owning a rental.

For special properties, use a scoped quote after reviewing readiness and records. A property with missing documents or major repair needs may not fit your standard offer. Give owners the facts before asking them to approve added work.

Use the [management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to compare cost scenarios. Keep the placement quote separate from estimates for ongoing monthly service.

## Do Leasing-Only Owners Convert to Full Management?

Treat conversion to full management as a possibility to measure, not a guaranteed source of future revenue. The placement service must work on its own economics.

Ask why an owner wants placement help. Some want a clean start before self-managing. Others need help only while away. Understanding that need lets you make a fitting offer without building the price on hoped-for upgrades.

At handoff, explain the work the owner now controls. Give a practical list of reporting, resident communication and repair tasks. Offer ongoing management if the owner wants help. Avoid making the handoff seem harder than it is to pressure the sale.

Track later requests for help by reason. A maintenance request, renewal question or ledger problem may show a scope gap. It may also show a separate service need. Ask permission before changing the ongoing service arrangement.

Measure how many placement owners later sign full management and what those accounts cost to serve. Keep the sales history with the property. Do not report verbal interest as a signed conversion. Review whether owners understood the original service before promoting a broader offer.

## What Liability Can Remain After Placement?

Ending the placement service does not prove that all duties or claims end. Have local counsel review the work performed and the duties that can continue.

The agreement should define screening authority, owner approvals, advertising responsibility and lease preparation. It should also explain who holds records, who handles funds and how a later complaint reaches the right person. Do not rely on a broad statement that all liability ends at move-in.

Keep evidence of the process. Save the approved criteria, decisions, notices and signed lease in the proper file. Use a consistent process that follows applicable fair housing and screening rules. Send questions about legal notices or disputed decisions to trained staff and counsel.

Review insurance with the actual service scope. Tell the adviser that you offer placement without continuing management. Ask how claims from prior placement work are handled after the handoff or after you change carriers.

Use local legal review for deposit handling, notices and required records. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney. Do not assume that a clause copied from another state fits your placement service.

## What Should the Placement Handoff Contain?

A good handoff gives the owner the records and open tasks needed to take over. Use a signed transfer checklist as an operating record.

- Provide the executed lease and approved amendments.
- Explain funds received, balances and transfer status.
- Transfer access items through a documented process.
- List unresolved repairs and promised follow-up.
- Give the resident the correct future contact details.
- Record who now handles payment and maintenance questions.
- Confirm where records will remain and who can request them.

Do not label open tasks complete merely because your service period ended. Name the receiving person and obtain confirmation. If the owner has not accepted a critical task, escalate before treating the handoff as done.

Review the first inquiry that arrives after transfer. If residents keep contacting your team, check the move-in instructions. Improve the process before accepting another placement account. Use our [property management guides](/guides) to review related service and reporting choices.

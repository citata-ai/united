## What is a lease renewal rate?

A lease renewal rate is the share of leases ending in a period that the tenant renews. It tells an owner how many tenants chose to stay. A high renewal rate means fewer move-outs, fewer vacant days and fewer make-ready bills.

Property managers track renewal rate by month, quarter and year. They also track it by property, so one weak building does not hide in a portfolio average.

Related terms:

| Term | What it counts |
|------|---------------|
| Renewal rate | Renewals signed / leases that expired |
| Retention rate | Often the same; some managers also count month-to-month holdovers |
| Turnover rate | Move-outs / total units, often per year |
| Offer acceptance rate | Renewals signed / renewal offers sent |

## How do you calculate renewal rate?

You calculate renewal rate by dividing renewals signed by leases that expired in the same period, then multiplying by 100.

Renewal rate = renewals signed / leases expired x 100

Worked example for one quarter, with made-up numbers:

| Line | Count |
|------|-------|
| Leases that expired in Q3 | 40 |
| Renewed for a new term | 24 |
| Moved to month-to-month | 2 |
| Moved out by choice | 11 |
| Owner did not offer renewal | 3 |
| Renewal rate (24 / 40) | 60% |
| Retention rate, counting month-to-month (26 / 40) | 65% |
| Offer acceptance rate (24 / 37 offers) | 65% |

Counting rules to write down:

1. **Period.** Count by lease end date, not by signing date.
2. **Month-to-month.** Decide if it counts as renewed. Report it on its own line.
3. **Owner non-renewals.** Keep them in a separate line. They are a choice, not a lost tenant.
4. **Transfers.** A tenant who moves to another unit you manage is retained. Count it on its own line.
5. **Window.** Large portfolios use a trailing 12 months to smooth seasons.

## What is a good renewal rate?

A good renewal rate depends on the property type and market, but recent apartment data gives a benchmark. LeaseLock cites RealPage's database of lease transactions. It reports that "56 percent of market rate apartment residents with leases expiring in the first half of 2026" signed renewal leases. The same article says renewal rent growth averaged 3.5% so far in 2026.

How to use the benchmark:

- **Apartments.** Compare with the 56% RealPage figure, but know it is a national average for market-rate units.
- **Single-family rentals.** Tenants in houses often stay longer, but we found no primary national source for single-family renewal rates. Use your own history.
- **Your own trend.** The best test is your rate this year against your rate last year, by property.

A renewal rate far below your past numbers is a warning. Check rent increases, repair response times and tenant complaints first.

## How does renewal rate link to turnover cost?

Renewal rate links to turnover cost because each lease that does not renew becomes a turnover. A turnover has 2 costs: the direct make-ready work and the rent lost while the unit is empty.

Belong, a property management company, reported data from 232 turnovers it ran in the SF Bay Area in 2025. The average direct cost was $957. That covered cleaning, small fixes, paint touch-ups, hardware, inspections, photos and listing setup. Belong estimated lost rent during vacancy separately at about $2,625.

Worked example: a 100-unit portfolio, 80 leases ending in a year, using Belong's 2 averages ($957 + $2,625 = $3,582 per turnover). These are Bay Area numbers, so replace them with your own.

| Renewal rate | Turnovers | Turnover cost |
|-------------|-----------|---------------|
| 50% | 40 | $143,280 |
| 56% | 35 | $125,370 |
| 65% | 28 | $100,296 |

Moving from 50% to 65% saves 12 turnovers and about $43,000 a year in this example. That money goes to owners, not to vendors.

Watch for: renewal rate is not a goal by itself. A renewal at a rent far below market can cost more than a turnover. Compare the renewal offer with current market rent before you decide.

## How do property managers raise renewal rates?

Property managers raise renewal rates with a set renewal process and good daily service. Steps that work in most portfolios:

1. **Start early.** Send the renewal offer 60 to 90 days before the lease ends, or earlier if local law requires a longer notice.
2. **Price with data.** Base the renewal rent on market comps, not on a flat rule. Explain the number to the tenant.
3. **Fix open repairs first.** A tenant with an open work order is less likely to renew.
4. **Offer choices.** For example, a 12-month term at one rent and a 15-month term at another.
5. **Ask why tenants leave.** Use a short move-out survey and track the top 3 reasons.
6. **Follow up.** Call or message each tenant who has not answered within 14 days.

Fair Housing: apply the same renewal offer rules to all tenants. Do not offer different terms based on a protected class.

Some cities and states limit rent increases or require a reason to not renew. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What renewal reports should an owner get?

An owner should get a renewal report each month that shows what is coming up and what happened. Ask for:

- **Upcoming expirations** for the next 90 days, with the offer status of each.
- **Renewal rate** for the last month, quarter and trailing 12 months.
- **Renewal rent change** compared with the old rent and with market rent.
- **Reasons for move-outs** from the survey.
- **Turnover cost** for each unit that turned.

Maine sets its late fee rule in a short section: 14 M.R.S. § 6028. I read it on the Maine Legislature site on 2026-10-19. It has 3 parts: a grace period, a cap and a notice rule. The section has not changed since 1987.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| When rent is late | Not paid within 15 days of the due date | § 6028(1) |
| Maximum late fee | 4% of the amount due for one month | § 6028(2) |
| Written notice | At signing, that up to 4% may be charged | § 6028(3) |
| Rent vs. fees | Rent excludes fees and penalties | § 6000(1-B) |
| 7-day notice for rent | Rent 7+ days in arrears; fees not counted | § 6002(1)(C) |
| Deposit use | Unpaid rent and other actual causes | § 6033(2) |

## What is the maximum late fee a landlord can charge in Maine?

The maximum Maine late fee is 4% of one month's rent. Section 6028(2) says a landlord "may not assess a penalty for the late payment of rent which exceeds 4% of the amount due for one month."

| Monthly rent | Maximum late fee (4%) |
|---|---|
| $900 | $36 |
| $1,200 | $48 |
| $1,500 | $60 |
| $2,000 | $80 |
| $2,500 | $100 |

We did the math from the statute. The cap ties to "the amount due for one month." Read it as the month's rent, not a share of a partial payment.

## Does Maine law require a grace period before a late fee?

Yes. A payment of rent "is late if it is not made within 15 days from the time the payment is due" (§ 6028(1)). Before then, the payment is not late, and no penalty applies.

Worked timeline for rent due on the 1st:

| Date | Status |
|---|---|
| 1st | Rent due |
| 2nd to 16th | Inside the 15-day period; no late fee |
| 17th | Rent not paid within 15 days; late fee may apply |

Count the days with care. If in doubt, wait 1 more day. A fee charged 1 day early has no basis under § 6028.

## Must the late fee be written in the lease in Maine?

Yes, in effect. Section 6028(3) bars a late fee "unless the landlord gave the tenant written notice at the time they entered into the rental agreement that a penalty, up to 4% of one month's rent, may be charged for the late payment of rent."

So the notice must be:

- **in writing,**
- **given at the start** of the rental agreement, and
- **clear** that the penalty may reach 4% of one month's rent.

A lease clause signed at move-in meets all 3. A notice added mid-lease may not meet the "at the time they entered into" rule for that tenancy.

Maine's 2023 law on fees matters too. PL 2023, c. 594 defines "mandatory recurring fee" and excludes late fees from it (§ 6000(1-A)). So a late fee is not a mandatory recurring fee under that definition.

## Can a Maine landlord charge a daily late fee?

Section 6028 does not use the word "daily." It caps "a penalty for the late payment of rent" at 4% of one month's rent. So the sum of all late charges for a month must stay at or below 4%.

Worked example for $1,250 rent (cap: $50):

| Fee design | Total charged | Legal? |
|---|---|---|
| Flat $50 on day 17 | $50 | Yes |
| $5 a day from day 17, capped at $50 | $50 | Yes |
| $5 a day from day 17, no cap, 20 days | $100 | No, above 4% |
| $25 on day 5 | $25 | No, before the 15 days end |

The simplest safe design is one flat fee at or below 4%, charged after day 15.

## Can a Maine landlord evict a tenant for unpaid late fees?

Not on the 7-day rent ground. Section 6002(1)(C) allows 7 days' notice when "the tenant is 7 days or more in arrears in the payment of rent."

Since PL 2023, c. 594, the chapter defines rent as "an amount of money paid on a predetermined recurring basis" under the lease. It adds that rent "does not include security deposits, utility service costs, fees, penalties or charges" (§ 6000(1-B)). A late fee is a penalty.

What the landlord can do:

- **Collect from the deposit.** At move-out, § 6033(2) lets the landlord keep the deposit for "actual cause." Check that the fee was lawful under § 6028.
- **End a tenancy at will with 30 days' notice,** which needs no cause (§ 6002). Do not use it to retaliate.
- **Sue for the fee** as a debt.

Ask a Maine attorney before you start a case tied to fees.

## Maine late fee checklist

- Put the late fee notice in the lease at signing, up to 4%.
- Wait until the 15-day period ends.
- Cap all late charges for a month at 4% of the month's rent.
- Track rent and fees on separate lines in the ledger.
- Use only rent arrears in a 7-day notice.

## How common is fake income proof on rental applications?

Fake income proof is the most common type of rental application fraud that large operators report. The National Multifamily Housing Council (NMHC) surveyed 75 apartment owners and managers from November 15, 2023, to January 9, 2024. In that survey, 93.3% had seen fraud in the past 12 months. And 84.3% had seen applicants fake pay stubs, employment references or other income documents.

The same NMHC survey found two more numbers:

- 70.7% saw more fraudulent applications and payments than the year before.
- On average, 23.8% of eviction filings over 3 years linked to fraudulent applications and the unpaid rent after them.

A small landlord sees fewer applications. But the checks below cost little, so run them on every file.

## What are the signs of a fake pay stub?

A fake pay stub usually fails one of 12 checks. Run all of them on every stub. Most take under a minute.

| # | Check | What a real stub shows | Red flag |
|---|-------|------------------------|----------|
| 1 | Net pay math | Gross minus deductions equals net | Totals do not add up |
| 2 | Year-to-date math | YTD gross is close to gross × pay periods so far | YTD too high or too low for the date |
| 3 | Tax lines | Federal, Social Security, Medicare, and state tax where the state has one | No Social Security or Medicare line on a W-2 job |
| 4 | Round numbers | Odd cents, like $2,413.87 | Gross of exactly $3,000.00 every period |
| 5 | Pay dates | Dates fall on a fixed cycle (weekly, biweekly) | Pay date on a Sunday or a holiday |
| 6 | Employer address | Full street address that maps to an office | P.O. box only, or a home address |
| 7 | Employee details | Name, last 4 of SSN, employee ID | Missing ID, full SSN shown |
| 8 | Fonts and alignment | One font, numbers right-aligned | Mixed fonts, numbers out of line |
| 9 | Payroll provider | Name of a payroll company or the employer's system | No source at all |
| 10 | Stub sequence | Check numbers rise from stub to stub | Same check number on two stubs |
| 11 | File type | Original PDF from the payroll portal | Screenshot, photo of a screen, or edited image |
| 12 | Bank match | Deposits on the bank statement equal net pay | Net pay never shows up as a deposit |

Bank statements have their own red flags. Look for a running balance that does not follow from the deposits and debits. Look for a missing page number, such as "page 2 of 4" with only 2 pages sent. And compare the deposit amounts and dates to the pay stubs. Two fake documents rarely agree with each other.

Watch for: an applicant who sends only screenshots and refuses an original PDF. Ask once, in writing, with the same wording you use for every applicant.

## How do I check that an employer is real?

An employer check works only if you find the contact details yourself. A fake pay stub can print any phone number, including a friend who will "confirm" the job.

Use these 3 steps:

1. **Find the employer outside the file.** Search the company name and city. Use the phone number on the company's own website. For a small company, look it up in the state business registry of its home state.
2. **Call HR or payroll, not the supervisor named by the applicant.** Ask only to confirm employment, start date and job title.
3. **Use a verification service for large employers.** The Work Number by Equifax says it gets data from more than 4.88 million employers. It returns employer name, tenure, job title and gross income. It says it usually needs only the applicant's SSN.

For self-employed applicants, ask for tax records. The IRS lets a person view and download transcripts in an IRS Online Account. By mail, transcripts arrive in 5 to 10 calendar days, says the IRS Get Transcript page. A transcript the applicant downloads in front of you is hard to fake.

## Do document fraud tools work?

Document fraud tools catch edits that a person cannot see. They read the file's metadata (hidden data about how a file was made) and compare it to known templates from banks and payroll companies.

Snappt, one vendor, says its tool checks more than 10,000 document features against more than 2,000 financial institutions. It says a result arrives in under 10 minutes. Those are the vendor's own claims, not an outside test.

Know the limits before you buy:

- **Originals only.** These tools need the original PDF from the bank or payroll portal. A screenshot or a scan gives the tool little to read.
- **They flag, they do not decide.** A "fail" result is a reason to ask for more proof. It is not proof of a crime.
- **Notice rules may apply.** The FTC lists a reference-checking service that verifies application data as a consumer reporting agency. If such a report plays any part in a denial, give an adverse action notice. Ask your vendor in writing how it handles FCRA notices.

For most small landlords, the free checks above catch the crude fakes. A paid tool pays off when you screen many applications each month.

## What do I do if I find fraud?

Treat suspected fraud as a screening result, not a confrontation. Stay calm, stay written, and stay consistent.

Use this step list:

1. **Pause the file.** Do not hand over keys or sign a lease.
2. **Ask for other proof in writing.** For example, ask for an original PDF, a bank-linked income check, or an IRS transcript. Give the same deadline you give every applicant.
3. **Record what you found.** Save the document, the math that failed, and the date of each request.
4. **Decide with your written criteria.** Deny if the applicant cannot show income that meets the standard you publish for all applicants.
5. **Send the right notice.** If a credit, background or reference-check report played any part, the FTC says you must give an adverse action notice. It must name the screening company and say that the company did not make the decision. It must also tell the applicant about the right to a free report within 60 days and the right to dispute it. If a credit score was used, the notice must also show the score.
6. **Do not share accusations.** Do not tell other landlords or post about the applicant.

Apply every step the same way to every applicant. The Fair Housing Act bars discrimination based on race or color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status and disability, says the U.S. Department of Justice. Extra checks for some applicants and not others can look like discrimination, even when you mean well.

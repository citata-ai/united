## How does a tenant affect property showings?

A tenant makes showings an access and coordination issue that must follow the lease and local law.

Do not assume a listing agreement gives an agent unrestricted entry. Ask counsel to review entry rules for your location. Agree on a practical scheduling and contact process with the tenant. Keep notice records and avoid sharing access codes beyond authorized people.

Florida provides a specific example. Section 83.53 addresses access to exhibit a dwelling to prospective purchasers and other visitors. It also sets conditions on entry and bars abuse or harassment. That Florida rule does not establish a national notice period for showings.

Ask the listing agent to explain how showings will work in an occupied home. Set expectations for notice, escorts, pets, photography, and protection of personal belongings. A tenant is not your unpaid staging service. Resolve proposed cooperation terms clearly and lawfully.

| Before listing an occupied rental | What to resolve |
|---|---|
| Entry | Lawful notice, purpose, and scheduling |
| Photos | Access and handling of personal items |
| Buyer plans | Lease assumption or lawful future possession |
| Repairs | Tenant access and completion records |
| Closing | Rent, deposits, records, and new contacts |

A clear showing plan can reduce confusion without promising constant access. Ask the agent to work within that plan. If a disagreement occurs, seek advice before using locks, threats, or repeated entry attempts.

## What lease terms support a planned sale?

A locally reviewed lease should explain the tenancy and sale-related process without promising rights the law does not allow.

Tell prospective tenants that the home is listed. Give an accurate description of the expected showing process. Do not promise a long stable term while secretly planning to demand an early move. Make the written agreement match the offer.

Ask counsel to review term length, entry language, notices, renewal rules, and any proposed sale provision. A month-to-month tenancy is not automatic permission for immediate removal. Applicable termination and other tenant protections still need review.

Consider the buyer you expect to attract. An investor may evaluate the lease and rent records. A buyer who plans to live in the home needs a clear, lawful possession date. Do not promise both parties incompatible dates in separate documents.

Read the existing mortgage and insurance terms before changing use. Ask the servicer and insurer about the proposed rental period. Keep written answers with the lease. A listing status does not remove rental-use questions.

Build a budget for leasing, management, repairs, vacancy, and sale coordination. Use the [management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) for service cost assumptions. Add any costs tied specifically to an occupied sale. Collected rent can be smaller than the extra costs of a short rental period.

## Does selling a house end the lease?

A sale is not a safe basis for assuming the lease ends, so check its terms and the law before promising possession.

The Texas State Law Library explains that a new owner generally remains bound by the existing lease. Its guidance discusses lease provisions that address a sale and warns that foreclosure is a separate situation. That is Texas guidance, not a universal rule for every transaction.

Give the buyer the current lease, amendments, deposit information, and payment record through an appropriate process. Protect tenant personal data while supplying necessary due diligence. Do not hide an oral agreement that affects the tenant's rights or payments.

Ask the closing professional how rent and deposits will be handled. State who receives rent before and after closing. Obtain the required transfer records and notices for your jurisdiction. This guide does not supply a national deposit-transfer deadline.

If the buyer wants vacant possession, have counsel assess lawful options before you agree to a closing promise. A voluntary tenant agreement needs clear, lawful terms. Do not use pressure or self-help to meet a sales deadline.

Keep the manager informed of the signed sale terms. The manager needs correct payment instructions, ownership contacts, and repair authority during the transfer. A closing should not leave the tenant unsure who can fix an urgent defect.

## Is lease-to-own a better choice?

Lease-to-own is a different transaction that needs separate legal, lending, and tax review rather than a quick fix for a slow sale.

A rental agreement, purchase option, and purchase contract can create different duties. Ask counsel to identify which documents you need and how they interact. Define any option payment, rent credit, price method, expiration, repairs, and failure to close. Do not assume an online form covers your state's requirements.

Review financing with the current lender and the proposed buyer's lender. A plan that depends on future financing can fail even when rent payments stay current. Decide in advance how the tenancy and any credits work if a purchase never happens.

IRS Publication 527 addresses rental income under a lease with an option to buy. It also distinguishes rental expenses while property remains available for rent from some sale-only circumstances. Give your preparer the actual agreement and dates. Do not classify receipts by the label used in a sales pitch.

Compare a plain rental, a direct sale, and a proposed option using the same net-cash assumptions. Include legal work and the possibility of an extended holding period. Avoid treating a promised future sale price as cash available now.

Before accepting a tenant, have the agent and attorney agree on what you can promise buyers. Then have the tax professional review the expected use and receipts. Sign a lease only when the rental and sale plans can work together.

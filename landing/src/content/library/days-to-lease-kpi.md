## When does the days-to-lease clock start and stop?

Days to lease should measure a defined part of the rental process. For this guide, use the date a ready home goes live through the date all required parties sign the lease. This is a suggested operating definition, not an industry rule.

Keep the underlying dates. You may need another view for owner reporting or a comparison with a vendor's report. Never compare measures with different start and stop events without showing the difference.

| Measure | Suggested start | Suggested stop | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Make-ready time | Possession returned | Ready for marketing | Repair and preparation delay |
| Marketing-to-sign time | Ready listing live | Lease fully signed | Leasing process time |
| Vacancy time | Prior tenancy ends | New tenancy starts | Gap between tenancies |
| Open listing age | Ready listing live | Report date | Current unresolved exposure |

For an invented example, a ready listing goes live on October 1 and reaches full signature on October 16. Date subtraction gives 15 elapsed days. A move-in on October 20 does not change that signed-lease measure.

Choose whether your report uses calendar days and how it handles the launch day. Write that choice in the report notes. Keep paused listings visible with a reason instead of silently restarting the clock.

## What is a good days-to-lease number?

A useful days-to-lease target comes from comparable homes, seasons and service standards. This guide does not claim a verified national benchmark.

Buildium's lead-to-lease guide recommends tracking days on market alongside lead volume, showings and applications. That approach helps you see where the process stalls. A single final number cannot explain whether the problem is demand, access or follow-up.

Compare homes by relevant property features and market conditions. Keep the groups broad enough to show a pattern. Do not compare an occupied listing with restricted access against a vacant home with easy tours without noting that difference.

Use both a typical result and the range of outcomes. A median can describe the middle result, while older open listings reveal cases your completed-lease report excludes. Show the number of homes behind the result.

Set a target after you check the records. Ask what the team can control and what requires an owner decision. Price approval and repair funding may need a different escalation route from a missed follow-up.

Do not reward staff for short leasing time alone. The report should also show concessions, documentation quality and unresolved application issues. A quick signature is only useful when the process meets your service and compliance standards.

## Which steps add the most days?

Find the largest delay by recording dates at each handoff. Do not assume that more advertising solves every slow lease.

Buildium identifies unclear next steps before showings and weak follow-up after tours as possible losses in the leasing process. Treat those as points to inspect in your own records, not proof that they cause every delay.

Use this review sequence:

- **Check readiness.** Confirm that the home is available and the listing accurately describes its condition.
- **Check response.** Read the first reply and confirm that the prospect received a clear next step.
- **Check access.** Compare requested tour times with the slots your team can offer.
- **Check applications.** Identify missing information and show applicants how to complete the process.
- **Check signing.** Find out which signature or approved term remains open.

Assign a person to each delayed case. Give that person a next action and a review date. A dashboard with no named follow-up only reports the delay again.

Keep screening decisions within the approved process. The FTC says adverse action based partly or fully on a consumer report requires notice. Faster leasing does not remove that duty. Ask your adviser to review the screening workflow and notices.

## How should I report leasing time to owners?

Report the dates, current stage and next action beside the headline measure. An owner should understand why a home remains vacant without opening the full activity log.

Use a short update: listing date, current asking rent, inquiries, tours, completed applications and pending decision. Explain where figures come from. Distinguish a scheduled showing from a completed showing and a started application from a complete file.

If you recommend a price change, show current comparable listings and observed response. Explain what you propose and when you will review the result. Do not promise a signed lease by a date you cannot control.

Keep the same reporting format during slow periods. Silence makes it harder for the owner to judge whether work continues. Report that there are no new applications when that is the fact, then state the next step.

If the owner delays a decision, record the request and the response date. Show the effect as a separate interval. Do not edit the start date to improve your team's number.

Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) when discussing fee scope. Explain which leasing services your plan includes before you compare the fee with the vacancy cost.

## How can I improve the measure without distorting it?

Improve days to lease by testing a specific change while keeping the same clock. Start with a delay your records show, such as unavailable tours or incomplete follow-up.

Define the change and who owns it. Record the date it starts so you can compare similar cases before and after. Watch for changes in pricing, property mix or season that might explain the result.

Keep withdrawn listings and owner pauses in a separate report. Excluding them may help answer a narrow question, but the exclusion must remain clear. Include open cases in the review so an old vacancy cannot disappear from attention.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Open your current vacancy list and add the missing stage dates. That small data repair gives you a sounder starting point than adopting another company's target.

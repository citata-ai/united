## Which rental tasks need a local person?

Four rental tasks need a person on site: showings, repairs, emergencies and inspections. Everything else works from a laptop. Online tools now handle listings, applications, screening reports, e-signed leases, rent collection and bookkeeping.

Split your work before you buy or move. This table shows which tasks are remote and which need local help.

| Task | Remote? | Who does it on site |
|---|---|---|
| Listing and photos | Mostly. Photos need one visit | Photographer or local agent |
| Inquiries and pre-screening | Yes | You |
| Showings | Partly | Showing agent, lockbox, or video tour |
| Screening and lease signing | Yes | You, with e-signature |
| Rent collection | Yes | You, with online payments |
| Routine repairs | No | Handyman and licensed trades |
| Emergencies | No | Local contact plus on-call vendors |
| Move-in and move-out inspections | Partly | Local contact with a photo checklist |
| Legal notices | Partly | Server or local contact, per state rules |

If you cannot fill the "on site" column with names, you are not ready to manage from far away.

## What does the law ask of a landlord who lives far away?

Most states ask you to tell the tenant who manages the property and where to send notices. Distance does not change this duty. Two examples show how the rules differ.

- **California:** Civil Code 1962 says the lease must disclose the name, phone number and street address of each person authorized to manage the premises. The lease must also say where and how to pay rent.
- **Florida:** Florida Statutes 83.50 says the landlord must disclose in writing the name and address of the landlord or the person who receives notices. That person keeps the authority until the tenant hears otherwise.

Check your own state's law before you sign the first lease. Put your local contact's details in the lease, not only in an email.

A license question also comes up. California Business and Professions Code 10131 defines a broker as a person who leases or collects rent for another person for pay. Florida Statutes 475.01 has a similar broker definition. If you pay a friend to lease your unit, check if your state's license law covers that person.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I do showings remotely?

You have three main options for remote showings. Pick one by price, risk and how fast your market rents.

1. **Run a live video tour.** A local helper walks the unit on a video call while you answer questions. This costs the least but still needs a person on site.
2. **Hire a showing agent.** Many local agents and apps sell single showings. The agent meets the renter, opens the door and locks up.
3. **Use a self-showing lockbox.** The renter verifies ID in an app and gets a one-time code. This is fast, but it leaves the unit open to a stranger.

For each option, pre-screen first. Send every renter the same short list of questions about move-in date, budget and pets. Use the same list for everyone, so you treat all renters the same under Fair Housing law.

## How do I handle emergencies from far away?

Write an emergency plan before the tenant moves in. A burst pipe at 2 a.m. does not wait for your flight. The plan needs five parts.

- **One emergency number.** Give the tenant one number that someone answers 24 hours a day.
- **A clear list.** Define an emergency: active water leak, no heat in winter, gas smell, sewage backup, lockout, fire or break-in.
- **Pre-approved vendors.** Name a plumber, an HVAC company, a locksmith and a water damage company. Call each one before you need it.
- **A spending limit.** Let your local contact approve up to a set amount without you. Pick an amount you can afford, for example $500.
- **Shutoff map.** Photograph the water main, gas valve and breaker panel. Share the photos with the tenant and the contact.

Entry rules still apply in an emergency, but they are looser. California Civil Code 1954 lets a landlord enter without notice to respond to an emergency. Florida Statutes 83.53 lets the landlord enter at any time to protect or preserve the premises.

For routine repairs, the notice rules are stricter. California Civil Code 1954 presumes 24 hours' written notice is reasonable. Florida Statutes 83.53 asks for at least 24 hours' notice and entry between 7:30 a.m. and 8:00 p.m. Your local contact must know these rules too.

## How do I check the property when I am not there?

Plan regular checks and keep a photo record. Remote owners often learn about damage late, so a schedule helps.

- **Move-in inspection:** a dated photo or video of every room, signed by the tenant.
- **Seasonal check:** a short visit by your local contact, with notice, to look at smoke alarms, filters, leaks and the yard.
- **Move-out inspection:** the same rooms and angles as move-in, so you can compare.

Store every photo in one shared folder, named by date and room. A dispute over a deposit often comes down to these photos.

## When should I hire a property manager?

Hire a property manager when your local team cannot cover the on-site tasks, or when your time costs more than the fee. Do the math first.

**Worked example (hypothesis, not market data):** your rent is $1,800 a month. A manager charges 8% of rent, or $144 a month. You spend 6 hours a month on the unit and value your time at $40 an hour, or $240. Here the manager costs less than your time.

Now change one input. If you spend 2 hours a month, your time costs $80, and self-managing wins on cost. Run your own numbers in the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

Other signs you need a manager:

- You own units in a city you rarely visit.
- You cannot answer the emergency phone at night.
- Your state's notice and eviction rules are new to you.
- You have more than a few units in one place.

## Remote landlord checklist

Use this list before your first remote tenant moves in.

- [ ] Local contact named in the lease, with phone and address
- [ ] Online rent payment set up
- [ ] Written pre-screening questions, the same for every renter
- [ ] Showing method chosen and tested
- [ ] Emergency number, emergency list and spending limit set
- [ ] Plumber, HVAC, locksmith and water damage vendor on file
- [ ] Shutoff photos shared
- [ ] Move-in photo record done and signed
- [ ] Entry notice rules for your state written down
- [ ] Decision made: self-manage or hire, with the numbers behind it

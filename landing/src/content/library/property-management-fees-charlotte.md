## What leasing and renewal fees do Charlotte managers charge?

Charlotte leasing fees cluster at half a month's rent. Six of our 10 managers charge 50% of one month, so the median is $892.50 at a $1,785 rent.

- **50% of one month:** Evernest ($500 minimum), TouchPoint, Sunnon, White, Alarca and Mynd.
- **50% with a higher floor:** FCPM, with a $1,000 minimum.
- **75% of one month:** Northpoint.
- **$1,500 flat:** Ziprent, and Carod Properties as a setup fee that covers the first tenant.

Renewal fees are low in Charlotte. The median is $224.50, and 2 managers charge about $100. Northpoint's 25% renewal is the outlier at $446.25.

Watch for setup fees and markups. White charges a $499 setup fee, TouchPoint $150, and Carod's setup starts at $1,500. Carod adds a 15% surcharge on contracted labor; FCPM and White say they add no maintenance markup. Sunnon lists a $359 smart lock installation on its plans.

## Which local rules add cost for Charlotte owners?

North Carolina keeps city costs low. N.C. G.S. 160D-1207(c) says a local government may not require an owner or manager to get a permit or register a rental, except for properties with more than 4 verified violations in 12 months, 2 or more in 30 days, or a top-10% crime or disorder ranking under a local ordinance. The same section caps any special fee on such a property at $500 in 12 months.

State deposit rules set your limits:

| Rule | Limit | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit, month-to-month | 1.5 months' rent | G.S. 42-51(b) |
| Deposit, terms longer than month-to-month | 2 months' rent | G.S. 42-51(b) |
| Return and itemized list | Within 30 days after the tenancy ends | G.S. 42-52 |
| If damages are not known yet | Interim accounting in 30 days, final in 60 days | G.S. 42-52 |

## Must a Charlotte property manager hold a NC license?

Yes. N.C. G.S. 93A-2(a) defines a real estate broker as a person or business who, for compensation, "leases or offers to lease" or "rents or offers to rent any real estate" for others. A manager who rents your home for a fee needs a North Carolina Real Estate Commission license.

G.S. 93A-2(c) lists exemptions. The main one is an owner or lessor business that manages its own property, along with its W-2 employees and, for closely held entities, its owners. A third-party manager does not fit that exemption.

How to check: look up the firm and its broker-in-charge on the North Carolina Real Estate Commission's license search. A provisional broker must work under a broker-in-charge, per G.S. 93A-2(a2).

## Flat fee or percent: which model is common in Charlotte?

Percent pricing dominates in Charlotte. Eight of our 10 managers charge a percent of rent, from 8% to 10%. Only Mynd and Ziprent charge a flat monthly fee.

| Rent | 8% fee | 10% fee | $129 flat | $150 flat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1,500 | $120 | $150 | $129 | $150 |
| $1,785 | $142.80 | $178.50 | $129 | $150 |
| $2,500 | $200 | $250 | $129 | $150 |
| $3,000 | $240 | $300 | $129 | $150 |

At 8%, a $150 flat fee wins once rent passes $1,875. At 10%, it wins above $1,500. Charlotte rents rose 5.9% this year, so percent fees rose with them while flat fees did not.

Minimums matter at low rents. TouchPoint and FCPM charge at least $125 a month, so on a $1,400 rent their 8% ($112) becomes $125.

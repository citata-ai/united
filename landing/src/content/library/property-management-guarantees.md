## Which guarantees do property management companies offer?

Most property management guarantees fall into 6 types. Each one promises a result and names a remedy if you miss it. The table uses published terms from 3 managers, read on 2026-10-19.

| Guarantee | Promise | Remedy | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leasing time | A signed lease within a set number of days | Fee cut or fee waiver | RexMont: 30 days or the placement fee drops 50%. PMI Hudson: 21 days or 1 month of management fees waived. |
| Tenant | The placed tenant stays for a set time | Free re-placement | RexMont: tenant leaves or is evicted in the first 12 months, the next placement fee is waived. |
| Eviction | You cover eviction costs | Cash cap or fee waiver | PMI Hudson: costs up to $1,500 with its Eviction Protection Plan. RexMont: waives its own eviction fees only. |
| Pet damage | You cover pet damage above the deposit | Cash cap | RexMont: up to $1,000 per tenancy. Robert C. White: animal damage protection of $1,000. |
| Payout date | Owner money arrives by a set date | Fee refund | PMI Hudson: payout by the 10th business day, or that month's management fee back. |
| Satisfaction or exit | Easy exit or money back | No penalty, refund or payment | Robert C. White: 90-day money back on management fees, no termination fees, and it pays the first month at the next manager. PMI Hudson: cancel with 30 days notice, no penalty. |

Two patterns stand out. First, most remedies are waived fees, not cash. Second, every set of terms has conditions: the home must be rent-ready, the rent must match the manager's advice, and the owner must not override the screening.

## What does a guarantee cost me when it fails?

A guarantee costs you its payout times how often it triggers. Model it per door per year before you publish it.

Here is a worked example for 100 doors. The trigger rates and the fee are examples, not market data. Use your own history.

| Guarantee | Payout per trigger | Triggers per year (example) | Annual cost | Cost per door per year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leasing in 30 days (50% of a $1,000 placement fee) | $500 | 4 | $2,000 | $20 |
| Tenant guarantee (waived $1,000 re-placement) | $1,000 | 3 | $3,000 | $30 |
| Eviction cost cap | $1,500 | 1 | $1,500 | $15 |
| Pet damage cap | $1,000 | 2 | $2,000 | $20 |
| **Total** | | | **$8,500** | **$85** |

At $85 per door per year, the 4 guarantees cost about $7 per door per month in this example. Compare that with your monthly fee per door. If your fee is $200, the guarantees use about 3.5% of it.

The real cost also depends on your process. A strict screening process means fewer tenant and eviction claims. A good rent price means fewer missed leasing deadlines. That is why the conditions in the RexMont terms all protect the screening and the price.

Watch for: a guarantee that pays cash for lost rent or full damage is insurance. Robert C. White sells rent loss protection and $5,000 damage protection only in its top plan, at 14% of gross rents. Price that kind of promise from claim data, or buy cover from an insurer.

## How do guarantees change owner trust?

A guarantee builds trust when the owner can read the full terms before signing. It hurts trust when the owner finds a hidden condition the day they try to use it.

RexMont says this on its terms page: it publishes the full conditions "because a guarantee with hidden conditions isn't a guarantee". Its terms list every exclusion. For example, the 12-month tenant guarantee does not cover lost rent, turnover repairs, utilities or legal costs. It is also void if the owner picked a tenant against the manager's written advice.

That honesty has a sales effect. An owner who reads the exclusions up front does not feel tricked later. An owner who reads only "eviction guarantee" in a headline may expect you to pay lost rent too.

What to do:

- **Name the remedy in the headline.** "Placement fee cut 50% if no lease in 30 days" beats "30-day leasing guarantee".
- **Link the full terms** from every page that names the guarantee.
- **Put the terms in the management agreement**, so the promise binds you.
- **Pay fast** when a guarantee triggers. A slow payout cancels the trust you built.

## What rules apply to a guarantee in an ad?

Federal guidance covers how you advertise a guarantee. The FTC Guides for the Advertising of Warranties and Guarantees, 16 CFR part 239, apply to the advertising of any warranty or guarantee, except section 239.2, which covers consumer products only.

Three points matter for a property manager:

- **Satisfaction and money-back claims (239.3).** Use "satisfaction guarantee" or "money back guarantee" only if you refund the full price on request. Disclose any material limits or conditions clearly in the ad.
- **"Lifetime" claims (239.4).** If you say "lifetime", say whose life or what term it means.
- **Performance (239.5).** Advertise a guarantee only if you promptly and fully do what it promises.

State real estate rules can also apply. For example, Oregon's rule OAR 863-025-0020 requires a property management agreement to state the manager's compensation and the specific charges the owner pays. Check the rules in your state.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I write guarantee terms?

Write guarantee terms as a short list of conditions, a clear remedy, a cap and the exclusions. Then put them in the agreement.

Use this checklist for each guarantee:

- [ ] **Promise**: one sentence with a number (days, dollars, date).
- [ ] **Start of the clock**: for example, the day the home is rent-ready and listed.
- [ ] **Owner conditions**: price at or below your advice, approved screening criteria, showing access.
- [ ] **Remedy**: fee waiver, fee cut or cash, with the exact amount.
- [ ] **Cap**: per event and per tenancy.
- [ ] **Exclusions**: lost rent, legal costs, owner-picked tenants, law-protected lease breaks.
- [ ] **Claim process**: how and by when the owner claims, for example within 30 days of move-out.
- [ ] **Fair housing check**: assistance animals are not pets. RexMont's pet terms exclude them, because fair housing law does not treat them as pets.
- [ ] **Where it lives**: in the agreement, and on a public terms page.

Start with 1 or 2 guarantees you are sure you can honor. A leasing-time guarantee paid in fee cuts is a low-risk first step. Add cash guarantees only after you have 12 months of claim data.

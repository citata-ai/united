## Which Cook County towns does the RTLO cover?

The Cook County RTLO covers almost every rental unit in suburban Cook County, including mobile homes and subsidized units. The Cook County Board passed it in January 2021, and it took effect on June 1, 2021. The county's flyer names 3 exempt towns: Chicago, Evanston and Mount Prospect. Each has its own tenant-landlord ordinance.

Some units are exempt from the RTLO, except for the anti-lockout rules, per the Cook County Commission on Human Rights:

- **Small owner-occupied buildings.** The owner lives in a building of 6 units or less.
- **One-home owners.** A single-family home or condo, if the owner rents only that one property and the owner or an immediate family member lived there in the past 12 months.
- **Single room occupancy housing** that serves vulnerable residents.
- **Hotels, motels and rooming houses**, unless rent is paid monthly and the stay is over 32 days.
- **Dorms, shelters, employee quarters, non-residential rentals and owner-occupied co-ops.**

If your unit is exempt, you must tell the applicant so before you accept any fee. The anti-lockout rule covers every unit, with no exceptions. Only the Cook County Sheriff can carry out an eviction order.

Watch for: unincorporated Cook County adds a license. The Cook County Department of Building and Zoning requires a Residential Rental License for each unit in buildings of 4 or more units in unincorporated areas, with an interior inspection.

## How does the Cook County RTLO differ from the Chicago RLTO?

The Cook County RTLO and the Chicago RLTO share a structure, but key numbers differ. Both exempt owner-occupied buildings of 6 units or less. The table compares the points owners ask about most.

| Rule | Cook County RTLO (suburbs) | Chicago RLTO (Mun. Code 5-12) |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit cap | 1.5 times monthly rent | No cap found in Sec. 5-12-080 |
| Deposit account | Federally insured, Illinois institution, separate | Federally insured, interest-bearing, Illinois institution |
| Deposit interest | Not in the county summary | Yearly, at the city's set rate, if held over 6 months |
| Deposit return | Within 30 days after move-out | Within 45 days after move-out |
| Late fee cap | $10 plus 5% of rent over $1,000 | $10 plus 5% of rent over $500 |
| Notice for non-payment | 5 days | Not compared here |
| Non-renewal notice | 60 days | Not compared here |
| Single-home exemption | Yes, for one-property owners who lived there in the last 12 months | Not in Chicago's exemption list |

Sources: Cook County Commission on Human Rights RTLO page and summary; City of Chicago RLTO page; Chicago Municipal Code 5-12-080 and 5-12-140.

Worked example: a two-bedroom at $2,011 a month, HUD's FY2027 Fair Market Rent for the Chicago metro. In a suburb under the RTLO, the late fee cap is $10 + 5% × $1,011 = $60.55. In Chicago, it is $10 + 5% × $1,511 = $85.55. The RTLO deposit cap is 1.5 × $2,011 = $3,016.50. See the [Chicago Fair Market Rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/chicago-fair-market-rent-2027) for other sizes.

## What are the security deposit rules under the Cook County RTLO?

The Cook County RTLO deposit rules are in Sec. 42-811. The county summary lists these duties:

1. **Charge no more than 1.5 times monthly rent.**
2. **Give a signed receipt** with the owner's name, the date and the unit. For an electronic payment, an electronic receipt is allowed.
3. **Hold the deposit** in a federally insured account at an Illinois financial institution, apart from your other accounts.
4. **Tell the tenant in writing** which institution holds it, and tell the tenant again if you move it.
5. **Return it within 30 days** after the tenant moves out.
6. **Deduct only** for unpaid rent, court fees from a case you filed (not attorney's fees), or reasonable repair costs beyond ordinary wear and tear.
7. **Itemize repair deductions** within 30 days.

The penalty is steep. If you overcharge, do not return the deposit, or do not prove repair costs, the tenant may sue for 2 times the deposit plus attorney's fees. If you skip the receipt, the tenant is entitled to the deposit back at once.

Landlords get one protection here. For a paperwork mistake, such as not naming the bank, you have a 2-business-day right to cure after the tenant's notice. The county also lists a 14-day right to cure for material noncompliance with the lease.

## What does the Cook County Just Housing Amendment mean for tenant screening?

The Just Housing Amendment (JHA) to the Cook County Human Rights Ordinance limits how you use criminal history. It took effect January 1, 2020, with enforcement from February 1, 2020, per the Cook County Commission on Human Rights.

The core rules:

- **No arrests.** You may not deny an applicant for an arrest without a conviction.
- **3-year lookback.** You may not consider a conviction more than 3 years old.
- **No check box.** Your application may not ask if the applicant has a criminal background.
- **No blanket ad language.** "No felons" or "no criminal history" is not allowed. "No registered sex offenders or persons under a current child sex offender residency restriction" is allowed.

The screening process has 3 steps:

1. **Prequalify.** Check income, rental history, credit and pets. No criminal check yet. Then prequalify or deny on those criteria.
2. **Criminal check and individualized assessment.** Only after prequalification. Weigh the facts of any conviction from the past 3 years.
3. **Approve or deny.** A denial must be in writing and explain why it is needed to protect against a demonstrable risk to safety or property. The applicant may dispute it.

Before you take an application fee, give the applicant your tenant selection criteria and a notice of the right to dispute. The JHA does not require a criminal check at all. You do not have to hold the unit off the market while an applicant disputes.

## How do I get a suburban Cook County rental ready?

A short checklist for a unit under the RTLO:

- **Attach the county summary** of the ordinance to each lease.
- **Disclose** the owner, authorized agents and the deposit bank in writing.
- **Set the deposit** at or below 1.5 months of rent.
- **Set the late fee** at or below the RTLO formula.
- **Give 60 days' notice** if you will not renew.
- **Give 2 days' notice** before entry, except for emergencies.
- **Write your screening criteria** to match the JHA steps.
- **Check the town.** Evanston and Mount Prospect have their own rules, and unincorporated areas may need a rental license.

The county does not enforce the RTLO itself. The Commission on Human Rights says the RTLO is enforced by private lawsuits. That makes clean records your best defense. If you want help to run these steps, see our [property management page](/property-management).

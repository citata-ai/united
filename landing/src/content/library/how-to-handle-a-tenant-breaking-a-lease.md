## What should I do first when a tenant wants to break the lease?

Get the request in writing, then check whether the tenant has a legal right to leave. The answer decides what you may charge.

Use this order:

1. **Ask for written notice** with the move-out date and a forwarding address.
2. **Check for a legal exit.** Military orders, domestic violence, or another state right. See the section below.
3. **Read your lease.** Look for an early termination or lease break clause.
4. **Start marketing the unit.** In many states you must try to re-rent.
5. **Offer the tenant options.** A fee, a replacement tenant, or rent until re-rented.
6. **Document everything.** Ads, showings, applications and dates.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What can I charge a tenant who breaks a lease?

You can usually charge the rent you lose until a new tenant moves in, plus reasonable costs, minus what you could have avoided. The rules differ by state.

| State | What you may recover | Duty to re-rent | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | Unpaid rent, plus future rent minus the loss the tenant proves could be "reasonably avoided," plus other damages caused | Yes, in effect: avoidable loss is subtracted | Civil Code 1951.2 |
| Texas | Damages under the lease | Yes; a lease clause that waives it is void | Property Code 91.006 |
| Florida | One of four remedies, including a capped early termination fee | Yes, if you retake possession for the tenant's account | Statutes 83.595 |
| New York | Damages under the lease | Yes; a lease may not exempt the landlord from mitigating | NY Attorney General guide (RPL § 227-e) |

Worked example, rent $1,600, tenant leaves with 6 months left:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Unit re-rents after 45 days at the same rent | |
| Lost rent: 1.5 months × $1,600 | $2,400 |
| Advertising and showing costs (receipts) | $150 |
| Total claim | $2,550 |
| Minus deposit applied, after damage deductions | −$1,600 |
| Balance owed by tenant | $950 |

If you leave the unit empty for 6 months and bill $9,600, a court in a mitigation state can cut the claim. You must show you tried.

## What is a lease break fee clause?

A lease break fee clause sets a fixed price for leaving early. It trades an uncertain claim for a known amount. Both sides avoid a fight about how hard you tried to re-rent.

Florida sets the clearest rules in Statutes 83.595(4). You may charge liquidated damages or an early termination fee only if:

- [ ] The amount does not exceed 2 months' rent.
- [ ] For an early termination fee, the tenant must give no more than 60 days' notice.
- [ ] The tenant agreed when the lease was signed, by signing a separate addendum in the form the statute gives.
- [ ] The addendum says the landlord waives rent beyond the month the landlord retakes possession.

Other states have no rule this specific. Keep any fee close to a real estimate of lost rent and costs, and ask a local attorney before you use one.

## Do I have to re-rent the unit (mitigate)?

Yes, in many states you must make a real effort to re-rent. This is the duty to mitigate damages.

- **Texas:** Property Code 91.006(a) says "a landlord has a duty to mitigate damages if a tenant abandons the leased premises." Section 91.006(b) voids any lease clause that waives this duty.
- **Florida:** If you retake possession for the tenant's account, 83.595(2) requires good faith efforts. That means "at least the same efforts" you used to rent it the first time or use for similar units. You do not have to rent it before your other vacant units.
- **California:** Under Civil Code 1951.2, you recover future rent only above the loss the tenant proves "could be reasonably avoided."

Proof of effort is simple to keep: listing screenshots with dates, the asking rent, a log of inquiries and showings, and applications received.

## When can a tenant break a lease without penalty?

Some tenants have a legal right to leave early. Check these before you send a bill.

| Reason | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Military service | Service members may end the lease after entering service, or after orders for a permanent change of station or a deployment of 90 days or more. For a monthly lease, it ends 30 days after the next rent due date after notice. No early termination charge. | 50 U.S.C. 3955 |
| Military, Texas | A similar state right for service members and dependents | Property Code 92.017 |
| Domestic violence and other listed crimes, California | Tenant gives notice with documents; owes rent for no more than 14 days after notice | Civil Code 1946.7 |
| Family violence, Texas | Tenant may vacate and avoid future rent with notice and a qualifying court order or other listed documents | Property Code 92.016 |
| Domestic violence, New York | Notice at least 30 days before leaving; documents within 25 days | NY Attorney General guide |

Under the federal rule, the tenant still owes prorated rent up to the end date and lease charges such as excess wear.

## How do I document the move-out?

Treat a lease break like any move-out, with extra records. Each record supports your claim if the tenant disputes it.

- [ ] Written notice from the tenant, with the date received.
- [ ] Any signed early termination agreement or addendum.
- [ ] Move-out inspection with dated photos.
- [ ] Itemized deposit statement by your state's deadline.
- [ ] Marketing log: ads, rent, showings, applications.
- [ ] New lease start date and rent.
- [ ] Final statement of rent owed, credits and costs.

A tax note: IRS Publication 527 says that if a tenant pays you to cancel a lease, the payment is rent. You include it in income in the year you receive it.

This is general tax information. Ask a tax professional about your return.

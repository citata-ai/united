## What is a holdover tenant?

A holdover tenant is a tenant who stays in the unit after the lease ends. The lease term is over, but the tenant has not moved out.

There are 2 kinds of holdover:

| Type | What happened | Usual result |
|---|---|---|
| Holdover with consent | You let the tenant stay, or you accepted rent | A new tenancy, often month-to-month |
| Holdover without consent | You told the tenant to leave, and the tenant stayed | You may start an eviction case |

New York law describes the second kind. Under Real Property Actions and Proceedings Law 711, a landlord may bring a case when a tenant stays in possession after the term ends without the landlord's permission.

Your first step is a decision. Do you want this tenant to stay on new terms, or do you want the unit back? Every next step depends on that answer.

## Does accepting rent create a new tenancy?

Accepting rent for a period after the lease ends often creates a new tenancy. This is the most common holdover mistake. Here are 2 state examples.

| State | Rule when the landlord accepts rent after the lease ends | Source |
|---|---|---|
| California | The lease is presumed renewed on the same terms, for no more than 1 month when rent is paid monthly, and never more than 1 year | Cal. Civ. Code 1945 |
| New York | The acceptance of rent creates a month-to-month tenancy, unless the parties agree otherwise | N.Y. Real Prop. Law 232-c |

A new tenancy means you must end it again with proper notice. That can add a month or more to the timeline.

What to do if you want the unit back:

1. **Turn off autopay.** Stop online rent payments for any period after the end date.
2. **Return or hold payments.** Ask a local attorney how to treat a payment that arrives anyway. Some states treat partial or late payments in special ways.
3. **Say it in writing.** If you accept money, state in writing what it covers, for example "use and occupancy" for named days. Ask an attorney if that wording works in your state.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Can I charge holdover rent?

You can charge holdover rent in some states, and through the lease in many others. The rules differ.

- **By statute.** Florida Statutes 83.58 lets a landlord recover double the rent for the time a tenant refuses to leave without permission after the agreement ends.
- **By lease clause.** Many leases set a higher daily or monthly rent for any holdover period. Courts in some states limit clauses they see as a penalty.

Here is an example of the math with a lease clause. The numbers are an example, not a rule.

| Line | Example |
|---|---|
| Monthly rent | $1,500 |
| Daily rent ($1,500 × 12 ÷ 365) | about $49.32 |
| Holdover clause | 150% of daily rent |
| Holdover daily rent | about $73.97 |
| Tenant stays 20 days past the end date | 20 × $73.97 = about $1,479 |

A higher charge is only useful if you can collect it. Many holdover tenants leave owing money. Do not count on the extra charge.

## When do I start an eviction?

Start the eviction process when the required notice period runs out and the tenant has not left. You cannot skip the notice. Here is a common order of steps.

1. **Confirm the end date.** Read the lease. Check if it converts to month-to-month at the end.
2. **Check your notice.** Did you give a valid non-renewal or termination notice on time, in the right form?
3. **Talk to the tenant.** Ask for a move-out date in writing. Many holdovers end here, often because the next home fell through.
4. **Serve the notice to vacate.** Use the period and delivery method your state requires.
5. **File in court.** If the tenant stays after the notice period, file an eviction case.
6. **Let the sheriff or marshal act.** Only a court officer removes a tenant after a judgment.

Texas is a good example of a short notice. Texas Property Code 24.005 says the landlord gives a tenant who holds over at least 3 days' written notice to vacate before filing, unless the lease sets a shorter or longer period. Texas changed eviction procedure through SB 38 for cases filed from January 1, 2026. The Texas Tenant Advisor lists new delivery methods, such as email when the tenant agreed in writing. Check the current rules before you serve.

Other states require longer notice, and some need a reason to end the tenancy at all. Never change the locks, cut utilities or remove belongings. These self-help steps are illegal in most states.

## How can I prevent a holdover?

Most holdovers start with a missed step at renewal time. Use this checklist 90 days before each lease ends.

- [ ] Decide: renew, change terms, or not renew.
- [ ] Check the notice period in your state and city.
- [ ] Send a written renewal offer or a non-renewal notice, with a clear end date.
- [ ] Ask the tenant to confirm a move-out date in writing.
- [ ] Book the move-out inspection.
- [ ] Turn off autopay for any date after the end of the lease, if the tenant is leaving.
- [ ] Keep copies of every notice and message.

Holdover rules come from state law and the lease. If you manage many units, a property manager who knows local court steps can save weeks. See how owners [choose a property manager](/property-management).

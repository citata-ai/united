## Which category of tool comes first?

The core property system comes first because accounting and shared records shape the rest of the stack. Approve that design before connecting outside tools.

At the article's assumed 100-door size, compare systems using the same work and price scope.

| Core option | Public price basis | Important condition |
|---|---|---|
| Yardi Breeze residential | $1 per unit monthly | $100 monthly minimum; annual agreement |
| Buildium Essential | From $62 monthly | Exact size and usage need a quote |
| AppFolio Core | From $1.49 per unit monthly | Minimum spend applies |
| Rentvine | As low as $1.50 per active unit | Applicable rate and minimum need a quote |
| DoorLoop One | $2.50 per unit monthly displayed | $199 monthly minimum displayed |

Sources: [Yardi](https://www.yardibreeze.com/pricing/), [Buildium](https://www.buildium.com/pricing/), [AppFolio](https://www.appfolio.com/pricing), [Rentvine](https://www.rentvine.com/pricing) and [DoorLoop](https://www.doorloop.com/pricing), read 2026-10-19. Confirm DoorLoop's billing term.

A public rate supports a short list. It does not define the whole stack cost. Ask for payments, onboarding and required services separately.

## Which tools does the core system replace?

A core system may replace separate leasing, maintenance, reporting and document tools when its functions meet your requirements. Do not cancel an outside service until the replacement passes a test.

Vendor pages describe combinations of accounting, work orders, leases, portals and communication. Those labels do not prove equal depth. Give the core product the same tasks your outside tools perform.

Make a replacement register:

| Existing category | Test before removal |
|---|---|
| Rent tracking | Charges, receipts, returns and balances |
| Owner reports | Fees, reserves, bills and distributions |
| Repair tracking | Request, assignment, approval and invoice |
| Lease files | Signed documents and renewal dates |
| Tenant messages | Shared history with controlled access |
| Task list | Named owner, deadline and completion |

Keep a responsible person for every category. A tool can store the record without ensuring someone acts on it.

Check historical access before cancelling old services. Download documents and open the files outside the product. A link that needs an old account may not serve as an archive.

## Which showing, maintenance and CRM add-ons are worth it?

An add-on is worth testing when the core process has a documented gap and a person owns the result. Buy for the task, not the category.

A showing tool may help when staff cannot manage scheduling and follow-up reliably. Test availability changes, access rules and prospect status. Confirm the exact connection to your property system.

A maintenance add-on may help with intake or after-hours routing. Define where the service stops and staff responsibility begins. Test urgent escalation and vendor approval.

A CRM tracks leads and contact work. An owner-sales CRM should follow the owner inquiry through agreement and property setup. Do not confuse that process with tenant application tracking.

[LetHub's pricing page](https://www.lethub.co/pricing) describes leasing responses, scheduling and system connections. It also lists optional voice and lockbox services. Ask whether its exact integration supports your current plan.

Start with a trial of the most costly work gap. Measure completed tasks after corrections before adding another subscription.

## What is the monthly cost per door?

Monthly cost per door is the full recurring stack bill divided by managed doors. Include required subscriptions and company-paid usage.

A worked base example uses the article's assumed 100 residential doors. Yardi Breeze's published residential formula produces a $100 monthly software base. Dividing that base by the assumed doors produces $1 per door.

A second worked example adds LetHub at its published $199 monthly platform minimum. The combined base would be $299 monthly, or $2.99 per assumed door.

These calculations use Yardi and LetHub pricing pages read 2026-10-19. They are budget scenarios, not a verified combined offer. They do not prove the products connect or that the minimum covers your workload.

Keep exclusions visible:

- **Usage:** payments, screening and other activity.
- **Options:** voice, lockboxes and outside services.
- **Setup:** onboarding, conversion and cleanup.
- **Other work:** email, storage and business accounting if separate.
- **Tax:** any applicable invoice additions.

Ask each provider to price the actual scope. Do not call the example a complete operating stack until every required item has a quote.

## How do you budget setup and the first year?

Keep one-time setup separate from the normal recurring bill. Then add it to a first-year budget with your own stated planning assumptions.

Yardi's pricing page states no onboarding, training or support fees for its listed offers. LetHub lists onboarding from $199 and a 12-month contract billed monthly. Those terms were read on 2026-10-19.

If the worked $299 combined base applied unchanged for 12 months, the base total would be $3,588. Adding LetHub's stated starting onboarding amount produces $3,787.

That is conditional arithmetic, not an all-in quote. It excludes the items listed above and any changed service scope. Confirm the actual contract terms before using it for cash planning.

Also budget your staff work. Someone must clean records, map fields, approve balances and train users. Label the estimate as your own assumption.

Keep a normal-year budget beside the first-year budget. That makes temporary costs and ongoing commitments easy to distinguish.

## Which integrations matter most?

The most important integrations move a required record reliably and show failures clearly. Test the transfer direction, fields and responsible support team.

An API is a software connection. Its availability does not prove that a partner tool supports your workflow. AppFolio and Buildium vary API access by plan; ask which tier your connection needs.

Use this integration test:

| Action | Acceptance result |
|---|---|
| Create record | Correct property and person appear |
| Change status | Required update reaches the destination |
| Attach evidence | Document remains linked and readable |
| Fail transfer | Staff see the error and can recover |
| Remove access | Former users lose the right permissions |
| Export history | Records remain usable outside the stack |

Assign a business owner for each connection. A vendor can support software while your team still owns data quality and process decisions.

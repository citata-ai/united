The down payment is often the largest check a new landlord writes. It is also a choice, not just a rule. The lender sets the minimum. You decide whether to put down more.

This guide gives the current minimums by loan type, explains reserves, and shows what the down payment does to your cash flow.

## What Down Payment Do Conventional Investment Loans Need?

Fannie Mae sets the limits most lenders follow for conventional loans. Its eligibility matrix, dated August 5, 2026, lists the maximum loan-to-value (LTV) ratio for each case. LTV is the loan amount divided by the value.

| Purchase type (Fannie Mae, standard loans) | Maximum LTV | Minimum down payment |
|---|---|---|
| Principal residence, 1 unit, fixed rate | 97% | 3% |
| Principal residence, 2 to 4 units | 95% | 5% |
| Second home, 1 unit | 90% | 10% |
| Investment property, 1 unit | 85% | 15% |
| Investment property, 2 to 4 units | 75% | 25% |

Two notes. First, the matrix lists exceptions and extra conditions, such as credit score and debt-to-income limits. Second, a lender can ask for more than Fannie Mae's minimum. Ask each lender for its own rule.

Below 20% down, your lender may add mortgage insurance to the cost. Ask for the full monthly payment, not only the rate.

## Can I Use FHA or VA for a Rental?

Yes, but only if you live in it. FHA and VA loans are for homes you occupy. They can work for a 2 to 4 unit home where you live in one unit and rent the others.

| Loan | Units allowed | Down payment | Must you live there? |
|---|---|---|---|
| FHA | 1 to 4 family homes | At least 3.5% of appraised value in cash (12 U.S.C. 1709(b)(9)) | Yes; 24 CFR 203.18 defines a principal residence as where you live most of the year |
| VA | Single-family home up to 4 units | Often none, if you qualify (VA) | Yes; VA says you must live in the home you buy |
| Conventional, 2 to 4 units, owner lives there | 2 to 4 units | 5% (Fannie Mae matrix) | Yes |

FHA law also lets borrowed funds from a family member count as cash in some cases, per 12 U.S.C. 1709(b)(9)(B). VA charges a one-time funding fee, which VA says lowers the cost of the program for taxpayers.

Fannie Mae defines a principal residence as a property the borrower occupies as a primary residence. Plan to live there. Do not use an owner-occupied loan for a home you plan to rent out in full.

## How Do Reserve Requirements Work?

Reserves are liquid or near-liquid money you still have after closing, such as bank balances. They show you can pay the mortgage if rent stops.

Fannie Mae's reserve rules (B3-4.1-01) for Desktop Underwriter loans:

| Situation | Reserves required |
|---|---|
| Second home purchase | 2 months of the payment (PITIA) |
| Investment property purchase | 6 months of PITIA |
| 2 to 4 unit principal residence | 6 months of PITIA |
| Other financed homes: 1 to 4 | Plus 2% of their total loan balances |
| Other financed homes: 5 to 6 | Plus 4% of their total loan balances |
| Other financed homes: 7 to 10 | Plus 6% of their total loan balances |

PITIA means principal, interest, taxes, insurance and association dues. Here is an example with invented numbers. The new rental's PITIA is $2,300, so 6 months is $13,800. You also own one other rental with a $150,000 loan, so you add 2%, or $3,000. Total reserves: $16,800, on top of the down payment and closing costs.

## How Does a Bigger Down Payment Change Cash Flow?

A bigger down payment means a smaller loan, a lower payment and higher monthly cash flow. It also ties up more of your cash. So the return on your cash can go either way.

Here is a worked example with invented numbers: a $300,000 single-family rental with $18,000 of yearly NOI (net operating income, before the mortgage). Closing costs are $9,000. The rate is 7.40%, the Freddie Mac 30-year average on 2026-10-15, per FRED.

| Down payment | Loan | Yearly payment | Yearly cash flow | Monthly cash flow | Cash invested | Cash-on-cash |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15% | $255,000 | $21,187 | −$3,187 | −$266 | $54,000 | −5.9% |
| 25% | $225,000 | $18,694 | −$694 | −$58 | $84,000 | −0.8% |
| 40% | $180,000 | $14,955 | $3,045 | $254 | $129,000 | 2.4% |
| 100% cash | $0 | $0 | $18,000 | $1,500 | $309,000 | 5.8% |

At this rate, every extra dollar down raises the cash-on-cash return. That is because the loan costs more each year (about 8.3% of the balance) than the property earns (a 6% cap rate). When rates fall below the cap rate, the result flips. A smaller down payment can then raise your return on cash.

The table leaves out mortgage insurance, principal paydown and value growth. Those change the full return.

## What Should You Do Before You Apply?

Use this checklist:

- **Choose the occupancy.** Investor, second home, or owner in one unit of a 2 to 4 unit home.
- **Check the matrix.** Note the maximum LTV for your case.
- **Count reserves.** Add 6 months of PITIA, plus 2% to 6% of other loans.
- **Run cash flow** at 3 down payment levels, as in the table.
- **Ask 3 lenders** for a full quote with the same down payment.
- **Price management.** Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) so NOI is real.

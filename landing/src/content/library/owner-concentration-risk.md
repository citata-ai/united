## How do I measure owner concentration?

Measure concentration by grouping related owners and comparing their doors, recurring fees and service contribution with company totals. Do not let separate legal entities hide a common decision-maker. Record how you define each group.

Door share is a useful start: owner-group doors divided by total managed doors. Fee share uses recurring fees from that group divided by total recurring fees. Keep the same reporting period and state which income you exclude.

Service contribution adds another view. Subtract direct service costs from the group's earned fees. Use a consistent cost method and have the accountant review it. A large discounted account may provide less support for overhead than its door count suggests.

Use this proposed risk sheet:

| Measure | Source | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Door share | Current property register | How much field work belongs to the group? |
| Recurring fee share | Company fee ledger | How much repeat income could stop? |
| Service contribution | Fees less assigned direct costs | How much support for overhead could disappear? |
| Departure rights | Signed agreements | When and how might the work end? |
| Cash exposure | Forecast and unpaid balances | What would the exit do to company liquidity? |

SBA's finance guidance supports financial records and cash planning. Apply that method to the owner group instead of using a guessed industry threshold.

Keep the group's ownership and authority records current. Ask who makes decisions when an owner retires, sells or changes asset managers.

## Why might buyers discount a concentrated book?

A buyer may value concentrated income less if a large relationship can leave after closing. Treat that as a deal question to investigate, not a universal discount formula. Ask the buyer which evidence changes its assessment.

Show the signed agreements, account history and responsible service staff. Explain whether the relationship belongs to the company or mainly to the founder. A documented team process can make the risk easier to assess.

Separate actual terms from a headline valuation. A buyer may propose contingent payments, retained amounts or conditions tied to owner retention. Those terms can matter as much as the stated price.

Do not apply a general acquisition multiple to the entire owner group's fees. First ask what earnings measure the buyer uses and which costs it expects after transfer. Founder work may need a replacement salary.

Ask counsel whether assignment, notice or consent is needed for the proposed transaction. Do not assume a management agreement transfers merely because the business sells.

Prepare a neutral account review that shows strengths and unresolved issues. Hiding a pending sale or service dispute can delay diligence. Deal planning starts with accurate records, not a lower concentration figure produced by regrouping entities.

## How do I lower concentration?

Lower concentration by adding suitable independent owners while keeping the current large account well served. Do not accept weak-fit homes merely to improve a ratio. New work can consume cash before it supports overhead.

Set a target service profile by area, property type and owner needs. Track whether leads fit that profile. A wider client list helps only when the company can deliver the promised work.

Review the sales pipeline by independent owner group, not only doors. Another large portfolio from a related decision-maker may increase the same exposure. Ask how the acquisition changes the departure scenario.

Build relationships beyond the founder. Introduce the service manager, accounting contact and escalation lead. Keep the owner record shared within proper access limits so a staff absence does not interrupt service.

Use the [owner discovery guide](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai) to review how prospects find and assess your firm. Publish accurate service facts, but do not promise that a channel will produce diversification.

Check whether pricing supports the work. If the large account has unusual service demands, review them with evidence and at the proper contract point. Avoid sudden charges framed as a penalty for being large.

## What agreement terms help manage the risk?

Agreement terms help when they make service scope, authority and exit work clear. Have counsel review notice, termination, record transfer, unpaid fees and assignment for the applicable state. A harsh clause cannot replace a sound cash plan.

Oregon's Real Estate Agency gives a useful state example. Its agreement guidance includes the term, termination method and transfer of property records after termination. It also requires fees and duties to be stated. Do not treat that list as every state's rule.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Check who may give instructions for the owner group. Record a primary authorized contact and the path for a change in authority. Conflicting directions from related entities can create service and billing problems.

Plan the practical exit. Identify who exports records, completes final accounts and transfers open work. Estimate the staff time that remains after recurring fees stop.

Keep owner funds separate from the company's departure reserve. Ask the accountant to identify which cash the company can actually use. Do not count client money as a buffer against losing client income.

## How should I test a large-owner departure?

Test a departure by removing that owner's future company fees from your cash forecast. Add the work and costs needed to close the account properly. Keep the assumptions visible for review.

List costs that can stop and costs that remain. Payroll, software commitments and office costs may not change on the same date. Ask what service impact would follow each cost reduction.

Check whether the remaining company can support other owners without rushed cuts. Protect account controls, urgent coverage and required supervision in the scenario. A paper saving that breaks service is not a useful solution.

Record the actions you can take now: broaden suitable leads, improve records, build cash and train backups. Give each action a lead.

Use the [fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) for price scenarios, then replace estimates with the actual fee ledger. Start with the owner group whose departure would change the most decisions.

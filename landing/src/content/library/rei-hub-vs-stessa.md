## What is double-entry bookkeeping, and do you need it?

Double-entry bookkeeping records linked effects across accounts rather than only listing money received and spent. Choose the system that can produce the books your accountant needs.

| Tool | Vendor-described accounting focus | Price reference | Main test |
|---|---|---|---|
| REI Hub | Double-entry books and full financial statements | Professional up to 3 units: $15 monthly or $9 monthly billed annually; free option listed | Opening balances and linked accounts |
| Stessa | Rental income, expense, and owner reporting | Essentials free; Manage $15 monthly; Pro $35 monthly | Required reports and transaction detail |

Sources: REI Hub FAQ and pricing, and Stessa pricing, read on 2026-10-19. REI Hub prices paid tiers by portfolio size. Stessa also offers annual billing.

REI Hub explicitly describes itself as double-entry software. Stessa's pricing page describes reporting and tracking features. That page alone does not establish an equivalent accounting structure or prove a missing capability.

Ask your accountant what the business needs before drawing a conclusion. A clear year-end handoff matters more than which interface looks simpler during signup.

## How do the reports differ?

REI Hub documents full financial statements, while Stessa lists basic and advanced reports by plan. Compare actual output rather than matching report names.

REI Hub's FAQ lists a profit and loss statement, balance sheet, and statement of cash flows. It also says accurate balance sheets need starting balances. Treat setup as part of the accounting work.

Stessa's pricing page separates basic reporting from advanced reports and transaction tracking. Ask to see the report required for your next accountant handoff in the exact plan you will use.

Choose a closed period with checked source records for the trial. Enter or import approved sample data. Compare income, expenses, loans, transfers, and opening balances with your existing books.

Inspect how you reach the underlying transaction. A total is easier to review when the accountant can trace the source and attached evidence. Check the exported detail too.

Do not treat a tax-labeled report as a finished tax return. This guide compares software output, not filing duties or deductions. Have your accountant approve the final reporting process.

## How do prices compare?

Compare your required paid plan and portfolio size under the same billing term. A free plan is useful only if its limits fit your work.

REI Hub's pricing page lists paid tiers for different unit counts. Its entry Professional price covers up to 3 units. Stessa's paid plans use a different plan structure. Avoid comparing the smallest REI Hub tier with a Stessa plan that serves a different task.

The prices in the opening table come from the vendors' pricing pages read on 2026-10-19. Use those pages for current terms. Do not take a price from a general FAQ when a newer pricing page differs.

Write down your required bank feeds, receipt capture, reporting, user access, and leasing work. Mark which plan provides each required task. Ask about charges outside the subscription.

Include setup work in the decision. Staff time spent cleaning old transactions or entering opening balances is a real project cost, even when the app is free.

Choose a billing term after the workflow passes. A lower annual rate does not help if the software fails your accountant's required report.

## Which does your CPA prefer?

Your CPA should choose based on usable records, access controls, and the work needed to close the books. Do not assume a professional preference from a brand name.

REI Hub says users can invite an accountant to a portfolio. Stessa lists shared account access and full data export in its pricing comparison. Test permissions and exported records before relying on either.

Send your accountant a sample report packet with transaction detail. Ask what is missing, what needs recoding, and which records should remain outside the app.

Use the same sample in both systems. Include a transfer, a loan payment, an owner contribution, and a corrected expense. Confirm the classification with your accountant rather than assuming the imported category is right.

Ask how adjustments return to your working records. If the accountant changes a category outside the app, staff need a clear way to keep the source records consistent.

Check access when the accountant's work ends. Remove a sample user and confirm that exports and private documents remain under your control.

Keep the accountant's acceptance notes with the software decision. That gives future staff a clear reason for the chosen setup.

## Which handles many entities or LLCs?

Test separate entities as accounting structures, not just as labels on a dashboard. LLC means limited liability company. The required reporting setup depends on your ownership and accounting needs.

A portfolio view can group properties without proving that entity books, bank accounts, and intercompany activity remain distinct. Ask the vendor to demonstrate your intended structure.

Use this setup checklist with your accountant:

- **Name entities:** Match each entity to its properties and approved account structure.
- **Map banks:** Confirm which transactions enter which records.
- **Set balances:** Check opening amounts before reviewing ongoing results.
- **Record transfers:** Keep transfers distinct from rental income and operating expenses.
- **Test access:** Limit each invited user to the records needed for the job.
- **Export detail:** Confirm that entity names and account codes survive the export.
- **Review changes:** Document who can alter categories or past-period records.

Ask what happens when a property moves between portfolios or an entity stops operating. Historical reports should remain understandable after the change.

Choose REI Hub if its accounting model and reports pass the required close. Choose Stessa if its tracking and reporting pass the same review with less unwanted work.

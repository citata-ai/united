## Who should call the owner who wants to cancel?

The company owner or the most senior manager calls, within 1 business day of the notice. A senior voice tells the owner the notice matters. It also brings someone who can approve a fix on the call.

Do not send the person the owner is unhappy with. That person will defend past work, and the owner will hear excuses. Do not reply by email only. Email hides tone, and the owner may not answer.

Open the call with a question, not a pitch:

- "I saw your notice. Before we do anything, can you tell me what went wrong?"
- "What would have had to be different for you to stay?"
- "Is anything else going on with the property, like a sale or a move?"

Listen and take notes. Do not argue with the facts as the owner sees them. Read the notes back at the end, so the owner knows you heard.

## Why do owners cancel, and which reasons can I fix?

Owners cancel for a small set of reasons, and only some of them are yours to fix. Sort the reason first. The fix depends on it.

Blanket's guide on owner churn names the common causes: a gap between price and value, slow replies, competitors, and generic service. It calls owners selling their property the biggest driver of churn. Use the table to sort the reason you hear.

| Reason the owner gives | Can you fix it? | Fair response |
|---|---|---|
| "Nobody answers me." | Yes | New account lead, a reply time you can keep, a weekly update for 90 days |
| "Repairs take too long or cost too much." | Often | Show the repair log; set an approval limit; get a second bid on large jobs |
| "Your fee is too high for what I get." | Sometimes | Show what the fee covers; offer a lower tier if you sell one |
| "I found a cheaper company." | Sometimes | Compare services line by line, not only the fee |
| "I am selling." | Partly | Offer help with the sale or a buyer who may keep you |
| "I want to manage it myself." | Rarely | Respect it; offer a lease-only or a la carte service if you sell one |
| "The vacancy lasted too long." | Often | Show the pricing and showing data; agree a plan for the next turn |

## What offers are fair to make to an owner who wants to leave?

A fair offer fixes the stated problem and is one you would give any owner in the same position. Make one offer, not three. Many offers sound like panic.

Good offers are specific and short in time:

1. **A named fix with a date.** "Maria takes over your account Monday. She sends you an update every Friday for 3 months."
2. **A trial period.** "Stay 90 days. If we miss the reply time we promised, cancel with no fee."
3. **A fee change tied to the service.** "Move to our lease-only plan at a lower fee." Only if you sell that plan.
4. **Help with a sale.** If you or a partner hold a broker license, offer to list the property or find a buyer who keeps it rented.

Blanket's guide suggests the sale case as the big chance: list the home to your other owners, so the door can stay under management. That page promotes Blanket's own platform for this, so treat it as a vendor's view.

Do not offer a fee cut you cannot keep. Other owners will hear about it. Then a cancel notice becomes the way to get a lower price.

## When should I let the owner go?

Let the owner go when the fit is wrong, or when the cost to serve is higher than the fee. Keeping a bad fit costs your team time that good owners pay for.

Blanket's guide uses the term "forced attrition" for ending relationships with owners who do not fit. It notes that this may cost some revenue at first.

Let the owner go when one of these is true:

- **The owner refuses needed repairs.** Habitability repairs are not optional for you or the owner.
- **The owner asks for something illegal.** For example, screening or treating renters in a way that breaks fair housing law.
- **The owner wants a service you do not sell.** And you do not plan to sell it.
- **The door loses money.** The fee does not pay for the time it takes, even at full rate.

To test the last point, run the door through the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) with your real hours.

## How do I hand off well if the owner leaves?

Hand off fast, in full, and in writing. A clean exit is the last thing the owner remembers about you, and the next manager will see your files.

Some states set deadlines for this. Here are three examples from rule and statute text.

| State | What the rule says | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Oregon | Within 60 days after termination: disburse obligated funds, give the owner a final ledger accounting, owner funds, a deposit accounting and current leases, unless the owner directs a transfer in writing. By the next calendar day after termination, notify each tenant whose deposit you hold where it will go. | OAR 863-025-0070(2), (3) |
| New Mexico | Final accounting of trust account funds to the owner within 60 days of the effective date of termination. | 16.61.24.12(D) NMAC |
| Florida | On a change of rental agent, deposits and advance rent go to the new owner or agent, with interest and an accounting per tenant. | Fla. Stat. 83.49(7) |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Use this handoff list for every exit:

- [ ] Confirm the end date from the notice and the agreement
- [ ] Ask the owner in writing where funds, deposits and files go
- [ ] Notify tenants of the change, by your state's deadline
- [ ] Send leases, ledgers, inspection reports and open work orders
- [ ] Return keys and codes, with a signed key list
- [ ] Transfer deposits with an accounting per tenant, and get a receipt
- [ ] Send the final owner statement and pay the final balance
- [ ] Note the year's rent paid over, for the owner's 1099
- [ ] Record the exit reason in your churn log

## How do I track owner churn after the call?

Track churn each month with one formula. Divide owners lost by owners at the start of the period, then multiply by 100. Blanket's guide gives the example of 10 lost out of 120, which is 8.3%.

Log 5 facts for each exit: the owner, the start date, the fee, the exit date and the reason in the owner's words. After 12 months, sort the log by reason. The top reason tells you where to fix the business.

A bad exit can also show in search: reviews from former owners can appear in search results and AI answers. Our [property management](/property-management) page shows what AI search reads when owners ask for a manager.

## Which three balances must match?

Three-way reconciliation checks bank cash, the trust cash ledger, and the total funds assigned to clients. Rentvine describes these as the bank statement, trust ledger, and property ledgers.

Use an adjusted bank figure. The printed ending balance may differ from ledger cash because a deposit or payment has not cleared. Keep those timing items visible.

| Balance | Record to inspect | Key question |
|---|---|---|
| Adjusted bank cash | Statement plus supported outstanding items | Does bank cash match book cash? |
| Trust cash ledger | Cash account detail for the same period | Did every receipt and payment post? |
| Client ledger total | Property or beneficiary balance report | Can you assign all held money correctly? |

Check the account scope carefully. Do not compare a combined portfolio ledger with a single bank account. Use the software's supported reports for the relevant account.

A bank match alone is not the full check. Cash can match while the money belongs to the wrong property. Review negative client balances and unexplained funds separately.

## Which reports do you pull?

Pull the bank statement, cash detail, client balance report, and outstanding transaction list for the same cutoff. Save the report settings as well as the output.

Start with the prior approved reconciliation. Confirm that its closing figures match the new opening figures. If they do not, find the change before matching current transactions.

Use the bank statement as independent evidence. A bank feed helps with entry and matching, but the feed does not replace the statement review. Check missing dates and disconnected accounts.

Rentvine's Reconciliation help describes statement upload, bank sync, and manual workflows. It also describes a matching banner within its interface. Do not confuse an interface's matching checks with the separate beneficiary-ledger review.

Add supporting schedules for deposits and money held for specific purposes. Keep the detail linked to the ledger balance. A total without a list of whose money it represents is hard to review.

## What causes a mismatch?

A mismatch can come from timing, missing entries, wrong accounts, or incorrect client allocation. Treat the difference as a question to trace, not a figure to erase.

A timing item has a real transaction and evidence that it clears later. A missing bank fee needs an entry. A receipt on the wrong property needs a correction to its allocation.

Check imports for duplicate entries. Then compare dates, amounts, account selection, and transaction references. A bank feed and manual entry can describe the same receipt twice.

| Symptom | Check first | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Bank exceeds books | Missing receipt or outstanding payment | Posting vague income |
| Books exceed bank | Missing fee, return, or duplicate receipt | Deleting a real charge |
| Client totals differ | Allocation and excluded ledgers | Moving money between owners |
| Prior period changed | Backdated edits and reversals | Ignoring the opening gap |

These are diagnostic paths, not a complete list of causes. Follow the source document through every affected record.

## How do you fix common errors?

Fix the entry that caused the error and preserve the correction trail. Do not use a general adjustment to make the report turn green.

If a payment is on the wrong unit, inspect its receipt and bank reference. Use the software's supported correction method. Check the tenant ledger, client ledger, and cash effect after the change.

If a return is missing, record it against the correct original payment. Check whether fees and resident balances update separately. Do not assume the return cancels every linked entry.

For old outstanding items, look for evidence of clearance, cancellation, or replacement. Ask your accountant how to handle unresolved items. Do not carry a stale item forward without an assigned follow-up.

Use a close checklist:

- **Verify the opening period.** Confirm that the prior close still agrees.
- **Match source transactions.** Review deposits, payments, fees, and returns.
- **Review client funds.** Trace negative or unusual balances.
- **Resolve exceptions.** Attach evidence and record the reason for corrections.
- **Obtain review.** Have another authorized person check the work.
- **Save the close.** Keep statements, reports, and approval together.

## How often should you reconcile?

A monthly close is a useful operating schedule, with interim checks when money moves. Your applicable trust-account rules may require a different process. Confirm those duties for your business.

Review returned payments and bank changes as they occur. Waiting until close can leave wrong balances in owner reports. Assign daily receipt checks and payment approvals to named staff.

Reconcile before relying on cash for distributions. Review reserves, liabilities, and held funds as part of the payment decision. A positive bank total does not establish that every dollar is available.

Restrict backdated edits after approval through the controls your software supports. If a closed period changes, record why and obtain review again.

Finish with a close file that another person can follow. The best check is not a green badge. It is a clear path from the bank statement to the ledger and each client's funds.

## How should you test the choice before you pay?

Test the full workflow with sample records before you move live data. Use the same case in each product. Ask the person who will do the daily work to run the test. A sales demo alone does not show where your team will need help.

Keep a decision sheet with the task, result, missing step, and person who checked it. Mark each result as shown, promised, or still open. Ask for written terms when a feature needs an upgrade or another service. Include staff setup time in your cost review.

Check the exit path while you still control the choice. Export the sample records and open them outside the platform. Confirm that notes, files, dates, and links remain useful. Ask how you will get records after you close the account.

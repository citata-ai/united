A guest slips on an icy step at your rental. The injury claim is larger than your liability limit. Two questions decide what happens next. Who owes the claim? And who pays it?

An LLC answers the first question. An umbrella policy answers the second. That is why the "LLC or umbrella" debate on landlord forums often ends with "both". This guide compares them for an owner of 1 or 2 rentals.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What Does Each One Protect?

An LLC separates the rental's debts from you. An umbrella pays covered claims above your base policy.

**LLC.** A limited liability company is a separate legal entity created by state law. Florida's LLC law is a clear example. Florida Statutes 605.0304 says a debt or liability of an LLC is solely the debt of the company. A member is not personally liable for it solely because they are a member. Other states have similar laws, with their own exceptions. An LLC does not protect you from your own wrongful acts in most states. Ask a local attorney what your state's law covers.

**Umbrella.** An umbrella policy is extra liability insurance. The Texas Department of Insurance (TDI) says home policies give limited liability coverage, and you can buy a separate umbrella policy for more. The umbrella pays after the base policy reaches its limit.

| Question | LLC | Umbrella policy |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | Keeps rental debts and claims inside the company | Pays covered liability claims above your base limit |
| Who pays a claim | The LLC's assets and its insurance | The insurer, up to the umbrella limit |
| Protects other personal assets | Can, under state law, with exceptions | Yes, up to the policy limit |
| Protects the rental itself | No; the LLC's property can still be reached | Yes, for covered claims |
| Covers claims the policy excludes | Separation still applies | No |
| Paperwork | State filing, annual reports, separate bank account | One policy, renewed each year |

## What Does Each One Cost Per Year?

LLC costs are set by your state. Umbrella costs are set by the insurer and your risk.

| Cost item | Example | Source |
|---|---|---|
| LLC formation, Florida | $125 (filing $100 + registered agent $25) | Florida Division of Corporations |
| LLC annual report, Florida | $138.75 per year | Florida Division of Corporations |
| LLC annual tax, California | $800 per year | California Franchise Tax Board |
| Registered agent service | Varies by vendor | Vendor quote |
| Umbrella premium | Quote only; varies by insurer, limits and risk | Insurer quote |

California's FTB says every LLC doing business or organized in California must pay the $800 annual tax, even with no business, until you cancel the LLC. FTB also charges an extra LLC fee when total income is above $250,000.

We could not find a regulator source for typical umbrella prices. Quote sites give ranges that conflict. Get 2 or 3 quotes. The insurer will tell you the base liability limits it requires under the umbrella.

## What Hidden Costs Does an LLC Bring?

An LLC adds steps beyond the state fee. Plan for these before you file:

- **Your mortgage.** Federal law (12 U.S.C. 1701j-3) lists transfers of 1 to 4 unit homes that a lender can't treat as a sale. A transfer into a living trust where you stay a beneficiary is on the list. A transfer into an LLC is not. A lender may call the loan due if you deed the home to an LLC. Ask first.
- **Insurance in the right name.** The LLC should be the named insured on the property policy. NCDOI says dwelling policies typically do not include liability, so check that liability is in place for the LLC.
- **Separate money.** Use a separate bank account for the LLC. It keeps the records clean and shows the LLC is its own business. Ask an attorney what your state's courts look at.
- **Taxes.** The IRS treats a single-member LLC as a disregarded entity by default, so the rental still goes on your own return. A multi-member LLC is a partnership by default and files its own return.
- **New financing.** Ask each lender whether it lends to an LLC, and on what terms, before you buy the next rental.

## Why Do Many Owners Use Both?

The two tools cover each other's gaps. An umbrella has a limit and exclusions. An LLC does not pay anything; it only limits who owes.

Here is how they work together, with invented numbers:

| Event | Base liability $500,000 only | + Umbrella $1,000,000 | + LLC owns the rental |
|---|---|---|---|
| $300,000 covered claim | Insurer pays $300,000 | Same | Same |
| $1,200,000 covered claim | $700,000 unpaid; your assets at risk | Umbrella pays $700,000 | Umbrella pays; extra exposure stays with the LLC under state law |
| Claim the policies exclude | All unpaid; your assets at risk | Still unpaid | LLC assets at risk; personal assets more separate |

The umbrella must match the owner. If the LLC owns the rental, a personal umbrella may not respond to a claim against the LLC. Ask your insurer to confirm in writing.

## Which Fits One or Two Rentals?

For many small owners, insurance comes first. A claim is paid with money, and only insurance brings new money. An LLC adds yearly cost and steps.

Use this checklist to decide:

- **Start with liability on every rental.** Confirm the limit on the declarations page.
- **Add an umbrella** if your net worth is larger than your base limit.
- **Consider an LLC** when you add more rentals, have large equity, or your state's fees are low.
- **Talk to your lender** before any deed transfer.
- **Talk to an attorney** about your state's LLC law and exceptions.
- **Talk to a tax professional** about state taxes on the LLC.

Neither tool fixes a poorly run rental. Safe steps, fast repairs and clear records cut claims at the source. If you plan to hire a manager, compare costs with the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator). More owner guides are at [/guides](/guides).

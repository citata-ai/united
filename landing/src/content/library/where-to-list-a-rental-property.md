## Which listing sites should a small landlord use?

A small landlord should use 2 free network listings plus 1 local channel. That covers most renters without paying for ads you cannot track. Here is what each site's own page said on 2026-10-19.

| Site | Landlord cost to list | Reach (as the site states it) | Paid option |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zillow Rental Manager | Free | "Over 30 million visitors each month" across its network | Premium listing, one-time fee |
| Apartments.com | Free basic listing | 6 websites across the Apartments.com Network | Premium, price varies by state and market |
| Craigslist | Free in most areas; $5 for apartment rentals in Boston, Chicago, NYC areas | Local, by city | None for housing beyond the $5 fee |
| Avail (syndication) | Free plan: "List your rental on the top 19 sites" | Partners include Zumper, PadMapper, Walk Score, Realtor.com, ApartmentList | Unlimited Plus, $9 per unit |
| TurboTenant (syndication) | Free plan: "List on 25+ sites" | Its partner sites | Paid plans and a $1,500 placement service |

Source: each company's own page, linked below. Reach numbers are the companies' own claims, not our tests.

## Is Zillow Rental Manager free?

Yes. Zillow's "Post a listing" page says you can list your rental property for free. It also offers a premium listing for a one-time fee, with more exposure and market data. The page does not show the price; Zillow shows it at upgrade.

Two more costs to know:

- **Applications:** free for landlords. Zillow's FAQ says renters pay a $40 application fee, and it lets them apply to unlimited participating rentals for 30 days.
- **Rent collection:** Zillow says landlords can collect rent and move-in fees for free.

Watch for: some states cap application fees. Virginia, for example, caps the fee at $50 plus actual screening costs. Check that a site's renter fee fits your state's law.

## What is listing syndication?

Listing syndication sends one listing to many sites at once. You write the ad, add photos and set the rent one time. The tool then posts the same ad to its partner sites and pulls the leads back to one inbox.

Two free examples, from their pricing pages:

1. **Avail** says its free plan lists your rental on "the top 19 sites." Its $9-per-unit Unlimited Plus plan names partners such as Zumper, PadMapper, Walk Score, Realtor.com and ApartmentList.
2. **TurboTenant** says you can "List on 25+ sites and find qualified renters, for free."

Why it helps: the same ad text, rent and photos appear everywhere. That makes fair housing review easier, because you check one ad, not 20.

## Is Apartments.com free for landlords?

Yes, a basic Apartments.com listing is free. Its page says the ad appears on 6 websites across the Apartments.com Network, including ForRent.com and ApartmentFinder.com. A Premium package adds a larger ad, up to 6 videos and more visibility. Apartments.com says Premium pricing varies by state and market.

Apartments.com also offers free rent comparison reports. Use them to check your price before you post.

## Is Craigslist still worth it?

Craigslist is worth a test in a city where renters still search it. It is free for housing in most of the US. Its posting fees page lists one exception: apartment rentals in the Boston, Chicago and NYC areas cost $5.

Watch for: the FTC warns that scammers copy the photos and text of real rental listings, then repost them with their own contact details. Search your address now and then. Put a line in your ad that you never take money by wire, gift card or crypto.

## Is Facebook Marketplace worth it for rentals?

Facebook Marketplace is worth a test only if you track the results. We could not read a Facebook help page on rental listing rules or fees for this guide, so check Facebook's current terms before you post. Judge it by complete applications, not by message count.

What to do:

- Link to your full listing so renters see the criteria first.
- Paste the same ad text you use elsewhere.
- Reply with one template that points to the application.

## Which sites charge landlords to list?

Most listing sites are free for a basic ad and charge for more exposure. From the pages we read:

| Charge type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Free basic listing | Zillow Rental Manager, Apartments.com, Avail free plan, TurboTenant free plan |
| Small flat fee | Craigslist, $5 for apartment rentals in Boston, Chicago, NYC areas |
| One-time premium upgrade | Zillow premium listing |
| Market-based premium | Apartments.com Premium |
| Per-unit subscription | Avail Unlimited Plus, $9 per unit |

## How do I track which site works?

Track leads by source, or you will pay for sites that do nothing. Use this simple log for each vacancy:

1. **Ask every lead "Where did you see the ad?"** Write the answer down.
2. **Count 3 numbers per site:** inquiries, showings, complete applications.
3. **Check at day 14.** Drop any site with zero applications. Keep the ones that send qualified renters.
4. **Note days on market** for each unit, so you can compare the next vacancy.

Idaho changed the numbers on its rent rules in 2025. A 2025 law (chapter 65) moved the rent notice section from § 55-307 to § 55-304 and added § 55-306 on local rent control. Many older guides still cite § 55-307, which is now the section on fixtures. I read the current text on the Idaho Legislature site on 2026-10-20.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| State cap on rent increases | None | § 55-305(4) says the fee rules do not limit rent |
| Local rent, fee or deposit rules | Barred | § 55-306 |
| Residential rent increase notice | At least 30 days, in writing | § 55-304(2)(b) |
| Residential nonrenewal notice | At least 30 days, in writing | § 55-304(2)(a) |
| Change of terms, month to month (general rule) | 15 days before the end of the month | § 55-304(1) |
| End a tenancy at will | At least 1 month, in writing | § 55-208 |
| New or higher fees in a written lease | 30 days' written notice | § 55-305(2) |
| Fees, including late fees | Must be reasonable | § 55-305(1) |

## Does Idaho have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

Idaho has no cap on rent increases and no rent control. No Idaho statute limits the amount of an increase. Idaho Code § 55-305(4) says that "nothing in this section shall be construed to limit the amount that can be charged for rent".

Local governments cannot add rent control. Idaho Code § 55-306, added in 2025, says a local government unit "shall not enact, maintain, or enforce" a rule that would regulate "rent, fees, or deposits charged for leasing private residential property". The same section bars local rules that force owners into an optional federal housing assistance program.

The section keeps one local power. A local government can still manage residential property in which it holds a property interest.

## How many days notice does an Idaho landlord need to raise the rent?

An Idaho landlord needs at least 30 days' written notice before a rent increase takes effect. Idaho Code § 55-304(2) applies to "all leases of residential property". It requires written notice of any increase at least 30 days before the increase is intended to take effect.

Section 55-304 has 2 parts that read differently:

| Subsection | Applies to | Notice |
|---|---|---|
| (1) | Month-to-month leases of land or tenements in general | 15 days before the month ends; new terms bind if the tenant stays |
| (2) | All residential leases | 30 days before a rent increase or nonrenewal |

Subsection (2) starts with "notwithstanding subsection (1)". So for homes and apartments, use 30 days. The 15-day rule still matters for other terms on non-residential month-to-month leases.

Worked example: you want new rent to start on March 1. Count back 30 days. Deliver written notice no later than January 30 in a common year. Many managers send notice 45 days out to cover mail time.

## Can an Idaho landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

An Idaho landlord can raise the rent during a fixed-term lease only if the lease allows it. No Idaho statute gives a landlord the power to change the rent mid-term. The signed lease controls the rent for the term.

If the lease does allow an increase, § 55-304(2) still requires 30 days' written notice for residential property. At the end of the term, a landlord who will not renew must give 30 days' written notice of nonrenewal under the same subsection.

Fees follow a separate rule. Under Idaho Code § 55-305(2), an owner may not charge a fee, fine, interest or other cost that is higher than the written lease allows or not in it. The exception: the owner gives the tenant 30 days' written notice of the change. All fees, including late fees, must be reasonable (§ 55-305(1)). That section applies to agreements made or renewed on or after July 1, 2023.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Idaho?

An Idaho month-to-month tenancy ends with written notice of at least 1 month. Idaho Code § 55-208 covers a tenancy "at will, however created". It lets either side end it:

- **Landlord:** written notice to the tenant to leave within a period of at least 1 month, stated in the notice. Serve it as the code of civil procedure says.
- **Tenant:** written notice of the move-out date, at least 1 month from the notice date.

For residential property, § 55-304(2) also requires at least 30 days' written notice of nonrenewal. Give whichever of the 2 periods is longer.

After the notice period ends, and not before, the landlord may re-enter or go to court to recover possession (Idaho Code § 55-209). Idaho has no just-cause rule for ending a tenancy at will.

## Do cities in Idaho add their own rent or notice rules?

Idaho cities cannot add their own rules on rent, fees or deposits. Idaho Code § 55-306 bars any local ordinance or resolution that would regulate them for private residential rentals.

The 2025 law did not add a general preemption of all landlord-tenant topics. It names rent, fees and deposits, and forced participation in optional federal housing programs. Before you rely on a local rule on another topic, check the city code and ask a local attorney.

## Rent increase checklist for Idaho managers

1. **Confirm the lease type.** Fixed term or month to month.
2. **Read the lease** for any rent change clause.
3. **Count back 30 days** from the date the new rent starts.
4. **Send written notice.** Keep a copy and proof of delivery.
5. **Check fees too.** A new or higher fee needs 30 days' written notice under § 55-305.
6. **Apply the same rule** to every tenant in the same position.

## What does Google allow for service-area businesses?

Google allows a service-area business to hide its address and list the cities or ZIP codes it serves. Google's guidelines define a service-area business as one that visits or delivers to customers but does not serve them at its own address. A property manager who meets owners at their rentals, and has no customer-facing office, fits that definition.

Google names three setups. Pick the one that matches what you do today, not what you plan:

| Your setup | Google type | Address on profile | Service area |
|---|---|---|---|
| Office with signage; staff meet owners there during posted hours | Storefront | Show | Optional |
| Staffed office that owners visit, plus visits to properties across a region | Hybrid | Show | Yes, up to 20 areas |
| Home office or no office that receives customers | Service-area | Hide | Yes, up to 20 areas |
| Mailbox, virtual office, unstaffed coworking desk | Not eligible | No profile at that address | No |

Google's service area help page sets two limits. You can add up to 20 service areas. The whole area should not reach more than about 2 hours of driving time from where the business is based. Google also says you cannot set a radius around your office; you must use cities, postal codes or other named areas.

## Can a home-based property manager show an address?

No, not in most cases. Google's guidelines give a direct example: a plumber who runs the business from a home address should clear the address from the profile. The same rule fits a property manager who works from home and meets owners at their properties.

A home address can stay visible only if it works like a real office. Google says a business that shows an address should keep permanent fixed signage there, and a hybrid location should be staffed and able to receive customers during its stated hours. Few home offices meet that bar.

Hiding the address also protects your privacy. Owners and tenants will not show up at your door, and your profile still lists the areas you serve.

### Home office checklist

1. Clear the address field in the profile editor.
2. Add your service areas: list the cities and ZIP codes where you manage doors today.
3. Keep the business name exactly as it appears on your license and website.
4. Use a local phone number you control, not a call center number.
5. Save proof of the address (license, utility bill) in case Google asks.

## Does a service area change Google Maps rankings?

A service area does not, by itself, make you rank in every city you list. Google's local ranking help page names three main factors: relevance, distance and prominence. Distance means how far the business is from the person who searches. Google still knows where your business is based, even when the address is hidden.

So expect this pattern. You rank best near your base. You rank less in cities farther away, even when they are in your service area. Listing a city tells owners you serve it; it does not move your location to that city.

What helps in a city far from your base:

- **A real owner page for that city** on your website, with the doors you manage there, local rent facts and a local review.
- **Reviews from owners in that city**, written in their own words.
- **Listings that name that city** where it is true, such as directory profiles that show your service area.

Do not try to fix distance with a second fake location. See our separate topic on profiles in more than one city before you add one.

## Can changing the service area setting trigger a suspension?

A change to the address or business type can trigger a review, and a review can lead to a new verification. Google's verification help page says verification methods are chosen by Google and cannot be changed, and some businesses must verify with more than one method. Video verification may ask you to show signage, branded items and proof that you manage the business.

A Local Search Forum thread shows the risk. A manager of a two-location garage door business switched a suspended location to a service-area profile. The switch triggered verification, and the profile was suspended again. That is one user report, not Google data. Still, the safe plan is simple:

1. **Make one change at a time.** Change the address setting, then wait. Change the service areas later.
2. **Keep evidence ready.** Business license, tax certificate, a utility bill in the business name, and photos of any signage.
3. **Keep names and phone numbers stable.** Do not edit the name, phone, address and category in the same week.
4. **Wait 48 hours.** Google's help page says approved service area edits may take up to 48 hours to show.
5. **Do not create a new profile** if the old one goes into review. Google's guidelines allow only one profile per business per location.

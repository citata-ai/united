## What is Section 8 portability?

Portability is a voucher family's right to rent a unit outside the area of the PHA that gave it the voucher. 24 CFR 982.4 defines it as renting "outside the jurisdiction of the initial PHA." 24 CFR 982.353(b) says a family may use its voucher "anywhere in the United States" where a PHA runs a voucher program.

Two PHAs take part:

| Term | Meaning | Your contact? |
|---|---|---|
| Initial PHA | The PHA that issued the voucher, in the city the family leaves | Rarely |
| Receiving PHA | The PHA where your unit is | Yes, for everything |

One limit applies to new families. Under 982.353(c), if neither the head nor spouse lived in the initial PHA's area when they applied, the family has no portability right for its first 12 months. The initial PHA may allow it anyway.

## Which PHA pays me, the initial or the receiving PHA?

The receiving PHA pays you. 24 CFR 982.355(a) says the receiving PHA "must administer assistance for the family." It signs the HAP contract with you and sends the monthly HAP.

Behind the scenes, the receiving PHA pays from one of two sources (982.355(d)-(e)):

1. **Absorption.** The receiving PHA moves the family into its own program and pays from its own funds.
2. **Billing.** The receiving PHA pays you, then bills the initial PHA for the full HAP. The initial PHA must reimburse "promptly."

For you, the difference is mostly invisible. You get one HAP from one PHA either way. Also, 982.355(b) says a receiving PHA "cannot refuse to assist incoming portable families," unless HUD approves in writing.

## Whose payment standard applies?

The receiving PHA's payment standard applies. 24 CFR 982.355(c)(10) says that under portability, the voucher is run "in accordance with the receiving PHA's policies." The receiving PHA also sets the family unit size by its own subsidy standards (982.355(c)(12)). And 982.355(e)(2) says the HAP is figured "in the same manner as for other families in the receiving PHA program."

A worked example, with made-up numbers:

| Item | Initial PHA (family's old city) | Receiving PHA (your city) |
|---|---|---|
| 2-bedroom payment standard | $1,300 | $1,750 |
| Family's Total Tenant Payment | $450 | $450 |
| Your rent plus utility allowance (gross rent) | n/a | $1,700 |
| HAP to you | n/a | $1,250 (gross rent − TTP) |
| Family pays toward rent and utilities | n/a | $450 |

The family's old payment standard does not follow it. Under 24 CFR 982.505(b), the HAP is the lower of (payment standard − TTP) or (gross rent − TTP). Here gross rent is lower, so the HAP is $1,700 − $450 = $1,250.

To run your own numbers, use the [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator). To see payment standard ranges by metro, read [Section 8 rent limits by metro](/guides/section-8-rent-limits).

## Does portability slow the lease-up?

Portability adds steps before the family can submit your unit. 24 CFR 982.355(c) lists them:

1. **The family tells the initial PHA** where it wants to move.
2. **The initial PHA checks** that the family may move.
3. **The initial PHA contacts the receiving PHA** to ask if it will absorb or bill.
4. **The initial PHA sends the file**: form HUD-52665, the latest form HUD-50058 and verification papers.
5. **The family contacts the receiving PHA** and follows its process.
6. **The receiving PHA issues its own voucher.** It may not expire before 30 days after the initial voucher would have expired (982.355(c)(13)).
7. **The family submits a Request for Tenancy Approval** for your unit to the receiving PHA.

Two rules help you. The receiving PHA does not redo eligibility for a current participant (982.355(c)(9)). If it runs a new reexamination, it "may not delay issuing the family a voucher or otherwise delay approval of a unit" (982.355(c)(11)).

After step 7, the lease-up is normal. The receiving PHA inspects and approves under 24 CFR 982.305.

## Can the tenant port out of my unit mid-lease?

A voucher tenant cannot port out freely in the middle of your lease. 24 CFR 982.354(b) lists when a family may move with its voucher:

- The lease has ended, including by mutual agreement with you.
- You gave a notice to vacate, started an eviction, or got a judgment.
- The tenant gave a lawful notice to end the lease.
- A family member is a victim of domestic violence, dating violence, sexual assault or stalking, and the move protects their safety.

The PHA may also limit moves. Under 982.354(c)(2), it may bar a move during the initial lease term, and bar more than one move a year. The safety exception still applies.

And a broken lease has a cost. 982.353(b) says the initial PHA must not give portable assistance to a family that "moved out of the assisted unit in violation of the lease," except for the safety case above. The family must also notify you and the PHA before it moves (982.354(d)).

## What paperwork does the landlord see?

The landlord sees almost the same paperwork as in any voucher lease-up. The forms between PHAs stay between PHAs.

- [ ] The family's voucher from the receiving PHA.
- [ ] A Request for Tenancy Approval, submitted to the receiving PHA.
- [ ] The receiving PHA's inspection notice and result.
- [ ] Your lease with the HUD tenancy addendum.
- [ ] A HAP contract signed with the receiving PHA.
- [ ] The receiving PHA's payment setup forms.

Watch for: make sure every form names the receiving PHA. If the family hands you papers from its old PHA, call the receiving PHA before you sign.

Tennessee puts most of its deposit rules in one section: Tenn. Code 66-28-301. That section is part of the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA). The URLTA applies "only in counties having a population of more than seventy-five thousand (75,000), according to the 2010 federal census" (66-28-102(a)). So check your county before you use the rules below.

## What is the maximum security deposit in Tennessee?

Tennessee sets no maximum security deposit. Tenn. Code 66-28-301 says how you hold, inspect and refund a deposit, but it names no cap. The lease and the market set the amount.

## How many days does a Tennessee landlord have to return a deposit?

Tenn. Code 66-28-301 sets no fixed refund deadline in days. Instead, it uses a notice rule. If the tenant leaves owing no rent and is due a refund, you must send a notice of the refund amount to the tenant's last known or reasonably determinable address (66-28-301(f)).

If the tenant does not respond within 60 days of that notice, you may remove the deposit from the account. You then keep it "free from any claim of the tenant" (66-28-301(f)).

If the tenant leaves owing rent or other amounts, you may take money from the deposit account to pay that debt (66-28-301(e)).

## What can a Tennessee landlord deduct from a deposit?

A Tennessee landlord can deduct unpaid rent, other amounts due and the cost of physical damage. Tenn. Code 66-28-301(e) covers "unpaid rent or other amounts due and owing". 66-28-301(b) covers "physical damages" and your estimated cost to repair them.

Here is a worked example for a $1,500 deposit:

| Line | Amount | Basis in 66-28-301 |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit held | $1,500 | (a) separate account |
| Unpaid rent, last half month | −$600 | (e) unpaid rent |
| Broken door, estimated repair on the signed list | −$250 | (b) damage list |
| Stain found 10 days after move-out, before a new tenant | −$150 | (g) later damage, within 30 days |
| Refund due to the tenant | $500 | (f) send notice of the refund |

You may also recover "contractual damages" beyond the deposit (66-28-301(g)). Keep repair invoices and photos for each line. A tenant can dispute items in court, as the next section explains.

## Does a Tennessee landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. Tenn. Code 66-28-301 builds the deposit process around a "comprehensive listing" of damage and the estimated repair cost. If you do not keep a separate account and do not give a damage list, you lose the right to keep any part of the deposit (66-28-301(c)).

Follow these steps at move-out:

1. **Offer the inspection.** When you ask the tenant to leave, or within 5 days after the tenant's written notice to vacate, you may tell the tenant of the right to be present.
2. **Set the date.** Hold the inspection on move-out day or within 4 calendar days after it.
3. **Make the list together.** List each damage item and its estimated cost. You and the tenant both sign. The tenant may refuse and must then name the disputed items in writing.
4. **Handle a no-show.** If the tenant schedules the inspection and does not attend, the tenant waives the right to contest. This works only if your lease states the waiver.
5. **Inspect alone when allowed.** You may skip the joint inspection if the tenant left without written notice, abandoned the unit, was judicially removed, or did not respond. If the tenant asks in writing, send the list by certificate of mailing.
6. **Send the refund notice.** Then start the 60-day clock in 66-28-301(f).

A signed list is "conclusive evidence" of its accuracy, except for damage you find later under 66-28-301(g). Damage found later counts only if you find it before the earlier of 30 days after move-out or 7 days after a new tenant moves in.

## Must a Tennessee landlord pay interest or keep the deposit separate?

A Tennessee landlord in a URLTA county must keep the deposit separate. Tenn. Code 66-28-301(a) requires "an account used only for that purpose" at a bank or other lender regulated by the state or a federal agency.

At lease signing, you must tell the tenant where the account is. You do not have to give the account number (66-28-301(h)).

Tenn. Code 66-28-301 has no rule about interest. It neither requires interest nor bars it. Check your lease and any program contract that may add a rule.

## What is the penalty for keeping a Tennessee deposit wrongly?

The main penalty is loss of the deposit. Under Tenn. Code 66-28-301(c), you may not keep any part of the deposit if you skipped the separate account and the damage list.

A tenant who disputes the final damage list may sue in a circuit or general sessions court (66-28-301(d)). The claim is limited to the items the tenant disputed in writing. And if you lock out a tenant or cut essential services and the lease ends, you must return all prepaid rent and deposits, plus pay damages and fees (66-28-504).

Use this checklist to stay safe:

- [ ] Separate deposit account at a regulated bank.
- [ ] Account location in the lease or a signed notice.
- [ ] Inspection waiver language in the lease.
- [ ] Signed damage list with estimated costs.
- [ ] Refund notice sent to the last known address, with the date.
- [ ] Calendar reminder at 60 days.

## What does rent-ready mean?

A rent-ready unit needs no more work before a tenant moves in. It is clean, safe, fully working and documented. If a crew must come back after move-in, the unit was not rent-ready.

The problem is that "ready" means different things to different people. A cleaner thinks the job is done when the floors shine. A tenant thinks the job is done when the dishwasher drains. Write one standard and use it for every turnover.

The checklist below has five parts. They follow the order a turnover crew works: clean, make safe, test systems, fix surfaces, then inspect.

## What is the cleaning standard for a rental?

The cleaning standard is simple: no dust, no grease, no hair, no smell, and nothing left by the last tenant. Clean inside every cabinet, drawer and appliance, not only the surfaces a tenant sees first.

| Area | Clean to this standard |
|---|---|
| Kitchen | Inside oven, range hood filter, fridge and freezer, cabinets, drawers, sink drain |
| Bathrooms | Toilet base and behind it, tub and grout, exhaust fan cover, mirror, under the sink |
| Floors | Vacuum and mop; clean baseboards; clean carpet if it stays |
| Windows | Glass, tracks, sills, screens |
| Walls and doors | Spot clean marks, switch plates, door handles |
| Other | Light fixtures free of bugs, vents dusted, no items left in closets or garage |

**Watch for:** smells. Pet, smoke and mold smells end showings fast. Find the source. Do not only cover it.

## Which safety items must a rental have before move-in?

Every rental needs working smoke alarms, carbon monoxide (CO) alarms where fuel-burning appliances or an attached garage exist, safe electrical outlets, and secure locks. The exact list depends on your state and city code. Check it each year.

Federal standards give a good baseline. For HUD housing, the NSPIRE rule at 24 CFR 5.703 asks for:

- At least one working smoke detector on each level.
- GFCI (ground-fault) protection on outlets within 6 feet of a water source.
- A guardrail on any walking surface with a drop of 30 inches or more.
- Fixed light fixtures in the kitchen and each bathroom.
- No unvented space heaters that burn gas, oil or kerosene.

The U.S. Fire Administration says to test each smoke alarm monthly and replace the whole alarm 10 years from its manufacture date. Check the date on the back of each alarm at every turnover.

The CPSC advises CO alarms on every level and outside sleeping areas. It says more than 200 people in the U.S. die each year from accidental non-fire CO poisoning linked to consumer products.

**Lead paint:** for most housing built before 1978, the EPA Lead-based Paint Disclosure Rule requires you to share known lead paint information before the tenant signs. Some housing is exempt, such as leases of 100 days or less. Check the EPA page for the full list.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Which systems and appliances should I test?

Test every system and appliance by running it, not by looking at it. A tenant will find a broken item in the first week. You want to find it first.

Use this checklist and write the result next to each line:

- [ ] Heat runs and reaches the set temperature. Filter is new.
- [ ] Air conditioning runs and cools (if present).
- [ ] Water heater gives hot water at each tap within a normal time.
- [ ] All faucets run with no drip. All drains clear fast.
- [ ] Toilets flush and refill with no running.
- [ ] Every outlet works. GFCI outlets trip and reset.
- [ ] Every light works. Replace dead bulbs.
- [ ] Fridge cools. Freezer freezes.
- [ ] Range burners and oven heat.
- [ ] Dishwasher fills, washes and drains.
- [ ] Washer and dryer run (if provided). Dryer vent is clean.
- [ ] Windows open, close and lock. Screens fit.
- [ ] Doors close and latch. Locks work. Keys work.
- [ ] Garage door opener works and its safety reverse works.

**Rekey the locks** at each turnover. You do not know who has copies of the old keys.

## What standard should paint and flooring meet?

Paint and flooring meet the standard when they have no holes, stains, scuffs, burns, tears or loose pieces. A tenant should not be able to tell where the last tenant's furniture stood.

| Surface | Pass | Fix or replace |
|---|---|---|
| Walls | Even color, touch-ups match | Holes, marks that do not wash off, patchy touch-ups |
| Trim and doors | Clean, no chips | Chips, dents, peeling |
| Carpet | Clean, no odor, seams flat | Stains, burns, odor, ripples |
| Hard floors | No lifting planks, no deep scratches | Loose planks, water damage, cracks |

In a pre-1978 home, paint work that disturbs old paint is covered by the EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) rule. If you pay someone for that work, the firm must be certified. Plan for this before you sand or scrape.

## How do I run the final walk-through?

The final walk-through is the last check before you hand over keys. A person who did not do the turnover work should do it. Fresh eyes find the gaps.

1. **Walk in the tenant's order.** Front door, living area, kitchen, bedrooms, bathrooms, outside.
2. **Use the same checklist** as the crew, and mark each line pass or fail.
3. **Test, do not look.** Run a tap in each room. Flip each switch. Press each alarm test button.
4. **Take dated photos** of every room, every wall, the floors, and the inside of each appliance.
5. **Fix every fail** before move-in, then check those lines again.
6. **Give the tenant a move-in condition form** to sign. Keep a copy with your photos.

The photos matter later. If the tenant disputes a deposit deduction, your move-in photos show the start condition. In California, Civil Code 1950.5(g) now requires photos of the unit at the start of tenancies that begin on or after July 1, 2025. Other states may not require them, but photos help you in every state.

## What you can do next

Print the checklists above. Use them on your next turnover and time each step. After 3 turnovers, you will know how many days a normal turnover takes, and you can plan showings around it.

## Which documents do you collect from a new owner?

Collect the documents that prove ownership, set the rules and let you act. Without them, you cannot collect rent, pay bills or file the year-end 1099. Ask for all of them in one request, with a due date.

Phase 1 checklist:

- [ ] **Signed management agreement.** Both sides sign before you touch money or tenants.
- [ ] **Form W-9.** The IRS says Form W-9 gives your correct taxpayer ID to a person who must file an information return.
- [ ] **Proof of ownership.** Deed or title record, and the legal owner name (person, LLC or trust).
- [ ] **Insurance.** Landlord policy, agent and the request to add your company as an additional insured.
- [ ] **Bank details for the owner draw.** ACH form, signed.
- [ ] **Current leases and addenda.** One file per unit.
- [ ] **Tenant ledgers and the deposit list.** Balance and deposit amount for each tenant.
- [ ] **Keys, fobs, garage and gate codes.** Count them and log them.
- [ ] **HOA contacts and rules.** Dues, due dates and the board contact.
- [ ] **Utility accounts.** Which are in the owner's name and which are in the tenant's name.
- [ ] **Warranties and service contracts.** Roof, HVAC, appliances, home warranty.
- [ ] **Lead paint records (built before 1978).** The EPA says landlords must disclose known lead-based paint information and give renters the EPA pamphlet. The EPA also says property managers share this duty.
- [ ] **Permits and rental licenses.** City registration, if the city has one.

## How do you set up the owner in accounting and the trust account?

Set up the owner, the property and the money rules in your software before the first rent payment arrives. The setup follows the agreement, line by line.

Phase 2 checklist:

- [ ] **Owner record.** Legal name, tax ID from the W-9, ownership share if there are 2 or more owners.
- [ ] **Property and units.** Address, unit numbers, bedrooms, year built.
- [ ] **Fee settings.** Management fee basis (rent collected or rent due), leasing and renewal fees, as the agreement states.
- [ ] **Reserve.** Amount and purpose. Arizona's ARS 32-2173 says the agreement must state the amount and purpose of any operating reserve.
- [ ] **Spend limit.** The repair amount you may approve without the owner.
- [ ] **Trust account mapping.** Rent and deposits go to the trust account, not the operating account.
- [ ] **Draw schedule.** Day of the month and the method (ACH or check).
- [ ] **Owner portal invite.** Send it after the setup is complete, not before.

Deposit timing is a legal rule in some states. In California, 10 CCR 2832 gives a broker 3 business days after receipt to place trust funds. The funds go to the owner, a neutral escrow or a trust account. Check your own state's rule.

## What do you check at the first property inspection?

The first inspection sets the baseline for every later repair, claim and deposit decision. Take dated photos of every room and every system. Store them with the property record.

Phase 3 checklist:

- [ ] **Exterior.** Roof, gutters, siding, windows, walkways, fences.
- [ ] **Systems.** HVAC age and filter, water heater age, electrical panel, plumbing leaks.
- [ ] **Safety.** Smoke and CO alarms, handrails, locks, fire extinguisher where required.
- [ ] **Each room.** Walls, floors, doors, fixtures, appliances.
- [ ] **Meter readings.** Water, gas, electric, if vacant.
- [ ] **Repair list.** Urgent, soon and later, with estimates for the owner.

If the unit is occupied, give the tenant notice of entry under your state's law and the lease.

## How do you move tenants and deposits from the old manager?

Ask the old manager for a written handover: tenant list, ledgers, deposits, keys and open work orders. Then tell each tenant how to pay rent and who to call.

Phase 4 checklist:

- [ ] **Handover request in writing.** Send it to the old manager with a date.
- [ ] **Security deposits.** Amount by tenant, and the funds themselves. In California, Civil Code 1950.5 lets the landlord transfer the remaining deposit to a successor, or return it to the tenant with an accounting.
- [ ] **Ledger balances.** Prepaid rent, unpaid rent and credits by tenant.
- [ ] **Open work orders.** Vendor, status and any money already paid.
- [ ] **Tenant letter.** New rent payment method, maintenance contact and office hours.
- [ ] **Disclosure deadline.** California Civil Code 1962 says a successor owner or manager must comply with its disclosure rules within 15 days.
- [ ] **Vendor list.** Keep, test or replace each vendor.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What should the owner get in the first 30 days?

The owner should get 4 things in 30 days: a welcome message, the inspection report, a repair plan and the first statement. Each one shows that you did what the agreement promised.

| Day | What the owner gets | Owner action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Welcome letter, contacts, document list | Send missing documents |
| 3–7 | Owner portal access | Check bank and tax details |
| 7–14 | Inspection report with photos | Approve or decline repairs |
| 14–21 | Tenant handover summary and deposit list | Confirm the numbers |
| 30 | First owner statement and draw | Read and reply with questions |

Arizona's ARS 32-2173 says the agreement must state the type and frequency of status reports. So the 30-day plan should match the reports your agreement promises.

## Which onboarding fees should you tell the owner about?

Tell the owner about every setup cost before the work starts. In a sample of 722 branches, iPropertyManagement found an average setup fee of $185.24 per contract (updated 2026-09-23). If you charge one, put it in the agreement and on the first statement.

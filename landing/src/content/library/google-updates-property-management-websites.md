Google listed 7 ranking updates on its Search Status Dashboard in 2026 up to September: 2 core updates, 4 spam updates and 1 Discover update. Each one sets off a wave of agency posts. Some of them blame the wrong update. This guide gives you the dates, a simple check, and what to do when a drop is real.

Why it matters: in the Goodjuju Marketing fall 2026 study of 35 property management companies, Google Search and the Map Pack gave 50.6% of owner leads. A real ranking loss costs owner calls.

## Which Google updates hit property management sites recently?

Google core updates hit all sites, not property management sites in particular. Google says it makes "significant, broad changes" several times a year and calls them core updates. Pages that move down "aren't necessarily bad"; other pages simply rank higher.

These are the ranking updates on Google's Search Status Dashboard, read on 2026-10-20:

| Update | Start | Rollout time |
|---|---|---|
| September 2026 spam update | 2026-09-24 | 13 days, 16 hours |
| August 2026 spam update | 2026-08-18 | 2 days, 16 hours |
| June 2026 spam update | 2026-06-24 | 2 days, 1 hour |
| May 2026 core update | 2026-05-21 | 11 days, 21 hours |
| March 2026 core update | 2026-03-27 | 12 days, 4 hours |
| March 2026 spam update | 2026-03-24 | 19 hours, 30 minutes |
| February 2026 Discover update | 2026-02-05 | 21 days, 17 hours |
| December 2025 core update | 2025-12-11 | 18 days, 2 hours |
| June 2025 core update | 2025-06-30 | 16 days, 18 hours |
| March 2025 core update | 2025-03-13 | 13 days, 21 hours |

Note the February 2026 entry. Property Manager Websites wrote that the February 2026 update "officially targets Google Discover, not traditional Search," and that the SEO community still saw Search shifts. Google itself listed no Search core update in February. If your drop started in February, check other causes first: a site change, a lost link, or a tracking error.

No public dataset lists which property management sites lost traffic in each update. Agency posts describe their own clients, without full data. We do not have our own update-by-update numbers either.

## How do I check if an update hit my site?

Use Google Search Console and follow Google's own steps from "Google Search's core updates and your website":

1. **Wait.** Wait at least a full week after the update completes. The dashboard shows the end date.
2. **Compare the right dates.** Compare that week with a week before the rollout started.
3. **Check top pages and queries.** Find out if the drop is small or large.
4. **Split search types.** Look at Web, Images and Video one at a time.

Google gives the scale itself:

| Drop | Google's example | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Small | Position 2 → 4 | No drastic action; do not change pages that still perform well |
| Large | Position 4 → 29 | A deeper review of the site as a whole |

### Worked example

Your "property management [city]" page sat at an average position of 3.1 before the May 2026 core update (May 14–20). The update ended about June 2. You compare June 3–9 with May 14–20.

- Position 3.1 → 4.0: small. Leave the page alone.
- Position 3.1 → 18.5, and 6 other owner pages fell the same way: large and site-wide. Start the review below.

Also check your lead data, not only rank. A 10% fall in clicks in summer can be seasonal. Compare the same weeks last year.

## What content does Google reward?

Google rewards "helpful, reliable, people-first content." Its guide "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content" was last updated on 2026-10-05. Goodjuju Marketing wrote that this update asks raters to judge effort, originality, skill and accuracy in a page's main content.

The guide gives clear warning signs. A property management site fails these when it:

- writes posts mainly to attract search visits, not to help owners;
- writes to a word count ("Google has a preferred word count? No, we don't.");
- publishes many pages on topics outside its real work;
- hides who wrote the page and how.

And it rewards content that shows first-hand expertise. Google says E-E-A-T (experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trust) "isn't a specific ranking factor," but its systems look for signals of it. They give more weight to it for "Your Money or Your Life" topics, which include financial stability. Rental income and property decisions fit there.

For a property manager, first-hand content looks like this:

- your own fee table, in full;
- your own vacancy and days-on-market numbers;
- local rules with the statute linked;
- a named author with a license number and real experience.

## What should I do after a drop?

After a large, site-wide drop, review the site as a whole, fix the weak pages, and wait. Google says some changes take effect in a few days, but "it could take several months" for its systems to confirm the site is helpful. If nothing changes after a few months, the next core update may be the point where it shows.

Recovery checklist:

- [ ] Confirm the dates match a listed update, not a site change
- [ ] List pages with large drops, by owner-lead value
- [ ] Remove or merge thin pages written for keywords only
- [ ] Add first-hand data and a named author to key owner pages
- [ ] Check fee, service area and contact facts match your Google Business Profile
- [ ] Leave pages that still rank well alone
- [ ] Recheck in Search Console monthly; note the next core update date

Google also says there is "no guarantee" any change gives a visible result. Positions move all the time, up and down. Watch owner leads and signed doors, not rank alone. If leads fell while rank held, read [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping) for other causes.

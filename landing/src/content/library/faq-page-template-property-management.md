## Which questions do owners ask AI most?

Owners ask AI the same questions they ask on a sales call. The top ones are about money, risk and control. What does it cost? What if the unit is empty? Can I leave? Who picks the tenant? Who approves a $900 repair?

ClearLead Digital, a property management marketing agency, lists questions like these in its AI search guide: "what does a property manager do", "average property management fees" and "is a property manager worth it". It also names flat fee versus percentage, and "Do you charge during vacancy?" ClearLead's main advice is to build FAQs from your own inbox and call notes. We agree. Your owners already told you what they want to know.

Group the questions so an owner can scan them. Five groups cover most owner questions:

| Group | What the owner wants to know |
|---|---|
| Fees | Monthly fee, leasing fee, renewal fee, vacancy charge, hidden fees |
| Contract | Term, cancel notice, cancel fee, what happens to the tenant |
| Tenants | Screening rules, who decides, evictions, guarantees |
| Money and repairs | Payout date, reserve, repair approval limit, reports |
| Fit | Service area, property types, door count, how to start |

## What is the FAQ page template?

The FAQ page template below has 20 questions in 5 groups. Copy it, delete the questions that do not apply, and fill in your own facts. Every blank needs a real number or a real rule from your management agreement.

```text
PAGE TITLE (H1): Owner FAQ: <Company name>

## Fees
1. What is your monthly management fee?
   "<Company> charges __% of collected rent (or $__ per month). We charge
   nothing in a month when no rent is collected."
2. Do you charge a leasing fee to place a new tenant?
3. Do you charge a lease renewal fee?
4. Do you charge anything while the property is vacant?
5. Are there any other fees? (List every one: setup, inspection, eviction,
   maintenance markup.)

## Contract
6. How long is the management agreement?
7. How do I cancel, and is there a cancel fee?
8. What happens to my tenant if I cancel?

## Tenants
9. How do you screen tenants? (Name the checks and the written criteria.)
10. Who makes the final approval on a tenant?
11. Do you offer a lease or eviction guarantee? What are the terms?
12. How do you handle evictions, and what do they cost me?

## Money and repairs
13. When do I get my rent each month?
14. Do you hold a reserve? How much?
15. Up to what amount do you approve repairs without asking me?
16. Do you add a fee on top of vendor invoices?
17. What reports do I get, and how often?

## Fit
18. Which cities and ZIP codes do you serve?
19. Which property types do you manage? How many doors today?
20. How do I start, and how long does onboarding take?
```

Keep tenant screening answers neutral. Describe the same written criteria for every applicant. Do not describe the kind of tenant you prefer.

## How long should each FAQ answer be?

Each FAQ answer should open with one direct sentence that answers the question. Then add 1 to 3 sentences of detail: the number, the condition, the exception. That gives about 40 to 80 words.

ClearLead Digital reports that AI systems lift 40–80 word passages that answer a question directly. It also suggests a 40–60 word answer block under the H1 of key pages. Treat these numbers as a working range from one agency, not a fixed rule.

Here is one answer, weak and strong:

| Version | Answer |
|---|---|
| Weak | "Our fees are competitive and transparent. Contact us for a custom quote tailored to your property." |
| Strong | "We charge 8% of collected rent, with a $100 monthly minimum. We charge nothing in a month with no rent collected. Leasing is 50% of one month's rent. Renewal is $150. We add no markup to vendor invoices." |

The weak answer gives AI nothing to quote. The strong answer has 5 facts an owner can compare. Use these checks on each answer:

- [ ] First sentence answers the question alone.
- [ ] At least 1 number, date or named rule.
- [ ] Same number as your fee page, agreement and listings.
- [ ] Repeats the noun ("the leasing fee"), not "it".
- [ ] No "contact us for details" as the whole answer.

## Should the FAQ use schema?

FAQ schema is optional now, and it no longer changes how Google shows your page. Google stopped showing the FAQ rich result in Search on May 7, 2026. It removed the FAQ rich result documentation in June 2026.

Google's guide to AI features also says there is no special schema you need to add for AI Overviews or AI Mode. The Ahrefs schema study, published 2026-05-11, found no clear AI citation lift when 1,885 pages added JSON-LD.

If you already have FAQPage markup, you can keep it. Make sure it matches the visible text word for word. Google lists "making sure your structured data matches the visible text on the page" as a best practice. Do not spend new budget on FAQ markup. Spend it on better answers.

## One FAQ page or one per service?

Use one main FAQ page plus short FAQ blocks on service and city pages. Each question should live in one place only, with one answer.

| Question type | Where it goes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Company-wide | Main FAQ page | "How do I cancel?" |
| One service | That service page | "Do you manage HOAs?" on the HOA page |
| One city | That city page | "Do you serve Oak Park?" on the Oak Park page |
| Fee detail | Fee page, linked from FAQ | "What is the leasing fee?" |

Do not copy the same 20 questions onto 15 city pages. Duplicate blocks add little for readers. Instead, add 3 to 6 questions that are true only for that city or service, such as local permit rules or local rent ranges.

## Can a partial payment waive a pay-or-quit notice?

A partial payment can waive a pay-or-quit notice in many states. Arizona (A.R.S. 33-1371(C)) and Florida (83.56(5)(a)) both state the base rule: if you accept rent while you know the tenant is in default, you give up the right to end the lease for that default. States then add their own exceptions for partial rent. The 3 states below show how much the details differ.

| State | Must you accept partial rent? | When does acceptance keep your right to evict? | Statute |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arizona | No | Only if the tenant signs a contemporaneous written agreement with a due date for the balance | A.R.S. 33-1371 |
| Oregon | No | Before the notice: the tenant agreed to pay the balance by a set time, and you serve the notice no earlier than allowed. After the notice: a written agreement says acceptance is not a waiver | ORS 90.417 |
| Florida | Not stated as a duty | Acceptance of partial rent alone is not a waiver. After a posted notice, you must give a receipt, deposit the money with the court, or post a new 3-day notice | Fla. Stat. 83.56(5) |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

### Arizona

Arizona law says plainly: "A landlord is not required to accept a partial payment of rent or other charges." If you do accept one, you keep the right to proceed against the tenant only if the tenant agrees "in a contemporaneous writing" to the terms. The writing must include the date the balance is due. If you already served the notice for unpaid rent, you do not need a new notice when the tenant breaks the plan.

### Oregon

Oregon ORS 90.417 says a landlord may refuse a rent tender that is less than the full amount or late. A landlord may also accept a partial payment. The waiver rules then depend on timing. If you accept part of the rent after you serve the termination notice, you need a written agreement that the acceptance is not a waiver. That agreement may let you go to court without a new notice if the tenant misses the balance date.

### Florida

Florida Statute 83.56(5) says a landlord "does not waive the right to terminate" by accepting partial rent for the period. But if you accept partial rent after you post the nonpayment notice, you must do one of 3 things:

1. Give the tenant a receipt with the date, the amount, the agreed date and the balance, before you file.
2. Put the partial rent into the court registry when you file for possession.
3. Post a new 3-day notice with the new amount due.

## What is a partial payment agreement?

A partial payment agreement is a short signed document that turns a loose promise into a clear plan. It protects both sides. The tenant knows the exact balance and date. You keep your right to proceed if the plan fails, where state law allows it.

Checklist for a partial payment agreement:

- [ ] Tenant name, unit address and lease date
- [ ] Rent period the payment covers
- [ ] Amount paid today and how it was paid
- [ ] Balance still owed, split into rent and other charges
- [ ] Exact due date for the balance (one date, or a short schedule)
- [ ] Statement that the payment does not waive any notice already served
- [ ] What happens if the tenant misses the date
- [ ] Signatures and date for both sides, signed the same day as the payment

Watch for: Oregon ORS 90.417(3) treats a written plan to pay monthly rent in regular installments as an installment schedule, not partial rent. Label the document clearly so no one confuses the two.

## How do I apply partial payments to fees vs rent?

Apply partial payments in the order your lease states, and keep the order the same for every tenant. If the lease is silent, one clear choice is to apply money to the oldest rent first, then fees. That order keeps the rent balance, which is the basis of a nonpayment case, as small and as clear as possible.

Worked example. Rent is $1,500. The tenant also owes a $75 late fee. The tenant pays $1,000.

| Order | Rent balance after | Fee balance after | Notice amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rent first | $500 | $75 | $500 rent (plus fee, if your state allows fees in the notice) |
| Fee first | $575 | $0 | $575 rent |

The rent-first order gives a smaller and cleaner rent balance. Some states limit which charges a nonpayment notice may include, so check your state's notice rule before you put fees in it.

Housing vouchers need care. Arizona A.R.S. 33-1371(B) says a landlord's acceptance of a housing assistance payment is not acceptance of partial rent. Florida's partial-rent subsection does not apply to the subsidy part of the rent. For voucher units, track the tenant share and the housing authority share on separate lines. ## Can I refuse partial rent?

You can refuse partial rent in Arizona and Oregon, because their statutes say so. In other states, the answer depends on the statute and on your lease. Refusal is a business choice as much as a legal one.

| Accept partial rent when | Refuse partial rent when |
|---|---|
| The tenant has a good history and a short, clear problem | The tenant has missed several months |
| The tenant signs a written plan with a near due date | The tenant will not sign anything |
| Your state has a clear rule that keeps your notice valid | You have filed, and your state's rule after filing is unclear |
| Court time and turnover cost more than the gap | The balance grows each month |

Steps when a partial payment arrives:

1. **Check the timing.** Has the notice been served? Has the case been filed?
2. **Check your state's rule.** Find the statute that covers partial rent and waiver.
3. **Decide.** Accept with the right paperwork, or return the payment promptly.
4. **Document.** Give a receipt, sign the plan, and update the ledger the same day.
5. **Follow up.** Put the balance due date in your calendar. Act on the date.

Do not leave a payment in limbo. Decide quickly, then act, so your records show a clear choice.

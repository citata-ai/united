## Texas security deposit rules at a glance

Texas security deposit law is in Property Code Chapter 92, Subchapter C, Sections 92.101 to 92.111. Section 92.101 applies the subchapter to all residential leases. The table sums up the rules.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | No cap in the statute | Prop. Code 92.101–92.111 |
| Refund deadline | On or before the 30th day after surrender | Prop. Code 92.103(a) |
| Forwarding address | No duty to refund or itemize until the tenant gives one in writing | Prop. Code 92.107 |
| Advance notice of move-out | Binding only if underlined or in bold in the lease | Prop. Code 92.103(b) |
| Deductions | Damages and charges the tenant is legally liable for | Prop. Code 92.104(a) |
| Normal wear and tear | Cannot be deducted | Prop. Code 92.104(b) |
| Itemized list | Required for any deduction, with one rent exception | Prop. Code 92.104(c) |
| Records | Keep accurate records of all deposits | Prop. Code 92.106 |
| Bad faith penalty | $100 + 3 times the amount wrongly kept + attorney's fees | Prop. Code 92.109(a) |
| Missed 30 days | Presumed bad faith | Prop. Code 92.109(d) |
| Tenant withholds last month's rent | 3 times the rent held back + attorney's fees | Prop. Code 92.108 |

## What is the maximum security deposit in Texas?

Texas sets no maximum security deposit. Subchapter C defines a deposit and sets the refund rules, but it names no cap. So the lease sets the amount.

Section 92.102 defines a security deposit as any advance of money meant mainly to secure performance under a lease. It excludes a rental application deposit and an advance payment of rent. That matters at move-out. Prepaid rent is not deposit money, and the deposit rules do not apply to it.

A fee in lieu of a deposit is allowed under 92.111. If you offer one, you must also offer a normal deposit. You may not use the tenant's choice to approve or deny the application.

## How many days does a Texas landlord have to return a deposit?

A Texas landlord has 30 days. Property Code 92.103(a) says you must refund the deposit "on or before the 30th day after the date the tenant surrenders the premises."

Two rules change the clock:

- **Forwarding address.** Under 92.107(a), you need not refund or itemize until the tenant gives you a written forwarding address. The tenant does not lose the deposit just by not giving one (92.107(b)).
- **Advance notice of move-out.** A lease can make the refund depend on advance notice. That term works only if it is underlined or in bold in the lease (92.103(b)).

The 30 days run from surrender. Mark that date in your files.

## What can a Texas landlord deduct from a deposit?

A Texas landlord may deduct "damages and charges for which the tenant is legally liable under the lease or as a result of breaching the lease" (92.104(a)). You may not keep any part for normal wear and tear (92.104(b)).

Worked example. The deposit is $1,800. The tenant gave a forwarding address and owes no rent.

| Item | Type | Deducted? | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hole in a bedroom wall, patched and painted | Damage | Yes | $250 |
| Broken blind slats, 2 windows | Damage | Yes | $90 |
| Carpet worn in walk paths after 4 years | Normal wear and tear | No | $0 |
| Faded paint from sunlight | Normal wear and tear | No | $0 |
| Unpaid water bill the lease makes the tenant pay | Lease charge | Yes | $60 |
| **Total deductions** | | | **$400** |
| **Refund due by day 30** | | | **$1,400** |

The statute does not list which items count as normal wear and tear. The split in the table is an example, so judge each item on its facts. Keep photos, invoices and the move-in report for each line. Under 92.109(c), the landlord has the burden to prove that each amount kept was reasonable.

## Does a Texas landlord have to itemize deductions?

Yes, in most cases. Under 92.104(c), you must send the balance with "a written description and itemized list of all deductions."

There is one exception. You need not itemize if the tenant owes rent at move-out and there is no dispute about the amount of rent owed. If you deduct anything else, itemize it.

Use this move-out checklist:

- [ ] Record the surrender date.
- [ ] Ask for the tenant's forwarding address in writing.
- [ ] Walk the unit and take dated photos.
- [ ] Split each item into damage or normal wear and tear.
- [ ] Get an invoice or a quote for each deduction.
- [ ] Send the refund and the itemized list on or before day 30.
- [ ] Keep a copy of the list and the proof of mailing.

## Must a Texas landlord pay interest or keep a separate account?

No. Subchapter C has no interest rule and no separate-account rule. Section 92.106 has one record rule: "The landlord shall keep accurate records of all security deposits."

The deposit still has protection. Under 92.103(c), the tenant's claim to the deposit comes before any creditor of the landlord, including a bankruptcy trustee.

When you sell, the deposit moves with the building. Under 92.105, the new owner is liable for deposits from the date of title. The new owner must give each tenant a signed statement with the exact deposit amount. The seller stays liable until the new owner receives the deposit or takes on the liability, unless a written contract says otherwise.

## What is the penalty for wrongly keeping a deposit in Texas?

The penalty depends on bad faith. A landlord who keeps a deposit in bad faith owes 3 amounts (92.109(a)):

1. **$100.**
2. **3 times the part of the deposit wrongly kept.**
3. **The tenant's reasonable attorney's fees.**

A landlord who in bad faith does not send an itemized list loses more. That landlord forfeits the right to keep any part of the deposit or to sue the tenant for damage to the unit (92.109(b)).

Missing day 30 is costly. If you neither return the deposit nor send the itemized list by then, the law presumes bad faith (92.109(d)).

Example: you keep $600 of a deposit with no good reason. A court could award $100 + 3 × $600 = $1,900, plus attorney's fees.

The rule cuts both ways. A tenant may not skip the last month's rent and tell you to use the deposit (92.108). A tenant who does is presumed to act in bad faith. That tenant can owe 3 times the rent held back plus your attorney's fees.

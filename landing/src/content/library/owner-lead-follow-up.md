## How fast should the first reply be?

The first reply should come within 5 minutes. The best data on this is the InsideSales.com and MIT lead response study, by Dr. James Oldroyd. It looked at 3 years of data from 6 companies, more than 15,000 web leads and more than 100,000 call attempts.

Here is what it found:

| Wait before the first call | What happened to the odds |
|---|---|
| 5 minutes vs 10 minutes | Odds to contact the lead fell 5 times |
| 5 minutes vs 30 minutes | Odds to contact fell 100 times; odds to qualify fell 21 times |
| First hour | Odds to contact fell more than 10 times; odds to qualify fell more than 6 times |
| After 20 hours | Each extra dial hurt the odds to qualify the lead |

Source: InsideSales.com and MIT Lead Response Management Study, 2007.

Two limits. The study is from 2007, and its companies sold things like mortgages and insurance, not property management. The study also did not measure close rates. Still, the pattern is clear: an owner who just filled in your form is near the phone and thinking about the rental. An hour later, the owner is doing something else, or talking to the next manager on the list.

What to do: send form leads to a phone, not only to an inbox. Set an alert for the one person on duty. If no one can call within 5 minutes, send an instant text or email that says who you are and when you will call.

## How many follow-ups before I stop?

Plan about 10 touches over 14 days, then stop or move the lead to a monthly email. No published study gives a number for property management, so treat this as a starting plan and test it.

| Day | Touch | Channel | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0, minute 0 | Instant reply | Email (and text if the owner gave consent) | "We got your request. I will call in 5 minutes." |
| 0, minute 5 | Call 1 | Phone | Talk now. Leave a short voicemail if no answer. |
| 0, hour 2 | Call 2 | Phone | Second try, same day |
| 1 | Value email | Email | Send the rental analysis or your fee table |
| 2 | Call 3 | Phone | Try a different time of day |
| 4 | Text | Text (with consent) | One line, one question |
| 6 | Call 4 | Phone | Short voicemail with your direct line |
| 8 | Proof email | Email | One case, one review, one number |
| 11 | Call 5 | Phone | Last call |
| 14 | Break-up email | Email | "Should I close your file?" |

Stop at once when the owner says no or asks you to stop. Mark the reason in your CRM: price, timing, sold the home, chose another manager, self-manage. That list is your best feedback on your offer.

## Which channel works best: call, text or email?

The call works best for the first touch, because one talk does what five emails cannot. The MIT study measured calls, not texts or emails. Use email to send things the owner can read later: a rental analysis, a fee table, a management agreement.

Text is fast, but it has legal rules. The FCC rule at 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(2) bars telemarketing calls made with an autodialer or a prerecorded voice to cell numbers without the person's "prior express written consent". Many text tools send in bulk. So:

- **Add a consent line** to your owner form, with a box the owner checks.
- **Text by hand** from a person, not a bulk blast, unless you have written consent.
- **Honor "stop"** at once.

Rules differ by state, too. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Time of day matters a little. In the same study, 4 to 6 pm was the best block to make contact, and 8 to 9 am and 4 to 5 pm were the best to qualify a lead. Wednesday and Thursday beat the other weekdays. These are 2007 numbers from other industries. Check your own call log after 3 months.

## Which phone habits lose leads?

A good lead can still die on the phone. Watch for these habits:

- **No one owns the lead.** Fourandhalf says a business development manager should own lead contact. If you have none, write a workflow so each person knows the role.
- **The call goes to a phone tree.** An owner who hears "press 3 for maintenance" hangs up. Give owners their own number.
- **The caller is not ready.** Fourandhalf advises you to have the owner's name, the property address and the request in front of you before you dial.
- **The voicemail is vague.** Say your name, the owner's name, the property street, and one direct number. Then send an email the same minute.
- **The first call tries to close.** Fourandhalf says the goal of first contact is to start a talk, not to close the deal. Ask about the property and the owner's plans first.
- **No one calls after 5 pm.** The study's best contact block was 4 to 6 pm. That is when many offices close.

## How do I measure follow-up?

Measure 3 numbers each week: minutes to first call, share of leads you reached, and share that booked a meeting.

| Metric | How to get it | Target to set |
|---|---|---|
| Minutes to first call | Form time vs first call time in the CRM | Under 5 during work hours |
| Contact rate | Leads you talked to / all owner leads | Your own baseline, then raise it |
| Meeting rate | Meetings booked / leads you talked to | Your own baseline |
| Signed doors | Doors signed / owner leads, per source | Compare channels |

The source of the lead matters too. In the Goodjuju fall 2026 study of 35 companies, AI tools sent 3.4% of owner leads, and those visits converted at 3.9%, far above other channels. ## A 30-second first-call script

Use this as a start and put it in your own words.

> "Hi, this is [name] from [company]. You asked about management for [street] a few minutes ago. Is now a good time for 2 minutes? Great. Tell me about the property: how many units, is it rented now, and what made you look for a manager?"

Then listen. Before you hang up, book the next step: a walk-through, a rental analysis, or a call with the owner's partner. Send the fee table the same day. If you do not publish fees yet, set them with the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

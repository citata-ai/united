## What are the signs a tenant abandoned the unit?

The signs of abandonment are facts you can prove: unpaid rent, a long absence, and an empty or nearly empty unit. An empty-looking unit alone is not enough. Most states tie abandonment to unpaid rent plus an absence of a set length.

Common signs you can record:

- Rent is late and the tenant does not answer calls, texts or email.
- Mail piles up at the door or in the box.
- Neighbors say they saw a moving truck.
- Utilities in the tenant's name were shut off.
- Furniture and clothes are gone when you look through a window.
- The tenant returned keys, or left them in the unit.

Write down each sign with the date. Take photos from outside the unit. This record helps if the tenant comes back and says the move-out was not real.

The legal test is stricter than the signs. Here is how 3 states set it.

| Rule | California | Arizona | Florida |
|---|---|---|---|
| Statute | Civil Code 1951.3 | A.R.S. 33-1370 | Fla. Stat. 83.59(3)(c) |
| Unpaid rent needed | 14 days in a row | 10 days (or 5 days if no property is left) | Rent not current |
| Absence needed | You reasonably believe the tenant left | 7 days without notice (or 5 days if no property is left) | Half the rent period |
| Notice | Written notice of belief of abandonment | Certified mail plus a door posting for 5 days | Not set in 83.59 |
| When you can retake | Date in your notice: 15 days after hand delivery, 18 after mailing | 5 days after notice is posted and mailed | When the presumption applies |

Rules differ in other states. Read your state statute before you act.

## Do I post a notice of abandonment?

Many states require a written notice, and a notice is a good habit even where the law is silent. The notice gives the tenant one last chance to say "I still live here". It also builds your record.

- **California.** Civil Code 1951.3 sets a 14-day rule. You can give a notice of belief of abandonment only after rent is unpaid for at least 14 days in a row. You hand it to the tenant or mail it first class to the last known address. The statute gives the exact form of the notice. The lease ends on the date in the notice unless the tenant replies in writing first.
- **Arizona.** A.R.S. 33-1370(A) requires a notice of abandonment by certified mail, return receipt requested. You send it to the last known address and to any other address you know. You also post the notice on the door for 5 days.
- **Florida.** Fla. Stat. 83.59 presumes abandonment after an absence of half the rent period. The presumption does not apply if rent is current or the tenant told you in writing about the absence.

In California, a tenant can stop the process. The tenant sends a written notice that they do not intend to abandon, with an address for service. Payment of some of the rent in the notice period also stops it.

## When can I re-enter and re-rent?

You can re-enter and re-rent when your state's test is met and the notice period has run. Before that point, a changed lock can be an illegal lockout.

Use these steps:

1. **Check the rent ledger.** Confirm how many days the rent is unpaid.
2. **Try to reach the tenant.** Call, text and email. Contact the emergency contact on the application. Log each try.
3. **Send the state notice.** Use the method the statute names. Keep proof of mailing.
4. **Wait out the notice period.** For example, 5 days in Arizona after posting and mailing.
5. **Enter with a witness.** Take photos and video of each room.
6. **Inventory what is left.** List each item and its condition.
7. **Change the locks.** Do this only after the steps above.
8. **List the unit.** Start your normal marketing.

California's entry statute, Civil Code 1954, lets a landlord enter when the tenant abandoned or surrendered the unit. That is the entry right. It does not replace the 1951.3 notice that ends the lease.

## Do I have to try to re-rent the unit?

In many states you must make a fair effort to re-rent if you want to charge the tenant for the rest of the lease. Arizona says the landlord "shall make reasonable efforts" to rent the unit at a fair rent (A.R.S. 33-1370(C)).

Florida gives the landlord 4 choices after abandonment (Fla. Stat. 83.595):

- End the lease and retake the unit for your own account. The tenant owes no more rent.
- Retake the unit for the tenant's account. You must try in good faith to re-rent. The tenant owes the gap between the lease rent and what you collect.
- Do nothing and hold the tenant liable for rent as it comes due.
- Charge liquidated damages or an early termination fee of no more than 2 months' rent, if the tenant signed a separate addendum for it.

Here is a worked example of the Florida "tenant's account" option:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Lease rent, 4 months left at $1,500 | $6,000 |
| New tenant pays for 3 of those months at $1,450 | -$4,350 |
| Balance the old tenant still owes | $1,650 |

The numbers are an example, not a rule. Your lease and state law decide what you can claim.

## What happens to the security deposit?

The deposit goes toward unpaid rent and damage, under your state's deposit rules. Arizona is direct: after you retake the unit, the deposit is forfeited and applied to accrued rent and reasonable costs (A.R.S. 33-1370(B)).

Other states use their normal deposit deadlines:

- **California:** 21 calendar days after the tenant vacates to return the deposit or send an itemized statement (Civil Code 1950.5).
- **Texas:** refund on or before the 30th day after the tenant surrenders the unit (Property Code 92.103).

Send the statement to the last known address, even if you think the tenant will not read it. Keep a copy.

## What do I do with belongings left behind?

You follow your state's personal property rules. Do not throw items out on day one.

- **Arizona.** You make an inventory and tell the tenant where the items are stored and the cost. You hold the items for 14 calendar days. After that you can sell them or donate them to a charity. If the tenant returned the keys, you may dispose of the items at once unless you agreed in writing to something else (A.R.S. 33-1370(D), (F), (I)).
- **California.** Civil Code 1983 requires a written notice to the tenant and any person you think owns the items. The claim date is at least 15 days after hand delivery or 18 days after mailing.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

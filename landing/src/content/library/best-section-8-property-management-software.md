## How does each tool split tenant rent and HAP?

The software must keep tenant responsibility distinct from the housing authority's payment. Hemlane explicitly describes tracking tenant and subsidy payments. DoorLoop lists HAP reconciliation reports. ExactEstate describes connected household, certification and accounting workflows. Ask each vendor to demonstrate the actual ledger structure.

Use a sample lease and the authority's current rent notice. Record the effective date of each split. Then change the subsidy amount mid-lease and inspect how the system preserves earlier periods.

Under [24 CFR 982.451(b)(4)(iii)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/982.451), the family is not responsible for the rent portion covered by the HAP contract. The software must not turn an unpaid authority balance into tenant debt.

Ask for separate receivable views and a combined property view. Staff should explain the full rent without losing the distinction between payers. An attractive total balance is not enough.

## Which track inspection dates?

Inspection tracking should connect the scheduled visit, result, repair work and follow-up. Hemlane describes inspection help and follow-ups on its affordable-housing page. For the other products, require a demonstration of dates, documents and reminders rather than assuming every compliance module includes your workflow.

Create a failed inspection in the demo. Attach the report, assign the repair and record the next required action. Ask who receives reminders when the responsible person is away.

Keep the inspection result separate from a staff note saying work is complete. The authority's acceptance and your contractor's completion are different records. Ask how both remain visible.

Do not infer a universal inspection deadline from software defaults. Confirm the current authority instructions for the actual case. Link the applicable notice to the record so staff can see the source of the date they follow.

## Which apply late fees only to the tenant portion?

Choose a tool that can demonstrate the exact fee basis and exclude the authority's share from tenant late-fee calculations. The reviewed vendor pages do not prove this behavior for every plan. We do not name a winner without the test.

Use a case where the renter pays their full portion and the authority payment remains open. Run the proposed late-fee process. Check the tenant balance, notice and owner report.

Then reverse or adjust an authority payment. Confirm that the system does not quietly assess a renter charge. Inspect automated notices as well as the ledger. A correct ledger paired with a wrong demand letter still creates a problem.

The federal rent-responsibility rule is a guardrail, not a complete local late-fee analysis. Have counsel review the tenant fee policy, lease and notices before you automate them.

## Which handle housing authority wires?

The right system must allocate an authority deposit across the intended households and periods. A bank feed that imports the total deposit does not complete HAP reconciliation. Ask each finalist to show allocation against the authority's remittance detail.

Use a deposit that covers several households. Include a correction for an earlier period and an unmatched amount. Staff should be able to leave the unresolved amount visible without forcing it onto a tenant.

Save the remittance document with the deposit or an accessible linked record. Ask how a later correction affects the original allocation. Review whether staff can trace the payment from bank entry to household balance.

Check the export too. Your accountant should receive enough detail to explain each authority deposit after the software contract ends. Do not rely on a vendor dashboard as the only record.

## What do landlords use today?

Landlords can shortlist products with explicit subsidy features, but public vendor pages do not establish market share. We have not verified a representative survey showing what most voucher landlords use. Hemlane, DoorLoop and ExactEstate provide different starting points for a buying test.

Match the product to the program. Tenant-based vouchers and broader subsidized-property operations can involve different records. Ask the vendor to name the exact supported workflow and show your case.

Do not pay for a large certification system if you need only split rent and inspection tracking. Equally, do not assume a basic rent app can handle a complex regulated portfolio.

Bring the staff member who reconciles authority payments to the demo. Their exceptions are more useful than a generic sales presentation. Ask what work would still remain in a spreadsheet after launch.

## What should your acceptance test include?

Approve the product only when the ledger and documents support the full payment cycle.

- **Split:** preserve tenant and authority amounts with effective dates.
- **Deposit:** allocate a combined authority payment correctly.
- **Exceptions:** keep unmatched funds visible for review.
- **Fees:** exclude the authority balance from tenant fee calculations.
- **Inspection:** link result, repair and follow-up records.
- **Reporting:** explain open balances by payer and period.
- **Export:** download ledgers and the supporting remittances.

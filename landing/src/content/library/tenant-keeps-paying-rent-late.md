## Why does a tenant pay late every month?

A tenant pays late every month for one of a few reasons. Find the reason first. The fix depends on it.

| Cause | Sign | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Payday after the due date | Rent arrives the same few days late each month | Change the due date |
| Forgets | Late by a random number of days | Autopay or reminders |
| Short on money | Partial payments, rising balance | Payment plan, aid referral, or exit plan |
| Tests the rules | Pays just before the fee starts | Enforce the fee each time |
| Dispute | Holds rent over a repair | Fix the repair; follow state rules on rent withholding |

Ask the tenant directly and calmly: "Your rent has come in on the 6th for 4 months. Is there a reason? Can we fix it together?" Many tenants answer honestly.

## Should I change the due date?

Change the due date when the tenant's payday falls after it. A tenant paid on the 3rd will never pay on the 1st. Moving the due date to the 5th can turn a late payer into an on-time payer.

Do it in writing:

1. **Write a lease addendum** with the new due date.
2. **Keep the same rent and fee terms.** Only the date changes.
3. **Pro-rate the first month** if the change moves the date forward.
4. **Both parties sign.**

Check your state's late fee rules against the new date. In Oregon, ORS 90.260 says a late fee is allowed only if rent is not received by the 4th day of the rental period. Your written lease must state the due date and the date late charges start.

## Do I enforce late fees every time?

Enforce your late fee the same way every time. A fee you skip for 3 months and then charge in the 4th looks unfair, and it can be harder to defend.

Late fee rules vary by state. Here are 3 examples:

| State | When a fee may start | Cap | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Texas | Rent unpaid 2 full days after due date; fee in written lease | Presumed reasonable up to 12% of rent (4 units or fewer) or 10% (more than 4 units) | Prop. Code 92.019 |
| New York | Rent not paid within 5 days of the due date | $50 or 5% of monthly rent, whichever is less | RPL 238-a(2) |
| Oregon | Rent not received by the 4th day of the period; fee in written lease | A reasonable flat amount, a daily fee capped at 6% of that amount, or 5% per 5-day period | ORS 90.260 |

Texas also sets a penalty for breaking its rule: $100, plus three times the fee collected wrongly, plus attorney's fees (Prop. Code 92.019(c)). A wrong fee can cost you more than the late rent.

**Waivers:** if you want to waive a fee once, say so in writing: "I am waiving the May fee one time. The lease fee applies from June." That keeps your rule clear.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Is chronic late payment grounds to non-renew?

Chronic late payment is grounds to non-renew in many places, but not all. Some states and cities have just-cause rules that limit when you may end a tenancy.

California is an example. Civil Code 1946.2 says that after a tenant has lawfully occupied a unit for 12 months, the owner may not end the tenancy without just cause, stated in the notice. The law lists "default in the payment of rent" as an at-fault just cause. Some properties are exempt, and some cities have stricter rules.

Before you non-renew:

- [ ] Check state and city just-cause rules.
- [ ] Check notice periods for non-renewal.
- [ ] Review your log. Is the pattern clear and recent?
- [ ] Confirm you treated other late payers the same way.
- [ ] Consider a final written warning first.

Never non-renew because of a protected trait, a complaint to a housing agency or a repair request. That can be retaliation or discrimination.

## What do I document?

Document every late payment, every fee and every message. If you go to court or a housing agency, your log is your proof.

A simple log has these columns:

| Month | Due | Paid | Amount | Fee charged | Notice sent | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jan | 1st | 6th | $1,800 | $50 | Reminder on 3rd | Payday 5th |
| Feb | 1st | 7th | $1,800 | $50 | Reminder on 3rd | — |
| Mar | 1st | 1st | $1,800 | — | — | New due date talk |

Also keep:

- **Copies of every message** about rent, by email or in your portal.
- **Any payment plan** in writing, signed by both.
- **Any notice to pay or quit** with proof of how you served it.

## What steps work in practice?

The steps that work are calm, quick and the same each month.

1. **Day 1:** rent is due.
2. **Day 2:** send a friendly reminder if unpaid.
3. **Day the fee starts under your lease and state law:** charge it and send a notice.
4. **After 2 late months:** meet with the tenant. Find the cause. Offer a due date change or autopay.
5. **After 3 late months:** send a written warning that refers to the lease.
6. **Before renewal:** decide based on your log and local law.

Autopay can solve the "forgets" cause. See the steps in your rent platform, and keep one non-electronic option where your state requires it.

## What you can do next

Start a late-payment log today for every tenant, not only the late ones. A log for all tenants shows you treat everyone the same. If rent collection takes too much of your week, read how [property management](/property-management) handles it.

## Which tasks should I hand off first?

Hand off repeat tasks whose inputs, rules and results you can describe. Keep the work in a shared system so you can inspect it without becoming the person who completes it.

Start by recording where staff wait for you. Separate missing authority from missing knowledge and missing information. Each cause needs a different fix. A new hire cannot solve every delay if all decisions still require the owner.

Buildium's automation guide distinguishes routine work from tasks that need judgment. Use that distinction when choosing what to delegate or automate. Routine reminders and report preparation differ from an unresolved safety concern or owner dispute.

| Work type | Suggested first change | Evidence to review |
|---|---|---|
| Repeat message | Approved draft and sending rule | Accurate message and record |
| Routine scheduling | Named coordinator and backup | Confirmed appointment |
| Standard report | Defined data source and review | Checked report and exceptions |
| Owner question | Clear service scope and authority | Answer and follow-up |
| High-risk decision | Named escalation route | Decision by responsible person |

Do not delegate only unpleasant work while keeping every useful decision. Give staff the authority that matches the task. State the limit and the point where they must escalate.

Choose a current repeat task and let the new owner of that task explain the process back to you. Fix unclear instructions before withdrawing daily help.

## How do I build a second leader?

Build a second leader by transferring operating decisions with training, review and a clear reporting relationship. A title alone does not make someone able to run the company.

Define the role's scope. State whether the person owns scheduling, staff priorities, owner escalations, service quality or all of these. Identify decisions reserved for the responsible broker, owner or qualified adviser.

Use observed work to choose the person. Look for careful follow-up, fair staff decisions and a habit of asking for missing facts. Strong individual production does not automatically show management skill.

Let the operations lead run a regular work review while you observe. Ask the lead to explain the largest unresolved cases and proposed actions. Give feedback after the meeting rather than answering every question first.

Increase authority in steps tied to evidence. When a decision goes wrong, review the rule, information and training before taking the whole role back. Keep a record of the change so the next case uses the improved process.

Tell staff where decisions now belong. If employees can bypass the lead and get a different answer from you, the new role remains weak. Redirect routine requests while keeping an accessible route for serious concerns.

## Which reports let me manage without daily intervention?

Use reports that show unresolved work, cash, service quality and decisions outside authority. A large dashboard can still hide the case that needs action.

Start with open maintenance by age and urgency, overdue owner decisions, leasing stages and unresolved account differences. Include the person responsible and the next action. A count without ownership does not support a decision.

Buildium's guide describes automated owner reporting while keeping staff involved in explanations and exceptions. Apply the same principle internally. Software can assemble the report, but a person must review unusual results.

Keep financial review separate from general task review. Ask the responsible accounting lead to explain reconciliation differences and pending obligations. Do not accept a green status without knowing what it means.

Track company cash separately from money held for clients. California DRE guidance describes trust funds as money belonging to clients or beneficiaries. That state example shows why a healthy bank total does not establish available company cash.

Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) when you review recurring fee economics. Compare revenue with staff and system costs using your own records. The tool does not replace account reconciliation or a company cash forecast.

Keep a short exception report for matters that need your authority. Ask staff to include the facts, options and recommended action so your review adds a decision.

## How long does stepping back take?

The time needed depends on process clarity, staff capability and the decisions you currently control. This guide does not claim a universal door threshold or handoff timetable.

Use readiness tests instead of a fixed promise. Can staff complete normal work without you? Can the lead handle an exception? Can a backup find the records and continue the work?

Try a planned absence after you confirm coverage. Tell the team which events still require contact and how to reach you. Keep the test realistic while protecting resident service and legal duties.

After the test, review every contact that reached you. Was your approval truly required, or did staff lack information? Change the process when the contact exposed a gap.

Do not use silence as the only success measure. Staff may avoid calling while work stalls. Inspect open cases and ask whether the lead could see and resolve delays.

Keep duties tied to a required license with the appropriate person. Delegation inside the company does not remove the need to check local supervision and licensing rules.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What should the owner keep doing?

The owner should keep the responsibilities that support direction, oversight and company health. Define those duties as clearly as the work you hand off.

Review service standards, financial performance, key risks and leadership needs. Spend time on decisions the operations lead cannot make within the agreed authority. Avoid turning the review into a second daily work queue.

Meet the lead with a consistent agenda. Ask what changed, what remains unresolved and what support is needed. Record decisions so the team can act without asking again.

Check whether the company relies on the new lead as much as it relied on you. Train backups and keep procedures current. A single replacement decision-maker does not provide resilient coverage.

Start by listing the approvals you gave during a normal work period. Pick one repeat decision, define its limit and transfer it with a clear review point.

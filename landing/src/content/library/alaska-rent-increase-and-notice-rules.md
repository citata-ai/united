Alaska law says little about rent increases. We read all of AS 34.03, the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, on the Alaska Legislature site on 2026-10-20. The word "increase" shows up in one section only: the ban on retaliation. This table shows what the act sets and where it is silent.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Statewide cap on increases | None in the act | AS 34.03 |
| Notice of a rent increase | No section sets one | AS 34.03 |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | 30 days before the rental due date | AS 34.03.290(b) |
| End a week-to-week tenancy | 14 days, while rent is current | AS 34.03.290(a) |
| Rent during a fixed term | Set by the rental agreement | AS 34.03.020(a) |
| Tenancy with no fixed term | Week-to-week if rent is weekly, else month-to-month | AS 34.03.020(d) |
| Retaliatory increase | Not allowed | AS 34.03.310(a) |
| Increases allowed despite a complaint | Tied to taxes, costs, improvements or market rent | AS 34.03.310(d) |

## Does Alaska have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

No cap appears in the state act. AS 34.03 does not limit how much the rent may rise, and it has no rent control section.

The act lets the parties set the rent. AS 34.03.020(a) says the landlord and tenant may include rent and the term in the rental agreement. If they do not agree on a rent, subsection (b) says the tenant pays the fair rental value.

The act does not apply to every unit. AS 34.03.330 excludes some arrangements, such as hotels and other transient stays, employee housing tied to work on the premises, and some transitional housing programs.

## How many days notice does an Alaska landlord need to raise the rent?

AS 34.03 has no section that sets a notice period for a rent increase. We read the whole chapter and found none.

The notice to end a tenancy sets the practical floor. For a month-to-month tenancy, AS 34.03.290(b) lets either side end the tenancy with written notice at least 30 days before the rental due date in the notice. A landlord who wants new terms can end the old tenancy on that notice. So give at least 30 days' written notice before a rental due date.

Worked example for rent due on the 1st:

1. **Pick the new rent** and the start date, 2027-01-01.
2. **Count back 30 days.** The notice must reach the tenant by 2026-12-02.
3. **Put it in writing.** Keep a copy and proof of the date.
4. **Plan for a no.** If the tenant will not pay, the tenancy ends on the date in the notice.

If the tenant stays past that date, you need a notice to quit and a court case. AS 09.45.090(a)(2)(F) covers a tenant who stays after a periodic tenancy ends.

## Can an Alaska landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

Usually not without the tenant's consent. AS 34.03.020(a) makes the rent and the term part of the rental agreement. If the lease fixes the rent for 12 months, that rent binds both sides for 12 months.

Some leases include a clause that allows a set raise during the term. If yours has one, follow its words. If it does not, wait for renewal or ask the tenant to sign an amendment.

Checklist before any raise:

- [ ] Check the lease type: fixed term or month-to-month.
- [ ] For a fixed term, check for an increase clause.
- [ ] For month-to-month, give written notice at least 30 days before a rental due date.
- [ ] Check whether the tenant made a recent complaint (see the last section).
- [ ] Apply one rule to all tenants in the same type of unit.

The last item matters for Fair Housing. Base each raise on the unit and the market, never on who the tenant is.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Alaska?

At least 30 days' written notice before the rental due date named in the notice. AS 34.03.290(b) gives this rule to both sides.

| Tenancy type | Notice | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Month-to-month | 30 days before the rental due date | AS 34.03.290(b) |
| Week-to-week | 14 days before the termination date, while rent is current | AS 34.03.290(a) |

The date matters. For month-to-month, the 30 days count to a rental due date. So name a rental due date as the end date in the notice.

If the tenant stays after the end date without consent, AS 34.03.290(c) lets the landlord serve a notice to quit and sue for possession. If the holdover is wilful and not in good faith, the landlord may also recover up to 1.5 times actual damages.

## Do cities in Alaska add their own rent or notice rules?

AS 34.03 does not bar or allow local rent rules, as far as we read. We did not find a state preemption section in the act. Before you raise rent in Anchorage, Fairbanks, Juneau or another city or borough, check the local code.

The state act does limit the reason for a raise. AS 34.03.310(a) says a landlord may not retaliate by raising rent after the tenant:

- complained to the landlord about a repair duty under AS 34.03.100
- used rights under the act
- organized or joined a tenant union
- complained to a government agency about housing, wage, price or rent controls

AS 34.03.310(d) still allows a raise in three cases:

1. **Higher costs.** The landlord became liable for a substantial rise in property taxes or operating costs at least 4 months before the demand, and the raise bears a reasonable relation to it.
2. **Capital improvement.** The raise does not exceed the straight-line depreciation of the improvement, split among the units it serves.
3. **Market rent.** The new rent does not exceed the rent for similar units in the building, or the fair rental value.

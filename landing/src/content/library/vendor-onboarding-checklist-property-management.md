## What should a property manager collect before a new vendor starts work?

A property manager should collect 4 things before a new vendor starts work: a W-9, a certificate of insurance, license proof, and signed terms. These protect the owner's money, the tenant's safety and your own tax filing. Collect all 4 before the first job, not after the first invoice.

### Full vendor onboarding checklist

**Part 1: Identity and tax**

- [ ] Legal business name and any trade name (DBA)
- [ ] Signed Form W-9, current revision
- [ ] Business type from the W-9 (sole owner, LLC, corporation)
- [ ] Business address, phone and email for invoices
- [ ] Payment method and bank details, confirmed by phone

**Part 2: Insurance**

- [ ] Certificate of insurance (COI) for general liability
- [ ] Workers' compensation proof, or the state's written exemption
- [ ] Commercial auto, if the vendor drives to jobs
- [ ] Your company (and owner, if your agreement asks) listed as certificate holder
- [ ] Additional insured endorsement, if your policy requires one
- [ ] Policy end dates entered in your tracker

**Part 3: License and safety**

- [ ] Trade license number for licensed trades (electrical, plumbing, HVAC, pest, roofing)
- [ ] License checked on the state or city lookup site, with date and screenshot
- [ ] EPA lead-safe firm certificate, if the vendor works in pre-1978 homes
- [ ] Any city contractor registration your area requires

**Part 4: Terms**

- [ ] Signed vendor terms sheet (see below)
- [ ] Emergency contact for after-hours calls
- [ ] Service area and trades covered
- [ ] Agreement to the photo and invoice rules

**Part 5: System setup**

- [ ] Vendor record created in your property management software
- [ ] 1099 flag set from the W-9 business type
- [ ] Vendor portal access sent, if you use one

This is a generic checklist, not a state legal form. Check your state's laws and your management agreements.

## Why do you need a W-9 and a certificate of insurance?

You need a W-9 for tax reporting and a certificate of insurance for risk. Each one answers a different question. The W-9 answers "Who do we report this money to?" The certificate answers "Who pays if this job goes wrong?"

**The W-9.** The IRS says Form W-9 gives "your correct Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN)" to a person who must file an information return. The vendor gives the form to you, not to the IRS. The current form is the March 2024 revision (IRS, page updated 2026-06-27).

The W-9 matters for 2 reasons:

1. **Form 1099-NEC.** IRS instructions (12/2026 revision) say you file Form 1099-NEC for each person you paid at least $2,000 for services in the year. That $2,000 threshold applies to tax years beginning after 2025.
2. **Backup withholding.** The IRS says payers must withhold at "the current rate of 24 percent" when a payee fails to give a correct TIN.

The business type on the W-9 also matters. IRS instructions say payments to a corporation are generally not reportable, with exceptions such as legal services. Payments by credit card or a third-party network go on Form 1099-K from the payment company, not on your 1099-NEC.

A worked example. You pay a handyman $3,400 for services in 2026 by bank transfer. That is above $2,000, so you file a 1099-NEC. If the handyman gave no correct TIN, backup withholding applies at 24%. On $3,400, that is $3,400 × 0.24 = $816 you must withhold and send to the IRS. Collect the W-9 first and you avoid that problem.

**The certificate of insurance.** A certificate of insurance shows the vendor's policies, limits and end dates. If a vendor without insurance damages a unit or gets hurt, the claim may land on the owner's policy. Ask your own insurance agent which limits to require. Write those limits into your vendor policy.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## Which license checks should you do?

Check every license the trade and the job need, on the official lookup site. Trades like electrical and plumbing often need a state or city license, and the rules change by place. A license number on an invoice is not proof. A lookup result with a date is proof.

Use this table to plan your checks:

| Trade | What to check | Where to check | How often |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electrician | Trade license, status active | State or city license lookup | At onboarding, then yearly |
| Plumber | Trade license, status active | State or city license lookup | At onboarding, then yearly |
| HVAC | License and any refrigerant certification | State lookup, vendor's card | At onboarding, then yearly |
| Pest control | Pesticide applicator license | State agriculture department | At onboarding, then yearly |
| General handyman | Any state or city limits on job size | State contractor board | At onboarding |
| Any trade in pre-1978 homes | EPA lead-safe firm certification | EPA or your state program | At onboarding, then at renewal |

The lead-safe check comes from federal law. The EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) Program covers housing built before 1978. Anyone paid to do work there that disturbs painted surfaces must be certified. The EPA page names property management firms that do this work. Some states run their own program instead of EPA.

Save a screenshot of each lookup in the vendor record. Add a reminder for each license end date.

## What rates and response terms should you agree in writing?

Agree on rates, response times, approval rules and invoice rules in a signed terms sheet. Written terms stop the most common vendor disputes: a surprise trip charge, a slow emergency response, or an invoice with no photos.

### Vendor terms sheet template

| Term | Agreed value |
|---|---|
| Hourly rate, standard hours | $________ |
| Hourly rate, after hours and weekends | $________ |
| Trip or service call charge | $________ |
| Markup on parts | ____% or at cost |
| Emergency response (on site) | Within ____ hours |
| Urgent response | Within ____ hours |
| Routine response | Within ____ business days |
| Spend limit without new approval | $________ per job |
| Photos required | Before and after, on each job |
| Invoice due | Within ____ days of job close |
| Payment terms | Net ____ days |
| Warranty on labor | ____ days |

Fill in the blanks with your own numbers. Then link the terms to your work order process. The spend limit for vendors should stay below the owner approval limit in your management agreement. A vendor should never start a job that the owner had to approve first.

## How do you keep vendor files current?

Keep vendor files current with one tracker and 3 dates per vendor: insurance end date, license end date and last W-9 date. Review the tracker each month.

1. **30 days before an end date,** email the vendor for a new certificate or license.
2. **On the end date,** pause new jobs for that vendor until the file is current.
3. **Each January,** confirm W-9 data before you file 1099s.

A clean vendor file helps when an owner asks who worked in their unit. For more on what owners look for in a manager, see the [guides](/guides) page.

## Are HAP payments rental income?

Yes, HAP payments are rental income. IRS Publication 527 says: "Rental income is any payment you receive for the use or occupation of property. It isn't limited to amounts you receive as normal rental payments."

The HAP (housing assistance payment) is the part of the rent the PHA pays for the family. Under 24 CFR 982.451(b)(2), the HAP "is credited toward the monthly rent to owner". So for tax purposes, your rent on a voucher unit has 2 parts, and both are income:

| Part | Who pays you | Taxable? |
|---|---|---|
| HAP | The housing authority (PHA) | Yes, rental income |
| Tenant share | The tenant | Yes, rental income |
| Security deposit you will return | The tenant | No, while you plan to return it |
| Security deposit you keep for lease breach | The tenant | Yes, in the year you keep it |
| Deposit used as last month's rent | The tenant | Yes, as advance rent when received |

The deposit rows come from Publication 527. Most individual landlords use the cash method, which Publication 527 says means you report income in the year you actually or constructively receive it.

## Does the PHA send a 1099?

Yes, the PHA sends a 1099-MISC. The IRS instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC (12/2026 revision) say: "Public housing agencies must report in box 1 rental assistance payments made to owners of housing projects." They cite Rev. Rul. 88-53.

The threshold changed. Those instructions say that for tax years beginning after 2025, the reporting floor for rents in box 1 rose to $2,000. So for 2026, expect a 1099-MISC from each PHA that paid you $2,000 or more.

Two examples of how PHAs deliver them:

- **Tacoma Housing Authority** uses track1099.com to produce its yearly 1099s. Any 1099 you do not open online is mailed.
- **NYC HPD** asks each payee for a Substitute Form W-9 with a correct tax ID, so it can report income paid to you to the IRS.

Watch for: if your property manager collects the HAP, the IRS instructions say the manager "must use Form 1099-MISC to report the rent paid over to the property owner". You may then get a 1099 from the manager, not the PHA.

## Are landlord incentive payments taxable?

Plan to treat landlord incentive payments as income. These include signing bonuses, vacancy loss payments, damage payments and re-rent bonuses. We did not find an IRS page that names PHA landlord incentives one by one. So do 3 things:

1. **Ask the PHA how it reports them.** Some may appear in box 1 with HAP; some may be in a different box or not reported.
2. **Keep the PHA letter** that says what each payment was for.
3. **Ask a tax professional** how to report each type, mostly damage payments, which can interact with repair expenses.

## How do I match PHA statements to my books?

Match PHA statements to your books every month, not in January. A year-end 1099 is one number. Your books need 12 months of detail behind it.

Monthly steps:

1. **Record each HAP deposit** by unit and month, from the PHA statement or portal.
2. **Record the tenant share** separately, by unit.
3. **Note adjustments.** Back pay, prorated months and amounts the PHA takes back.
4. **Flag timing gaps.** A December HAP that lands in January counts in the year you receive it, under the cash method.

Year-end check, with example numbers:

| Line | Unit 1A | Unit 2B | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| HAP received in 2026 (from your books) | $14,400 | $10,800 | $25,200 |
| 2026 1099-MISC box 1 from the PHA | | | $25,200 |
| Difference | | | $0 |
| Tenant share received | $4,800 | $7,200 | $12,000 |
| Kept security deposit | $0 | $350 | $350 |
| Total rental income to report | $19,200 | $18,350 | $37,550 |

If the 1099 and your books differ, look for a December payment, a repayment to the PHA, or a payment for a unit you sold. Ask the PHA for a payment history by month. To see how each month's rent splits between HAP and tenant, use our [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator).

## What records should I keep?

Keep records that prove each dollar of rent and each expense. Publication 527 covers rental records in general. For voucher units, add the PHA papers.

- [ ] The lease, the HAP contract and the tenancy addendum.
- [ ] Monthly PHA payment statements or portal exports.
- [ ] Bank statements showing each HAP deposit.
- [ ] The tenant ledger for the tenant share.
- [ ] PHA notices that change the HAP (income reviews, rent changes).
- [ ] Abatement notices and any repayment to the PHA.
- [ ] Letters for incentive payments, with the purpose of each.
- [ ] Each year's 1099-MISC from each PHA.
- [ ] Your W-9 as sent to each PHA, so names and tax IDs match.
- [ ] Security deposit records, including any amount kept.

Name and tax ID mismatches cause trouble. NYC HPD's W-9 says the legal business name must match the IRS letter or the Social Security card. If you hold units in an LLC, give the PHA the LLC's name and EIN, not your own.

## Where do I report it?

Most individual landlords report rental income and expenses on Schedule E (Form 1040), per Publication 527. Publication 527 points to Schedule C instead when you provide substantial services to tenants. Report the total rent, HAP plus tenant share, on the right form. Then deduct your rental expenses, such as repairs, insurance, interest and depreciation, under the Publication 527 rules.

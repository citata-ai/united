Nevada has a fast eviction track called summary eviction. Its rules sit in NRS 40.253 and NRS 40.254. We read both in the current code on the Nevada Legislature site (Rev. 4/15/2026). Here is the short version.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice for unpaid rent | Pay or leave by close of business, 7th judicial day after service | NRS 40.253(1)(a), NRS 40.2512 |
| Short-term tenancy option | Noon of the 4th full day, weekly rent, tenancy of 45 days or less | NRS 40.253(1)(b) |
| Notice for a lease breach | 5 days to fix the breach or leave | NRS 40.2516, NRS 118A.430 |
| Notice for nuisance, waste, unlawful business, illegal sublet | 3 days to leave | NRS 40.2514 |
| Tenant contest, non-rent cases | Affidavit by close of the 5th judicial day | NRS 40.254(1)(c) |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | At least 30 days' notice | NRS 40.251(1)(b) |
| End a week-to-week tenancy | At least 7 days' notice | NRS 40.251(1)(b) |
| Posting of removal order | Within 24 hours after the officer receives it | NRS 40.253(5)(a) |
| Removal | 24 to 36 hours after posting | NRS 40.253(5)(a) |
| Lockout without court | Prohibited; up to $2,500 plus actual damages | NRS 118A.480, NRS 118A.390 |

## What notice must a Nevada landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

A Nevada landlord must serve a written notice to pay the rent or leave. Under NRS 40.253(1)(a), the deadline is the close of business on the seventh judicial day after the day of service. "Close of business" means the close of business of the court (NRS 40.253(13)).

The notice must also include set content. NRS 40.253(3) requires it to:

- **Name the court** that has jurisdiction.
- **Explain the tenant's right to contest** by filing an affidavit with that court before the deadline.
- **Explain removal.** If the court finds against the tenant, the sheriff or constable posts an order and removes the tenant 24 to 36 hours later.
- **Explain lockout rights.** The tenant may seek relief under NRS 118A.390 for an unlawful lockout or a cut in essential services.

NRS 40.280 says who may serve the notice. It lists the sheriff, a constable, a licensed process server or the agent of a Nevada attorney. Proof of service must be on file before the court issues a removal order (NRS 40.280(4)).

One more rule protects tenants who try to pay. After the notice, you may not refuse rent only because the tenant did not also pay collection fees or attorney's fees (NRS 40.253(11)).

## How long does an eviction take in Nevada?

A Nevada eviction for unpaid rent takes at least 7 judicial days plus the court and removal steps. The statutes fix the notice period and the removal window. They do not fix how long the court takes to sign an order.

The steps for an uncontested case:

1. **Serve the notice.** Day 0 is the day of service.
2. **Wait 7 judicial days.** Judicial days skip weekends and court holidays.
3. **File the affidavit of complaint** in justice court if the tenant did not pay, leave or file an affidavit (NRS 40.253(5)).
4. **The court issues an order.** The sheriff or constable posts it within 24 hours after receipt.
5. **Removal.** The officer removes the tenant 24 to 36 hours after posting.

If the tenant files an affidavit, the court holds a hearing (NRS 40.253(6)). If the court finds a legal defense, the case moves to a regular unlawful detainer action under NRS 40.290 to 40.420. That path takes longer.

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Nevada, and what does it cost?

A Nevada landlord files in the justice court of the township where the unit is. NRS 40.253(5)(a) also allows the district court of the county, whichever has jurisdiction.

The affidavit of complaint must contain 9 items, including:

- the date the tenancy started and the periodic rent;
- any cleaning, security or rent deposits paid in advance;
- the date rent became late and the amount claimed;
- a copy of the notice and a statement that it was served under NRS 40.280;
- a copy of the signed lease, if any.

Each court sets its fees. The Las Vegas Justice Court fee page lists these amounts (schedule effective February 8, 2024):

| Filing | Fee |
|---|---|
| Complaint for Summary Eviction | $71.00 |
| Writ of restitution | $75.00 |
| Unlawful detainer, notice to quit not served, no damages claimed | $71.00 |

Check the fee page of your own court before you file.

## Can a Nevada landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes. Nevada allows eviction for a lease violation and for the end of a periodic tenancy. Each ground has its own notice:

- **Lease breach.** A 5-day notice to fix the breach or leave (NRS 40.2516). NRS 118A.430 also gives the tenant 5 days to remedy a breach that can be fixed.
- **Nuisance, waste, unlawful business or illegal sublet.** A 3-day notice to leave (NRS 40.2514).
- **End of a periodic tenancy.** At least 30 days for month-to-month and at least 7 days for week-to-week (NRS 40.251).

NRS 40.251 does not list a cause to end a periodic tenancy. But a tenant 60 or older, or with a disability, may ask in writing for 30 more days (NRS 40.251(2)). Your notice must tell the tenant about that right (NRS 40.251(5)).

Retaliation is not allowed. NRS 118A.510 bars an eviction to punish a tenant for a good-faith code complaint, for joining a tenant union, or for a fair housing complaint.

For these non-rent grounds, the summary track uses NRS 40.254. The tenant may contest by affidavit before the close of the fifth judicial day after service.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Nevada?

The sheriff or constable of the county removes the tenant. NRS 40.253(5)(a) says the officer posts the order on the premises within 24 hours after receiving it. The officer then removes the tenant not earlier than 24 hours and not later than 36 hours after posting.

After removal, NRS 118A.460 sets 2 duties for the landlord:

- **Store property.** Keep the tenant's property safe for 30 days. You may charge reasonable costs of inventory, moving and storage.
- **Allow retrieval.** For 5 days after the eviction, give the tenant a fair chance to get essentials such as medicine and basic clothing.

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Nevada?

No. A self-help eviction is not legal in Nevada. NRS 118A.480 says a landlord may take back the unit only in 3 ways: a court action, a surrender by the tenant, or an abandonment.

A lockout or a willful cut in essential services has a real cost. Under NRS 118A.390, the tenant may get back in and recover actual damages, up to $2,500 set by the court, or both. The tenant may file for expedited relief within 5 judicial days. The court must hold a hearing within 3 judicial days after the filing.

There is one narrow exception in the summary track. If the tenant files no affidavit, NRS 40.253(5)(b) lets the landlord lock the tenant out "in a peaceable manner". Get advice before you rely on it.

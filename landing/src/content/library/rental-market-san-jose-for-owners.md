## How much did HUD's fair market rent change in San Jose for FY2027?

HUD's fair market rent for a 2-bedroom in San Jose fell 6.1% for FY2027, from $3,483 to $3,272. The FY2027 figures took effect on 2026-10-01 (Federal Register notice FR-6553-N-03). The area is all of Santa Clara County.

| Unit size | FY2026 FMR | FY2027 FMR | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Studio | $2,621 | $2,458 | -6.2% |
| 1 bedroom | $2,982 | $2,778 | -6.8% |
| 2 bedrooms | $3,483 | $3,272 | -6.1% |
| 3 bedrooms | $4,602 | $4,396 | -4.5% |
| 4 bedrooms | $5,010 | $4,688 | -6.4% |

Source: HUD FY2027 and FY2026 FMR files. Every size fell.

What this means for you: under 24 CFR 982.503, a housing authority can set its payment standard at 90% to 110% of the FMR without HUD approval. At 110%, the 2-bedroom ceiling would be $3,599.20, still below Zillow's typical asking rent. Check the Santa Clara County Housing Authority's current standard on the [San Jose fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/san-jose-fair-market-rent-2027).

## Are San Jose rents rising or falling?

San Jose rents are rising fast. Zillow's Observed Rent Index (ZORI, the typical asking rent for all home types) reached a record for the San Jose metro in September 2026.

| Point in time | Zillow rent index | Change |
|---|---|---|
| September 2019 | $3,051 | Before the pandemic |
| September 2025 | $3,519 | +15.3% from 2019 |
| September 2026 | $3,812 | +8.3% in 1 year, a record |

We did the math from the Zillow file. The US index rose 2.7% in the same year, so San Jose grew about 3 times as fast. The September monthly gain was 0.68%.

Yardi Matrix measures apartments only. Its July 2026 report put the San Jose average asking rent at $3,414, up 3.6% year over year and 0.8% over the 3 months through May. Yardi also counted a net gain of 17,400 jobs in the 12 months to February 2026, and unemployment of 3.7% in April.

The gap is the story for owners: market rents rose while HUD's figure fell. The two sources measure different homes over different periods. For your asking rent, use current local listings.

## What vacancy rate does San Jose have?

San Jose has very low vacancy. Yardi Matrix put occupancy in stabilized properties at 96.6% in April 2026, up 0.1 points in a year. That leaves about 3.4% of those units empty.

New supply is thin. Yardi Matrix counted only 283 units completed in 2026 through May.

Watch for: low vacancy does not mean a free hand on price. Many San Jose units have a legal cap on increases, so the first rent you set for a new tenant matters more than in other markets.

## Which local rules affect San Jose rental returns?

City and state caps decide how fast your rent can grow in San Jose. These rules matter most.

| Rule | Which units | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| San Jose Apartment Rent Ordinance (Mun. Code 17.23.310) | Rental units in 3+ unit buildings with a certificate of occupancy from 1979-09-07 or earlier | 5% cap, 1 increase in 12 months; registry required before an increase |
| San Jose Tenant Protection Ordinance (Mun. Code 17.23.1230) | ARO units, other multiple dwellings, guesthouse rooms, unpermitted units | Just cause from the first day of tenancy |
| Cal. Civil Code 1947.12 | Other covered units, not exempt | 5% + cost of living change, max 10%, until 2030-01-01 |
| Cal. Civil Code 1950.5 | All | Deposit of 1 month's rent; 2 months for a small landlord |

Worked example on a $3,812 rent:

| Unit type | Most you can add in 12 months |
|---|---|
| ARO unit (5%) | $190.60 |
| State-capped unit, if the CPI change were 3% (8%) | $304.96 |
| State-capped unit at the 10% ceiling | $381.20 |

The ARO cap is flat at 5% with no CPI link. Check the actual CPI change before you use the state cap. For details on the ARO, the registry and the Ellis Act Ordinance, read San Jose Municipal Code Chapter 17.23.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Should I rent or sell my San Jose home now?

Rent or sell in San Jose depends on your loan rate and how long you plan to hold, because the rent yield is thin. Zillow's home value index (ZHVI) for the San Jose metro is $1,523,615 for September 2026. That is 0.2% more than a year ago and 4.3% below the January 2025 peak.

| Metro | Typical rent (ZORI) | Typical value (ZHVI) | Gross yield |
|---|---|---|---|
| San Jose | $3,812 | $1,523,615 | 3.00% |
| United States | $1,932 | $366,157 | 6.33% |

We did the math from the Zillow September 2026 files. A gross yield is before tax, insurance, repairs, vacancy and management.

Questions to answer before you decide:

- [ ] Is the home a single-family house or condo? Those are often outside the ARO and may be exempt from the state cap with the right notice.
- [ ] Does the rent cover your mortgage, tax, insurance and HOA? An old, low-rate loan helps a lot here.
- [ ] Do you expect rents to keep rising faster than values? Over the last year they did: 8.3% vs 0.2%.
- [ ] Will you manage it yourself, or pay a manager? Check the cost in the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

Strong rent growth and low vacancy favor renting. A 3% gross yield and strict local rules favor selling if you need cash flow now.

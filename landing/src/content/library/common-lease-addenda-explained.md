## What is a lease addendum?

A lease addendum is a separate page that adds to or changes the lease. Both sides sign and date it. It then becomes part of the lease.

Addenda keep the main lease short and let you add rules for one unit or one tenant. They fall into three groups:

| Group | Examples | Why you use it |
|---|---|---|
| Federal | Lead-based paint disclosure (pre-1978 housing) | Federal law requires it |
| State or local | Bed bug notice (California), flood disclosure (Texas), radon notice (Florida) | State law requires it |
| Optional | Pet, smoke-free, mold, early termination, parking, utilities | Makes your rules clear |

Use plain words and the same names as the lease. If the addendum and the lease conflict, say which one controls.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Which addenda are required by federal law?

The lead-based paint disclosure is the one federal addendum most landlords need. It applies to most housing built before 1978.

The EPA says that before a renter signs a lease, the landlord must:

- [ ] Give the renter the pamphlet "Protect Your Family From Lead In Your Home."
- [ ] Disclose any known lead-based paint and lead-based paint hazards in the home.
- [ ] Give all available records and reports, including common areas in multi-unit buildings.
- [ ] Include a "Lead Warning Statement" in the lease or as an attachment.
- [ ] Get signatures and dates of the landlord, any agents and the tenants.

Under 40 CFR 745.113(c), the landlord and any agent must keep the signed form "for no less than 3 years from the commencement of the leasing period."

The EPA lists exceptions. They include zero-bedroom units, leases of 100 days or less, housing for the elderly or disabled (unless a child under 6 lives there), housing certified lead-free by an inspector, and housing built after 1977.

## Which addenda do states require?

States require their own notices, and many of them must be in writing before the lease starts. Three examples:

| State | Notice | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | Bed bug information | Written notice to a prospective tenant before a new tenancy, in at least 10-point type | Civil Code 1954.603 |
| Texas | Flood disclosure | Whether the landlord knows the unit is in a 100-year floodplain, and whether it flooded in the past 5 years; in a separate written document at or before signing | Property Code 92.0135 |
| Florida | Radon gas | A set statement on at least one document signed at or before the rental agreement | Statutes 404.056(5) |

Florida also has an addendum rule for early termination fees. Statutes 83.595(4) requires a separate signed addendum, in a set form, before you can charge liquidated damages or an early termination fee of no more than 2 months' rent.

Check your state and city for others. Common topics are mold, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, utilities, and rent control notices.

## When do I need a pet addendum?

Use a pet addendum whenever you allow a pet. It turns a verbal "yes" into clear rules.

A pet addendum usually covers:

- [ ] The animal: type, breed, name, weight, and a photo.
- [ ] Vaccination and license records, where local law requires them.
- [ ] Pet deposit or fee, within your state's deposit cap.
- [ ] Rules: leash, waste, noise, areas that are off limits.
- [ ] Liability for damage and injury.
- [ ] What happens if the pet breaks the rules.

An assistance animal is not a pet. HUD's rule, 24 CFR 100.204, makes it unlawful to refuse a "reasonable accommodation" in rules or policies when a person with a disability needs it. The rule gives the example of a blind applicant with a seeing eye dog in a no-pets building. Handle these requests under a reasonable accommodation process, not the pet addendum. Ask a local attorney before you apply pet fees to an assistance animal.

## What goes in a smoke-free addendum?

A smoke-free addendum says where smoking is not allowed and what counts as smoking. Private landlords may choose one.

Public housing agencies must have one. HUD's rule, 24 CFR 965.653, requires a policy that bans prohibited tobacco products in all units and interior areas, and in outdoor areas "within 25 feet" of buildings. Prohibited products include cigarettes, cigars, pipes and waterpipes (hookahs).

A private smoke-free addendum can use the same structure:

| Section | What to write |
|---|---|
| Products | Cigarettes, cigars, pipes, hookahs; say whether vaping and cannabis count |
| Places | Units, balconies, common areas, a distance from doors and windows |
| Guests | Tenants are responsible for guests |
| Breach | Notice, then cleaning charges for smoke damage, then lease remedies |

## What goes in a mold addendum?

A mold addendum explains how to prevent mold and how to report water and mold fast. It sets duties for both sides.

A mold addendum usually says:

1. **The tenant ventilates.** Use bath and kitchen fans, and do not block vents.
2. **The tenant reports.** Leaks, condensation and visible mold, in writing, within a set time.
3. **The landlord responds.** Fixes the water source first, then cleans or removes mold.
4. **Inspection at move-in.** Both sides note any existing water damage.

A mold addendum does not let a landlord skip repairs. In New York, for example, the Attorney General says a lease clause that waives the warranty of habitability is void.

## What do HOA fees cover?

HOA fees cover the costs of the shared parts of a community and the association's operations. What they include varies a lot, so read the budget line by line.

Community associations are common. The Foundation for Community Association Research says more than a third (35.2%) of US housing is in a community association. It estimates 373,000 associations with 78.1 million residents.

What HOA and condo dues often pay for:

| Item | Condo (often) | Single-family HOA (often) |
|------|---------------|---------------------------|
| Master insurance on the building | Yes | Rare |
| Exterior and roof upkeep | Yes | Rare |
| Common area landscaping | Yes | Yes |
| Pool, gym, clubhouse | If present | If present |
| Water, sewer, trash | Sometimes | Sometimes |
| Management company fee | Yes | Yes |
| Reserve contribution | Yes | Yes |
| Legal and accounting | Yes | Yes |

Why it matters for a rental: dues that include water, trash or exterior upkeep replace costs you would pay on a house. Compare total costs, not dues alone.

Worked example: effect of dues on cash flow, with example numbers.

| Line | No HOA | HOA $400/mo |
|------|--------|-------------|
| Yearly rent | $26,400 | $26,400 |
| Operating costs without dues | $9,000 | $7,800 (water and exterior in dues) |
| HOA dues | $0 | $4,800 |
| NOI | $17,400 | $13,800 |
| Mortgage | $14,955 | $14,955 |
| Cash flow | $2,445 | −$1,155 |

A $400 fee turned a positive deal negative, even after it replaced $1,200 of other costs.

## What is a special assessment?

A special assessment is an extra charge on owners beyond regular dues. Boards levy one when a large cost comes due and reserves fall short. Common causes are roof replacement, structural repair, paving, elevator work, storm damage and legal claims.

How it hits a rental owner, example numbers:

| Assessment | Your share | Paid as | Effect |
|------------|-----------|---------|--------|
| $1.2 million roof and facade project | $15,000 | Lump sum | Wipes out about 6 years of a $2,445 yearly cash flow |
| Same project | $15,000 | $250 a month for 60 months | Monthly cash flow falls by $250 |

Rules can force larger reserves. Florida is one example. Section 718.112 requires a residential condominium association to complete a structural integrity reserve study at least every 10 years. The rule applies to each building of 3 habitable stories or higher. The law requires reserves for the items it lists, based on the study. Boards may fund required work with a special assessment, a line of credit or a loan. Other states have their own rules. Check yours.

Watch for these signs of a coming assessment:

- **Low reserve balance** compared with the reserve study's target.
- **Deferred projects** in the minutes ("we will revisit the roof next year").
- **Engineering reports** on structure, balconies or garages.
- **Rising insurance** on the master policy.
- **Lawsuits** naming the association.

## How do I read the HOA reserve study?

A reserve study is the association's plan for big repairs. It lists each shared component, its remaining life, its replacement cost, and how much the association should save each year. Read it before you buy, and each year after.

What to look for, step by step:

1. **Date of the study.** An old study can miss rising costs.
2. **Component list.** Roof, paving, siding, elevators, pool, boilers. Note each remaining life.
3. **Near-term items.** Anything due in the next 5 years.
4. **Funded share.** The study compares current reserves with the ideal balance. A large gap means a higher chance of an assessment or a dues increase.
5. **Recommended contribution.** Compare it with what the budget actually puts into reserves.
6. **Board response.** Check the minutes to see if the board follows the study.

Documents to request:

- [ ] Current budget and last year's actual results
- [ ] Reserve study and any updates
- [ ] 12 months of board minutes
- [ ] Any approved or proposed special assessment
- [ ] Master insurance policy summary
- [ ] Rules on leasing: minimum term, caps, registration
- [ ] List of pending lawsuits

## Are HOA fees tax deductible on a rental?

Regular dues are generally deductible on a rental. Special assessments for improvements are not deducted, but you may recover them through depreciation. IRS Publication 527 sets out the rule for condominiums you rent:

| Payment | Tax treatment on a rental (Publication 527) |
|---------|---------------------------------------------|
| Dues or assessments for maintenance of the common elements | Deductible as rental expenses |
| Special assessments for improvements | Not deductible; you may recover your share through depreciation |

Publication 527 also says you can deduct, as rental expenses, all the usual expenses in its chapters 1 and 2 for a rented condo. If the home is your own residence, the dues are personal, and the rental rules do not apply.

Keep the assessment notice. It shows what the money was for, which decides the tax treatment.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## How to protect your cash flow from HOA surprises

- [ ] Put the full dues in your deal analysis, plus an expected yearly increase.
- [ ] Read the reserve study and the last 12 months of minutes before you buy.
- [ ] Ask the manager or board in writing about planned assessments.
- [ ] Keep a cash reserve for an assessment, not only for your own unit.
- [ ] Check leasing rules every year; boards can change them.
- [ ] Track dues as a separate line in your books.

If you hire a manager, check that it handles HOA notices for you and passes violations to the tenant.

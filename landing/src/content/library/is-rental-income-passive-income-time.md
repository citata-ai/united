## How many hours does one rental take per year?

A rental has no fixed annual workload. A newer home with a stable tenant can have quiet months. A failed water line can consume the next weekend. Neither case tells you what the following year will require.

Treat your time estimate as a property budget. List the work, assign a person, and track the actual time. Include calls, travel, document review, and decisions. Those tasks count even when you never touch a repair tool.

Use this planning log. The categories are a suggested work plan, not a survey of landlord hours.

| Work category | What to record | Who can help |
|---|---|---|
| Rent and books | Payment checks, receipts, bank review | Bookkeeper or software |
| Tenant contact | Questions, notices, repair updates | Property manager |
| Maintenance | Diagnosis, bids, access, completion | Licensed trade contractor |
| Turnover | Inspection, repairs, ads, showings | Leasing agent or manager |
| Owner oversight | Budget, insurance, taxes, approvals | Owner with professional advice |

Record time when the work occurs. A year-end guess tends to miss short calls and repeated follow-ups. Keep separate notes for time spent learning the business. Your next rental may require less learning but more oversight.

## Which landlord tasks take the most time?

Turnover and unusual repairs deserve separate planning because they involve many linked tasks. A move-out can require access, condition records, contractor work, pricing, screening, and a new lease. A delay in a small task can delay the whole process.

Build a turnover plan before the current lease ends. Identify who receives notice, who inspects, and who approves spending. Ask your local adviser which deadlines apply. Do not copy a deposit deadline from another state.

Routine work also takes attention. Rent collection requires more than seeing a bank deposit. You need to identify the payment, record its purpose, and match the tenant ledger. Keep deposits separate from earned rent in your records.

IRS Publication 527 explains that a refundable security deposit generally is not income when received if you plan to return it. Advance rent follows a different rule. These tax labels show why a payment tool alone cannot replace sound records.

Maintenance has both planned and urgent work. Keep service records and equipment details in a shared folder. Record the location of shutoff valves. Give the tenant a clear way to report urgent problems without waiting for an email reply.

## How does a property manager change owner hours?

A property manager can take over tenant contact, local visits, and contractor coordination. The written agreement sets the actual scope. Ask about nights, weekends, vacant homes, and work outside the base fee.

Your remaining work should be clear. Review statements, approve major projects, keep funds available, and check insurance. Ask for invoices when a line item lacks detail. A monthly distribution does not prove the property has enough repair cash.

Compare managers by the tasks they accept, not by a promise of passive income. Request a sample statement and a sample repair record. Ask who checks completed work and how the tenant receives updates.

Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to model quoted fees. Add leasing, renewal, inspection, and project charges when the contract includes them. Keep these inputs separate from your estimate of saved hours.

A local manager can help most when distance makes small tasks hard. Yet poor reporting can create more owner work. Choose someone whose process you can inspect without making repeated requests.

## What does passive mean for the IRS?

Passive is a tax term, not a promise about your calendar. IRS Publication 925 says rental activities generally remain passive even when you materially participate. Specific exceptions can change that result.

Under Publication 925, real estate professional status has separate tests. More than half of your personal services must be in qualifying real property businesses where you materially participate. You must also perform more than 750 hours in those businesses. Material participation in the rental activity still matters.

Do not treat the hour threshold as a target for an ordinary rental budget. It is a legal test with detailed rules. A time log that helps management may need more detail for a tax claim.

Publication 925 also describes a special allowance for some rental real estate losses with active participation. Income, filing status, ownership, and other limits affect that allowance. Ask a tax professional before counting a loss against wages.

IRS Publication 527 explains ordinary rental income and expense reporting. Read it alongside Publication 925 when your rental shows a tax loss. A tax loss does not mean the property has no cash, and positive cash does not prove every deduction is allowed.

## How should you test whether the work fits your life?

Run a task audit before you renew your commitment to self-management. Start with the work you cannot defer during your normal job. Then test whether your backup person can act when you cannot.

- **List open tasks.** Include overdue repairs, records, renewals, and tenant requests.
- **Assign a local contact.** Confirm authority, access, payment, and backup coverage.
- **Set approval rules.** Define who can act during an urgent repair.
- **Review the time log.** Compare routine work with turnover work separately.
- **Price the alternative.** Get a manager's full written proposal for this property.

Choose the arrangement you can sustain during a difficult month. A plan that works only when the home stays quiet needs revision. Use the [guide library](/guides) for related rental planning, then write down the tasks you will keep.

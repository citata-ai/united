Most owners hire a manager after one bad week. A tenant calls at night about a leak. Another applicant asks a question you are not sure you can answer. Rent is late, and you do not know the next legal step. A good manager takes those weeks off your plate.

This guide lists what a full-service manager does, phase by phase, and what stays with you.

## Which Tasks Are in Full-Service Management?

Full-service management covers the rental from listing to move-out. The exact list is in your management agreement. This table shows the common tasks:

| Phase | Tasks a full-service manager usually does |
|---|---|
| Leasing | Sets rent from comps, photos, listings, showings, applications, screening, lease signing |
| Move-in | Collects deposit and first rent, move-in inspection with photos, keys |
| Rent | Collects rent, sends late notices, starts legal steps when the owner approves |
| Maintenance | Takes repair requests, sends vendors, runs emergencies, routine inspections |
| Money | Holds deposits and rent in trust accounts, pays bills, sends owner payouts |
| Compliance | Follows landlord-tenant, fair housing and screening laws |
| Renewal and move-out | Renewal offers, move-out inspection, deposit accounting, turnover |
| Reports | Monthly statement, yearly 1099, year-end summary |

Licensing is part of the job in many states. Florida Statutes 475.01 defines a broker to include a person who rents property for another for pay. Texas Occupations Code 1101.002 lists "controls the acceptance or deposit of rent" from a single-family tenant as a broker act. Check your manager's license with your state's real estate commission.

## What Does a Manager Do in a Vacancy?

In a vacancy, the manager's job is to find a qualified tenant fast, at market rent, within the law. A typical sequence:

1. **Price the home** from 3 or more similar rentals nearby.
2. **Prepare the home**: cleaning, small repairs, safety checks, photos.
3. **List the home** on rental sites and the manager's own site.
4. **Show the home**, in person or by self-guided tour.
5. **Screen applicants** with written criteria the owner approved.
6. **Sign the lease** and collect funds.

Screening has rules. The FTC says landlords who use consumer reports must follow the Fair Credit Reporting Act. If an applicant is denied based partly on a report, the applicant gets an adverse action notice. The Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. 3604, bans discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status or national origin. A good manager uses the same criteria for every applicant.

## How Does a Manager Handle Repairs and Emergencies?

A manager runs repairs within a spending limit you set in the agreement. Below the limit, the manager acts and reports. Above it, the manager asks you first, except in an emergency.

| Repair type | Example | Usual process |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency | No heat in winter, gas smell, major leak | Manager acts at once, tells you after |
| Routine, under the limit | Clogged drain, broken handle | Manager acts, cost shows on statement |
| Routine, over the limit | Water heater replacement | Manager sends a quote, waits for your OK |
| Capital project | Roof, HVAC system | Manager gets 2 or 3 bids, you decide |

Ask how the manager picks vendors, whether it adds a markup, and who answers calls at night. Those three answers tell you a lot about the service.

## What Reports Does the Owner Get Each Month?

You should get a monthly owner statement and a yearly tax form. The monthly statement usually shows:

- **Rent collected** and any late fees.
- **Expenses paid**, with invoices for repairs.
- **Management fees** charged.
- **Reserve held**, if the agreement sets one.
- **Net payout** to your bank account.

The yearly form is set by the IRS. The Form 1099-MISC instructions say the property manager must use Form 1099-MISC to report the rent paid over to the owner.

Deposits sit in a separate account in many states. Florida Statutes 83.49 says the landlord or agent must hold deposits in a separate account in a Florida bank, without mixing them with other funds, or post a surety bond. Ask your manager where your tenant's deposit sits.

Keep the statements. IRS Publication 527 lists management fees, repairs, insurance and taxes as common rental expenses, and your statements prove them.

## What Does a Manager Not Do?

A manager runs the rental, but some jobs stay with you or other experts:

- **Tax advice.** A manager gives you records. A tax professional tells you what to deduct.
- **Legal advice.** A manager follows standard steps. An attorney handles a lawsuit or a complex eviction.
- **Big financial choices.** Selling, refinancing and major renovations are your decisions.
- **Insurance.** You buy and keep the landlord policy. The manager may need to be named on it.
- **Guarantees.** No manager can guarantee rent, zero vacancy or zero damage.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What is a property manager trust or escrow account?

A trust or escrow account holds money for others instead of treating the money as the manager's own business cash.

Start with the account's purpose and records, rather than its marketing name. Ask whose money the manager receives, who controls payment, and which ledger records each balance. A pooled bank balance cannot show your property's available cash by itself.

California's Department of Real Estate gives a useful state-specific example in its October 6, 2026 trust fund guidance. DRE describes a control record and separate records for each beneficiary or transaction. The guidance also explains reconciliation between those records and the adjusted bank balance.

That California guidance is not a national rule. Ask your state's real estate regulator which requirements apply to your manager. Deposit laws may add duties separate from broker trust rules. Your ownership of the property does not automatically make a tenant deposit available for your expenses.

| Money type | Record to request | Question to answer |
|---|---|---|
| Collected rent | Property income ledger | Has payment cleared? |
| Owner reserve | Owner balance and agreement | What spending needs approval? |
| Tenant deposit | Deposit ledger and lease | Who holds the tenant's money? |
| Management fee | Contract and statement entry | Does the charge match the agreement? |
| Vendor payment | Invoice and payment record | What work supports the charge? |

Ask the manager to explain the entire path of a rent payment. The explanation should connect the receipt, ledger, expenses, and owner distribution.

## When does the owner receive rent each month?

Owner payment timing follows the agreement and applicable rules, so confirm the schedule before you sign.

Do not assume rent arrives in your account when the tenant submits it. Collection, returned payments, bills, and reserve needs can affect the amount available. Ask the manager which events delay a distribution and how the manager reports a delay.

Request a sample statement that matches the proposed payment schedule. Find the opening balance, income, charges, reserve, and closing balance. Ask whether the statement covers cleared funds or includes pending receipts. The distinction matters when your mortgage payment is due.

Use an illustrative statement to test your understanding. Assume opening owner funds of $400 and collected rent of $2,000. Assume expenses of $300 and a retained reserve of $500. The owner payment would be $1,600. These are invented planning figures, not a required reserve or industry fee.

Ask who resolves a missing distribution and how you reach that person. Keep the agreed bank details in a secure channel. Treat an unexpected request to change payment instructions as something to verify directly.

A manager should explain an unusual charge in terms you can trace to a receipt or agreement. Request the supporting record when the statement is unclear. Avoid allowing unexplained entries to accumulate across reporting periods.

## What is an owner reserve and who can spend it?

An owner reserve is money retained for property costs under your management agreement, subject to applicable handling rules.

The reserve is different from the manager's earned fee. It also differs from a tenant deposit. Ask the manager to show each category separately. A single line called funds held can hide duties you need to understand.

Choose the reserve amount using the property and service plan. Consider utility bills, urgent repairs, recurring vendors, and the speed of owner funding. Do not select an amount just because another owner uses it. Ask what happens if a bill exceeds available owner funds.

Set approval terms in writing. Describe routine work, urgent work, and work that needs your approval. Ask how the manager contacts you and records the decision. Confirm what happens when you cannot respond during an urgent repair.

Keep reserves separate from expected profit in your own budget. Money retained for future repairs is still unavailable for personal spending. Compare the retained balance with open work orders before requesting a withdrawal.

Use the [fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to compare management charges, then add your planned reserve needs separately. A reserve transfer is not automatically an expense. Your books should distinguish cash movement from the cost of actual work.

## How can I check that my money is safe?

Check the manager's authority, records, reconciliation process, and exit procedure before relying on a portal balance.

California DRE's guidance describes a reconciliation that compares separate client records with the control record. It then compares the result with the adjusted bank balance. Ask your manager to explain the applicable process for your state. You need a clear answer even if privacy rules limit what other clients' records you can see.

Check the firm's license through the relevant official regulator where a license is required. Confirm the legal name on your agreement matches the firm that receives money. Ask about staff authority and review controls for payments.

Request sample documents before onboarding. A useful packet includes an owner statement, invoice example, deposit record, and explanation of the reserve. Ask who investigates mismatches and how corrected statements reach you.

Read termination terms while the relationship is still new. Identify how money, deposits, leases, and vendor records move to a successor. Require a closing statement and a clear list of open obligations. Ask a local attorney which transfer duties the law adds.

If a balance looks wrong, preserve statements, receipts, and messages. Send a specific written question that names the transaction. Ask for a correction or explanation supported by records. Use your regulator's complaint process when the facts warrant it.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

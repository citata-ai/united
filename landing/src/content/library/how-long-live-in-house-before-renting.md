## What is the 12-month occupancy rule?

The 12-month occupancy rule is the promise in your mortgage to live in the home for at least 1 year. It comes from section 6 of the Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac uniform security instrument, used for most conventional loans.

The Illinois version (Form 3014) says the borrower must occupy and use the property as a principal residence within 60 days after signing. The borrower must continue to live there for at least 1 year after the move-in date. Two exceptions apply: the lender agrees in writing (the form says consent will not be unreasonably withheld), or extenuating circumstances beyond the borrower's control exist.

Your clock, step by step:

| Step | Rule | Example date |
|------|------|--------------|
| Closing | Clock for move-in starts | 2026-04-10 |
| Move-in deadline | Within 60 days | 2026-06-09 |
| Actual move-in | Your date | 2026-05-01 |
| 1-year mark | 1 year after move-in | 2027-05-01 |
| Earliest lease start without consent | On or after the 1-year mark | 2027-05-01 |

Each state has its own version of the form. Read section 6 in the copy you signed.

Watch for: the promise is made at closing. Section 8 of the same form adds a risk. A false statement about intended occupancy in your loan application is a default.

## Do FHA, VA and conventional loans differ?

Yes. All 3 are for homes you live in, but each states the rule in its own way.

| Loan | Rule | Source |
|------|------|--------|
| Conventional (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) | Move in within 60 days; live there at least 1 year | Uniform security instrument, section 6 |
| FHA | A principal residence is where you live and spend most of the calendar year; you can have only one at a time | 24 CFR 203.18(f)(1) |
| VA | You certify at application and closing that you intend to occupy the home, and move in within a reasonable time | 38 U.S.C. 3704(c) |

Notes on each:

- **FHA.** FHA loan papers set the exact occupancy term. Ask your servicer for it before you plan a move.
- **VA.** If you are on active duty and cannot occupy the home, the statute lets a spouse meet the occupancy rule. In some cases a dependent child can.
- **Conventional.** Fannie Mae's Selling Guide treats an active-duty service member away on military service as an owner occupant. The orders must show the absence.

A home bought with an investment property loan has no occupancy rule. It also costs more to finance.

## What counts as a valid reason to move early?

A valid reason is one beyond your control, or any reason your lender accepts in writing. The uniform instrument names "extenuating circumstances" but does not list them. In practice, owners ask about these cases:

| Reason | Likely beyond your control? | What to keep |
|--------|-----------------------------|--------------|
| Employer transfers you to another city | Usually yes | Transfer letter |
| Military orders | Yes; also covered by specific rules | Orders |
| Serious illness or care for a family member | Usually yes | Doctor's letter |
| Divorce or death in the family | Often yes | Court or death records |
| New job you chose in another city | Less clear | Offer letter; ask the lender |
| You want a bigger house | No | Wait for the 1-year mark |
| You found a tenant at a good rent | No | Wait for the 1-year mark |

What to do:

1. **Write to your servicer** before you move. Explain the reason and the date.
2. **Attach proof,** such as a transfer letter or orders.
3. **Ask for written consent** to rent the home.
4. **Keep paying on time.** Late payments make every request harder.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Does the waiting time affect taxes?

Yes. The IRS clock for the home sale exclusion is separate from your lender's clock, and it is longer.

IRS Publication 523 says you may exclude up to $250,000 of gain, or $500,000 if married filing jointly, if you meet the eligibility test. The core of the test: you owned the home and lived in it as your main home for 24 months (2 years). Both must fall in the 5 years before the sale.

How the 2 clocks fit together, example dates:

| Event | Date | Lender clock | IRS clock |
|-------|------|--------------|-----------|
| Move in | 2026-05-01 | Starts | Use as home starts |
| 1-year mark | 2027-05-01 | Met; you may rent | 1 year of use |
| You keep living there | to 2028-05-01 | Not needed | 2 years of use met |
| You move out and rent it | 2028-05-01 | Not needed | Rental period starts |
| Latest sale to keep the 2-of-5 test | 2031-05-01 | Not needed | The 5 years before the sale still hold your 2 years of use |

So you can rent the home for up to 3 years after you move out and still meet the use test. If you move out at the 1-year mark instead, you have only 1 year of use. You would need to move back, or qualify for a partial exclusion, to exclude gain.

Partial exclusion. Publication 523 allows a partial exclusion in 3 cases. The main reason for the sale must be a change in workplace location, a health issue, or an unforeseeable event. A work-related move qualifies when, for example, the new job is at least 50 miles farther from the home than the old one.

Two more tax rules once you rent:

- **Depreciation.** IRS Publication 527 sets the basis for depreciation of a home you convert to a rental. It is the lesser of its fair market value or adjusted basis on the conversion date.
- **Recapture.** Publication 523 says you cannot exclude gain equal to depreciation allowed or allowable after May 6, 1997. That part is taxed when you sell.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

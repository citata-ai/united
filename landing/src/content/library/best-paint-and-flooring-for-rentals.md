## What should I compare?

Compare paint and flooring by cost per year of life. A cheap floor that lasts 5 years can cost more than a mid-price floor that lasts 20. The formula is simple:

**Cost per year = installed cost ÷ years of life in a rental**

Add one more factor: turnover work. A finish that needs a full redo at each move-out adds labor and vacant days. A finish you can clean and touch up saves both.

## Flat, eggshell or satin paint?

For rental walls, eggshell or satin is the usual choice. Flat paint hides flaws well but marks easily and does not scrub. Satin and eggshell wipe clean and still look even after a touch-up.

| Finish | Where to use it | Cleans well | Hides flaws | Touch-up blends |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flat / matte | Ceilings | Poor | Best | Good |
| Eggshell | Living areas, bedrooms | Fair to good | Good | Good |
| Satin | Halls, kids' rooms, kitchens | Good | Fair | Fair |
| Semi-gloss | Trim, doors, bathrooms | Best | Poor | Poor |

These are general traits. Check the label of the product you buy for washability and recoat time.

**Pick one wall color and one sheen** for all your units. Write the brand, color code and sheen in a file. Then a touch-up between tenants matches, and you buy paint in bulk.

InterNACHI says surface preparation and paint quality are the main factors in how long paint lasts. Sun and high humidity shorten its life. Good prep beats expensive paint.

## LVP vs carpet vs tile

Vinyl plank (LVP) and tile usually beat carpet in a rental. They handle spills, pets and heavy use, and they clean fast at turnover. Carpet costs less to put in but wears out sooner.

InterNACHI lists these life spans for flooring:

| Flooring | Life (InterNACHI) | Water resistance | Turnover work | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Carpet | 8 to 10 years | Poor | Deep clean or replace | Stains and odor from pets |
| Laminate | 15 to 25 years | Fair; check the product | Clean | Can swell if wet |
| Vinyl | 25 years | Good | Clean | InterNACHI lists "vinyl"; plank quality varies |
| Tile | 75 to 100 years | Best | Clean grout | Higher install cost; can crack |
| Engineered wood | 50+ years | Fair | Clean; refinish over time | Scratches |

InterNACHI says flooring life depends on maintenance and foot traffic. A rental with frequent turnovers and pets wears floors faster than an owner-occupied home. Treat the chart as an upper range.

## How long does each last in a rental?

Each option lasts less in a rental than the chart says, because use is heavier. Plan with a lower number for your budget. For example, if InterNACHI says carpet lasts 8 to 10 years, plan for the low end, or less if you allow pets.

Signs a floor is at the end of its life:

- [ ] Stains that do not come out after a professional clean.
- [ ] Odor that comes back after cleaning.
- [ ] Planks that lift, gap or swell.
- [ ] Cracked tiles or loose grout.
- [ ] Wear paths in halls and doorways.

Track the install date of each floor in each room. Then you can plan replacements during a turnover, not in the middle of a lease.

## Cost per year of life table

The table below uses example installed costs for a 1,000-square-foot unit. They are not national averages. Get local quotes, then fill in your own numbers.

| Option | Example installed cost | Planning life in a rental | Cost per year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carpet | $3,500 | 7 years | $500 |
| Laminate | $5,000 | 15 years | $333 |
| Vinyl plank | $5,500 | 20 years | $275 |
| Tile | $10,000 | 40 years | $250 |

In this example, vinyl plank and tile cost the least per year. Carpet costs the least up front but the most per year. Turnover cleaning adds to the gap: carpet needs a professional clean at each move-out, and hard floors need a mop.

Tile wins on cost per year only if you keep the property long enough. If you plan to sell in 7 years, the extra up-front cost of tile may not pay back. Use your own hold period.

**Taxes:** IRS Publication 527 lists carpeting used in residential rental real estate as 5-year property for depreciation. Other flooring may be treated differently. This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current rules or ask a tax professional.

## What about lead paint in older rentals?

Lead paint rules apply when you work on paint in housing built before 1978. The EPA says about three-quarters of U.S. homes built before 1978 still have some lead-based paint.

The EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) rule requires anyone paid to do work that disturbs painted surfaces in pre-1978 homes to be certified. Their workers must be trained in lead-safe work practices. The rule generally does not apply to homeowners working on their own home, but rental work by paid crews is covered.

Before you sand, scrape or remove old flooring glued over painted surfaces in an older rental:

1. **Check the build year.**
2. **Hire an RRP-certified firm** for paid work that disturbs paint.
3. **Keep the records** of the work.
4. **Give the lead disclosure** to new tenants where the EPA disclosure rule applies.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What is a good default spec?

A good default spec keeps every unit the same, so repairs are fast and parts match.

- **Walls:** one neutral color, eggshell.
- **Ceilings:** flat white.
- **Trim and doors:** semi-gloss white.
- **Bathrooms:** satin or semi-gloss.
- **Living areas and bedrooms:** vinyl plank, one color.
- **Bathrooms and entries:** tile or vinyl.
- **Spare stock:** one box of planks and one gallon of each paint, stored on site.

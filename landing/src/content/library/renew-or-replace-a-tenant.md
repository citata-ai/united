## What does a tenant turnover cost?

A tenant turnover costs a vacant month or more, plus cleaning, repairs and leasing costs. The vacant month is often the biggest line. Each owner's numbers differ, so build the cost from your own records, not from a national average.

The U.S. Census Bureau reported a national rental vacancy rate of 7.3% for Q2 2026 (FRED). That rate tells you the market is not tight everywhere. In a slow local market, a unit can sit empty longer than you plan.

Turnover cost lines to track:

| Cost line | What it covers | Where to find your number |
|---|---|---|
| Vacancy | Rent lost from move-out to the new lease start | Your rent roll, days vacant |
| Cleaning | Deep clean, carpet clean | Vendor invoices |
| Paint and repairs | Touch-up or full paint, small fixes | Work orders |
| Leasing | Listing, showings, screening, leasing fee | Your manager's fee schedule |
| Utilities | Power, water and gas while vacant | Utility bills |
| Your time | Showings, calls, move-in | Your hours times your rate |

If you pay a manager, the leasing fee is a fixed line. Use our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to see that cost for your rent.

## How many months of higher rent pay back a vacancy?

The payback period equals the turnover cost divided by the monthly rent gain. The formula is short:

Payback months = turnover cost ÷ (new market rent − renewal rent)

A small rent gap makes the payback very long. A large gap makes it short. That is why the size of the gap decides most cases.

## Worked comparison table

This example uses assumed numbers to show the method. Replace each one with your own.

Assumptions (example only):

- Current rent: $1,800 a month.
- Renewal offer: 3% raise, so $1,854 a month.
- Market rent for a new tenant: $1,950 a month.
- Vacancy if the tenant leaves: 1 month.
- Clean and paint at turnover: $1,200.
- Leasing fee: 50% of 1 month's rent, so $975.
- Light work at renewal: $200.

| Line, first 12 months | Renew | Replace |
|---|---|---|
| Months of rent collected | 12 | 11 |
| Monthly rent | $1,854 | $1,950 |
| Rent collected | $22,248 | $21,450 |
| Make-ready cost | −$200 | −$1,200 |
| Leasing fee | $0 | −$975 |
| Net for the year | $22,048 | $19,275 |
| Difference | +$2,773 for renew | |

Now the payback:

- Turnover cost = 1 vacant month ($1,950) + $1,200 + $975 = $4,125.
- Monthly gain = $1,950 − $1,854 = $96.
- Payback = $4,125 ÷ $96 = about 43 months.

In this example, the new tenant must stay about 3.5 years before the owner comes out ahead. Many tenants do not stay that long. If the vacancy runs 2 months, the payback grows to about 63 months.

Change one input and run it again. If market rent were $2,250, the gap would be $396 a month. Turnover cost would be $2,250 + $1,200 + $1,125 = $4,575. Payback would be about 12 months, and replacing could make sense.

## When is non-renewal the right call?

Non-renewal is the right call when the tenant costs more than the turnover. Money is only one part of the test. Use written criteria and apply them to every tenant the same way, so the decision stays within the Fair Housing Act.

Reasons that often justify non-renewal:

1. **Repeated late rent.** Set a number in your policy, such as late in 3 of the last 12 months.
2. **Lease violations.** Unauthorized occupants, pets or noise after written warnings.
3. **Damage.** Damage beyond normal wear found at the renewal check.
4. **Major work.** A full remodel that needs the unit empty.
5. **Large rent gap.** The payback math above is short, often under a year.

Check the law before you act. In California, Civil Code 1946.2 requires just cause to end a tenancy once a tenant has lived in the unit for 12 months, for units the statute covers. A substantial remodel can be a no-fault just cause, but only when the work needs the unit empty for at least 30 days. The owner then owes relocation help equal to 1 month's rent.

In New York, Real Property Law 226-C requires 30, 60 or 90 days of notice when a landlord does not plan to renew, based on how long the tenant has lived there. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I make the renewal offer win?

A renewal offer wins when it is fair, early and easy to sign. Send it 90 to 120 days before the lease ends, so you have time to list the unit if the tenant says no.

Renewal checklist:

- [ ] Pull the payment history for the last 12 months.
- [ ] Do a short unit check, with the notice your state requires.
- [ ] Price the market rent with 3 to 5 nearby listings.
- [ ] Run the payback math above.
- [ ] Send a written offer with the new rent and the date.
- [ ] Offer one small upgrade if the gap is small, such as a new appliance.
- [ ] Set a reply date and a follow-up reminder.

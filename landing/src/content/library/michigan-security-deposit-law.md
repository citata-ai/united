## Michigan security deposit rules at a glance

Michigan's security deposit rules are in 1972 Public Act 348 (MCL 554.601 to 554.616). Here are the limits and deadlines, from the 2025 Michigan Compiled Laws (read on Justia, 2026-10-19).

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | 1.5 months' rent | 554.602 |
| Written notice to the tenant (landlord, bank, forwarding address duty) | Within 14 days after move-in | 554.603 |
| Where the money goes | Regulated financial institution, or bond with the Secretary of State | 554.604 |
| Move-in inventory checklist | Give 2 blank copies; tenant returns 1 within 7 days | 554.608(2), (3) |
| Tenant gives a forwarding address | Within 4 days after move-out | 554.611 |
| Itemized list of damages plus balance | Mailed within 30 days after move-out | 554.609 |
| Tenant answers the list | Within 7 days, by mail | 554.612 |
| Landlord sues to keep disputed damages | Within 45 days after move-out | 554.613(1) |
| Penalty for not complying | Waive damages; pay double the amount kept | 554.613(2) |
| Interest on the deposit | No rule in the act | n/a |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Michigan?

The maximum security deposit in Michigan is 1.5 months' rent. MCL 554.602 says a deposit "shall not exceed 1 1/2 months' rent" for each rental unit.

The definition is wide. Under MCL 554.601(d), a security deposit includes:

- any required prepaid rent other than the first full rental period;
- any rent in a period above the average rent for the term;
- any other money returnable if the tenant leaves the unit in the required condition.

So "last month's rent" collected at move-in counts toward the 1.5-month cap. Here is a worked check for a $1,400 monthly rent:

| Item collected at move-in | Counts toward cap? | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| First month's rent | No (first full rental period) | $1,400 |
| Security deposit | Yes | $1,400 |
| Prepaid last month's rent | Yes | $1,400 |
| Total that counts | | $2,800 |
| Cap (1.5 x $1,400) | | $2,100 |

In this example, you are $700 over the cap. Lower the deposit to $700 or drop the prepaid rent.

You must also give a written notice within 14 days after the tenant takes possession (MCL 554.603). It names you and your address, and the bank or surety that holds the deposit. It also states the tenant's duty to give a forwarding address within 4 days after move-out. That warning is in 12-point bold type, at least 4 points larger than the body text. If you do not give this notice, the tenant does not need to send you a forwarding address.

## How many days does a Michigan landlord have to return a security deposit?

A Michigan landlord has 30 days after the tenant moves out. MCL 554.609 says you must mail the itemized list of damages within 30 days after termination of occupancy. A check or money order for the balance goes with the list.

If you miss the 30 days, MCL 554.610 treats it as your agreement that no damages are due. You must then return the full deposit at once.

There is one exception. Say the tenant gives no written forwarding address within 4 days after move-out. Then you do not have to send the notice of damages (MCL 554.611). The tenant can still claim the deposit later.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Michigan?

A Michigan landlord can deduct only 2 types of cost. MCL 554.607 limits use of the deposit to:

1. **Actual damage** to the unit or a related facility that is the direct result of conduct "not reasonably expected in the normal course of habitation".
2. **Money owed:** rent in arrears, rent due because the tenant ended the lease early, and utility bills the tenant did not pay.

You also may not charge for damage listed on an earlier termination checklist from before this tenant moved in (MCL 554.609).

The inventory checklists are your proof. MCL 554.608 requires a move-in checklist and a move-out checklist for each unit. The checklist covers carpeting, appliances, walls, paint, doors, plumbing and electrical fixtures, and more. At move-out, you list all damage you claim the tenant caused.

## Does a Michigan landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes, a Michigan landlord must mail an itemized list of deductions. Under MCL 554.609, the list must show:

- [ ] each damaged item and the estimated cost to repair it;
- [ ] the amounts and the reasons for each charge;
- [ ] a check or money order for the balance of the deposit;
- [ ] this sentence, in 12-point bold type at least 4 points larger than the body: "You must respond to this notice by mail within 7 days after receipt of same, otherwise you will forfeit the amount claimed for damages."

The tenant then has 7 days to answer by mail with agreement or disagreement (MCL 554.612). The mailing date counts as the date of the answer.

If the tenant disputes a charge, MCL 554.613(1) gives you 45 days after move-out to sue for a money judgment. Without a judgment, you may keep a disputed amount only if one of these is true:

- the tenant gave no forwarding address;
- the tenant did not answer the notice of damages within 7 days;
- you and the tenant agreed in writing;
- the amount is only unpaid rent for a period the tenant had possession.

## Must a Michigan landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

The Michigan Security Deposit Act has no interest rule. We read MCL 554.602 to 554.616 and found no duty to pay interest.

The act does control where the money goes. MCL 554.604(1) says the deposit "shall be deposited in a regulated financial institution". You may use the money for any purpose only if you post a cash or surety bond with the Secretary of State. The bond must cover all deposits up to $50,000, plus 25% of any amount above $50,000.

The deposit is the tenant's property until you establish a right to it (MCL 554.605). If you sell the building, you stay liable for the deposit until one of 3 things happens (MCL 554.614). You transfer it to the buyer and notify the tenant by mail. The buyer complies with the bond or bank rule. Or you return the deposit.

## What is the penalty for a Michigan landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

The penalty is double the amount you kept. MCL 554.613(2) says that failure to comply fully with the section "constitutes waiver of all claimed damages". It also makes you "liable to the tenant for double the amount of the security deposit retained".

Tenants cannot waive these rules in the lease (MCL 554.606). The Attorney General or any affected person can sue to enforce the act (MCL 554.615).

A worked example: you keep $900 of a $1,500 deposit for carpet damage. The tenant disputes it on time. You do not sue within 45 days. You then waive the $900 claim and owe the tenant $1,800.

The act took effect on 1973-04-01. We found no 2024 to 2026 amendment to the deposit sections (554.602 to 554.616) in the history notes.

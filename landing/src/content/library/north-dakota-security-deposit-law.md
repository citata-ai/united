North Dakota puts almost all of its deposit rules in one statute: N.D. Cent. Code section 47-16-07.1. I read the official chapter 47-16 text on 2026-10-19. The ndlegis.gov server did not answer from our network, so I read the Internet Archive copy of the same PDF, captured 2026-10-03.

## North Dakota security deposit rules at a glance

The table below sums up each rule, its limit or deadline, and the statute that sets it.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Standard deposit cap | 1 month's rent | N.D. Cent. Code 47-16-07.1(1) |
| Higher cap, felony conviction | Up to 2 months' rent | 47-16-07.1(1)(a) |
| Higher cap, prior judgment for breaking a rental agreement | Up to 2 months' rent | 47-16-07.1(1)(b) |
| Pet deposit | Greater of $2,500 or 2 months' rent | 47-16-07.1(2) |
| Where the money goes | Federally insured, interest-bearing savings or checking account | 47-16-07.1(1) |
| Interest to the tenant | Required, except when occupancy was under 9 months | 47-16-07.1(3) |
| Return or itemize | Within 30 days after lease end and delivery of possession | 47-16-07.1(3) |
| Penalty for bad withholding | Treble damages | 47-16-07.1(4) |
| Move-in condition statement | Signed by landlord and tenant | 47-16-07.2 |
| Sale of the building | Deposit and interest move to the new owner | 47-16-07.1(5) |

## What is the maximum security deposit in North Dakota?

The maximum security deposit in North Dakota is 1 month's rent. Section 47-16-07.1(1) says a landlord may not demand or receive security "in an amount or value in excess of one month's rent". The words "however denominated" mean a new label does not avoid the cap. A "move-in fee" that works as security still counts.

The statute allows 2 exceptions, each up to 2 months' rent:

1. **Felony conviction.** A landlord may accept up to 2 months' rent from a person convicted of a felony, as an incentive to rent to that person.
2. **Prior rental judgment.** A landlord may demand up to 2 months' rent from a person with a judgment against them for breaking a previous rental agreement.

A pet deposit is separate. Section 47-16-07.1(2) caps it at the greater of $2,500 or 2 months' rent. The pet deposit does not apply to a service animal, or to a companion animal that a tenant with a disability needs as a reasonable accommodation under fair housing laws.

### Worked example: deposit caps at 3 rent levels

| Monthly rent | Standard cap | Cap with an exception | Pet deposit cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| $900 | $900 | $1,800 | $2,500 |
| $1,250 | $1,250 | $2,500 | $2,500 |
| $1,600 | $1,600 | $3,200 | $3,200 |

At $1,600 rent, 2 months' rent is $3,200. That is more than $2,500, so the pet deposit cap is $3,200.

## How many days does a North Dakota landlord have to return a security deposit?

A North Dakota landlord has 30 days to return a security deposit. The clock starts after 2 events: the lease ends, and the tenant delivers possession. Both must happen. If the tenant stays after the lease date, the clock waits.

Within those 30 days, the landlord must deliver or mail 3 things to the last address the tenant gave:

- The refund due, if any.
- An itemized list of every amount kept.
- A written notice that states any amount the tenant still owes, or the refund due to the tenant.

If the tenant does not claim the money within 1 year after the lease ends, section 47-16-07.1(3) points to the unclaimed property rules in section 47-30.2-04.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in North Dakota?

A North Dakota landlord can deduct 3 types of cost from the deposit and its interest, under section 47-16-07.1(3):

1. **Damage.** Harm to the unit from the tenant's pet, or from the negligence of the tenant or a guest.
2. **Unpaid rent.** Any rent the tenant still owes.
3. **Cleaning and repairs.** Costs that were the tenant's job and that return the unit to its move-in state, "reasonable wear and tear excepted".

Normal wear and tear is not a deduction. The statute does not list examples, so your move-in record is the best proof of what changed.

Section 47-16-07.2 helps here. At the start of the rental agreement, the landlord must give the tenant a statement of the condition of the unit. Both sides sign it. The statement is prima facie proof (proof unless shown wrong) of the move-in condition.

## Does a North Dakota landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. A North Dakota landlord must itemize every part of the deposit that the landlord does not pay back. The list goes out with the written notice within the 30-day window. The statute does not require receipts, but a clear list with costs makes a dispute shorter.

Use this checklist for each move-out:

- [ ] Signed move-in condition statement on file (47-16-07.2).
- [ ] Move-out walk-through done, with dated photos.
- [ ] Each deduction tied to damage, unpaid rent, or cleaning and repair beyond wear and tear.
- [ ] Interest added, if the tenant stayed 9 months or more.
- [ ] Itemized list, notice and refund sent within 30 days.
- [ ] Sent to the last address the tenant gave you.
- [ ] Copy of everything kept in the tenant file.

## Must a North Dakota landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

Yes. Section 47-16-07.1(1) requires the landlord to put the deposit in a federally insured, interest-bearing savings or checking account "for the benefit of the tenant". The deposit and its interest go back to the tenant at the end of the lease, less lawful deductions.

The statute gives one exception for interest. A landlord does not have to pay interest if the tenant occupied the unit for less than 9 months. The statute sets no interest rate. The tenant gets what the account actually earned.

When the building is sold, the deposit and its interest move to the new owner. The seller stays liable until that transfer happens. The owner at the end of the lease is bound by the statute, even if a past owner took the deposit.

## What is the penalty for a North Dakota landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

The penalty is treble damages. Section 47-16-07.1(4) makes a landlord liable for treble damages for any deposit money "withheld without reasonable justification".

Worked example: a landlord keeps $400 for "carpet wear" in a unit with 6 years of normal use. A court finds no reasonable justification. Treble damages mean three times $400, so the landlord is exposed to $1,200 on that item alone.

The rule applies to public landlords too. Section 47-16-07.1(6) covers the state and its political subdivisions when they lease property and take a deposit.

## What should a North Dakota property manager change in practice?

Most deposit disputes start with a missing record or a late letter. Three habits cover most of the risk:

1. **Set a 30-day calendar alert** at the date the tenant gives back the keys.
2. **Open one interest-bearing account** at a federally insured bank for all deposits, and track interest per tenant.
3. **Get the condition statement signed** at every lease start, not only for new tenants.

## Is a verbal lease valid?

An oral agreement can create a tenancy, so start with facts rather than the missing signature. Virginia Code section 55.1-1200 expressly includes written and oral rental agreements. That definition does not prove every claimed term. It shows why an unsigned arrangement still needs legal review. [Read Virginia's definition](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title55.1/chapter12/section55.1-1200/).

Write down how the person moved in. Did you agree on rent, a room, a home or a fixed stay? Did a former owner make the agreement? Did you accept money after a prior lease expired?

Keep separate files for facts and conclusions. A payment memo is a fact. Your belief that someone is a guest is a conclusion. Counsel needs the underlying facts before choosing the notice.

Do not ask the occupant to sign a backdated lease. Record the current facts and preserve the original messages. A new agreement can clarify future terms when both parties choose that route.

## What notice ends a tenancy at will?

The notice must match the tenancy and the law where the property sits. There is no national notice that ends every informal rental.

Florida Statutes section 83.57 requires at least 30 days before the end of a monthly period for month-to-month tenancies. Virginia section 55.1-1253 generally uses at least 30 days before the next rent due date, unless the agreement provides otherwise. Virginia also has a separate rule for certain mass nonrenewals. These are state examples, not universal instructions. [Florida statute](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0083/Sections/0083.57.html), [Virginia statute](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title55.1/chapter12/section55.1-1253/).

Confirm whether rent regulation, subsidy terms or a required cause changes the route. Then check how to serve the notice and count time. A correct period with invalid service can still fail.

Distinguish ending a periodic tenancy from enforcing a breach. A nonpayment notice serves a different purpose from a nonrenewal notice. Do not mix the demands in an unreviewed form.

Keep a copy of the exact notice you served. Save proof of service and the calendar calculation. Put the proposed end date in your management system without treating it as completed possession.

## How do I prove the tenancy terms?

Build a dated record that connects permission to occupy with the agreed payments. The strongest file explains what happened without relying on memory alone.

| Record | What it can help show | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Rent receipts | Amount and payment period | Missing months and cash payments |
| Messages | Agreed use and move-in terms | Full conversation and sender identity |
| Prior written lease | Earlier terms and named tenants | Expiry and later agreements |
| Bank records | Transfers and payer details | Payments made by another person |
| Inspection notes | Occupancy and condition | Date, author and lawful access |
| Witness account | What someone heard or saw | Direct knowledge rather than rumor |

Export messages in full. A cropped image may hide the statement that changes the meaning. Keep the original file and a readable copy.

Reconcile the ledger before preparing a demand. Separate rent, deposits, refunds and disputed charges. Do not call every transfer rent merely because it came from the occupant.

If a prior manager held the records, request them before choosing a court date. Missing records can change your claim and the evidence you need.

## What if a family member or friend moved in?

A family connection does not answer the legal status question. Ask how permission arose, what space the person controls and whether payment was expected.

Give counsel the full history, including promises made by other owners. Shared ownership, probate, domestic relations or an accommodation request can change the analysis. A notice form cannot resolve those issues by itself.

Avoid threats during the review. Do not remove belongings, turn off services or make entry unpleasant to force a move. Those actions can create a second dispute before you resolve the first.

A voluntary written move-out agreement may help if both sides want a clear exit. State the date, belongings plan and key return process. Have local counsel check the terms before you exchange funds or waive claims.

Do not treat silence as acceptance. Confirm the agreement through the required signing process. Keep communication calm and factual even when the personal relationship is strained.

## What happens if the occupant does not leave?

A notice does not itself give you physical possession. Follow the applicable court process when the occupant stays.

California Courts describes eviction as a sequence that includes notice, a court case and enforcement of a judgment. Its landlord guide warns against removing a tenant outside that process. Use the court guide for your jurisdiction rather than copying California forms elsewhere. [Read the California Courts guide](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/eviction-landlord).

Prepare the file before you file a case. Include the ownership record, occupancy history, ledger, notice and service proof. Ask the clerk or self-help center which local forms apply. Check who may represent your ownership entity.

Keep repair requests active while the case proceeds. Save new payments and messages without making unreviewed changes to the legal strategy. Ask counsel how accepting money affects your specific notice.

Use the [property management guide](/property-management) to assess whether a local manager can handle records and routine communication. Court representation and legal decisions need the proper professional.

Your next step is to classify the occupancy and check the current local notice rule. Do that before setting a move-out deadline.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What fields should a maintenance work order have?

A maintenance work order needs fields for the request, triage, approval, the job and close-out. If one group is missing, the order breaks at that step. A missing photo starts a deposit dispute. A missing approval starts an owner dispute.

Copy the template below into your property management software, a form tool or a shared sheet. Each line is one field.

### Copy-ready work order template

**1. Request**

- Work order number:
- Date and time received:
- Received by (portal, phone, email, inspection):
- Property address and unit:
- Year built (for lead paint rules):
- Tenant name and best contact:
- Problem, in the tenant's words:
- Location in the unit (room, fixture):
- Photos or video from the tenant: attached / none

**2. Triage**

- Priority: emergency / urgent / routine / cosmetic
- Category: plumbing / electrical / HVAC / appliance / pest / structure / other
- Permission to enter: yes / no / call first
- Pets in the unit: yes / no
- Best times for access:
- Tenant-caused? yes / no / unknown (decide after the visit)

**3. Approval**

- Cost estimate:
- Owner spend limit (from the management agreement):
- Owner approval needed: yes / no
- Owner approval: name, date, method (portal, email, text)
- Billed to: owner / tenant / warranty / insurance claim

**4. Job**

- Vendor name and trade:
- Vendor license and insurance on file: yes / no
- Lead-safe certified firm (pre-1978 homes): yes / no / not needed
- Scheduled date and time window:
- Tenant told of the visit: date and method
- Work done (vendor's words):
- Parts used:
- Follow-up needed: yes / no, what

**5. Close-out**

- Photos before and after: attached
- Invoice number and amount:
- Markup or coordination fee (if any):
- Tenant confirmed fixed: yes / no, date
- Date closed and closed by:

This is a generic template, not a state legal form. Check your lease, your management agreement and local law before you use it.

## How do you track a work order from request to close?

Track each work order through a short, fixed list of status steps. Fixed steps let you count open orders and see where jobs stall. Use the same steps for every property.

| Step | Status | Who moves it | Exit rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | New | Tenant or manager | Request has unit, problem and contact |
| 2 | Triaged | Manager | Priority and category set |
| 3 | Waiting for approval | Manager | Owner says yes, or cost is under the limit |
| 4 | Assigned | Manager | Vendor accepts the job and a date |
| 5 | In progress | Vendor | Vendor is on site |
| 6 | Waiting for invoice | Vendor | Photos and invoice uploaded |
| 7 | Closed | Manager | Tenant confirms, invoice is posted to the owner ledger |

Watch for: orders that sit in "Waiting for approval" for days. What to do: set a reminder to the owner after 24 hours, and log each reminder on the order.

Two numbers tell you how your maintenance desk works:

1. **Time to first response.** The hours from "New" to "Triaged".
2. **Time to close.** The days from "New" to "Closed".

A worked example: in one month you close 40 orders. The total time to close is 160 days. Your mean time to close is 160 ÷ 40 = 4 days. Look at the slowest 5 orders first. Most often, they stall in one step.

## Which approvals does the owner need to give?

The owner needs to approve any job with a cost above the spend limit in the management agreement. The agreement sets that limit, not the manager. Many agreements also say what the manager can do in an emergency without approval.

Put the rules in a table so that each person reads them the same way:

| Job type | Example | Approval rule (sample) |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency | Burst pipe, no heat in winter, gas smell | Manager acts now, tells the owner the same day |
| Under the limit | $180 faucet repair, limit $300 | Manager approves |
| Over the limit | $1,200 water heater | Owner approves before work starts |
| Capital project | New roof | Owner approves a written bid |
| Tenant-caused | Broken window from a ball | Manager approves, bills the tenant per the lease |

The dollar amounts are an example. Use the numbers in your own agreement.

Record each approval on the work order: who said yes, when, and how. A text that says "ok" is weak proof. A portal click with a date is strong proof. If the owner says no, record that too, with the risk you explained.

## How do you attach photos and invoices?

Attach photos and invoices to the work order itself, not to an email thread. The work order is the record the owner, the tenant and an auditor will look at.

Use this photo checklist for each job:

- [ ] 1 photo of the problem before work starts, with the room in view
- [ ] 1 close photo of the damaged part
- [ ] 1 photo of the finished work, from the same angle
- [ ] 1 photo of the serial plate for any new appliance or water heater
- [ ] Date and time on each photo (most phones store this)

Then check the invoice before you post it:

- [ ] Invoice number, vendor name and date
- [ ] Unit address matches the work order
- [ ] Labor hours and parts are listed apart
- [ ] Amount matches the estimate, or the change has a reason
- [ ] Any markup or coordination fee is shown as its own line

The invoice then goes on the owner's statement. The photos stay on the order. If a tenant later disputes a charge, you have the full record in one place.

## What changes for homes built before 1978?

Homes built before 1978 add one more check before you assign the vendor. The EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) Program says anyone paid to do work that disturbs painted surfaces in housing built before 1978 must be certified. The EPA page names property management firms that do or offer this work.

The rule has a small-job exception. Under 40 CFR 745.83, "minor repair and maintenance" means 6 square feet or less of painted surface per room inside, or 20 square feet or less outside. Window replacement and demolition of painted surfaces do not count as minor, at any size.

So add two fields to the work order: "Year built" and "Lead-safe certified firm". If the year is before 1978 and the job disturbs paint above those limits, send a certified firm. Some states run their own program, so check your state's rules too.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

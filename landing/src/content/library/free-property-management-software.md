## Which property management tools have a free plan?

Avail and TurboTenant publish ongoing free software plans. Compare their included work and paid services before treating either as a complete free system.

| Plan | Published software price | Included work described | Added cost to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Avail Unlimited | $0 | Listings, applications, leases, rent and maintenance | Payments and screening |
| TurboTenant Free | $0 monthly | Marketing, screening requests and rent collection | Screening and paid upgrades |
| Avail Unlimited Plus | $9 per unit monthly | Added speed and customization | Total at your door count |
| TurboTenant Essentials | From $149 annually | Added lease-related tools | Portfolio price band |
| TurboTenant Pro | From $199 annually | Accounting and added features | Portfolio price band |

Sources: [Avail pricing](https://www.avail.com/pricing) and [TurboTenant pricing](https://www.turbotenant.com/pricing/), read 2026-10-19.

These are software offers, not promises that every transaction is free. Paid reports, renter options and processing can sit outside the subscription.

A free plan can fit a landlord's basic work. A professional manager should separately test owner accounting and payment controls. Do not assume a landlord product is a complete client-fund system.

## Who pays the fees on a free plan?

The payer depends on the service, plan and payment method. Make the payer visible in your comparison.

Avail's free Unlimited plan lists a $2.50 bank-transfer fee paid by tenants. Its pricing page says bank-transfer fees are waived for Unlimited Plus. It lists a 3.5% card processing fee across subscriptions.

Those figures come from Avail's pricing page, read 2026-10-19. They explain why a landlord's free bill does not mean a tenant's payment is free.

TurboTenant's page lists different renter charges alongside its plans. Confirm which fee table applies to your selected plan. Ask for the amount the renter sees before submission.

Use a payer register:

| Activity | Record before choosing |
|---|---|
| Application | Applicant or landlord pays; when charged |
| Screening | Package, price and refund process |
| Rent | Bank and card options, payer and timing |
| Documents | Included signatures or paid tools |
| Optional services | User choice and recurring charge |

Do not assume a fee is permitted merely because a product can collect it. Check the local requirements before using fee settings.

## Which features sit behind paid plans?

Paid plans add particular tools, speed or fee benefits; they do not necessarily change every part of the product. Test the feature that would make you pay.

Avail lists faster payments, waived bank-transfer charges and more customization in Unlimited Plus. Ask whether those benefits fit your actual collection process.

TurboTenant presents Essentials and Pro as paid tiers. Its pricing page describes built-in accounting in Pro and publishes annual bands by unit count.

A short feature label can hide a scope question. For accounting, ask which reports and exports you receive. For leases, test editing, signing and final downloads.

Compare upgrade value this way:

- **Name the problem.** State the task the free plan cannot finish.
- **Test the feature.** Complete that task in the paid plan.
- **Count the difference.** Include review and correction time.
- **Check the bill.** Use the annual or monthly amount actually due.
- **Review renewal.** Record the normal price after any offer.

Do not upgrade for a feature you will not use. Also do not retain a free plan if its workarounds weaken your records.

## How do free tools make money?

The published offers show revenue through paid upgrades and services, but do not establish every vendor's revenue mix. Avoid guessing percentages or motives.

Avail publishes a paid per-unit tier and transaction fees. TurboTenant publishes paid annual tiers and renter service charges. These are visible parts of the offer.

A vendor may also describe optional insurance or reporting services. Check which services are required, who selects them and when charges begin. Do not treat optional services as part of your base budget unless you choose them.

The useful buying question is whether the arrangement is clear to everyone. A landlord should understand the own-account bill. A renter should understand the amount shown before a transaction.

Review the end-to-end screens rather than relying only on a headline. Ask support to explain any fee that appears late in the process.

Keep copies of the plan terms and your selected settings. That makes later changes easier to review.

## When should you move to a paid plan?

Move to a paid plan when a tested function improves your work enough to justify its full cost. Growth alone does not require an upgrade.

A landlord may need better expense records or faster access to settled rent. A management firm may need owner statements, access roles and more robust financial controls. These are different purchase cases.

Use your own trial evidence. Record the task before and after the upgrade. Include the time spent reviewing or fixing outputs.

If the free plan works, keep it. If it forces staff to copy balances and documents across tools, compare the complete process with a broader product.

[Buildium's pricing page](https://www.buildium.com/pricing/), read 2026-10-19, lists Essential from $62 monthly. That is a possible comparison point, not proof that a paid system solves your specific gap.

Check first-year setup and normal-year usage before moving. A low starting subscription is only part of the decision.

## What should you test before relying on free software?

Test a full tenancy and an exit before using any product as your only record store. The price does not change that requirement.

Create a sample listing and application. Store a signed agreement. Record rent, a repair expense and a refund. Download the ledger and supporting files.

Open those files without the product. Confirm that document links do not depend on continued login access. Ask how cancellation affects historical records.

Give the tool a difficult case, such as a returned payment or changed tenant. Check whether the history remains clear and staff can explain the result.

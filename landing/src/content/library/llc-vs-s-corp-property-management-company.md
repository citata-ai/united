## What Are the Tax Differences Between an LLC and S Corporation?

An LLC describes the state entity, while S corporation describes a federal tax status. The categories can overlap rather than form a simple either-or choice.

The IRS explains that an LLC's default federal classification depends partly on its members. An eligible entity can elect corporate treatment. The IRS S corporation guidance describes an election that generally passes income, losses and other items through to shareholders, subject to its rules.

Use this table to organize the decision. It lists issues to review rather than a recommendation for a particular entity.

| Question | LLC entity review | S corporation tax review |
|---|---|---|
| Formation | State filing and ownership terms | Eligible entity and election process |
| Owners | Membership and operating agreement | Shareholder eligibility rules |
| Tax reporting | Default or elected treatment | Pass-through reporting and filings |
| Owner work | Duties and payment method | Reasonable compensation and payroll |
| License | Entity form accepted by regulator | Tax election does not replace licensing |
| Administration | State and internal records | Added payroll and tax compliance |

Ask for a model using your expected profit and the services you perform. Do not use gross rent handled as company profit. Client money, company revenue and owner distributions need clear treatment in the model. Keep the rental owner's property entity separate from the management company's entity decision.

## Does the State License the Entity or the Person?

Check the regulator's rules before choosing an entity form or signing management agreements. A personal license does not automatically answer the entity question.

California provides a specific example. The Department of Real Estate says a corporation can obtain a real estate broker license through qualified broker officers. That shows why an entity decision needs a licensing review. It does not establish that every entity form qualifies in every state.

Give counsel and the licensing adviser the proposed ownership and service scope. Explain who negotiates leases, signs agreements and controls client funds. Ask which licenses, designated people and business registrations are needed before operating.

Check the exact legal name used on agreements, invoices and bank records. An informal brand name can differ from the licensed entity name. Resolve those differences before staff create documents or ask owners to transfer money.

Plan for changes too. A new owner, broker departure or entity conversion may affect the license and contracts. Ask the regulator how those changes should be handled. Do not assume a tax election or new filing leaves every operating approval unchanged.

## When Can an S Corporation Election Make Sense?

Consider an election only after a tax professional models the net result and ongoing duties. There is no universal profit threshold that fits every management company.

The IRS says S corporations must pay reasonable compensation for shareholder-employee services before non-wage distributions. It can reclassify distributions as wages. That makes the owner's actual work central to the comparison.

List the owner's tasks and hours. Include sales, supervision, accounting review and service work. Ask the adviser to support reasonable compensation from relevant facts rather than a desired tax result.

Compare payroll, tax preparation, state charges and bookkeeping with any projected benefit. Model a slower period and a stronger period. If the business barely funds normal operations, added administration can matter as much as an estimated tax saving.

Review ownership plans and eligibility before filing an election. A future partner or investor can change the analysis. Ask about election timing, effective dates and the cost of reversing course. Record the advice and assumptions so the decision can be reviewed when the business changes.

## What Do You Need for Business and Trust Bank Accounts?

Confirm account documents with the bank and client-fund duties with the regulator. An entity filing does not by itself establish a correct trust process.

Prepare the entity records, tax identity information, ownership details and signer authority the bank requests. Ask whether each account title matches the approved business structure. Have the broker review who may handle client funds under the applicable rules.

Separate company operations from money held for others. Ask counsel and accounting to identify which receipts belong in each account and when company fees become earned. Do not make the tax election the reason for moving client money.

Test the accounting flow before taking over an owner account. Trace a receipt, a vendor payment, an owner payout and an earned fee. Confirm that each entry reaches the correct ledger and account. Keep approval records for transfers.

## How Do You Make the Entity Decision Reviewable?

Write a decision record that connects the entity choice to licenses, ownership and costs. A short record helps future advisers check what changed.

- State the management services and markets you plan to serve.
- List the proposed owners and each person's work.
- Confirm acceptable entity forms with the licensing authority.
- Obtain a tax comparison using supported compensation assumptions.
- Confirm bank documents, account names and signer controls.
- Assign responsibility for filings, payroll and future reviews.

Keep the formation records, election documents and license approvals together. Tell the accountant when ownership or work duties change. Tell counsel before converting the entity or moving agreements to a new company.

## What must a termination clause cover?

A termination clause must say how either side can end the agreement and what happens next. Some states require this in writing.

Two state rules show the minimum:

- **Oregon, OAR 863-025-0020(2)(d)** requires every property management agreement to include "the term of the agreement and the method for termination". Item (h) also requires the plan for your records after termination.
- **Iowa, 481 IAC 2015.1(1)(h)** requires the "terms and conditions for termination of the property management agreement by the broker or the owner".

A full clause answers 6 questions:

1. How much notice does each side give, and in what form?
2. Can either side exit early, and at what cost?
3. Who keeps the lease and the tenant?
4. What do you hand over, and by what date?
5. Which fees apply at exit?
6. What happens to bills and repairs that are still open?

## What notice period is normal?

Published exit terms often use 30 days written notice. The table shows 3 managers' public terms, read on 2026-10-19.

| Manager | First term | Notice after that | Exit fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| PMI Hudson (NY) | Not stated on page | 30 days, no penalty (Happiness Guarantee) | None, if vendor invoices are paid |
| RexMont (WA) | 9 months | 30 days written notice, month to month | None after the first term |
| Robert C. White & Company (CT) | No long-term contract | Not stated on page | No termination fees |
| Tiner Properties (Sacramento), April 2026 sample | Not stated in source | Not stated in source | $500 if the owner cancels before 6 months of fees are collected |

The Tiner figure comes from Utopia Management's review of published Sacramento rate cards, checked 2026-09-30.

PMI Hudson explains why it asks for 30 days. The time lets it close open repair items and invoices, and assign the lease and the deposit to the owner. That is the honest reason for a notice period: handover work, not a penalty.

RexMont also lists cases where the owner can leave early with no penalty, for example if RexMont breaches the agreement and does not fix it within a set time. A clause like that shows owners you do not plan to trap them.

## Who keeps the tenant after the owner cancels?

The owner keeps the tenant. The lease belongs to the owner, and you hand over your role in it.

Oregon makes this explicit. OAR 863-025-0070(6) says the manager "must transfer and assign by written agreement" any interest in a lease to the owner or to a new manager. Item (2)(b)(E) requires you to give the owner copies of all current leases, unless the owner waives that in writing.

Some agreements add a fee if the owner keeps a tenant you placed and then manages the home alone. If you use one, write the amount and the time limit in the agreement. A vague "tenant ownership" rule leads to disputes.

## How are deposits and files handed over?

Deposits and files go to the owner, or to the new manager if the owner says so in writing. The deadline depends on your state.

| State and rule | Funds and deposits | Final accounting | Tenant notice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oregon, OAR 863-025-0070 | Within 60 days, to the owner or a person the owner names in writing | Within 60 days, plus an accounting of all deposits | By the next calendar day, with the name and address of the new holder |
| Iowa, 481 IAC 2015.1(5) | Unobligated funds within 30 days | Within 60 days, with why any funds are still held | Immediately, with the name and address of the new holder |
| Alabama, 790-X-3-.03(6)(c) | Deposits, prepaid rent and escrows within 7 business days | Within 7 business days, to the owner or new manager | Written notice to the tenant ends your liability for later events |

Oregon and Iowa also forbid you to spend a tenant's deposit on fees the lease does not allow. Oregon lets you tell the owner you may destroy the records after 6 years.

Use this handover checklist:

- [ ] Tenant ledger for each unit, with current balance.
- [ ] Owner ledger and final statement.
- [ ] Security deposits, pet deposits and prepaid rent, by tenant.
- [ ] Signed leases, addenda and move-in inspection reports with photos.
- [ ] Keys, fobs, garage remotes and codes.
- [ ] Open work orders and unpaid vendor invoices.
- [ ] Warranties, utility accounts and HOA contacts.
- [ ] Written proof of what you sent and when.

Oregon item (7) requires written proof of transmittal or receipt for the documents. Keep it even where your state does not require it.

## What fees apply at termination?

Only the fees written in the agreement apply. Typical exit lines are an early termination fee, unpaid management fees to the notice date, open vendor invoices, and a fee for a tenant you placed.

Write them as a short table in the agreement:

| Exit item | Write in the agreement |
|---|---|
| Early termination fee | The amount and the time window, for example "$500 before month 6" |
| Fees during notice | Management fee until the effective date |
| Open invoices | Paid from owner funds before transfer |
| Placed tenant fee | Amount, or "none" |
| Admin or transfer fee | Amount, or "none" |

Watch for: a large exit fee may keep owners on paper, but it does not keep them happy. Several managers now sell "no termination fee" as a feature.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## A sample termination clause outline

Use this outline as a starting point and have a local attorney adapt it to your state.

1. **Term.** The agreement starts on [date] and runs for [X] months, then month to month.
2. **Notice.** Either party may end the agreement with [30] days written notice.
3. **Early exit.** The owner may exit during the first term for a fee of [$X], or with no fee if the manager breaches and does not cure within [X] days.
4. **Leases.** The manager assigns all leases to the owner or the new manager in writing.
5. **Funds.** The manager transfers deposits, prepaid rent and owner funds within [your state's deadline].
6. **Records.** The manager delivers the items in the handover list within [X] days.
7. **Fees.** Only the fees in this section apply at termination.

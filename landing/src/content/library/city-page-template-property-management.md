## What should a city page for rental owners do?

A city page for rental owners answers one question: can you manage my rental here, and what will it cost? An owner who lands on "property management Naperville" wants proof that you work in Naperville. A page that repeats your home page with a new city name does not give that proof.

All 3 direct competitors we studied build one owner page per real service city (our competitor study, 2026-10-19). They link each page to services, reviews and a rental analysis. So the format is normal in this market. The quality of each page is what differs.

## Why do copied city pages fail?

Copied city pages fail because Google treats them as doorway pages. Google's spam policies list "having multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as doorway abuse (Google Search Central, updated 2026-08-28). The same policy names "substantially similar pages" as a second example.

We saw this on a real site. Fridman Properties, a Chicago-area manager, had 29 city pages. Two of them, Palatine and Skokie, were 96% the same text (our audit, 2026-10). Fridman Properties is a related party: its owner is CEO of MyRemotely, our partner. In our AI baseline on 2026-10-16, AI answers named the company in 0 of 42 answers about Chicago's South Side, where most of its units are. City pages were not the only cause, but they were the first item in the fix plan. See the [Fridman Properties case study](/case-studies/fridman-properties).

## The city page template: 10 blocks

Use these blocks in this order. Each block has a test: if the text in it could be true for any city, rewrite it.

| # | Block | What goes in it | Uniqueness test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Answer block | 2–3 sentences: you manage rentals in this city, how many doors, your main fee | Has the city name and a city number |
| 2 | Local rent data | Rent by bedroom count, with the source and year | Numbers differ from other city pages |
| 3 | Local rules | Licenses, rental registration, inspections, deposit or notice rules that apply here | Cites a city, county or state source |
| 4 | Fees for this city | Your full fee table, or a note that fees match your main fee page | Shows any city-specific fee |
| 5 | Services here | Which services you offer in this city, and which you do not | Lists real limits (for example, no HOA work here) |
| 6 | Neighborhoods and property types | Areas and home types you manage in this city | Names real areas |
| 7 | Proof from this city | Reviews from owners here, door count, a short local example | Each review or example is from this city |
| 8 | Team | Who handles this city, and where they work from | Names a person or an office |
| 9 | City FAQ | 3–5 questions owners in this city ask | At least 2 answers use local facts |
| 10 | Next step | Rental analysis or call, plus links to services and fees | Links work and point to real pages |

## Which local facts belong on a city page?

The best local facts are rent numbers, landlord rules and housing stock for that one city. They are facts a copy cannot fake.

**Rent data.** HUD Fair Market Rents give one public number per bedroom count for each metro. HUD sets them at the 40th percentile of rents paid by recent movers, including utilities except phone (24 CFR 888.113). For example, the FY2027 two-bedroom Fair Market Rent for the Chicago-Joliet-Naperville metro is $2,011, up from $1,781 in FY2026 (HUD FY2027 data). Your own rent roll for that city is the second source. Say how many leases it covers and from which months.

**Local rules.** Many cities have rental registration, licensing or inspection rules that the state does not. Name the rule, link the city page that states it, and add the date you checked it. Add this line under the block: "This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney."

**Housing stock.** Say which property types you manage there: single-family homes, 2–4 flats, condos. This helps an owner see a match fast.

## How do I show fees and services per city?

Show your full fee table on each city page, or link to one fee page and say the fees are the same in every city. Do not hide fees on city pages only. If a city costs more to serve, say so and say why: for example, a higher fee for inspections where the city requires them.

A fee table for a city page:

| Fee | Amount | When you pay it |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly management | __% of rent collected, or $__ flat | Each month rent comes in |
| Leasing or placement | __% of first month's rent, or $__ | When a new lease starts |
| Lease renewal | $__ | When a lease renews |
| Inspection (city required) | $__ | Per city inspection |
| Setup | $__ or none | Once |

Fill in your real numbers. To check your fee against the market, use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## Which reviews and proof go on the page?

Put proof from this city on this city page. A review from an owner in Evanston means more on the Evanston page than 5 reviews from other towns.

Proof checklist:

- [ ] 2–4 reviews from owners or tenants in this city, with first name and month
- [ ] Door count in this city, with the date
- [ ] One short example: a vacancy filled, a city inspection passed, a repair done
- [ ] License number, if your state requires one
- [ ] Average days to lease in this city, only if you track it

Do not invent a review or a number. If you have no doors in a city yet, do not make a page for it.

## How do I link city pages to services?

Link each city page to your service pages, your fee page and your rental analysis. Then link back from each service page to the city pages. Google says AI features use the same base as search: content that is "easily findable through internal links" and text that matches your structured data (Google Search Central, updated 2025-12-10).

A link plan for one city:

1. **From the city page:** link to full management, leasing only, fees and rental analysis.
2. **From each service page:** list the cities you serve, each as a link.
3. **From the home page or footer:** link to all city pages in one list.
4. **From nearby city pages:** link 2–3 neighbor cities.

## How do I check a city page before it goes live?

Run 5 checks before you publish each page.

1. **Compare the text** with your 2 most similar city pages. If more than half the sentences match, rewrite.
2. **Check each number** has a source and a date.
3. **Read the answer block** alone. It must make sense without the rest of the page.
4. **Check the fee table** matches your main fee page.
5. **Ask an AI assistant** "Who manages rentals in [city]?" and save the answer. Ask again in 60 days.

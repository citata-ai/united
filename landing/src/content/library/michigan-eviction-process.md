## Michigan eviction rules at a glance

Michigan calls an eviction case a "summary proceeding to recover possession of premises". Chapter 57 of the Revised Judicature Act (MCL 600.5701 to 600.5759) sets the court steps. MCL 554.134 sets the notice to end a tenancy. Here is the core of both, from the 2025 Michigan Compiled Laws (read on Justia, 2026-10-19).

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Demand for possession, unpaid rent | 7 days to pay or leave | 600.5714(1)(a), 554.134(2) |
| Notice to quit, drug activity with police report | 24 hours | 600.5714(1)(b), 554.134(4) |
| Notice to quit, physical injury or threat (police notified) | 7 days | 600.5714(1)(e) |
| Health hazard or serious damage | 7 days to leave or repair | 600.5714(1)(d) |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | 1 rental period (1 month for monthly rent) | 554.134(1) |
| Demand served by mail | Service date is the next mail day | 600.5718(1)(c) |
| Court | District court | 600.5704 |
| Filing fee, possession only | $45 | 600.5756(1) |
| Trial date | Within 10 days of summons issue; served 3+ days before | 600.5735(2)(b) |
| Writ of restitution | Not before 10 days after judgment | 600.5744(5) |
| Writ fee | $15 | 600.5757 |
| Lockout or utility shutoff | Illegal; actual damages or $200, whichever is greater | 600.2918(2) |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What notice must a Michigan landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

A Michigan landlord gives a written demand for possession with 7 days to pay. MCL 600.5714(1)(a) lets you file when the tenant holds over after failing to pay rent within 7 days from service of that demand. MCL 554.134(2) gives the same 7-day notice to quit for unpaid rent.

The demand has a required form. Under MCL 600.5716, the demand must:

- [ ] be in writing and name the person in possession;
- [ ] give the address or a short description of the unit;
- [ ] state the reason and the time to fix the problem;
- [ ] state the amount due on the date of the demand;
- [ ] be dated and signed by you, your attorney or your agent.

Watch for: "rent due" does not include late rent you accelerate because of a lease breach (MCL 600.5714(1)(a)). Put only the rent that is due now in the demand.

MCL 600.5718 allows 4 ways to serve the demand. You can hand it to the tenant or leave it with an adult household member at the unit. You can also send it by first-class mail or by email. Email service needs the tenant's written consent first. If you mail it, the service date is the next regular mail day after you mail it. You may not refuse a lease because a tenant says no to email service.

## How long does an eviction take in Michigan?

A Michigan eviction for unpaid rent takes about 4 weeks at the statutory minimums. Court calendars, a jury demand or an appeal add time. Here is a worked timeline for a demand mailed on a Monday, 2026-11-02.

| Step | Statute | Earliest date |
|---|---|---|
| Mail the 7-day demand | 600.5718(1)(c) | Mon 2026-11-02 |
| Service date (next mail day) | 600.5718(1)(c) | Tue 2026-11-03 |
| 7 days end | 600.5714(1)(a) | Tue 2026-11-10 |
| File the complaint, court issues summons | 600.5735(1) | Wed 2026-11-11 |
| Trial, within 10 days of issue | 600.5735(2)(b) | By Fri 2026-11-20 (day 10 is a Saturday) |
| Judgment for possession | 600.5741 | Trial date |
| 10-day wait ends, writ may issue | 600.5744(5) | About Mon 2026-11-30 |

MCL 600.5735(6) says the court must hear the case within 7 days after the tenant's appearance or trial date. The court may not adjourn it beyond that, unless both parties agree in writing or on the record. A court may also use a local rule: the tenant then appears within 5 days after service of the summons (MCL 600.5735(4)(b)).

The tenant can still stop the writ. In a judgment for unpaid rent, MCL 600.5744(7) bars the writ if the tenant pays in time. The tenant pays the amount in the judgment plus taxed costs within the 10 days. An appeal with a stay bond also pauses the 10-day period (MCL 600.5744(6)). Appeals go to the circuit court of the same county (MCL 600.5753).

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Michigan, and what does it cost?

A Michigan landlord files in district court. MCL 600.5704 gives the district court, municipal courts and the common pleas court of Detroit jurisdiction over summary proceedings.

The fees come from the statute:

- **Possession only:** $45 filing fee (MCL 600.5756(1)).
- **Possession plus a money claim:** $45 plus a supplemental fee equal to the normal money-claim fee in that court (MCL 600.5756(2)). Ask the clerk for the current amount.
- **Writ of restitution:** $15 per writ (MCL 600.5757).

Service of the summons and the court officer who carries out the writ may charge more. Ask your district court clerk before you file.

## Can a Michigan landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes, a Michigan landlord can evict for some lease breaches and can end a month-to-month tenancy with no cause. MCL 600.5714(1)(c) lets you recover possession when the tenant holds over after the lease ends. That covers 3 cases. The lease term runs out. You end the lease under a termination power in the lease or in law. Or you serve a notice to quit under MCL 554.134.

The notice depends on the reason:

1. **No cause, month-to-month.** MCL 554.134(1) lets either party end a tenancy at will with 1 month's notice. If rent is due at intervals under 3 months, notice equal to the rent interval is enough.
2. **Drug activity.** 24 hours after a written notice to quit. The lease must have a drug clause, and a formal police report must exist (MCL 554.134(4), 600.5714(1)(b)).
3. **Injury or threat of injury.** 7 days after a written notice to quit, if the police were notified (MCL 600.5714(1)(e)). This does not apply when the victim is the tenant or a household member.
4. **Health hazard or serious damage.** 7 days after a demand to leave or to repair (MCL 600.5714(1)(d)).

Watch for: 2 groups need just cause. Local government housing tenants and mobile home park tenants are not "holding over" without just cause (MCL 600.5714(2), (3)).

Retaliation is a defense. MCL 600.5720 bars a judgment when the termination was mainly a penalty for a lawful act. Examples are a code complaint, membership in a tenant organization, or an attempt to enforce rights. If the tenant took official action within 90 days before you filed, the law presumes retaliation. You must then prove otherwise.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Michigan?

A court officer, bailiff, sheriff, deputy sheriff or local police officer removes the tenant. MCL 600.5744(1) tells the court to issue a writ to one of them. The officer puts you in full, peaceful possession. The officer removes the occupants and their property, and leaves the property in a public area or delivers it to the sheriff.

The writ can issue at once in a few cases, on proof and with notice (MCL 600.5744(3)). These include drug cases under 600.5714(1)(b), forcible entry, and a tenant who keeps causing a serious health hazard or damage.

## Is a self-help eviction legal in Michigan?

No, a self-help eviction is not legal in Michigan. MCL 600.2918(2) lets a tenant recover actual damages or $200, whichever is greater, for each occurrence of unlawful interference. The list includes:

- changing locks without giving the tenant a key at once;
- removing or keeping the tenant's property;
- boarding up the unit, or removing doors, windows or locks;
- cutting off heat, running water, hot water, electric or gas service;
- using force, threats, noise or odor.

A forcible ejection costs more: 3 times actual damages or $200, whichever is greater (MCL 600.2918(1)). Tenants cannot waive these rules (MCL 600.2918(7)). The safe route is the court process above, carried out by a court officer.

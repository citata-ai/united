## Which lease length is best?

The best lease length depends on your market, your tenant and your turnover cost. For most rentals, 12 months is the right default. Use other lengths for a reason.

| Term | Best for | Main risk | Pricing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 months | Tenant in transition; filling a gap to reach peak season | Two turnovers a year | Premium over 12-month rent |
| 12 months | Most rentals | Lease end may land in a slow season | Market rent |
| 18 months | Moving the lease end to peak season; stable tenant | Rent locked longer | Market rent, maybe a step |
| 24 months | Proven tenant; slow market; costly turnover | Rent locked 2 years | Market rent with a year-2 step |
| Month-to-month | Short stays; tenant after the term ends | Tenant can leave with short notice | Premium over 12-month rent |

## Why do most leases run 12 months?

Most leases run 12 months because the term balances income and flexibility. You get a full year of rent with at most one turnover. You also get a yearly point to adjust rent and terms.

A 12-month term is also what most tenants, lenders and rent platforms expect. Applicants compare listings by 12-month rent. A different term can confuse them, so explain it in the listing.

A lease longer than one year adds a formal step in many states. California Civil Code 1624(a)(3) says an agreement to lease for longer than one year must be in writing to be enforceable. A written lease is good practice for every term.

## When is a 2-year lease smart?

A 2-year lease is smart when keeping the tenant is worth more than the chance to raise rent in year 2. Three cases fit:

1. **A proven tenant.** They paid on time for a year and kept the unit well.
2. **A flat or falling market.** If rents may drop, a locked rent protects you.
3. **High turnover cost.** A large or unusual home may take a long time to lease.

Put a rent step in the lease for year 2, for example 3%. Write the exact dollar amount so there is no dispute.

Check local law. Some cities and states limit rent increases or require just cause to end a tenancy. California Civil Code 1946.2 limits ending a tenancy without just cause after a tenant has lawfully occupied a unit for 12 months. A long lease does not change those rules.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do I align lease ends with peak season?

Align lease ends with peak season by choosing an odd lease length. Many markets lease faster in late spring and summer, but check your own market's data. If your 12-month leases end in December, each turnover lands in a slow month.

**Worked example:** a lease starts on November 1.

| Term | Lease ends | Season of next leasing |
|---|---|---|
| 12 months | October 31 | Fall |
| 15 months | January 31 | Winter |
| 18 months | April 30 | Spring |
| 20 months | June 30 | Summer |

An 18-month or 20-month term moves the turnover into spring or summer. After that, offer 12-month renewals so it stays there.

Track your own days on market by month for 2 years. Then you will know your real peak season, not a guess.

## Should I charge more for short leases?

Charge more for a short lease, because it costs you more. A 6-month lease can mean 2 turnovers a year instead of 1.

**Worked example:** rent is $2,000 for a 12-month lease. Each turnover costs about $3,000, including vacant days, cleaning and paint (example numbers).

| Term | Turnovers per year | Turnover cost per year | Extra cost per month vs 12-month |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 months | 1 | $3,000 | — |
| 6 months | 2 | $6,000 | $250 |

So a 6-month lease needs about $250 a month more to break even, in this example. Many landlords add a smaller premium and accept some extra risk. Use your own turnover cost.

Short leases also change some federal rules. The EPA Lead-based Paint Disclosure Rule does not apply to leases of 100 days or less where no renewal or extension can occur. For longer leases of pre-1978 housing, the disclosure is required unless another exemption applies.

## What about month-to-month?

Month-to-month gives both sides flexibility, at the cost of stability. The tenant can leave with the notice your state sets. You can change terms with notice, within local rent and just-cause rules.

Use month-to-month in two cases:

- **After a fixed term ends,** when the tenant wants to stay but not sign again.
- **For a short planned stay,** at a premium.

Many leases convert to month-to-month by default at the end of the term. Write what happens at the end of the term in your lease, so neither side is surprised.

## How do I decide for one unit?

Use this checklist for each unit:

- [ ] What is my turnover cost? (vacant days × daily rent + work)
- [ ] When is my local peak leasing season?
- [ ] When would a 12-month lease end?
- [ ] Is the market rising, flat or falling?
- [ ] Is this tenant proven?
- [ ] What do state and city rules say about rent changes and just cause?

Then pick the term and write the end date and any rent step in the lease.

The national rental vacancy rate was 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026, says the U.S. Census Bureau. Your local rate tells you more about how fast you can re-lease.

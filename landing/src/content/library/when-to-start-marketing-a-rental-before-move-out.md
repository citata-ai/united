## How early should I list a rental?

List a rental as soon as the current tenant gives notice. Each day you wait is a day the unit may sit empty later. If the tenant gives 30 to 60 days of notice, you can often sign a new lease before the old tenant leaves.

Vacancy is a real cost. The U.S. Census Bureau reported a national rental vacancy rate of 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026. Your local rate may be higher or lower. A short gap between tenants still costs rent.

**Example vacancy cost:** rent is $2,100 a month, or about $69 a day. If early marketing cuts the gap from 30 days to 8 days, you keep 22 days of rent: about $1,518.

Before you list, have 3 things ready:

- [ ] The new rent, based on current local listings.
- [ ] Your turnover plan: what work the unit needs and how long it takes.
- [ ] Your screening criteria in writing.

## Can I show a unit while the tenant still lives there?

You can usually show an occupied unit, if the lease allows it and you follow your state's entry law. Most state laws list showings to prospective tenants as a valid reason to enter.

California Civil Code 1954 lists exhibiting the unit to prospective tenants as a reason to enter. It also says the landlord may not abuse the right of access or use it to harass the tenant.

Washington RCW 59.18.150 says a tenant shall not unreasonably withhold consent to a showing to prospective tenants after at least one day's notice. It also says a landlord shall not unreasonably interfere with the tenant's enjoyment by excessive showings.

Respect the current tenant. They still pay rent and still live there. A cooperative tenant helps you lease faster.

**Ways to make showings easier:**

1. **Group showings** into 1 or 2 time blocks each week.
2. **Pre-screen applicants** by phone or form before any showing.
3. **Ask the tenant** which times work best.
4. **Offer a thank-you,** such as a small credit, if your state allows it and the tenant helps.
5. **Use a video tour** for early-stage interest, so only serious applicants come in person.

## How much notice do I give for showings?

Give the notice your state's entry law requires, and give it in writing. Notice rules differ by state.

| State | Notice for showings | Source |
|---|---|---|
| California | Reasonable written notice; 24 hours presumed reasonable; 6 days if mailed; normal business hours | Civil Code 1954(d) |
| Washington | At least 2 days' written notice for entry in general; tenant may not unreasonably refuse a showing after 1 day's notice | RCW 59.18.150 |

Washington requires the notice to state the date and time window and a phone number to object or reschedule. Use that format in every state. It is clear and fair.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What photos do I use for an occupied unit?

Use photos from the last vacancy if the unit has not changed. Empty-room photos show the space better than photos full of someone else's furniture.

If you have no good photos:

- **Ask the tenant's consent** before you photograph their home. Agree on the time.
- **Shoot the fixed features:** kitchen, bathrooms, windows, floors, outside.
- **Avoid personal items:** family photos, mail, medicine, children's rooms.
- **Retake photos** after the turnover and update the listing.

Write the listing with care. Fair Housing rules apply to every ad. The HUD rule at 24 CFR 100.75 bans ads that show a preference based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status or national origin. That includes the words and the photos you choose. Describe the home, not the ideal tenant.

## How do I set the available date?

Set the available date as the move-out date plus your normal turnover time. Do not list the move-out date itself. If the old tenant leaves on the 31st, you need time to inspect, clean, paint and repair.

| Turnover scope | Typical plan | Available date if move-out is the 31st |
|---|---|---|
| Light (clean, touch-up) | 3 to 5 days | 5th of next month |
| Medium (paint, carpet clean, small repairs) | 7 to 10 days | 10th of next month |
| Heavy (flooring, appliances) | 14 days or more | 15th of next month or later |

Use your own records. Time your last 3 turnovers. Then add a buffer of 2 days.

**Watch for:** a tenant who does not leave on time. Write in the new lease what happens if the unit is not ready, and follow your state's rules on delayed possession.

## What is the step-by-step plan?

Use this plan from the day notice arrives:

1. **Day 0:** confirm the move-out date in writing. Remind the tenant of showing terms in the lease.
2. **Day 1:** set the rent and the available date.
3. **Day 2:** post the listing with past photos.
4. **Day 3 onward:** pre-screen leads and book group showings with proper notice.
5. **Day 7 to 21:** take applications and screen.
6. **Before move-out:** sign the new lease with the planned available date.
7. **Move-out day:** inspect and start the turnover.
8. **Available date:** final walk-through and new move-in.

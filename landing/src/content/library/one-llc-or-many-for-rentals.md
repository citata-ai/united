## What is the risk of one LLC holding all rentals?

One LLC holds its properties within the same legal entity, so ask counsel which assets may face that entity's obligations.

Do not assume a single entity separates each building merely because your bookkeeping uses property tags. Separate accounting helps identify income and costs. It does not by itself create a separate legal owner for each property.

An LLC also does not remove every form of personal exposure. Ask counsel about your own conduct, guarantees, funding, and the way you operate the entity. Review the actual loan and insurance documents. A generic claim about asset protection cannot answer those questions.

| Structure | Main question to review |
|---|---|
| Direct personal ownership | Which risks do insurance and state law address? |
| One LLC | Which assets share the entity's obligations? |
| Separate LLCs | Do the legal benefit and upkeep justify the cost? |
| Series LLC | Does the relevant state recognize and support the structure? |

Before moving title, ask the lender what the loan permits. Ask the insurer how the named insured and property owner should align. Ask the title professional about transfer costs and other effects. Creating an entity and transferring a mortgaged home are separate actions.

Write the purpose of the structure in plain terms. If you cannot explain which risk it addresses, ask for a clearer legal analysis before paying to add entities.

## What does a series LLC do?

A series LLC can establish separate series under a state's law, but its benefits and duties require a state-specific review.

The Texas Secretary of State explains that a Texas series LLC can designate series with separate rights, obligations, assets, and purposes. The office also says required formation language and separate asset records matter. That description concerns Texas law and its stated conditions.

Do not assume another state treats the structure the same way. Ask counsel about the property's location, the entity's formation state, and any registration duties. Also ask how courts, lenders, insurers, and title firms will treat the proposed arrangement.

A lower filing count does not mean no record work. The Texas FAQ specifically stresses separate records for series assets. Discuss how rent, bills, contracts, and bank activity will remain traceable. Ask who reviews those records for compliance.

Avoid buying a series package based solely on a marketing claim that it works nationwide. Request a written explanation for each state involved. Have your tax professional review the reporting treatment as well. State legal structure and federal tax classification answer different questions.

For a small portfolio, ordinary separate entities may be easier to understand in some circumstances. That is a choice for your advisers and facts, not a universal rule. Compare the complete operating burden before deciding.

## How much does each additional LLC cost?

Each additional LLC can create formation, annual, registration, professional, banking, and record costs that depend on the state and structure.

California's Franchise Tax Board says an LLC doing business or organized in California generally owes an $800 annual tax. Its page explains exceptions and timing. That is a California rule, not a nationwide LLC price. Review current rules before budgeting.

Use a narrow illustrative comparison. If 3 LLCs each owe that stated $800 tax, the combined annual tax is $2,400 before other costs. The example assumes the tax applies to each entity. It is not a complete cost estimate or a recommendation to form in California.

Request a full cost schedule from counsel and your tax professional. Include registered agent service, foreign registration where needed, state reports, tax work, and closure costs. Ask what happens when you sell a property but leave its entity active.

The IRS says a domestic single-member LLC generally defaults to disregarded status for income tax, unless it elects otherwise. A domestic LLC with multiple members generally defaults to partnership treatment. Those federal defaults do not erase state fees or legal duties.

Compare recurring entity costs with operating costs in your rental budget. The [fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) can help with management fees. Keep legal and entity administration on separate lines so the full annual burden stays visible.

## How should bank accounts work with multiple LLCs?

Set accounts and records to match the legal owners and the separation requirements your advisers identify.

Ask the bank which documents it needs for each entity. Use accurate legal names and authorized signers. Avoid paying personal expenses from rental entity accounts. Record every owner contribution, distribution, and loan clearly.

Match leases, vendor contracts, insurance, and management instructions to the correct owner. A separate bank account cannot correct a contract signed by the wrong party. Review the whole file when you transfer a property or add an owner.

Keep property-level records even when several homes share an entity. You still need to know which home earns income and incurs costs. IRS Publication 527 lists common rental expense categories and explains reporting. Give your preparer records that connect transactions to the right property and owner.

If a manager holds client money, review how the manager identifies each owner and property. An owner operating account and a manager trust account serve different purposes. Ask the manager to explain the flow of funds before changing payment details.

Before forming more entities, create a written maintenance plan. Name who handles filings, bank review, insurance updates, and tax records. Choose a structure you can operate correctly throughout ownership, not only on the day it is formed.

## Which 1099 forms does a property manager send to owners and vendors?

A property manager sends 2 kinds of 1099 forms: Form 1099-MISC to owners for rent, and Form 1099-NEC to vendors for services. A 1099 is an IRS information return. It tells the IRS, and the person paid, how much money changed hands in the year.

The rules below come from the IRS Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC, revision 12/2026. That revision covers 2026 payments, filed in early 2027.

| Who you paid | Form | Box | Threshold (tax year 2026) | Due to recipient | Due to IRS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Owner (rent you collected and paid over) | 1099-MISC | Box 1, Rents | $2,000 | January 31 | February 28 paper, March 31 electronic |
| Vendor for services (plumber, cleaner, handyman) | 1099-NEC | Box 1a | $2,000 | January 31 | January 31 |
| Attorney for legal fees | 1099-NEC | Box 1a | $2,000 | January 31 | January 31 |
| Vendor paid by credit card or payment app | None from you | Reported on 1099-K | Not applicable | Not applicable | Not applicable |
| Most corporations | Generally none | Not applicable | Not applicable | Not applicable | Not applicable |

For tax years beginning after 2025, the IRS raised the minimum threshold to $2,000. The IRS says it may be adjusted for inflation from 2027.

## Does a property manager send owners a 1099-MISC?

Yes. A property manager who collects rent from tenants sends each owner a Form 1099-MISC. The IRS instructions cover the case where a tenant pays a real estate agent or property manager. The agent "must use Form 1099-MISC to report the rent paid over to the property owner".

The tenant does not send the 1099. The IRS says rent paid to a property manager is generally not reported by the payer. The duty sits with the manager, who passes the rent to the owner.

A few points to get right:

1. **Use the owner's name and TIN from the W-9.** An owner can be a person, an LLC, a trust or a partnership.
2. **One form per owner, not per property.** Sum all rent paid over to that owner for the year.
3. **Report the rent paid over.** Check with your tax adviser how your software treats fees and expenses you paid from the rent.
4. **Owners who leave mid-year still get a form.** Report the months you managed.

## What box shows rent on a 1099-MISC?

Box 1, Rents, shows rent on Form 1099-MISC. For tax year 2026, the IRS instructions say to "enter amounts of $2,000 or more for all types of rents" in box 1.

A worked example. A manager runs 2 rentals for one owner in 2026.

| Line | House A | House B | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rent collected | $21,600 | $18,000 | $39,600 |
| Management fee (8%) | $1,728 | $1,440 | $3,168 |
| Repairs paid from rent | $900 | $1,250 | $2,150 |
| Net paid to owner | $18,972 | $15,310 | $34,282 |

The owner gets one Form 1099-MISC for all rent across both houses. Which figure goes in box 1 depends on how your accounting treats the fee and the repairs. Check how your software sets this. Agree on the method with your tax adviser, then use it the same way each year.

## When must 1099s go out?

Form 1099-NEC and Form 1099-MISC both go to recipients by January 31. The IRS filing dates differ:

- **Form 1099-NEC:** File with the IRS by January 31. The IRS instructions cite section 6071(c) for this date.
- **Form 1099-MISC:** File with the IRS by February 28 on paper, or March 31 if you file electronically.

For tax year 2026, those dates fall in early 2027. If a date falls on a weekend or holiday, check the IRS rule for the next business day.

Use this January timeline:

| Week | Task |
|---|---|
| First week of December | Ask every vendor and owner without a current W-9 for one |
| Last week of December | Lock vendor and owner records; check business types |
| First week of January | Run 1099 reports; review vendors near $2,000 |
| Second week of January | Fix names and TINs that do not match |
| By January 31 | Send 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC to recipients; file 1099-NEC with the IRS |
| By February 28 or March 31 | File 1099-MISC with the IRS |

## Do vendors need a W-9 first?

Yes. Collect a Form W-9 from each vendor before you pay them. The IRS says Form W-9 is how a person gives "your correct Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN)" to the payer who must file an information return. The vendor gives the W-9 to you. You do not send it to the IRS.

Without a W-9, you risk 2 problems:

1. **Wrong 1099s.** You cannot file a correct 1099-NEC without the vendor's legal name and TIN.
2. **Backup withholding.** The IRS says payers must withhold at "the current rate of 24 percent" when a payee fails to give a correct TIN.

The W-9 also shows the vendor's business type. IRS instructions say payments to a corporation are generally not reportable, with exceptions such as legal services. So a plumber that is an S or C corporation often needs no 1099-NEC. An LLC taxed as a sole owner does.

Two more IRS rules that cut your 1099 count:

- **Card and app payments.** Payments by credit card or third-party network go on Form 1099-K from the payment company. They "are not subject to reporting on Form 1099-MISC or Form 1099-NEC".
- **Goods only.** Payments for merchandise, freight, storage and similar items are not reported on 1099-NEC. Parts used in a service job are part of the service payment.

## What should a manager tell owners about 1099s?

Tell owners 3 things each December. These stop most January questions.

1. **When the form arrives.** By January 31.
2. **What box 1 means.** The rent you collected and paid over, by the method you agreed.
3. **What the owner sends out.** If the owner pays any vendor directly, the owner may have their own 1099 duties. Send them to their tax adviser.

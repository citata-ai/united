## Why is ranking in a second city hard?

Ranking in a second city is hard because Google's local results favor businesses close to the searcher. Google names three factors for local ranking: relevance, distance and prominence. It defines distance as "how far each business is from the customer who's searching" (Google Business Profile Help). You cannot pay to fix it: "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google."

Local search still matters most for owner leads. In the Goodjuju fall 2026 study of 35 property management companies, Google Search and Map Pack brought 50.6% of all leads (Goodjuju, 2026). So a city where you do not show in Google is a city where you get few owner leads.

There are three places to rank, and each one works in a different way:

| Where | What decides it | Can one office win a far city? |
|---|---|---|
| Map pack (the 3 map results) | Relevance, distance, prominence of your Business Profile | Rarely; distance works against you |
| Organic results (the blue links) | The page content, links and site quality | Yes, with a strong city page |
| AI answers (ChatGPT, Gemini, AI Overviews) | Public facts about you on your site and directories | Yes, if sources say you serve that city |

The plan: win the map pack near your office, and win organic and AI answers in the other cities with real city pages.

## Do I need a page for each city?

Yes, you need one page for each city where you manage homes and want owner leads. A single "Areas we serve" list with 30 city names gives Google and AI answers nothing to match a search like "property manager in Oak Park."

The page must be about that city, not a copy with a new name. Google's spam policy defines doorway abuse as "when sites or pages are created to rank for specific, similar search queries." One example it names is "multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" (Google Search Central). Google also lists scaled content abuse: "many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings."

A city page must hold facts that are only true for that city:

- [ ] The number of homes you manage in that city, with the date
- [ ] Local rent levels, with a source and date
- [ ] Local rules: rental license, registration, inspection, or rent rules, with a link to the city site
- [ ] The neighborhoods or ZIP codes you serve there
- [ ] One or two reviews from owners in that city
- [ ] A local photo you took, not a stock skyline
- [ ] The same owner button as your other pages: a rental analysis for that city
- [ ] Links to your fee page and owner services page

If you cannot fill most of this list for a city, you are not ready for a page there. Write the page after you sign your first few owners in that city.

## Can one office rank in a city 30 miles away?

One office can rank in organic results and AI answers for a city 30 miles away, but the map pack is a long shot. Distance is one of the three map factors, and your office is far from the searcher.

You can still add the city to your Business Profile service area. Google says a service area "shouldn't extend farther than about 2 hours of driving time from where your business is based," and that "for some businesses, larger service areas may be appropriate" (Google Business Profile Help). A service area tells Google where you work. It does not move your pin.

What helps in the far city:

1. **A strong city page.** It is the page Google can rank in organic results for "property management [city]."
2. **Directory listings that name the city.** Expertise.com, Yelp and the BBB list service areas. In our research, Expertise.com was a source in 93 of 150 AI answers about property managers (our data, 2026-10).
3. **Local reviews.** Ask owners in that city to name the city in their own words. Do not write the words for them.
4. **Local links.** A local landlord group, a chamber of commerce, a local news story.

## How do I avoid thin copy-paste city pages?

Avoid thin city pages with a fixed template of local facts, and a rule that no page goes live with fewer than 5 of them. "Thin" means a page with little that is unique to it.

A worked check, for a company with 6 city pages:

| City page | Doors there | Local rent source | Local rule link | Local reviews | Unique facts (of 5) | Keep? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home city | 180 | Yes | Yes | 12 | 5 | Keep |
| Suburb A | 45 | Yes | Yes | 4 | 5 | Keep |
| Suburb B | 20 | Yes | No | 2 | 3 | Fix |
| Suburb C | 6 | No | No | 0 | 1 | Merge |
| Suburb D | 0 | No | No | 0 | 0 | Remove |
| City 40 miles away | 0 | No | No | 0 | 0 | Remove |

The 5 facts in this example: doors there, rent source, rule link, local reviews, and a local photo. Numbers are an example.

What to do with weak pages:

- **Fix** pages with 3 or 4 facts. Add the missing ones.
- **Merge** small suburbs into one county or area page with a section for each.
- **Remove** pages for places where you have no doors. Redirect them to the nearest real page.

Do not use AI to write 50 city pages in one day. That is the pattern Google calls scaled content abuse. AI can help draft, but a person must add the local facts and check them.

## When does a second office make sense?

A second office makes sense when you already manage doors in that area, you can staff the office during business hours, and the map pack there is worth the cost. A second office gives you a second Business Profile with its own pin, which is the only real way to compete on distance.

Google's rules are strict here:

| Rule | Google's words |
|---|---|
| One profile per location | "Do not create more than one page for each location of your business" |
| No virtual offices | For a virtual office, "that location isn't eligible for a Business Profile" |
| No remote mailboxes | "P.O. boxes or mailboxes located at remote locations aren't acceptable" |
| Staffed hours | A service-area business "can't list a 'virtual' office unless that office is staffed during business hours" |
| Real name | "Your name should reflect your business's real-world name" |

Source: Google Business Profile guidelines. A fake office can get your profile suspended, which costs you the map pack in your home city too.

Checklist before you open a second office:

- [ ] You manage enough doors there to cover rent and staff
- [ ] A team member works there during the hours you list
- [ ] The office has a real address that you can show on signage
- [ ] Your name is the same on both profiles, with no city keywords added
- [ ] The second city page on your site links to the new profile

If you do not meet these, stay with one office. Win the map pack near home, and win the other cities with real pages, directories and reviews. Our [AI search checklist for a property management website](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website) covers the site side.

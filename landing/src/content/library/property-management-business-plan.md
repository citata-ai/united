## What should a property management business plan include?

A property management business plan should include the 8 sections the SBA lists for a traditional plan, plus a compliance section that fits this industry. The SBA names these sections: executive summary, company description, market analysis, organization and management, service or product line, marketing and sales, funding request, and financial projections.

Buildium's 2026 guide for property managers adds two sections that matter here: an operations plan (office, daily work, software) and compliance and risk management (licensing, insurance, risk). Property management handles other people's money. So a reader will look for how you protect it.

| Section | What to put in it for a PM company |
|---|---|
| Executive summary | Doors today, doors in 3 years, fee model, what you ask for |
| Company description | Entity, license holder, area served, property types |
| Market analysis | Rental stock, rents, competitors and their fees |
| Organization and management | Owners, broker of record, first hires |
| Services and fees | Management fee, leasing, renewal, maintenance fees |
| Marketing and sales | Owner lead sources, conversion rate, cost per new door |
| Operations | Software, maintenance model, office or remote |
| Compliance and risk | License, trust account, insurance, fair housing |
| Funding request | Amount, use of funds, repayment |
| Financial projections | Door forecast, revenue, costs, cash flow, break-even |

The SBA also describes a lean plan: a one-page model with key partnerships, activities, resources, value proposition, customer relationships, customer segments, channels, cost structure and revenue streams. Use a lean plan to think. Use a traditional plan when you ask a lender for money.

## Which market data do I need?

You need four kinds of market data: rental stock, rent levels, competitor fees and owner demand. Each one feeds a number in your forecast.

1. **Rental stock.** How many renter homes exist in your area, by type. Write down the source and the survey year for each count.
2. **Rent levels.** HUD Fair Market Rents give one consistent rent figure per bedroom size. Our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) lists FY2027 Fair Market Rents for the 50 largest metros. Add current asking rents from listing sites.
3. **Competitor fees.** List 10 local managers and their published fees. Note who hides prices.
4. **Owner demand.** How owners find managers in your city: referrals, search, AI answers, directories. Our guide on [why owner leads are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping) explains one shift in where owners look.

Write the source and date next to each number. A lender will ask.

## How do I forecast doors and revenue?

Forecast doors first, by month. Then build revenue from doors, rent and fees. A revenue goal with no door forecast under it is a wish.

Here is a worked example. All inputs are example inputs. Replace them with your own.

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Doors at start | 40 |
| New doors per month | 5 |
| Doors lost per month | 1 |
| Average rent | $1,600 |
| Management fee | 8% of rent collected |
| Leasing fee | 75% of one month's rent |
| Share of doors with a new lease each year | 40% |

The steps for year 1:

1. Net doors added: (5 − 1) × 12 = 48. Doors at month 12: 40 + 48 = 88.
2. Average doors in year 1: (40 + 88) ÷ 2 = 64.
3. Management fee revenue: 64 × $1,600 × 8% × 12 = $98,304.
4. Leasing fee revenue: 64 × 40% × $1,600 × 75% = $30,720.
5. Year 1 revenue: $98,304 + $30,720 = $129,024.

The example skips rent loss from vacancy and new doors that come in vacant. Add both in your own plan. Show a low, base and high case. In the low case, cut new doors per month in half.

To test your fee level against local rents, run the numbers through the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## Which costs do I plan for year 1?

Plan for fixed costs that start on day 1 and variable costs that grow with doors. Most new firms underplan the second group.

| Cost | Fixed or variable | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Broker license and renewals | Fixed | Check your state real estate commission |
| Entity setup, legal, accounting | Fixed | Management agreement and lease reviewed by a local attorney |
| Errors and omissions and general liability insurance | Fixed | Get quotes before you set prices |
| Property management software | Variable | Many vendors price per unit, often with a minimum |
| Trust account bank fees | Mostly fixed | Separate account for owner and tenant money |
| Website and owner lead marketing | Fixed plus variable | Cost per new door is the key number |
| Staff | Step cost | Each hire adds a full salary at once |
| Your own pay | Fixed | Put a real salary in the plan |
| Phone, office, vehicle | Fixed | Remote work cuts office cost |

Get a written quote for each fixed cost. Put the quote date in the plan.

## Do banks want a plan for a PM company?

Yes, most lenders want a written plan, and the SBA says traditional plans are the ones "lenders and investors commonly request." For lean plans, the SBA notes that "some lenders and investors may ask for more information."

If you apply for an SBA 7(a) loan, the SBA says the maximum loan is $5 million and that "your lender will help you determine which documents you'll need." A property management company has few hard assets. So a lender reads the door forecast, the owner contracts and the cash flow with care.

Make the plan easy to check:

- [ ] Every forecast input is in one table.
- [ ] Every market number has a source and a date.
- [ ] The plan shows break-even month and the cash you need to reach it.
- [ ] Signed management agreements are listed by door count.
- [ ] The license holder and trust account are named.

This is general information, not legal or tax advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney or a tax professional.

## Fill-in template

Copy this outline into your document.

```text
1. Executive summary
   - Company name, entity, license holder
   - Doors today: __  Doors in 36 months: __
   - Fee model: __% of rent / $__ flat
   - Funding request: $__ for __

2. Company description
   - Area served: __
   - Property types: single-family / small multifamily / HOA / other
   - Owner profile: __

3. Market analysis
   - Renter-occupied homes in area: __ (source, date)
   - Rent by bedroom size: __ (source, date)
   - 10 competitors with published fees: table
   - How owners find managers here: __

4. Organization and management
   - Owners and roles
   - Broker of record
   - Hiring plan by door count

5. Services and fees
   - Management fee, leasing fee, renewal fee, maintenance fees
   - What each fee covers

6. Marketing and sales
   - Owner lead channels and expected leads per month
   - Lead-to-signed rate: __%
   - Cost per new door: $__

7. Operations
   - Software, maintenance model, office
   - Key SOPs: move-in, move-out, arrears, owner payouts

8. Compliance and risk
   - License, trust account, insurance, fair housing training

9. Funding request
   - Amount, use of funds, repayment plan

10. Financial projections (36 months)
   - Door forecast by month
   - Revenue: management, leasing, renewal, other
   - Costs: fixed, variable, staff
   - Cash flow and break-even month
   - Low, base, high cases
```

Next step: fill in section 10 first. If the door forecast does not reach break-even inside your cash runway, change the plan before you write the rest.

## When do lead paint rules apply to a voucher unit?

Lead paint rules apply to a voucher unit when the building is older than 1978 and a child under 6 lives there or will live there. That is the scope of 24 CFR part 35, subpart M, the HUD lead rule for tenant-based rental help. It covers the unit, the common areas the family passes through, play areas, and exterior painted surfaces.

Some housing is exempt under 24 CFR 35.115:

| Exempt case | Rule |
|---|---|
| Built on or after 1978-01-01 | 35.115(a)(1) |
| Zero-bedroom unit or SRO | 35.115(a)(2) |
| Housing for the elderly or for persons with disabilities, unless a child under 6 lives or is expected to live there | 35.115(a)(3) |
| A certified lead-based paint inspection found no lead-based paint | 35.115(a)(4) |
| All lead-based paint removed with clearance (not by enclosure or encapsulation) | 35.115(a)(5) |

One more rule applies to most pre-1978 rentals, child or not. Before the lease, you must give the tenant the EPA pamphlet "Protect Your Family From Lead in Your Home" and disclose any known lead-based paint or hazards (24 CFR 35.88). For subpart M units, you must also build ongoing lead-safe maintenance into your building routine (24 CFR 35.1220).

## When does a visual check apply?

A visual check applies at every initial and periodic inspection of a covered unit. Under 24 CFR 35.1215(a), the PHA's inspector, trained in visual assessment, checks all painted surfaces for deteriorated paint.

24 CFR 35.110 defines deteriorated paint as paint that is "peeling, chipping, chalking or cracking", or paint that is damaged or separated from the surface under it. So chalky exterior trim counts, not just big flakes.

If the inspector finds deteriorated paint, you must stabilize it. The deadline depends on the inspection:

| Inspection | Your deadline | Extension |
|---|---|---|
| Initial (before move-in) | Before assisted occupancy starts | — |
| Periodic (family already in the unit) | 30 days after the PHA notice | PHA may extend for reasonable cause, up to 90 days from the notice |

Source: 24 CFR 35.1215(b) and (d). Paint stabilization is complete only when the unit passes a clearance exam under 24 CFR 35.1340. Until then, the unit violates the housing quality standards, which can lead to HAP abatement.

## What if a child under 6 lives in the unit?

A child under 6 is what makes subpart M apply at all. When a child under 6 lives in a pre-1978 unit, every initial and periodic inspection includes the visual check above.

Also do the ongoing maintenance that 24 CFR 35.1355(a) requires. The rule calls for a visual check for deteriorated paint and bare soil at each unit turnover and every 12 months. It also requires safe work practices for any work that disturbs paint that may contain lead.

Watch for: a family can grow during a lease. A new baby brings a unit into subpart M at the next inspection, even if it passed before.

## What is an EBLL case?

An EBLL case is a child under 6 with an elevated blood lead level. 24 CFR 35.110 sets the level as the one in the most recent HHS (US Department of Health and Human Services) guidance for an environmental response. HUD publishes a notice when that level changes.

When a doctor or health department reports an EBLL case, a fixed sequence starts under 24 CFR 35.1225:

1. **PHA verifies.** If the report comes from someone other than a medical provider, the PHA verifies it with the health department or provider at once.
2. **Environmental investigation, 15 days.** The PHA must complete it within 15 calendar days of notice. It sends you the report at once.
3. **Hazard reduction, 30 days.** You must reduce the hazards within 30 calendar days of receiving the report, by interim controls or abatement.
4. **Clearance.** The work is complete only when clearance shows all identified hazards were treated.
5. **Notice to residents.** You notify building residents of the evaluation and work (35.125).
6. **Reports, 5 business days.** If another medical provider tells you of the case, report the child's name and address to the public health department within 5 business days. Also report each confirmed case to the HUD field office and the HUD Office of Lead Hazard Control and Healthy Homes within 5 business days.

If you miss step 3, the unit is in violation of the housing quality standards.

## Who can do the paint repairs?

A trained worker must do paint repairs above small-area limits. Under 24 CFR 35.1330(a)(4), a person doing interim controls must have OSHA hazard communication training (29 CFR 1926.59). That person must also either work under a certified lead abatement supervisor or complete an approved course, such as an EPA-accredited renovator course.

The de minimis limits in 24 CFR 35.1350(d) set when safe work practices are not required:

- 20 square feet on exterior surfaces;
- 2 square feet in any one interior room or space;
- 10% of a small component, such as a window sill, baseboard or trim.

Above those limits, the worker must protect occupants, prepare the worksite, and clean with HEPA vacuums or an equal method (35.1350(b), (c)). Some paint removal methods are banned (35.140).

Worked example: a bedroom has 3 peeling spots on a wall, 1.5 sq ft in total, and a peeling window sill. The wall work is under the 2 sq ft room limit. Scraping the whole sill is more than 10% of that small component. Use a trained worker for the sill, and still pass clearance for the stabilization.

## What records must I keep?

Keep the lead disclosure attachment or lease for at least 3 years from the start of the lease (24 CFR 35.92(c)). Keep your own copies of everything else in the unit file. The PHA, as the designated party, must keep notices, evaluations and clearance reports for at least 3 years (24 CFR 35.175). Your copy protects you if the PHA asks again.

Checklist for each pre-1978 voucher unit:

- [ ] Signed lead disclosure and EPA pamphlet receipt, with the lease.
- [ ] Year built, and any lead inspection that found no lead-based paint.
- [ ] PHA visual assessment results from each inspection.
- [ ] Work orders for each paint stabilization, with square footage.
- [ ] Worker training certificates (renovator or abatement course).
- [ ] Clearance exam reports.
- [ ] Notices to occupants of clearance results (35.1215(c)).
- [ ] Turnover and 12-month visual checks under 35.1355.
- [ ] Any EBLL notices, investigation reports and your reports to HUD.

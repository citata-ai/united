## What are fixed and variable rental costs?

Fixed rental costs stay about the same each month whether the home is full or empty. Variable costs move with tenants, repairs and the season. Split them, because fixed costs set your break-even rent.

**Fixed costs**

| Expense | How to budget it |
|---------|------------------|
| Mortgage principal and interest | Loan statement |
| Property tax | County bill; ask what it is at your price |
| Landlord insurance | Policy declarations page |
| HOA dues | HOA budget |
| Rental license or registration | City fee schedule |
| Flood insurance, if needed | Separate quote |
| Software and bank fees | Vendor price |

**Variable costs**

| Expense | How to budget it |
|---------|------------------|
| Repairs and maintenance | A share of rent, then your own history |
| Owner-paid utilities | Last 12 months of bills |
| Vacancy | Days empty × daily rent |
| Management fee | Percent of rent collected |
| Leasing fee | Percent of one month's rent, per new lease |
| Lease renewal fee | Flat fee per renewal |
| Turnover cleaning and paint | Quote per move-out |
| Legal and eviction costs | Rare, but budget a small amount |
| Advertising and screening | Per vacancy |
| Travel to the property | Mileage log |

For the fee lines, iPropertyManagement's 2026 survey reports an average management fee of 8.49% of monthly rent. It reports an average leasing fee of 70.6% of one month's rent, when the manager also manages the home. Plug your own quotes into our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

For vacancy, the Census Bureau reported a national rental vacancy rate of 7.3% in the second quarter of 2026. Use a local number where you can.

IRS Publication 527 lists the most common deductible rental expenses. They are advertising, auto and travel, cleaning and maintenance, commissions, depreciation and insurance. The list goes on with other interest, legal and professional fees, local transportation and management fees. It ends with mortgage interest, points, rental payments, repairs, taxes and utilities.

## What are capital expenses vs repairs?

A repair keeps the home in its normal working state. A capital expense makes it better, restores it, or adapts it to a new use. The tax treatment differs, so keep the two apart in your books.

IRS Publication 527 sets the test. An expense is an improvement if it results in a betterment, restores the property, or adapts it to a new or different use. You capitalize improvements and depreciate them. You can deduct repairs in the year you pay them, if you do not have to capitalize them.

| Usually a repair | Usually a capital expense |
|------------------|---------------------------|
| Fix a leaking faucet | Replace all the plumbing |
| Patch a roof leak | New roof |
| Repaint a room between tenants | Add a bedroom |
| Replace a broken window pane | Install storm windows across the house |
| Service the furnace | Replace the furnace |
| Fix a section of fence | Build a new fence |

Publication 527's Table 1-1 lists improvements. Examples are a new roof, a heating system, central air, a water heater, a fence, a deck and wiring upgrades.

Two IRS safe harbors can help small owners. The de minimis safe harbor lets you deduct items up to $2,500 per invoice or item if you do not have an audited financial statement. The safe harbor for small taxpayers covers yearly work on a building with an unadjusted basis of $1 million or less. The cap is the lesser of $10,000 or 2% of that basis (IRS Tangible Property Regulations FAQ).

This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

## How much to set aside for maintenance?

Set aside 2 amounts: a monthly maintenance budget for small repairs, and a capital reserve for the large items. One fund for both runs dry at the worst time.

A starting point for maintenance: Baselane reports that at the median rental property, maintenance spending equaled 8.0% of rent receipts in 2023. It cites an analysis of the Census Rental Housing Finance Survey. The same Baselane page reports that 90.2% of rental properties reported a capital reserve balance of zero in 2023.

Worked example, with example rent:

| Line | Monthly | Yearly |
|------|---------|--------|
| Rent | $2,000 | $24,000 |
| Maintenance at 8% of rent | $160 | $1,920 |
| Capital reserve (your choice, for example 5%) | $100 | $1,200 |
| Total set aside | $260 | $3,120 |

How to size the capital reserve: list each large item, its age, and a replacement quote. Divide the quote by the years left. For example, a $9,000 roof with 10 years left needs $900 a year. Sum the lines.

Watch for: older homes need more. A 1960s house with original pipes is not an 8% house.

## What one-time costs come at the start?

One-time start costs come before the first rent check. Many owners leave them out of the first-year budget.

| Start cost | Tax treatment (IRS) |
|------------|---------------------|
| Purchase closing costs: title, recording, transfer taxes, legal fees, surveys | Added to basis (Publication 551) |
| Loan costs: mortgage commissions, abstract and recording fees | Capital costs, part of basis (Publication 527) |
| Points | Prepaid interest, deducted over the loan term in most cases (Publication 527) |
| Make-ready repairs before the first tenant | Repair or improvement; apply the test above |
| Appliances and furniture | Depreciated, or deducted under the de minimis safe harbor if eligible |
| Manager setup fee | iPropertyManagement found a 2026 average of $185.24 |
| First leasing fee | Percent of one month's rent |
| Rental license and first inspection | City fee |
| Locks rekeyed, smoke and CO alarms | Small items, often deductible |

Publication 527 also covers the start date. You can deduct ordinary and necessary costs to manage, conserve or maintain a rental from the time you make it available for rent.

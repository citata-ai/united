## How much higher are investment property rates?

There is no fixed rate premium that applies to every rental mortgage. Fannie Mae's occupancy guide applies a loan-level price adjustment to investment properties. A lender can reflect loan costs through the offered rate, points, or other pricing terms.

Do not turn a pricing adjustment into a claimed interest-rate increase without a lender quote. The final offer also depends on the borrower, property, loan purpose, and market conditions. A headline average cannot price your file.

Compare offers on the same basis:

| Quote detail | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Date and lock | Market prices can change |
| Occupancy | Rental and primary-home treatment differ |
| Loan amount | Different balances can change pricing |
| Term | Payment and total cost differ |
| Points | Upfront cash can change the offered rate |
| Property | Unit count and other features matter |
| Purpose | Purchase and cash-out offers can differ |

The CFPB advises comparing loan offers consistently and notes that rates can change daily. For a business-purpose loan, ask which disclosures apply. Request a written cost breakdown even if a standard consumer Loan Estimate is not part of the transaction.

## What lender rules differ for rentals?

Investment occupancy can change required cash, reserves, eligibility, and pricing. Ask the lender to identify the program and its rules for your property. Do not assume a previous home mortgage approval covers a rental purchase.

Fannie Mae's reserve guide lists 6 months of reserves for an investment property transaction through Desktop Underwriter. Additional financed properties can require more. Other products and lender rules can differ, so get the amount for your file.

Ask about down payment, credit, debt, title, lease records, and property condition. Ask which facts could change the offer after appraisal or underwriting. Keep a note of documents still required.

Tell the lender whether you intend to live in the property. Fannie Mae distinguishes principal residences, second homes, and investment properties. Choose the category that matches the facts rather than the one with the lowest advertised rate.

Also get a rental-use insurance quote and a supported property tax estimate. The mortgage payment is only part of the cost of ownership. A loan that qualifies can still leave too little cash for repairs, leasing, and future replacements.

## Does rental income help you qualify?

Rental income can help under the loan program's documentation and calculation rules. It does not automatically count dollar for dollar. Ask the lender to show how much income it accepts and why.

Fannie Mae's current rental income guide considers documents, property use, and management experience. It can restrict how qualifying income enters the borrower calculation. That calculation serves underwriting, not your personal spending plan.

Prepare signed leases, tax returns, a rent history, and other records requested by the lender. Explain a vacancy, recent purchase, conversion, or major repair period. Missing context can make a rent figure hard to assess.

For a purchase with tenants, compare the seller's rent roll with actual receipts. Do not assume a projected increase belongs in qualifying income. Ask the lender how it treats the existing lease and supported market rent.

Build your own budget separately. Use expected collections less actual operating costs and the full debt payment. Include management if you will hire help. The [management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) helps you keep that cost visible rather than hiding it inside an optimistic net-rent assumption.

## How can you get a better loan offer?

You can improve the comparison by giving lenders the same accurate file and asking for complete terms. Ask which borrower or property facts drive the price. Do not assume buying points is always the best answer.

Request options with different upfront costs. Compare the cash paid now with the payment difference over your likely holding period. Keep enough liquidity for closing and operations before choosing a higher cash requirement.

In an assumed example, paying $3,000 upfront saves $50 a month. Simple break-even is 60 months. That example ignores tax effects, investment returns on the cash, and a later sale or refinance. It shows a method, not a recommended point purchase.

Ask whether the rate is locked and what can change it. Check the term, rate type, prepayment costs, lender fees, and third-party charges. A low quote with incomplete terms is not ready for comparison.

Use the CFPB's guidance on comparing offers as a question framework. For any loan outside consumer disclosure rules, ask for an equivalent written schedule. Keep the same planned use and closing timeline in every request.

## What should you check before choosing?

Choose the offer that fits your total cash plan and risk, not only the smallest rate on the page. Confirm the usable loan amount, funds needed, ongoing payment, and reserve rules.

Stress-test lower rent and a repair in the same period. Keep a separate case for any variable payment. Ask whether the property can carry the loan without a future refinance.

Check the final paperwork against the written offer. Resolve new charges and changed conditions before signing. Keep copies of the terms used in the decision so you can review performance later.

Your next step is to request matched quotes for the actual rental and occupancy. Give each lender the same facts. Then compare total costs over the period you expect to own the loan, with enough cash left to operate the property.

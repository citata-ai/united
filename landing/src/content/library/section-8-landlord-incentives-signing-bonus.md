## What is a landlord signing bonus?

A signing bonus is a one-time payment when you sign a HAP contract with the PHA. Delaware State Housing Authority (DSHA) shows how it works in its May 2026 flyer:

- **First unit:** "New landlords who sign a HAP contract for their first HCV leased unit, are eligible for a $1,000 signing bonus."
- **More units:** existing and new landlords get "$500 for each new unit they bring into the HCV program."
- **Cap:** five units, "for a total of $3,000."

Some bonuses target special voucher types. The Santa Cruz County housing authority pays "a one-time $2,000 bonus" for renting to a holder of certain vouchers. The list includes Family Unification, VASH (for veterans), Mainstream and Stability Vouchers. The bonus applies only to units in Santa Cruz County.

## What is a vacancy loss payment?

A vacancy loss payment covers rent you lose between two voucher tenants. It rewards you for keeping the unit in the program. DSHA offers "up to one month's rent" when a unit that housed a voucher tenant is rented to a new voucher tenant. The amount "will match the contract rent paid by the previous HCV tenant." DSHA pays only "upon completion of a new Housing Assistance Payment (HAP) contract."

Santa Cruz County's Owner Assurance Program also lists "vacancy loss due to lease termination" as a cost it can repay.

## How does a damage mitigation fund work?

A damage fund repays costs above the security deposit. The rules differ a lot by program.

| Program | Maximum | Main conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Santa Cruz County Owner Assurance Program | Up to $5,000 | Costs exceed the deposit; you tried to collect from the tenant; you have records; you file within 60 days of move-out |
| DSHA damage claim | Repair cost or two months' contract rent, minus the deposit | Proof of damage; you re-lease to another voucher holder on DSHA's waiting list |
| Oregon Housing Choice Landlord Guarantee (ORS 456.378) | $500 to $5,000 per tenancy | A court judgment against the tenant; claim within one year |
| Washington Landlord Mitigation Program | Up to $1,000 for pre-move-in improvements, plus other claim types | Damage beyond normal wear; landlord pays the first $500 of improvements |

Santa Cruz County covers tenant-caused damage, unpaid rent and utilities, vacancy loss, and legal fees tied to lease termination. It warns that "reimbursement is dependent on availability of funds."

## Which PHAs offer incentives?

There is no national list we could verify. Each PHA or state decides. These are the programs we read for this article, on 2026-10-19:

| Where | Incentive types | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Delaware (DSHA) | Signing bonus, vacancy loss, damage claims, security deposit up to first month's rent, landlord liaison | DSHA flyer, May 2026 |
| Santa Cruz County, CA | Damage cover up to $5,000; $2,000 bonus for special voucher types | HACSC owner incentives page |
| Oregon (state) | Landlord guarantee for damage, $500 to $5,000 | PRRAC, ORS 456.378 |
| Washington (state) | Landlord mitigation fund | PRRAC, ESHB 2578 (2018) |
| Virginia (state) | Tax credit of 10% of the fair market rent for qualified units; $250,000 cap a year statewide | PRRAC, Va. Code 58.1-439.12:04 |
| Illinois (state) | Property tax abatement in housing opportunity areas | PRRAC, 35 ILCS 200/18-173 (tax years 2004 to 2024 unless extended) |

To find your local offer, follow these steps:

1. **Open your PHA's landlord page.** Look for "incentives," "owner assurance" or "landlord partnership."
2. **Ask the landlord liaison.** DSHA, for example, names one in its flyer.
3. **Check your state housing agency.** State funds like Oregon's and Washington's sit outside the PHA.
4. **Get the rules in writing.** Note deadlines, caps and the claim form.

## Do incentives work, per HUD OIG?

HUD's Office of Inspector General (OIG) looked at this in Memorandum 2021-LA-0803. NLIHC summarized it. OIG sent a questionnaire to the 39 PHAs in the Moving to Work (MTW) program. 34 answered.

| OIG finding (2016 to 2019) | Result |
|---|---|
| PHAs that used any incentive | 28 of 34 (82%) |
| Of those, used nonmonetary incentives | 27 (96%) |
| Used monetary incentives | 20 (71%) |
| Used both | 19 (68%) |
| Said incentives kept landlords in the program | 82% |
| Said incentives were less effective at adding units | 61% |

The monetary incentives PHAs rated most effective were new landlord bonuses, property damage repayment and vacancy loss or re-rent bonuses. Among nonmonetary ones, direct deposit and landlord portals rated highest. One PHA said its $100 bonus did not work for corporate landlords, because it created an accounting problem.

OIG also noted that the incentives cited as most effective met common landlord concerns, such as faster inspections and one point of contact. For an owner, that means a bonus matters less than a PHA that inspects and pays on time. Ask about both.

## What should I check before I count on an incentive?

Treat an incentive as a bonus, not as part of your budget. Before you rely on one, check these points:

- [ ] The program is open now and has funds left. Santa Cruz County warns that payment depends on funds.
- [ ] Your unit and your tenant's voucher type qualify.
- [ ] You know the trigger: a signed HAP contract, a court judgment or a re-lease.
- [ ] You know the deadline, such as 60 days after move-out in Santa Cruz County.
- [ ] You keep receipts, photos and the move-in report for any damage claim.

Incentives are extras. The base is the rent. Use our [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator) to estimate the HAP for a unit, and our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) for local Fair Market Rents.

Tax treatment of incentive payments is outside this article. This is general information, not tax advice. Ask a tax professional.

## Why can a home or landlord policy exclude short stays?

The policy's permitted use and exclusions determine whether short stays fit its coverage. NAIC warns that most homeowners or dwelling policies are not designed for accidents from short-term rentals. Tell the insurer exactly how you intend to host.

Describe whether you rent a room, the whole home, or a separate unit. Explain stay length, booking routes, personal use, and any services. Disclose pools, hot tubs, water access, pets, and unusual equipment where relevant.

Ask for written confirmation, policy wording, and any required endorsement. A general statement that rentals are allowed may not explain which rental use the insurer accepted. Keep the application and the final issued documents together.

Do not assume a landlord policy covers every short stay simply because it covers rent. Compare the stated occupancy and business-use terms. Ask what happens when the property sits empty or returns to personal use.

NAIC recommends discussing home sharing with the insurer and reviewing local and association restrictions. Insurance does not by itself grant permission to host. Check those permissions separately before you spend money on furnishings or accept a booking.

## What does platform host protection cover?

Platform protection covers only what its terms say it covers. Airbnb's AirCover page separates host damage protection from host liability insurance. Its published limits are not a complete description of eligible losses.

Airbnb advertises $3 million in host damage protection and $1 million in host liability insurance. Read the eligibility, exclusions, claims process, and applicable terms. Do not treat the damage program as identical to a property insurance policy.

Ask what applies to a stay booked outside the platform. Review when a guest's booking starts and ends for coverage purposes. Ask about a loss discovered after departure and about documentation deadlines in the actual terms.

Keep a property inventory and dated condition records. Save booking details, communication, repair quotes, and receipts. These records can support a claim, but they cannot make an excluded event covered.

Use this review table:

| Question | Document to check |
| --- | --- |
| Building damage | Your property policy and endorsements |
| Guest liability | Liability policy and platform terms |
| Lost bookings | Income-loss section and covered causes |
| Direct bookings | Policy wording for those stays |
| Guest damage process | Platform rules and evidence needs |
| Overlapping coverage | Insurer's explanation of coordination |

## What does a dedicated short-term rental policy cost?

A useful insurance price comes from a quote for your actual property and use. Do not budget from a broad national average without checking the terms. Location, building value, features, use, and deductibles can change the offer.

Give each broker the same facts and ask for the same coverage basis. Compare the building limit, contents, liability, loss of income, deductible, and excluded causes. A cheaper premium may buy less protection or leave more cash risk with you.

Ask how the insurer sets replacement cost and how a claim values damaged contents. Review whether cleaning staff, equipment, or guest activities need separate treatment. Note any conditions you must maintain during the policy term.

Keep the premium and deductible in different budget rows. The premium is a planned cost. A deductible is cash you may need when a covered loss occurs. Do not spend the deductible reserve on routine furnishing upgrades.

Compare the quote with your conservative booking plan. Include platform fees, cleaning, utilities, maintenance, and management. Use the [management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) only as a service-cost starting point. Ask for a quote that reflects short-term rental work.

## What liability limits should a host choose?

Choose liability limits with a licensed insurance adviser who understands your property and assets. There is no single limit that fits every host. Ask how the limit applies to each event and across the policy period.

Describe guest access and property features in detail. A broker cannot assess a pool or boat it does not know about. Ask whether the policy covers your ownership entity and the people involved in management.

If you consider an umbrella or excess policy, confirm that it accepts the underlying rental activity. Ask how it responds after the primary policy's limit. Do not assume a personal umbrella automatically follows a business-related rental claim.

Review legal defense costs and any separate sublimits in the actual contract. Ask what remains your responsibility after a large claim. A high headline limit can coexist with an exclusion that matters to your property.

NAIC advises owners to understand both their insurer's coverage and the home-sharing company's program. Keep the documents side by side. Request a written explanation of gaps and overlap rather than relying on the platform's summary page alone.

## What should you do before the first booking?

Finish the insurance review before the first guest arrives. Confirm the policy is in force for the correct use and address. Keep proof of coverage where the person managing the property can find it.

Walk through the home and record its condition. Create an inventory of furnishings and equipment. Keep receipts for major items and a secure backup of the records.

Set a claim response plan. Name the insurer contact, broker, platform contact, and person who can secure the property after a loss. Explain how staff should preserve evidence and report an incident.

Review coverage when you add amenities, change booking channels, alter stay length, or change the ownership structure. Tell the insurer before the change creates uncertainty. Your next step is a written coverage comparison for the exact way you plan to host, with every unresolved exclusion clearly marked.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

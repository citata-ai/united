## Which property management searches show an AI Overview?

AI Overviews show mostly on question searches, not on "near me" searches for a company. Ahrefs found that 99.2% of keywords that trigger an AI Overview have informational intent. Google says it in plainer words. An AI Overview shows only when it adds to classic results, so it "often" does not trigger.

For a property manager, that splits your owner searches in two groups.

| Search type | Example | Likely result |
|---|---|---|
| Owner question | "how much do property managers charge" | AI Overview plus links |
| Owner question, local | "is a leasing fee normal in Illinois" | AI Overview plus links |
| Company search | "property management company Naperville" | Local Pack, sometimes an AI Overview |
| Brand search | "Acme Property Management reviews" | Business Profile panel |

The local rows are less certain. Whitespark reports that AI Overviews showed on an average of 68% of local searches in its Q1 2025 study. Your own city may differ. The only safe way to know is to check your 20 most important owner searches by hand, in a private browser window, once a month.

## Does a top-10 ranking help you get cited?

A top-10 ranking helps a lot. Ahrefs studied 1.9 million citations from 1 million AI Overviews. It found that 76.10% of cited pages rank in Google's top 10. Another 9.50% rank in positions 11 to 100, and 14.40% do not rank in the top 100 at all.

The first link in an AI Overview had a median Google rank of 2 in the same study. So classic SEO is not dead for a property manager. It is the entry ticket.

Google's own page confirms the floor. To show as a supporting link, a page "must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet". Google adds that there are no other technical requirements. You do not need an llms.txt file, an "AI text file" or new schema to be eligible.

What this means for you:

1. **Fix the basics first.** Allow Googlebot in robots.txt and at your CDN or host. Check that each owner page is indexed in Search Console.
2. **Rank for the question, not only the city.** A page that answers "what does a property manager charge in Denver" can get cited for many fan-out searches.
3. **Use our checklist.** The [AI search checklist for property management websites](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website) covers crawl access, schema and page facts in one list.

## Do AI Overviews take clicks from property management sites?

AI Overviews take clicks from informational pages. Three studies point the same way.

| Study | Data | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Ahrefs, April 2025 | 300,000 keywords | 34.5% lower click rate for the top result when an AI Overview shows |
| Ahrefs, February 2026 | Same method, December 2025 data | 58% lower click rate for the top result |
| Pew Research, July 2025 | 900 US adults, 68,879 searches | Users clicked a result in 8% of visits with an AI summary, 15% without |

Pew also found that users clicked a link inside the AI summary in just 1% of visits. They ended the browsing session on 26% of pages with a summary, against 16% without one.

Here is a worked example with the Ahrefs 2026 numbers. Say your fee guide ranks first for a question with 1,000 searches a month. Without an AI Overview, Ahrefs forecasts a 3.7% click rate for position 1: about 37 visits. With an AI Overview, the measured rate was 1.6%: about 16 visits. The page still matters, because the AI Overview may quote it to all 1,000 searchers. But visits alone will under-count its value.

Google gives a different view. Its Search Central page says clicks from results pages with AI Overviews are "higher quality", meaning people spend more time on the site. We cannot check that claim with public data. Track your own owner form fills by source, as our guide on [why owner leads from Google are dropping](/guides/why-owner-leads-are-dropping) explains.

## What page formats does Google cite?

Google does not publish a list of formats it prefers for AI Overviews. Its guidance points to the same things it asks of every page. Here they are, as a property manager would apply them.

- **Text, not pictures of text.** Google asks that "important content is available in textual form". A fee table in an image cannot be quoted. Put fees in an HTML table.
- **Structured data that matches the page.** If your schema says "8% management fee", the visible page must say 8% too.
- **Internal links.** Google asks that content be "easily findable through internal links". Link each city page to your fee page and your owner FAQ.
- **A current Business Profile.** Google lists "Business Profile information is up-to-date" as one of its SEO basics for AI features.
- **Images and video that support the text.** Google asks for high-quality images and videos "when applicable". For a manager, that can be a photo of a real rental you manage next to the text about it.

From the Ahrefs data, the pages AI Overviews cite most are pages that already rank. Our working hypothesis: pages that answer one question in the first two sentences are easier to quote. We test this on our own site and do not treat it as proven.

## How do you write an owner page that AI Overviews can quote?

Write each owner page so the first sentence under each heading is the answer. A model can then lift it without your other text.

Use this step list for each page:

1. **Pick one owner question.** Example: "What does a property manager charge in Phoenix?"
2. **Answer it in the first 2 sentences.** Give a number, a range or a yes/no.
3. **Add a table.** Fees, what each fee covers, and when you charge it.
4. **Add 4 to 6 short FAQs.** Use the words owners type, such as "leasing fee" and "tenant placement fee".
5. **Date the page.** Show "Updated" with a real date. Change it only when you change facts.
6. **Link it.** Link from the home page, the city page and the fee calculator. Our free [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) is one example of a tool page owners use.
7. **Check it in Search Console.** Use URL Inspection to see that Googlebot gets the full text.

## How do you control or measure AI Overview results?

Google gives two controls and one report. The controls are robots.txt for Googlebot and the snippet rules: nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet and noindex. These limit what Search shows, AI features included. Most property managers should not use them, because they also cut normal snippets.

The report is the Search Console Performance report. Google says AI Overview and AI Mode traffic is counted there under the "Web" search type. It is not split out. To see the effect, compare clicks and impressions for your owner question pages over time. Rising impressions with flat clicks is the usual sign that an AI Overview now answers the question.

Watch for: a change to robots.txt or snippet rules is not instant. Google says a recrawl can take from several days to several months.

## What leasing and renewal fees do Las Vegas managers charge?

Las Vegas leasing fees run from a flat $200 to one full month of rent. Half of one month's rent is the most common figure in our table: Evernest, Keyrenter and Rentals America all use it. Utopia lists no leasing fee at all.

Renewal fees in Las Vegas are flat dollar amounts on every page that lists one: $100 (Shelter Realty), $250 (Evernest, Ziprent) and $300 (Rentals America). None of the 10 pages charges a percentage for a renewal.

The leasing fee often costs more than three months of management. That is why you should compare the cost of a full year, not the monthly rate.

## Which local rules add cost for Las Vegas owners?

Nevada law sets a few limits that change what a manager can collect for you. Three rules matter most for fees.

1. **Deposit cap.** NRS 118A.242 says a landlord may not take security, including the last month's rent, of more than 3 months' periodic rent.
2. **Deposit return.** The same statute sets a 30-day limit to return the rest of a deposit. A landlord who misses it can owe the full deposit plus up to the same amount again.
3. **Late fee cap.** NRS 118A.210 limits a late fee to 5% of the periodic rent. A $1,700 rent can carry a late fee of no more than $85.

Ask each manager who keeps the late fee. Some managers keep it as income, and that is a fee to you in all but name.

Rents also matter. HUD's FY2027 fair market rent for a 2-bedroom in the Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas area fell 2.8%, from $1,735 to $1,686. A lower rent makes a percentage fee smaller in dollars, but a flat fee stays the same. See the full table on the [Las Vegas fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/las-vegas-fair-market-rent-2027).

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Must a Las Vegas property manager hold a Nevada license?

Yes. A Las Vegas property manager needs two things under Nevada law. NRS 645.6052 says only a licensed real estate broker, broker-salesperson or salesperson may apply for a permit to engage in property management. The applicant must show at least 24 classroom hours of property management study.

The permit expires and renews with the license. Each renewal needs at least 3 hours of property management continuing education. Nevada also requires a signed property management agreement before the broker acts as your manager (NRS 645.6056). That agreement must state the fee.

Checklist before you sign:

- Ask for the license number and the property management permit.
- Check both with the Nevada Real Estate Division.
- Find the fee, the term and the cancellation terms in the written agreement.

## Flat fee or percent: which model is common in Las Vegas?

A percentage of rent is the common model in Las Vegas. Seven of the 10 managers in our table charge a percentage. Three charge a flat monthly fee: Rentals America ($99), Ziprent ($150) and Rytell ($159 to $199).

The break-even point is simple to find. Divide the flat fee by the percentage.

- $99 flat vs 8%: $99 / 0.08 = $1,238. Above $1,238 rent, the flat fee is cheaper.
- $150 flat vs 8%: $150 / 0.08 = $1,875.
- $159 flat vs 8%: $159 / 0.08 = $1,988.

At the FY2027 2-bedroom fair market rent of $1,686, a $99 flat fee is cheaper than 8%. A $150 or $159 flat fee costs more. But the monthly rate is only one line. Ziprent's $1,500 leasing fee is larger than its yearly savings at most Las Vegas rents.

What to do: ask three managers for the full fee schedule in writing. Put each into the year-1 and year-2 table above. Pick the lowest two-year total that still fits the service you need.

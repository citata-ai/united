## Which AI search claims are not supported by data?

Four claims come up again and again in agency pitches to property managers. Here is each one next to the best data we could find on 2026-10-19.

| Claim | What the data says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Add llms.txt and AI will cite you." | No measurable link between llms.txt and citations, across about 300,000 domains | SE Ranking |
| "Schema gets you into AI answers." | No clear citation gain on 1,885 pages that added JSON-LD | Ahrefs, 2026 |
| "SEO is dead." | Google AI features use the normal index and the same SEO basics | Google Search Central |
| "AI is already your main lead source." | AI was 0.5% of Ahrefs visits, but 12.1% of signups | Ahrefs, 2025 |

None of these tools is useless. The problem is the promise. Each one is a small part of the work, sold as the whole job.

I count citata.ai in this too. Our site has an llms.txt file and schema on every page. In our own test on 2026-10-18 and 2026-10-19, ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude named citata.ai in 0 of 108 non-brand answers (our data). The files did not make us visible.

## Does llms.txt make AI cite you?

No study shows that llms.txt makes AI cite a website. An llms.txt file is a plain text file at the root of a site that lists its key pages for AI tools.

SE Ranking checked about 300,000 domains. It found the file on 10.13% of them. It then tested if the file predicts how often AI cites a domain. It used a correlation test and a machine learning model. The model got more accurate when the team removed the llms.txt variable. SE Ranking's conclusion: llms.txt "doesn't impact how AI systems see or cite your content today."

Google also says you do not need "new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup" to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode.

What to do: if an agency adds llms.txt, fine. It takes an hour. Do not pay for it as a main deliverable, and do not expect citations from it.

## Does schema alone get AI citations?

Schema alone does not get AI citations. Schema is structured data in a page's code, often JSON-LD, that tells machines what the page is about.

Ahrefs tested this in 2026. It tracked 1,885 pages that added JSON-LD between August 2025 and March 2026. It compared them with control pages that never added it, 30 days before and after.

| Platform | Change in citations after schema |
|---|---|
| Google AI Overviews | -4.6% (small, cause unclear) |
| Google AI Mode | +2.4% (not different from zero) |
| ChatGPT | +2.2% (not different from zero) |

Ahrefs' summary: "Adding schema produced no major uplift in citations on any platform." Ahrefs also found that cited pages had schema more often. It explains this as site quality: well-kept sites tend to have schema, good content and links.

Google says there is "no special schema.org structured data" you need for AI features. Structured data must match the visible text.

What to do: keep valid, truthful schema for your company, address and reviews. It helps Google read your facts. Do not buy it as an AI citation fix.

## Is SEO dead?

SEO is not dead, because AI answers in Google come from the same index. Google says a page needs to be indexed and eligible to show a snippet to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. There are "no additional requirements" and no special tricks.

Google lists the same basics as before:

- **Let Google crawl the page** in robots.txt.
- **Link to important pages** from other pages on your site.
- **Put key content in text**, not only in images or PDFs.
- **Match structured data** to the visible text.

What changed is the click, not the index. Pew Research found that users clicked a normal result on 8% of visits with an AI summary and on 15% without one. So SEO still decides which pages AI reads. Fewer people click through after they read.

Watch for two opposite claims. "SEO is dead, buy GEO" is wrong. "Nothing changed, keep doing old SEO" is also wrong. GEO (getting cited by AI answers) uses SEO as its base and adds answers, listings and citation tracking. ## Is AI already the main lead source?

AI is not yet the main lead source for most websites. No public study shows that AI sends most leads to property managers.

The best public data point comes from Ahrefs, a large SEO tool company. In 30 days in mid-2025:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Share of Ahrefs visits from AI search | 0.5% |
| Share of Ahrefs signups from AI search | 12.1% |
| AI visitor conversion rate vs organic search | 23x higher |

So AI traffic was small but valuable. Ahrefs sells to marketers, not to rental owners. Your share can be different.

Our own data agrees on the small size. AI referral counts on citata.ai are low, and Google AI Overviews show up as normal google.com traffic. We use the numbers for trends, not totals.

What to do: measure your own share. Our guide on [tracking AI traffic](/guides/track-ai-traffic-property-management) shows how. Add a "How did you hear about us?" question to your owner intake form.

## What should a property manager do instead?

A property manager should spend first on the sources AI actually reads. We asked 3 AI engines "What is the best property management company in [city]?" for 50 US cities. In those 150 answers, the top sources were directories, not manager sites (our data, 2026-10-19):

| Source | Answers that cited it (of 150) |
|---|---|
| expertise.com | 93 |
| ipropertymanagement.com | 50 |
| yelp.com | 50 |
| reddit.com | 22 |
| bbb.org | 14 |

Checklist, in order:

1. **Fix your listings** on the directories AI cites for your city.
2. **Write direct answers with real numbers**: fees, leasing fee, service area, door count.
3. **Ask for honest reviews** from owners and tenants after real events.
4. **Track AI answers monthly**: same prompts, same engines, same log.
5. **Then add the small extras**: llms.txt and schema, at low cost.

Ask any agency for its before-and-after AI citation data for a client. If it has none, the claim is a hypothesis.

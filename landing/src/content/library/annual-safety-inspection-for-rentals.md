## Why do an annual safety check?

An annual safety check finds the hazards that tenants do not see or do not report. A dead smoke alarm makes no noise until a fire. A dirty dryer vent looks fine from the laundry room.

The numbers show why it matters. NFPA says roughly 3 out of 5 home fire deaths happen in homes with no smoke alarms or no working smoke alarms. NFPA also says working smoke alarms cut the risk of dying in a home fire in half.

Schedule the check once a year for each unit. Fall works well, before the heating season. Give the entry notice your state requires.

Codes differ by state and city. The list below is a baseline. Add any item your local code requires.

## Smoke and CO detectors

Smoke and carbon monoxide (CO) alarms are the first items to check. Test each one with its test button, then check its date.

- [ ] **Smoke alarm in each required location.** NFPA lists every bedroom, outside each sleeping area, and every level, including the basement.
- [ ] **Test each smoke alarm.** It must sound when you press the test button.
- [ ] **Check the manufacture date.** The U.S. Fire Administration says to replace the whole alarm 10 years from that date.
- [ ] **Replace batteries** in 9-volt alarms. The U.S. Fire Administration says at least once a year.
- [ ] **CO alarm on every level and outside sleeping areas.** The CPSC advises this placement and says interconnected alarms are best.
- [ ] **Test each CO alarm** and check its end-of-life date.

The CPSC says more than 200 people in the U.S. die each year from accidental non-fire CO poisoning linked to consumer products. Any home with a gas appliance, a fireplace or an attached garage needs CO alarms. Your state may require them by law.

Tell tenants to test alarms monthly, as the U.S. Fire Administration advises. Put the reminder in your move-in packet.

## Water heater and gas lines

The water heater and gas lines need a look each year and service from a licensed pro when something is wrong.

- [ ] **No water at the base** of the water heater. No rust on the tank.
- [ ] **Temperature-pressure relief valve** has a discharge pipe that points down to a safe spot.
- [ ] **Vent pipe** on a gas unit is attached and sloped up, with no gaps.
- [ ] **Clear space** around the heater. No boxes, paint or chemicals nearby.
- [ ] **Seismic straps** if your state or city requires them.
- [ ] **No gas smell** near appliances, meters or lines.
- [ ] **Furnace or boiler service** by a licensed technician, with the report kept on file.

If you smell gas, leave the unit and call the gas utility from outside. Do not try to find the leak yourself.

## Railings, stairs and windows

Railings, stairs and windows protect against falls. Push on each railing and walk each stair.

- [ ] **Railings firm.** No wobble when you push.
- [ ] **Guardrail on any drop of 30 inches or more.** The HUD NSPIRE standard (24 CFR 5.703) asks for this in HUD housing. Your code may set more detail.
- [ ] **Stair treads** firm, with no loose or broken steps.
- [ ] **Handrails** in place on stairs.
- [ ] **Stair lighting** works at the top and bottom.
- [ ] **Windows open and close.** Bedroom windows used for escape are not painted or nailed shut.
- [ ] **Window locks** work.
- [ ] **Screens** fit and are not torn.

## Electrical panel and GFCI outlets

The electrical panel and outlets cause hidden fire and shock risks. Look and test, and call a licensed electrician for any repair.

- [ ] **Panel cover** in place, with no missing knockouts or open slots.
- [ ] **Breakers labeled.**
- [ ] **No scorch marks,** buzzing or warm spots at the panel or outlets.
- [ ] **GFCI outlets trip and reset** with the test and reset buttons. The NSPIRE standard asks for GFCI protection on outlets within 6 feet of a water source in HUD housing.
- [ ] **Outlet covers** on every outlet and switch.
- [ ] **No extension cords** used as permanent wiring.
- [ ] **Exterior outlets** have covers.

## Dryer vents and fire risks

Dryer vents and lint cause many home fires. The U.S. Fire Administration studied clothes dryer fires in residential buildings from 2008 to 2010. It estimated 2,900 such fires each year, with 5 deaths, 100 injuries and $35 million in property loss. Failure to clean was the leading factor, at 34%. Dust, fiber and lint were the item first ignited in 28% of cases.

- [ ] **Clean the dryer vent** from the dryer to the outside cap.
- [ ] **Check the outside flap** opens when the dryer runs.
- [ ] **Replace plastic or foil duct** with the type the dryer maker requires.
- [ ] **Remind tenants** to clean the lint filter after each load.
- [ ] **Range hood filter** clean.
- [ ] **Fireplace and chimney** inspected and swept if used.
- [ ] **Fire extinguisher** in the kitchen, charged, if you provide one.
- [ ] **No unvented space heaters** that burn gas, oil or kerosene. NSPIRE bans them in HUD housing.

## How do I record the check?

Record each check with the date, the unit, each item, the result and the fix. A record protects tenants and protects you.

| Item | Result | Fix needed | Fixed on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Smoke alarm, bedroom 1 | Pass, made 2021 | — | — |
| CO alarm, hallway | Fail, end of life | Replace | (date) |
| Dryer vent | Lint buildup | Clean | (date) |

Keep the record for each unit with photos. Repeat next year and compare.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

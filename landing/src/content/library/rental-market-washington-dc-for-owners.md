## How much did HUD's fair market rent change in Washington, DC for FY2027?

HUD's fair market rent for a 2-bedroom in the Washington, DC metro rose 8.5% for FY2027, from $2,246 to $2,438 a month. That is $192 more a month, or $2,304 a year. Every bedroom size rose, from 8.1% (studio) to 9.8% (4 bedrooms).

The HUD area covers DC and nearby counties in Virginia and Maryland. The FY2027 numbers took effect on 2026-10-01. Housing authorities such as the DC Housing Authority use fair market rent to set voucher payment standards. For all bedroom sizes, see the [Washington, DC fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/washington-dc-fair-market-rent-2027).

## Are Washington, DC rents rising or falling?

Washington, DC rents are rising, but slower than the US. The Zillow Observed Rent Index (ZORI, a typical asking rent) for the metro was $2,407 in September 2026. That is up 1.4% from $2,375 a year before.

| Measure (Zillow ZORI, Sept 2026) | Washington, DC metro | United States |
|---|---|---|
| Typical asking rent | $2,407 | $1,932 |
| Change in 1 year | +1.4% | +2.7% |
| Change in 3 years | +7.0% | +7.5% |

HUD's 8.5% jump and Zillow's 1.4% rise measure different things at different times. HUD's FMR is a voucher benchmark. It is not a sign that open-market tenants will pay 8.5% more. Price your lease from 5 local comparable listings.

## How did federal job cuts change DC rental demand?

Federal job losses are the main drag on DC rental demand in 2026. The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) counts federal government jobs in the Washington-Arlington-Alexandria metro each month.

| Month | Federal jobs in the DC metro (BLS, not seasonally adjusted) |
|---|---|
| January 2025 | 375,800 |
| August 2025 | 353,500 |
| January 2026 | 314,300 |
| August 2026 | 311,900 |

From August 2025 to August 2026, the metro lost 41,600 federal jobs, or 11.8%. From January 2025 to January 2026, the loss was 61,500 jobs, or 16.4%. The count was about flat from January to August 2026, so the steepest drop seems to be behind us. That is our reading of the data, not a forecast.

Watch for: neighborhoods near federal offices and tenants who are federal workers or contractors. Ask about job stability in screening, within Fair Housing rules, and apply the same income test to every applicant.

## What vacancy rate does Washington, DC have?

Washington, DC vacancy rose in 2025 and is now expected to ease. Marcus & Millichap's 2Q 2026 report says the metro's apartment stock grew nearly 2% a year from 2019 to 2025. In 2025, new deliveries met almost no growth in renter demand. Vacancy jumped 130 basis points (1.3 points).

The same report expects both trends to shift in 2026. New units in NoMa and Navy Yard will drop sharply this year, and so will deliveries in Crystal City-Pentagon City and Silver Spring. The report puts vacancy in those submarkets in the 7% range as of March 2026.

What this means for you: in 2026 you still compete with lease-up buildings that offer concessions. As fewer new buildings open, that pressure should fade.

## Which local rules affect Washington, DC rental returns?

DC and Montgomery County both cap rent increases on covered units. We did not review Virginia rules for this article, so check them before you rely on market pricing there.

| Rule | 2026 cap | Who is covered | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| DC rent control | 4.1% (CPI-W + 2%); 2.1% for registered elderly or disabled tenants | Units not exempt under D.C. Code 42-3502.05 | DC Office of the Tenant Advocate |
| DC exemptions | No cap | Buildings with a permit issued after 1975; units of an owner of 4 or fewer units who is a natural person with no other DC rental interest and files a claim of exemption | D.C. Code 42-3502.05 |
| Montgomery County rent stabilization | 5.2% (CPI-U + 3%, max 6%), 2026-07-01 to 2027-06-30 | Licensed units at least 23 years old, unless exempt | Montgomery County DHCA |
| Montgomery County frequency | Once every 12 months, at renewal or a new lease | Regulated units | Montgomery County DHCA |

A worked example: a DC rent-controlled 2-bedroom at $2,438 can go up by no more than 4.1%, or $99.96 a month. In Montgomery County, a regulated unit at the same rent can go up by 5.2%, or $126.78. A post-1975 DC building can follow the market, which rose 1.4% last year.

Montgomery County rent stabilization does not apply in Gaithersburg, Rockville, Takoma Park, Barnesville or Laytonsville, per DHCA.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Should I rent or sell my Washington, DC home now?

Rent or sell in the DC metro depends on your building's rules and your tenant base. Rents still rise a little, and new supply is slowing. But federal job losses cut demand, and rent caps limit how fast you can catch up.

Checklist before you decide:

1. **Find your rent rule.** Is the unit in DC, Montgomery County or elsewhere? Is it exempt? In DC the cap is 4.1%, in Montgomery County 5.2%.
2. **Price the rent** from 5 local listings. Do not use the HUD FMR as your asking rent.
3. **Stress test vacancy.** Add one extra month of vacancy to your plan if your area has new buildings in lease-up.
4. **Count management.** At 8% of $2,438, a manager costs about $195 a month. Our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) runs other rates.
5. **Compare with a sale.** Get a broker's price opinion. Compare after-tax proceeds with 5 years of net rent.

If you own an exempt DC unit, you keep pricing power when the market recovers. If your unit is rent-controlled and the rent is well below market, a sale may give you a better return than a capped hold.

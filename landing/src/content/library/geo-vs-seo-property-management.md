## How is GEO different from SEO for a property manager?

GEO and SEO differ in the goal, not in most of the work. SEO (search engine optimization) aims for a high rank in Google results. GEO (generative engine optimization) aims for a citation or a mention inside an AI answer, such as ChatGPT, Gemini or Google AI Overviews.

| Question | SEO | GEO |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Rank in the list of links | Get named or cited inside the answer |
| Where the owner sees you | Google results, Maps | ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, AI Overviews, AI Mode |
| Unit of success | Position, clicks | Mention, citation, share of answers |
| Main sources | Your site, links, Google Business Profile | Your site plus the listing and review sites the AI reads |
| How you measure | Search Console, GA4 | Prompt tests, citation logs, form answers |
| How fast it shifts | Weeks to months | Can change from one answer to the next |

A landlord who asks ChatGPT "best property manager for a duplex in Denver" does not see 10 links. The landlord sees a short list. If your company is not on it, you get no click to win.

## What work overlaps between GEO and SEO?

Most GEO work for a property manager is also good SEO. Google says so for its own AI features: there are "no additional requirements" for AI Overviews or AI Mode, and no special schema. A page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet (Google Search Central, updated 2025-12-10).

This work helps both:

- **Crawl access.** Googlebot must reach your pages. For ChatGPT search, OpenAI uses a separate crawler, OAI-SearchBot. If your robots.txt blocks it, your pages do not show in ChatGPT search answers (OpenAI crawler docs).
- **Clear owner pages.** Fees, contract terms, what you manage, and each city you serve.
- **Google Business Profile.** Correct name, address, phone, services and service area.
- **Reviews.** Real reviews from owners, with replies.
- **Consistent facts.** The same name, address and phone on every site.

## What work is only GEO?

Three jobs belong to GEO and get little attention in classic SEO.

1. **Fix the third-party sources AI cites.** AI answers about local managers lean on list and review sites. On 2026-10-19 we asked ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini for the best property manager in 50 US cities. Expertise.com was a source in 93 of 150 answers, Yelp in 50 and iPropertyManagement.com in 50 (our data, 2026-10-19). In SEO, these are "citations" for local rank. In GEO, they are often the page the AI quotes.
2. **Write answers AI can quote.** The GEO research paper (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024) tested page changes on 10,000 queries. Adding quotes raised visibility by about 43%, adding statistics by about 33%, and citing sources by about 28%, by our math from its Table 1. Keyword stuffing scored below the baseline.
3. **Track prompts, not only keywords.** You ask the same owner questions each month and log who the AI names. Search Console does not show this for ChatGPT.

The paper has one more finding that matters for small companies. Lower-ranked pages gained most. For pages in rank 5, "cite sources" raised visibility by 115.1%. For rank 1 pages, it fell by 30.3% (Aggarwal et al., Table 2). The test used a research engine, not ChatGPT itself, so read it as a direction, not a promise.

## Does ranking on Google help AI citations?

Ranking on Google helps AI citations, but a top-10 rank is neither needed nor enough. Ahrefs checked about 4 million AI Overview URLs across 863,000 keyword results. Here is where the cited URLs ranked in normal blue links (Ahrefs, 2026-03-02):

| Organic rank of the cited URL | Share of AI Overview citations |
|---|---|
| Top 10 | 37.1% |
| 11 to 100 | 26.2% |
| Not in the top 100 | 36.7% |

So about 1 in 3 cited URLs ranks in the top 10. About 1 in 3 does not rank in the top 100 at all. Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode may use "query fan-out": many related searches across subtopics. A page can rank low for the main query and still answer a sub-question well.

For ChatGPT, the gap is wider. ChatGPT search uses its own crawler and leans on listing sites. In our 50-city run, ChatGPT cited a listing site in 96% of answers and a company site in 30% (our data, 2026-10-19).

## How should you split the budget between GEO and SEO?

Split the budget by where owner leads come from today, and move it as your own data changes. The best public numbers come from Goodjuju Marketing. Its fall 2026 study of 35 property managers found:

- Google Search and Map Pack: 50.6% of owner leads.
- Direct and Maps: 35.6%.
- AI tools: 3.4%, up from 2.1% in spring 2026.
- AI visitors converted at 3.9%, against 1.2% for Google.

That data supports a simple plan for a 50 to 500 door company.

| Bucket | What it pays for | Suggested share |
|---|---|---|
| Shared base (SEO and GEO) | Fee table, city pages, Google Business Profile, reviews, crawl fixes | Most of the budget |
| GEO only | Directory and listing fixes, quotable facts, monthly prompt tests | A small fixed share |
| Measurement | Form field "How did you hear about us?", GA4 AI channel | An hour or two a month |

The shares are our hypothesis, not a study result. Grow the GEO share only when your own forms show more owners who found you through AI. Our [AI traffic tracking guide](/guides/track-ai-traffic-property-management) shows how to count them.

## Do you need both GEO and SEO?

Yes, a property manager needs both, because they feed each other. Google is still the volume channel. AI answers pull from pages that search engines index and from the listing sites that local SEO already cares about. GEO adds the parts that SEO skips: the sources AI quotes and the habit of checking what AI says about you.

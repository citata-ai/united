An owner types an address into a "free rental analysis" form at 10 p.m. The next morning, three managers send a reply. One sends a single number. One sends a sales brochure. One sends a 2-page report with comps, a range, a net figure and a phone number. The owner calls the third.

The rental analysis is often the first piece of work an owner sees from you. Goodjuju's fall 2026 lead source report found an average website conversion rate of 0.9% across 35 property management sites. So each owner who fills the form is rare. The report should earn the call.

## Which comps should I show?

Show 3 to 6 comps that match the owner's home on bedrooms, property type and area. Comps (comparable rentals) are the proof behind your number. An owner trusts a number they can check.

Rules for good comps:

- **Same bedroom count.** A 3-bedroom comp for a 2-bedroom home misleads.
- **Same type.** Single-family with single-family, condo with condo.
- **Close in distance.** Note the distance for each one.
- **Recent.** Use listings or leases from the last 90 days where you can. State the date.
- **Mixed status.** Leased comps show what renters paid. Active listings show what owners ask. Label each.
- **A source for each.** Your own leases, your MLS, or a public listing site. Name it.

Leave out comps you cannot verify. Two strong comps beat six guesses.

## How do I show a rent range, not one number?

Show the rent as three points: low, likely and high. One number invites a fight. A range shows you know what moves the price.

Then explain the gap in plain terms. Each line names one feature and its effect, based on your comps. For example: "The 2 comps with in-unit laundry leased $75 to $100 higher."

Add one public benchmark next to your range. The HUD Fair Market Rent (FMR) works well. Under 24 CFR 888.113, HUD sets most FMRs at the 40th percentile rent: the amount below which 40% of standard-quality units rented by recent movers fall. So the FMR is a floor-like reference, not a price for one home. For example, the FY2027 FMR for a 2-bedroom in the Chicago-Joliet-Naperville HUD Metro FMR Area is $2,011 (HUD FY2027 FMR file). See all metros in our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits).

## Should I add a vacancy and cost estimate?

Yes. Add a short vacancy and cost estimate that ends in a net monthly number. Owners decide on net income, not gross rent.

For the vacancy line, you can cite the national rate as context. The Census Bureau reports a national rental vacancy rate of 7.3% for the second quarter of 2026. Your local days-to-lease data is better, if you track it.

Here is a worked example. All numbers except the FMR are made up for the example. Use your own.

| Line | Monthly | How we got it |
|---|---|---|
| Likely rent | $2,150 | Middle of the comp range |
| Vacancy allowance (4%) | -$86 | About 2 weeks empty per year |
| Management fee (8% of collected rent) | -$165 | 8% x ($2,150 - $86) |
| Repair reserve (5% of rent) | -$108 | Owner's choice |
| Net before mortgage, tax, insurance | $1,791 | $2,150 - $86 - $165 - $108 |

Show the fee line even though it is your fee. Owners will ask. Let the owner test other fee levels with our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## How do I end the report with a next step?

End with one next step, one link and one person's name. Three choices make the owner stop and think. One choice makes the next move clear.

Good next steps:

1. **Book a 15-minute call** to go over the numbers.
2. **Reply with photos** so you can tighten the range.
3. **Ask for a walk-through** if the owner is local.

Pick one. Name the person who will answer. Say what the call costs (nothing) and how long it takes.

## What should I leave out?

Leave out anything you cannot back up or that creates legal risk.

- **Promises.** Do not guarantee a rent or a lease-up date.
- **Tenant profiles.** Do not describe the "kind of renter" the area attracts. That can steer by a protected class under the Fair Housing Act.
- **Old comps** without a date.
- **Sales copy.** A list of awards on page 1 pushes the numbers down.
- **Unchecked law.** If you mention a local rule, cite it, or say "check the current law".

## Checklist before you send

- [ ] 3 to 6 comps, each with date, status and source
- [ ] Low, likely and high rent
- [ ] One public benchmark, labeled as reference
- [ ] Net monthly estimate with every line marked as an estimate
- [ ] Your fee shown in plain numbers
- [ ] No text about who should live there
- [ ] One next step, one name, one phone number
- [ ] Sent within one business day of the request

## South Dakota eviction rules at a glance

South Dakota calls an eviction an action of "forcible entry and detainer." The rules sit in SDCL chapter 21-16. We read each section on the South Dakota Legislature site, and the court forms on the Unified Judicial System (UJS) site, on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute or source |
|---|---|---|
| Earliest filing for unpaid rent | Rent unpaid 3 days after it is due | SDCL 21-16-1(4) |
| Notice to quit before filing | Not required since 2024 | 2024 SL ch 75, repealing SDCL 21-16-2 |
| Court | Circuit court or magistrate court in the county | SDCL 21-16-3 |
| Service of the summons | At least 2 attempts, 1 week apart, within 30 days | SDCL 21-16-6 |
| Tenant's time to answer | 5 days after service (or 30 days after publication, if sooner) | SDCL 21-16-7 |
| Delay of the case | Max 14 days, unless the tenant posts a bond for rent | SDCL 21-16-7 |
| Trial | On 2 days' notice after the answer | SDCL 21-16-8 |
| Filing fee | $72 total | UJS court costs schedule, 2026 |
| Removal | By the sheriff, in the daytime only | SDCL 21-16-12, Form UJS-117 |
| Lockout or utility shutoff | Damages of 2 months' rent, plus deposit | SDCL 43-32-6 |

## What notice must a South Dakota landlord give before filing for unpaid rent?

A South Dakota landlord does not have to serve a notice to quit before filing for unpaid rent. Senate Bill 90 (2024 Session Laws, chapter 75), signed March 14, 2024, repealed SDCL 21-16-2. That section had required "three days' written notice to quit" before an unpaid rent or holdover case.

The 3-day wait still exists, but inside the grounds. SDCL 21-16-1(4) allows a case when a tenant "fails to pay his rent for three days after the same shall be due."

Watch for: older guides and forms. Many still describe the repealed notice. Your lease may also promise a notice. If it does, give it.

## Can a South Dakota landlord evict for a lease violation or with no cause?

A South Dakota landlord can evict for a lease violation. SDCL 21-16-1(7) allows a case when a tenant commits waste, or does or fails to do an act that ends the lease under its terms. SDCL 43-32-18 lets a landlord end a lease early when the tenant uses the unit against the lease, or fails to make required repairs within a reasonable time after a request.

A South Dakota landlord can also end a tenancy without cause, at the right time:

- **Fixed-term lease:** the lease ends at the end of the agreed term (SDCL 43-32-22). A tenant who holds over can be evicted under SDCL 21-16-1(4).
- **Month-to-month lease:** give notice at least as long as the rent period, max 1 month (SDCL 43-32-15).

Two limits apply. A notice to vacate that follows a tenant's good-faith code complaint, written repair request or union activity can be retaliation, unless you gave it more than 180 days after that event (SDCL 43-32-27). And no lease may allow eviction because a tenant called police about domestic abuse, unlawful sexual behavior or stalking (SDCL 43-32-18.1).

## Where does a landlord file, and what does it cost?

A South Dakota landlord files in the circuit court or magistrate court of the county where the property is (SDCL 21-16-3). The complaint must be in writing and verified (SDCL 21-16-6).

The court's Form UJS-109 lists the landlord's steps:

1. **Complete** the Case Filing Statement (UJS-232).
2. **Complete** the Summons (UJS-111) and the Verified Complaint (UJS-112).
3. **File** all three with the Clerk of Court and pay the $72 filing fee.
4. **Serve** the tenant by sheriff or process server, for a fee.
5. **File** proof of service with the Clerk of Court.

The UJS court costs schedule, effective July 1, 2026, splits the $72 into a $25 eviction filing fee, a $40 court automation surcharge and a $7 law library fee. A tenant who answers pays a $25 answer fee. The sheriff's service fee is extra and differs by county.

Watch for: an LLC or a corporation needs a lawyer. Form UJS-109 says a plaintiff filing for a corporation or LLC "will need to obtain an attorney." The court may award reasonable attorney fees to the winning side, if a licensed attorney represents that side (SDCL 21-16-11).

## How long does an eviction take in South Dakota?

A South Dakota eviction can end in a few weeks when the tenant does not answer. The statute sets the short periods. Court calendars and service set the rest.

### Worked timeline: unpaid rent, no answer

This is an estimate from the statutory periods, not court data.

| Day | Step | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Rent is due and unpaid | Lease |
| Day 5 | Rent unpaid 3 full days; you file and pay $72 | SDCL 21-16-1(4), UJS-109 |
| Day 6 | First service attempt succeeds | SDCL 21-16-6 |
| Day 6 + 5 business days | Tenant's answer deadline passes | SDCL 21-16-7, UJS-109 |
| Next court day | You file the motion for default judgment | UJS-109 |
| Judge's date | Sheriff removes the tenant on or after 8:00 AM | Form UJS-117 |

Three things add time:

- **Service fails.** The server must make at least 2 attempts, at least 1 week apart, both within 30 days. On the second attempt, the server may post the summons on the property and mail a copy (SDCL 21-16-6).
- **The tenant answers.** Then the case goes to trial on 2 days' notice (SDCL 21-16-8).
- **A delay is granted.** A delay may not exceed 14 days, unless the tenant gives a bond for the rent that comes due (SDCL 21-16-7).

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in South Dakota?

The county sheriff removes the tenant. The court's default judgment form (UJS-117) directs "the local Sheriff's Department" to remove the tenant and all others in possession on or after 8:00 AM on a date the judge sets. SDCL 21-16-12 says execution for possession is served only in the daytime.

The judgment can also award past-due rent, late fees and costs (SDCL 21-16-10, UJS-117). Property worth more than $500 left in the unit must be stored for 30 days before you treat it as abandoned (Form UJS-117, SDCL 43-32-26).

## Is a self-help eviction legal in South Dakota?

A self-help eviction is not legal in South Dakota. SDCL 43-32-6 covers a landlord who "unlawfully removes or excludes the tenant" or cuts electric, gas, water or other essential service. The tenant may sue to get back in, or end the lease. In each case, the tenant recovers damages "equal to two months rent," plus any advance rent and deposit.

| Self-help act | Tenant's recovery under SDCL 43-32-6 |
|---|---|
| Change the locks | 2 months' rent + advance rent + deposit |
| Remove the tenant's things to force a move | 2 months' rent + advance rent + deposit |
| Shut off water, gas or power | 2 months' rent + advance rent + deposit |

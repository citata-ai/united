## What are DoorLoop's plan names and prices?

DoorLoop's reviewed pricing page lists Starter, Pro and Premium with separate billing terms. The displayed figures are starting prices for the smallest configuration. They do not quote your full portfolio.

| Plan | Monthly billing shown | Monthly equivalent with yearly billing | Published feature difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | $99 | $69 | Basic accounting, rental tasks and tenant portal |
| Pro | $189 | $149 | Budgeting, listings, roles and broader support |
| Premium | $239 | $209 | API access, audit logs and added reporting |

Source: [DoorLoop pricing](https://www.doorloop.com/pricing), directly reviewed October 19, 2026. Starter shows a 10-unit cap. The page contains inconsistent discount and fee examples, so confirm the actual order form.

Treat the table as a dated record, not a binding offer. The page also carries a promotion with an earlier end date. Do not assume an expired banner still applies.

## How does price change with unit count?

DoorLoop uses a unit selector and asks larger portfolios to request a demo. The smallest displayed price should not be multiplied or extended without a confirmed pricing rule. A plan floor does not establish the incremental price of another unit.

Ask sales to price your current count and a plausible future count. Specify occupied units, vacancies and any units you are preparing to onboard. Confirm which categories remain billable.

Request the rule for removing units too. A growing manager can also lose an owner. Ask when the subscription falls after a removal and whether a minimum still applies.

Keep billing term and unit count fixed when comparing offers. A yearly commitment with a lower monthly equivalent is different from a monthly payment plan. Record the cash due at signup alongside the recurring rate.

## Which features need a higher tier?

DoorLoop's pricing page places budgeting, listings and user access roles in Pro, with API access, audit logs and added features in Premium. Some feature descriptions and comparison rows differ, so ask the vendor to confirm the exact scope.

Choose the tier by required work. If another system needs API access, test that connection before assuming a lower plan fits. If staff need restricted roles, confirm how those roles work in the selected plan.

Review AI features separately. The page's presentation does not make every AI function's inclusion equally clear. Ask for the module name, allowance and extra fee in the quote.

Have staff operate the proposed tier during the demo. Features shown in a sales account may belong to a higher configuration. Mark any required upgrade and revise the cost comparison before signing.

## Are there onboarding fees?

DoorLoop states that onboarding fees depend on portfolio size, while its promotional copy offers selected waivers. The public page does not establish a universal setup fee for every buyer.

Ask for a written implementation scope. List imported contacts, properties, balances, leases and attachments. Confirm which historical records move and which stay in your own archive.

Assign responsibility for data cleanup. A migration fee does not prove the vendor will resolve unexplained balances or duplicate owner records. Ask what happens if staff find an error after the first import.

Record training and payment-account setup separately. These can affect launch even when an onboarding fee is waived. Accept a waiver only when it appears in the final offer with clear conditions.

Do not cancel the old software before a trial import and accounting review pass. The cost of a failed move can exceed the setup charge you negotiated away.

## What does DoorLoop cost at fifty and a hundred fifty units?

DoorLoop's starting cards do not verify either portfolio total. Use the unit selector or sales quote for the actual selected tier and billing term. This guide does not invent prices by extending the smallest bracket.

Send the same request for both scenarios:

- **Portfolio:** state current units, vacancies and expected additions.
- **Plan:** name the features your staff tested and require.
- **Billing:** specify monthly or yearly commitment.
- **Setup:** list migration, training and account-approval work.
- **Payments:** include bank transfers, cards and owner payouts.
- **Documents:** estimate screening and signature use.
- **Renewal:** ask for the later bill after discounts end.

Have the vendor return a full monthly total and initial cash requirement. Compare the scenarios using identical assumptions.

If the quote omits a fee category, ask whether the amount is included or unknown. A blank line should not silently become zero in your budget.

## How should you handle conflicting price details?

Use the final written fee schedule to resolve conflicts before purchase. DoorLoop's page shows inconsistent annual examples and payment-fee details in different sections. We therefore avoid treating every number on that page as a current universal charge.

Save the offer, selected unit count and billing date. Ask the salesperson to mark which terms govern the account. Confirm whether a later plan name or fee schedule supersedes an earlier description.

Test the renter-facing payment screen before launch. Check who pays fees and what appears on the receipt. A software setting does not establish whether your agreement allows a charge to be passed through.

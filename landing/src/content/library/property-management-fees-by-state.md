## What is the average property management fee by state?

The average property management fee by state goes from 7.10% to 9.79% of collected rent in 2026. These numbers come from iPropertyManagement's survey of advertised fees, updated 2026-09-23. The national average in the same survey is 8.49%. The 16 state samples average 8.71%.

The dollar column is our math: $2,000 times the state average. The gap from the lowest state to the highest is 2.69 points. On $2,000 rent, that is $53.80 a month, or $645.60 a year.

All Property Management gives a wider rule of thumb: 8% to 12% of the monthly rent. That page does not give a survey date or sample size.

## Which states have flat-fee property management, and at what price?

Flat-fee property management exists in most sampled states, but few companies offer it. iPropertyManagement found a national flat-fee average of $101.04 per unit per month, with a range of $49 to $250.

Some states had only 1 or 2 flat-fee companies, so the source gives single prices, not an average. Michigan: $99 and $100. Oregon: $50 and $99. Tennessee: $83. New Mexico: $49.

A flat fee is cheaper than a percentage fee only above a break-even rent. At $101.04 a month and 8.49%, the break-even rent is about $1,190. Above that rent, the flat fee costs less. Below it, the percentage costs less.

## How should you read state fee averages?

State fee averages are a starting point, not a price. Read them with 4 limits in mind:

- **Advertised fees only.** iPropertyManagement counts fees that companies publish. Managers that quote only by phone are not in the data.
- **Small samples.** Wisconsin has 10 locations. Oregon has 12. One company can move the average.
- **Metros, not whole states.** Each state sample covers 2 to 14 metro areas. Rural fees can differ.
- **16 of 50 states.** The source has no average for the other states. We do not guess them.

Before you sign, get the written fee schedule from 3 managers in your area. Then compare the first-year cost, not the monthly percentage. Our guide [How Much Does a Property Manager Cost](/guides/how-much-does-a-property-manager-cost-owner) explains each fee line.

## Should property managers publish their fees by state?

Property managers that publish fees give owners and AI assistants a number to quote. iPropertyManagement's state data exists only because companies put their fees on their sites. A manager with no fee page is not in the average and cannot be compared. See our guide on [publishing fees for AI search](/guides/publish-fees-for-ai-search).

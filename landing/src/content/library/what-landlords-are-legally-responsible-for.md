## What is the implied warranty of habitability?

The warranty of habitability concerns the duty to provide livable housing under applicable law. States define duties and remedies differently. Local codes and the lease can also affect what must happen.

Minnesota Statutes Section 504B.161 gives a concrete state example. It requires covered premises to be fit for the intended use and kept in reasonable repair. The section also addresses health and safety compliance and specified exceptions.

Do not treat that wording as the exact rule everywhere. Identify your state's law and local housing code before setting a repair policy. Ask a local attorney how the rules apply to your property and lease.

| Duty area | What an owner should verify |
|---|---|
| Condition | State fitness duties and local housing standards |
| Repairs | Notice, urgency, process, and available remedies |
| Entry | Permitted purpose, notice, timing, and exceptions |
| Screening | Lawful criteria and consumer-report notices |
| Advertising | Fair housing limits and local protections |
| Disclosures | Required federal and local information |

Use the table to assign research and operating tasks. It is not a national list of every legal duty. Keep the actual local sources with your property records.

## Which repairs must a landlord make?

Required repairs depend on the condition, legal duties, and applicable process. Distinguish an immediate safety problem from routine upkeep, but do not invent a universal response deadline. Get local advice for the actual hazard.

Minnesota's Section 504B.161 illustrates duties involving reasonable repair and health and safety. It also discusses disrepair caused by certain tenant conduct. Read the whole provision before assuming a tenant-caused issue removes every owner responsibility.

Build a clear reporting process. Tell tenants how to report urgent problems and ordinary requests. Keep the date, condition, access attempts, contractor response, and completion evidence in the work record.

A lease clause is not a reason to ignore a housing code. Ask counsel which duties can lawfully be assigned and which remain with the owner. Use contractors qualified for the work and document the result.

If a tenant and owner disagree about fault, address the safety issue through the lawful process. Record the facts separately from any charge decision. Ask for advice before withholding services or using rent pressure to resolve a repair dispute.

## Which fair housing rules apply?

The federal Fair Housing Act prohibits covered housing discrimination. DOJ lists race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, and disability. Review advertising, screening, leasing, and resident treatment under the applicable protections.

Do not assume that a small property has no duties. The federal statute has limited exemptions with conditions. Under 42 USC Section 3603(b), the listed exemptions do not remove the advertising prohibition in Section 3604(c).

State and local protections may differ or go further. Ask counsel which rules apply rather than using a federal exemption as a complete operating policy. Keep written criteria focused on lawful, relevant factors.

Describe the home and its features in advertisements. Avoid language that selects residents by protected class. Apply the same process to comparable applicants and preserve the reason for a decision.

Ask for advice when an accommodation or modification request arises. A standard pet rule or application setting does not answer every disability-related request. Record the request and follow a lawful review process.

## What does a landlord owe on entry and privacy?

A landlord needs a lawful purpose and must meet applicable entry conditions. Ownership alone does not authorize unrestricted visits. State examples show why copying a notice rule can fail.

Minnesota Statutes Section 504B.211 generally requires a reasonable business purpose and a good-faith effort to give at least 24 hours' advance notice. The statute defines timing, tenant agreement, and exceptions. Read those conditions before scheduling entry.

Washington RCW 59.18.150 generally requires at least 2 days' written notice for ordinary covered entry, subject to exceptions. It also has a separate rule for showing the dwelling to prospective tenants or buyers. Use the correct provision for the purpose.

These examples are not interchangeable. Keep local notice language, delivery method, and timing in your process. Give contractors only the access authority that the owner can lawfully provide.

For an urgent entry, record the facts supporting the exception. Emergency language should not become a routine shortcut. Notify and document as applicable law requires.

## What federal screening and lead duties matter?

Consumer-report use can trigger federal notice duties. FTC guidance says an adverse action based partly or wholly on a consumer report requires notice. A denial is not the only adverse action; certain changed terms can also qualify.

The FTC notice includes reporting agency information, a statement about the agency's role, and consumer rights. Credit-score use can add written or electronic disclosure duties. Review the full guidance rather than relying on a platform's rejection button.

EPA describes lead disclosure duties for most housing built before 1978. Before a covered lease is signed, landlords must provide specified information, available records, a warning statement, and the required pamphlet. Check exemptions and the actual lease process.

Keep those disclosures with the signed rental file. A manager's involvement does not justify leaving responsibility unclear. Ask who prepares, delivers, and verifies each required document.

## How should you turn these duties into a work plan?

Assign a person and a record to each duty. A good work plan makes the local rule easy to follow during a busy week. Review it when the law, lease, or property use changes.

- **Identify the sources** for repairs, entry, deposits, licensing, and notices.
- **Prepare reviewed forms** for the actual jurisdiction.
- **Assign repair cover** with a local lead and backup.
- **Check application decisions** and required notices.
- **Save evidence** of disclosures, entry notices, and completed work.

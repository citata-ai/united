## Who signs the HAP contract, owner or manager?

The HAP contract is between the owner and the public housing agency (PHA). The owner carries the duties. 24 CFR 982.452(a) says "the owner is responsible for performing all of the owner's obligations under the HAP contract and the lease." For owner approval, the rules count a "principal or other interested party" as the owner too (24 CFR 982.306(f)).

A manager can act for the owner with the right papers. PHAs differ on the form:

| Housing authority | What it needs for a manager | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| Tacoma (WA) | Copy of the management agreement | "If you have a Management Company, we can pay the management Co., once we have a copy of the management agreement." |
| Maricopa County (AZ) | Property management agreement with the RFTA | Lists it as a required document if "using a Property Mgmt company" |
| Santa Cruz County (CA) | Landlord Signature Authorization form | "You may designate a third party to act on your behalf." |

Tax reporting follows the payee. Tacoma reports payments on a 1099 under the TIN on file and checks it with the IRS. Make sure the W-9 matches the account that receives the HAP.

## What does a Section 8 manager do that a regular manager does not?

A Section 8 manager adds program tasks to normal work. 24 CFR 982.452(b) lists the owner's base duties, which a manager usually carries out. They are: select the tenant, maintain the unit and follow equal opportunity rules. The owner also gives the PHA required information, collects the tenant share and deposit, enforces the lease and pays owner utilities.

The voucher program adds these, each with a rule behind it:

| Task | Deadline or rule |
|---|---|
| Fill out and return the RFTA packet | Incomplete packets delay approval (Maricopa) |
| Prepare the unit for the first inspection | Utilities on and a working refrigerator (Maricopa) |
| Fix inspection items | 24 hours (life-threatening), 30 days (other) (24 CFR 982.404) |
| Attach the HUD tenancy addendum to the lease | Word for word (24 CFR 982.308(f)) |
| Send lease changes to the PHA | In writing, at once (982.308(g)(1)) |
| Request rent increases | At least 60 days' notice to the PHA (982.308(g)(4)) |
| Copy eviction notices to the PHA | Every owner eviction notice (982.310(e)(2)) |
| Report vacancies | Notify the PHA in writing at once (Maricopa landlord certification) |
| Reconcile HAP | Check each payment against the HAP contract |
| Return overpayments | No HAP for any month after move-out (982.311(d)) |

## How do managers handle inspections?

Good managers treat inspections as a deadline system. The cost of a miss is clear. If repairs miss the cure period, the PHA must abate the HAP. If the unit still fails 60 days after the abatement notice, the PHA must end the HAP contract (24 CFR 982.404(d)).

A sound inspection routine:

1. **Pre-inspect.** Walk the unit with the PHA's checklist the week before. Maricopa publishes a list of common fail items.
2. **Attend.** Be there or send a technician, so you see what the inspector sees.
3. **Triage the report the same day.** Split 24-hour items from 30-day items.
4. **Fix and document.** Take before-and-after photos. The PHA may accept photos as proof (24 CFR 982.405(i)).
5. **Flag tenant damage.** Ask the PHA to waive owner duty for damage beyond normal use (982.404(a)(4)).
6. **Track the reinspection.** Confirm the pass in writing.

NSPIRE is coming. PHAs must switch voucher inspections from HQS to NSPIRE by February 1, 2027. A manager should know which standard each PHA in its area uses.

## Do managers charge more for Section 8 units?

We do not know, and we found no public survey to cite. Fee practice varies by company. Ask each manager to put any voucher-specific charge in writing. Examples: an RFTA processing fee, an inspection attendance fee or a charge for rent increase requests.

## What reports should the owner get?

The owner should get a monthly report that covers program risk, not only cash. Use this as a minimum:

- [ ] HAP received, by unit, against the HAP contract amount.
- [ ] Tenant share received and any arrears.
- [ ] Any withheld or abated HAP, with the reason and the fix date.
- [ ] Inspection dates: last, next and any open deficiencies.
- [ ] Lease end dates and rent-change notice deadlines (60 days before).
- [ ] Notices sent to tenants, with proof of copies sent to the PHA.
- [ ] PHA letters received: HAP changes, payment standard notices, ownership or contact changes.

## How do I choose a Section 8-ready manager?

Ask questions that test the deadlines, not the sales pitch:

1. **Which PHAs do you work with now?** Ask for the number of voucher units per PHA.
2. **What is your abatement history?** Ask how many units had HAP abated in the last 12 months, and why.
3. **Do you know NSPIRE?** Ask which local PHAs have switched.
4. **How do you handle rent increases?** Listen for 60-day notices and comparable rents.
5. **What paperwork do you file for me?** Expect the management agreement, W-9 and direct deposit forms.
6. **Can I see a sample owner report?** Check it against the list above.

## How much did HUD's fair market rent change in Kansas City for FY2027?

HUD's fair market rent for a Kansas City-area 2-bedroom rose 13.8% for FY2027, from $1,358 to $1,546. That is the biggest 2-bedroom rise among the 50 largest HUD metro areas, ahead of Chicago (+12.9%) and Columbus, Ohio (+12.0%) (our ranking of HUD's FY2027 data). The new FMRs took effect on 2026-10-01 (Federal Register, 91 FR 56156).

| Unit size | FY2026 FMR | FY2027 FMR | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Studio | $1,095 | $1,257 | +14.8% |
| 1 bedroom | $1,197 | $1,366 | +14.1% |
| 2 bedrooms | $1,358 | $1,546 | +13.8% |
| 3 bedrooms | $1,769 | $2,020 | +14.2% |
| 4 bedrooms | $2,103 | $2,419 | +15.0% |

Source: HUD FY2027 and revised FY2026 FMR files for the Kansas City, MO-KS HUD Metro FMR Area. It covers both states, including Jackson, Clay, Cass and Platte counties in Missouri and Johnson and Wyandotte counties in Kansas.

What it means for a voucher owner: the 2-bedroom basic range (90% to 110% of FMR, 24 CFR 982.503) moved from $1,222–$1,494 to $1,391–$1,701. Each housing authority picks its own payment standard inside that range. The full table is on our [Kansas City fair market rent 2027](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/kansas-city-fair-market-rent-2027) page.

Note the gap: the FY2027 2-bedroom FMR ($1,546) is now $145 above Apartment List's Kansas City, Missouri 2-bedroom median ($1,401). The two measure different things. The FMR is a 40th percentile gross rent, which includes utilities. The Apartment List median is a rent without utilities. Still, the gap suggests voucher rents can compete with market rents in much of the metro.

## Are Kansas City rents rising or falling?

Kansas City rents are rising slowly. Apartment List's October 2026 report for Kansas City, Missouri shows:

| Measure | Kansas City, MO | KC metro | Missouri | US |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Median rent, all sizes | $1,368 | $1,376 | — | $1,388 |
| 1-bedroom median | $1,187 | — | — | $1,220 |
| 2-bedroom median | $1,401 | — | — | $1,374 |
| Change, past 12 months | +2.6% | +2.6% | +1.9% | -0.4% |
| Change, September 2026 | -0.1% | — | — | -0.1% |

Source: Apartment List, Kansas City rent report and national rent report, October 2026.

Three points stand out:

- **Steady, not hot.** Kansas City rents rose 2.1% from January to September 2026, about the same as the 2.0% rise in the same months of 2025.
- **Near the national median.** The city median is 1.4% below the US median, while US rents fell and Kansas City's rose.
- **Part of a Midwest trend.** Apartment List names Kansas City among Midwest markets in the national top 10 for rent growth, along with Detroit, Chicago, Milwaukee and Cleveland.

## Which local rules affect Kansas City rental returns?

Kansas City rules differ by side of the state line. The metro spans Missouri and Kansas, and each state has its own landlord law.

| Rule | Kansas City, Missouri | Kansas side |
|---|---|---|
| Rent control | Banned: no county or city may regulate rent (RSMo 441.043) | Banned: no political subdivision may control rent (K.S.A. 12-16,120) |
| Deposit cap | 2 months' rent (RSMo 535.300) | 1 month unfurnished; 1.5 months furnished; plus up to 0.5 month for pets (K.S.A. 58-2550) |
| Deposit return | Within 30 days after the tenancy ends, with an itemized list of deductions | Balance within 14 days after costs are known, and no later than 30 days after the tenancy ends, possession and demand |
| Penalty for wrongful withholding | 2 times the amount withheld | The amount due plus 1.5 times the amount withheld |
| Where to hold it | A federally insured bank or credit union; interest belongs to the landlord | Not stated in 58-2550 |
| City rental permit | Healthy Homes permit, $25 per unit for 2026 | Check your city |

The Kansas City, Missouri permit: under Ordinance 180248, all rental property owners in the city must register under the Healthy Homes Rental Inspection Program. Permits run January 1 to December 31. For 2026, the fee is $25 per unit per year, plus a one-time $25 application fee (City of Kansas City, MO).

Worked example, a 4-unit building in Kansas City, Missouri:

| Year | Application | Permits | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| First year | $25 | 4 × $25 = $100 | $125 |
| Each later year | — | $100 | $100 |

Worked example, wrongful deposit withholding of $600: in Missouri, the tenant recovers 2 × $600 = $1,200 in damages. In Kansas, the tenant recovers the $600 due plus 1.5 × $600 = $900, for $1,500 total.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What vacancy rate does Kansas City have?

We could not verify a Kansas City metro vacancy rate. The Census Bureau publishes metro rental vacancy rates in its Housing Vacancy Survey, but census.gov refused our requests on 2026-10-19. We do not quote a number we did not read.

From Apartment List's national report (2026-09-29): the national multifamily vacancy rate fell to 7% in September 2026, and units took 34 days on average to lease. With Kansas City rents rising faster than the nation's, a well-priced unit should not need longer than that.

Track your own days-on-market for each turnover. If a unit sits more than 30 days, check its price against the Apartment List median for its size.

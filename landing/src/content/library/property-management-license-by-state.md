## Which states require a broker license for property management?

Most states require a broker license for property management, because their law defines leasing and rent collection for others as broker work. We read the statute text for 7 states on 2026-10-19. We did not verify all 50, so this page is a sample, not a full list.

Why only 7: vendor blogs publish 50-state license lists, and the lists do not fully agree. We use only text from the statute or the state code.

| State | License for leasing or rent work for others | Separate PM license | Exemption we read | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arizona | Broker (or licensee under a broker) | Not in the sections we read | Not checked | A.R.S. 32-2101(51), 32-2173 |
| California | Broker | No separate license in 10131 | Not checked | Bus. & Prof. Code 10131(b) |
| Delaware | License, unless duties are limited | Not checked | Limited-duty staff | 24 Del. Admin. Code 2900-9.0 |
| Florida | Broker | Not in the sections we read | Onsite apartment leasing staff; salaried condo or co-op managers | Fla. Stat. 475.01, 475.011(4)-(5) |
| Oregon | Broker or licensed property manager | Yes | Not checked | ORS 696.010 |
| South Carolina | Licensee | Yes, with its own exam | Owners, attorneys, receivers and others | S.C. Code 40-57-30, 40-57-240 |
| Texas | Broker | Not in the section we read | Not checked | Tex. Occ. Code 1101.002 |

## What do the statutes say, state by state?

Each state uses its own words. Here is the key line for each one.

- **Arizona.** A.R.S. 32-2101(51) defines a "real estate broker" as a person who, for another and for compensation, "sells, exchanges, purchases, rents or leases real estate". A.R.S. 32-2173 adds that a property management agreement must be signed by the firm's designated broker or the broker's authorized licensee.
- **California.** Business and Professions Code 10131(b) lists, as broker acts, a person who "leases or rents or offers to lease or rent, or places for rent, or solicits listings of places for rent". It also covers soliciting tenants and collecting rents.
- **Florida.** Florida Statutes 475.01 defines a "broker" to include a person who, for compensation, "rents, or offers, attempts or agrees to" negotiate the rental of real property for another.
- **Oregon.** ORS 696.010 defines "management of rental real estate" to include advertising, finding tenants, checking credit, collecting rent and contracting for repairs. A "real estate property manager" is a licensed real estate property manager, a principal broker, or a broker supervised by a principal broker.
- **South Carolina.** S.C. Code 40-57-30 defines a "property manager" as a supervised licensee who passes the exam for a property manager license. A "property manager-in-charge" has control over trust accounts.
- **Texas.** Texas Occupations Code 1101.002 defines a broker to include a person who, for pay, "leases real estate" for another or "controls the acceptance or deposit of rent from a resident of a single-family residential real property unit".

## Which states have a separate property manager license?

Oregon and South Carolina have a separate property manager license, among the 7 states we checked. A separate license lets a person manage rentals without a full sales broker license.

| Feature | Oregon | South Carolina |
|---|---|---|
| License name | Licensed real estate property manager | Property manager license |
| Who else may manage | Principal broker, or broker under a principal broker | Other licensees under the chapter |
| Supervision | Brokers supervised by a principal broker | Property manager-in-charge controls trust accounts |
| Statute | ORS 696.010 | S.C. Code 40-57-30 |

If your state has a PM-only license, check what it does not cover. Read the license scope before you list a client's rental for sale.

## Which states have no license rule for residential property management?

We did not confirm any state with no license rule for residential property management. Some online lists name one or more such states, but the lists conflict with each other. So we leave that column empty instead of guessing.

What we did find are narrow exemptions:

- **Delaware.** 24 Del. Admin. Code 2900-9.0 exempts a person who does only limited duties: maintenance, clerical work, collecting rent payable to the owner or brokerage, showing units, giving out published information and passing on applications. The person may not negotiate leases, change the rent, approve applications or present themselves as the person in charge.
- **Florida.** Fla. Stat. 475.011(4) exempts a salaried employee of an owner, or of a registered broker for an owner, who works in an onsite apartment rental office in a leasing capacity. Section 475.011(5) exempts a salaried manager of a condominium or cooperative apartment complex for rentals of 1 year or less.
- **South Carolina.** S.C. Code 40-57-240 exempts unlicensed owners who rent their own property, attorneys acting for a client, government staff and court-appointed receivers and trustees.

Owner exemptions protect owners who manage their own doors. They do not cover a company that manages doors for other people.

## Can I work under another broker?

Yes, in the 3 states below the statute text shows that a licensee can manage rentals under a broker. In those states you do not need your own broker license on day 1. Check the rule in your state.

The supervision rules differ:

1. **Oregon** lets a broker manage rentals while associated with and supervised by a principal broker (ORS 696.010).
2. **South Carolina** calls a property manager a "supervised licensee" and puts a property manager-in-charge over trust accounts (S.C. Code 40-57-30).
3. **Arizona** requires the designated broker or an authorized licensee to sign each management agreement (A.R.S. 32-2173).

- [ ] Set up a trust account in the broker's name as trustee, if your state requires it.

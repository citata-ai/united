A California tenant moves out on March 1. The landlord has until March 22 to send the itemized statement and the refund. That 21-day clock is the rule most managers know. Since 2024, California Civil Code 1950.5 added a lower cap, photo duties and new payment rules. This page puts the current text in one place, as read on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov on 2026-10-19.

## What Is the Maximum Security Deposit a Landlord Can Charge in California?

The California deposit cap is 1 month's rent for most landlords. Civ. Code 1950.5(c)(1) bars any "security, however denominated" above 1 month's rent, in addition to the first month's rent paid at move-in. "Security" covers any fee or charge collected at the start of the tenancy, except the screening fee in Section 1950.6.

A small landlord may take up to 2 months' rent. To qualify, the landlord must meet both tests in 1950.5(c)(5):

1. **Owner type.** The landlord is a natural person, or an LLC in which all members are natural persons. A family trust counts as a natural person.
2. **Portfolio size.** The landlord owns no more than 2 residential rental properties, with no more than 4 units in total.

The small-landlord exception does not apply when the tenant is a service member. The landlord also may not refuse to rent to a service member because of this limit.

Other points in 1950.5(c):

- The cap does not apply to security collected before July 1, 2024.
- A landlord may take 6 months' rent or more in advance if the lease term is 6 months or longer.
- On or after April 1, 2025, if a landlord charges a service member a higher deposit because of credit or rental history, the landlord must explain it in writing. The extra amount goes back after no more than 6 months if the tenant has paid all rent.

**Worked example.** Rent is $2,800. A corporate owner may collect $2,800 rent plus a $2,800 deposit at move-in: $5,600 total. A qualifying small landlord may collect $2,800 rent plus up to $5,600 deposit: $8,400 total.

## How Many Days Does a California Landlord Have to Return a Security Deposit?

A California landlord has 21 calendar days after the tenant vacates. Within that time, Civ. Code 1950.5(h)(1) requires an itemized statement of the deposit and how the landlord used it, plus a refund of any remaining amount.

How to pay the refund:

- **Default.** Personal delivery, or a check mailed first-class to the tenant.
- **Electronic payers.** If the tenant paid the deposit or rent electronically, the landlord returns the rest electronically to an account the tenant names in writing, unless both sides agree in writing on another method. The current text of 1950.5(h)(1)(A)(ii) holds this rule (amended by AB 414, effective January 1, 2026).
- **Several adult tenants.** Without a written agreement, the landlord sends one check payable to all adult tenants on the lease.

If a repair cannot be done within 21 days, the landlord may deduct a good faith estimate. The landlord then sends the final bill within 14 calendar days after the repair is done.

## What Can a Landlord Deduct From a Security Deposit in California?

A California landlord may deduct only amounts "reasonably necessary" for 4 purposes in 1950.5(b):

1. Unpaid rent.
2. Repair of damage beyond ordinary wear and tear, caused by the tenant or a guest.
3. Cleaning to return the unit to the same level of cleanliness as at move-in.
4. Restoring or replacing personal property, if the lease allows it.

The landlord may not charge for damage that existed before the tenancy, or for ordinary wear and tear. Professional carpet cleaning is allowed only if it is reasonably necessary to return the unit to its move-in condition (1950.5(e)(2)).

The tenant may ask for an initial inspection before move-out. The landlord must tell the tenant about this right in writing. The inspection takes place no earlier than 2 weeks before the end of the tenancy, with 48 hours' written notice. After it, the landlord gives a list of proposed deductions so the tenant can fix them.

## Does a California Landlord Have to Give an Itemized List of Deductions?

Yes. A California landlord must send an itemized statement within 21 days, and attach proof for each repair or cleaning charge (1950.5(h)(2)):

- [ ] **Own staff did the work:** describe the work, the time spent and the hourly rate.
- [ ] **A vendor did the work:** a copy of the bill or receipt, with the vendor's name, address and phone.
- [ ] **Materials:** a copy of the bill, receipt or vendor price list.
- [ ] **Photos:** the photos required by 1950.5(g), plus a written cost explanation. Mail, email, a flash drive or an online link all work.

The receipts are not needed if repairs and cleaning together are $125 or less, or if the tenant signed a valid waiver. But if the tenant asks within 14 days, the landlord must send the documents within 14 days.

The photo rules have two dates. For tenancies that begin on or after July 1, 2025, the landlord photographs the unit at move-in. Since April 1, 2025, the landlord photographs the unit after move-out, before repairs, and again after repairs.

## Must a California Landlord Pay Interest or Keep the Deposit in a Separate Account?

Civil Code 1950.5 has no rule on interest and no separate-account rule. The statute says the landlord holds the security for the tenant, and the tenant's claim comes before any creditor of the landlord (1950.5(d)). Some California cities have their own deposit interest rules. Check the city code for each property.

When a property is sold, the landlord must either transfer the deposit to the buyer and notify the tenant, or return it to the tenant with an accounting (1950.5(i)). If the seller does neither, the buyer is liable with the seller (1950.5(k)).

## What Is the Penalty for a California Landlord Who Keeps a Deposit Wrongly?

A bad faith claim or retention can cost the landlord statutory damages of up to twice the deposit, plus actual damages (1950.5(m)). The court may award this even if the tenant did not ask for it. The landlord has the burden to prove the deductions were reasonable.

Two more rules carry weight:

- A landlord who in bad faith fails to follow subdivision (h) may not keep any part of the deposit (1950.5(h)(7)).
- A tenant may sue in small claims court if the claim is within the small claims limit (1950.5(o)).

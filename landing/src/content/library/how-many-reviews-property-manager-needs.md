## Is there a review count AI needs before it recommends you?

No. No AI engine and no Google help page gives a review count or star rating that unlocks a recommendation. Any vendor that says "you need 50 reviews for ChatGPT" is guessing.

What Google does say is general. Its Business Profile help page lists prominence as a local ranking factor and says: "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking." It gives no number.

Human Level, an SEO agency, tested AI Mode for local searches in 2025. It saw AI Mode show ratings taken from the Business Profile next to each business. The article gives no threshold either.

So the question changes. It is not "what number does AI need". It is "what number do the managers AI names in my city have, and what do people expect".

## What do studies show about review counts and stars?

Studies of people show clear cutoffs, even though engines publish none. BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 asked 1,002 US adults through SurveyMonkey.

| Finding (BrightLocal, 2026) | Share of consumers |
|---|---|
| Read reviews for local businesses | 97% |
| Would not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews | 47% |
| Would use a business with 5 or fewer reviews | 9% |
| Only use a business with 4 stars or more | 68% (55% in 2025) |
| Only use a business with 4.5 stars or more | 31% (17% in 2025) |
| Only use a business with 5 stars | 10% |
| Look for reviews from the last 3 months | 74% |
| Use ChatGPT or other AI tools for local recommendations | 45% (6% in 2025) |

Watch for: this survey covers all local businesses, not property management. An owner who hands over a rental house may read more reviews than a person who picks a coffee shop. Use the numbers as a floor, not a target.

## Why are review thresholds for AI a guess?

Thresholds are a guess because the engines do not count reviews in one place. Each engine reads different sources, and each source shows a different count.

In our 50-city test on 2026-10-19, we asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude for the best property manager in each city (our data, 150 answers). The sources they used:

- **Expertise.com** was a source in 93 of 150 answers.
- **Yelp** in 50 answers, all of them from Claude.
- **BBB** in 14 answers, 11 of them from ChatGPT.
- **Birdeye** review pages in 19 answers.

A manager with 200 Google reviews and 3 Yelp reviews looks strong to one engine and thin to another. That is why one number cannot work. Your reviews need to exist where each engine reads.

## How do you compare with local competitors?

Compare yourself with the managers that AI already names for your city. Their numbers are the real bar in your market.

1. **Ask 3 engines** "What is the best property management company in <your city>?" Save the names.
2. **List the top 5 names** that show up most.
3. **Record each one's Google review count and star rating**, plus Yelp and BBB if they have them.
4. **Take the median** of the 5 counts. That is your target count.
5. **Compute the gap** and the months you need to close it.

Worked example with made-up numbers for a city:

| Manager | Google reviews | Stars |
|---|---|---|
| Manager A | 410 | 4.8 |
| Manager B | 260 | 4.6 |
| Manager C | 180 | 4.7 |
| Manager D | 95 | 4.4 |
| Manager E | 60 | 4.9 |
| You | 40 | 4.7 |

The median of A to E is 180. Your gap is 180 − 40 = 140 reviews. At 10 new reviews a month, you close it in 14 months. At 20 a month, in 7 months. The star rating is fine: 4.7 is above the 4.5 line that 31% of consumers use.

## How fast should reviews come in?

Reviews should come in every month, not in one burst. In BrightLocal's 2026 survey, 74% of consumers looked for reviews from the last 3 months. 32% wanted reviews from the last 2 weeks, up from 20% in 2025.

A property manager has natural review moments. Count them to set a monthly target:

| Moment | Example volume for 300 doors | Ask whom |
|---|---|---|
| New lease signed | 8 to 12 a month | Tenant |
| Repair closed | 40 to 80 a month | Tenant |
| First owner statement | 2 to 4 a month | Owner |
| Lease renewal | 10 to 15 a month | Tenant and owner |
| Year-end owner report | Once a year, all owners | Owner |

These volumes are an example, not data. Put your own counts in. If 10% of 60 asks turn into a review, you get 6 reviews a month.

## What review practices are not allowed?

Some ways to get reviews break Google rules or federal law. Google's review policy bans payment, discounts or free services in exchange for a review. It also bans asking only happy customers and setting review quotas for staff.

The FTC rule on consumer reviews, 16 CFR 465.4, makes it unlawful to give an incentive tied to a review with a set sentiment, positive or negative. The FTC also says asking only customers you think are happy can be misleading if it skews the reviews.

Safe practice:

- **Ask everyone** at the same moments, happy or not.
- **Offer nothing** in return.
- **Reply to each review** with facts. Never share tenant or owner private data in a reply.
- **Do not post reviews** from staff, family or past employees.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

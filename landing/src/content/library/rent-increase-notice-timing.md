## How much notice is common for a rent increase?

Rent increase notice is set by state law, and common periods are 30, 60 or 90 days. There is no federal rule for private market rent. Some states also scale the notice to the size of the raise or to how long the tenant has lived there.

The table shows 3 states with clear, different rules. Use it as an example of how the rules vary, not as a rule for your state.

| State | Notice rule | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| California | 30 days if the increase is 10% or less in 12 months. 90 days if it is more than 10%. | Civil Code 827(b) |
| California (most units covered by the statewide cap) | Increase may not exceed 5% plus the change in cost of living, or 10%, whichever is lower, in 12 months | Civil Code 1947.12 |
| Oregon | No increase in the first year. After that, written notice at least 90 days before the increase. | ORS 90.323 |
| New York | For an increase of 5% or more: 30 days (under 1 year), 60 days (1 to 2 years), 90 days (2 years or more) | Real Property Law 226-C |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Can I raise rent during a fixed-term lease?

A fixed-term lease usually locks the rent until the term ends. The lease is a contract, and the rent is one of its terms. You can raise rent mid-lease only if the lease has a clause that allows it, and only if state law allows that clause.

Oregon shows how state law can add limits on top of the lease. ORS 90.323 says a landlord may not raise rent "during the first year after the tenancy begins." The 90-day notice rule then applies to every tenancy other than week-to-week, so it covers fixed-term leases too.

The practical rule: plan the raise for the renewal date. Put the new rent in the renewal offer, and send the offer early enough to meet the longest notice your state requires.

## How does notice differ for month-to-month tenants?

A month-to-month tenancy has no fixed rent term, so a landlord can change the rent with proper notice. The notice period is where states differ most.

California Civil Code 827 covers tenancies "from week to week, month to month, or other period less than a month." For these, the notice must arrive at least 30 days before an increase of 10% or less. It must arrive at least 90 days before an increase of more than 10%. The 10% test counts all increases in the prior 12 months together.

New York Real Property Law 226-C uses a different trigger. The rule applies when the increase is "equal to or greater than five percent above the current rent," and also when the landlord does not plan to renew. The days depend on the longer of the tenant's time in the unit or the lease length.

## Worked example: which notice applies?

The notice period can change with the size of the raise. Here is a California month-to-month unit at $2,000 a month.

| Step | Increase A | Increase B |
|---|---|---|
| Current rent | $2,000 | $2,000 |
| New rent | $2,150 | $2,250 |
| Increase in dollars | $150 | $250 |
| Increase in percent | 7.5% | 12.5% |
| Notice under Civil Code 827 | 30 days | 90 days |

Increase B also needs a second check. If the unit is covered by Civil Code 1947.12, the cap is 5% plus the change in cost of living, and never more than 10%. A 12.5% raise would break that cap. Many owners skip this second check and send a notice they cannot enforce.

Civil Code 1947.12 stays in effect until January 1, 2030, per its own text. Local rent control can set lower limits.

## How do I deliver a rent increase notice?

Deliver the notice in writing, by a method your state statute names. California Civil Code 827 allows 2 methods for a rent increase: deliver a copy to the tenant in person, or mail it under Code of Civil Procedure 1013. Oregon ORS 90.323 requires written notice.

Use this checklist for each notice:

- **Write the full new rent.** State the dollar amount, not only the percent.
- **Write the effective date.** Count the notice days from the day the tenant gets the notice.
- **Add the mail days.** If you mail, check how many extra days your state adds for mail service.
- **Name the unit and the tenants.** Use every adult on the lease.
- **Keep proof.** Keep a copy, a mail receipt or a signed delivery note.
- **Check local rules.** A city rent control or just-cause law can add steps.

## What mistakes make a rent increase notice invalid?

A rent increase notice fails most often on timing. If the notice arrives late, the new rent may not start on the date you wrote.

Common mistakes:

1. **Too few days.** You used 30 days when the raise needed 90.
2. **Wrong count.** You started the count on the send date, not the delivery date.
3. **Mid-lease raise.** The lease had no clause that allowed it.
4. **Over a cap.** The raise was over a state or local cap.
5. **Wrong method.** You sent a text when the statute names mail or personal delivery.
6. **Retaliation timing.** The raise came soon after a tenant complaint. California Civil Code 1942.5 bars a rent increase within 180 days of certain tenant complaints, if the tenant is not behind on rent. Other states have their own rules.

## When should I start planning the next increase?

Start about 120 days before the lease end date. That gives room for the longest common notice period (90 days) plus mail time. It also gives the tenant time to decide, which lowers turnover.

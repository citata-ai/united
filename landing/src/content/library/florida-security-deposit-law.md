Florida puts its deposit rules in one section: Fla. Stat. § 83.49, part of the Florida Residential Landlord and Tenant Act. I read the 2026 text on the Florida Senate site on 2026-10-19. The two clocks that matter start the day the lease ends: 15 days and 30 days.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | No cap in the statute | § 83.49 |
| How to hold the deposit | Separate Florida account (with or without interest), or a surety bond | § 83.49(1) |
| Written notice of where the deposit is held | In the lease or within 30 days after receipt (landlords with 5+ units) | § 83.49(2) |
| Notice after a change in where it is held | Within 30 days after the change | § 83.49(2) |
| Full return, no claim | Within 15 days after the lease ends | § 83.49(3)(a) |
| Notice of a claim on the deposit | Within 30 days, by certified mail or e-mail under § 83.505 | § 83.49(3)(a) |
| Tenant objection | Within 15 days after receipt of the claim notice | § 83.49(3)(b) |
| Pay back the balance after a claim | Within 30 days after the date of the claim notice | § 83.49(3)(b) |
| Interest payment, when owed | At least once a year | § 83.49(9) |
| Local deposit rules | Preempted to the state | § 83.425 |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Florida?

Florida sets no maximum security deposit. Section 83.49 controls how the landlord holds, discloses and returns deposit money. It does not limit the amount.

Two related rules apply:

- **Local rules do not apply.** Fla. Stat. § 83.425 preempts local regulation of security deposits to the state. A city or county cannot add its own cap.
- **A fee can replace the deposit.** Fla. Stat. § 83.491 lets a landlord offer a fee in lieu of a security deposit. The tenant can switch to a normal deposit at any time. The written agreement must contain a set disclosure, and the fee may not go up during the lease.

Section 83.49(4) also says the section does not apply where the rent or deposit is regulated by law or by a public body. That includes federally regulated programs such as Section 8 of the National Housing Act. If you manage voucher units, check the program rules too. Our guide on [Section 8 rent limits](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) covers the rent side.

## How many days does a Florida landlord have to return a security deposit?

A Florida landlord has 15 days to return the full deposit when the landlord makes no claim. When the landlord wants to keep part of it, the landlord has 30 days to send a written claim notice. The clock starts at termination of the rental agreement, after the tenant vacates.

Here is a worked timeline for a lease that ends on March 31:

| Day | Date | Step |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | March 31 | Lease ends, tenant moves out |
| 15 | April 15 | Last day to return the full deposit if there is no claim |
| 30 | April 30 | Last day to send the claim notice |
| Notice + 15 | Example: May 15 | Tenant's objection period ends (counted from receipt) |
| Notice + 30 | May 30 | Last day to pay back the balance, counted from the notice date |

The tenant has a duty too. Under § 83.49(5), a tenant who leaves must give at least 7 days' written notice with a forwarding address, unless the lease says otherwise. If the tenant does not, the landlord is relieved of the § 83.49(3)(a) notice duty. The tenant does not lose the right to the deposit.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Florida?

A Florida landlord can deduct the amount of a claim that the landlord sets out in the 30-day notice. Section 83.49 does not list allowed deductions. It controls the process. The deposit secures "performance of the rental agreement", so claims usually come from the lease and from Part II of Chapter 83.

The process is strict:

1. **Send the notice in time.** Within 30 days, by certified mail to the last known address, or by e-mail under § 83.505.
2. **State the amount and the reason.** The statute gives the exact wording to use.
3. **Wait 15 days.** If the tenant does not object in writing within 15 days of receipt, you may deduct the claim.
4. **Pay the balance.** Send what is left within 30 days of the notice date.

A tenant who does not object in time can still sue for damages later. The failure to object does not waive that right (§ 83.49(3)(b)).

## Does a Florida landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

A Florida landlord must give a written notice that states the claim amount and the reason for it. The statute calls this a "notice of my intention to impose a claim for damages". It must also tell the tenant to object in writing within 15 days and give the address for the objection.

The statute does not use the word "itemized". In practice, a notice that lists each charge with its reason makes the 15-day objection step easier to defend. Use the statutory text word for word and add your line items under it.

## Must a Florida landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

A Florida landlord must keep the deposit separate. Section 83.49(1) gives 3 choices:

| Option | Interest to tenant | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Separate non-interest account in a Florida financial institution | None | No commingling, no pledging |
| Separate interest-bearing account in a Florida financial institution | At least 75% of the account's annual average rate, or 5% simple, landlord's choice | No commingling, no pledging |
| Surety bond with the circuit court clerk | 5% simple interest per year | Bond equals total deposits held or $50,000, whichever is less |

A landlord with units in 5 or more counties may post one bond with the Secretary of State. That bond equals total deposits held or $250,000, whichever is less. When interest is owed, the landlord pays it or credits it against rent at least once a year (§ 83.49(9)).

Landlords with 5 or more units must also send the written disclosure in § 83.49(2). It names the bank or the bond, says if interest applies, and contains a set paragraph in capital letters.

When a property is sold, all deposits and earned interest move to the new owner or agent with an accounting (§ 83.49(7)).

## What is the penalty for a Florida landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

A Florida landlord who misses the 30-day notice forfeits the right to claim against the deposit. The landlord must return it and may then file a separate suit for damages. The statute does not set a double or triple penalty.

In a court case over the deposit, the prevailing party gets court costs plus a reasonable attorney fee (§ 83.49(3)(c)). For a small deposit, the fee award can be larger than the deposit. That makes the 30-day notice the most important step in the whole process.

## Florida deposit checklist for property managers

- Put the account choice and the § 83.49(2) disclosure in the lease.
- Keep each deposit out of your operating account.
- Diary 15 and 30 days from each move-out date.
- Send claim notices by certified mail or by e-mail that meets § 83.505.
- Keep the mailing receipt and photos with the file.
- Treat a renewal as a new lease with a new deposit (§ 83.49(6)).

Two property management websites with the same traffic can end the month far apart. Goodjuju Marketing found that the top 25% of its 35 client sites got about 3 times the owner leads of the bottom 25%, from the same number of visitors (July 8 to October 5, 2026). But a form fill is only the start. What happens in the next hour decides if that owner signs with you. A mystery shop shows you that hour.

## How do I set up a test lead?

Set up a test lead that looks like a real owner and that your team does not recognize. Plan the whole test before you send anything.

Setup steps:

1. **Write 3 owner stories.** For example: "Single-family home, 3 bed, moving for work in 6 weeks." "Two duplexes, current manager is slow." "Inherited a condo, never rented before."
2. **Make a test identity.** A new email address and a second phone number that you control. Do not use a real person's name without consent.
3. **Pick real addresses** in your service area, but not homes you manage.
4. **Choose the shopper.** A friend, a family member or a hired shopper. Not someone your staff knows.
5. **Tell staff in general terms.** "We test our lead handling from time to time." Do not name dates.
6. **Check recording rules.** Federal law (18 U.S.C. 2511(2)(d)) allows recording when you are a party to the call or a party consents, unless the purpose is a crime or tort. Some states require more, so check your state's law first.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What should I time and record?

Time each step in minutes and record facts, not feelings. Use one row per test.

| Metric | How to measure | Target (set your own) |
|---|---|---|
| Time to first human reply | Lead sent → first real reply, not an autoresponder | Under 15 minutes in business hours |
| Phone answered live | Yes / no, and number of rings | Live answer in 4 rings |
| Fees stated | Did the reply include the fee or a fee page link? | Yes |
| Next step offered | A call, a rental analysis or a meeting time | Yes, with a time |
| Follow-ups in 7 days | Count calls, emails and texts | 3 or more |
| Name of the person | Did one person own the lead? | Yes |
| Question answered | Did they answer the owner's real question? | Yes |

The targets in the table are examples. Pick targets your team can hit, then raise them.

Fourandhalf, a property management marketing agency, names 4 phone problems that lose leads: not answering every call, automated receptionist systems, full voicemail inboxes, and staff who are not trained to answer. Your test should catch each one.

### Scorecard: one test

| Item | Points |
|---|---|
| First human reply under 15 minutes | 3 |
| Under 60 minutes | 2 |
| Same day | 1 |
| Fees stated or linked | 2 |
| Clear next step with a time | 2 |
| 3+ follow-ups in 7 days | 2 |
| Owner's question answered | 1 |
| Maximum | 10 |

Average the scores per channel. A channel under 5 is your first fix.

## How do I check calls, forms and chat?

Test every channel an owner can use, because owners pick the one that suits them. Run this checklist for each.

**Phone**
- [ ] Call the main number in business hours, at lunch, after 5 pm and on a weekend
- [ ] Note who answers, and if an owner call gets routed to tenant support
- [ ] Leave a voicemail once; time the callback
- [ ] Check that the voicemail box is not full

**Web form**
- [ ] Fill in the owner form on desktop and on a phone
- [ ] Check the confirmation page and email
- [ ] Time the first human reply
- [ ] Check that the form shows as a lead in Google Analytics

**Chat or chatbot**
- [ ] Ask a fee question and a "can you manage my condo" question
- [ ] Check if a human takes over, and how fast
- [ ] Check if the chat captures contact details correctly

**Google Business Profile**
- [ ] Tap "Call" and "Website" from the Map Pack listing
- [ ] Send a message if messaging is on
- [ ] Check that the Performance report counts the call and the website click

**Email**
- [ ] Email the address on your contact page
- [ ] Time the reply and check it reaches a person

### Worked example: what the test found

| Channel | Tests | Avg first reply | Avg score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phone | 4 | 2 of 4 live; callbacks 3 hours | 5.5 |
| Web form | 2 | 41 minutes | 7 |
| Chat | 2 | Bot only, no handoff | 3 |
| Email | 2 | 26 hours | 3 |

These are example results. The fix list writes itself: route chat to a human in business hours, fix the email inbox, and answer the phone live at lunch.

## How do I compare with competitors?

Run a lighter version of the same test on 3 local rivals. Use public channels, and respect their time.

Rules for a fair comparison:

- Use the same owner story and the same day and time for each company.
- Send a form or a short call. Do not book long appointments you will not keep. If you book, cancel right away.
- Record only what an owner sees: speed, clarity of fees, follow-up and next step.
- Do not record rivals' calls unless you have checked your state's law.

| Company | First reply | Fees clear | Follow-ups in 7 days | Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| You | 41 min | Yes | 2 | 6 |
| Rival A | 8 min | Yes | 4 | 9 |
| Rival B | Next day | No | 1 | 3 |
| Rival C | 22 min | Link only | 3 | 7 |

If a rival beats you on speed, an owner who contacts 3 managers may sign before you call back. Repeat the test every quarter, and share the scores with your team, not to blame, but to fix the process.

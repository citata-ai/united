## What do Section 8 owners worry about?

Section 8 owners worry about 4 things: inspections, the first payment, the rent cap and repair deadlines. These owners rent to Housing Choice Voucher tenants. A housing authority (PHA, the local agency that runs the voucher program) pays part of the rent. The tenant pays the rest.

Most of the fear comes from not knowing the rules. A manager who explains each rule in plain words looks safer than one who says "we do Section 8 too." So your marketing job is simple: name the fear, cite the rule, then show your process.

Here are the 4 fears and the federal rule behind each one. All rules come from 24 CFR part 982, read on ecfr.gov on 2026-10-19.

| Owner fear | Federal rule | What the rule says |
|---|---|---|
| "The unit will fail inspection." | 24 CFR 982.405(a), (b) | The PHA inspects before the lease starts, then at least every 2 years. Small rural PHAs inspect every 3 years. |
| "I will wait months for money." | 24 CFR 982.305(c) | No payment until the HAP contract is signed. A contract signed more than 60 days after the lease starts is void, unless HUD grants an extension. |
| "The rent will be too low." | 24 CFR 982.503(c) | The basic payment standard range is 90% to 110% of HUD's fair market rent. |
| "A repair call will cost me the tenant." | 24 CFR 982.405(d) | Life-threatening items: fix within 24 hours of PHA notice. Other items: fix within 30 days or an approved extension. |

The HAP contract (housing assistance payments contract) is the contract between the owner and the PHA. Under 24 CFR 982.451, its term is the same as the lease term.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney. Your state and city may add rules, such as source-of-income protection.

## Which facts should a Section 8 management page show?

A Section 8 management page should show local rent facts, the process and the timeline. Owners compare managers on proof, not on adjectives.

Use this checklist for the page:

- **Local fair market rents.** Show the HUD fair market rent by bedroom count for your metro. Link to the source. Our [Section 8 rent limits guide](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) lists FY2027 numbers by metro.
- **Payment standard range.** Explain that each PHA sets its own payment standards. Under 24 CFR 982.503, the basic range is 90% to 110% of the fair market rent. Link to each local PHA's schedule.
- **A rent example.** Show one worked example. Let owners try their own numbers in the [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator).
- **The lease-up timeline.** List each step: request for tenancy approval, inspection, rent approval, HAP contract, first payment.
- **Your inspection process.** Say when you do a pre-inspection, who attends the PHA inspection, and how fast your team fixes items.
- **Your fee for voucher units.** If the fee is the same as for other units, say so. If you charge more for inspections, show the amount.
- **What you will not promise.** Do not promise a fixed rent or a payment date. The PHA sets the payment, and it can change during the contract (24 CFR 982.451(b)).

## How do I explain the rent math to an owner?

Explain the rent math with one example and one rule. The payment standard is not the rent. The PHA compares the gross rent to the payment standard, then pays its share. The tenant pays the rest.

Worked example, payment standard range only (hypothetical fair market rent):

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| HUD fair market rent, 2 bedrooms (example) | $1,500 |
| Lowest basic payment standard, 90% | $1,350 |
| Highest basic payment standard, 110% | $1,650 |

So in this example, a PHA can set the 2-bedroom payment standard anywhere from $1,350 to $1,650 without HUD approval (24 CFR 982.503(c)(1)). A higher amount needs an exception payment standard.

Two more rules matter to owners. First, the rent must be reasonable for the area (24 CFR 982.305(a)(4)). Second, the 40% rule applies at first lease-up when the gross rent is above the payment standard. The family share may then not exceed 40% of its monthly adjusted income (24 CFR 982.305(a)(5)). A manager who knows these rules can set a rent that the PHA will approve.

## How do I show inspection experience?

Show inspection experience with your process and your own numbers, not with claims. "We know Section 8" is a claim. "We pre-inspect every unit against the HQS list 7 days before the PHA visit" is a process.

HQS means housing quality standards. Under 24 CFR 982.401, HQS refers to the minimum standards HUD sets under 24 CFR 5.703. Ask your PHA which inspection standard it uses now.

Steps to build proof:

1. **Write down your pre-inspection list.** Publish the top 10 items you check, such as smoke alarms, GFCI outlets and window locks.
2. **Count your results.** Track first-time pass rate for the last 12 months. Publish it only if you can show the records.
3. **Show the repair timeline.** Explain how your team meets the 24-hour and 30-day deadlines in 24 CFR 982.405(d).
4. **Name your PHAs.** List the housing authorities you work with. Owners trust a manager who knows the local office.
5. **Add one real story.** With the owner's consent, describe one failed inspection and how you fixed it. Give dates and days to pass.

## What should the page avoid?

The page should avoid promises, fear and vague claims. Do not write "guaranteed rent" or "never miss a payment." The PHA must pay promptly under the HAP contract (24 CFR 982.451(b)(5)), but payments can still be late. Late-payment penalties apply only in the cases that rule lists.

Do not use an owner's fear of "bad tenants" as a hook. That language can steer, and it is not true to the program. Write about rules, timelines and your process. Owners who rent to voucher tenants will see that you know the work.

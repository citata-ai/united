## Why does Section 8 content matter for AI answers?

Section 8 content matters because owners ask AI about vouchers, and few manager sites give a clear answer. In our Chicago baseline of 2026-10-16, Fridman Properties was named in 0 of 6 answers to a Section 8 owner prompt (our data). Its old site had 1 FAQ answer about Section 8.

Disclosure: Fridman Properties' owner is CEO of MyRemotely, our partner. We do not claim Fridman is a Section 8 specialist.

The gap is wide across the market too. We studied 31 clients of the 3 largest marketing agencies for property managers. Only ClearLead Digital had a narrow Section 8 owner guide idea (our data, 2026-10). So a manager with real voucher experience has room to be the clear source.

## Which Section 8 questions do owners ask?

Owners ask Section 8 questions about money, time and risk. Each question below maps to one federal rule. Write one page, or one H2 section, per question.

| Owner question | The short answer | Rule to cite |
|---|---|---|
| How much rent will the voucher pay? | The PHA sets a payment standard from 90% to 110% of the FMR | 24 CFR 982.503(c) |
| Can I charge more than the FMR? | Yes, if the rent passes rent reasonableness and the 40% test | 24 CFR 982.507, 982.508 |
| What must happen before the lease starts? | The unit passes inspection, the lease has the tenancy addendum, the rent is reasonable | 24 CFR 982.305(a) |
| How fast is the first inspection? | Within 15 days at a PHA with up to 1,250 budgeted units | 24 CFR 982.305(b)(2) |
| When does the first payment come? | Not before the HAP contract is signed, at most 60 days after the lease starts | 24 CFR 982.305(c) |
| Can the tenant pay me extra on the side? | No. The owner must return any excess rent | 24 CFR 982.451(b)(4) |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

Watch for: state and city laws can add rules on top of these. Check your state and city law before you publish.

## Which official sources should you cite?

Cite primary sources: the HUD notice, the federal regulation and your local housing authority. Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode have "no additional requirements" beyond normal indexing (Google Search Central). So a clear page with good sources is the work.

Use these sources, in this order:

1. **The Federal Register notice** for the FMR year. The FY2027 notice (91 FR 56156) was published on 2026-09-01.
2. **24 CFR part 982** for the program rules. Link to the exact section, such as 982.503.
3. **Your PHA's payment standard page.** The PHA, not HUD, sets the number the voucher pays.
4. **Your own data,** labeled as yours. For example: "Our average days from RFTA to HAP contract in 2026: [your number] (our data)." Use only numbers you measured.

Do not cite a blog that copied the HUD table. Link to the place that holds the fact.

## How do FMR tables help?

FMR tables help because they turn a vague owner question into a number per bedroom size. HUD publishes Fair Market Rents (FMRs) for each metro area, at least once a year (Federal Register). Our [Section 8 rent limits pages](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) show the FY2027 FMRs for the 50 largest metro areas.

Here is a worked example for a 2-bedroom unit in the Chicago metro FMR area.

| Step | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| FY2027 2-bedroom FMR | $2,011 | HUD FY2027 FMR file, via our Chicago page |
| FY2026 2-bedroom FMR | $1,781 | HUD FY2026 FMR file |
| Change | +$230, or +12.9% | Our math |
| Payment standard floor (90%) | $1,809.90 | 24 CFR 982.503(c) |
| Payment standard ceiling (110%) | $2,212.10 | 24 CFR 982.503(c) |

So a Chicago owner page with the FY2026 number is $230 low for a 2-bedroom. An AI engine that reads the old page repeats the old number. The table also tells owners the truth: the PHA picks a number inside the range, and the rent must still be reasonable.

Give owners a way to check their own unit. The [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator) does the math for one unit.

## What does a Section 8 owner page need?

A Section 8 owner page needs a direct answer, a table and a date. Google lists 3 basics for AI features: text content, helpful images or video, and structured data that matches the visible text (Google Search Central).

Use this checklist for each page:

- **Answer first.** Put a 2–4 sentence answer at the top.
- **One question per section.** AI engines cut pages into parts. Each H2 must stand alone.
- **Numbers with units.** "$2,011 a month, 2 bedrooms, FY2027", not "about $2,000".
- **A rule citation** next to each rule, such as "24 CFR 982.508".
- **A "last checked" date** at the top.
- **Your process.** Explain how your team handles the RFTA, the inspection and the HAP contract, in steps.
- **Honest limits.** Say what you do not do, such as project-based vouchers.
- **No steering.** Write about the program and the unit, never about who the renters are.

## How do you keep the numbers current?

Keep the numbers current with a fixed yearly calendar. HUD FMRs take effect on October 1 each year (Federal Register). A PHA must revise its payment standards within 3 months if they fall outside the basic range (24 CFR 982.503(c)(3)).

| When | Task |
|---|---|
| September | Read the new FMR notice in the Federal Register |
| October 1 | Update FMR tables and the "last checked" date |
| By January 1 | Check your PHA's new payment standard page |
| Each quarter | Ask AI your 5 Section 8 owner prompts and log who it names |
| On a rule change | Update the CFR section text and its date |

Watch for: a stale page can hurt more than no page. If an owner reads last year's rent, then gets a lower offer, you lose trust.

What to do: list your Section 8 pages in a sheet with the source and the date checked. Review the sheet each October.

## How do you test if AI cites your Section 8 page?

Test by asking the same owner prompts each month and logging the answers. Use prompts like "Section 8 property manager in [your city]" and "how much does Section 8 pay for a 2 bedroom in [your city]".

1. **Run each prompt** in ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude.
2. **Log the names** each answer gives and the sources it links.
3. **Compare** with last month.
4. **Fix the page** that AI skips first.

AI answers change from run to run. Look at the trend over 3 months, not one answer.

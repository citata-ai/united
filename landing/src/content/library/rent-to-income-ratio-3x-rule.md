## How do I calculate rent-to-income?

The rent-to-income ratio is the monthly rent divided by the applicant's gross monthly income. A 3x rule means the income must be at least 3 times the rent. Both views give the same test.

**Worked example:**

- Rent: $1,600 a month.
- 3x minimum income: $1,600 × 3 = $4,800 a month, or $57,600 a year.
- Applicant earns $62,400 a year: $62,400 ÷ 12 = $5,200 a month.
- Ratio: $1,600 ÷ $5,200 = 30.8%, or 3.25 times rent. The applicant passes.

Use this table to check a range of rents at a glance.

| Monthly rent | 2.5x income | 3x income | 3x income per year |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1,000 | $2,500 | $3,000 | $36,000 |
| $1,250 | $3,125 | $3,750 | $45,000 |
| $1,500 | $3,750 | $4,500 | $54,000 |
| $2,000 | $5,000 | $6,000 | $72,000 |
| $2,500 | $6,250 | $7,500 | $90,000 |
| $3,000 | $7,500 | $9,000 | $108,000 |

## Where does the 3x rule come from?

The 3x rule tracks the 30% affordability line used in federal housing. HUD treats a household as cost-burdened when it spends more than 30% of income on housing, the Census Bureau notes. HUD's own voucher math uses the same idea: under 24 CFR 5.628, total tenant payment is the highest of several amounts, starting with 30% of monthly adjusted income.

A 3x rule means rent is 33.3% of gross income. That is a little looser than the 30% line.

Many renters already pass that line. The Census Bureau reported that 49.7% of renter households for whom rent burden is calculated spent more than 30% of income on housing costs in 2023. The median renter spent 31%. So a strict 3x rule, with no other path, turns away a large share of the market.

## Is 3x rent a law?

No. No federal statute sets a minimum income multiple for renters. The 3x rule is a screening custom. You may use 2.5x, 3x or a different test, as long as you write it down and apply it the same way to everyone.

The law steps in on how you apply it. Fair housing laws ban rules that treat a protected group worse. The next sections cover the common limits.

## Should I use gross or net income?

Use gross income, before taxes, unless you have a clear reason not to. Gross income shows on pay stubs, offer letters and benefit letters, so you can check it. Net income changes with tax choices and retirement savings, which makes it harder to compare.

Whatever you choose, state it in your written criteria. Count every lawful, verifiable income source the same way: wages, self-employment, Social Security, child support, alimony and vouchers. For self-employed applicants, ask for the same proof each time, such as 2 years of tax returns or 3 months of bank statements.

## How do I count co-applicants' income?

Add the gross income of all adult co-applicants who will sign the lease. Then run the test on the total. Write that rule down too.

**Example:**

- Rent: $2,400. The 3x minimum is $7,200 a month.
- Applicant A earns $4,000 a month. Applicant B earns $3,500 a month.
- Combined: $7,500 a month. Ratio: $2,400 ÷ $7,500 = 32%. They pass.

A guarantor is different from a co-applicant. You may set a higher multiple for a guarantor, because the guarantor also pays a home of their own. Write that multiple in your criteria. If you ask for a guarantor because of a consumer report, the FTC says that counts as adverse action and needs a notice.

## What about cities and states that limit the income rule?

Several places limit the income rule for renters with housing vouchers. The usual fix: apply the multiple only to the share of rent the tenant pays. Three examples:

| Place | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Cook County, Illinois | A landlord should count only the tenant's share of the rent. Rent $1,500, tenant share $500: income test is $1,500, not $4,500. | Cook County Commission on Human Rights memo, 2022 |
| Oregon | A landlord may refuse for inability to pay rent only after it takes into account the value of the applicant's housing assistance. | ORS 659A.421(2)(a)(B) |
| Colorado | Landlords must accept lawful sources of income, including vouchers, with limited exceptions. | Colorado Attorney General, 2026 |

**Voucher example, Cook County math:**

- Contract rent: $1,500.
- Housing authority pays $1,000. Tenant pays $500.
- 3x test on the tenant's share: $500 × 3 = $1,500 a month.
- A 3x test on full rent ($4,500) would count rent the tenant never pays.

Check your state and city before you apply the rule. Local laws on source of income change, so check again each year. For voucher rent levels in your metro, see our [Section 8 rent limits](/guides/section-8-rent-limits) and the [Section 8 rent calculator](/tools/section-8-rent-calculator).

## When should I make an exception?

Make exceptions only through a written rule. A fair policy names the other paths up front, so each applicant has the same options:

1. **A qualified guarantor** at a set multiple.
2. **Proof of savings** equal to a set number of months of rent.
3. **A strong rental history**, such as 2 years of on-time rent at a similar amount.

Do not invent a path for one applicant you like. That is how a fair rule becomes unfair in practice.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Do local news mentions help AI recommend a property manager?

Local news mentions can help, but they are a small part of what AI reads for property managers. The strongest public data is about brand mentions in general, not news alone.

Ahrefs studied 75,000 brands and Google AI Overviews (published 2025-05-26). It used Spearman correlation, where a bigger number means a stronger link:

| Factor (Ahrefs, 2025) | Correlation with AI Overview brand visibility |
|---|---|
| Brand web mentions | 0.664 |
| Brand anchors (links that use your name) | 0.527 |
| Brand search volume | 0.392 |
| Backlinks | 0.218 |
| Branded ad traffic | 0.216 |

The top 3 are all off-site. People and pages that name you track AI visibility better than raw links do. Ahrefs measured correlation, so the data cannot prove that mentions cause visibility. Big brands get more mentions and more AI visibility for the same reason: they are big.

Our own data puts news in its place for property managers. On 2026-10-19 we asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude "What is the best property management company in <City, ST>? Give names." for the 50 largest US cities (our data, 150 answers). We sorted every source the engines used:

| Source type (our data) | Answers that used it |
|---|---|
| Directories (Expertise.com, Yelp and others) | 134 of 150 |
| Company websites | 106 of 150 |
| Forums (Reddit and others) | 24 of 150 |
| Local news and city blogs | 16 of 150 |
| Associations (such as NARPM chapters) | 6 of 150 |

News is real but small. For "best manager" questions, fix your directories first. Use news to add proof and a name that other sites repeat.

## Which mentions count?

A mention counts when an independent site names your company in a useful context. A link is a bonus, not the point. Recomaze, a GEO vendor, also advises digital PR because brand mentions correlate with AI trust signals; it gives no study of its own.

| Mention | Value for AI (our view) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Local paper quotes your founder on rents | High | Independent, local, names the expert and the city |
| City business journal "fastest growing" list | Medium to high | Independent list with facts |
| Local TV piece on a tenant or owner issue | Medium | Often has a text version online |
| NARPM or state association page | Medium | Trust and a topic link to property management |
| Podcast or YouTube interview | Medium | YouTube was a source in 7 of 120 answers in our GEO prompt test (2026-10-19) |
| Sponsored article in a local outlet | Low | Paid, often marked as sponsored |
| Press release on a wire service | Low | Your own text, copied many times |

The table is our view, labeled as hypothesis. No engine publishes source weights.

## How do you pitch local news?

Pitch data and a person, not your company. Reporters need a fresh local fact and someone who can explain it today.

1. **Find your data story.** Use your own portfolio: median rent change for 3-bedroom houses, average days on market, repair cost changes. Use real numbers and the date range. Remove any tenant or owner data that could identify a person.
2. **Tie it to news.** A new city rental rule, a state law change, a season (move-out peak, winter repairs).
3. **Find the right reporter.** Search the outlet's site for "rent" or "landlord" and note who writes those stories.
4. **Write a 5-sentence email.** The fact, why it matters to readers, your source, who you are, and when you can talk.
5. **Offer a chart or a table.** Reporters reuse clear visuals with credit.
6. **Answer fast.** A reporter on deadline calls the first expert who picks up.
7. **Put the story on your site.** Publish the full data on a page with the date, and link to the news story.

Watch for: Fair Housing rules apply to every quote. Talk about properties, prices and rules. Never describe which renters live where by any protected class.

## Do paid press releases work for AI citations?

No public data shows that paid press releases improve AI citations. Google's spam policies list, as an example of link spam, "links with optimized anchor text in articles, guest posts, or press releases distributed on other sites." The same policy names advertorials that pass ranking credit for payment.

A wire release has 3 problems for AI search:

- **It is your own text.** An engine that reads it learns only what you said about yourself.
- **It is copied many times.** Many copies of one text add little new information.
- **Few people read it.** No reporter, no real story, no new mentions after it.

A release still has one use. If a reporter writes a story, a short release on your own site gives the facts in one place. Keep links plain and use your brand name, not a keyword.

## How do you track mentions?

Track mentions with alerts each week and with AI checks each month. Google Alerts is free and takes 5 minutes to set up.

1. Go to Google Alerts.
2. Type your company name in quotes. Make one more alert for your founder's name.
3. Click "Show options". Set how often, the source type (for example News), and your region.
4. Click "Create Alert".

Then add an AI check once a month:

| Check | How | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Who cites you | Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude "What is <your company>?" | Note the sources each engine lists |
| Do you show up in the city question | Ask "best property manager in <city>" in each engine | Save the full answer |
| Did a new story change anything | Compare with last month's answers | One answer is a sample, not a rank |
| Links from the story | Search Console, Links report | Not every mention links |

Log every new mention in one sheet: date, outlet, link, quote. After 6 months, compare that log with your AI answers. That is your own data, better than any vendor's claim.

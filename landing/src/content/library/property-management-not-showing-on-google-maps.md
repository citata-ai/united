## Why does the map pack matter for owner leads?

The map pack is the first place most owners look for a manager. Goodjuju Marketing studied 35 PM companies in fall 2026. Google Search and the Map Pack gave 50.6% of 1,262 owner leads. With Direct / Maps added, Google's local results gave 86.2%.

So when your company is missing from the map, you lose the largest lead source. The good news: Google tells you how the ranking works. Its Business Profile help names 3 factors and says nobody can pay for a better local rank.

| Factor | What Google means | What you control |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | How well the profile matches the search | Category, description, services, complete facts |
| Distance | How far the business is from the searcher | Office address, service area (only a little) |
| Prominence | How well known the business is | Reviews, rating, links from other websites |

Source: Google Business Profile Help, "Tips to improve your local ranking on Google".

## Is my Google Business Profile verified and active?

Verification comes first, because Google says a verified profile is more likely to show in results. Open your profile while signed in. If you see a "Get verified" prompt, finish it before you change anything else.

Next, check for a suspension. Google may suspend or disable a profile that breaks its guidelines. A suspended profile drops out of the map. Google's appeal tool lets you send proof such as a business license, a business registration or a utility bill. You can submit an appeal only once, so attach all proof the first time. Do not make a new profile for the same business: Google says not to.

Then check that the facts are complete. Google says businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show in local results. Fill in:

- Your address, or your service area if owners do not visit your office.
- Hours, including holiday hours.
- Your primary category and any extra categories.
- Phone number and website.
- Photos of your office, team and a few managed homes (with owner consent).

## How does distance from the searcher affect property management results?

Distance is a ranking factor you cannot fully fix. Google measures how far each business is from the person who searches. If the searcher does not share a location, Google uses what it knows about them.

So a manager with an office in one suburb often ranks well near that office and poorly 15 miles away. A service area tells owners where you work. It does not move your pin.

Google sets 2 rules here, from its business guidelines:

1. **One profile per office.** A service-area business should have one profile for the central office with a set service area.
2. **No virtual offices.** You cannot list a virtual office unless staff work there during business hours.

Watch for: vendors who sell "a profile in every city" with mailbox addresses. Those profiles break the guidelines and can get the whole account restricted. What to do: rank near your real offices, and use city pages on your website to reach farther.

## Do my categories and reviews match my competitors?

Categories carry the relevance signal. Google says to choose categories that finish the sentence "This business IS a", not "this business HAS a". For most managers, "Property management company" is the primary category. Do not add categories only as keywords: Google lists that as a guideline breach.

The business name works the same way. Google says unnecessary information in the name is not permitted and can lead to suspension. "Smith Realty" is fine. "Smith Realty Best Property Management Chicago" is not.

Reviews and links feed prominence. Google says more reviews, positive ratings and links from other websites can help local rank. Compare your profile with the 3 managers who show in the pack for your main search:

## Are spam listings pushing me down in Google Maps?

Fake or keyword-stuffed listings can take a map spot that should be yours. Look for these signs in the pack for your city:

- A name stuffed with city and service words that differ from the real sign.
- An address that is a mailbox store or a shared virtual office.
- Many profiles with the same phone number or website.
- A "company" with no website, no photos and a sudden burst of 5-star reviews.

For fraud in a business name, phone number or URL, Google offers the Business Redressal Complaint Form. Google says it cannot promise action and will not send status updates. Add proof: a street photo of the address, the real name on the state business register, or the real company's website. For a simple wrong fact, use "Suggest an edit" on the listing instead.

## Step list: an afternoon map pack check

Use this order. Each step takes 10 to 30 minutes.

1. **Verify.** Confirm the profile shows as verified and not suspended.
2. **Fix the name.** Match the name on your signs and site, with no extra words.
3. **Set the category.** Pick "Property management company" or the closest match as primary.
4. **Check the address.** Use a staffed office, or hide the address and set a service area.
5. **Complete the facts.** Add hours, phone, website, services and photos.
6. **Tag the website link.** Add `?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp` so GA4 shows map visits on their own line, as Goodjuju recommends.
7. **Compare with rivals.** Fill in the table above for the top 3.
8. **Report spam.** Send fake listings through the redressal form, with proof.
9. **Recheck in 30 days.** Search from 3 points in your city and note your position.

## What if the map pack still does not show my company?

Your website has to back up the profile. Google reads your site to judge relevance, and links to your site count toward prominence. A thin home page with no city or service facts gives Google little to match.

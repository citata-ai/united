## What are CAM charges?

CAM charges are what tenants pay a landlord to maintain the shared parts of a property. CAM stands for common area maintenance. Tango Analytics defines it as "what tenants pay a landlord to maintain shared spaces", such as lobbies, parking lots and restrooms.

CAM shows up most in commercial leases: retail centers, office buildings and industrial parks. In a net lease, the tenant pays base rent plus a share of CAM, taxes and insurance. In a gross lease, the landlord folds those costs into one rent.

CAM is a pass-through. The landlord collects estimates, pays the bills, and settles the difference once a year. Some leases add an admin fee on top, so read the clause.

## What costs go into CAM?

CAM includes the costs to run, clean, repair and protect the shared areas. The lease defines the exact list. Tango Analytics splits CAM costs into 2 groups:

| Group | What it means | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Controllable | The landlord has real control over the cost | Landscaping, repair and maintenance, admin salaries, garage costs |
| Non-controllable | The landlord has little choice | Water, gas, electricity, trash removal, snow removal, taxes |

The split matters because many leases cap controllable costs. Tango Analytics says "controllable CAM expenses often come with an operating expense cap". Costs above the cap fall to the landlord, not the tenant.

Use this checklist when you read the CAM clause in a lease:

- [ ] Which costs are included, by name
- [ ] Which costs are excluded (for example, costs already paid back by insurance)
- [ ] Whether capital improvements count, and how they spread over years
- [ ] Whether a management or admin fee is added, and at what percent
- [ ] Whether a cap applies, and to which costs
- [ ] How the tenant's share is measured
- [ ] When the reconciliation is due
- [ ] The tenant's right to audit the landlord's books

Tango Analytics also tells tenants to make sure the lease grants "reasonable rights to review the landlord's books".

## How do you calculate a tenant's CAM share?

You calculate a tenant's CAM share by dividing the tenant's square feet by the total square feet in the lease, then multiplying by the CAM cost. This method is called pro rata share.

The formula:

**CAM share = (tenant square feet ÷ total square feet) × annual CAM cost**

A worked example. A retail center has 20,000 square feet. The annual CAM budget is $90,000.

| Tenant | Square feet | Pro rata share | Annual CAM | Monthly estimate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coffee shop | 2,000 | 10% | $9,000 | $750 |
| Dental office | 3,000 | 15% | $13,500 | $1,125 |
| Grocery | 12,000 | 60% | $54,000 | $4,500 |
| Salon | 1,500 | 7.5% | $6,750 | $562.50 |
| Vacant unit | 1,500 | 7.5% | $6,750 | Landlord pays |
| Total | 20,000 | 100% | $90,000 | |

The math for the coffee shop: 2,000 ÷ 20,000 = 10%. Then 10% × $90,000 = $9,000 a year, or $750 a month.

The total square feet is the number to watch. Tango Analytics notes that landlords may measure "leased", "leasable", "occupied" or "existing" space. If the lease divides by leased space only, the vacant unit's share moves to the other tenants. The coffee shop's share would rise to 2,000 ÷ 18,500 = 10.8%.

For measurement, BOMA International publishes separate standards for office (ANSI/BOMA Z65.1-2024), retail (Z65.5-2025, focused on gross leasable area) and multifamily (Z65.4-2023). The lease should name the method.

## What is a CAM reconciliation?

A CAM reconciliation compares what a tenant paid in CAM estimates during the year with the tenant's share of the actual costs. The tenant then pays the gap, or gets a credit. Tango Analytics says reconciliations "may be due within 30 to 90 days after December 31", depending on the lease.

A worked reconciliation for the coffee shop:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Monthly estimates paid ($750 × 12) | $9,000 |
| Actual CAM costs for the center | $96,400 |
| Coffee shop share (10%) | $9,640 |
| Balance due from tenant | $640 |

The math: $96,400 × 10% = $9,640. Then $9,640 − $9,000 = $640 due.

Now add a cap. Say the lease caps controllable CAM increases at 5% a year. Last year's controllable costs were $60,000, so this year's cap is $60,000 × 1.05 = $63,000. If actual controllable costs were $66,000, the landlord absorbs the extra $3,000 before the split. The tenant's share then uses $93,400, not $96,400. The coffee shop owes $9,340, so the balance due drops to $340.

Send each tenant a reconciliation statement with:

1. **The total actual costs**, by category.
2. **The tenant's share percent** and how it was measured.
3. **Any cap applied**, with the math.
4. **The estimates paid** and the balance due or credit.
5. **The next year's new monthly estimate.**

## Are CAM charges used in residential leases?

CAM charges are rare in residential leases. Most apartment and single-family leases are gross: the rent covers the landlord's costs for shared areas, from hallway lights to lawn care.

Residential leases do pass through some costs, under other names:

| Residential charge | What it covers | Similar to CAM? |
|---|---|---|
| HOA dues | Shared costs in a condo or planned community, paid by the unit owner | Yes, but the owner pays the HOA, not the tenant |
| Utility billing | Water, sewer or trash billed apart from rent | Partly |
| Amenity or parking fee | A pool, gym or parking space | No, a flat fee |

If you manage a mixed-use building, you may run CAM for the shops and a gross lease for the apartments in the same building. Keep the 2 cost pools apart in your books.

Local law may limit which costs you may pass to residential tenants and how you must disclose them. Check your state and city rules before you add any charge outside rent.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How should a property manager handle CAM?

A property manager should handle CAM with a clear budget, a monthly estimate, a clean reconciliation and records a tenant can audit. Disputes start when tenants cannot see the math.

1. **Budget in the fall.** Build next year's CAM budget from this year's actual costs.
2. **Bill estimates monthly.** Divide each tenant's annual share by 12.
3. **Code each bill.** Post every CAM bill to a CAM account, tagged controllable or not.
4. **Reconcile on time.** Send statements within the lease deadline.
5. **Keep the proof.** Keep invoices ready for a tenant audit.

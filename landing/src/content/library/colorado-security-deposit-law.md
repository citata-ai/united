## Colorado security deposit rules at a glance

Colorado deposit law sits in part 1 of article 12 of title 38 of the Colorado Revised Statutes (C.R.S.). The biggest recent change is HB25-1249. The Governor signed it on 2025-06-03, and it took effect on 2026-01-01. We read the session law text on leg.colorado.gov on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | 2 monthly rent payments | C.R.S. 38-12-102.5(1) |
| Pet deposit | Up to $300 extra, refundable | C.R.S. 38-12-106(1) |
| Pet rent | Greater of $35 a month or 1.5% of monthly rent | C.R.S. 38-12-106(2) |
| Return deadline | 30 days after lease end or surrender, whichever is later | C.R.S. 38-12-103(1)(a) |
| Longer period by lease | Up to 60 days, if the lease says so | C.R.S. 38-12-103(1)(a) |
| Itemized statement | Required for any deduction, with exact reasons | C.R.S. 38-12-103(1)(a) |
| Documents on request | Photos, reports, receipts, if the tenant asks within 14 days of the statement | C.R.S. 38-12-103(8) |
| Returned refund check | Hold at least 1 year, pay within 15 days of the tenant's request | C.R.S. 38-12-103(9) |
| Wrongful retention | 3 times the amount withheld, plus attorney fees and costs | C.R.S. 38-12-103(3) |
| Tenant demand before suit | At least 7 days' written notice | C.R.S. 38-12-103(3) |
| Gas hazard move-out | Return within 72 hours | C.R.S. 38-12-104(4) |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Colorado?

A Colorado landlord may not require a deposit above 2 monthly rent payments under the rental agreement. SB23-184 added this cap as C.R.S. 38-12-102.5, from 2023-08-07.

SB26-054 adds one narrow exception, from 2027-01-01. The cap does not apply when the landlord bought the home from the tenant and both signed a post-closing occupancy agreement. That is a seller who stays on after the sale, not a normal rental.

Pets have their own limits under C.R.S. 38-12-106:

- **Pet deposit:** up to $300 on top of the regular deposit, and it must be refundable.
- **Pet rent:** no more than $35 a month or 1.5% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater.

Worked example for a $2,000 unit:

| Item | Maximum |
|---|---|
| Security deposit (2 × $2,000) | $4,000 |
| Pet deposit | $300 |
| Pet rent (1.5% × $2,000 = $30, so the $35 floor applies) | $35 a month |

## How many days does a Colorado landlord have to return a security deposit?

A Colorado landlord must return the deposit within 30 days after the lease ends or the tenant surrenders the unit, whichever happens last. The lease may set a longer period, but never more than 60 days. Before 2026-01-01 the rule said 1 month. HB25-1249 changed it to 30 days.

The landlord complies by sending the statement, the refund and the documents to the tenant's last-known address. The landlord may also send them to any email address of the tenant that the landlord has actual notice of. With the tenant's consent, the refund may go by secured electronic transfer (C.R.S. 38-12-103(10)).

If the mailed refund comes back, the landlord must hold it for at least 1 year. The landlord must pay it within 15 calendar days after the tenant asks (C.R.S. 38-12-103(9)).

One special case: if a gas hazard is not repaired within 72 hours and the tenant moves out, the landlord has 72 hours after the move-out to return the deposit. A late return in that case costs twice the deposit plus attorney fees (C.R.S. 38-12-104).

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Colorado?

A Colorado landlord may keep reasonable amounts only for the reasons in C.R.S. 38-12-103(1)(b), as amended by HB25-1249:

1. **Unpaid rent.**
2. **Unpaid utility charges.**
3. **Other lawful charges** that the lease lists.
4. **Necessary repairs** for damage beyond normal wear and tear that did not exist before the tenancy.

The landlord may not keep money for normal wear and tear. Since 2026-01-01, the landlord also may not keep money for damage or a defect that existed before the tenancy. A lease term that charges the tenant for wear and tear or for earlier damage is void (C.R.S. 38-12-103(7)(b)).

HB25-1249 also widened "normal wear and tear" in C.R.S. 38-12-102(4). It now covers deterioration, damage or uncleanliness from normal use. It does not cover a unit that is "substantially less clean" than at move-in.

Carpet and paint have special rules in C.R.S. 38-12-103(11):

- **Full carpet replacement:** only for substantial and irreparable damage beyond wear and tear. A partial replacement for a damaged area is still allowed.
- **Old carpet:** a landlord may not call carpet substantially and irreparably damaged if it was not replaced with new carpet in the 10 years before move-out.
- **Full repaint:** only for substantial damage to the paint throughout the whole unit. Spot repainting of damaged areas is still allowed.

Either side may ask for a walk-through inspection before move-out, in person or by video, if it is reasonable and practicable. At the tenant's request, the landlord must offer one after the tenant can remove furniture (C.R.S. 38-12-103(1.5)). This rule and the carpet rule do not apply to mobile homes in a mobile home park.

## Does a Colorado landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. A Colorado landlord who keeps any part of the deposit must give a written statement with the exact reasons. The refund of the rest goes with the statement (C.R.S. 38-12-103(1)(a)).

If the tenant asks in writing within 14 days after getting the statement, the landlord must also give the relevant documents in the landlord's possession or control. The statute names photos, inspection forms or reports, receipts, invoices and estimates (C.R.S. 38-12-103(8)).

A move-out checklist for your team:

- [ ] Offer a walk-through if the tenant asks for one.
- [ ] Take dated photos at move-in and move-out.
- [ ] Mark each item as wear and tear, earlier damage or new damage.
- [ ] Check the carpet install date before you charge for carpet.
- [ ] Send the statement, refund and documents by day 30 (or the lease date, max 60).
- [ ] Log the tenant's email and forwarding address.
- [ ] Keep any returned refund check for 1 year.

## Must a Colorado landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

No Colorado statute in part 1 of article 12 of title 38 requires interest on a deposit or a separate account. We checked the list of sections in that part (38-12-101 to 38-12-106). None of them sets an interest or escrow rule. Some Colorado cities add their own rental rules, so check the city code where the unit sits.

When the landlord's interest ends, for example by a sale, the person who holds the deposit has 60 days to deal with it under C.R.S. 38-12-103(4). HB25-1249 changed this from "a reasonable time" to 60 days.

## What is the penalty for a Colorado landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

A Colorado landlord who breaks C.R.S. 38-12-103 forfeits the right to keep any part of the deposit. Since 2026-01-01, the law treats these acts as wrongful withholding (C.R.S. 38-12-103(2.5)):

- No statement, or no required documents, on time.
- A statement without the exact reasons.
- No refund of the balance on time.
- A retention in bad faith.

Bad faith includes an amount that unreasonably exceeds actual damages. The law presumes this when the landlord keeps 125% or more of the actual damages (C.R.S. 38-12-103(3.5)(b)). In court, the landlord must prove the actual damages.

A wrongful retention makes the landlord liable for 3 times the amount wrongfully withheld, plus reasonable attorney fees and court costs. The tenant must first give at least 7 days' written notice of the demand and the plan to sue. The tenant may sue for treble damages only if the landlord does not pay within those 7 days (C.R.S. 38-12-103(3)).

Worked example: a landlord keeps $1,500 for paint, but actual damage is $1,000. That is 150% of actual damages, so bad faith is presumed. If the court finds the full $1,500 was wrongful, the landlord owes 3 × $1,500 = $4,500, plus fees and costs. If the landlord acted in good faith and followed every rule, the landlord owes only the $500 excess and court costs (C.R.S. 38-12-103(3.5)(d)).

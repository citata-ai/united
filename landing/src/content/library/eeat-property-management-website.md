## What is E-E-A-T for a property management website?

E-E-A-T is Google's name for experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trust. Google's guide to helpful content says "trust is most important" of the four. The same page says E-E-A-T itself "isn't a specific ranking factor." Google's systems use a mix of signals to find content that has it.

So E-E-A-T is not a box you tick for Google. It is the proof a rental owner looks for before they hand you the keys to a rental they paid for. The same proof helps search engines and AI answers trust your pages.

Property management is close to a "Your Money or Your Life" (YMYL) topic. Google says its systems give more weight to strong E-E-A-T on topics that can affect "the financial stability" of people. An owner's rent, deposit and eviction risk fit that test.

Here is how each letter maps to a page on your site.

| Letter | What it means | Where a PM site shows it |
|---|---|---|
| Experience | You did the work yourself | Case notes, portfolio facts, photos from your own jobs |
| Expertise | You know the subject | Named licensed author, credentials, correct local law |
| Authoritativeness | Others know you | NARPM listing, local press, reviews on other sites |
| Trust | You are honest and easy to check | Fees, agreement, license number, real address, clear contact |

## Who should be the author of property management blog posts?

The author of a property management post should be the licensed person who knows the topic and checked the facts. That is often the broker, the owner of the company or a senior manager. Google says it "strongly" encourages accurate bylines where readers expect them. It also asks whether a byline leads to more information about the author.

A good author setup has four parts:

1. **A byline on each post.** A real name and a role, for example "Broker and owner."
2. **An author page.** Photo, license type and number, years in the business, doors managed, and areas served.
3. **A link from the byline to the author page.** Google's guide asks for this.
4. **A "checked by" line.** If a marketer or an agency drafts the post, name the manager who checked it.

Do not invent a person. Google's guide calls fabricated creator profiles deceptive. A post "by Admin" or "by the Team" gives the owner nothing to check.

AI drafts are a special case. Google asks whether the use of automation, including AI, is clear to visitors where they would reasonably wonder. If an AI tool wrote most of a post, say so. Then let a licensed manager check each number and each legal rule before you publish.

## Which credentials help: license, NARPM, RMP?

The credentials that help most are the ones an owner can verify in one click. A license number with a link to the state lookup page is the strongest. Rules about who needs a license to manage rentals differ by state, so check your state's real estate board.

| Credential | Why it helps | How to show it |
|---|---|---|
| State real estate or PM license | Owners can check it on the state site | License number on the team page, linked to the state lookup |
| NARPM membership | Industry body with a public member list | Logo plus a link to your listing |
| NARPM RMP | Needs real volume to apply | Name the designation on the author page |
| NARPM MPM or CRMC | Other NARPM designation and company certification | Same, with the year earned |
| Local landlord or investor groups | Local authority | Name the group and your role |

NARPM says an RMP candidate must already manage "100 units over a two-year period." NARPM also lists members by designation on its website. So a designation gives you a public, third-party page that confirms your claim.

A logo alone is weak proof. Always pair a badge with a link to a page you do not control.

## How do I show real experience on pages?

You show real experience with facts that only you could know. Google's guide asks whether content shows "first-hand expertise and a depth of knowledge." For a property manager, that means details from your own portfolio.

Swap generic lines for checkable ones:

| Generic line | Experience line |
|---|---|
| "We are experts in Section 8." | "We manage 42 voucher units with 3 housing authorities." |
| "We find great tenants fast." | "Our median days on market in 2025 was 18, from our PM software." |
| "We know local law." | "Our broker wrote this after the 2025 change to our state's deposit rule." |
| "Trusted by many owners." | "112 owners, 38 of them out of state." |

The numbers in the right column are examples of the format, not real data. Use only your own numbers, with the date and the system they came from.

Other ways to show experience:

- **Photos you took.** Your team at a move-in inspection, not stock images.
- **Short case notes.** One owner problem, what you did, what changed. Get the owner's written consent first.
- **Process pages.** Show your real lease-up steps, inspection checklist or owner statement sample.
- **Dates.** Put "updated" dates on posts and fee pages. Old facts hurt trust.

## Which trust pages does a property management website need?

A property management website needs pages that answer the questions owners fear to ask. Trust is the letter Google ranks highest. Here is a checklist of trust pages.

- [ ] **About page** with the founder's name, photo and story.
- [ ] **Team page** with names, roles and license numbers.
- [ ] **Fee page** with your real fees, including leasing, renewal and markup.
- [ ] **Sample management agreement** as a PDF, with the cancellation terms.
- [ ] **Contact page** with a real address, phone number and office hours.
- [ ] **Reviews page** that links to the source, such as Google or Yelp.
- [ ] **Privacy policy** that names the tools you use for forms and tracking.
- [ ] **Author pages** for each person who signs posts.

Two rules keep the reviews page honest. Google's contribution policy says a business must not offer payment, discounts or free services for reviews. It also says a business must not "selectively solicit positive reviews." Ask every owner the same way.

Your address must also be real. Google's Business Profile guidelines say a virtual office you do not work from is not eligible. Show the same name, address and phone on your site and your profile.

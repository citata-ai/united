## What do ACH payments cost?

ACH is a bank payment network. A bank transfer through a rental platform can have a different charge from a debit card payment, even when both draw from a checking account.

| Platform and plan | Published incoming bank fee | Card fee shown |
|---|---|---|
| Buildium Essential | $2.35 per transaction | Credit: 2.99% |
| Buildium Growth | $1.35 per transaction | Credit: 2.99% |
| TenantCloud Starter | $1.95, tenant paid | Confirm current card schedule |
| TenantCloud Pro | $1.50, tenant paid | Confirm current card schedule |

Sources: Buildium and TenantCloud pricing pages, read October 19, 2026. These are transaction fees, not subscription prices. Check who pays in your actual setup.

A low bank fee is useful only when the method works for residents. Test account setup, authorization and receipts. Ask how a return changes the ledger and who receives the notice. Do not assume a payment shown as submitted is fully settled.

## What do debit and credit card payments cost?

Use the specific schedule for each card type. A debit card is not an ACH payment, and it may not share the credit-card rate.

Buildium publishes a credit-card rate of 2.99% per transaction. The comparison above does not establish a separate debit rate. Ask the vendor for that rate in writing instead of filling the gap from an old review.

Here is our worked calculation for a hypothetical $2,000 rent payment using Buildium's displayed credit rate:

- Rent amount: $2,000.
- Rate: 2.99%.
- Calculated card fee: $2,000 × 0.0299 = $59.80.
- Calculated total if the payer adds the fee: $2,059.80.

This example assumes a simple percentage charge with no other costs. Confirm the checkout calculation before using it in resident instructions. Ask whether refunds, disputes or other services create separate fees.

## Can you pass payment fees to tenants?

Do not treat a software switch as permission to impose a charge. Review the lease, applicable law and the processor's terms before changing who pays.

Ask whether the charge is optional, tied to a method or unavoidable. Check the payment route available to residents who do not use a bank or card. Describe the total clearly before payment.

Separate a processing cost from a management markup. If you add a charge beyond the vendor fee, identify it plainly in your internal review. Do not label the whole amount as a bank cost without evidence.

Have a local attorney review the proposed lease language and payment choices. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney. Keep the approved rule with the payment settings so staff can verify that the software matches the policy.

## Do state laws limit convenience fees?

State rules need a location-specific check. No single fee rule in this guide applies to every property.

Minnesota section 504B.120 requires landlords to disclose nonoptional fees in the lease. It also requires the combined rent and nonoptional fees on the first lease page and disclosure in advertisements. That is a disclosure example, not a nationwide permission or cap.

Do not infer legality from a vendor page that offers tenant-paid fees. A platform serves different jurisdictions and contracts. Its settings cannot resolve every property's rule.

Before rollout, record the state, city, lease type, fee type and payment alternatives. Ask counsel which rules apply and what disclosures are required. Check subsidized or other restricted programs separately if relevant. Avoid adding a legal conclusion when the governing text has not been verified. Keep the approved decision accessible to the staff who answer resident questions.

## How do fees compare across platforms?

Compare the same transaction on the same terms. A free landlord subscription can still involve resident-paid charges or paid options.

Use this comparison checklist:

- Identify the plan and billing term.
- Enter the same rent amount.
- Separate ACH, debit and credit.
- Name the payer for each charge.
- Check account setup and subscriptions.
- Ask about returns and failed payments.
- Check refund and dispute costs.
- Confirm settlement and payout status.
- Save the dated fee schedule.

Use actual payment activity for your budget. Keep any future payment mix labelled as an assumption. Do not expect every resident to select the lowest-cost route. Review the final checkout screen as well as the price page. It should show the method, charge and total before authorization.

## How should you choose a payment policy?

Choose a clear policy that your approved lease, local rules and software can support. Make the resident's choices easy to understand.

Test a successful payment, return and refund with sample records. Confirm the ledger, receipt and owner report. Ask staff to explain each stage without calling a submitted payment settled.

Keep changes controlled. Save old settings, approved terms and the date new instructions apply. Tell residents what action they need to take without conflicting payment links.

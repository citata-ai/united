A tenant misses rent on a Thursday. The landlord serves a 3-day notice that afternoon. In California, the weekend does not count, so the deadline falls on Tuesday, not Sunday. Small rules like this decide whether a California eviction case stands or fails. This page lists each step with the current statute text, read on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov and the California Courts Self-Help Guide on 2026-10-19.

## Summary Table: California Eviction Steps

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice for unpaid rent | 3 days, excluding weekends and judicial holidays | CCP 1161(2) |
| Notice for a curable lease breach | 3 days to cure or quit, same day count | CCP 1161(3) |
| Notice for waste, nuisance, illegal use | 3-day notice to quit, no cure | CCP 1161(4) |
| Time to serve a rent notice | Within 1 year after rent is due | CCP 1161(2) |
| Fee to serve a notice | Landlord may not charge the tenant | CCP 1161(6) |
| Just cause | Required after 12 months for covered units | Civ. Code 1946.2(a) |
| Tenant response | 10 court days after service | CCP 1167(a) |
| Trial date | No later than 20 days after the request to set trial | CCP 1170.5(a) |
| Sheriff lockout | 5 days after the writ is served | CCP 715.020(c) |
| Self-help eviction | Illegal; up to $100 a day, $250 minimum | Civ. Code 789.3 |

## What Notice Must a California Landlord Give Before Filing an Eviction for Unpaid Rent?

A California landlord must serve a written 3-day notice to pay rent or quit. Under Code of Civil Procedure 1161(2), the 3 days exclude Saturdays, Sundays and other judicial holidays. The notice must include:

- [ ] The amount of rent due.
- [ ] The name, phone number and address of the person who takes payment.
- [ ] If payment can be made in person, the usual days and hours that person is available.
- [ ] Or a bank account number and the bank's name and street address, if the bank is within 5 miles of the property.
- [ ] Or the electronic payment method, if one was set up before.

The landlord may serve the notice at any time within 1 year after the rent is due. The landlord may not charge the tenant a fee for serving the notice (1161(6)). The current version of Section 1161 has been operative since February 1, 2025 (SB 611).

## How Long Does an Eviction Take in California?

A California eviction usually takes 30 to 45 days or more, from service of the court papers until the tenant must move out (California Courts Self-Help Guide). The statute deadlines add up as follows for an uncontested rent case:

| Step | Time | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 3-day notice runs | 3 court days | CCP 1161(2) |
| File and serve summons and complaint | Depends on service | Self-Help Guide |
| Tenant response period | 10 court days | CCP 1167(a) |
| Default judgment, or trial if tenant answers | Trial within 20 days of the request | CCP 1170.5(a) |
| Writ issued, sheriff serves notice to vacate | 5 days to move | CCP 715.020(c) |

If the tenant answers and asks for a jury, or files motions, the case takes longer. The court may extend the trial date only if both sides agree or after a hearing (1170.5(b)).

## Where Does a Landlord File an Eviction Case in California, and What Does It Cost?

A landlord files an unlawful detainer case in the superior court of the county where the rental is located (California Courts Self-Help Guide). The landlord files a Summons and Complaint, then has each tenant served. Some courts allow filing by mail or online.

Filing fees, as listed by the Self-Help Guide:

| Amount claimed | Usual filing fee |
|---|---|
| Up to $10,000 | $240 |
| Over $10,000 up to $35,000 | $385 |
| Over $35,000 | $435 |

San Bernardino, San Francisco and Riverside counties may charge slightly more. A landlord who cannot pay may ask for a fee waiver. Service costs and attorney fees come on top.

After service, the tenant has 10 days, excluding weekends and judicial holidays, to file a response (CCP 1167(a)). This 10-day period is in the text as amended by AB 2347, effective January 1, 2025. If the tenant does not respond, the landlord may ask for a default judgment.

## Can a California Landlord Evict a Tenant for a Lease Violation or With No Cause?

A California landlord may evict for a lease violation, but a no-cause eviction is limited. For a curable breach, CCP 1161(3) requires a 3-day notice to perform or quit, with the same court-day count. For waste, nuisance, unlawful use or illegal subletting, CCP 1161(4) allows a 3-day notice to quit with no chance to cure.

Civil Code 1946.2 adds just cause rules. After a tenant has lived in a covered unit for 12 months, the owner may end the tenancy only for a just cause stated in the notice:

- **At-fault causes** include unpaid rent, a material lease breach, nuisance, waste, criminal activity and refusal to allow lawful entry.
- **No-fault causes** include owner or family move-in, withdrawal from the rental market, a government order to vacate, and demolition or substantial remodel.

For a no-fault cause, the owner must pay relocation help equal to 1 month's rent within 15 calendar days of the notice, or waive the last month's rent in writing (1946.2(d)). A notice that does not strictly follow this rule is void. For a curable breach, the owner first gives a notice to cure (1946.2(c)).

Some units are exempt, for example housing built within the last 15 years and some single-family homes owned by individuals that gave the tenant the exact exemption notice (1946.2(e)). Cities may have stricter just cause rules. The current text of 1946.2 was amended by AB 1529, effective January 1, 2026, and it is repealed on January 1, 2030, unless extended.

## Who Removes the Tenant After a Judgment in California?

The sheriff, as the levying officer, removes the tenant. After a judgment for possession, the landlord gets a Writ of Execution (form EJ-130) from the clerk and takes it to the sheriff (California Courts Self-Help Guide). The sheriff serves the writ on an occupant, or posts it and mails a copy. If the occupants do not leave within 5 days of service, the sheriff removes them and puts the landlord in possession (CCP 715.020(c)).

## Is a Self-Help Eviction (Lockout, Utility Shutoff) Legal in California?

No. Civil Code 789.3 bars a landlord, with intent to end a tenancy, from these acts:

1. **Cut utilities.** This includes water, heat, light, electricity, gas, phone, elevator or refrigeration.
2. **Change the locks.** A bootlock or any similar device is also barred.
3. **Remove doors or windows.**
4. **Remove the tenant's property.** The exception is the abandoned property process in Civil Code 1980 and after.

A landlord who breaks this rule owes actual damages plus up to $100 for each day of the violation, and no less than $250 for each cause of action. The court awards reasonable attorney fees to the winning side.

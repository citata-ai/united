## Who needs a Baltimore City rental license?

Every rental in Baltimore City needs two things: a yearly property registration and a rental license. The Baltimore City Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD) says all non-owner-occupied properties must register each year. That includes 1-2 family homes, multifamily buildings, vacant buildings and vacant lots. If the property is a rental, it also needs a license.

The registration rule is wide. DHCD's FAQ says you must register a non-owner-occupied property even if it is empty, not rented, or earns no income. A two-unit house where you live in one unit still registers as two units. DHCD does not bill you, or ask for an inspection or lead data, for the unit you live in.

Key dates from DHCD:

- **Yearly registration.** Due by January 1. The registration year runs January to December.
- **After a sale.** Register within 10 days of the transfer.
- **New license after a sale.** Within 60 days of the transfer. Licenses no longer transfer to a buyer (Strengthening Renters' Safety Act, in effect January 1, 2026).

City Code Article 13, Section 5-4 is the legal base. No person may rent, offer to rent, or collect rent for a rental dwelling without a current license. The only exception in that section is housing owned and run by the Housing Authority of Baltimore City.

## What does the Baltimore rental license cost and require?

The Baltimore rental license has 5 conditions on the DHCD page. You must meet all of them.

1. **Register online** with the owner's current contact data.
2. **Pass an inspection** by a Maryland licensed home inspector who is registered with Baltimore City, or a passing Section 8 inspection, for each unit. Multifamily buildings follow a DHCD chart for how many units to inspect.
3. **Enter lead certificate data** for each unit, with the MDE tracking and certificate numbers.
4. **Pay all registration fees** due.
5. **Have no open code violations.**

Fees come from City Code Article 13, Section 4-8:

| Property type | Yearly registration fee |
|---|---|
| 1 or 2 dwelling units | $30 per dwelling unit |
| Multifamily dwelling | $35 per dwelling unit, plus $25 per rooming unit |
| Rooming house | $25 per rooming unit |
| Vacant residential structure | $100 per structure, on top of the unit fee |

The city does not set the inspection price. Inspectors set their own fees, so get 2 or 3 quotes from the city's list of registered home inspectors.

Worked example: you own a duplex and a 10-unit building. The duplex costs 2 × $30 = $60 a year. The 10-unit building costs 10 × $35 = $350 a year. Over one two-year license term, registration alone is ($60 + $350) × 2 = $820, before inspection fees.

## What does the Baltimore rental inspection check?

The Baltimore City Rental License Inspection Form (DHCD, April 2026) has 17 pass-or-fail items, plus 4 items an inspector can refer to DHCD. One form covers one unit.

| Area | What the inspector checks |
|---|---|
| Stairs | A railing on interior and exterior steps with more than 3 risers |
| Utilities | Gas and electric service metered and active |
| Electrical | No live wires visible; cover plates on outlets and switches; working light fixtures |
| Alarms | Smoke detectors and carbon monoxide alarms installed and working |
| Water | Hot and cold running water, hot water at least 110°F; no leaks; toilets flush |
| Windows and doors | Windows open, close and lock; unit entry doors close and lock |
| Envelope | No openings that let rain or snow in; gutters and downspouts carry water away |
| Heat | An operable heat supply system |
| Refer items | Clean and sanitary interior, no rodent burrows, signs of pests, egress from a basement bedroom |

The inspector must certify no financial interest in the unit or the owner. Upload all pages within 90 days of the inspection, or you need a new one. A Section 8 inspection report must be no older than one year. For a renewal, DHCD asks for the inspection within 90 days before the old license expires.

## What is a Maryland lead risk reduction certificate?

A Maryland lead certificate is the inspection certificate number the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) issues for a rental unit. The Maryland District Court brochure for Form DC-CV-082 explains when you need it.

The rule covers "affected property." The court brochure defines it as property built before 1978 with at least one rental unit. It also covers any rental whose owner chooses to join the inspection and registration rules. Property certified lead-free is not affected property.

For affected property, the court brochure says you must give the MDE inspection certificate number from the inspection done before the tenant moved in. Baltimore DHCD asks for the same MDE tracking and certificate numbers before it issues a license. DHCD also checks the lead data at every license renewal.

Watch for: MDE's own pages blocked our reader on 2026-10-19, so we do not list MDE fees or renewal cycles here. Check them on mde.maryland.gov before you plan the budget.

## Can a Baltimore owner file in rent court without a license?

A Baltimore owner who files for failure to pay rent must answer license and lead questions on the court form. The Maryland District Court form DC-CV-082 asks if the property must be licensed to operate as a rental. If yes, you give the license number and the expiration date. If you have no license, you must check a reason, such as an exemption.

Paragraph 3 of the same form asks about lead. You certify the property is affected property with a current MDE registration and a valid certificate number for the current tenancy, or that it is not affected. If you cannot give the number, you must say why.

The city adds its own penalties. City Code Article 13, Section 5-26 makes a license violation a misdemeanor, with a fine of up to $1,000 for each offense. Each day counts as a new offense. A registration violation under Section 4-17 carries up to $500 for each offense.

## What changed for Baltimore owners in 2026?

The Strengthening Renters' Safety Act took effect on January 1, 2026. DHCD lists 3 changes that matter to every owner:

- **Flat two-year license term.** The old tiered terms are gone.
- **No license transfers.** A buyer needs a new license within 60 days.
- **Post the license number.** Priority properties must post it in common areas, leases and ads. A missing posting is a $250 citation.

The Act also targets buildings with 20 or more units and a record of violations. DHCD calls them "priority dwellings" and inspects the worst ones at least twice a year.

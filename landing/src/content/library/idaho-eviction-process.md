Idaho runs evictions under chapter 3 of Title 6, "Forcible Entry and Unlawful Detainer". I read each section below on the Idaho Legislature site on 2026-10-20. The process is one of the faster ones in the US on paper, because the court must set a rent trial within 12 days.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice for unpaid rent | 3 days, in writing, with the amount due | § 6-303(2) |
| When the rent notice can be served | Any time within 1 year after the rent is due | § 6-303(2) |
| Notice for a lease breach | 3 days to perform or give up possession | § 6-303(3) |
| End a tenancy at will (month to month) | At least 1 month, in writing | § 55-208(1) |
| Residential nonrenewal notice | At least 30 days, in writing | § 55-304(2) |
| Where to file | District court of the county | § 6-305 |
| Trial date in a rent case (5 acres or less) | Within 12 days of filing | § 6-310(2) |
| Service before trial | At least 5 days | § 6-310(2) |
| Continuance without a bond | 2 days at most | § 6-311 |
| Time to remove belongings, residential | 72 hours after judgment | § 6-316(2) |
| Sheriff restores possession | After the 72 hours and 3 days after the finding | § 6-316(2) |
| Civil filing fee | $175 district court; $120 magistrate division | § 31-3201A(1) |

## What notice must an Idaho landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

An Idaho landlord must serve a written 3-day notice that requires payment of the rent or possession of the property. Idaho Code § 6-303(2) sets 3 content rules:

1. **State the amount due.**
2. **Demand payment or possession.**
3. **Warn about belongings.** Tell the tenant that after a judgment, a residential tenant has 72 hours to remove belongings before the landlord may remove and dispose of them under § 6-316.

The notice can be served any time within 1 year after the rent becomes due. If a subtenant lives in the unit, serve the subtenant too.

Service methods come from Idaho Code § 6-304:

- Hand a copy to the tenant.
- If the tenant is away from home and work, leave a copy with a person of suitable age at either place, and mail a copy to the residence.
- If neither works, post a copy in a conspicuous place on the property, give a copy to a person living there if possible, and mail a copy to the property.

Watch for: attorney fees. Under Idaho Code § 6-324, fees go to the prevailing party. But in a case that needs the 3-day rent notice, the notice must tell the tenant that attorney fees will be awarded to the prevailing party. Put that line in your form.

## How long does an eviction take in Idaho?

An Idaho rent eviction can finish in about 3 to 4 weeks, based on the statutory periods. That is my estimate from the sections below, not a court figure.

| Step | Period | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 3-day notice runs | 3 days | § 6-303(2) |
| File the complaint | Day 4 at the earliest | § 6-310(1) |
| Trial | Within 12 days of filing | § 6-310(2) |
| Service of summons and trial notice | At least 5 days before trial | § 6-310(2) |
| Continuance, if any | 2 days, unless the tenant posts a bond for rent | § 6-311 |
| Tenant removes belongings | 72 hours after judgment | § 6-316(2) |
| Sheriff restores possession | After the 72 hours and 3 days after the finding | § 6-316(2) |

The 12-day trial rule applies to actions only for possession of land of 5 acres or less, for nonpayment of rent or for drug activity on the premises (§ 6-310(1)). Other cases, such as a lease breach, follow the normal civil timeline. A rent case on 5 acres or less is tried by the judge, without a jury (§ 6-311A). If the tenant does not appear, the court enters a default judgment (§ 6-312).

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Idaho, and what does it cost?

An Idaho landlord files in the district court of the county where the property, or part of it, is located (Idaho Code § 6-305). The complaint for a rent case needs 5 items (§ 6-310(1)):

- A description of the premises.
- That the defendant is in possession.
- That the defendant holds the premises and is in default of rent.
- That all required notices were served correctly.
- That the plaintiff is entitled to possession.

Filing fee: Idaho Code § 31-3201A(1) sets $175 for a civil case in district court. A case assigned to the magistrate division costs $120. That section adds that fees in chapter 20 of title 1 and § 31-3201 apply on top. Ask the clerk which division takes your case and the total due. The sheriff may also charge to serve papers and deliver the writ.

In the judgment, the court also assesses damages and any rent due (§ 6-316(1)). The prevailing party gets attorney fees under § 6-324, if the rent notice gave the required warning.

## Can an Idaho landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

An Idaho landlord can evict for a lease violation after a 3-day notice to perform. Idaho Code § 6-303 lists these grounds:

| Ground | Notice | Cure right |
|---|---|---|
| Holding over after the lease ends | None for a fixed term; a tenancy at will needs a notice under the civil code | No |
| Unpaid rent | 3 days to pay or leave | Pay within 3 days |
| Breach of another lease term | 3 days to perform or leave | Perform within 3 days |
| Breach that cannot be cured | No cure notice needed | No |
| Assigning, subletting or waste against the lease | 3 days' notice to quit | No |
| Drug delivery, production or use on the premises | Per § 6-303(5) | No |

Idaho has no just-cause rule. A landlord can end a tenancy at will with written notice of at least 1 month (Idaho Code § 55-208(1)). For residential leases, § 55-304(2) also requires at least 30 days' written notice of nonrenewal. After the notice period ends, the landlord may proceed by law to recover possession (§ 55-209).

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Idaho?

The sheriff removes the tenant in Idaho. The landlord or an agent may deliver the writ of restitution, or ask the sheriff to deliver it (§ 6-316(2)). Idaho Code § 6-311C gives the form of the writ.

The timing rules in § 6-316(2):

- A residential tenant has 72 hours to remove belongings.
- A commercial tenant, or a tenant on 5 acres or more, has 7 days, or longer if the court allows.
- After that time, and 3 days after the court's finding, the sheriff restores possession by "immediate removal of the tenant".

After that, the landlord may remove and dispose of any property left on the premises, subject to security interests under chapter 9, title 28. On good cause, the court can award the landlord reasonable removal and restoration costs.

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Idaho?

Self-help eviction is a high-risk choice in Idaho. Idaho Code § 6-301 defines forcible entry as entry by breaking doors or windows, by violence, or by turning out the person in possession "by force, threats or menacing conduct". Under § 6-317, a person who recovers damages for a forcible or unlawful entry or detention can get a judgment of up to 3 times the actual damages.

The Idaho statutes I read do not contain a single section that names lockouts or utility shutoffs. I found no text that allows them either. The safe path is the court process above. Ask a local attorney before you take any step to remove a tenant without a writ.

## Idaho eviction checklist

1. **Use a notice form** with the amount due, the 72-hour belongings warning and the attorney-fee line.
2. **Serve it** by a method in § 6-304 and keep proof.
3. **Count 3 days**, then file in district court.
4. **Watch the 12-day trial date** and confirm service at least 5 days before.
5. **Get the writ** and coordinate with the sheriff.

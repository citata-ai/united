## How much do property managers charge for a leasing fee?

Property managers most often charge 50% to 100% of the first month's rent for a leasing fee. The exact range depends on the source and the market. Here is what three fee guides publish.

| Source | Leasing (placement) fee | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Rentec Direct | 75% to 100% of first month's rent | Also called a placement fee |
| Home365, 2026 guide | 50% to 100% of first month's rent | Some managers charge a flat $300 to $600 |
| Home365, Chicago | 50% to 100%, often about 75% | Some firms charge a full month |
| Home365, Phoenix | About 50% for full management | Near 100% for lease-only service |
| Utopia Management, Seattle 2026 | 50% to 100% of first month's rent | Published by a Seattle manager |

Utopia Management's Seattle page also lists the published fees of local firms. One charges 60% of one month's rent with a $1,850 minimum. Another charges 3/4 of the first month's rent. Others charge no leasing fee on fully managed homes, or none on self-showing plans. So a "free leasing" competitor is real in some markets. Check what your local competitors publish before you set your number.

## Is a leasing fee a percent of first month's rent or a flat fee?

A leasing fee can be either. A percent of the first month's rent is the most common form in the guides above. A flat fee is the other form.

| Model | Example on $2,000 rent | Good for | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50% of first month | $1,000 | Competitive markets | May not cover a slow lease-up |
| 75% of first month | $1,500 | Middle of the range | Owner compares to cheaper offers |
| 100% of first month | $2,000 | Lease-only service, high-touch leasing | Harder to sell to full-management owners |
| Flat $500 | $500 | Low-rent units, simple pitch | Loses money on high-rent homes |
| Percent with a minimum | 60%, min $1,850 → $1,850 | Mixed rent levels | Harder to explain |

The percent model ties your fee to the rent you get. A higher rent pays more, so you have a reason to push rent up. A flat fee is easy to explain but loses money on large homes that need more showings. A percent with a minimum, as one Seattle firm uses, protects you on low-rent units.

## What costs does the leasing fee cover?

The leasing fee covers the work to find, screen and sign a new tenant. Rentec Direct lists the tasks: advertising, showings, application review, tenant screening, lease paperwork, and getting the home ready for move-in.

Price the fee from your cost per lease. Use this worked example and replace each value with your own.

| Cost line | Hours or cost | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Listing setup: photos, ad copy, syndication | 2 h | 2 × $30 = $60 |
| Inquiries and showings | 8 h | 8 × $30 = $240 |
| Application review and screening | 2 h | 2 × $30 = $60 |
| Lease prep, signing, move-in inspection | 3 h | 3 × $30 = $90 |
| Paid listing and lockbox costs | flat | $75 |
| Overhead share (software, office) | flat | $100 |
| Total cost per lease | 15 h | $625 |

At a $2,000 rent, a 50% fee pays $1,000 and leaves $375 over cost. At 75%, it leaves $875. A flat $500 fee loses $125 on this lease. The hours and the $30 rate are examples, not survey data. Measure your own hours on 10 leases before you change your fee.

## Should a leasing fee change with days on market?

A leasing fee usually does not change with days on market, but your cost does. Each extra week adds showings and ad time. Your fee stays the same, so a slow lease-up cuts your margin.

You have three options:

1. **Keep one fee and price for the average.** Use your mean days on market from the last 12 months.
2. **Add a guarantee.** Promise a lower fee if the home is not leased in a set number of days. This sells well, but only if you control the asking rent.
3. **Tie the guarantee to the rent.** If the owner refuses your rent advice and the home sits, the guarantee ends.

Days on market also matter for owner trust. Rent Manager notes that owners are more likely to leave during a vacancy. A long vacancy plus a large leasing fee is a common reason for an owner to switch managers.

## Do I charge a leasing fee to existing owners on turnover?

Most managers charge the leasing fee on every new lease, including turnovers for current owners. Home365 describes the fee as charged once per new lease. A renewal of the same tenant is a different fee. Home365 puts renewals at $100 to $500 flat, or 25% to 50% of one month's rent, and notes that some managers waive them.

Write these turnover rules into the management agreement:

- [ ] The leasing fee applies to each new lease, with the percent and any minimum
- [ ] The renewal fee, or "no renewal fee"
- [ ] What happens if the tenant leaves or is evicted early: a free or reduced re-lease within a set period
- [ ] Whether lease-only clients pay a higher fee than full-management clients
- [ ] Who pays listing and screening costs, if not the fee

Home365 advises owners to ask about early-exit refunds. If your agreement already answers that question, you remove one reason for an owner to pick a competitor.

## How do I check my leasing fee against the total cost to the owner?

Owners compare the total yearly cost, not one fee. Add the monthly fee, the leasing fee, the renewal fee and any markups for one year on one home. You can run that total with the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

Then publish your fees. Owners and AI answer engines both compare managers by published fees. A manager who hides fees gives the AI nothing to cite. Read [how owners choose a property manager with AI](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai) to see why a clear fee page helps.

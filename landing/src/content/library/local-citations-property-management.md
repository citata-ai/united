## What is NAP consistency for a property management company?

NAP consistency means your business name, address and phone number read the same on every listing. A local citation is any web page that shows those three facts: a directory profile, a map listing, a chamber of commerce page. Search engines and AI tools compare these pages to decide which facts are true.

Google's guidelines set the base. Your business name should match the real-world name on your signage, website and stationery. Your phone number should connect to your location and be under your control. Every other listing should copy your Google Business Profile.

Write down one master NAP and never change it by hand on a single site:

| Field | Master value example | Common mistakes |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Smith Property Management | "Smith PM", "Smith Property Management LLC Chicago" |
| Address | 120 N Main St, Suite 4, Springfield, IL 62701 | "Ste 4", old office, home address on a service-area profile |
| Phone | (217) 555-0142 | Call-tracking numbers, an old line, a cell phone |
| Website | https://smithpm.com | http vs https, a tracking URL, a subdomain |
| Hours | Mon to Fri 9:00 to 5:00 | Different hours on each site |

Small format changes, like "Suite" or "Ste", matter less than wrong facts. Fix wrong facts first: old addresses, dead phone numbers and old names after a rebrand.

## Which citation sites matter for property managers?

The sites that matter most are the ones owners and AI tools read. Google's local ranking help page says prominence depends partly on how many websites link to your business. AI answers add another reason: they often quote directories.

Our own test shows it. For a Chicago-area property manager, we ran 144 AI answers on 2026-10-16. The most cited sources were manager websites, then Yelp (35 answers), Expertise.com (22) and Thumbtack (17) (our data; the client is a related party, its owner is CEO of our partner MyRemotely). Goodjuju Marketing's 2026 study adds that ChatGPT's local results lean on Yelp and Bing data, not Google's local index.

Start with this list, in this order:

1. **Google Business Profile.** The master record.
2. **Bing Places for Business.** Feeds Bing and, per Goodjuju, ChatGPT local results.
3. **Apple Business Connect.** Apple's tool to claim your place card in Apple Maps.
4. **Yelp.** A top AI source in our Chicago test.
5. **Better Business Bureau (BBB).** Owners check it before they sign.
6. **Property management directories AI cites for your city.** Expertise.com, iPropertyManagement, PropertyManagement.com, NARPM member search if you are a member.
7. **Local sources.** Chamber of commerce, local real estate board, landlord associations.
8. **Data aggregators and social profiles.** Facebook, LinkedIn company page, Nextdoor business page.

To find your city's directories, ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude "best property management company in [city]" and write down every source URL in the answers. Those sites are your priority list.

## Should a property manager use a tool like Yext?

Most single-office property managers do not need Yext. Yext's listings page says its system pushes verified data directly to 200+ publishers and AI tools. That helps a brand with dozens of locations. For one office, a manual audit of 20 to 30 listings costs less and fixes more.

| Option | Fits | Cost to check | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Manual audit and edits | 1 to 3 offices | Staff time, 4 to 8 hours first pass | Takes discipline to repeat yearly |
| Citation building service (one-time) | New business, rebrand or move | Ask vendors for a quote | Check the list of sites before you pay |
| Subscription sync (Yext and similar) | Many locations, frequent changes | Yext's pricing page shows no list price on 2026-10-19; quote only | Some sync tools change listings only while you pay |

Ask any vendor one question before you sign: "What happens to my listings if I cancel?" Get the answer in writing.

## How do I fix old wrong property management listings?

Fix old listings by hand, one site at a time, and keep a log. A spreadsheet stops you from fixing the same site twice or forgetting one.

### Cleanup steps

1. **Search for old facts.** Search your old address, old phone and old name in quotes. List every page that shows them.
2. **Search your current facts.** Find listings with small errors.
3. **Rank the list.** Put Google, Bing, Apple, Yelp, BBB and AI-cited directories first.
4. **Claim each listing.** Most sites let you claim with a phone or email code.
5. **Edit to the master NAP.** Copy and paste; do not type.
6. **Close duplicates.** Ask each site to merge or remove a second listing for the same office.
7. **Log it.** Site, URL, date, what you changed, login owner.
8. **Check again in 30 days.** Some directories copy data from others and undo your fix.

| Site | URL | Problem | Action | Date | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yelp | yelp.com/biz/... | Old phone | Claimed, edited | 2026-10-19 | Done |
| BBB | bbb.org/... | Old address | Request sent | 2026-10-19 | Waiting |

## How much did HUD's fair market rent change in Columbus, Ohio for FY2027?

HUD's fair market rent for a 2-bedroom in Columbus, Ohio rose 12.0% for FY2027, from $1,430 to $1,602 a month. That is the third-largest 2-bedroom rise among the 50 biggest HUD metro areas, after Kansas City and Chicago. Every bedroom size rose between 11.3% and 13.2%.

A 12% rise is $172 a month on a 2-bedroom, or $2,064 a year. The FY2027 numbers took effect on 2026-10-01. Housing authorities use fair market rent to set voucher payment standards. So a 12% rise can lift what a Section 8 tenant's voucher covers in Columbus. For payment standards and the housing authority link, see the [Columbus fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/columbus-oh-fair-market-rent-2027).

## Are Columbus, Ohio rents rising or falling?

Columbus, Ohio market rents are rising, but at a slow pace. The Zillow Observed Rent Index (ZORI, a typical asking rent) for the Columbus metro was $1,525 in September 2026. That is up 2.9% from $1,482 a year before.

| Measure (Zillow ZORI, Sept 2026) | Columbus metro | United States |
|---|---|---|
| Typical asking rent | $1,525 | $1,932 |
| Change in 1 year | +2.9% | +2.7% |
| Change in 3 years | +9.8% | +7.5% |

Two facts sit side by side. HUD's fair market rent jumped 12% in one step. Zillow's market rents rose about 3% in a year. HUD's number is not a forecast of what tenants will pay on the open market. Price your lease from local comparable listings, not from the FMR table.

## What vacancy rate does Columbus, Ohio have?

Columbus, Ohio vacancy depends on where the unit is. Marcus & Millichap's 2Q 2026 Columbus multifamily report says downtown vacancy ended 2025 at a record high of 8.1%. Suburban vacancy fell to 4.2% in the same period.

The report gives two causes. A wave of new deliveries came to the core. Faster population growth outside the core lifted suburban demand. Net absorption (new units rented, minus units emptied) reached its highest level since at least 2001.

What this means for you:

- **Suburban single-family home:** demand is strong, and a 4.2% vacancy rate leaves little empty stock.
- **Downtown condo or small building:** you compete with new buildings that may offer concessions. Price to fill fast.

The report covers apartments. A single-family rental can behave a little differently.

## Which local rules affect Columbus, Ohio rental returns?

Columbus rules add paperwork and small fees, but no rent cap. Here are the rules that touch your returns.

| Rule | What it requires | Cost to you |
|---|---|---|
| Franklin County rental registration (ORC 5323.02) | File owner contact details with the County Auditor within 60 days of a transfer or tax bill, and update after changes | $150 fine per property per tax year if missed |
| Columbus rental registry (Columbus Code Chapter 4515) | Register each rental with the city, starting January 2027, with pre-registration from October to December 2026 | $15 per unit per year, capped at $1,500 per complex |
| Registry inspections | City inspection of common areas and building systems once every three years | Covered by the fee |
| Pay to Stay (Ordinance 2109-2023) | Tenants can work to get rental assistance up until a court judgment | Can slow an eviction |
| Third-party payments (Ordinance 2108-2023) | Accept rent paid by a third party for a tenant who is not in breach | None, but staff must accept it |
| Rent increase notice (Ordinance 3525-2024) | At least 180 days' notice of a monthly rent increase at renewal, as City Council describes it | Plan increases 6 months ahead |
| No rent control (Ohio HB 430, 2022) | State law preempts local rent control | None |

Sources: Franklin County Auditor, Columbus City Council's Housing For All page, a Kegler Brown Hill + Ritter summary of Chapter 4515, and a Vorys summary of HB 430. The unregistered-unit penalty under the city registry is $2 per unit per day, with caps tied to the fee.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What about Intel and job growth in Columbus?

Intel's Ohio fab is years away, so do not price your rent on it. In its 2025-02-28 timeline update, Intel said it plans to finish construction of the first New Albany factory (Mod 1) in 2030. It plans to begin operations between 2030 and 2031. Mod 2 would finish in 2031 and open in 2032.

Intel also said it will "continue construction at a slower pace," with room to speed up if demand warrants. Treat the fab as a long-term factor for the east side of the metro, not a 2027 rent driver.

## Should I rent or sell my Columbus, Ohio home now?

Rent or sell is a numbers question, and Columbus gives you two good signs and one warning. The good signs: market rents still rise, and suburban vacancy is low. The warning: new supply caps how fast rents can climb, above all near downtown.

Use this checklist before you decide:

1. **Price the rent.** Pull 5 local comparable listings. Do not use the HUD FMR as your asking rent.
2. **Count the full cost.** Add taxes, insurance, repairs, a vacancy allowance, the $15 city registry fee and management. A manager at 8% of $1,602 costs $128 a month. Our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) does the math.
3. **Plan rent increases early.** Under the city's 180-day notice rule as described by City Council, a renewal increase needs about 6 months of lead time.
4. **Check your location.** A suburban home faces 4.2% vacancy. A downtown unit faces 8.1% and newer competition.
5. **Compare with the sale.** Get a broker's price opinion. Compare the after-tax sale proceeds with 5 years of net rent.

If the net rent covers your costs with room to spare, Columbus still supports a hold. If your unit competes with new downtown buildings, a sale may be the cleaner choice.

## What fees does each payment app charge?

Check the business offer rather than a consumer transfer headline. A personal account's free payment route does not establish the correct terms for collecting rent.

| Service | Verified fee signal | What remains to check |
|---|---|---|
| Zelle | Business fees depend on the bank | Account eligibility and limits |
| Venmo business profile | Standard fee: 1.9% plus $0.10 | Rental eligibility and transfer options |
| Cash App Business | Terms allow payment processing fees | Current account rate and supported use |
| Rental platform | Plan and method determine fees | Subscription, payer and controls |

Sources: Zelle's business FAQ, Venmo's business page and Cash App Business terms, read October 19, 2026. Do not insert an unverified Cash App percentage from a forum.

Ask your bank whether the exact business account supports Zelle. Zelle says business availability and limits depend on the financial institution. Keep the response with your collection instructions.

## Does Venmo allow rent on a personal account?

Venmo describes authorized commercial payment routes; that is not blanket permission to label rent as a personal transfer. Confirm that your intended rental use is supported.

Venmo's help page allows payment to authorized business profiles and eligible purchase-tagged personal payments. A personal-profile label alone does not resolve commercial terms or rental eligibility.

Ask Venmo about the correct account type and the exact transaction. Save the answer and current terms. Do not tell residents to mislabel payments to avoid a fee.

Check Cash App's business eligibility too. Its terms distinguish Business Services from personal use and allow restrictions on supported activity. Confirm your rental business before publishing a payment link.

Keep the authorized recipient clear. Residents should know the company name and a trusted contact route. Test a small sample process under the provider's rules before moving live collections.

## Can you block partial payments?

Do not assume a payment app enforces your rent policy. A transfer request and a ledger-linked rent charge are different controls.

Ask the provider to demonstrate the exact block you need. Test a payment below the balance, a payment above it and a payment after account status changes. Inspect what reaches the bank and what remains pending.

The vendor pages reviewed here do not establish a configurable rent-balance block for every app and account. Treat that control as unverified until demonstrated.

TenantCloud's pricing page lists partial-payment settings among tenant-management functions. That makes a rental platform worth testing, but still does not prove your plan and account meet the requirement.

Partial-payment handling may affect a dispute or legal process. Do not infer a legal result from an app setting. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What records do you get for taxes?

Keep records that explain the payment, property, period and any fee. A notification or bank deposit alone may not explain how the money was allocated.

Zelle's FAQ says it does not report network transactions to the IRS. It also tells recipients to address tax obligations separately. That provider statement does not make rental income tax-free.

Ask your tax professional which reports and supporting records they need. Do not use a missing payment form as a reason to omit ledger entries. This is general information, not tax advice. Check the current law or ask a tax professional.

Use this record checklist:

- Payer and recipient identity.
- Property and rent period.
- Gross payment and deducted fee.
- Bank settlement record.
- Refund or reversal history.
- Ledger allocation and running balance.
- Supporting message or agreement reference.

Check exports before relying on them. Make sure staff can identify payments without exposing unnecessary resident information.

## What are better options for rent collection?

A dedicated rental platform deserves a test when you need charges, balances and payment records together. Better means a better fit for your workflow, not a universal brand winner.

Test a resident payment against a lease charge. Inspect the receipt, processing status and ledger. Then test a return and confirm the balance updates clearly.

Ask about payment choices, account setup and fees. Verify the current plan rather than assuming a free account includes every control. Check the route for residents who cannot use the preferred method.

A dedicated system can still need manual review. Match bank activity and handle unclear payments promptly. Keep responsibility visible when a transfer arrives from someone other than the named tenant. Ask for enough context to allocate it correctly without collecting unnecessary data.

## How should you decide?

Decide through eligibility, controls and records. Speed alone does not establish a safe collection process.

Write the approved account, fee payer and reconciliation steps. Use a trusted route for any change of payment instructions. Warn staff to verify a new destination independently before sending it to residents.

Test exports and archive access before leaving an app. Confirm how pending transfers and reversals will be handled after cutover. Keep old records available to authorized reviewers.

Keep a named reviewer for unmatched transfers. Ask residents for the property and payment period through an approved route. Resolve the ledger entry before including the receipt in an owner payout calculation.

## What are the requirements for RMP?

The Residential Management Professional designation requires a complete candidacy record, not just a course purchase. Use the detailed checklist linked by NARPM's designation forms page. Check the form version with NARPM before making a training plan.

NARPM's linked RMP checklist specifies 100 unit years of experience and at least 2 consecutive years. The checklist also addresses current managed units, membership, education, recommendations, event participation and service. Read those requirements together rather than using experience alone as eligibility.

A unit year measures management over time, not just the number of homes on today's rent roll. NARPM's form defines the term. Keep management dates and verification records so the application reflects your actual work.

Short overview wording can be less precise than the checklist. If a page describes units while the application describes unit years, ask NARPM to confirm the intended measure. Do not guess based on a search snippet.

NARPM's RMP checklist calls for 18 hours of NARPM education and its ethics course. It also requires 50 elective points. Check the listed conditions and evidence rules before choosing activities.

Build a candidate folder with the checklist, proof of work, course records and recommendations. Keep private owner and resident details out of unnecessary copies. Ask NARPM how to submit sensitive evidence safely.

Assign time for the application work as well as classes. A plan that covers tuition but ignores document gathering can stall before submission.

## What does MPM add?

Master Property Manager builds on the RMP and adds its own experience and development requirements. NARPM's MPM checklist specifies an existing RMP and 500 unit years. It also sets education, recommendation and participation requirements.

Use the current MPM checklist to assess gaps. Do not assume every course or service record from RMP automatically satisfies the next designation. Ask NARPM to confirm any credit or alternate path before booking work.

NARPM's MPM checklist calls for 24 hours of education and its ethics course. It sets 200 elective points, including credit from the RMP. It also specifies license-experience evidence where a license is required. Confirm your particular path with NARPM.

Treat the process as a professional development project. Choose learning that addresses weak parts of your management system. A credential has more practical value when staff use the resulting skills.

Use this planning table:

| Designation | Applies to | First document to review | Work file to prepare |
|---|---|---|---|
| RMP | Individual | Current RMP candidacy checklist | Experience and development evidence |
| MPM | Individual with RMP | Current MPM candidacy checklist | Additional eligibility and completion evidence |
| CRMC | Company | Current CRMC handbook and audit requirements | Company procedures and audit records |

The table shows a planning sequence, not the full application rules. NARPM's official forms control the requirements.

Before you apply, ask for the current fee schedule and treatment of incomplete applications. Do not use an old blog price as your training budget.

## What does CRMC require of a company?

CRMC evaluates the company rather than only a person's training history. NARPM's CRMC page requires an MPM designee on staff, verification of 500 unit years and a successful approved audit. It says the audit must finish within 3 years of application.

The same NARPM page states that renewal occurs annually and recertification every 3 years. Confirm the current forms before planning those dates. Put the work into the company's compliance calendar.

Prepare the operating evidence before treating the audit as a marketing project. Gather the procedures, records and review responsibilities requested in the current handbook. Check that staff use the documented process in real work.

Identify who maintains eligibility if the MPM designee leaves. Ask NARPM how a staff change affects the company's status. Do not assume a credential continues unchanged after a key person departs.

Budget for audit preparation and any outside assistance. The CRMC page discusses audit arrangements, including a virtual option and expenses for an on-site auditor. Ask for the current terms rather than quoting an unverified total.

The useful internal test is reproducibility. Can another qualified employee find the records, explain the controls and perform the process without the founder guiding every step?

## Do owners know these designations?

Owner recognition needs evidence from your own prospects; do not assume everyone understands NARPM initials. Explain what the designation means in plain language and show the current holder. Avoid claiming a guaranteed sales effect.

Ask leads whether they noticed the credential and what they thought it represented. Record the answer separately from the source of the lead. A prospect can value the training without knowing the acronym before visiting your site.

Use the full name at first mention. Explain whether it applies to an individual or the company. Do not present membership as equivalent to an earned designation.

Link to the official verification or explanation where available. Keep the holder's name and current status accurate. Review marketing materials after staff changes and renewals.

Pair the credential with evidence of service: a clear reporting process, a sample owner update and a practical contact plan. Owners still need to know who handles their property.

For the wider discovery process, read [how owners choose a property manager with AI](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai). A designation can be a fact on your site, but it does not guarantee that an assistant will cite your company.

## How should I plan the application work?

Plan the application from the official checklist and record each requirement's status. Use fields for evidence needed, responsible person, source document and next action. Mark questions for NARPM before committing money.

Keep a separate list of operational changes you want the training to support. Examples include clearer owner reports, better staff supervision and more consistent record review. Those are your goals, not promised designation outcomes.

Ask the candidate to share practical changes with the team after learning. Review whether those changes improve real work. Do not measure the entire project by adding letters to an email signature.

Before using a badge, confirm approval and the current rules for its use. Save the approval record with your brand assets. Keep unfinished candidacy distinct from an earned credential.

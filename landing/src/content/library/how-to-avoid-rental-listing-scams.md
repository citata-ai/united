## How do rental listing scams work?

A rental listing scam uses your real listing to take money from renters. The scammer copies your photos and text, posts the unit at a lower rent, and collects a deposit for a home they do not control.

The FTC lists the signs a renter sees:

| Sign | What the scammer does |
|---|---|
| Different owner details | Other ads for the same address show a different owner or company |
| A deal that is too good | Low rent or extra features |
| No showing | Claims to be "out of the country" |
| Pressure | Pushes a fast decision |
| Untraceable payment | Asks for wire transfer, gift card or cryptocurrency |
| Personal data too early | Asks for a Social Security number before the renter agrees to rent |

The problem is large. The FBI's 2025 IC3 report logged 12,368 real estate fraud complaints with $275,110,419 in losses. That is up from $173,586,820 in 2024. The FBI defines real estate fraud as "loss of funds from a real estate investment or fraud involving rental or timeshare property." The report does not split out rental scams alone.

Your loss is real too. Renters who were scammed may come to your door, call you angry, or post bad reviews.

## How do I check if my listing was copied?

Check while the unit is listed, at least once a week. A copy can appear at any time while the unit is on the market.

1. **Search the address.** Search the full street address on Google, Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist and the large rental sites.
2. **Reverse image search.** Upload your main photo to Google Lens or a similar tool.
3. **Search a unique phrase.** Copy one sentence from your listing text and search it in quotes.
4. **Compare details.** Look for a different name, phone number, email or a much lower rent.
5. **Save proof.** Take screenshots with the URL and the date.

Add a small, unique detail to your photos or text, such as your company name in the first photo. Copies are then easier to find and to prove.

## Where do I report a fake listing?

Report the fake listing in several places on the same day. The FTC says to report to:

- [ ] The website where the ad was posted.
- [ ] The FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
- [ ] Your local law enforcement agency.
- [ ] Your state attorney general.

Also file with the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center at ic3.gov. That is where the FBI collects the complaints counted in its annual report.

When you report, include the URL, the screenshots, your proof that you own or manage the unit, and your real listing link. Ask the site to remove the copy. Then check again in 2 days.

## What should my listing say to warn renters?

Your listing should tell renters how to tell you apart from a copy. Put a short warning near the top of the listing and on your website.

Sample warning text (adapt it to your business):

```text
Scam warning: We list this home only on [site 1], [site 2] and
[our website]. We never ask for payment by wire transfer, gift card
or cryptocurrency. You can see the home in person or by live video
before you pay anything. Our phone number is [number]. If you see
this home listed elsewhere at a different price, please report it
to us at [email].
```

Also make your process hard to copy:

- **One payment path.** Take application fees and deposits only through your screening or payment portal.
- **Showings first.** Let every applicant see the unit, in person or by self-tour with ID checks, before any payment.
- **Ask for data at the right time.** Ask for a Social Security number only on the formal application, as the FTC suggests.
- **Name your team.** List the names of people who may contact applicants.

If you screen applicants with a consumer report, the FCRA rules still apply. 15 U.S.C. 1681m requires an adverse action notice when a report leads to a denial.

## How do I protect a vacant unit from squatters?

Protect a vacant unit by making it look watched and by keeping a record. An empty home is easier to enter and occupy without permission.

Use this checklist:

- [ ] Visit at least once a week, and vary the day.
- [ ] Keep all doors and windows locked; change codes after each showing period.
- [ ] Use a smart lock or lockbox with an entry log.
- [ ] Add a camera at the front door, where local law allows.
- [ ] Keep lights on a timer and the yard mowed.
- [ ] Post "No trespassing" signs.
- [ ] Ask a neighbor to call you if they see activity.
- [ ] Keep your deed or management agreement ready to show police.

Removal rules differ by state. Florida Statutes 82.036 lets an owner ask the county sheriff to remove unauthorized occupants with a verified complaint. All conditions must be met. For example, the people entered unlawfully, the owner told them to leave, they are not current or former tenants under an agreement, and no related case is pending. The sheriff then serves a notice to vacate "without delay." Other states may require a court case, so ask a local attorney.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

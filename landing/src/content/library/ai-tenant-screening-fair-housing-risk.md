## How do screening algorithms score applicants?

Screening algorithms can turn records or application facts into a score, flag, or recommendation. Ask which output your vendor produces before deciding how staff should use it.

A credit score, document check, income calculation, and rental recommendation answer different questions. Snappt, for example, describes document analysis and source-connected income tools. Those checks do not establish that every flagged applicant is unsuitable.

Ask the vendor to separate verified facts from estimates and missing information. Staff should see the source and date behind a material finding. A label without evidence is hard to review.

Test a wrong name match and a supported income source outside payroll. Find out whether staff can correct the input and obtain a fresh result. Keep the original result and the correction trail.

Do not let staff infer protected traits from names, photos, or language. Use documented rental criteria and a review process approved for your jurisdiction.

## What has HUD said about screening tools?

HUD's archived announcement of May 2, 2024 discussed Fair Housing risks in AI screening and digital advertising. Treat the archive as a dated explanation, not proof of current binding requirements.

HUD's April 6, 2026 Federal Register notice withdrew specified FHEO guidance, effective September 17, 2025. Its list includes digital advertising guidance. That title is different from the tenant screening guidance.

Do not infer the status of every screening document from a related withdrawal. Check the exact title, issuing office, and current official text with counsel. This guide does not present the archive as a new rule.

The statute is a separate source. Under 42 USC 3604, covered housing decisions cannot discriminate on protected grounds. The statute also addresses disability discrimination and reasonable accommodation.

Ask counsel to review your criteria and the way software applies them. A vendor's compliance label does not decide whether your practice meets the law.

## What must an adverse action notice say?

When adverse action relies partly on a consumer report, 15 USC 1681m requires notice and related disclosures. An automated recommendation does not remove that requirement.

Under that section, identify the reporting agency with its name, address, and phone number. State that the agency did not make the decision and cannot give its specific reasons.

The notice must explain the right to dispute report accuracy or completeness. It must also state the right to request a free report within 60 days. Additional written or electronic disclosures apply when a numerical credit score drives the action.

Use a reviewed notice template rather than asking a chatbot to invent one. Confirm the agency details for the actual report used. Keep evidence of the notice in the application file.

Ask counsel about other duties that apply to your facts and location. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do you audit a screening vendor?

Audit a vendor by tracing its inputs, outputs, corrections, and the decisions staff make from them. Start with a written description of your current criteria.

| Audit area | Evidence to request | Review question |
|---|---|---|
| Inputs | Source list and data dates | Can staff identify stale or wrong records? |
| Matching | Identity and record-match method | How are similar names handled? |
| Recommendation | Criteria and reason codes | Can a person explain the result? |
| Corrections | Dispute and recheck process | Can an applicant fix an error? |
| Changes | Model and policy change notices | Will your process change without review? |
| Access | Roles and activity history | Who can view or alter a decision? |

Use permitted sample records to test the process. Ask the vendor to demonstrate an error correction from start to finish. Do not accept a policy statement as proof the workflow works.

Have qualified reviewers assess outcomes and criteria for potential bias. Do not collect sensitive applicant data casually to build your own experiment. Plan any audit data use with counsel and privacy staff.

## Which questions should you ask the vendor?

Ask who owns each step when the report is unclear or wrong. A vendor that can describe the product should also explain the exception process.

Request the exact meaning of each label. Does a flag mean suspected alteration, missing coverage, failed identity verification, or a rental recommendation? Staff need that distinction before contacting an applicant.

Ask whether a person can review the source material and change the decision. Confirm which findings need a new report and whether rechecks cost extra.

Then ask about model updates. Who approves changes to the criteria, score thresholds, and output wording? Require a way to review material changes before they affect applications.

Close the audit with an action list. Keep unsupported automation disabled until you can explain and test it. Train staff to document the reason for a decision in plain words.

## How should you handle an applicant correction?

Give applicants a clear contact route and staff a documented review process. Separate the reporting agency's dispute process from your own review of the rental application.

Keep the application status accurate while staff review new information. Avoid labels that imply confirmed fraud when a case remains unresolved. Record what evidence is missing and who will follow up.

Review the same type of correction consistently. Escalate accommodation requests and legal questions to trained staff. Confirm the handling with local counsel.

## How should you test the choice before you pay?

Test the full workflow with sample records before you move live data. Use the same case in each product. Ask the person who will do the daily work to run the test. A sales demo alone does not show where your team will need help.

Keep a decision sheet with the task, result, missing step, and person who checked it. Mark each result as shown, promised, or still open. Ask for written terms when a feature needs an upgrade or another service. Include staff setup time in your cost review.

Check the exit path while you still control the choice. Export the sample records and open them outside the platform. Confirm that notes, files, dates, and links remain useful. Ask how you will get records after you close the account.

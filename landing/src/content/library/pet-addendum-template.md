## What should a pet addendum to a lease include?

A pet addendum to a lease should include 6 parts: the parties, the pet, the money terms, the rules, damage and liability, and what happens on a breach. Each part answers one question that comes up later in a dispute. Without the addendum, the lease alone often says "no pets" or says nothing.

Use the template below. Fill in the blanks for each pet. One addendum can cover more than one pet, but list each pet on its own line.

### Copy-ready pet addendum template

**Pet Addendum to Lease**

This addendum is part of the lease dated ________ between ________ (Landlord) and ________ (Tenant) for the unit at ________ (Premises).

**1. Pet details**

| Field | Pet 1 | Pet 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Type (dog, cat, other) | | |
| Name | | |
| Breed or mix | | |
| Color | | |
| Weight (lb) | | |
| Age | | |
| License or tag number | | |
| Vaccinations current (date) | | |

Tenant may keep only the pets listed above. Any new or replacement pet needs a new signed addendum.

**2. Money terms**

- Pet deposit (refundable): $________
- Monthly pet rent: $________, due with rent
- One-time pet fee (not refundable): $________

The landlord holds the pet deposit under the same rules as the security deposit, as state law requires.

**3. Pet rules**

Tenant agrees to:

- Keep the pet on a leash or in a carrier in all common areas
- Pick up and dispose of pet waste at once
- Keep the pet from causing noise that disturbs neighbors
- Not leave the pet alone in the unit for an unreasonable time
- Keep the pet's license and vaccinations current, and show proof on request
- Not let the pet stay on a balcony or patio when no one is home
- Use flea and pest control as needed
- Allow entry for pest treatment with notice under the lease

**4. Damage and liability**

Tenant pays for all damage the pet causes beyond normal wear and tear. This includes carpet, floors, doors, screens, blinds and landscaping. Tenant is liable for any injury or property damage the pet causes to other people. The pet deposit does not limit what the tenant owes.

Optional: Tenant keeps renter's insurance with pet liability coverage of $________ and gives proof on request.

**5. Breach**

If the tenant breaks this addendum, the landlord may give written notice to fix the problem in ________ days. If the problem continues, the landlord may require the tenant to remove the pet, or take other action the lease and state law allow.

**6. Assistance animals**

This addendum does not apply to a service animal or other assistance animal approved as a reasonable accommodation. Those animals are not pets.

Signatures: Landlord ________ Date ________ / Tenant ________ Date ________

This is a generic template, not a state legal form. Check your lease and local law before you use it.

## What is the difference between a pet deposit, pet rent and a pet fee?

A pet deposit is refundable, pet rent is monthly, and a pet fee is a one-time charge you keep. Each one covers a different risk. Many landlords use one or two, not all three.

| Term | When paid | Refundable | What it covers | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pet deposit | Before move-in | Yes, less pet damage | Damage found at move-out | State deposit limits may include it |
| Pet rent | Each month | No | Extra wear over time | Must be in the lease or addendum |
| Pet fee | Once, at signing | No | Cleaning or admin cost | Check state rules on fees you keep |

A worked example. A tenant with 1 dog signs a 12-month lease. The addendum sets a $300 pet deposit and $35 a month in pet rent. Over the lease, the tenant pays $35 × 12 = $420 in pet rent. At move-out, the dog scratched a door that costs $180 to fix. The landlord returns $300 − $180 = $120 of the pet deposit, with an itemized list.

The numbers above are an example. Your state's deposit statute decides how much you may hold, whether the pet deposit counts toward a total cap, and the deadline to return it. Check it before you set the amounts.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How do you handle service and support animals?

Handle service animals and support animals as a reasonable accommodation, not as pets. The federal Fair Housing Act bans housing discrimination on 6 bases, and disability is one of them. The U.S. Department of Justice lists them: race or color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status and disability.

HUD set out the rules for animals in notice FHEO-2020-01 (January 2020). A summary by the National Low Income Housing Coalition quotes the notice: a housing provider may not "charge a fee or deposit" for service animals or support animals. The tenant can still be charged for actual damage the animal causes.

The ADA uses a narrower term. ADA.gov says a service animal is a dog "individually trained to do work or perform tasks" for a person with a disability. When the task is not obvious, staff may ask only 2 questions:

1. Is the dog a service animal required because of a disability?
2. What work or task has the dog been trained to perform?

ADA.gov also says the Fair Housing Act has a broader term, "assistance animal". In housing, that can include animals that give emotional support. So a "no pets" rule or a pet addendum does not decide an assistance animal request.

Use this short process for each request:

- [ ] Log the date of the request, in any form (spoken or written)
- [ ] Do not charge pet rent, a pet fee or a pet deposit for the animal
- [ ] If the disability or need is not obvious, ask for reliable information, as HUD guidance allows
- [ ] Decide in a reasonable time and reply in writing
- [ ] Apply normal lease rules on noise, waste and damage to the tenant's conduct

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Which pet rules should the addendum list?

The addendum should list rules you can check and enforce. "Be a good pet owner" is not a rule. "Pick up waste in common areas at once" is a rule.

Use this test for each rule:

1. **Can you see it?** A staff member can see a dog off-leash in a hallway.
2. **Is it fair to all pets?** Apply the same rule to every tenant with the same kind of pet.
3. **Does it protect someone?** Each rule should protect the unit, neighbors or the pet.

Pet policies often limit breed, weight or the number of pets. Those limits may conflict with state law, city ordinances or your insurance policy. Read your insurance policy first: some insurers exclude named breeds, and your rule should match your coverage.

Keep the rules the same across your portfolio where the law allows. One standard addendum makes it easier for your team to enforce rules the same way for each tenant.

## How does a pet policy affect leasing?

A pet policy changes who applies and how long tenants stay. Many renters own pets, so a "no pets" listing shrinks your pool of applicants. A clear pet addendum lets you say yes with terms, instead of no.

Put the pet terms in your listing: allowed pets, the deposit, the monthly pet rent and any limits. Applicants then self-select before they apply.

Louisiana runs evictions under the Code of Civil Procedure, Title XI, articles 4701 to 4735. I read them on the Louisiana State Legislature site on 2026-10-19. The process is short: a notice to vacate, a summary hearing, and a warrant.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice to vacate | At least 5 days from delivery | CCP art. 4701 |
| Waiver of notice | Allowed in a written lease clause | CCP art. 4701 |
| Fixed-term lease ending | Notice may go out up to 30 days before the end | CCP art. 4701 |
| Notice if tenant cannot be found | Attach to a door of the premises | CCP art. 4703 |
| Hearing on the rule | No earlier than the 3rd day after service | CCP art. 4732(A) |
| Judgment of eviction | Effective for at least 90 days | CCP art. 4732(B) |
| Warrant for possession | If tenant stays 24 hours after judgment | CCP art. 4733 |
| Suspensive appeal | Answer under oath + appeal and bond in 24 hours | CCP art. 4735 |
| Abandonment after notice | Landlord may retake without more court steps | CCP art. 4731(B) |

## What notice must a Louisiana landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

A Louisiana landlord delivers a written notice to vacate that allows "not less than five days from the date of its delivery" (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4701). The same notice covers nonpayment of rent, the end of the term, action by the lessor, or "any other reason" the right of occupancy ended.

Three details in art. 4701:

- **Waiver.** A tenant may waive the notice "by written waiver contained in the lease." Then the landlord may file at once when occupancy ends.
- **Lease with no set term.** For a month-to-month lease, the notice that ends the lease under the Civil Code counts as the notice to vacate. Civil Code art. 2728 sets that notice at 10 calendar days before the end of the month.
- **Fixed-term lease.** The landlord may give notice to vacate up to 30 days before the term ends.

If the premises are closed or the tenant cannot be found, attach the notice to a door. That has the same effect as delivery (art. 4703).

## How long does an eviction take in Louisiana?

A Louisiana eviction for unpaid rent can finish in about 2 weeks if the tenant does not contest it. The articles set short clocks:

| Step | Days | Running total |
|---|---|---|
| Notice to vacate | 5+ | 5 |
| File rule to show cause; serve tenant | 1 to 3 | 6 to 8 |
| Hearing no earlier than 3rd day after service | 3+ | 9 to 11 |
| Tenant does not leave within 24 hours | 1 | 10 to 12 |
| Warrant issued and executed | Varies by parish | about 2 weeks |

If the lease waives the notice, you skip the first 5 days. Court and sheriff calendars vary by parish, so plan for some slack.

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Louisiana, and what does it cost?

A Louisiana landlord can file in 1 of 3 kinds of court, within its territory:

- **Justice of the peace court.** Art. 4912(A)(1) gives jurisdiction over evictions from leased residential premises, "regardless of the amount of monthly or yearly rent."
- **City or parish court.** Art. 4844 gives these courts jurisdiction over evictions from leased residential premises, concurrent with the district court, regardless of the rent.
- **District court.** It shares jurisdiction with the courts above.

The landlord files a rule to show cause "why he should not be ordered to deliver possession" (art. 4731(A)). The rule must state the grounds for eviction.

Filing and service fees are set by each court and parish. They are not in the Code articles we read. Ask the clerk of the court where you file.

## Can a Louisiana landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes. Article 4701 applies when the right of occupancy ends "by expiration of its term, action by the lessor, nonpayment of rent, or for any other reason."

**Lease violation.** If the lease lets you end it for a breach, end it under its terms. Then deliver the 5-day notice to vacate, unless the lease waives it.

**No cause, month-to-month.** Give notice of termination at least 10 calendar days before the end of the month (Civil Code art. 2728). For a residence, it must be in writing (art. 2729). That notice also serves as the notice to vacate.

**No cause, fixed term.** The lease ends on its date. You may send the notice to vacate up to 30 days before that date (art. 4701).

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Louisiana?

The sheriff, constable or marshal removes the tenant. If the tenant does not comply with the judgment "within twenty-four hours after its rendition," the court "shall issue immediately a warrant" to the officer (art. 4733).

The officer executes the warrant "in the presence of two witnesses," clears the premises of property, and puts the landlord in possession. The officer may break open locked doors or gates when needed (art. 4734).

The judgment stays effective for at least 90 days (art. 4732(B)). An appeal does not stop the eviction unless the tenant answered under oath with an affirmative defense and filed the appeal and bond within 24 hours (art. 4735).

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Louisiana?

The Louisiana Code gives one route to possession without a judgment: abandonment. After the required notice, the landlord "may lawfully take possession of the premises without further judicial process, upon a reasonable belief that the lessee or occupant has abandoned the premises" (art. 4731(B)).

Signs of abandonment in the article include returned keys, an end of residential use, and removal of furniture and other movables.

In parishes under a federal disaster declaration, an end of residential occupancy is not proof of abandonment for 30 days after the declaration. A landlord who breaks that rule owes the residential tenant the greater of $500 or twice the monthly rent (art. 4731(C)).

Outside abandonment, use the court process. Ask a Louisiana attorney before you change locks or stop a service.

## Louisiana eviction checklist

- Check the lease for a written waiver of the notice to vacate.
- Deliver a written 5-day notice to vacate, or attach it to the door if the tenant is gone.
- File a rule to show cause in a justice of the peace, city, parish or district court.
- Serve the tenant; the hearing is no earlier than the 3rd day after service.
- After 24 hours with no move-out, ask for the warrant.
- Let the sheriff, constable or marshal execute it.

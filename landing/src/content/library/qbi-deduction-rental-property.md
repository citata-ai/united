## When is a rental a trade or business?

A rental can qualify through its business facts or through the IRS rental real estate safe harbor. IRS Revenue Procedure 2019-38 says failing the safe harbor does not automatically rule out business status. Ask your preparer to identify the route used.

Do not treat ownership, a separate account, or an LLC as proof by itself. Give your preparer the lease, work performed, service arrangements, and operating history. Explain what you do and what employees or contractors do.

The IRS QBI page describes a deduction of up to 20% of qualified business income, subject to limits. Qualified business income is not gross rent. Your own taxable income and other factors can affect the result.

Separate the eligibility question from the calculation. First establish whether the rental belongs in the QBI review. Then work through the return using the rules for that tax year.

Do not move rental income to a different form merely to claim a deduction. Ask your preparer to keep the reporting treatment consistent with the facts. Save the written explanation so the same position can be reviewed when leases, services, or ownership change.

## What is the IRS rental safe harbor?

The rental safe harbor treats a qualifying enterprise as a business for QBI purposes. It does not create a general business classification for every other tax rule. Revenue Procedure 2019-38 sets the conditions.

The IRS requires separate books, qualifying rental services, time records, and a statement attached to the return. The service test generally requires 250 hours each year for an enterprise younger than 4 years. For an older enterprise, it applies in 3 of the 5 years ending with the tax year.

Ask your preparer to confirm how the enterprise groups properties. Do not combine unrelated work without checking the rules. A grouping decision can affect the records needed in future years.

Use this review table:

| Safe harbor item | Evidence to gather |
| --- | --- |
| Enterprise scope | Property list and ownership records |
| Separate books | Income and cost ledger |
| Rental services | Task descriptions and work records |
| Hours | Dated logs by person and activity |
| Return statement | Preparer's completed attachment |
| Exclusions | Lease terms and personal-use facts |

Treat the table as a document request, not an eligibility certificate. Your preparer should check each condition against the full procedure before claiming the safe harbor.

## Which records does the safe harbor need?

Safe harbor records must show who performed qualifying services, what they did, when they worked, and the time spent. Revenue Procedure 2019-38 calls for contemporaneous records. Build the record during the work rather than guessing later.

Ask a manager or contractor how it can support the service log. A monthly management fee does not itself prove a number of qualifying hours. Keep the underlying work orders and communication records where possible.

The IRS distinguishes rental services from investor tasks and other excluded activities. Ask your preparer to classify tasks such as leasing, rent collection, repairs, financing, travel, and major improvements. Do not count every property-related hour without review.

Keep the log short enough to use consistently. Record the property, date, person, task, and time. If the worker serves several properties, explain how the time divides among them.

Review gaps before filing. Do not create precise-looking hours from a broad memory of a busy year. Give the preparer the actual evidence and its limits. If the safe harbor cannot be supported, ask whether the separate facts-and-circumstances business route applies.

## Do triple-net leases qualify?

Triple-net leases do not qualify for this rental safe harbor. Revenue Procedure 2019-38 excludes the covered lease arrangements. That exclusion does not by itself answer whether an activity qualifies under a separate business analysis.

The IRS describes a triple-net arrangement through the tenant's duties for taxes, fees, insurance, and maintenance. Read the whole lease. The document title alone may not reflect what the tenant actually must pay.

Also tell your preparer about any personal use of the property. The safe harbor has exclusions tied to dwelling-unit use and other arrangements. A property that earns rent can still raise an exclusion question.

Do not change a lease only to reach a tax label without checking the full cost and legal effect. A different allocation of duties changes your operating risk. Have local counsel review any proposed contract change.

Keep a copy of the lease used for the annual review. Flag renewals and addenda that shift responsibilities. Your QBI position should follow the actual agreement and operating facts rather than continue unchanged because a prior return claimed the deduction.

## How should you check the final deduction?

Ask your preparer to show both the eligibility decision and the deduction calculation. The IRS QBI guidance lists limits tied to taxable income and, where applicable, wages and qualified property. A positive rental result alone does not settle the final amount.

Keep QBI separate from the passive activity review. IRS Publication 925 explains passive activity and at-risk rules. These rules answer different questions, so do not assume one classification decides the other.

Use the current tax-year forms and instructions. Avoid copying income thresholds from an older article. Ask how losses, other businesses, or ownership changes affect your own return.

For an operating cost review, use the [management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator). Choose management for the service you need, then document the work. Do not buy a service merely to claim hours without checking whether those hours qualify.

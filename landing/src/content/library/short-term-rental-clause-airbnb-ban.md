## What lease clause bans short-term rentals?

A useful clause covers the conduct you intend to restrict, including listings and paid occupancy. A bare reference to Airbnb can miss a booking made through another site. Write the term around the use, not only the brand.

Ask local counsel to adapt the following drafting points. This is a planning checklist, not ready-to-sign lease text. Some places limit how landlords can restrict occupancy or subletting. Existing leases also need review before you change their terms.

- **Define paid stays.** Address paid use of the whole unit or part of it.
- **Address listings.** State whether advertising or accepting bookings needs written approval.
- **Separate guests.** Explain how ordinary guests differ from paid occupants.
- **Cover other channels.** Include direct bookings and future booking platforms.
- **Require consent.** Identify who can approve an exception and how approval must be recorded.
- **Preserve lawful process.** Do not promise automatic removal or an unlawful penalty.

Texas Property Code 91.005 says a tenant cannot rent the leasehold to another person without prior landlord consent during the lease term. That is a Texas rule. Do not present it as the default rule for every state.

## How do I find out if my unit is listed?

Search public listings and preserve the evidence before contacting the tenant. Use features that can identify the actual unit. A similar floor plan or a street-level location pin may not be enough.

Save the listing address, page URL, host profile, description and date of review. Capture visible photographs and the booking terms. Record how the listing matches your unit, such as a distinct fixture or interior finish. Keep the original capture as well as any marked copy.

Do not create a false booking to enter an occupied home. Do not ask staff to pose as guests without a legal review. That can confuse the evidence and create other problems. A public record check and a clear tenant inquiry are better starting points.

Ask the tenant to explain the match. The listing could be old, copied or posted without permission. Record the response and any supporting records. Distinguish a live listing from a completed stay. Your lease may address both, but they are different events.

## Is it a lease violation I can act on?

A listing becomes an enforcement issue only after you connect reliable evidence to an enforceable lease term and local law. Ask counsel which notice applies. Do not copy another state's deadline into your notice.

| Evidence | What it may support | What still needs checking |
|---|---|---|
| Matching public listing | Advertising activity | Unit identity and current status |
| Tenant admission | Agreed facts | Exact conduct and relevant dates |
| Booking records | Paid occupancy | Authenticity and who arranged the stay |
| Neighbor report | A reason to investigate | Direct knowledge and corroboration |
| Entry log | A record of access | Identity, purpose and lawful collection |

Keep the notice factual. Identify the relevant conduct and lease language. State any lawful route to cure if required. Avoid claims about income, guest identity or damages that your records do not establish.

Do not change locks, cut utilities or carry belongings outside. Florida Statute 83.67 prohibits landlord utility interruption and denial of reasonable access in covered tenancies. That state example shows why a lease clause cannot authorize every proposed response. Check your own jurisdiction before acting.

## What about rental arbitrage requests?

Treat a rental arbitrage request as a proposed change in use that needs a separate decision. Ask for the operating plan before negotiating rent. Higher promised rent does not answer questions about licensing, insurance or possession.

New York City's Office of Special Enforcement says whole apartments in permanent residential buildings cannot be rented to visitors for fewer than 30 days. Its host guidance describes conditions for permitted hosted stays. Landlord consent cannot remove those city restrictions.

Ask the applicant to identify the actual operator and booking channels. Review the leaseholder, guest contact, insurance policy and emergency contact together. Confirm who handles damage, noise, tax registration and complaints. Do not assume platform coverage replaces the owner's policy.

Ask your insurer about the exact proposed use in writing. Check building rules, use permissions and any lender restrictions that apply. Have counsel review the contract before you approve a change. If you decline, record the business and legal reasons without making personal assumptions about the applicant.

## How do I prevent repeat listings?

Prevent repeat listings with clear onboarding, periodic public checks and an accessible approval process. Tell tenants where to ask questions before accepting money from guests. Staff should give the same answer across similar properties unless a documented rule differs.

Keep a register of approved exceptions. State the property, permitted use, dates and person who signed. Store any required approvals beside the agreement. Remove expired exceptions from staff checklists so a past approval does not become an assumed permanent right.

For an existing incident, track the full closeout. Save the tenant response, proof of listing removal and any agreed corrective action. Do not close the file just because a search no longer shows the listing. Record what you checked and when.

Review the clause at renewal with local counsel. Use real disputes to find unclear wording. If staff repeatedly confuse guests with subtenants, fix the instructions. Avoid broad bans that staff cannot explain or apply consistently.

## Wyoming rent and notice rules at a glance

Wyoming has very few statutes on rent and notice. The rules that exist are in Title 34 (leases) and Title 1 (forcible entry and detainer, and the Residential Rental Property Act). We read the official Title 1 and Title 34 PDFs from the Wyoming Legislature on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Statewide rent cap | None in the titles we read | Titles 1 and 34 |
| Rent increase notice | None in the titles we read | Titles 1 and 34 |
| Increase during a fixed term | Only if the lease allows it | The lease |
| Notice to end month-to-month | None set in the titles we read; use the lease | The lease |
| Lease ends, tenant holds over | Tenancy by sufferance only, even if rent is accepted | Wyo. Stat. 34-2-128 |
| Renewal | Only by an express written contract signed by both | Wyo. Stat. 34-2-129 |
| Remove a holdover tenant | 3-day written notice to quit, then an FED case | Wyo. Stat. 1-21-1002(a)(i), 1-21-1003 |
| Change of duties in the Act | Only by explicit written agreement signed by both | Wyo. Stat. 1-21-1202(d) |

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## Does Wyoming have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

The Wyoming statutes we read set no cap on rent increases. Title 1, articles 10 and 12, and Title 34, chapter 2, hold no limit on how much a landlord may charge or raise.

So the size of a Wyoming rent increase depends on the lease and the market. One duty does not change with rent. The Residential Rental Property Act requires the owner to keep the unit safe, sanitary and fit, with working electrical, plumbing, heat, and hot and cold water (Wyo. Stat. 1-21-1202(a) and 1-21-1203(a)).

We did not review every city code. Check the municipal code where the unit is before you act.

## How many days notice does a Wyoming landlord need to raise the rent?

No Wyoming statute we read sets a rent increase notice. The lease decides. That makes the lease clause important. A good Wyoming lease states:

1. **Notice period.** How many days' written notice you give before a rent change.
2. **Method.** How you deliver it: in person, by mail or another agreed way.
3. **Timing.** When a change can take effect, such as only at renewal or at the start of a rental period.

If the lease is silent, give written notice of at least one full rental period before the new rent starts. That is a safe practice, not a statutory rule. It gives the tenant time to decide and gives you a clear record.

**Worked example.** Rent is $1,050 on a month-to-month lease that is silent on notice. You want $1,100 from September 1. Deliver written notice on or before July 31. The tenant then has all of August, one full period, before the change.

| Notice delivered | Full period before change | New rent starts |
|---|---|---|
| July 31 | August 1-31 | September 1 |
| August 5 | September 1-30 | October 1 |

## Can a Wyoming landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

Only if the lease allows it. A fixed-term lease sets the rent for the term. We found no Wyoming statute that lets a landlord change it mid-term without the tenant's agreement.

Wyo. Stat. 1-21-1202(d) supports a written approach. It lets the parties assign any duty or obligation in the Act to a different party, or modify it, only by explicit written agreement signed by both. Use the same discipline for rent changes: put them in a signed writing.

To plan an increase, use one of these:

- **Escalation clause.** A set increase on a set date in the original lease.
- **New lease at renewal.** A new written lease at the new rent.

The renewal step matters in Wyoming. Wyo. Stat. 34-2-129 says an expired lease may be renewed only by an express written contract signed by both parties. A verbal "same terms, new rent" does not renew it.

## How much notice is needed to end a month-to-month tenancy in Wyoming?

The Wyoming statutes we read set no fixed notice to end a month-to-month tenancy. The lease controls. Write the notice period into every periodic lease.

Wyoming law is strict about tenancies that arise by implication. Wyo. Stat. 34-2-128 says no landlord-tenant relation arises by implication or operation of law, except a tenancy by sufferance. When a lease term ends, holding over does not renew it. Your acceptance of rent during the holdover does not renew it either. The tenant is only a tenant by sufferance.

To remove a tenant who holds over, use the FED process:

| Step | Time | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Written notice to quit | At least 3 days before filing | Wyo. Stat. 1-21-1003 |
| File in circuit court | After the notice period | Wyo. Stat. 1-21-1001, 1-21-1002(a)(i) |
| Summons served | 3 to 12 days before trial | Wyo. Stat. 1-21-1004 |
| Writ executed | Within 2 days of receipt, Sundays excepted | Wyo. Stat. 1-21-1013 |

If you want a holdover tenant to stay at a new rent, sign a new written lease. Under 34-2-129, that is the only way to renew.

## Do cities in Wyoming add their own rent or notice rules?

We found no city rent rule in the sources we read, and we did not review every city code. A Wyoming city may still have housing, building or nuisance codes that affect rentals. Check the municipal code where the unit is.

State duties apply in every city. The owner must keep common areas sanitary and reasonably safe. The owner must also maintain electrical, plumbing, heating and hot and cold water (Wyo. Stat. 1-21-1203(a)). If the renter is current on rent and gives written notice of a problem, the owner must act within a reasonable time, or say in writing that the claim is disputed (Wyo. Stat. 1-21-1203(b)).

## Wyoming rent increase checklist

- [ ] Lease states the notice period and method for rent changes.
- [ ] Fixed term: increase only by a written escalation clause or a new written lease.
- [ ] Month-to-month: written notice of at least one full rental period, unless the lease says more.
- [ ] Expiring lease: new written lease signed by both to renew (34-2-129).
- [ ] Holdover tenant: 3-day notice to quit before any FED filing.
- [ ] City code checked for local housing rules.

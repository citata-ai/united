## When is cash for keys cheaper than an eviction?

Cash for keys is cheaper when the offer costs less than the expected cost of an eviction. That cost is mostly lost rent while the case runs, plus court and attorney costs.

Build your own estimate. This table uses example inputs, not market data. Replace each line with your local court fees, your attorney's quote and your rent.

| Cost line | Eviction (example inputs) | Cash for keys (example inputs) |
|---|---|---|
| Rent lost before the unit is empty | 3 months × $1,500 = $4,500 | 1 month × $1,500 = $1,500 |
| Court filing and service | $350 | $0 |
| Attorney | $1,500 | $0 to $300 for a review of the agreement |
| Payment to tenant | $0 | $1,500 |
| Deposit returned as part of the deal | $0 | $500 more than you would have returned |
| **Total** | **$6,350** | **$3,500 to $3,800** |

In this example, the deal saves about $2,500 and 2 months. It also avoids an eviction record on the tenant's file, which may help them agree.

Cash for keys is a poor fit when the tenant has stopped answering, when you suspect damage or crime, or when local law makes the deal hard. Then a court case may be the safer path.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How much should I offer?

Offer less than your expected eviction cost and more than the tenant's cost to move. Start with your numbers from the table above.

A simple way to size the offer:

1. **Estimate your eviction cost.** Months of lost rent plus fees.
2. **Estimate the tenant's moving cost.** A truck, a new deposit, first month's rent.
3. **Set a range.** The low end covers part of the tenant's move. The high end stays well below your eviction cost.
4. **Add non-cash terms.** Waive unpaid rent, return the full deposit, or give a neutral reference.
5. **Set a deadline.** The offer gets smaller or ends after a date.

Make the same kind of offer in the same situations. The Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. 3604(b), bars different "terms, conditions, or privileges" of rental because of race, color, religion, sex, familial status or national origin. Section 3604(f) covers disability.

## What conditions go in the agreement?

A cash for keys agreement must be clear, signed and specific. Use this checklist:

- [ ] Names of all tenants and adult occupants, and the unit address.
- [ ] Move-out date and time.
- [ ] Payment amount, method, and when it is paid.
- [ ] Condition at handover: empty, broom clean, no new damage.
- [ ] All keys, fobs, remotes and garage openers returned.
- [ ] What happens to the security deposit and any unpaid rent.
- [ ] What happens to property left behind.
- [ ] Mutual release of claims, if your attorney advises it.
- [ ] Any pending eviction case: dismissed or settled, and how.
- [ ] Forwarding address for the deposit statement.
- [ ] Signatures and dates of all parties.
- [ ] Local disclosures and cancel language, where required.

## Which cities regulate cash for keys deals?

Some cities treat cash for keys as a "buyout" and set strict rules. Two California examples:

| City | Units covered | Before you negotiate | Tenant's cancel right | Filing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| San Francisco | Rent Ordinance units | Serve the Rent Board's pre-buyout disclosure form (Section 37.9E) | 45 days after all parties sign; any time if the agreement does not comply | File with the Rent Board between day 46 and day 59 |
| Los Angeles | Rent Stabilization Ordinance (RSO) units | Give the RSO disclosure notice (LAMC 151.31) | 30 days after all parties sign; any time if requirements are not met | File with LAHD within 60 days of signing |

San Francisco's form says an agreement to settle a pending eviction case in court "is not considered a 'buyout agreement'". Los Angeles requires cancel language above the tenant's signature line.

Check your own city. Rules like these change, and other cities may have their own.

## When do I pay the cash?

Pay the cash at key handover, after a walk-through. This is the main protection in the deal.

A safe payment sequence:

1. **Walk-through** on the move-out day, with the tenant if possible.
2. **Check the list:** unit empty, keys returned, condition as agreed.
3. **Take dated photos.**
4. **Pay** by cashier's check or electronic transfer, as the agreement says.
5. **Get a signed receipt** and a signed statement of surrender of possession.

If the tenant needs money up front for a truck or a deposit, pay a small part early. Write that the rest depends on handover.

## Is cash for keys a risk?

Cash for keys has risks, but most are easy to control. The main ones:

- **No-show on the date.** The tenant stays. Keep your eviction option open until handover.
- **Property left behind.** Follow your state's rules for abandoned property.
- **Cancellation.** Where local law gives a cancel right, the deal is not final until the period ends.
- **Pressure.** Threats or a deadline that sounds like a lockout can break the law.

Do not use self-help to get the unit back. Florida Statutes 83.67 bars a landlord from cutting utilities or changing locks to keep a tenant out. Texas Property Code 92.0081 bars locking a tenant out "except by judicial process," with narrow exceptions and notice rules.

## Which Licenses Does a New State Require?

Check the destination regulator before offering management services or taking owner money. Do not assume that a license from your home market covers the new work.

California DRE describes a corporate broker licensing process involving qualified broker officers. That is a specific example of entity-level review. It is not a portable permission to work in another state.

Create a compliance file for the exact market. Ask about company and individual licenses, branch registration, business names and supervision. Give local counsel the proposed work, property locations and account flows. Ask which local permits or registrations also apply.

Review the operating documents for the market. Lease forms, management scope, notices and trust procedures need current local checks. A shared brand does not mean a shared legal process. Keep approved market versions clearly named so staff do not choose the wrong form.

Assign a person to maintain the file after launch. Record official source links, advice received and open questions. If a key license or approval is missing, pause the affected offer. A sales deadline should not decide whether the company can lawfully operate.

## Do You Need Local Staff in the New Market?

You need reliable local coverage for work that requires a person at the property. That coverage can take different forms after legal and service review.

Map the work before choosing an office. Showings, inspections, access problems and urgent repairs need specific people and travel plans. Remote staff can coordinate work, but they cannot replace every site visit.

Buildium's expansion guide recommends local partnerships and teams familiar with the market. Use that as a planning prompt rather than proof that opening a staffed office is always the best option.

Create a coverage map by service area and task. Name who performs each visit, who covers absence and how urgent requests reach them. Test access and dispatch with an approved property before selling broad coverage.

Count travel and handoff time in your cost model. Nearby cities can still require difficult routes or long waits for vendors. Avoid promising the same service times across areas until you know the constraints. Ask owners about property access and known defects before accepting the account.

## How Should You Pick the New Market?

Choose a market where verified demand and service economics fit your company. A growing population alone does not prove that owners will hire you.

Start with property types and owner needs you already serve well. Review current local manager offers, published fees and service gaps. Ask prospective owners what they cannot get now. Record those interviews without treating interest as a signed contract.

Use this market review table as a decision tool. The categories are suggested checks, not a scored national ranking.

| Area | Evidence to collect | Reason to delay |
|---|---|---|
| Legal readiness | Regulator guidance and counsel review | Missing operating authority |
| Owner demand | Qualified conversations and proposals | Interest without service fit |
| Local coverage | Named people and tested dispatch | No urgent repair backup |
| Financial plan | Quotes and company cost data | Cash gap without funding |
| Sales offer | Scope, fees and service area | Promises operations cannot meet |
| Management capacity | Named lead and review process | Existing service already strained |

Build the cash plan from current quotes and your own staffing costs. Include slower sales, travel and training. Keep assumptions visible. Use the [fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) for fee scenarios, then add your own actual operating costs.

## Should You Buy a Local Book or Start From Zero?

Compare the purchase of accounts with organic growth by transfer risk, total cost and service readiness. Neither route guarantees retained revenue.

For an acquisition, review agreement assignment, owner consent where needed, records and actual receipts. Have counsel assess transfer terms. Check why owners stay, which staff hold the relationships and whether promised services match your model.

Inspect sample property files and open work orders. Unresolved repairs or weak balances can create work that the purchase summary misses. Ask accounting to reconcile claimed revenue with supporting records. Do not value every listed door as an active paying account.

For organic growth, plan the sales ramp and unused local capacity. A new team may need funding before enough work arrives. Test the service area and offer before adding a large fixed cost.

Use the same cost categories for both options. Include legal review, intake, staff training, owner communication and reserves for unexpected work. Ask what must happen for the plan to stop losing cash. Avoid comparing a purchase price alone with only the advertising cost of organic growth.

## What Should the Market Launch Checklist Include?

Launch through a limited, reviewable intake plan with clear stop conditions. Capacity should set the pace of sales.

- Complete license, entity and local document checks.
- Approve the service area and accepted property types.
- Confirm staff, vendor and emergency coverage.
- Test records, funds and access handoffs.
- Give owners the market-specific scope and fees.
- Assign a lead to review new-account service issues.
- Define when poor service or cash results pause intake.

Use an early owner review to check whether sales promises match delivery. Ask about reporting, repair updates and contact routes. Fix repeated gaps before expanding the boundary.

Keep the home market visible during the test. If experienced staff spend their time rescuing the new office, include that cost. Growth that weakens both markets needs a different pace or scope.

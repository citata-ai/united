Owners rarely fire a manager after one mistake. It is usually a pattern: slow replies, surprise repair bills, statements that do not add up. By the time you decide, you want out fast. Speed is fine. Gaps are not. The risk in a switch is lost rent, a lost deposit record or a tenant who pays the wrong company.

This guide gives a 6-step plan, a handover checklist and a sample tenant letter.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What Does the Termination Clause Usually Require?

The termination clause in your management agreement sets the rules for leaving. Read it before you call the manager. Look for these points:

| Clause item | What to look for | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Notice period | 30, 60 or 90 days, in writing | Sets your end date |
| Termination fee | A flat fee or a share of fees left in the term | Sets your exit cost |
| Cause vs. no cause | Different rules if the manager broke the agreement | May lower the fee |
| Tenant placement fee | Fee for tenants the manager placed, even after you leave | Can surprise you |
| Handover duties | Records, deposits, keys, timing | Gets your records back on time |
| Final accounting | When you get the last statement and funds | Closes the books |
| Notice method | Email, mail, certified mail | A wrong method can delay the end date |

If the agreement is silent or unclear, ask a local attorney before you send notice.

## How Do You End the Contract Step by Step?

1. **Pick the end date.** Count the notice period from the day of delivery. Avoid the 1st of the month, when rent comes in.
2. **Line up the next step.** Sign with a new manager, or set up to self-manage, before the end date.
3. **Send written notice.** Use the method in the agreement. Keep proof of delivery.
4. **Ask for the handover list.** Send the checklist below with your notice.
5. **Tell the tenants.** Send the letter after the end date is fixed.
6. **Close the books.** Check the final statement and deposit ledger against your records.

Keep the tone calm and short. A sample notice line: "Under section [X] of our agreement dated [date], I give notice to end the agreement on [end date]. Please send the items on the attached list by that date."

## What Records and Deposits Must the Old Manager Hand Over?

The old manager must hand over everything that belongs to you and your tenants. Deposits come first. Florida Statutes 83.49(7) says that upon a change in the designated rental agent, all deposits and advance rents must move to the new agent. They must come with any earned interest and an accounting for each tenant. Washington RCW 59.18.270 has a similar rule when the landlord role passes to another.

Use this handover checklist:

- ☐ Tenant security deposits and advance rent, with a ledger per tenant
- ☐ Signed leases, addenda and renewal letters
- ☐ Rent ledgers for each tenant, with balances due
- ☐ Move-in inspection reports and photos
- ☐ All keys, fobs, gate codes and lockbox codes
- ☐ Open work orders and pending repair quotes
- ☐ Vendor list, warranties and appliance records
- ☐ HOA contacts, rules and any open violations
- ☐ Utility accounts in your name, and any in the manager's name
- ☐ Insurance certificates the manager held
- ☐ Any legal notices sent or court files open
- ☐ Final owner statement and the reserve balance

Taxes need one more item. The IRS Form 1099-MISC instructions say the property manager must report rent paid over to the owner on Form 1099-MISC. If you switch mid-year, expect a 1099 from each manager for the months each one paid you.

## How Do I Tell the Tenants?

Tell each tenant in writing, with the new details and the date they take effect. In Florida, Statutes 83.50 says the person named to receive notices keeps that authority until the tenant is told otherwise. It says notices of changes must go to the tenant's home, to another address the tenant named in writing, or by email under 83.505. Other states have their own disclosure rules.

Timing matters. Send the letter so tenants have it before the next rent date. Tell them clearly where to pay, and that their lease and deposit stay the same.

## What Does a Letter Announcing a New Property Manager Say?

A good letter is short and answers the tenant's 5 questions: who, when, where to pay, who to call, and what stays the same. Here is a template. Edit it for your state's rules.

```text
Date: [date]

Dear [tenant name],

Starting [effective date], [new manager name] will manage your home at
[property address]. [Old manager name] will no longer act for me after
[last day].

What stays the same:
- Your lease, rent amount and due date do not change.
- Your security deposit of $[amount] moves to [new manager name].

What changes:
- Pay rent starting [first rent date] to: [payment method or address].
- For repairs, contact: [phone], [email], [portal link].
- For emergencies after hours, call: [phone].
- Send legal notices to: [name and address].

Please do not send rent to [old manager name] after [last day].

Thank you for your patience during this change.

[Owner name]
[Owner phone or email]
```

Send it by the method your lease and state law allow, and keep a copy.

## How Do You Keep Rent Flowing During the Switch?

Rent gets lost in the gap between managers. Close the gap with these steps:

- **End on a mid-month date** so rent for the month is already in.
- **Ask the old manager to forward** any rent it gets after the end date.
- **Check each tenant's ledger** in the first month with the new manager.
- **Keep bills paid.** Confirm who pays the mortgage, HOA and utilities in the gap.
- **Keep one log** of every call and email with the old manager.

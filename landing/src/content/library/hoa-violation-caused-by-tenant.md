## Why does the HOA fine the owner?

An HOA may address the owner because the owner has duties under the association documents and state law. That does not mean every notice is valid. Start by identifying the association type, the cited rule and the person charged.

Florida Statutes section 720.305 requires members and their tenants, guests and invitees to comply with applicable association rules. The statute also permits enforcement against those groups. It does not support a blanket claim that only an owner can face an HOA action.

California Civil Code section 5855 addresses member discipline and charges for certain common-area damage caused by a member, guest or tenant. The text provides a notice and hearing process. The California rule should not be used as a national procedure.

Use this review table when a notice arrives:

| Question | Record to collect | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which rule applies? | Current rule and governing document | Confirms the claimed duty |
| What happened? | Dated photos and complaint details | Tests the facts |
| Who received notice? | Notice and delivery record | Supports process review |
| Can the issue be fixed? | Written correction request | Helps prevent repeat conduct |
| Who may owe money? | HOA notice, lease and local-law review | Separates the possible claims |

Forward the notice promptly to your rental manager. Ask the manager to confirm receipt and the next action. Do not let an unread portal message decide the outcome.

## Can you pass the HOA fine to the tenant?

Pass a fine to the tenant only after the facts, lease and applicable law support reimbursement. The HOA's decision does not settle your separate landlord-tenant claim. Obtain advice before adding a disputed charge to the tenant ledger.

Start with cause. Did the tenant park in a banned space, or did an owner-controlled condition cause the notice? A landscape problem may involve an unclear maintenance clause. A wrong vehicle plate may involve a basic factual error.

Next, review the lease. Identify the duty to follow HOA rules and any lawful charge provision. Check whether the tenant received the relevant rule. Ask counsel whether the amount, collection method and supporting notice meet local requirements.

Do not rename a fine as rent merely to improve collection options. Do not assume a security deposit can cover the charge. Deposit and enforcement rules depend on local law and the agreement. Keep the HOA charge separate until the review resolves the correct treatment.

If reimbursement is justified, give the tenant a clear statement. Include the rule, event, evidence, final amount and payment instructions. Show any reversal or reduction. Avoid adding unexplained handling fees to a disputed amount.

If the owner caused the problem, correct the owner-side process. That may mean updating a parking registration or completing required work. A charge against the wrong party can make the dispute harder to resolve.

## What should the lease say about HOA rules?

The lease should explain the tenant's rules, the owner's duties and how notices move between them. Ask a local attorney to prepare an addendum for that property. A generic clause may miss the association's actual requirements.

Give the attorney the current declaration, rules, rental restrictions and approval process. Identify shared facilities and any access devices. Ask which documents should accompany the lease and how to record delivery.

The addendum should clearly address these topics:

- **Name the documents.** Identify the rules by title or version so the tenant knows which set applies.
- **Set notice routes.** Tell the tenant where to send an HOA notice and how to report a disputed claim.
- **Assign routine tasks.** State who handles parking registration, bins, yard care and access devices where relevant.
- **Review reimbursement.** Use only terms that local law permits, with evidence and a clear charge process.
- **Explain changes.** State how the owner will provide lawful rule updates during the tenancy.

California DRE's Property Management chapter recommends a written agreement that clearly states the owner's and manager's responsibilities. Apply the same clarity to your instruction to the rental manager. Confirm who keeps the rules current and sends notices.

Do not assume the HOA manager handles lease compliance for you. Ask your rental manager to explain that part of the service in writing. Include any extra administrative cost in the ownership budget.

## How should you handle repeat violations?

Handle repeat violations with clear evidence, consistent communication and lawful next steps. A repeated complaint deserves review, not an automatic escalation. Confirm that each event occurred and that the tenant had a fair chance to understand the rule.

Keep a case log with the event, evidence, notice, reply and correction. Distinguish a new event from a continuing condition. Ask the HOA for a final written decision before treating a proposed fine as final.

State procedures differ. Florida Statutes section 720.305 includes a notice and hearing process for fines. California Civil Code section 5855 includes a member's opportunity to address the board and cure provisions. Have counsel apply the current local process.

Set a specific correction request that the tenant can follow. A request to stop parking across a marked access lane is clearer than a demand for better behavior. Confirm the correction with a dated record.

If the issue continues, have a local attorney review the lease remedies and notices. Do not use a lockout, utility shutoff or informal removal threat. Ask which lawful route fits the facts before sending an enforcement notice.

Review the service cost with your manager. The [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) can help you compare fees, but it cannot decide a tenant charge. For the next notice, collect the rule, lease and evidence before approving payment or reimbursement.

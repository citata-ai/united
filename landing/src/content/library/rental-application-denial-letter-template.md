## How should a landlord deny a rental application in a fair and legal way?

A landlord should deny a rental application in writing, for a reason from written screening criteria. Add an adverse action notice when a consumer report played a part. Those 3 steps cover most of the risk. A phone call that says "it didn't work out" covers none of it.

Follow these steps for each denial:

1. **Check the criteria.** Confirm the denial reason is in your written screening criteria.
2. **Check the consistency.** Confirm you applied the same rule to other applicants.
3. **Check the source.** Did a consumer report (credit, eviction or background report) play any part? If yes, you need an adverse action notice.
4. **Send the letter.** Use the template below.
5. **File the record.** Keep the application, the criteria, the report summary and a copy of the letter.
6. **Dispose of the report.** Shred or securely delete the report when you no longer need it.

### Copy-ready denial letter template

**Subject: Your rental application for [unit address]**

Date: ________

Dear ________,

Thank you for your application for [unit address, unit number], received on ________. After review, we are not able to approve your application at this time.

**Reason (from our written screening criteria):** ________

**Notice of adverse action**

Our decision was based in whole or in part on information in a consumer report from:

- Consumer reporting agency: ________
- Address: ________
- Phone (toll-free if a nationwide agency): ________
- Website: ________

The consumer reporting agency did not make this decision and cannot tell you the specific reasons for it.

You have the right to get a free copy of your report from the agency if you ask within 60 days of this notice. You also have the right to dispute with the agency any information in the report that is not correct or not complete.

**Credit score (delete if no score was used)**

- Your credit score: ________
- Range of possible scores for this model: ____ to ____
- Date the score was created: ________
- Source of the score: ________
- Key factors that hurt your score, most important first: 1. ________ 2. ________ 3. ________ 4. ________

If you have questions about this decision, contact ________ at ________.

Sincerely,
________, [title], [company]

This is a generic template, not a state legal form. Check your local law before you use it.

## When is an adverse action notice required?

An adverse action notice is required when you take an unfavorable action based in whole or in part on a consumer report. The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) page "Using consumer reports: what landlords need to know" gives these examples of adverse action:

| Action | Adverse action? | Notice needed if a report played a part? |
|---|---|---|
| Deny the application | Yes | Yes |
| Require a co-signer | Yes | Yes |
| Require a deposit other applicants do not pay, or a larger one | Yes | Yes |
| Charge higher rent than another applicant | Yes | Yes |
| Approve on standard terms | No | No |

The FTC says the notice is required even when the report was only a minor factor. The FTC also says oral notice is allowed, but written notice is the best practice. A written letter gives you proof of what you sent and when.

A consumer report includes more than a credit report. The FTC says "tenant background check reports are consumer reports" under the FCRA (Fair Credit Reporting Act). Its examples: a credit bureau report, a rental history report from a tenant screening company, and a criminal history report from a background check company.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What must the letter say about the credit report?

The letter must give 4 facts about the consumer report, plus the credit score if you used one. These come from 15 U.S.C. 1681m(a), the section of the FCRA for users of consumer reports.

1. **The agency.** The name, address and phone number of the consumer reporting agency. Use a toll-free number for an agency with nationwide files.
2. **The decision.** A statement that the agency did not make the decision and cannot give the specific reasons.
3. **The free report.** The applicant's right to a free copy of the report from the agency if they ask within 60 days.
4. **The dispute right.** The applicant's right to dispute wrong or incomplete information with the agency.

If you used a credit score, the FTC says to give the score and a description of it (source, date and range). Also list the key factors that hurt the score, ranked by importance. Your screening company often supplies these facts in its report. Copy them into the letter exactly.

Watch for: a screening company that gives you only a "pass" or "fail" result. What to do: ask the company for the agency name, contact data and score details before you send the notice.

## How do you avoid fair housing problems?

You avoid fair housing problems by using written criteria, the same way, for every applicant. The federal Fair Housing Act bans discrimination based on race or color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status and disability (U.S. Department of Justice). Your state or city may add more protected classes, so check the local list too.

Use this check before each denial:

- [ ] The reason is in the written criteria you gave the applicant
- [ ] The criteria are the same for every unit at that property
- [ ] Other applicants with the same facts got the same result
- [ ] The letter does not mention any protected class
- [ ] The letter does not mention children, a disability, a religion or an accent
- [ ] Any request for a reasonable accommodation was logged and answered
- [ ] The file shows who decided and on what date

Write criteria that you can measure. "Income of at least 2.5 times the rent" is measurable. "Good fit for the building" is not, and it invites a claim.

A worked example of an income rule. The rent is $1,600 and the rule is 2.5 times rent. The minimum monthly income is $1,600 × 2.5 = $4,000. An applicant with $3,700 a month in income does not meet the rule. Another applicant with the same income must get the same result, unless your written criteria allow an exception (such as a co-signer) for everyone.

Check your local law on income rules before you set the ratio.

## What should you keep in the file after a denial?

Keep the application, the written criteria, a summary of the decision and a copy of the letter. The file is your proof that you applied the same rules to everyone.

Do not keep the full consumer report longer than you need it. The FTC says you must securely dispose of consumer reports and the facts taken from them when you are done. Shred, burn or pulverize paper. Delete digital files so nobody can read or rebuild them.

| Keep | Dispose of when done |
|---|---|
| Application form | Full consumer report |
| Written screening criteria | Copies of ID documents you no longer need |
| Decision summary and date | Report printouts on desks |
| Copy of the denial letter | Old screening emails with report data |

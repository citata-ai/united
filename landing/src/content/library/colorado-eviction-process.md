## Colorado eviction rules at a glance

A Colorado eviction is a forcible entry and detainer (FED) case under article 40 of title 13 of the Colorado Revised Statutes (C.R.S.). HB24-1098 added for-cause rules in article 12 of title 38 on 2024-04-19. We read the statute text and the signed acts on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Unpaid rent notice | 10 days to pay or leave (5 days exempt agreement, 3 days employer housing) | C.R.S. 13-40-104(1)(d) |
| CARES Act covered dwelling | 30-day notice to vacate for unpaid rent | C.R.S. 38-12-1502; 15 U.S.C. 9058(c) |
| Lease violation notice | 10 days to comply or leave | C.R.S. 13-40-104(1)(e) |
| Repeat violation | Termination 10 days after notice | C.R.S. 13-40-104(1)(e.5) |
| Substantial violation | Termination 3 days after notice | C.R.S. 13-40-107.5(4) |
| Nuisance or negligent damage | 10 days to stop or leave | C.R.S. 13-40-104(1)(j) |
| No-fault eviction | At least 90 days' written notice | C.R.S. 38-12-1303(3) |
| Summons court date | 7 to 14 days after the summons issues | C.R.S. 13-40-111(1) |
| Pay to stop the case | Until judgment for possession | C.R.S. 13-40-115(4) |
| Writ issue | Not before 48 hours after judgment | C.R.S. 13-40-122(1)(a) |
| Writ execution | Not before 10 days after judgment (30 days for some benefit recipients) | C.R.S. 13-40-122(1)(b) |
| Writ expiry | 49 days after issue | C.R.S. 13-40-115(3) |
| Lockout or utility shutoff | Illegal; actual damages plus the greater of 3 × rent or $5,000 | C.R.S. 38-12-510 |

## What notice must a Colorado landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

A Colorado landlord must serve 10 days' written notice that demands the rent or possession of the unit (C.R.S. 13-40-104(1)(d)). Two shorter periods exist:

- **Exempt residential agreement: 5 days.** This is a lease of a single-family home by a landlord who owns 5 or fewer single-family rentals and who says in the lease that the 10-day period does not apply (C.R.S. 13-40-104(5)(b)).
- **Employer-provided housing: 3 days.**

A lease may not waive the notice. A late fee is not rent for this notice, so leave late fees out of the demand (C.R.S. 38-12-105(7)).

Federal law can make the notice longer. HB25-1240 added C.R.S. 38-12-1502 on 2025-05-29. It requires every landlord who files for nonpayment to follow 15 U.S.C. 9058(c), as enacted on 2020-03-27. For a "covered dwelling" (a unit in a covered federal housing program, or on a property with a federally backed mortgage loan, as 15 U.S.C. 9058(a) defines it), the tenant cannot be made to leave until 30 days after the notice to vacate.

The notice must meet C.R.S. 13-40-106, as amended by HB24-1098:

1. **In writing,** with the grounds and a specific description of the violation.
2. **Describe the premises** and the date and time to deliver them up.
3. **Signed** by the landlord, an agent or an attorney.
4. **State the mediation right** for a tenant who gets SSI, SSDI or Colorado Works cash aid.
5. **In English, Spanish or the tenant's primary language,** if the landlord knows or should know it.

Serve it by hand to the tenant, or to a household member over 15 who lives there. If no one is home after attempts on 2 separate days, you may post it in a clear place on the unit (C.R.S. 13-40-108).

## Can a Colorado landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

A Colorado landlord can evict for a lease violation, but most landlords can no longer evict with no cause. Since 2024-04-19, C.R.S. 38-12-1303 says a landlord may not serve a notice to end a tenancy or file an eviction unless a listed cause exists.

Fault causes include unpaid rent, a material lease violation, a repeat violation, a substantial violation as defined in C.R.S. 13-40-107.5, and nuisance or negligent damage.

No-fault causes, each with at least 90 days' written notice that states the legal and factual basis:

| No-fault cause | Key condition |
|---|---|
| Demolition or conversion | Proof such as a permit or permit application |
| Substantial repairs | Not repairs needed for habitability; return offer if under 180 days |
| Landlord or family moves in | Move in within 3 months; no similar vacant unit in the building (45 days' notice for active-duty military landlords) |
| Sale of the home | Single-family, townhome, duplex, triplex or condo unit |
| Tenant refuses a new lease | The new lease must have reasonable terms |
| History of late rent | Late more than 2 times in the lease, each over 10 days with a proper notice |

Most no-fault grounds apply only at the end of the lease term. The for-cause rules do not apply to some cases (C.R.S. 38-12-1302):

- Short-term rentals.
- A unit where the owner lives in the same single-family home, duplex or triplex, or next door.
- Employer-provided housing.
- A tenant who has not been a tenant for at least 12 months.

For those exempt cases, a landlord can end a periodic tenancy with written notice under C.R.S. 13-40-107. The notice is 91 days for a tenancy of 1 year or more, 28 days for 6 to 12 months, and 21 days for 1 to 6 months.

If a landlord breaks the for-cause rules, the tenant can raise it as a defense. If the landlord cannot prove compliance, the court must dismiss the case (C.R.S. 38-12-1306).

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Colorado, and what does it cost?

A Colorado landlord files a residential eviction in county court (C.R.S. 13-40-111(4)(c)). The clerk or the landlord's attorney issues a summons. The summons sets an appearance date not less than 7 and not more than 14 days after it issues (C.R.S. 13-40-111(1)).

The court cannot enter a default judgment for possession before close of business on the appearance date. Either side may appear in person or by phone or video. Court records stay private until the court grants possession.

The county clerk charges a filing fee, and the sheriff or a process server charges for service. The amounts change, so check your county court clerk before you file. A residential landlord who wins gets attorney fees only if the lease has an attorney fee clause (C.R.S. 13-40-123).

## How long does an eviction take in Colorado?

A Colorado eviction for unpaid rent takes at least about 4 to 5 weeks with no delays. Here is the minimum statutory path for a standard 10-day case:

| Step | Days (minimum) | Running total |
|---|---|---|
| 10-day demand served | 10 | Day 10 |
| File; summons sets court date | 7 to 14 | Day 17 to 24 |
| Judgment for possession | 0 | Day 17 to 24 |
| Writ may issue | 2 (48 hours) | Day 19 to 26 |
| Earliest removal | 10 after judgment | Day 27 to 34 |

Add time for a CARES Act unit (30-day notice), a contested hearing, mediation, or a tenant on SSI, SSDI or Colorado Works. For those tenants, the writ cannot be executed for 30 days after judgment. That 30-day rule does not apply to a substantial violation case or to a landlord with 5 or fewer single-family homes and 5 or fewer total units (C.R.S. 13-40-122(1)).

The tenant can stop a nonpayment case at any time before judgment. The tenant pays all amounts in the notice plus any rent now due, to the landlord or to the court. The court then vacates any judgment and dismisses the case with prejudice (C.R.S. 13-40-115(4)).

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Colorado?

A law officer removes the tenant under a writ of restitution, and the county sheriff's office may execute it (C.R.S. 13-40-122(1)(c)). The officer may act only in daytime, between sunrise and sunset. The writ stays in effect for 49 days after it issues, then it expires (C.R.S. 13-40-115(3)).

The officer must check the unit for pets. If the tenant is not there, animal control takes the pets (C.R.S. 13-40-122(2.5)). The landlord has no duty to store the tenant's property. A landlord who stores it may charge reasonable storage costs (C.R.S. 13-40-122(3) and (4)).

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Colorado?

No. A Colorado landlord may not remove or exclude a tenant without court process (C.R.S. 38-12-510). This includes a willful utility shutoff and the removal of doors, windows or locks. Narrow exceptions exist for mutual consent, a clear abandonment, and a drug lab cleanup.

The penalty is high. The tenant gets actual damages plus the higher of 3 times the monthly rent or $5,000, and attorney fees and costs. The court may also put the tenant back in the unit.

Worked example: rent is $1,400. 3 × $1,400 = $4,200, which is less than $5,000. So one lockout costs at least $5,000 plus actual damages and fees.

## What notice must a North Dakota landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

A North Dakota landlord must give 3 days' written notice of intention to evict before filing for unpaid rent. N.D. Cent. Code 47-32-02 sets this rule. It applies to cases under subsections 4, 5, 6 and 8 of 47-32-01.

Subsection 4 covers unpaid rent. The eviction action is available when a tenant "fails to pay rent for three days after the rent is due". So two separate rules apply:

1. **The rent is 3 days late.** That makes the action available (47-32-01(4)).
2. **You gave 3 days' written notice.** You must do this "before proceedings can be instituted" (47-32-02).

The statute does not say if the notice can run during the first 3 late days. Ask a local attorney before you set your dates.

How to serve the notice: 47-32-02 says you serve it the same way as a summons. If the tenant cannot be found, a sheriff or a process server may post the notice in a clear place on the premises.

## How long does an eviction take in North Dakota?

A North Dakota eviction has short statutory steps, but the total time depends on the court and on service. The statute gives these limits:

| Step | Statutory time | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Rent late | 3 days after the due date | 47-32-01(4) |
| Notice of intention to evict | 3 days | 47-32-02 |
| Appearance date | 3 to 15 days after the summons issues | 47-32-02 |
| Hardship stay after judgment | Up to 5 days | 47-32-04 |

A worked example, on paper only. Rent is due on the 1st. The rent is 3 days late at the end of the 4th. Say you serve the notice on the 5th and it runs 3 days. You file on the 8th or 9th, and the summons sets an appearance 3 to 15 days later. The hearing falls about 11 to 24 days after the due date. A 5-day stay can follow. Then the sheriff serves the execution.

Real cases often take longer. The clerk's calendar, failed service and a contested hearing all add days.

Service timing matters too. Personal service in the county must happen at least 3 days before the appearance. Service elsewhere, or by another mode, must happen at least 7 days before (47-32-02). If a sheriff or process server cannot find the tenant, 47-32-02 allows posting on the door. That needs an attempt between 6 p.m. and 10 p.m., an affidavit, and a copy mailed to the last-known address.

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in North Dakota, and what does it cost?

A North Dakota landlord files an eviction case in the proper district court. N.D. Cent. Code 47-32-01 says so. The case has limits under 47-32-04:

- You may join only claims for rents and profits, or for damages from the tenant's possession.
- The tenant may not bring a counterclaim, except as a setoff against your claim for damages or rent.
- In a mobile home park case, the tenant may raise a defense under 47-32-01.1.

## Can a North Dakota landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes, a North Dakota landlord can evict for some lease violations and can end a month-to-month tenancy without a reason. The grounds in 47-32-01 include:

- **Material lease breach.** The tenant violates a material term of the written lease (47-32-01(8)). The 3-day notice applies.
- **Disturbance.** The tenant, or a guest, acts in a way that unreasonably disturbs other tenants' peaceful enjoyment (47-32-01(7)). Subsection 7 is not in the 3-day notice list in 47-32-02. A judgment based on a disturbance of the peace also gets no hardship stay (47-32-04).
- **Holdover.** The tenant stays after the lease or the term ends (47-32-01(4)). The 3-day notice applies.

No cause: a fixed-term lease ends at the end of its term (47-16-14). For month-to-month, either party may end the tenancy with at least 1 calendar month's written notice (47-16-15(2)). A longer period applies if the parties agreed to it in writing. A tenant who stays past the end date holds over.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in North Dakota?

A sheriff removes the tenant after a North Dakota eviction judgment. When the landlord wins, the court "shall enter judgment that the plaintiff have immediate restitution of the premises" (47-32-04). If the tenant shows substantial hardship, the court may stay the special execution for up to 5 days.

N.D. Cent. Code 28-21-06 says the execution is "directed and delivered to a sheriff". For possession, the execution describes the property and the party entitled to it. The sheriff must then "deliver the property to the party entitled to the property".

After removal, 47-16-30.1 covers what the tenant leaves behind. If you remove property after the judgment and the service of the special execution, you have a lien for reasonable storage and moving costs. For abandoned property worth up to $2,500, you may dispose of it without legal process after 28 or more days.

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in North Dakota?

North Dakota law sends possession disputes through the court, not through a lockout. The statutes we read give a landlord no right to change locks or cut utilities to force a tenant out.

Several rules point the same way:

- **Forced removal is itself a ground for eviction.** Under 47-32-01(2), the action lies against a party who "turns out by force, threats, or menacing conduct the party in possession".
- **Quiet possession.** A lease binds the lessor to secure the tenant's quiet possession during the term (47-16-08).
- **Essential services.** A landlord must supply running water, reasonable hot water and reasonable heat, with a few exceptions (47-16-13.1(1)(f)).
- **Removal by a sheriff.** Possession after a judgment goes through an execution served by a sheriff (28-21-06).

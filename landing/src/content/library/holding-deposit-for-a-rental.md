## What is a holding deposit?

A holding deposit is money an approved applicant pays so you take the unit off the market until the lease is signed. It protects you from holding a unit for someone who then walks away. It protects the applicant from losing the unit to someone else.

A holding deposit is not:

- **A screening fee.** That pays for the background check.
- **Rent.** Rent starts with the lease.
- **A waiting list fee.** Washington bans fees to be placed on a waiting list (RCW 59.18.253(1)).

Take a holding deposit only after you approve the applicant. Oregon ORS 90.297(2) allows a deposit to secure the signing of a rental agreement "after approving the applicant's application but prior to entering into a rental agreement."

## What do state laws say?

State laws differ a lot. Some set caps and refund rules. Many say nothing specific, and then the written agreement and general contract law control.

| Rule | Washington (RCW 59.18.253) | Oregon (ORS 90.297) |
|---|---|---|
| When allowed | After the unit is offered to the applicant | After approval, before the rental agreement |
| Cap | 25% of the first month's rent | No cap in this section |
| Paperwork | Receipt plus written conditions for keeping it, at payment | Written statement of rent, fees, deposits, terms and refund conditions |
| If the tenant moves in | Credit to first month's rent or security deposit | Apply to moneys due, or refund immediately |
| If the applicant backs out | Landlord may keep up to the full amount, per the written conditions | Landlord may keep it |
| If the landlord backs out | Return under the statute | Return within 4 days |
| Penalty | See the statute | Applicant may recover the deposit plus $150 |

Washington adds a rule for applicants with rental assistance. A landlord may not keep any of the deposit if the unit fails a tenant-based rental assistance program inspection (RCW 59.18.253(4)(c)).

In California, Civil Code 1950.5 limits "security, however denominated." Most landlords may not take more than one month's rent as security, in addition to the first month's rent. Ask a local attorney how a holding deposit fits inside that limit.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How much is a normal holding deposit?

A normal holding deposit is a part of one month's rent, set within any state cap. In Washington, the cap is 25% of the first month's rent.

**Worked example (Washington):** rent is $2,000 a month.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| First month's rent | $2,000 |
| Maximum holding deposit (25%) | $500 |
| Applied at move-in | Credit $500 to first month's rent or security deposit |

In states with no cap, pick an amount that covers a few weeks of lost rent if the applicant backs out. Too high an amount scares off good applicants. Too low an amount does not protect you.

Use the same amount for every applicant for the same unit. Different amounts for different applicants can create Fair Housing risk.

## When can I keep a holding deposit?

Keep a holding deposit only when the applicant backs out under the written terms. Do not keep it when you change your mind, when the unit is not ready, or when you cannot sign the lease by the agreed date.

Common written terms:

- **The applicant has [number] days** to sign the lease.
- **If the applicant does not sign** by that date, you may keep the deposit.
- **If you do not offer the lease** as agreed, you return the deposit within the time your state sets. Oregon sets 4 days.
- **If the tenant signs,** the deposit is credited to rent or the security deposit.

Oregon shows the cost of a mistake. Under ORS 90.297(6), an applicant can recover the deposit plus $150 if the landlord does not follow the section.

## Is a holding deposit part of the security deposit?

A holding deposit becomes part of rent or the security deposit when the tenant moves in. Washington RCW 59.18.253(4)(a) requires the landlord to credit it to the first month's rent or to the security deposit. Oregon ORS 90.297(3) requires the landlord to apply it to moneys due or refund it immediately.

Write the credit into the lease and the move-in ledger. If you add it to the security deposit, the security deposit rules of your state then apply to that money.

## Do I need a written holding deposit agreement?

Yes. A written agreement is required in some states and wise in all of them. Washington requires a receipt and a written statement of the conditions at the time of payment. Oregon requires a written statement before you take the deposit.

**Sample holding deposit agreement (check against your state law):**

> Applicant: [name]. Unit: [address]. Monthly rent: $[amount]. Security deposit: $[amount]. Other fees: [list].
>
> Holding deposit received: $[amount] on [date], by [method].
>
> The landlord will hold the unit and not accept other applications until [date and time].
>
> If the applicant signs the lease by that date, the holding deposit will be credited to [first month's rent / security deposit].
>
> If the applicant does not sign the lease by that date, the landlord may keep the holding deposit.
>
> If the landlord does not offer the lease on these terms by that date, the landlord will return the full holding deposit within [number] days.
>
> Signed: [applicant], [landlord], [date].

Fill each bracket. Give the applicant a copy and a receipt at the time of payment.

## What is the step-by-step process?

1. **Approve the applicant** in writing.
2. **Give the written terms** before you take any money.
3. **Take the deposit** by a method you can trace.
4. **Give a receipt** at once.
5. **Mark the listing** as pending and stop showings.
6. **Sign the lease** by the agreed date.
7. **Credit the deposit** at move-in, or return or keep it under the terms.

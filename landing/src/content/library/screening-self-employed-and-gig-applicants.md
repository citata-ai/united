## Which documents show self-employed income?

Use documents that show both the source and current level of earnings. A self-employed applicant may have invoices, contracts, platform statements, bank records and tax filings instead of pay stubs.

Ask the applicant which records best show the work. Then request the documents your reviewed policy needs. Avoid collecting a full business archive when a narrower set can answer the question.

The IRS describes Schedule C as the form used to report profit or loss from a sole proprietorship. Its instructions distinguish gross receipts, expenses and net profit or loss. That makes it useful evidence, but not a landlord approval formula. [Read the IRS Schedule C guide](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-schedule-c-form-1040).

A platform payout statement may show payments after some fees. A business bank statement may show gross receipts alongside transfers. Ask what each field means before combining totals.

Record which documents support your decision. Keep the sensitive originals in restricted storage and put only the necessary conclusion in the general application record.

## How many months of bank statements should I request?

Set a consistent review period rather than choosing a different document demand for each applicant. The period is a policy choice subject to local law, not a national legal minimum.

Use enough history to understand the actual income pattern. Seasonal work can need a broader view than a steady recurring contract. Explain any additional request by the missing information, not by the applicant's job label.

Publish the standard evidence options before collecting documents. Tell applicants what they may redact and which fields must remain visible. Do not request passwords or unrestricted access to unrelated accounts.

If the records include personal transfers, ask for clarification rather than counting them as sales. Mark a disputed entry while you review it. Avoid treating every unfamiliar payment description as fraud.

Keep a consistent escalation route. Staff should know when a short history, new business or mixed wage and business income needs review. They should not invent a stricter threshold during the call.

## Do tax returns help with screening?

Tax returns help explain past income and business structure, but they do not prove that current work continues. Use them with current evidence where your policy calls for it.

IRS Schedule C instructions show that gross receipts and net profit are different figures. Do not compare gross platform revenue with a wage earner's documented income without explaining your method. [Read the Schedule C instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040sc).

| Evidence | Useful question | Important limit |
|---|---|---|
| Tax schedule | What income did the business report? | Historical period |
| Bank statement | What payments actually arrived? | May contain transfers and loans |
| Current contract | What work is agreed? | Future payment may be uncertain |
| Paid invoice | What completed work produced revenue? | Needs matching payment evidence |
| Platform statement | What did the service pay out? | Fee and period definitions vary |
| Accountant letter | What did the accountant review? | Scope may be narrow |

The table is a recommended evidence map. It does not make an applicant's tax treatment a rental rule.

Do not make tax filing judgments during the review. If the documents need expert interpretation, ask for a suitable explanation. The applicant's adviser handles their tax position.

Keep other household members' sensitive information out of the file when it is unnecessary. A joint return can contain details unrelated to the rental decision.

## How do I handle uneven monthly income?

Review the income pattern under a written method that you can apply consistently. A strong month and a weak month need context, not an automatic assumption about reliability.

Define the review period and which income counts. Distinguish recurring receipts from a one-time sale. Record business expenses and reserves according to your reviewed method without inventing deductions.

Avoid double counting the same payment. A paid invoice, platform statement and bank deposit can all describe the same earnings. Link them as evidence of a single receipt.

Ask the applicant to explain a major change. A new contract or a seasonal cycle can explain why recent statements differ from the tax return. Keep the explanation and supporting proof together.

Use a lawful process for alternative evidence or a guarantor when appropriate. Do not change the terms merely because the applicant works independently. Apply the same affordability goal with documents suited to the income source.

## How do I keep the decision fair and secure?

Use criteria tied to the tenancy and protect the information you collect. Do not judge an applicant by stereotypes about their work or household.

Federal Fair Housing law, including 42 USC 3604, prohibits covered housing discrimination. Have counsel review your criteria and any local source-of-income protections. [Read the federal provision](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/3604).

The FTC explains that adverse decisions based partly on consumer reports require notice. If you use a reporting service alongside direct income evidence, keep the sources distinct in the decision record.

Use this recommended review sequence:

- **Share criteria.** Explain the lawful standard and evidence options.
- **Collect relevant proof.** Use secure access and allow appropriate redaction.
- **Match payments.** Connect invoices or platform records with actual receipts.
- **Explain variation.** Record seasonal patterns and current changes.
- **Review the result.** Use the same decision process as other applicants.
- **Send required notices.** Keep the report source and decision basis clear.

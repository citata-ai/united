## Which blog topics bring owner leads?

Blog topics bring owner leads when an owner, not a tenant, asks the question. Most property management blogs mix the two. A post on "how to break a lease" gets traffic, but the reader is a renter who will never hire you.

Owner leads are a small part of all site traffic. Goodjuju's 2026 study counted only owner forms, not tenant forms or calls. Across 35 companies, 141,908 visits in the fall window turned into 1,262 owner leads, a 0.9% average rate (Goodjuju, 2026). So a blog post must pull the right reader, not just more readers.

Search habits are also moving. For the Fridman Properties site, search demand for 7 generic property management terms fell 46% in the Chicago area over one year (Google Keyword Planner, our data). We think AI search is part of the cause, but that is a hypothesis. Fridman Properties is a related party: its owner is CEO of MyRemotely, our partner.

## Which topics attract owners, not tenants?

Topics attract owners when they pass a 3-question test:

1. **Does an owner make this decision?** Rent price, repairs, screening rules, hiring a manager.
2. **Does the owner pay for the answer?** Fees, taxes, repairs, vacancy.
3. **Does the next step lead to you?** A rental analysis, a fee table, a call.

If a topic fails 2 of the 3, move it to your resident pages.

| Topic | Owner or tenant? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| How much does a property manager cost in [city]? | Owner | Owner pays, next step is your fee page |
| How to get my security deposit back | Tenant | Tenant asks, no owner decision |
| What rent can my 3-bedroom get in [city]? | Owner | Owner sets the price, next step is a rental analysis |
| How to file a maintenance request | Tenant | Tenant help, belongs in the portal |
| Should I sell or rent my house? | Owner | Owner decision, next step is a rent-vs-sell call |
| Best apartments near [school] | Tenant | Renter search |

## 24 owner topics by funnel stage

Use these as a start. Put your city and real numbers into each one.

| Stage | Owner state of mind | Topics |
|---|---|---|
| Early | "Should I rent this home at all?" | Rent or sell your house in [city]; what an accidental landlord must do first; rental income and costs for a [city] single-family home; how long homes sit empty in [city]; landlord insurance basics; what a rental license costs in [city] |
| Middle | "Can I do this myself?" | Self-managing vs hiring a manager; how to screen tenants in [state] without breaking Fair Housing rules; late fee rules in [state]; security deposit rules in [state]; eviction timeline in [county]; what repairs a landlord must make in [state] |
| Late | "Which manager should I hire?" | How much a property manager costs in [city]; what a leasing fee covers; questions to ask a property manager; how to switch property managers; what a management agreement should say; how managers handle Section 8 tenants |
| Decision | "Is this company right for me?" | Our fees, line by line; what happens in your first 30 days with us; how we price rent; our owner protections and cancel terms; homes we manage in [neighborhood]; what our owner statement shows |

Late and decision topics bring fewer readers but better leads. Early topics bring more readers and build trust. A small company should start with late and decision topics.

## Which local law topics work?

Local law topics work when each rule has a source an owner can check. Owners search for rules on deposits, late fees, notices, evictions, rental licenses and inspections. Many of these rules differ by state, and some by city.

Rules for a law post:

- [ ] Name the statute section or the city code section for each rule.
- [ ] Link the official text: the state legislature site or the city site.
- [ ] Put a summary table at the top: rule, limit or deadline, source.
- [ ] Add the date you checked the law.
- [ ] Add this line: "This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney."
- [ ] Set a reminder to check the post each year, or when the legislature meets.

Do not copy a rule from another manager's blog. Their rule can be old, or for a different state.

## How do I find questions owners ask?

Find owner questions in 4 places, and keep the exact words.

1. **Google Search Console.** The Performance report shows the queries that bring clicks and impressions to your pages (Google Search Console Help). Filter for words like "landlord", "owner", "rent my house", "fee" and your city.
2. **Your own calls.** Ask the person who takes owner calls to write down each question for 2 weeks. These are the questions that come right before a signed contract.
3. **AI answers.** Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude "What should I know before I hire a property manager in [city]?" Note the sub-topics each answer covers and the sources it cites. In our test of 150 AI answers about property managers in 50 cities, reddit.com was a source in 22 (our data, 2026-10-19).
4. **Owner forums.** Read owner threads on Reddit and BiggerPockets for your city. Look for questions that repeat.

Put the questions in one sheet: question, source, stage, your page that answers it. A question with no page is a topic.

## Which topics fit the bottom of the funnel?

Bottom-of-funnel topics are the ones an owner reads right before a call. They are about you, your price and your process. They convert well because the reader has already decided to hire a manager.

The best ones:

- **Fee breakdown.** Every fee, with a worked yearly example. See the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) for the math.
- **Your rent process.** How you set rent, with the data sources you use.
- **First 30 days.** Each step from signed agreement to first rent payment.
- **Switching managers.** What you do, what the owner does, how long it takes.
- **Owner protections.** Guarantees and cancel terms, in plain words.

AI answers can cite these posts too. Google says AI features need no special markup. Its advice is to make "important content available in textual form" and keep pages findable "through internal links" (Google Search Central, updated 2025-12-10). In Goodjuju's fall data, AI tools sent 0.8% of visits but 3.4% of owner leads (Goodjuju, 2026). That is a small sample of 43 leads, so treat it as a sign, not a rule.

## How do I check a post before it goes live?

Run this list on each post.

- [ ] The first sentence answers the title question.
- [ ] Each number and each rule has a source and a date.
- [ ] The post passes the 3-question owner test.
- [ ] It links to one decision page: fees, rental analysis or services.
- [ ] It says nothing that steers renters by a protected class.
- [ ] A person on your team checked every fact.

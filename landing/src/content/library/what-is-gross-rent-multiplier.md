## What is the gross rent multiplier?

The gross rent multiplier (GRM) is a rental property's price divided by its gross rent. It tells you how many years of gross rent it takes to equal the price. Wikipedia's entry defines it as "the ratio of a property's purchase price to its annual gross rental income."

Investors like GRM because it needs only 2 numbers: the price and the rent. Both are in almost every listing. So GRM lets you sort 20 listings in 10 minutes and pick the 3 worth a closer look.

GRM is a screen, not a verdict. Wikipedia notes that analysts "treat the GRM as a first-pass filter rather than a conclusive valuation tool."

## How do you calculate GRM?

You calculate GRM by dividing the property price by its gross annual rent.

**GRM = property price ÷ gross annual rent**

A worked example. A fourplex lists for $360,000. Each unit rents for $750 a month.

1. Monthly gross rent: 4 × $750 = $3,000.
2. Annual gross rent: $3,000 × 12 = $36,000.
3. GRM: $360,000 ÷ $36,000 = 10.

Some appraisers and investors use monthly rent instead. Then GRM = $360,000 ÷ $3,000 = 120. Wikipedia says "either convention is valid provided the basis is stated consistently." An annual GRM of 10 and a monthly GRM of 120 describe the same deal. Always check which one a broker or report uses.

Use gross scheduled rent from current leases, or market rent if the units are empty. Do not mix the two across properties you compare.

### Using GRM to estimate value

You can turn GRM around to estimate value from rent:

**Estimated value = gross annual rent × market GRM**

Say 3 similar fourplexes in the same area sold this year:

| Sale | Price | Gross annual rent | GRM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comp A | $420,000 | $38,400 | 10.9 |
| Comp B | $455,000 | $40,800 | 11.2 |
| Comp C | $398,000 | $36,600 | 10.9 |
| Average | | | 11.0 |

The math for Comp A: $420,000 ÷ $38,400 = 10.9. The average of the 3 GRMs is about 11.0.

Your subject fourplex earns $42,000 a year. Estimated value: $42,000 × 11.0 = $462,000. That is a starting point for a talk with an appraiser or broker, not a price.

## What is a good gross rent multiplier?

There is no single good gross rent multiplier. GRM depends on the market, the property type, its age and local rents. A GRM that is cheap in one city may be expensive in another.

Use these rules of logic instead of a fixed number:

| GRM vs local comps | What it may mean | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Lower than comps | Cheaper per dollar of rent | Deferred repairs, high taxes, high vacancy, rough area |
| Same as comps | Priced like the market | Expenses and condition |
| Higher than comps | You pay more per dollar of rent | Newer building, low expenses, rent below market with room to grow |

A lower GRM is better only if expenses are normal. A building with a GRM of 8 and a failing roof may cost more than one with a GRM of 11 and a new roof.

## What is the difference between GRM and cap rate?

GRM uses gross rent, and cap rate uses net operating income (NOI). That one difference changes what each number can tell you.

**Cap rate = NOI ÷ property price**

| Feature | GRM | Cap rate |
|---|---|---|
| Income used | Gross rent | Net operating income |
| Counts vacancy | No | Yes |
| Counts operating expenses | No | Yes |
| Data needed | Price and rent | Price, rent, vacancy, all expenses |
| Speed | Minutes | Hours |
| Best use | First screen of many listings | Compare finalists and price an offer |
| Higher number means | More expensive per dollar of rent | Higher return per dollar of price |

Wikipedia notes that because GRM ignores differences in operating expense ratios, 2 properties with the same GRM can have different cap rates.

A worked example. Two fourplexes each sell for $360,000 with $36,000 gross rent. Both have a GRM of 10.

| Line | Fourplex 1 | Fourplex 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Gross rent | $36,000 | $36,000 |
| Vacancy (5%) | − $1,800 | − $1,800 |
| Operating expenses | − $12,600 | − $18,000 |
| NOI | $21,600 | $16,200 |
| Cap rate (NOI ÷ $360,000) | 6.0% | 4.5% |

Same GRM, but Fourplex 1 earns $5,400 more a year. The difference is all in expenses: taxes, insurance, utilities or repairs. GRM cannot see it.

The 5% vacancy above is a sample input. For context, the U.S. Census Bureau rental vacancy rate was 7.3% in Q2 2026 (FRED series RRVRUSQ156N). Your market will differ.

## What are the limits of GRM?

GRM has 6 main limits. Each one is a reason to move to NOI and cap rate before you buy.

1. **No expenses.** GRM ignores taxes, insurance, utilities, repairs and management fees.
2. **No vacancy.** GRM assumes all rent is collected.
3. **No concessions.** Free months and discounts do not show. Wikipedia lists this limit too.
4. **No capital needs.** A roof or HVAC system near the end of its life does not show.
5. **No debt.** GRM says nothing about the mortgage payment or debt service coverage.
6. **One year only.** GRM ignores rent growth and the time value of money.

Use GRM to cut a long list to a short one. Then, for each finalist, build an income statement: gross potential rent, vacancy, other income, operating expenses, NOI. Ask the seller's property manager for 12 to 24 months of actual operating statements.

## Why do owners decide to sell?

Ask the owner why they plan to sell instead of assigning a reason from a broad market story. The stated goal helps you decide what support is useful and whether the home may remain a rental.

Possible reasons to discuss include a need for cash, a change in investment plans or concern about the property's costs. These are intake prompts, not a ranked list of proven sale causes. Record the owner's answer in their own words.

Separate a decision to sell from a complaint you can resolve. An owner may still sell after you fix a service problem. Do not make continued management the condition for providing accurate records.

| Question for the seller | Why it helps | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Is the sale decision final? | Distinguishes planning from active sale | Agree the contact schedule |
| Will the home be marketed occupied? | Identifies access and record needs | Coordinate with the authorized agent |
| Is there an identified buyer? | Sets the handover timing | Ask about an introduction |
| Does the buyer want a rental? | Shows whether management fits | Offer a separate discussion |
| Who handles closing questions? | Avoids conflicting instructions | Name the authorized contacts |

Prepare a factual property file: current lease, payment history, open work and account detail. Confirm what you may share and with whom. Do not send resident personal data to every interested party.

Ask what the owner wants from your team during the sale. Put extra work and any charges in the agreed scope before starting.

## Can I refer the sale to an agent partner?

An agent introduction may help the owner, but referral compensation and licensed activities need a local review. Do not promise a referral payment before checking the rules that apply.

TREC's property manager guidance shows why task boundaries matter. In Texas, paid showing or leasing for an owner requires a license. That example does not resolve sale referral rules in every state; ask the responsible broker about the actual arrangement.

Choose an agent based on the property's needs and the owner's choice. Explain any relationship or proposed compensation clearly. Avoid presenting a preferred partner as the only way to receive cooperation from your company.

Agree who handles sale questions, rental questions and resident contacts. The sale agent should not have to guess whether your team approved access or a statement about the lease. Keep relevant instructions in writing.

Provide checked records rather than a sales promise. If you prepare operating figures, state the period and source. Distinguish collected income from scheduled rent and actual bills from estimates.

Have counsel or the broker review any referral agreement. Confirm permitted parties, disclosures and payment conditions. A business opportunity does not remove licensing or conflict concerns.

Keep the resident's service route active during the sale. The sales process should not leave urgent maintenance reports between two teams with no owner.

## How should I pitch management to the buyer?

Offer the buyer a service proposal based on the home and their plan. Do not assume the seller's signed agreement binds the buyer or fits the buyer's needs.

Start with questions about ownership structure, rental goals and expected closing date. Confirm who has authority to engage your company. Ask whether the buyer plans to occupy, renovate, hold or resell the home.

Show the work your team already knows: lease administration, open maintenance, vendor history and reporting. Share accurate records through an approved route. Avoid suggesting that your existing knowledge removes the need for the buyer's own review.

Give the buyer a clear fee and service schedule. Use the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) to explain recurring fee calculations with the proposed terms. Keep leasing, inspections and special sale work separate.

Describe the transition steps and the person responsible. The buyer should know what happens before closing, at the effective handover and afterward. Do not charge or act without the required authority.

Ask for a decision date that fits the closing plan. If the buyer declines, prepare a clean transfer. A useful proposal does not guarantee that the account remains with your company.

## Can the tenant remain after the sale?

Whether the tenant remains depends on the lease, local law, sale facts and any applicable protections. Do not tell the seller or buyer that a sale automatically ends the tenancy.

Collect the signed lease and amendments before discussing options. Ask local counsel to review the effect of the sale, required notices, access rules and deposit handling. This guide does not set a national termination or transfer deadline.

Identify who will receive rent and handle maintenance after the change. Coordinate authorized notices so residents do not receive competing instructions. Confirm the payment route before you ask residents to change it.

Reconcile funds and open obligations as part of the transition. Buildium's owner distribution guide recommends checking unpaid invoices, reserves and holds before calculating cash available. Apply that discipline to the closing account.

Do not treat a deposit as sale income or a management fee. Ask counsel and accounting staff how the required transfer and accounting work locally. Keep proof of the transfer and the underlying ledger.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What should I measure after a sale?

Measure sale-related account outcomes separately from service-related cancellations. That distinction helps you see whether buyer outreach works without treating every sale as a service failure.

Track the seller's notice, buyer introduction, proposal, decision and final handover. Record whether the home remains a rental and whether your service area still fits. Use the same definitions across cases.

Do not count an account as retained merely because the buyer has not yet chosen another manager. Use a defined event, such as an effective signed agreement, for your internal measure.

Review why a buyer accepted or declined. A change to owner occupancy tells a different story from unclear fees or slow follow-up. Keep those reasons visible in the review.

Start with owners who have mentioned a sale. Ask about the plan, confirm what help they need and prepare the record file before closing creates a rushed handover.

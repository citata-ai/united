## What goes in each tier?

Each tier answers a different owner worry. The bottom tier covers the work every door needs. The middle tier adds speed and convenience. The top tier adds cover for the big risks: no rent, damage and eviction.

On the DoorGrow podcast (episode 230), host Jason describes a "three tier hybrid pricing model". The idea is to serve at least 3 types of owner, based on how much risk and cost each one wants to carry. One flat 10% fits nobody well.

Robert C. White & Company in Connecticut shows this on its public pricing page (read 2026-10-19):

| Feature | Gold, 10% ($150 min) | Platinum, 12% ($180 min) | Total Assurance, 14% ($210 min) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leasing (1 month fee) | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Online rent, reports, owner portal | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Repair coordination, 24/7 emergency | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Animal damage protection ($1,000) | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Fast pay, bill pay | No | Yes | Yes |
| Eviction protection program | No | Yes | Yes |
| Rent loss protection | No | No | Yes |
| Damage protection ($5,000) | No | No | Yes |
| Insurance claim oversight | No | No | Yes |

Percent is of gross rents, plus tax, with the monthly minimum in brackets.

A simple rule for your own tiers:

- **Bottom tier:** everything you must do well on every door. Rent, books, repairs, leasing, legal notices.
- **Middle tier:** services that save the owner time or speed up money. Faster payouts, bill pay, an eviction program, included renewals.
- **Top tier:** promises that move risk from the owner to you. Rent loss and damage cover. Price these only after you model their cost.

## How many tiers should I offer?

Offer 3 tiers. Both examples in this guide use 3 management plans, and both also sell a separate leasing-only service.

| Number of tiers | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | Simple, but you lose owners who want less service or more cover |
| 2 | Owners split "cheap vs full" and many pick cheap |
| 3 | A clear middle choice, easy to compare |
| 4 or more | Owners compare line by line and delay the decision |

Keep leasing-only out of the tier table. Robert C. White shows "Lease Only" at 100% of the first month's rent as a separate card. That keeps the management choice clean.

## How do I price the middle tier?

Price the middle tier as a small step up from the bottom, with a clear gain in value. Then check that each step pays for the services it adds.

Two published ladders:

| Company | Bottom | Middle | Top |
|---|---|---|---|
| Robert C. White & Company (CT) | 10%, $150 min | 12%, $180 min | 14%, $210 min |
| Legacy Property Management (Sacramento) | Basic 5.9%, $99 min | Classic 7.9%, $149 min | Complete 9.9%, $199 min |

The Legacy figures come from Utopia Management's review of published Sacramento rate cards, checked 2026-09-30. Legacy also ties renewals to the tier: $299 on Basic, $199 on Classic, included on Complete. That is a good model. The renewal price itself pushes owners up a tier.

Here is a worked check for the middle tier. The inputs are examples, not market data.

| Line | Value |
|---|---|
| Rent | $2,000 per month |
| Bottom tier fee at 10% | $200 per month |
| Middle tier fee at 12% | $240 per month |
| Extra revenue per door | $40 per month, $480 per year |
| Your cost of added services (example: faster payouts, bill pay, eviction program) | $15 per door per month |
| Margin on the step | $25 per door per month |

Run the same check on the top tier. Rent loss and damage cover can cost you thousands on one bad tenancy. Price that tier from your own loss history, or buy cover from an insurer and pass the cost through.

Watch for: monthly minimums change the real rate on low-rent doors. At $1,200 rent, a 10% plan with a $150 minimum is really 12.5%. You can model this with the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## How do I show the tiers on my website?

Show all 3 tiers on one public page, side by side, with exact numbers. Owners compare managers by price first, and AI answers quote what they can read.

A page layout that works:

1. **One card per tier**, in order from left to right: bottom, middle, top.
2. **Price line first**: percent or flat fee, plus the monthly minimum.
3. **Leasing fee** on each card, even if it is the same.
4. **A checklist of what is included**, with the same rows in each card.
5. **A mark on the middle tier**, such as "most chosen", only if it is true.
6. **A link to the full terms** for every guarantee or protection.
7. **A date** that shows when you last updated the prices.

Use the same row names in every card. If "eviction protection" is in row 6 of the top card, put "Eviction protection: no" in row 6 of the bottom card. Owners scan across.

Text matters for AI search too. A price inside an image is not readable to AI engines. Write the prices in plain HTML text. For more on how AI reads fee pages, see [how owners choose a property manager with AI](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai).

## Do tiers reduce owner churn?

We found no public study that measures owner churn by tier. Do not claim that tiers cut churn until your own data shows it.

What you can do is measure it. Track these 4 numbers each quarter:

- **Mix:** the share of doors on each tier.
- **Churn by tier:** cancellations per 100 doors per year.
- **Upgrades and downgrades:** how many owners change tier, and in which direction.
- **Revenue per door by tier:** management fee plus extra fees.

If top-tier owners cancel less, you have a case to move more owners up. If they cancel more, check whether you deliver what the top card promises.

## Common mistakes with tiered packages

Most tier problems come from a vague middle or a top tier you cannot afford to honor.

- **Too many add-ons in the bottom tier.** Owners then see no reason to move up.
- **Protections with hidden conditions.** Link every protection to its full terms.
- **A top tier priced from hope.** Model the payout cost before you publish it.
- **Different words on each card.** Owners cannot compare and they stop reading.
- **No date on the page.** Owners and AI engines cannot tell if the price is current.

Start with your current single plan. Split it into a core plan and a plan with 2 or 3 owner favorites. Add the top tier when you know what a bad year costs you.

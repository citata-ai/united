## Which NYC apartments are rent stabilized?

Rent stabilized apartments in NYC are most often in buildings of 6 or more units built before 1974. That is the test the city's Tenant Support Unit gives. Almost half of all NYC rental apartments are rent stabilized, the same page says.

HCR (NYS Homes and Community Renewal, the state rent agency) adds detail in Fact Sheet 1:

- **Building age:** stabilization generally covers buildings built after 1947 and before 1974.
- **Former rent control:** it also covers apartments that left rent control.
- **Tax programs:** buildings with J-51, 421-a and 421-g benefits are covered, under each program's own rules.
- **Older units:** rent control is the older system and generally applies to buildings built before 1947.

The NYC Rent Guidelines Board (RGB) says about 1 million units fall under the Rent Stabilization Law.

How to check a unit: the lease may say so. A tenant can also order the rent history from HCR. As an owner, you file a registration within 90 days after a unit first becomes stabilized. After that, you file each year with the April 1 rent and give each tenant a copy (HCR Fact Sheet 1).

## What did the 2019 HSTPA change for NYC landlords?

The Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act (HSTPA) took effect on June 14, 2019. It changed how stabilized rents rise and how units leave the system. Here is what HCR and the city say it changed:

| Before HSTPA | After June 14, 2019 | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A unit could leave stabilization above a rent threshold | High-rent deregulation repealed | HCR Fact Sheet 1 |
| A unit could leave on high tenant income plus high rent | High-income deregulation repealed | HCR Fact Sheet 1 |
| Vacancy leases had a statutory vacancy increase | No statutory or RGB vacancy rate | HCR Fact Sheet 1 |
| Overcharge look-back of 4 years | 6 years, with exceptions | HCR Fact Sheet 1 |
| Preferential rent could end at renewal | Kept while the tenant stays, if in place on or after June 14, 2019 | NYC Tenant Support Unit |
| MCI and IAI formulas | Changed, with "strict limitations" | HCR Fact Sheet 8 |

MCI means a major capital improvement, a building-wide upgrade. IAI means an individual apartment improvement. Both still allow a rent increase, but usually only with HCR approval (HCR Fact Sheet 8).

What this means for you: a stabilized unit now stays stabilized, no matter how high the rent goes. The city notes one exception for some 421-a buildings. Plan your building budget on the RGB increase, not on a future exit from the system.

## How does the Rent Guidelines Board set yearly increases?

The NYC Rent Guidelines Board sets one yearly increase for 1-year leases and one for 2-year leases. The rates apply to leases that start on or after October 1 each year (HCR Fact Sheet 1).

The RGB holds public meetings and hearings each year. Its staff studies operating and maintenance costs, financing costs, housing supply and the cost of living (RGB home page). The board then votes on the new rates.

Recent orders:

| Order | Leases starting | 1-year lease | 2-year lease |
|---|---|---|---|
| Order 57 | 2025-10-01 to 2026-09-30 | 3% | 4.5% |
| Order 58 | 2026-10-01 to 2027-09-30 | 0% | 0% |

Order 58 was adopted on June 25, 2026. The tenant picks the 1- or 2-year term at renewal (HCR Fact Sheet 1).

A worked example. A stabilized unit has a legal rent of $2,000. Under Order 57, a 2-year lease signed in March 2026 went up 4.5%, to $2,090. Under Order 58, a renewal that starts in November 2026 stays at $2,000. If your costs rise 4% in that year, the gap comes out of your margin.

## Which NYC buildings does the 2024 Good Cause Eviction law cover?

Good Cause Eviction is a New York State law in effect since April 20, 2024. It is mandatory in New York City (HCR notice, under Real Property Law 212). It covers many market-rate units, which means units with no other rent regulation.

In a covered unit, you need a "good cause" reason to evict, to refuse a renewal, or to end a tenancy (NYC HPD). The reasons include nonpayment, a lease breach, a nuisance, illegal use, refusal of access, and a planned move-in by you or a close family member. Some reasons, such as demolition or taking the unit off the market, need "clear and compelling evidence" in court.

The law also limits rent increases in an indirect way. A court can find a rise unreasonable if it is above the local rent standard: inflation plus 5%, with a 10% cap. HPD says the NYC standard is 8.38% as of May 4, 2026. HCR bases it on a 3.38% rise in the New York-Newark-Jersey City CPI from 2024 to 2025.

Use this check before a renewal:

- [ ] Current rent × 1.0838 = the highest rise that HPD calls reasonable. On $2,000, that is $2,167.76.
- [ ] A rise above 5% needs a Good Cause notice, 30, 60 or 90 days before, by length of tenancy.
- [ ] Your notice says whether Good Cause applies, and why or why not.
- [ ] A rise above 8.38% has a written reason, such as large repairs or a tax increase.
- [ ] You use the HPD or HCR template notice.

The notice rule started on August 18, 2024. It also applies to a new or renewal lease, a non-renewal, a 14-day rent demand and an eviction petition (NYC HPD).

## Which small buildings are exempt from Good Cause?

Good Cause does not cover every NYC rental. NYC HPD lists the exemptions. The ones that matter most to an owner:

| Exemption | Rule |
|---|---|
| Small landlord | You own 10 units or fewer in total in New York State. Your own home does not count. |
| LLC owner | Each person who owns part of the LLC owns 10 units or fewer in the state. |
| Owner-occupied building | 10 or fewer units, and you live in the building. |
| New building | Built on or after January 1, 2009. Coverage starts 30 years after the certificate of occupancy. |
| Condo or co-op | Units in condo and co-op buildings. |
| High rent | Rent above 245% of fair market rent. |
| Already regulated | Rent stabilized, rent controlled, NYCHA, project-based Section 8 and similar units. |

The 245% figures come from HCR's yearly notice. For all five NYC counties, the July 2026 notice sets $7,130 for a 2-bedroom and $6,505 for a 1-bedroom. HCR built that table on an older HUD fair market rent, $2,910 for a 2-bedroom. HUD's FY2027 figure for the New York metro is $2,971. See our [New York fair market rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/new-york-fair-market-rent-2027). The legal limit is HCR's number, not your own math.

Watch for: if you claim the small-landlord exemption in an eviction case, you must give the tenant a list of all units you own in New York State (NYC HPD). Keep that list current.

## What should a NYC owner do now?

Sort each unit first, then apply the rules for that unit type.

1. **Classify each unit.** Mark it stabilized, rent controlled, Good Cause or exempt. Write down the reason.
2. **File HCR registrations.** Do it each year for every stabilized unit, with the April 1 rent.
3. **Price renewals by type.** Use 0% for stabilized leases from October 2026. Use the 8.38% check for Good Cause units.
4. **Send the right notice.** Use the template for every renewal, non-renewal and rise above 5%.
5. **Check your fees.** Since June 11, 2025, the NYC FARE Act stops your broker from charging the tenant a fee (NYC DCWP).

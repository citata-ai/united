## What money goes into a trust account?

A trust account holds every dollar the manager collects for someone else. Buildium's dictionary names the main types: tenant security deposits, rent payments and owner funds. Money set aside for repairs belongs there too.

Typical trust account money:

- **Rent** collected from tenants, before the owner draw.
- **Security deposits**, held for the tenant until move-out.
- **Owner reserves**, kept for repairs and bills.
- **Owner contributions**, sent in to cover a large repair.
- **Prepaid rent** and other tenant credits.

The manager's own fee becomes company money only when the manager earns it and moves it out. California's 10 CCR 2832 says trust funds must go to the owner, a neutral escrow or a trust account within 3 business days after receipt.

The agreement should also say who gets any interest. Arizona's ARS 32-2173 requires the agreement to state how interest on trust money is handled.

## Can a property manager mix trust funds with operating funds?

No. Mixing trust funds with company money is called commingling, and real estate rules forbid it. Buildium calls commingling a serious but common mistake.

California's 10 CCR 2835 shows how narrow the exceptions are. A broker may keep up to $200 of the broker's own money in the trust account to pay bank service charges. A few other exceptions exist, for example when broker money must be split out within 25 days. Everything else stays separate.

Signs of commingling an owner can spot:

- Vendor bills for the manager's office paid from the trust account.
- Owner draws paid from the company's operating account.
- Management fees taken before rent arrives.
- One owner's reserve used to pay another owner's repair.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What is a three-way reconciliation?

A three-way reconciliation is a monthly check that 3 records match on the same date. The North Carolina Real Estate Commission names the 3 parts:

1. **Bank statement.** The balance the bank shows, adjusted for deposits in transit and outstanding checks.
2. **The books.** The trust account journal or check register.
3. **Trial balance.** The sum of every owner and tenant ledger, which is what the manager owes to clients.

The NCREC gives the bank-to-books formula: ending bank balance + deposits in transit − outstanding checks = book balance. The book balance must then equal the trial balance.

Worked example for one trust account on October 31. The numbers are an example.

| Record | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Bank statement balance | From the bank | $84,600 |
| Plus deposits in transit | Rent deposited October 31 | + $3,200 |
| Minus outstanding checks | 2 vendor checks not cashed | − $1,450 |
| **Adjusted bank balance** | $84,600 + $3,200 − $1,450 | **$86,350** |
| **Book balance** | Trust journal | **$86,350** |
| Owner ledgers | 14 owners | $52,350 |
| Tenant deposit ledgers | 30 tenants | $34,000 |
| **Trial balance** | $52,350 + $34,000 | **$86,350** |

All 3 match at $86,350, so the account reconciles. If they do not match, find the cause before you pay any owner draw. NCREC lists common causes: errors, bank charges, interest and timing.

California's 10 CCR 2831.2 requires this reconciliation at least once a month, except in months with no activity. It also requires a record of each one, with the account, the date and the amount owed to each beneficiary.

## Do all states require a trust account?

Hemlane's state guide says all 50 states require trust accounts for licensed brokers and property managers who hold other people's money. The rules differ by state. Buildium notes that some states require interest-bearing accounts or limit how long funds can stay.

What changes from state to state:

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Deposit deadline | California: 3 business days after receipt (10 CCR 2832) |
| Reconciliation | California: at least monthly (10 CCR 2831.2) |
| Broker money allowed | California: up to $200 for bank fees (10 CCR 2835) |
| Agreement terms on interest | Arizona: the agreement must say how trust interest is handled (ARS 32-2173) |
| Record keeping | Buildium: records are generally kept 3 to 5 years |

An owner who manages only their own rental is a different case. Hemlane says most states do not require a separate trust account for an owner's own rent. Deposit laws still apply.

## What happens if a manager misuses trust funds?

Misuse of trust funds puts the license at risk and can lead to civil or criminal penalties, Buildium says. The NCREC bulletin warns that skipped reconciliations can lead to missing client funds and loss of the real estate license.

What an owner can do:

- [ ] Ask your manager which bank holds the trust account and in whose name.
- [ ] Ask for the date of the last three-way reconciliation.
- [ ] Check that your security deposits appear on your statement as held funds.
- [ ] Check that the draw arrives on the same day each month.
- [ ] If numbers do not match, ask in writing and keep the reply.

Your monthly owner statement is the first place to see a problem. If you compare managers, ask each one how they reconcile the trust account. For more owner and manager guides, see [/guides](/guides).

## Which best-of sites does AI use for property managers?

AI engines lean on a small set of best-of list sites when an owner asks for the best property manager in a city. We asked ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini "What is the best property management company in [city]?" for the 50 largest US cities. That gave 150 answers (our data, 2026-10).

These list and directory sites were sources in the most answers:

| Site | Type | AI answers that cited it (of 150) |
|---|---|---|
| Expertise.com | Editor-picked city lists | 93 |
| iPropertyManagement | Directory and reviews | 50 |
| Yelp | Reviews | 50 |
| ThreeBestRated | Top 3 per city | 21 |
| TrustAnalytica | City lists | 17 |
| BestInHood | City lists | 14 |
| AmericaTop10 | City lists | 5 |
| HomeGuide | City lists and costs | 5 |

Expertise.com stands out. It was a source in 62% of answers. The full table by city and engine is in our [AI Property Manager Index 2026](/research/ai-property-manager-index-2026).

Our data matches a wider pattern. Ahrefs studied 750 prompts in ChatGPT and 26,283 source URLs. "Best X" blog lists were 43.8% of all cited page types (Ahrefs, December 2025). Ahrefs also found that brands ranked higher on third-party lists were more likely to appear in ChatGPT answers.

## How do best-of list editors choose companies?

Best-of list editors publish their criteria, and you can meet most of them. Read each site's method page before you contact it.

**Expertise.com** describes 5 steps on its "Our Selection Process" page. It says it researches more than 60,000 businesses each month.

1. **Availability.** It builds a list of local providers from public databases and customer referrals, and checks service areas.
2. **Qualifications.** It checks licenses, accreditations and awards.
3. **Reputation.** It scores the number of review sites that cover you, your average score and how much your ratings swing.
4. **Experience.** It picks finalists by main area of work, range of services and years in business.
5. **Professionalism.** Mystery shoppers call each company as potential customers.

**ThreeBestRated** uses what it calls a 50-Point Inspection. The groups include business history, services, hours, website, social media, reviews, complaints and legal standing, licenses, awards and pricing clarity. One point is that the address on the site must match online listings.

What this means for you:

- **Reviews on more than one site.** Expertise.com counts how many review sites cover you. So reviews on Google, Yelp and BBB count for more on this check than reviews on Google alone.
- **Steady ratings.** A rating that swings from 5 stars to 1 star and back hurts on "volatility".
- **A license on the site.** Put your broker or property management license number on your About page, if your state requires one.
- **A clear service list.** Name each service on its own page or section.
- **Answer the phone.** Expertise.com's mystery shoppers call. A missed call or a vague answer can cost you the spot.

## Are pay-to-play lists worth it?

Pay to advertise only after you know a list drives owners, and never pay for a ranking you did not earn. Read each site's own words on payment first.

| Site | What the site says about payment |
|---|---|
| Expertise.com | A provider that passes review and selection "may choose to advertise on the page" for more visibility. |
| ThreeBestRated | Listing is free. "Businesses cannot pay to be featured." It calls this a "No Pay to Play" policy. |

For any other list, ask 4 questions before you pay:

1. **Is the ranking separate from the ad?** Ask in writing.
2. **Does AI cite this list for your city?** Run the prompt and check the sources.
3. **How many owners came from it last year?** Ask other managers on the list.
4. **Can you cancel monthly?** Avoid 12-month contracts for an unproven list.

A list that no AI answer cites and no owner mentions is a weak buy, at any price.

## How do you check your rank on a best-of list?

Check your rank by running the owner's prompt, opening each list the AI cites and finding your name. Do this for each city you serve.

1. **Run the prompt.** Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity: "What is the best property management company in [city]?"
2. **Open the sources.** Click each best-of list in the answer.
3. **Record your place.** Write down the list, the date and your position, or "not listed".
4. **Read the companies above you.** Count their reviews and years in business. Note what they show that you do not.
5. **Repeat each quarter.** Lists change often.

Ahrefs found that 79.1% of dated lists that ChatGPT cited were last updated in 2025, and 26% in the previous two months (Ahrefs, December 2025). A list that skipped you in the spring may have a new edition by fall.

## What should you not do to get on a best-of list?

Do not buy fake reviews, and do not ask staff or family to post reviews. Expertise.com and ThreeBestRated both look at review quality and rating patterns. ThreeBestRated says it checks review authenticity and reviewer credibility.

Do not publish a page that ranks your own firm first under a neutral-sounding title. Ahrefs found self-promoting lists are common in software, but it also flags trust concerns. Owners pick a manager for years, and a false "independent" list damages trust when they spot it.

Do not chase every list site. In our data, 2 sites, Expertise.com and ThreeBestRated, cover most best-of citations. Start there, then fix your Yelp and Google profiles. Our guide on [how landlords use AI to choose a property manager](/guides/how-owners-choose-a-property-manager-with-ai) shows where these lists fit in the owner's search.

We cannot promise you a place on any list or in any AI answer. Editors and AI engines change their picks. You can control the facts they check: reviews, license, services and a phone that someone answers.

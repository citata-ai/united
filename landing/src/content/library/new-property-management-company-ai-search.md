## Why is AI search hard for a new property management company?

AI search is hard for a new company because AI answers lean on listings and reviews that a new company does not have yet. On 2026-10-19 we asked ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini "What is the best property management company in <city>?" for 50 US cities. That gave 150 answers (our data, 2026-10).

What the answers cited:

| Source type | Share of the 150 answers |
|---|---|
| A listing or directory site | 88% |
| At least one company website | 70.7% |
| Expertise.com | 62% (93 of 150) |
| Yelp | 33% (50 of 150) |
| iPropertyManagement | 33% (50 of 150) |

The engines differ. ChatGPT cited a listing site in 96% of its answers and a company site in only 30%. Gemini cited a company site in 96% of its answers. So a new company needs both: correct listings and a site with clear facts.

## What can you do before you have reviews?

You can fix every fact that does not depend on reviews. That is most of the work. Use this order.

1. **Pick one name and one phone number.** Use them on the site, the listings and the license. AI joins facts by name, so one spelling matters.
2. **Write your facts down once.** Name, address or service area, phone, license number, cities served, door count range, fees. Keep this as one text file. Copy from it every time.
3. **Publish a fee page.** Owners ask AI what a manager costs. A full fee table with real numbers gives AI a fact to quote. See the [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) for the fee types owners compare.
4. **Publish one page per real service city.** Write a real local answer on each, not the same text with a new city name.
5. **Publish your owner terms.** Cancel notice, guarantees and what is included.
6. **Allow the crawlers.** Check that robots.txt and your firewall let Googlebot, Bingbot and the AI crawlers in.

To appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, a page must be "indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet" (Google). Google also says you do not need special AI files or schema. So the first job is plain: make sure Google and Bing can index your pages.

## Which listings go first?

Set up Google Business Profile first, then Bing Places, Yelp and BBB. After that, work toward the directories that pick companies on their own.

| Order | Listing | Why | Can you add yourself? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Google Business Profile | Your listing in Google local results and Maps | Yes, if eligible |
| 2 | Bing Places | Your listing in Bing search and Bing Maps | Yes |
| 3 | Yelp | Cited in 50 of our 150 city answers | Yes |
| 4 | BBB | Cited in 14 of our 150 city answers | Yes |
| 5 | Expertise.com | Cited in 93 of our 150 city answers | No, it selects companies |
| 6 | Local association directory | Trust signal, such as a NARPM chapter | Through membership |

Google Business Profile has rules for new and home-based companies. A business that travels to clients "should hide your business address from customers" and list a service area. A virtual office is not eligible. A coworking office counts only if it has signs, receives clients and has your staff there during business hours.

Expertise.com explains its own selection. It finds candidates from "public databases and customer referrals". It checks licenses, reputation across review sites, years of experience and service range. It also uses mystery shoppers who call each company. Once a company passes, it "may choose to advertise on the page". So answer every call well, keep your license current and build reviews on several sites.

## How do you get the first reviews honestly?

Ask every real client for a review, in the same way, and offer nothing for it. Google's policy for Maps reviews is strict. It bans reviews "that have been paid for, directly or in kind". It bans reviews posted "due to an incentive offered by a business". It also bans review gating: you may not "selectively solicit positive reviews from customers".

What to do:

- **Ask at a natural moment.** After the first rent payment lands, or after a fast repair.
- **Ask every client,** not only the happy ones.
- **Send one direct link** to your Google Business Profile review form.
- **Reply to every review,** good or bad, in calm and specific words.
- **Do not ask staff, friends or family.** Google counts a "conflict of interest" as a policy breach.

Watch for: a burst of 20 reviews in one week from a new account looks fake. Slow and steady is safer.

## How long until AI knows you?

No AI engine publishes a timeline, so plan for months and measure each month. Our own site is a fair warning. On 2026-10-19, citata.ai was named in 0 of 108 non-brand answers. Our how-to guides were 1 day old, and the engines cited Google's own help pages instead (our data, 2026-10).

A simple monthly test:

1. **Write 10 prompts** an owner in your city would ask. Include "best property manager in [city]" and "property manager fees in [city]".
2. **Run them** in ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude on the same day each month.
3. **Record three things:** are you named, which sources the answer cites, and are your facts right.
4. **Act on the sources.** If a directory appears often and you are not in it, that is your next listing.
5. **Fix wrong facts** at the source: your site first, then the listing.

## What not to do as a new company

Do not take shortcuts that break rules or trust. Each of these can cost more than it gives.

- Do not put keywords in your Business Profile name. Google says unnecessary information in the name "isn't permitted".
- Do not buy reviews or swap reviews with other companies.
- Do not publish 30 city pages on day one with the same text.
- Do not claim a door count, award or result you cannot show.
- Do not pay for a "guaranteed AI ranking". No one controls AI answers.

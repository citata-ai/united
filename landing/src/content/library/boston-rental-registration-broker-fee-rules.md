## What is the Boston Rental Registration and Inspection ordinance?

The Boston Rental Registration and Inspection ordinance makes every rental owner register each unit every year and accept a city inspection about once every 5 years. Boston Inspectional Services (ISD) runs the program. The deadline is July 1, and the online period runs from January 2 to June 30.

The fees, from the ISD page:

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| First-time registration | $25 per unit |
| Renewal registration | $15 per unit |
| Cap | $2,500 per building, $5,000 per complex |
| Late registration | Up to $300 penalty each month until you register |
| City inspection, 1-3 unit building | $50 per unit |
| City inspection, 4+ units or a condo | $75 per unit |
| Private inspection report | $15 filing fee per unit report |

Source: City of Boston ISD, pages updated 2025-10-30, read 2026-10-19.

Worked example: you own a triple-decker and a 12-unit building, both registered before. Renewal is 15 × $15 = $225 a year. In the year ISD selects the triple-decker for inspection, add 3 × $50 = $150 for a city inspector. If you never registered, ISD says you owe fees back to 2013.

## Who must register a rental in Boston?

Every rental unit in Boston must register, even if it earns no rent. ISD lists cases that still count:

- **Vacant units** and units under renovation.
- **Relatives as tenants** who are 18 or older and not on the deed.
- **Units where you collect no rent.**

ISD exempts properties owned by a city, state or federal agency, and rooming houses, dorms or lodging houses with a valid lodging license. For inspections only, ISD also exempts buildings of 6 or fewer units where the owner lives in one unit.

You must give ISD the owner's contact data, and the manager's if there is one. An owner who lives outside Massachusetts must name a Boston-area emergency contact. ISD also asks if the building is from before 1978, if it has external fire escapes, and if it is smoke-free.

## What does a Boston rental inspection check?

A Boston rental inspection checks the unit against the Massachusetts State Sanitary Code (105 CMR 410). When ISD selects your property, it mails a notice. You then pick one of 3 paths:

1. **City inspector.** Pay $50 or $75 per unit, as in the table.
2. **Authorized private inspector.** Submit one passed report and a signed affidavit per unit, plus the $15 filing fee. ISD also accepts reports from Boston Housing Authority or Metropolitan Boston Housing Partnership inspectors.
3. **Five-Year Alternative Inspection Compliance Plan.** Only for owners with a record of meeting state and city codes.

Lead is a separate duty. The Massachusetts Attorney General's guide says that for a unit built before 1978, landlord and tenant must sign the Tenant Lead Law Notification and Tenant Certification. If a child under 6 will live there, the landlord must delead or put the lead hazards under interim control.

## What did the 2025 Massachusetts broker fee change do?

The 2025 broker fee law says the party that hires a broker pays that broker. It took effect August 1, 2025, as part of the FY26 state budget that Governor Healey signed on July 4, 2025. The Massachusetts Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities (EOHLC) publishes an FAQ on it.

What the EOHLC FAQ says for landlords:

- **You hire, you pay.** If you hire a broker to list and lease a unit, you pay the fee. There are no exceptions.
- **No renamed fees.** Admin, selection, finder's and leasing fees for your broker are not allowed.
- **No hidden surcharge.** You may set the rent, but you may not add a broker fee surcharge to it, for example by raising first or last month's rent to pay the broker back.
- **No forced broker.** You may not make a tenant hire your preferred broker to see the unit.
- **Penalty.** A landlord may owe up to 3 times the amount charged, plus attorney's fees.

A tenant still pays a broker that the tenant hired to work only for the tenant. The broker must give a written fee disclosure before the lease is signed, stating who hired the broker, who pays and how much.

What this means for your budget: a Boston leasing fee is now an owner cost, like a manager's leasing fee. Use HUD's FY2027 two-bedroom Fair Market Rent for the Boston metro, $3,008, as a test rent. A fee of one month's rent is $3,008 on your books, not the tenant's. See the [Boston Fair Market Rent 2027 page](/guides/section-8-rent-limits/boston-fair-market-rent-2027) for other sizes, and test the cost with our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).

## What are Massachusetts security deposit rules?

Massachusetts limits what you may collect at the start of a tenancy to 4 items, per the Attorney General's guide: first month's rent, last month's rent, a security deposit no larger than one month's rent, and the actual cost of a new lock and key.

The AG guide lists these deposit duties:

1. **Bank it.** Put the deposit in an interest-bearing account at a Massachusetts bank, within the first month, apart from your own money and safe from your creditors.
2. **Tell the tenant.** Give the bank name, address and the account number.
3. **Pay interest each year.** Pay the tenant the interest, or let the tenant deduct it from rent. Interest on last month's rent is due too.
4. **Give a statement of condition.** Within 10 days of the start of the tenancy or of receiving the deposit, whichever is later. The tenant has 15 days to add to it.
5. **Return it within 30 days.** Return the deposit plus interest within 30 days after the tenancy ends. To keep part of it for damage, give a written list and a repair estimate in the same 30 days.

The AG guide also says landlords may not charge up-front pet fees, broker fees or application fees.

## Does Boston have rent control?

No. Boston has no rent control, because state law bans it. The Massachusetts Trial Court Law Libraries list MGL c. 40P as "the Massachusetts rent control prohibition act."

A 2026 ballot question tried to change that. It would have capped yearly rent increases. On June 23, 2026, the Supreme Judicial Court ruled it could not go on the November 2026 ballot, because it held a religious exemption (WBUR). Supporters said they may try again for 2028.

So in 2026 a Boston owner sets rent by the market and the lease. The registration fee, the inspection, the deposit rules and the broker fee rule are the real local costs.

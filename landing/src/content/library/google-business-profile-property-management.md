## How do I set up a Google Business Profile?

A Google Business Profile is free. Google's help page says: "You can add or claim your business at no charge." Follow these steps.

1. **Search for your company** in Google Maps. If a profile exists, select it and choose to claim it.
2. **Add the business** at business.google.com/add if no profile exists.
3. **Enter the real name**, the category and the address or service area.
4. **Pick a verification method** and finish it. Google needs proof that you run the business.
5. **Fill every field** in the list below before you share the profile.

If someone else already verified the profile, ask that person for ownership. A former employee or an old agency often holds it.

## Which fields matter most for ranking?

Four fields carry the most weight: primary category, name, location and reviews. Google ranks local results by relevance, distance and prominence. The Whitespark 2026 survey of 47 local search experts put primary category first among map pack factors.

| Field | Google's rule | What to enter as a property manager |
|---|---|---|
| Business name | Your real-world name, as on signs and stationery | Your legal or brand name only |
| Primary category | The most specific category for the core business | The category for property management |
| More categories | Up to 9, as few as possible | Only what the firm truly is, for example a real estate agency if you also sell |
| Address | Staffed, with permanent signage, no virtual office | Show it only if owners can visit |
| Service area | Up to 20 areas, about 2 hours of driving or less | The cities and ZIP codes you serve |
| Phone | A local number under your direct control | The line a person answers |
| Website | The page for that location | Your home page or the city page |
| Hours | Customer-facing hours | The hours your phone is answered |

Watch for: Google bans keywords in the name. "Acme Property Management - Best Rentals Chicago" can get the profile suspended. The name must match the real world.

## What is the difference between a storefront and a service-area profile?

A property manager is often a hybrid. Google defines three types:

- **Storefront.** Customers visit the address. You show the address.
- **Service-area business.** You visit customers and do not receive them at the address. You hide the address and set a service area.
- **Hybrid.** You receive customers at the office and also work in the field. You show the address and set a service area.

Google says a hybrid office must be staffed and able to receive customers during its stated hours. Google also bans a co-working desk unless it has signage and staff during business hours. If you run the company from home, hide the home address.

## Which services and description should I add?

Add each service an owner pays for. Google's services editor lets you pick suggested services or add custom ones. It also allows a description and a price per service. Google rejects custom service names that hold prices or phone numbers.

A services list for a residential manager:

- Full-service property management
- Tenant placement (leasing only)
- Rent collection
- Tenant screening
- Maintenance coordination
- Owner statements and accounting
- Eviction coordination
- Section 8 / Housing Choice Voucher properties
- HOA or condo association management, if you do it

Write the description in plain words. Google sets a limit of 750 characters. Google says to include what you offer, what makes you different and how long you have been in business. Do not add links or HTML. Do not focus on promotions, prices or sales.

A model you can adapt (about 540 characters):

> [Company] manages [number] single-family homes and small multifamily buildings in [city] and [cities]. Since [year], we have handled leasing, screening, rent collection, maintenance and owner accounting for rental owners. Owners get a monthly statement and an online portal. We work with Housing Choice Voucher tenants and local housing authorities. We are licensed in [state] under license [number]. Call to ask about a free rental analysis for your property.

Use only true numbers. If you do not know your door count, leave it out.

## How often should I post updates and photos?

Post at least once a month. Google says posts older than 6 months are archived unless you set a date range. Posts show in the "Updates" or "Overview" tab and in the "From the owner" section.

Google offers 3 post types:

| Post type | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Update | News, a guide, a market note | "Our 2026 rent report for [city] owners" |
| Offer | A deal with dates | First month of management free, if you offer it |
| Event | A dated event | A landlord law seminar |

Watch for: Google may reject a post with a phone number it cannot tie to your business.

For photos, Google says photos of your storefront, products and services make a profile more attractive. Add real ones: the team, the office front, a well-kept managed home (with the owner's consent). Never post a photo that shows a tenant's face, plate or private details.

## How do I link the profile to my site?

Link the profile to the page that best serves an owner. For one office, that is the home page. For several staffed offices with their own profiles, link each one to its city page.

Add a UTM tag (a source label in the link) so GA4 can count leads from the profile. Example:

`https://example.com/?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp`

Google's help page also says to make sure Googlebot is not blocked on your site. Check robots.txt once.

Then make the facts match. Google says it detects category facts from your website and from mentions across the web. Your site, your profile and your directory listings should show the same name, phone, address and services. Read the [AI search checklist for property management websites](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website) for the site side.

## What breaks a property manager's profile?

Six common mistakes can break a profile. Each one goes against a Google rule above.

1. **Keyword name.** City or service words added to the name.
2. **Virtual office.** A mailbox or rented address with no staff.
3. **Two profiles** for one business, often made by an old agency.
4. **Wrong primary category**, such as a real estate agency category for a firm that only manages.
5. **Call-center phone** or a tracking number that does not reach your office.
6. **Paid or gated reviews.** Google bans incentives for reviews and bans asking only happy customers.

Fix the profile first, then work on reviews. Reviews raise prominence, and Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help local rank.

## Google Business Profile checklist

- Claimed and verified, owned by a company account, not one person.
- Real name, no keywords.
- Primary category set, extra categories only if true.
- Address shown only if staffed with signage; service area otherwise.
- Up to 20 service areas, within about 2 hours of driving.
- Services list matches your website.
- Description under 750 characters, no links.
- 10 or more real photos.
- One post each month.
- Website link with a UTM tag.
- Every review answered.

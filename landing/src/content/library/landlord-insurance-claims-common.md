## Why are water claims important for rental owners?

Water deserves attention because leaks can spread before the owner sees them, but homeowners data does not prove a landlord-only ranking.

The Insurance Information Institute publishes ISO homeowners loss data by cause. Its table identifies water damage and freezing, wind and hail, fire and lightning, and other categories. The table states the policy types and exclusions. It is not a dedicated rental-owner claims study.

That distinction matters when a headline calls one event the most common landlord claim. A broad household sample cannot establish the same ranking for every rental portfolio. Local weather, building systems, vacancy, and policy terms change the risk. This guide makes no national landlord-only ranking claim.

Start a property water review with known weak points. Look at visible supply lines, drainage, roof condition, appliance connections, and prior leak locations. Ask a qualified contractor to inspect items outside your ability. Use a task record rather than assuming the tenant will detect every defect.

| Risk area | Prevention task to discuss |
|---|---|
| Supply plumbing | Review condition and known leak points |
| Roof and drainage | Check damage and blocked water paths |
| Heating | Review cold-weather operating needs |
| Appliances | Check connections and visible wear |
| Reporting | Give occupants a clear urgent contact |

Record defects and completed work. A repair plan without follow-up leaves the same risk in place.

## How do fire and liability claims happen?

Fire and liability risks involve both the building's condition and the way people use and move through the property.

Triple-I's homeowners data includes fire and lightning and liability categories. These are different loss types with different coverage questions. A damaged kitchen is not the same claim as an injury allegation. Ask the agent to explain both property coverage and liability coverage.

Review heating equipment, electrical concerns, cooking areas, and stored materials with qualified vendors. Confirm that required safety devices and inspections meet local rules. This guide does not supply a national inspection interval. Use the current requirements for your address and system.

Review paths, steps, handrails, lighting, and other visible hazards. Ask tenants to report defects promptly through a clear channel. Respond to reports and keep a dated work record. A message saying someone will look later is not proof that the defect was corrected.

Travelers describes landlord property, liability, and loss-of-use coverage on its landlord page. Each part remains subject to actual policy terms. Tell the agent about the property type and any unusual use. Do not assume a standard owner-occupied policy fits a rental.

Washington's insurance regulator also explains that renter coverage differs from the landlord's coverage. Tenant insurance does not replace the owner's building or liability policy. Treat the policies as separate parts of the risk plan.

## What maintenance helps prevent claims?

Use a property-specific maintenance plan with named duties, dated records, and a process for urgent defects.

Build the plan from inspections, vendor guidance, past repairs, and local requirements. Identify which systems need specialist work. Do not assign technical tasks to tenants who lack the skill or authority to perform them safely.

Make the reporting process easy to use. Give occupants a clear urgent number and a normal maintenance channel. Explain what information helps locate the defect. Ask the manager how reports move from intake to vendor assignment and completion.

Keep evidence of completed repairs. Save the work order, invoice, photos where useful, and any follow-up visit. Record unresolved recommendations as open items rather than marking the whole job complete. Ask the vendor to distinguish essential work from optional upgrades.

Review the property after a serious weather event or known incident when access is lawful and appropriate. Do not rely solely on an old inspection report. Ask the insurer whether the policy has specific vacancy, protection, or notification conditions.

Budget for maintenance alongside management. The [fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) helps assess service charges, but it does not fund repairs. Keep money available for work that prevents damage. A manager's monthly fee usually needs a separate property repair budget under the contract.

## Can an insurance claim raise my premium?

Ask the insurer how a claim may affect renewal and pricing, because the answer depends on underwriting and local rules.

This guide does not promise a fixed surcharge or a claim-free discount. Do not assume that an online estimate describes your rental. Ask the agent about claim history, coverage limits, deductibles, and the insurer's current options. Request any proposed change in writing.

When an incident occurs, first protect people and prevent further damage where safe. Contact emergency services when needed. Follow the policy's claim and notification requirements. Preserve photos, repair records, tenant reports, and damaged items when the insurer asks.

Ask the adjuster which costs require approval and which records support lost rent. Travelers says loss-of-use coverage can address fair rental value when a covered loss makes the home unfit to live in. That does not establish coverage for every water event or empty period.

Review the claim after the immediate work ends. Identify the cause, corrective work, uncovered costs, and remaining risks. Update the maintenance plan instead of treating payment as the end of the job.

Before renewal, send the agent accurate property details and completed repair records. Compare policies on coverage, exclusions, limits, and service as well as premium. Keep the final policy and endorsements where both you and the manager can find them when needed.

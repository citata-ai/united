## Can I leave the Section 8 program at the end of a lease term?

You can stop renting to a voucher tenant at the end of the initial lease term if you have a lawful ground and give proper notice. The federal rule is 24 CFR 982.310. During the lease term, you may end the tenancy only for:

1. **A serious or repeated lease violation,** such as not paying rent (982.310(a)(1)).
2. **A violation of federal, state or local law** about use of the unit (982.310(a)(2)).
3. **Other good cause** (982.310(a)(3)).

"Other good cause" changes after the initial term. 982.310(d)(1) gives examples:

| Reason | Initial lease term | After the initial term |
|---|---|---|
| Family did not accept a new lease or a lease revision | No | Yes |
| History of disturbing neighbors or damaging property | Yes | Yes |
| You want to live in the unit, or use it for family | No | Yes |
| You want to use the unit for a non-rental purpose | No | Yes |
| Sale of the property | No | Yes |
| Renovation of the unit | No | Yes |
| You want to rent at a higher rent | No | Yes |

Source: 24 CFR 982.310(d). In the initial term, 982.310(d)(2) allows "other good cause" only for something "the family did or failed to do."

The program itself does not bind you to future voucher tenants. 24 CFR 982.306(e) says nothing in the rule gives "any owner any right to participate in the program." Check your local law, though: some states and cities bar refusing a tenant because of a voucher.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## How much notice do I give the tenant and the PHA?

Federal rules set the form of the notice. State law sets the number of days. Under 24 CFR 982.310(e):

1. **Written notice of grounds to the tenant.** It must state the grounds for ending the tenancy. It may be part of your eviction notice.
2. **A copy to the PHA.** You must give the PHA a copy of "any owner eviction notice." The rule defines that as a notice to vacate, or the court filing that starts an eviction.
3. **Court action to evict.** If the family does not leave, you may evict only through a court action (982.310(f)).

The federal statute says the same. 42 U.S.C. 1437f(o)(7)(E) requires "written notice by the owner to the tenant specifying the grounds," with relief consistent with state and local law.

A worked timeline, with a made-up 60-day state notice rule:

| Date | Step |
|---|---|
| 2026-11-01 | Initial 1-year lease term ends; lease goes month to month |
| 2027-01-02 | You send written notice to vacate: "sale of the property, effective 2027-03-03" |
| 2027-01-02 | You send the PHA a copy of the notice |
| 2027-03-03 | Tenancy ends; HAP contract ends with it |

A notice to vacate lets the family move with its voucher. 24 CFR 982.354(b)(2) lists an owner's notice to vacate as one of the times a family may move with continued assistance.

## Do source-of-income laws limit nonrenewal?

Source-of-income laws can limit nonrenewal where they apply. They bar treating a tenant differently because of how the tenant pays rent. Virginia is one example: Code of Virginia 36-96.3 makes it unlawful to refuse to rent, or to set different terms, because of "source of funds."

What that can mean for you:

- **Do not end a lease only because of the voucher** where such a law applies.
- **Apply the same reason to every tenant.** If you sell a building, treat assisted and unassisted tenants the same.
- **Write down the business reason.** Keep the sale contract, renovation bids or rent survey.
- **Check city law too.** Some cities have their own rules on notice and good cause.

## What if I sell the property?

A sale counts as a business reason only after the initial lease term (982.310(d)). During the initial term, a sale is not a ground to end the tenancy.

Foreclosure has its own federal rule. Under 42 U.S.C. 1437f(o)(7), a new owner after foreclosure takes the property "subject to the lease between the prior owner and the tenant and to the housing assistance payments contract." Vacating the property before a sale is not good cause for that new owner. One exception: a new owner who will live in the unit may end the tenancy at transfer with at least 90 days' notice.

For a normal sale:

- [ ] Tell the PHA before closing.
- [ ] Ask the PHA how it moves the HAP contract to the buyer.
- [ ] Give the buyer the lease, the HAP contract and the last inspection.
- [ ] Agree who gets the HAP for the closing month.

## What happens to the HAP contract?

The HAP contract ends when the lease ends. 24 CFR 982.309(b) says the HAP contract term "begins on the first day of the lease term and ends on the last day of the lease term." It also ends if the lease is ended by the owner or the tenant.

Three more HAP rules at the end of a tenancy:

1. **Move-out month.** You may keep the HAP for the month the family moves out. The PHA pays nothing for later months (24 CFR 982.311(d)).
2. **During an eviction.** If the family stays while you evict, the PHA keeps paying HAP until you get a court judgment (982.311(b)).
3. **Automatic end.** The HAP contract ends by itself 180 days after the last HAP payment (24 CFR 982.455).

## Step list: end a voucher tenancy cleanly

1. **Check the date.** Is the initial lease term over?
2. **Pick a lawful ground** from 24 CFR 982.310.
3. **Check state and city law** for notice days and good-cause rules.
4. **Write the notice** with the grounds and the end date.
5. **Send the PHA a copy** the same day.
6. **Keep records** of delivery and of your business reason.
7. **Do not take HAP** for months after move-out.

## Rhode Island security deposit rules at a glance

Rhode Island puts its deposit rules in one section: R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-19, part of the Residential Landlord and Tenant Act. We read the official text on 2026-10-19. The rilegislature.gov server did not answer that day, so we read the Internet Archive copy of the official page from 2026-09-04. Its history line shows the last change in 2018.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | 1 month's periodic rent | § 34-18-19(a) |
| Furniture deposit | Up to 1 more month's rent, only if furniture is worth $5,000 or more | § 34-18-19(e) |
| Return deadline | 20 days after the latest of: tenancy end, possession, forwarding address | § 34-18-19(b) |
| Itemized list | Written notice of each deduction, sent with the balance | § 34-18-19(b) |
| Allowed deductions | Unpaid rent, reasonable cleaning, reasonable trash removal, damage beyond wear and tear | § 34-18-19(b) |
| Penalty | Amount due plus 2 times the amount wrongfully withheld, plus attorney fees | § 34-18-19(c) |
| Sale of the building | The owner at move-out is bound to return the deposit | § 34-18-19(g) |
| Lease waiver | Not allowed | § 34-18-19(h) |
| Interest or separate account | No rule in § 34-18-19 | § 34-18-19 |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Rhode Island?

The maximum Rhode Island security deposit is 1 month's periodic rent. Section 34-18-19(a) bars a deposit "in excess of one month's periodic rent." The words "however denominated" mean a new name does not help. A "move-in fee" that works as a deposit still counts toward the cap.

One exception exists for furnished units. If the furniture is worth $5,000 or more at lease signing, the landlord may charge a separate furniture deposit. That deposit is also capped at 1 month's rent (§ 34-18-19(e)).

| Monthly rent | Max standard deposit | Max furniture deposit (furniture worth $5,000+) | Max total |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1,400 | $1,400 | $1,400 | $2,800 |
| $1,800 | $1,800 | $1,800 | $3,600 |
| $2,500 | $2,500 | $2,500 | $5,000 |

Watch for: Since 2024-01-01, Rhode Island also bans rental application fees (§ 34-18-59). You may charge only the actual cost of a background or credit check. And you may charge it only if the applicant did not bring one from the last 90 days.

## How many days does a Rhode Island landlord have to return a security deposit?

A Rhode Island landlord has 20 days to return the deposit balance. The clock starts at the latest of three events in § 34-18-19(b):

1. **The tenancy ends.** The lease term runs out or a notice takes effect.
2. **The tenant gives back possession.** Keys returned, unit empty.
3. **The tenant gives a forwarding address.** The address must be for the deposit refund.

Worked example: the lease ends on June 30. The tenant moves out and returns keys on July 2. The tenant emails a forwarding address on July 10. The latest date is July 10, so the deadline is July 30.

The same 20-day rule applies to a furniture deposit (§ 34-18-19(f)).

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Rhode Island?

A Rhode Island landlord may deduct four kinds of cost, and no others, under § 34-18-19(b):

- **Unpaid accrued rent.** Rent the tenant owes at move-out.
- **Reasonable cleaning.** The cost must be reasonable.
- **Reasonable trash disposal.** For items or trash the tenant left.
- **Physical damage.** Damage beyond ordinary wear and tear that comes from the tenant's breach of § 34-18-24, the tenant's maintenance duties.

Section 34-18-11(8) defines "ordinary wear and tear." It is deterioration from the tenant's "normal nonabusive living." It also includes damage caused by the landlord's own failure to prepare for expected conditions or to meet the landlord's duties. So faded paint from age is not a deduction. A hole in a wall is.

For a furniture deposit, § 34-18-19(f) allows reasonable cleaning, repair, and damage to the furniture beyond ordinary wear and tear.

## Does a Rhode Island landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. Each Rhode Island deduction must be "itemized by the landlord in a written notice delivered to the tenant" (§ 34-18-19(b)). The notice goes out with the balance, inside the same 20 days.

Use this checklist for each move-out:

- [ ] Note the three trigger dates and pick the latest.
- [ ] Take dated photos at move-in and at move-out.
- [ ] List each deduction on its own line, with the amount.
- [ ] Attach invoices or receipts for cleaning, trash and repairs.
- [ ] Separate wear and tear from damage, line by line.
- [ ] Send the list and the refund check together.
- [ ] Keep proof of mailing or delivery.

## Must a Rhode Island landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

Section 34-18-19 does not require interest on deposits. It also does not require a separate or escrow account. We did not find such a rule in the deposit section. A lease, a housing program, or a local rule can still add duties, so check those before you set up the account.

One related rule covers lockouts and cut services. If the tenant then ends the lease, the landlord must return all recoverable security and all prepaid rent (§ 34-18-34).

## What is the penalty for a Rhode Island landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

The Rhode Island penalty is the amount due plus twice the amount wrongfully withheld, plus reasonable attorney fees (§ 34-18-19(c)). Other damages under the chapter stay open to both sides (§ 34-18-19(d)).

Worked calculation:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Deposit held | $1,800 |
| Valid deductions (cleaning $150, unpaid rent $250) | $400 |
| Amount due to tenant | $1,400 |
| Landlord returned | $800 |
| Amount wrongfully withheld | $600 |
| Statutory damages (2 × $600) | $1,200 |
| Tenant recovers before attorney fees | $600 + $1,200 = $1,800 |

So the landlord pays $1,800, not $600, plus the tenant's lawyer. A new owner cannot escape this: the owner at the end of the tenancy is bound by the section (§ 34-18-19(g)). A lease clause that waives these rules has no effect (§ 34-18-19(h)).

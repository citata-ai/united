## Oregon security deposit rules at a glance

ORS 90.300 holds almost every state deposit rule in Oregon. The table puts the limits and deadlines in one place.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | No state cap | ORS 90.300 |
| Receipt for deposit | Required | ORS 90.300(2)(a) |
| Deposit listed in written lease | Required | ORS 90.300(3) |
| New or higher deposit in first year | Not allowed, except for an agreed change such as a pet | ORS 90.300(5)(a) |
| Time to pay a new deposit after year one | At least 3 months | ORS 90.300(5)(b) |
| Pet deposit for an assistance animal | Not allowed | ORS 90.300(4) |
| Return or written accounting | 31 days after tenancy ends and tenant delivers possession | ORS 90.300(12), (13) |
| Delivery of accounting | In person, first class mail, or email if agreed | ORS 90.300(14) |
| Penalty for failure or bad faith | Twice the amount withheld | ORS 90.300(16) |
| Interest or separate account | Not required by state law | ORS 90.300 |

## What is the maximum security deposit a landlord can charge in Oregon?

Oregon state law sets no maximum security deposit. ORS 90.300 lets a landlord require a deposit but names no dollar or month limit.

The statute still controls how you charge it:

- **Receipt.** Give the tenant a receipt for the deposit.
- **Lease.** List the deposit in the written rental agreement.
- **No increase in year one.** You cannot add or raise a deposit in the first year, except for an agreed change, such as a new pet.
- **Time to pay later.** After year one, give the tenant at least 3 months to pay a new or higher deposit.
- **Assistance animals.** No pet deposit for a service or companion animal that a tenant with a disability needs.
- **No minimum-stay forfeit.** ORS 90.300(8) bars keeping a deposit because a month-to-month tenant left early.

A "last month's rent deposit" counts as a security deposit under ORS 90.300(1). You must apply it to the last month's rent in most move-outs, under ORS 90.300(9).

## How many days does an Oregon landlord have to return a security deposit?

An Oregon landlord has 31 days to return a security deposit. The clock starts when the tenancy ends and the tenant delivers possession. ORS 90.300(13) sets the return deadline. ORS 90.300(12) sets the same deadline for the written accounting.

ORS 90.300(14) allows 3 ways to deliver the refund or accounting:

1. **Personal delivery** to the tenant.
2. **First class mail.**
3. **Email,** only if a signed email addendum under ORS 90.155 allows it.

Electronic payment to the tenant's bank also works. Both sides must agree in an addendum signed after move-in, under ORS 90.300(13).

### Worked example: a 31-day deadline

| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| Lease ends | June 30 |
| Tenant returns keys | July 2 |
| Day 1 of the count | July 3 |
| Day 31, last day to mail refund or accounting | August 2 |

The count starts from possession, not from the lease end date. Keep proof of the date the tenant returned the keys.

## What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit in Oregon?

An Oregon landlord may deduct only the amount reasonably necessary for 2 things. ORS 90.300(7)(a) names them:

- **Lease defaults,** such as unpaid rent.
- **Damage the tenant caused,** but not ordinary wear and tear.

ORS 90.300(7)(b) and (c) add details:

- You do not have to do the repair before you charge for it.
- Labor must use a reasonable hourly rate. You may charge your own labor at that rate.
- Carpet cleaning is allowed only if 3 things are true. A carpet machine does the work. The carpet was cleaned or replaced before move-in. The written lease allows the charge even if the tenant cleans.
- Loss of use during timely repairs is a valid claim.

### Deduction checklist

| Item | Deduct? |
|---|---|
| Unpaid rent | Yes |
| Hole in a wall from tenant damage | Yes, reasonable cost |
| Faded paint from normal use | No, ordinary wear and tear |
| Machine carpet cleaning, lease allows it, cleaned before move-in | Yes |
| Owner's own repair work | Yes, at a reasonable hourly rate |
| Early move-out from month-to-month | No forfeit allowed |

## Does an Oregon landlord have to give an itemized list of deductions?

Yes. To keep any part of the deposit, an Oregon landlord must give a written accounting within 31 days. ORS 90.300(12) says the accounting must state "specifically the basis or bases of the claim." You must also give a separate accounting for prepaid rent.

A good accounting lists each item, the cost and the reason. Attach photos and invoices where you have them. In Portland, City Code 30.01.087 makes photos of damage part of the accounting.

## Must an Oregon landlord pay interest or keep the deposit in a separate account?

Oregon state law does not require interest or a separate account. ORS 90.300 contains no such rule. It does say a tenant's claim to the deposit comes before a claim by the landlord's creditors.

Portland is different. City Code 30.01.087, as the city shows it on 2026-10-19, adds these rules:

- **Cap.** 1 month's rent. If you also take last month's rent, the extra deposit is at most half a month's rent.
- **Separate account.** Deposit the funds within 2 weeks in an account apart from your personal and operating accounts.
- **Interest.** Interest from an interest-bearing account goes to the tenant, less an optional 5% for admin cost.
- **Condition report.** Do a signed move-in report with photos.
- **Labor proof.** Labor charges over $200 need proof the rate is reasonable.
- **Penalty.** Up to $250 per violation plus actual damages and attorney fees.

## What is the penalty for an Oregon landlord who keeps a deposit wrongly?

The penalty is twice the amount withheld. Under ORS 90.300(16), a tenant can recover double if the landlord:

- Misses the 31-day return rule, for money kept without a written accounting; or
- Keeps any part of the deposit in bad faith.

ORS 90.300(18) keeps other damages under chapter 90 open as well. A separate rule applies to illegal lockouts. Under ORS 90.375, the landlord must return all deposits and prepaid rent if the tenant ends the lease for that reason.

Watch for: a late accounting does not only cost interest. It can turn a valid $400 cleaning claim into an $800 debt to the tenant.

## Move-out steps for Oregon managers

1. **Record the possession date.** Note when the tenant returned keys.
2. **Inspect and photograph** the unit the same week.
3. **Price each repair** at a reasonable rate.
4. **Apply any last month's rent deposit** under ORS 90.300(9).
5. **Send the accounting and refund** by day 31.
6. **Keep proof of delivery,** such as a mail receipt.

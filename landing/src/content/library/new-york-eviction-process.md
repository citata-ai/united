New York lets you remove a tenant only through a court case. RPAPL 711 says no tenant "shall be removed from possession except in a special proceeding." Here are the deadlines from the statute text on nysenate.gov, read on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Late rent notice by certified mail | Rent not received within 5 days of the due date | RPL 235-e |
| Written rent demand before a nonpayment case | At least 14 days' notice | RPAPL 711(2) |
| Good Cause notice with demand, lease and petition | Attach to each | RPL 231-c |
| Notice to end a tenancy or not renew | 30, 60 or 90 days, by length of tenancy | RPL 226-c |
| Notice to cure a lease violation (Good Cause units) | 10 days from receipt | RPL 216(1)(b) |
| Serve a holdover petition | 10 to 17 days before the hearing | RPAPL 733 |
| Tenant answer in a nonpayment case | 10 days from service | RPAPL 732 |
| Trial after the tenant answers | 3 to 8 days | RPAPL 732 |
| Officer notice before the eviction | At least 14 days, in writing | RPAPL 749 |
| Stay in a holdover case | Up to 1 year | RPAPL 753(1) |
| Stay to cure a lease breach after judgment | 30 days | RPAPL 753(4) |
| Unlawful eviction penalty | $1,000 to $10,000 per violation | RPAPL 768 |

## What notice must a New York landlord give before filing for unpaid rent?

A New York landlord must serve a written rent demand with at least 14 days' notice. RPAPL 711(2) says the demand asks for "the payment of the rent, or the possession of the premises." You serve it as RPAPL 735 sets out. An oral demand does not meet the rule.

Two more notices come first or come with it:

1. **Late rent notice.** RPL 235-e says that if you do not receive rent within 5 days of the due date, you must send a written notice by certified mail. If you skip it, the tenant can use that as a defense in a nonpayment case.
2. **Good Cause notice.** RPL 231-c requires a form titled "Notice to Tenant of Applicability or Inapplicability of the New York State Good Cause Eviction Law." You attach it to leases, renewals, the RPAPL 711(2) demand and the petition. The form says if the unit is covered and, if not, which exemption applies.

## How long does an eviction take in New York?

A New York eviction for unpaid rent takes at least 5 to 7 weeks on paper. Real cases take longer. The statutes set only minimum times, and court calendars, stays and appeals add more.

Here is a worked minimum timeline for a nonpayment case, using the statute deadlines:

| Step | Days | Running total | Statute |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rent unpaid 5 days, send certified late notice | 5 | Day 5 | RPL 235-e |
| Rent demand period | 14 | Day 19 | RPAPL 711(2) |
| File and serve the petition, tenant answers | 10 | Day 29 | RPAPL 732 |
| Trial after the answer | 3 to 8 | Day 32 to 37 | RPAPL 732 |
| Judgment, warrant, officer notice | 14 | Day 46 to 51 | RPAPL 749 |

This table leaves out time for filing, service and the clerk. If the tenant does not answer, RPAPL 732 says the judge enters judgment for you. The judge may still delay the warrant for up to 10 days from service.

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in New York, and what does it cost?

A New York landlord files in the local court for the area where the property is. RPAPL 701 lists the courts: a county court, a village police justice court, a justice court, a city civil court or a district court. In New York City, that is the city's Civil Court.

The case must be heard in the area of the court where the property sits (RPAPL 701(2)). We could not open the fee pages on nycourts.gov when we wrote this. So we do not give a fee here. Ask the court clerk for the current index or filing fee before you file.

## Can a New York landlord evict for a lease violation or with no cause?

A New York landlord can evict for a lease violation. For no-cause evictions, the answer depends on Good Cause Eviction. Article 6-A of the Real Property Law is the Good Cause Eviction Law. The nysenate.gov text dates from 2024-05-03. It applies in New York City (RPL 212). Outside the city, it applies only where a town, village or city adopts it by local law (RPL 213).

In a covered unit, RPL 216 lets a court remove the tenant only on a listed ground:

- **Nonpayment** of rent, unless the rent came from an unreasonable increase.
- **Lease or rule violation**, if the tenant does not cure within 10 days of written notice.
- **Nuisance** or serious damage to the property.
- **Illegal use** of the unit, or a government order to vacate.
- **Refused access** for repairs or showings.
- **Owner use** as a main home for the landlord or a listed relative.
- **Demolition** or **removal from the rental market**, in good faith.
- **Refusal of reasonable lease changes** at renewal, after 30 to 90 days' written notice.

RPL 214 lists 15 exemptions. Here are 4 of them:

- a small landlord with no more than 10 units in the state;
- an owner-occupied building with 10 or fewer units;
- a certificate of occupancy issued on or after 2009-01-01, for 30 years;
- rent above 245% of fair market rent. Rent-regulated units fall under their own rules.

In a unit that Good Cause does not cover, you end the tenancy with notice. RPL 226-c sets 30, 60 or 90 days, by how long the tenant lived there. RPL 232-a uses the same notice for a month-to-month tenant in New York City. In a holdover case, RPAPL 753 lets the court stay the warrant for up to 1 year in some hardship cases.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in New York?

A sheriff, constable or city marshal removes the tenant, not the landlord. RPAPL 749 directs the warrant to "the sheriff of the county or to any constable or marshal of the city." The officer must give at least 14 days' written notice.

The eviction can happen only "on a business day between the hours of sunrise and sunset." Up to the moment of execution, the court can stay or vacate the warrant for good cause. If the case was for a lease breach, RPAPL 753(4) gives the tenant a 30-day stay to cure.

## Is a self-help eviction legal in New York?

No, a self-help eviction is illegal in New York. RPAPL 768 protects an occupant who lawfully lived in the unit for 30 days or more, or who has a lease. It forbids you to:

- use or threaten force to make the occupant leave;
- interrupt or stop essential services, such as heat or water;
- remove the occupant's things from the unit;
- remove the door or change the lock without a key for the occupant.

An intentional violation is a class A misdemeanor. The civil penalty is $1,000 to $10,000 for each violation. RPAPL 768 adds up to $100 a day, for up to 6 months, if you do not restore the occupant.

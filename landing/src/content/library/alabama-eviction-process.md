Alabama eviction rules sit in two places. The Alabama Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (Ala. Code 35-9A) sets the notices and the court steps. The older unlawful detainer article (Ala. Code 6-6-310 and on) covers the writ and the officer who enforces it. We read the current text of each section in the Code of Alabama on 2026-10-20.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice for unpaid rent | At least 7 business days after receipt | Ala. Code 35-9A-421(b) |
| Notice for a lease violation | 7 business days to cure | Ala. Code 35-9A-421(a) |
| Cure limit | No more than 2 cures in 12 months | Ala. Code 35-9A-421(d) |
| Noncurable acts (drugs, firearms, assault, repeat breach) | 7-day notice, no right to cure | Ala. Code 35-9A-421(d) |
| End a month-to-month tenancy | 30 days before the rental date | Ala. Code 35-9A-441(b) |
| End a week-to-week tenancy | 7 days | Ala. Code 35-9A-441(a) |
| Where to file | District or circuit court, county of the rental | Ala. Code 35-9A-461(b) |
| Stay of the writ after judgment | 7 days | Ala. Code 35-9A-461(e) |
| Appeal to circuit court | Within 7 days; trial within 60 days | Ala. Code 35-9A-461(d) |
| Lockout or utility shutoff | Not allowed | Ala. Code 35-9A-427 |

## What notice must an Alabama landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

An Alabama landlord must give a written notice to terminate the lease. Ala. Code 35-9A-421(b) says the notice must state the rent and any late fees owed. It must also say the rental agreement ends on a date at least 7 business days after the tenant receives it.

If the tenant pays in full within the 7 business days, the lease goes on. If the tenant does not pay, the rental agreement ends and you may file.

The notice should hold:

- [ ] The tenant's name and the rental address
- [ ] The exact rent owed and the late fees owed
- [ ] The termination date, at least 7 business days after receipt
- [ ] A statement that the agreement ends if the tenant does not pay
- [ ] How and when you delivered it

If the tenant both owes rent and breaks another lease term, the 7-day nonpayment rule governs (Ala. Code 35-9A-421(b)).

## Can an Alabama landlord evict a tenant for a lease violation or with no cause?

Yes, both are possible. The notice depends on the reason.

**Lease violation.** Ala. Code 35-9A-421(a) covers a material breach of the lease, a breach of tenant duties that affects health and safety, or a lie on the application. The notice must name the acts and give a termination date at least 7 business days after receipt. If the tenant fixes the breach before that date, the lease does not end. A lie on the application cannot be cured.

**Cure limit.** Under Ala. Code 35-9A-421(d), a tenant may not cure a breach more than 2 times in any 12-month period without the landlord's written consent.

**Noncurable acts.** The same subsection lists acts that allow a 7-day notice with no right to cure:

1. Illegal drugs in the unit or common areas
2. Illegal use or discharge of a firearm on the premises, except self-defense
3. A criminal assault on a tenant or guest on the premises, except self-defense
4. The same breach again within 6 months of a breach the tenant cured

**No cause.** Either side may end a month-to-month tenancy with written notice at least 30 days before the periodic rental date in the notice. A week-to-week tenancy needs 7 days (Ala. Code 35-9A-441). A fixed-term lease ends on its end date.

If a tenant stays after the lease ends without your consent, you may file for possession. If the holdover is willful and not in good faith, you may also claim up to 3 months' rent or actual damages, whichever is greater, plus attorney's fees (Ala. Code 35-9A-441(c)).

## Where does a landlord file an eviction case in Alabama, and what does it cost?

An Alabama landlord files in the district court or circuit court of the county where the rental is. Ala. Code 35-9A-461(b) sets this venue rule. The same subsection gives eviction cases precedence over other civil cases in scheduling.

Service follows the Alabama Rules of Civil Procedure. If the officer or process server cannot serve the tenant in person, Ala. Code 35-9A-461(c) allows two other ways:

- Deliver the papers to an adult who lives at the rental.
- If no one is found, post the papers on the door. Then mail a copy first class the same day or by the close of the next business day.

Service by posting is complete on the date of mailing.

Filing and service fees differ by county. The statutes we read do not set them. Ask the district court clerk in your county for the current fee before you file.

## How long does an eviction take in Alabama?

No statute sets a total length. But the fixed periods add up to more than 2 weeks before court time. Here is our calculation for a nonpayment case, using only the periods in the statutes.

| Step | Minimum time | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Notice to pay or end the lease | 7 business days, about 9 calendar days | Ala. Code 35-9A-421(b) |
| File, serve, get a hearing and a judgment | Depends on the court | Ala. Code 35-9A-461 |
| Automatic stay after judgment | 7 days | Ala. Code 35-9A-461(e) |
| Officer carries out the writ | Depends on the sheriff or constable | Ala. Code 6-6-337 |

So the fixed periods total at least 16 calendar days. Court and officer time come on top. An appeal adds more: the circuit court must set the trial within 60 days of the appeal (Ala. Code 35-9A-461(d)).

During an appeal, the writ can still issue unless the tenant pays into court all rent due since the filing and keeps paying as rent comes due. If the tenant misses a payment, the court issues the writ on motion.

## Who removes the tenant after a judgment in Alabama?

The sheriff or a constable removes the tenant. After a judgment for the landlord, a writ of possession issues on the landlord's application (Ala. Code 35-9A-461(e)). Ala. Code 6-6-337(a) says the writ commands the sheriff or the constable to put the plaintiff in possession.

The writ cannot issue during the automatic 7-day stay after judgment. If a tenant re-enters without just cause after removal, the tenant can be held in contempt, and more writs may issue (Ala. Code 35-9A-461(e)).

## Is a self-help eviction (lockout, utility shutoff) legal in Alabama?

No. Ala. Code 35-9A-427 says a landlord may not take possession of the unit except by court action, abandonment, surrender, or as the chapter permits. The section names the willful cutoff of heat, running water, hot water, electric, gas or other essential service as a banned method.

The penalty is real. Under Ala. Code 35-9A-407, a tenant who is locked out or cut off can recover possession or end the lease. In either case the tenant can recover up to 3 months' rent or actual damages, whichever is greater, plus attorney's fees.

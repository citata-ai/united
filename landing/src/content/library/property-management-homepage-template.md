## What should a property management home page include?

A property management home page should include 7 blocks. They are an owner headline, a path split, proof, services, fees, a short process and a closing call to action. Each block answers one question an owner asks. The order matters, because most attention goes to the top of the page.

In a 2018 Nielsen Norman Group eyetracking study, 120 users spent about 57% of their page-viewing time above the fold. They spent 74% in the first two screens. So the first two screens must carry the offer, the proof and the next step.

| # | Block | Owner question it answers | Keep it to |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Headline and subhead | Do you manage my kind of rental, here? | 2 lines |
| 2 | Owner / tenant split | Where do I click? | 2 buttons |
| 3 | Proof strip | Can I trust you? | 3 to 5 facts |
| 4 | Services | What do you do for me? | 4 to 6 items |
| 5 | Fee summary | What does it cost? | 3 lines and a link |
| 6 | How it works | What happens after I call? | 3 or 4 steps |
| 7 | Closing call to action | What do I do now? | 1 button, 1 line |

## Home page template you can copy

The template below is generic. Replace every bracket with your own facts. Delete any line you cannot prove.

```text
BLOCK 1: HEADLINE
H1: [Home type] property management in [City and nearby areas]
Subhead: We manage [number] rental homes for owners in [area].
Rent collection, repairs, tenant placement and monthly statements.
Button: Get a free rental analysis

BLOCK 2: PATH SPLIT
[Owners: Get a rental analysis]   [Tenants: Pay rent / Request a repair / See rentals]

BLOCK 3: PROOF STRIP
[Rating] stars from [number] Google reviews (link to profile)
[Number] doors managed | In business since [year]
Licensed [license type], [state], no. [number]

BLOCK 4: SERVICES
- Tenant placement and screening
- Rent collection and owner payouts by [day of month]
- Maintenance with [24/7 line / vendor network]
- Inspections: move-in, move-out and [frequency]
- Monthly statements and year-end tax forms
- [One service your competitors do not offer]

BLOCK 5: FEES
Management fee: [rate] of collected rent
Leasing fee: [amount]
Other fees: [list, or "none"]
Link: See every fee

BLOCK 6: HOW IT WORKS
1. Ask for a rental analysis. We reply within [time].
2. We visit the home and send a written proposal.
3. You sign the agreement. We take over on [date].
4. You get statements and payouts each month.

BLOCK 7: CLOSING CALL TO ACTION
Thinking about a manager for your rental in [City]?
Button: Get a free rental analysis
Phone: [number] | Office: [address]
```

Check your state's rules on what a broker must show in ads, such as a license number. This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney.

## What should the headline say to owners?

The headline should name the home type, the place and the reader in plain words. An owner who lands from a Google search wants to know one thing first: do you manage homes like mine, here?

Write it so a stranger can repeat it. Compare these two:

- **Weak:** "Stress-free property management."
- **Clear:** "Single-family rental management in [City] and [County]."

The clear version also helps AI answers. An engine that reads your page can quote the place and the home type. Our [AI search checklist for property management websites](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website) covers the rest of that work.

Put one proof fact in the subhead. A number of doors or years in business works if it is true.

## How do I route owners and tenants apart?

You route owners and tenants apart with two clear buttons near the top of the page. Property Manager Websites called this the "traffic cop" home page in 2013. It named four audiences: current tenants, future tenants, current owners and future owners.

Each audience needs a different next page.

| Visitor | What they want | Send them to |
|---|---|---|
| Future owner | Fit, price, trust | Rental analysis, fees, owner services |
| Current owner | Statements, payouts | Owner portal login |
| Current tenant | Pay rent, report a repair | Tenant portal, repair request |
| Future tenant | Homes for rent | Listings page |

Goodjuju's website benchmark of 35 property management companies lists "owner pages, separate from renters" as one of four fixes it sees make the biggest difference. That is the agency's view, not a measured effect. But the logic is simple: a tenant link in the main menu should not compete with the owner offer.

## Which proof belongs near the top?

Proof that an owner can check belongs near the top: reviews, doors managed, years in business, license and fees. Put each fact close to the claim it supports.

Reviews carry the most weight with local buyers. BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults found:

- 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses.
- 47% will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews.
- 74% look for reviews from the last three months.

So show the review count and link to the profile. Do not show a stars widget with no count.

Watch for: the FTC rule on reviews and testimonials, 16 CFR part 465, published on August 22, 2024 (89 FR 68077). It bans fake reviews and testimonials (465.2). It bans paying for reviews on the condition that they are positive or negative (465.4). A testimonial from your own staff needs a clear disclosure (465.5). Use only real reviews from real owners. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Where should the main call to action go?

The main call to action should go in the first screen and again at the end of the page. Use one owner action, with the same words in both places.

Good owner actions give value before the call:

- **Free rental analysis.** The owner gets a rent estimate for the home.
- **Fee calculator.** The owner sees the yearly cost. You can link to a tool such as our [property management fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator).
- **Written proposal.** For owners who are ready to compare managers.

Keep the form short. Goodjuju's benchmark lists "a short form on a fast site" as another of its four fixes. For speed, Google's web.dev guide sets "good" Largest Contentful Paint at 2.5 seconds or less, at the 75th percentile of page loads.

## Home page checklist before launch

Use this list before you publish the page.

- [ ] The H1 names the home type and the city.
- [ ] Owner and tenant paths are both visible without a scroll.
- [ ] The review count links to the review profile.
- [ ] Every number on the page is true today.
- [ ] The fee summary links to a full fee table.
- [ ] The main owner button appears at the top and the end.
- [ ] The form asks for 5 fields or fewer.
- [ ] Phone, address and license appear in text, not only in an image.
- [ ] The page loads in 2.5 seconds or less on a phone.
- [ ] No text describes ideal tenants by any protected class under the Fair Housing Act.

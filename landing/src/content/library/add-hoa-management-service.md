## How Is HOA Work Different From Rental Management?

HOA management supports an association and its board, which creates a different decision process from serving a rental owner. Build a separate service scope.

A rental manager may handle rent, leasing and property upkeep for an owner. Association work can involve shared property, budgets, assessments, meetings and governing documents. Define who decides, who carries out decisions and who can approve spending.

Florida's statutory definition in section 468.431 includes association financial, meeting and maintenance tasks. That is one state's defined scope, not a national list of everything a manager may do. Ask local counsel to review your proposed tasks.

Meet with the board before quoting. Ask about open projects, records, disputes and how decisions are approved. Review the governing documents with qualified advisers. A service proposal should reflect actual needs rather than a copied rental-management package.

Check staff capacity for meeting work and member questions. These tasks can arrive outside normal office hours and involve several decision makers. Plan how staff record board instructions so a private request does not become an unauthorized association decision.

## How Should You Price HOA Management?

Price association work from the scope, workload and approval process. A per-home figure alone can hide meetings, projects and difficult records.

Use a base service schedule with separately defined extra work. The table below lists suggested pricing categories. It does not state market rates.

| Work category | Cost questions to ask | Scope term to define |
|---|---|---|
| Financial records | What records need cleanup? | Routine reports and added accounting |
| Board meetings | What preparation and follow-up are needed? | Included meetings and extra work |
| Member communication | Who answers which questions? | Contact route and escalation |
| Vendor coordination | What work is routine or project-based? | Approval authority and added charges |
| Common property review | How far must staff travel? | Site visits and reports |
| Transition | What records and funds must transfer? | Intake tasks and completion evidence |

Estimate staff time using the proposed service calendar. Include preparation, minutes support where agreed, and follow-up. A short meeting can create substantial work afterward. Keep that work visible when discussing the fee.

Ask the board to identify upcoming projects before approving a base quote. Major repair or insurance work may need a separate scope and specialist support. Explain added charges before the board accepts the agreement. Use the [fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) only for rental-fee context, not as an HOA pricing benchmark.

## Which Licenses or Certifications Apply?

Association management can have distinct licensing duties, so verify the destination state's rules for both people and the firm. A rental license may not be enough.

Florida provides a concrete example. Section 468.431 defines covered compensated management at associations with more than 10 units or budgets exceeding $100,000. Section 468.432 also sets firm licensing duties, including a firm threshold of more than 10 units or a budget of $100,000 or greater. The wording differs, so check both sections rather than merging the thresholds.

Do not use that example as a rule for another state. Ask the relevant regulator which tasks require a license, which people need one and which entity may contract. Confirm supervision and continuing duties before advertising the service.

Treat professional credentials as a separate issue. A training certificate can help assess skills but does not automatically replace a required license. Verify any credential claim with the issuing body. State exactly what the person holds in the proposal.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current law or ask a local attorney. Give counsel the actual planned scope and association documents, not only the service name.

## How Do HOA Company Sale Multiples Compare?

The sources reviewed do not establish a reliable like-for-like valuation premium for HOA management. Do not build the launch case on a claimed higher multiple.

A sale multiple compares price with a defined financial measure. Without the measure, deal terms and verified earnings, a number gives little help. Ask a qualified adviser for evidence relevant to your company rather than repeating a headline range.

Build the service case from operating economics first. Estimate the fees you can earn, the staff cost and the work you must fund. Check owner concentration at the association level. Losing a board contract can remove many homes from your service list at once.

Review contract transfer and renewal terms with counsel. Ask how a board decision or change in leadership could affect the relationship. An attractive recurring fee does not prove that revenue will transfer to a buyer.

Keep rental and HOA results separate in management reports. You need to know whether the new service pays for its own staff and systems. Shared overhead can make an early service look stronger than it is if you omit the time senior staff spend supporting it.

## How Do You Test HOA Service Before Expanding?

Run a defined pilot with a suitable association and a named service lead. Accept only work your team and advisers can support.

- Review the association's documents, finances and open projects.
- Confirm licenses, insurance and specialist support.
- Agree a service calendar and decision process.
- Define routine work and separately approved projects.
- Set board contact, member contact and emergency routes.
- Test financial reporting and access controls.
- Review actual staff time against the quote before adding accounts.

Ask the board to confirm who can issue instructions. Record approvals so staff do not rely on memory. Route legal questions to counsel rather than letting a coordinator improvise an answer.

Use the pilot review to find missing skills and hidden work. If the company cannot deliver the promised scope at a sound margin, revise the offer. Start by mapping your current team against the tasks in the proposed association agreement.

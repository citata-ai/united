## South Dakota rent and notice rules at a glance

South Dakota rent rules sit in a few short statutes. We read each one on the South Dakota Legislature site on 2026-10-19.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| State cap on rent increases | None | No statute sets one |
| Local rent control | Banned for private residential property | SDCL 6-1-13 |
| Notice to change rent, month-to-month | Written, at least 30 days before the month ends | SDCL 43-32-13 |
| Tenant's exit after a change notice | Notice within 15 days; lease ends on the 1st of the next month | SDCL 43-32-13 |
| Notice to end a tenancy with no fixed term | As long as the rent period, max 1 month | SDCL 43-32-15 |
| Rent raise above fair market value after a protected act | Can be retaliation | SDCL 43-32-27 |

## Does South Dakota have a cap on rent increases or rent control?

South Dakota has no cap on rent increases. No state statute limits how much a landlord may raise the rent. And SDCL 6-1-13 stops local rent control.

SDCL 6-1-13 says: "No local governmental unit may enact, maintain, or enforce any ordinance, resolution, or other enactment that would have the effect of controlling the amount of rent charged for leasing private residential property." The law dates from 1990. It does not limit a city that manages property it has an interest in, such as public housing.

So the amount is a market choice. The timing is not. The notice rules below still apply.

## How many days of notice does a South Dakota landlord need to raise the rent?

A South Dakota landlord needs written notice at least 30 days before the end of the month to change a month-to-month rent. SDCL 43-32-13 lets the landlord "modify the terms of the lease to take effect at the expiration of the month."

The notice works on its own. Once you serve it, the new rent becomes part of the lease "if the tenant shall continue to hold the premises after the expiration of the month" (SDCL 43-32-13). The tenant does not have to sign anything.

### Worked example: a December 1 increase

Here is a sample timeline. The rent amounts are an example, not market data.

| Step | Date or amount |
|---|---|
| Current rent | $1,100 a month |
| New rent | $1,160 a month (+$60, or 5.5%) |
| Month the change follows | November (ends November 30) |
| 30 days before November 30 | October 31 |
| Last day to serve written notice | October 31 |
| New rent starts | December 1 |
| Tenant's window to give notice to leave | 15 days after the tenant receives your notice |

If you serve the notice on November 5, it is less than 30 days before November 30. Then the new rent waits one more month and starts on January 1.

What to do:

1. **Write the notice.** Name the new rent and the date it starts.
2. **Serve it early.** Aim for 35 to 45 days before the month ends, so a mail delay does not cost you a month.
3. **Keep proof.** Record how and when the tenant received the notice.
4. **Watch the 15 days.** If the tenant gives notice within 15 days, plan for a turnover on the 1st of the next month.

## Can a South Dakota tenant leave after a rent increase notice?

A South Dakota tenant may leave after a change notice. SDCL 43-32-13 says the tenant "may terminate his lease effective the first day of the next month" by notice to the landlord within 15 days after receiving the change notice.

So every increase is also a chance of a vacancy. Compare the extra rent with a month of lost rent and turnover cost before you send the notice.

## Can a South Dakota landlord raise the rent during a fixed-term lease?

SDCL 43-32-13 covers leases "from month to month." It does not give a landlord a right to change the rent in the middle of a fixed term. For a fixed-term lease, the lease itself sets the rent. Read the lease for any clause on rent changes, and ask a lawyer before you act on one.

When a fixed term ends and the tenant stays, SDCL 43-32-14 applies. If you accept rent after the term ends, the law presumes you renewed the lease "on the same terms and for the same time, not exceeding one year." So send any new rent terms before the lease ends, not after.

## How much notice ends a month-to-month tenancy in South Dakota?

Notice to end a tenancy with no fixed term must be at least as long as the rent period, but not more than one month (SDCL 43-32-15). Without that notice, the law presumes the tenancy renews for another period.

| Rent period | Minimum notice to end |
|---|---|
| Week to week | 1 week before the week ends |
| Month to month | 1 month before the month ends |

The statute does not say the notice must be in writing. Give it in writing anyway, so you can prove the date.

## Can a rent increase be retaliation in South Dakota?

A rent increase can be retaliation under SDCL 43-32-27. A tenant may sue if the landlord "increases rents above fair market value" after one of three events:

- The tenant complained in good faith to a government agency about a housing code problem that affects health and safety.
- The tenant gave written notice of a repair under SDCL 43-32-9.
- The tenant organized or joined a tenant union.

The same section covers cuts to electric, gas, water or sewer service, and a notice to vacate that is not based on a lease breach. A notice to vacate given more than 180 days after the event is a defense. Not renewing a written lease is not retaliation. A tenant who wins gets the remedies of SDCL 43-32-6, and the court may award attorney's fees (SDCL 43-32-28).

## Do South Dakota cities add their own rent rules?

South Dakota cities cannot cap rent. SDCL 6-1-13 bars any local rule that controls the amount of rent on private residential property. Cities may still have rules on other topics, such as housing codes. Check your city's code before you lease a unit.

## Massachusetts security deposit rules at a glance

Massachusetts puts its whole deposit law in one section: M.G.L. c. 186 §15B. We read the full text on the Massachusetts Legislature site (version as of 2026-09-29). Here are the rules a landlord must track.

| Rule | Limit or deadline | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum deposit | First month's rent | c. 186 §15B(1)(b)(iii) |
| Other move-in charges | First month, last month, lock and key cost only | §15B(1)(b) |
| Deposit receipt | At the time you take the deposit | §15B(2)(b) |
| Statement of condition | When you take the deposit or within 10 days of move-in, whichever is later | §15B(2)(c) |
| Bank account | Separate, interest-bearing, in a Massachusetts bank | §15B(3)(a) |
| Bank receipt to tenant | Within 30 days of taking the deposit | §15B(3)(a) |
| Interest | 5% per year or the bank's lower rate, paid each year | §15B(3)(b) |
| Return deadline | 30 days after the tenancy ends | §15B(4) |
| Itemized damage list | Within the same 30 days, sworn under penalty of perjury | §15B(4)(iii) |
| Keep records | 2 years after the tenancy ends | §15B(2)(d) |
| New owner notice | Within 45 days of a transfer | §15B(5) |
| Penalty | 3 times the deposit, plus 5% interest, costs, attorney's fees | §15B(7) |

## What is the maximum security deposit in Massachusetts?

The maximum Massachusetts security deposit is equal to the first month's rent. §15B(1)(b) lists the only 4 amounts you may require at or before the start of a tenancy:

1. **First month's rent.**
2. **Last month's rent,** at the same rate as the first month.
3. **A security deposit** equal to the first month's rent.
4. **The purchase and installation cost** for a key and lock.

So for a $2,000 unit, the most you can collect at move-in is $6,000 plus the lock cost. After the tenancy starts, §15B(1)(d) bars you from asking for more than the current month's rent in advance.

Watch for: the text changed on August 1, 2025 (St. 2025, c. 9, §§54–55). The rule now says no lessor "or agent of the lessor" may require the tenant to pay the lessor or the agent more than those 4 amounts. The same amendment lets the Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities write rules for an optional fee in place of a deposit. That fee, plus any deposit, may not exceed 1 month's rent in total. Check whether those rules are in force before you offer it.

## How many days does a Massachusetts landlord have to return a deposit?

A Massachusetts landlord has 30 days to return the deposit or its balance. The clock starts when occupancy ends under a tenancy at will, or at the end of the term in a written lease (§15B(4)).

Interest is due on the same schedule. If the tenancy ends before an anniversary date, the tenant gets all accrued interest within 30 days (§15B(3)(b)). Ask each tenant for a forwarding address. The last-month's-rent receipt must tell the tenant to give you one (§15B(2)(a)).

## What can a Massachusetts landlord deduct from a deposit?

A Massachusetts landlord can deduct only 3 things, and §15B(4) says no deduction may be made for any other purpose:

- **Unpaid rent or water charges** that the tenant did not lawfully withhold.
- **Unpaid real estate tax increases** the tenant owes under a tax escalation clause that meets §15C.
- **Damage repair,** a reasonable amount, caused by the tenant or a person under the tenant's control. Reasonable wear and tear is excluded.

You may not deduct for damage listed on the statement of condition, or on the tenant's own signed damage list. The exception: you repaired it, and you can prove the new damage is unrelated and the tenant caused it.

## Does a Massachusetts landlord have to give an itemized list?

Yes. For any damage deduction, a Massachusetts landlord must give an itemized list within the 30 days (§15B(4)(iii)). The list must:

- [ ] be sworn by you or your agent under the pains and penalties of perjury;
- [ ] describe in precise detail the damage and the repairs needed;
- [ ] include written evidence of cost, such as estimates, bills, invoices or receipts.

If you miss the list, you forfeit the right to keep any part of the deposit (§15B(6)(b)). You also lose the right to counterclaim for damage in the tenant's suit.

The list depends on the statement of condition from move-in. §15B(2)(c) requires a separate signed statement, with a fixed notice in 12-point bold type at the top of the first page. The tenant has 15 days to return it or add a damage list. If the tenant adds a list, you have 15 days to sign agreement or attach a clear statement of disagreement.

## Must a Massachusetts landlord pay interest or use a separate account?

Yes to both. §15B(3)(a) requires a separate, interest-bearing account in a bank located in Massachusetts. The account must keep the deposit beyond the reach of your creditors, and it must transfer to a new owner. Within 30 days, give the tenant a receipt with the bank's name and location, the amount and the account number.

Interest runs from the first day of the tenancy if you hold the deposit for 1 year or more. The rate is 5% per year, or the lower rate the bank actually paid (§15B(3)(b)). At the end of each year, send a statement and either pay the interest or tell the tenant to deduct it from the next rent.

Worked example, at the 5% cap: a $2,000 deposit earns up to $100 a year. If your bank paid 1%, you owe $20. Last month's rent paid in advance earns interest under the same rate rule (§15B(2)(a)).

## What is the penalty for keeping a deposit wrongly in Massachusetts?

The penalty is 3 times the deposit, plus interest at 5% from the due date, plus court costs and reasonable attorney's fees (§15B(7)). It applies if you:

- fail to put the deposit in a proper account;
- fail to transfer the deposit to a new owner as §15B(5) requires;
- fail to return the deposit or balance, with interest, within 30 days.

For a $2,000 deposit, triple damages alone are $6,000, before the tenant's legal fees. Two smaller lapses carry their own sanction. No bank receipt within 30 days lets the tenant demand the deposit back at once (§15B(3)(a)). Refusing to show your deposit records does the same (§15B(2)(d)). Any lease term that conflicts with §15B is void (§15B(8)).

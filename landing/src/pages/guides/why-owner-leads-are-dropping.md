Landlord lead loss is the drop in landlords who contact a property manager after a web search. You see it in your lead form before you see it in a report. Fewer landlords write in each month, and paid ads cost more to fill the gap. Two changes cause most of the drop. Both are measurable, and you can act on both.

## Is search demand for property managers really down?

Search demand for generic terms is down in the data we checked. For a Chicago client, we pulled 7 generic terms from Google Keyword Planner. The yearly average fell 46% in the Chicago area and 43% across the US (2025/26 against 2024/25).

Two limits apply to this number:

- **It is one sample.** Seven terms, one client, one tool. Your city and terms can differ.
- **The cause is a hypothesis.** We think landlords now ask AI assistants instead of typing "property management near me". We cannot prove that from Keyword Planner data.

The client is Fridman Properties. Its owner is CEO of MyRemotely, our partner. The full baseline is in the [Fridman Properties case study](/case-studies/fridman-properties).

To check your own market, open Keyword Planner. Compare the last 12 months with the 12 months before for your 5 to 10 main terms.

## Why do fewer searches turn into clicks?

Fewer searches turn into clicks because Google now answers the question on the results page. The AI summary at the top is called an AI Overview. Landlords read it and often stop there.

The data from two large studies:

| Finding | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Users who click a result when an AI summary shows | 8% of visits | Pew Research, 2025 |
| Users who click a result when no AI summary shows | 15% of visits | Pew Research, 2025 |
| Users who click a link inside the AI summary | 1% of visits | Pew Research, 2025 |
| Searches in the Pew sample with an AI summary | 18% | Pew Research, 2025 |
| Click rate of the top result when an AI Overview shows | 58% lower | Ahrefs, 2026 |

Pew tracked 900 US adults in March 2025. Ahrefs compared 300,000 keywords from December 2023 and December 2025. Ahrefs calls its result a correlation, not proof of cause.

For you, this means a top ranking brings fewer visits than it did in 2023. A landlord can read "what does a property manager charge" in the AI Overview and never reach your fee page.

## Where do landlords ask now?

Landlords now have three places to ask besides the classic Google results. We do not have a survey of landlords. The usage data for all users is clear:

- **ChatGPT** sends 74.78% of all AI referral traffic to websites (SE Ranking, 2026).
- **Gemini** is second with 11.56%, and grew 231% from 2025 to 2026 (SE Ranking, 2026).
- **Google AI Mode** passed 1 billion monthly active users, and its queries are 3 times longer than classic searches (Google, 2026).

Longer queries matter for landlords. A landlord types "property manager in Evanston for a 2-flat, I live in Texas, what fees are normal". The AI answer names 3 to 5 companies. If your company is not one of them, the landlord never sees your site.

## Is SEO still worth it for a property management company?

SEO is still worth it for your company. AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT search and Gemini all build answers from pages that a search engine can find and read. A page that Google and Bing do not index rarely gets cited.

What changes is the goal. In 2020, the goal was to rank and get the click. Now the goal is to rank, get cited in the AI answer, and get named as a choice.

AI traffic is small but it converts. AI platforms send 0.32% of all website traffic (SE Ranking, 2026). At Ahrefs, AI search was 0.5% of visits but 12.1% of signups in one 30-day period (Ahrefs, 2025). A landlord who arrives from an AI answer has often read your name already.

## What does AI cite when landlords ask about property managers?

AI cites a mix of manager websites and directories when landlords ask for a manager. On 2026-10-16, we asked ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini 24 questions about one Chicago manager, 6 answers each. These sources came up most:

| Source type | Example | Answers that cited it |
|---|---|---|
| Review site | Yelp | 35 |
| Directory list | Expertise.com | 22 |
| Service marketplace | Thumbtack | 17 |
| Manager websites | Company sites | Top source type |

The manager was named in 8 of 132 non-brand answers. For the Section 8 question, it was named in 0 of 6.

Your own site is one input. Directories, reviews and lists are the others. In our view, AI names a company with more confidence when many sources describe it the same way.

## What should a 50 to 500 door manager change?

A 50 to 500 door manager should change pages, listings and tracking, in that order. Each step below fits a small team.

1. **Write one page per landlord question.** List the 10 questions landlords ask on intro calls: fees, leasing fee, evictions, Section 8, landlords out of state. Answer each one in the first 2 sentences of its own page or section.
2. **Give exact numbers.** Write your real fees, for example "8% of monthly rent". A phrase like "competitive pricing" gives AI nothing to quote.
3. **Make each city page different.** Name the neighborhoods, the local rules and the property types you manage there. Near-copies of one template give AI no reason to pick a page.
4. **Fix your listings.** Use the same name, address and phone on Google Business Profile, Yelp, Bing Places, Expertise.com and Thumbtack. Answer every review.
5. **Get named on the lists AI reads.** Ask local directories and "best property managers in [city]" lists to include you. Ask happy landlords to post honest reviews.
6. **Let AI crawlers in.** Check that robots.txt allows Googlebot, Bingbot, OAI-SearchBot and Claude-SearchBot. Submit your sitemap to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.

For more site details, see the [AI search checklist for property management websites](/guides/ai-search-checklist-property-management-website).

Do not buy fake reviews or mass-produced pages. They break platform rules and give AI wrong facts about you.

## How do you measure landlord leads from AI search?

You measure landlord leads from AI search with three numbers, checked each month. Search clicks alone now miss part of your leads.

| What to measure | Tool | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| AI citations | A fixed list of 20 to 30 landlord prompts, run in ChatGPT, Gemini and AI Mode | If AI names or links you |
| AI referral visits | GA4 or Ahrefs Web Analytics, filtered by chatgpt.com, gemini.google.com, perplexity.ai | Visits that AI sends |
| Landlord source | A "How did you hear about us?" field on the landlord form | What the landlord says |

Watch for two gaps. ChatGPT drops the referrer on some links. AI Overviews and AI Mode show as plain google.com. So the self-reported field often catches AI leads that analytics misses.

Run the same prompts each month and keep the answers. AI answers change from run to run. A trend over 3 months tells you more than one result.

We track our own site this way. In our first check on 2026-10-18, AI named citata.ai in 0 of 108 non-brand answers. We will publish the next number. The setup is in our guide to [tracking AI traffic for property management](/guides/track-ai-traffic-property-management).

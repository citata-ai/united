## Do landlords use AI to find a property manager?

Choosing a property manager with AI is asking ChatGPT, Gemini or Google AI for a short list of firms, then checking the names. More than half of small landlords say they have done this. Harris Poll asked 500 US landlords with 1 to 10 units about AI tools in December 2025 (PM Trends, 2026). Here are their answers:

| Answer to the survey question | Share |
|---|---|
| Used AI tools for this, and plan to again | 45% |
| Used AI tools for this, and do not plan to again | 9% |
| Never used AI tools for this, but plan to | 30% |
| Never used AI tools for this, and do not plan to | 16% |

Add the rows and you get 2 numbers: 54% have used AI for this, and 75% plan to.

Watch for: the survey records what landlords say, not what they did. The margin of error is 6.1 points. Vendors sponsor the report, and Rentvine is a title sponsor. So use 54% as a strong signal, not an exact count.

Consumer data points the same way. BrightLocal asked 1,002 US adults in 2026. Use of ChatGPT and other AI tools for local recommendations rose from 6% to 45% in one year (BrightLocal, 2026). The same survey found that 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses.

Surveys tell you that landlords ask. Our own test shows what they get. The rest of this guide uses that data.

## What do landlords ask ChatGPT and Gemini?

Landlords ask AI the same things they used to type into Google, but in full sentences. Most landlord questions include a city, a property type, a fee or a special need. These are real prompts from our Chicago test (our data, 2026-10-16):

| Type | Example prompt |
|---|---|
| City | "Best property management company in Arlington Heights, IL?" |
| Small landlord | "What are the best property management companies in Chicago for a small landlord?" |
| Fees | "How much does property management cost in Chicago, and which companies offer the lowest fees?" |
| Contract | "Chicago property management company with no leasing fee and no long-term contract?" |
| Single-family | "I own a single-family rental home in Chicago. Which property management company should I hire?" |
| Out-of-state landlord | "I live out of state and bought a rental house in Chicago. Which property management company should I use?" |
| Section 8 | "Which Chicago property management companies work with Section 8 / CHA Housing Choice Voucher tenants?" |

AI engines break each prompt into many small searches. This is called query fan-out. One fee question can trigger searches for "management fee Chicago", "leasing fee" and "contract terms". So the manager with exact answers on each small topic has more chances to be found.

## What does a typical AI answer look like?

A typical AI answer is a short list of companies with one line about each. In our Chicago test, the median answer named 5 companies (our data, 2026-10-16). ChatGPT's median was 4 names. Claude's median was 10.

Each answer usually has these parts:

- **A short list.** Company names, often with the area each one serves.
- **A reason per name.** "Focus on single-family homes", "no leasing fee" or "4.9 stars on Google".
- **Links to sources.** The manager's own site, plus Yelp, a directory or a review site.
- **A caveat.** "Ask about fees when the unit is empty" or "check reviews before you sign".

In the ChatGPT app, logged out, some answers also showed a map with star ratings (our test, 2026-10-16). So a Google Business Profile with correct data and reviews feeds the answer too.

## Which sources does AI read to pick a property manager?

AI engines pick names mostly from manager websites, review sites and local directories. In our Chicago baseline of 144 answers on 2026-10-16, the most cited sources were manager sites. After those came Yelp (35 answers), Expertise.com (22 answers) and Thumbtack (17 answers). The test ran 24 prompts on ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, 2 runs each (our data).

Google AI Overviews and ChatGPT do not read the web in the same way:

| Engine | Where its sources come from | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Google AI Overviews | 38% of cited pages rank in Google's top 10 for the query | Ahrefs, March 2026 |
| ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot | Only 12% of cited links rank in Google's top 10 for the same prompt | Ahrefs, 2025 |
| ChatGPT | "Best X" lists were 43.8% of the page types in its sources | Ahrefs, 2025 |

What this means for your company:

- **For Google AI Overviews,** classic SEO still matters. A page in the top 10 has a good chance to be cited.
- **For ChatGPT,** "best property managers in [city]" lists matter. Directories like Expertise.com publish these lists.
- **For all engines,** your own site must state facts clearly. The engines quote short, exact answers.

## How do landlord questions differ from renter questions?

Landlord questions and renter questions get different sources. Renters ask for an apartment. Landlords ask for a company to hire.

For renters, listing sites win. Peek's AI Discovery Black Book found that 72% of the sources AI cited for apartments were listing sites (Peek, 2026). Corporate home pages got under 0.3% of AI citations (Peek, 2026). So for renters, work on Zillow, Apartments.com and your Google Business Profile.

For landlords, the picture is different. In our Chicago test, manager websites were the top sources for landlord questions (our data, 2026-10-16). So your own site has a real chance to be the source for landlord questions. Landlords are also the people who pay you. ## What do landlords want from a property manager?

Landlords judge a property manager on service. Buildium surveyed 300 US owners with small portfolios in early 2026, and 95% of them own 1 to 20 units. Only 23% say their property manager gives excellent value (Buildium, 2026).

| Buildium 2026 finding | Top answers |
|---|---|
| Top priorities when choosing a manager | Tenant management 57%, maintenance coordination 43%, customer service 34%, responsiveness 31% |
| Reasons to switch managers | Poor communication 57%, declines in service quality 54%, lack of transparency 34% |
| Expected response time | Same day 43%, next business day 42% |

Buildium sells property management software, so read these numbers as vendor data. Its article does not give the exact group behind each percentage.

What to do: put these topics on your landlord pages. State how you screen tenants, how you handle repairs and how fast you reply. A short, exact sentence on each topic gives AI something to quote.

## How does a property manager get into the AI answer?

A firm gets into the AI answer when the engines find clear, matching facts about the company in many places. You cannot control the answer. You can improve the sources the engines read.

1. **Write landlord pages with exact answers.** State your fee, leasing fee, contract length and service area in plain numbers. Our free [fee calculator](/tools/property-management-fee-calculator) shows how landlords add up those fees. Add one page per city you serve, with real local facts.
2. **Answer the special questions.** Write a clear page on Section 8 for landlords, out-of-state landlords and single-family homes, if you serve them. Have a lawyer check the legal parts.
3. **Collect real landlord reviews.** Ask landlords, not only tenants, to review you on Google and Yelp. AI answers often quote star ratings and review counts.
4. **Complete your directory profiles.** Claim your profiles on Yelp, Thumbtack and Expertise.com. Fill in services, areas and prices.
5. **Keep your name, address and phone the same everywhere.** AI engines join facts from many sites. A different address or an old phone number can make AI see two companies.
6. **Earn mentions on lists and local sites.** Ask local investor groups, chambers and "best of" list writers to review you. Third-party mentions tell AI that others know you.

## How can you test what AI says about your company?

You can test what AI says about your company with one short check each month. Use the same questions each time, so you can compare results.

1. **Write 5 landlord questions.** Use your city, your property types and your fees. Copy the style of the table above.
2. **Log out first.** Use a private browser window. A logged-in account can change the answer.
3. **Ask the same 5 questions** in ChatGPT, Gemini and Google (look at the AI Overview and AI Mode).
4. **Record 3 things per answer.** Is your company named? Is your site linked? Which other sources are linked?
5. **Repeat monthly.** AI answers change by day and engine, so one test is not enough.

Watch for: directories and review sites that appear again and again. Those are the sources to fix first.
